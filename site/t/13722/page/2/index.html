Cold1 November 2009#28
Let's break this down for you. What you are building is several boxes inside a larger box (refer to xspaces pic) the larger box is your original structure. This is your first leaf. Let's make sure this is only one leaf, no air pockets anywhere inside the walls? Now this outside leaf has as much mass added to it as possible without creating air pockets right? And there is no holes in it anywhere either ? If you filled it up with water it wouldn't leak anywhere, even if the building was turned upside down ? now the inner boxes. Every room inside of this building will be absolutely independent from every other room (except for the floor). No boards, bracing, anything, touching between rooms. Now each of these rooms will also have an independent ceiling. The 2nd leaf is created by putting mass on only one side of the studs on the walls and ceiling. At this point you will have 2 independent, isolated leaves of mass separating every inhabited space within your studio and also 2 leaves of mass separating the interior from the exterior. This is the basic concept for everything you do in studio construction. For details on how to build this refer to Rod's book. I just want to make sure you are absolutely clear on this concept because some of your questions lead me to believe you are not. Steve

xSpace11 November 2009#49
"I've decided to go for an inside out wall ... I'm planning on putting 4-layers of plasterboard on the walls" A typical inside out wall is going to be raised to an existing wall. This means you will be attempting to raise a four layer sheetrock wall and I can tell you not only is this getting into the area of the impossible it is approaching waste:) If you are going to use four layers then you may as well install the sheetrock on the interior side of the framing and get the benefit of the air to enhance the low frequency reduction. It makes very little sense when you consider the reduced air pocket and the reduced low frequency isolation to attempt some thing like this. It will be difficult to raise a wall of this type, so you guys be careful.
