grant wrote:
As far as permits and such:Regarding access plans, John's drawing looks pretty good. Are those sliding doors pretty expensive?
I will not mince words. Yes. These will not be your garden variety doors but ones with thick laminated glass and really good seals. However they are replacing the functions of BOTH the doors and the windows. They will provide excellent sight lines and a feeling of spaciousness that an ordinary door/window arrangement will not. They will also be much easier to operate and move stuff through than pairs of swinging doors. They will also be easier to operate than pairs of swinging doors.
Other main questions involve windows to exterior. The thing is, I've been planning (perhaps unintelligently) to have the builder put in normal windows. Then I thought I'd build a (floating) room within the basement room having windows that simply line up with the actual exterior windows. Is this a bad idea?
Again, in a word, Yes. you want the window to match both the mass per square foot of the leaf that it is in and to have good solid seals. If it was a matter of years before you would be completing the studio I would say go ahead and get the cheapies to start on a present dollars/future dollars consideration. But if it is right away you will be ripping it out right away.
Is this double paned window something I can have the builder put in, then my troubles are over?
Double pane windows are good for thermal insulation. For sonic isolation you want a single heavy pane of glass that matches the weight per square foot for the walls.
Are these windows safe for fire escape?
Your building inspector is the final arbiter but generally for a room of few occupants a sliding door is a legal exit. When a room gets to a certain occupancy the exit hardware has to be of the type where you need only push on a bar in order to exit. That is usually the point where a sliding door would no longer be legal (The automatic sliding doors you see on businesses have a special breakaway feature.)
And by the way, neighbors will be close. But the main reason for sound isolation is to get clean recordings.
Isolation is a two-way street. If you can't hear them they can't hear you. You may think that your isolation is not as important because you just care about clean recordings but it is really the opposite. See if they are mowing their lawn and can hear you playing softly...no big deal. But if you are recording and you can hear their lawn mower quietly in the background...wasted track.

Oversights: in my previous posts I did not mention the importance of the size of the gap between the glass. Vagueness: I blandly accepted you describing the installation of the glass in the concrete as "the normal way" without getting clarification what "the normal way" is in your region. Details: glass location is one detail. A related detail is that the frame that glass be put in is NOT hollow, like a metal extrusion or metal formed channel.
