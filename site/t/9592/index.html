gullfo19 October 2007#11
you want the speaker to be isolated from the box. the box can contact the frame as longs as its solid and won't vibrate. if you're building the box to fit tightly around the speaker, then isolate the box. what you don't want is to isolate the speaker from the box and then the box from the concrete box since then you're building a membrane absorber... generally speaking you want the soffits/bezel to act as an extension of the speaker housing without significant diffraction, resonant or absorption effects. this means the face of the speaker is level with the soffit and the soffit is massive and rigid and damped. this also means the speaker should be isolated from the structure (structure borne transfer) and also not move (high frequency phase effects from movement). then to top it off you have to create some sort of step baffle compensation for the additional 3-6db bass increase as well as consider the transfer of heat away from a powered monitor (if it is one), the bass port on the back (if it has one), or just air flow between top and bottom cavities as part of any bass trapping system built-in there. so if you seem to be getting conflicting answers, its because you are trying to balance a variety of conflicting requirements. which is one consideration for not soffit mounting the speakers...
