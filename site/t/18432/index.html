Soundman20204 July 2013#2
Hi there "mosa". Welcome! :) It sounds like what you need basically is an isolation booth big enough to fit you and your guitar in, and with isolation on the order of 40 dB or so. You clearly already have the basic idea on how to do this, from what you say, but there are other issues that you it seems you haven't considered yet. The first is weight: to isolate sound you need mass, and lots of it. Yes, 3/4" MDF is one option, and yes 5/8" drywall is another, but they are both heavy, and indeed they MST be heavy in order to isolate. When you say you are on the first floor of the building, that can be confusing in some countries, so I'd like to clarify that: In some places, "first floor" means the one sitting on the ground, while in other places "first floor" means the next one up, since the one on the ground is called the "ground floor". Since you are in the USA, I'm assuming that "first floor" means the one sitting on the ground, and hopefully that the floor under you is a concrete slab poured directly on Mother Earth, with no other rooms below you. Please say Yes! :) If that's the case you are probably OK, but if your floor is actually just wood supported on joists, and there is something below you, then there might be an issue: the structure might not be able to safely carry the amount of extra mass you need. So it's rather important to confirm that! Second is ventilation: You are basically planning to build a closet where you'll be singing and playing hard, working up a sweat, and breathing. In order that you can keep on breathing after a few minutes, you absolutely need some type of ventilation system to feed you clean air and exhaust the stale air. Either that, or you have to figure out how to sing without breathing... :) The ventilation system doesn't have to be complicated, but it does have to isolate enough. You can't just chop a hole in the wall of your booth, as that would defeat the entire purpose of the booth, and wouldn't work anyway: you need a small silent fan to move air through the booth, and you need a simple basic silencer box to keep the sound contained. Third is the door: it has to be just as massive as the walls, and it has to seal absolutely air tight, and there can be no penetrations through it, such as for the lock set. That complicates things a bit, so it's something that you do need to consider. Forth is light: I'm pretty sure you don't like singing and playing in pitch blackness, so you need either a window, or you need electrical power. Either one complicates things a bit more. Probably electrical power is the least expensive of the two, but it needs to be done safely and without compromising your isolation. So those things that are rather important, and often overlooked in the type of project you are planning. OK, back to the basic issue: how to isolate. Your basic plan is fine, but is only half of the solution. MDF has a density of around 800 kg/m3, so the surface density of 1.5" of MDF is roughly 30 kg/m2, or 6 PSF. According to mass law, theoretically you'd be getting about 34 dB of isolation from that, but call it about 30 to be realistic. The good news is that that might actually be enough for what you want. The other good news is that if it isn't enough, you can always add resilient channel to the inside of your booth and put a layer of 5/8" drywall on that, which will greatly increase your isolation. The bad news is that it breaks your budget! Yes you can substitute 5/8" drywall instead of 3/4" MDF, but then your theoretical isolation drops to about 30 dB, but call it more like 25 to be realistic. Not good enough. The other issue is structural integrity: MDF will provide that for your booth, drywall will not. But there's an alternative: Make the first layer 3/4" MDF, and the second layer 5/8" drywall. The MDF gives you the strength (and the cost!), then the drywall adds useful extra mass (at less cost). You'd be back up around 30-something dB of isolation, in theory. By the way, your singing plus guitar is probably closer to 100 dB than 85... So getting that down to 65 needs about 35 dB of isolation. But 65 is still pretty loud: that roughly the level of normal conversation. Anyway, having said all that, I suspect that you'll have enough isolation with the MDF + 5/8" drywall plan, even though this is just a single leaf solution, and if you find it isn't enough, then your "Plan B" is still available: Resilient Channel plus drywall on the inside of the booth. That makes it a full 2-leaf system, which isolates much better. For your final question, to protect the landlord's carpet I would use a sheet of plywood or OSB as the base for the entire booth, and build on top of that. One more thing: it will sound really awful in there! You WILL need to add some acoustic treatment as well, probably in the form of ordinary wall insulation, so you need to budget for that too. Hope that helps! - Stuart -
