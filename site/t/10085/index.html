newbie wrote:
Will that be sufficient or do I need to hire a designer to design this room first and then talk to contractor?
Some contractors have this as a provided service - design, drawing out the view of the new floor plan, how the load is carried etc.. that set of drawings will be agreed upon by the builder and town for the permit to do the work. There will be a couple or a few points where they will want to see proof of the work before proceding - framing and electric are two key points. You can't put wall board up and expect to just explain what you did to the inspector. For me - I like to have someone do one thing and do it well. That is why for our home addition we are set for contruction start this spring, I chose an architect to draw up plans after reviewing the property. My reasoning is that his entire career is devoted to doing this stage of the process and from working alongside contractors he knows what needs the contractors are going to have as well as the town and the home owner. Plus, any architect should have experience with several contractors and could recommend a few for the work based upon the needs. Then you can shop around and basically interview yourself to have differing opinions and price points on the work. But for construction higlighted in this forum for sound isolation, we're dealing with a different beast. That is why it's important for you to do research and/or find a contractor or architect that has some experience with what you are considering.

You want a floor that is overhead in an existing structure that may or may not be able to support it. And how this floor gets supported may be of some concern to the inspection department. This is not an area of isolation(well, it is...but), this is an overhead floor. Flanking is already built into your want of this design, decoupling is not going to happen;)
britune16 March 2008#9
I have a garage with a room over it. There are 12" TJI joists spaced 12" apart. It spans 24'. The end caps are 2.5" wide. It seems to have enough support. If you have a TJI joist, there are several factors in it's design that go into making it's strength, including the thickness of the OSB, and the size of the end caps. But without knowing the details, it looks like you'd have more support than my floor does. As you read this forum, you'll see that an elevated wooden deck as a floor is not considered a good design for a soundproofed room. Rod says it is a mistake to put money into such a design in his book. But, like you, that's the space I have. Good Luck!
