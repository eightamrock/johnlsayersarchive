sharward8 March 2005#8
You're fortunate in some respects in that you don't have an existing building that you're having to retrofit to accommodate a studio. You truly do have a blank pallette from which to work. I think this particular forum (a.k.a., the "Studio Construction" forum on the John Sayers Productions Web site) is more geared towards those who already have a design in mind, but need advice on how to properly construct it. The official description of this Studio Construction forum is "How thick should my walls be, how do you float a floor, what is plasterboard?? etc." As no doubt you've already learned here, there are few ways to do it properly and countless ways to do it incorrectly. In your case, you're probably best served at this stage to thoroughly read the many months' worth of posts on the Design forum (a.k.a, the "Design" forum on the John Sayers Productions Web site). That forum is one of several here on this site, and its official description is "Plans and things, layout, style, where do I put my near-fields etc." The moderator of this forum is also a moderator on that forum, and many of us members are frequent visitors/posters on both. I point out these differences to you because, in my opinion, you're very early in the planning stage and the kinds of questions you have posted (or implied) so far are more aligned with the Studio Design forum. That's not to say that you're not welcome here; quite the opposite is true. However, I think you'll likely find more success at this stage in your project to absorb as much information as you can in both forums, but particularly the design forum, so that you can craft a solution that will meet your needs. This community is here to help you help yourself, but you really have to take the lead and the initiative to educate yourself as much as possible. When you're stuck, this is a great place to get unstuck... But you have to be willing to get your brain dirty (kind of the opposite of getting your hands dirty :? ) first. I wish you the best of luck on your project... I'll be interested in seeing how it takes shape. --Keith
