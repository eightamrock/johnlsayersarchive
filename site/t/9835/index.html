I think I was the "bay area guy" you quoted, and I did get my chiller room chillin finally, mostly by brute force. My workroom is a simple freestanding (insulated) plywood building 10' by 12', and we added a lean-to structure to one side 4 x 12. A normal, cheap window AC unit is installed there, venting to the outside. On the floor in front of it is a 400 CFM squirrel cage fan, which pulls air from the AC and pushes it into the workroom. There is a return vent that allows air to be pushed out of the workroom back into the chill room. I also have a smaller squirrel cage fan that can suck air out of the work room to the outside via the same vent. Usage of this rig is as follows: hot days in the summer: AC on w/ 400 CFM fan pushing cool air into the workroom and the passive return bringing air back; cool fall days: AC off (vent open) w/ 400 cfm fan pushing outside cool air into the workroom w/the passive vent open. Cold days (now): both AC and 400 CFM fan off, passive return routed to the smaller fan pulling warm air out of the room (and cool air in thru the other fan and its duct from the chill room). I do have a few things stored in the chill room, but no active computers. This is working quite well for me--thanks to Rod et al. A key to this is having the room pretty well sealed up when the AC/big fan are operating. Philip Perkins

If what you want to do is record in the room you are making I don't recommend my approach. I do sound editing for film and video, and can live with a certain amount of baseline noise, and I record in another room I have which is differently ventilated and much quieter. As has been pointed out many places--having a big fan pushing air into a space requires a lot of tricks and good design to make it quiet. What I have is certainly quieter than having that equipment in the same room, but not quiet by real studio standards. Now, I think that you COULD make it quiet enough to record in if you made sure to isolate the equipment from the structure, used silencers and/or the right sort of bends in the airways etc.. When work slows a bit I'm going to reasses my system and see what I can do to quiet it down a bit. That said, I spent a blissfully cool summer working in my shack with the current system, where in the past I would have to periodically run out of the room gasping for breath and pouring sweat. (I have computers in here too.) On my list is reducing noise in here by moving more gear OUT before I hassle w/ the AC some more. The Minisplit idea is very attractive, but Rod has made it very clear that it isn't really a solution by itself since it exchanges no air w/ the outside. I also happen to live in an area where they aren't popular, and couldn't find any place where I could go and actually hear how noisy the fan units were. I like the idea of the fans, whatever type they are, not being in the room. good luck Philip Perkins
