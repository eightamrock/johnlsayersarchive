knightfly13 January 2006#2
Spock, try placing your speaker in a corner (as close to the tri-corner as possible) and your mic in the opposite corner (example - speaker in north-west corner at floor level, mic at south-east corner at ceiling level) - test there and with the mic at other ceiling corners as a comparison - a speaker placed in a tri-corner should excite ALL modes in the room, and a mic in the corner will pick all modes up equally, since ALL modes terminate at corners. So this method should show you all your room's response with as little effect from SBIR and "MBIR" as possible (that's Speaker Boundary Interference REsponse, and same effect with Microphone :wink: Good luck... Steve

Ethan Winer5 February 2006#7
Petar, I'm sorry you haven't gotten any replies because your posts definitely deserve some attention. Here's my two cents: You need to set the Gate Time longer in ETF for those low frequency waterfall plots in order to get the highest resolution. You do not need to measure again! Just load the files you already have and set the Gate Time to the 200 millisecond maximum, then set the Time to as long as needed to see the complete decay. Also, the real value from such tests is to verify the improvement you got from treatment, and to ensure that the reverb time is relatively uniform at most frequencies. Regardless of what is measured initially, the treatment goal is generally the same: Broadband absorption that works well to as low a frequency as possible. --Ethan

knightfly7 February 2006#12
Ethan, thanks for the helping hand; been running on 4 hrs sleep for so long now it seems normal... :cry: I've not even had time to look at the zip files yet, hope to tonite at work if things are half-assed reasonable for a change. Petar, broadband is NOT a helmholtz or tuned panel, it's just more absorption like you've been doing. The next thing I'd do is heavy diagonal fill in room corners; I've seen quite a bit worse dips in rooms, so you're already gaining on it. It takes a LOT of volume to see marked changes in a room, I'd try to make the faces of your corner absorption at least a meter wide, more if you can, and at least 100mm thick (200 is better). Saw one comment about the "dead room with first layer particle stoper" - that's all you want, otherwise too much HF reflection most likely... Steve
