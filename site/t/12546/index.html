Whoa!!! Floating floor???? Are you SURE you want do to that? Do you understand what is involved in doing that? Have you done the math? Did you read the sticky about floating floors? Hint: It is really, really hard to float a floor correctly, and really, really easy to TRY to float it but get it WRONG, which means that you made things worse than if you would have not even tried... FLOATING FLOORS: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 Apart from that, your inner leaf construction plan sounds fine: a separate second frame with a large air gap (larger is better: if you can afford more than 2 inches, then do it), fill the entire air gap with wool (either mineral wool or fiberglass), and a few layers of drywall with GG.

-Should I open up the interior faces and go in and attempt to cut the inner leaves out. Replacing the insulation and then facing it with QRock-GreenGlue-QRock?
That sounds like the best plan to me. I'm not sure that I understand exactly what you mean, and a SkethUp model or photos of the actual structure would be good. But the idea is to get the "extra" leaves off, and leave only two on each wall / ceiling. Don't forget that you can actually take off just the leaves facing into the rooms, and leave the leaves that face each other (inside the wall) in place! That would give you what is called "inside-out" construction, where you end up with studs facing the room and drywall on the other side of those studs. It's actually a good thing, since you can use the space between the studs for your acoustic treatment... So all is not lost: Your solution might be as simple as just taking off the drywall from the studs in the room. You could even use that drywall to beef up the leaf on the other side of the studs, if you want to. But post a diagram, photos, or best of all a SketchUp model of your walls, so that we can understand them better, then offer suggestions. - Stuart -
