dymaxian7 March 2004#2
Having a swinging shutter for the window isn't a bad idea. The only drawback is that you'll have to keep it's swing path clear of other room treatment- this may or may not be a big deal. To be honest, if you're thinking of doing a hinged shutter of any sort, you might be better off making 2 shutters, and having them swing open sideways- you won't have to reach for the ceiling when you want to open or close it, and they really won't take up that much space to the sides of the window. If you know what frequencies you'll be having problems with in the room, you can build corner slot resonators to help with those particular freq's, but you may be better off just framing 703 across the corners and going for broadband absorption. The panel trap idea is always good- try to build them all slightly different (varying the membrane thickness, building some deeper than others) and they'll cover a wider spectrum. If your back wall needs to absorb over the entire spectrum, you can probably space 703 away from the wall (as John recommends for a rear wall absorber) and place all the panel traps along the front wall. What are you going to be doing about that exterior window on the rear wall? You could build another swinging shutter for that, I suppose... Good luck! Kase www.minemusic.net
