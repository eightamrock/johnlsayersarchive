It seems like there's a communications problem here, so let's start all over again: The confusion seems to be where you say first that you are going to use the area above the garage (second floor) for your studio, but then you give the dimensions saying that they are for the garage , not the second floor: "The Garage is 26'x18'x9'11" at the peak internal dimensions." Anyway, now that it is clear that you want to build on the second floor, the following points are clear: - You have a plywood floor - The dimensions of the actual inner area where you could build a studio are: 26' x 11'8" x 7' - There is a large window measuring 42"x6' at one end - There are two skylights in the center of the roof, which are not marked on your SketchUp
I'm not at all opposed to changing the framing of the internal walls to add beams and eliminate 2x4's if needed, and was thinking of a design splitting the area in half at an angle down the near center with a glass slider on the wall.
That may nor may not be possible, and nobody here can tell you for sure. That is something you will need to check with a structural engineer. He will need to examine the structure, and see how much extra weight it can handle safely, beyond what it is already supporting. This is your limiting factor, not the size or shape of the room.
i'm not too concerned about how loud it will be outside, but a little dampening would be nice.
Unfortunately, sound going out isn't the entire extent of your problem. Sound coming IN is also a major factor: wind, rain, thunder, cars/trucks/tractors close by, aircraft flying overhead, people, radio, TV, phones, etc. If you plan to actually record in there, then you need to isolate those. In order to figure out HOW to do that, you need to get a sound level meter and measure how loud those things are inside the current room and out in the open. Then you need to figure out how quite you need those to be, in order to record successfully. Subtract "B" from "A" and you get your "number": how much isolation you need, in terms of transmission loss. Based on that number you can look at the various types of construction that will get you that amount of transmission loss on your budget. (And as Brien said, getting good isolation from a second floor room on a wooden floor, is not easy due to the flanking paths.) But anyway, based on that construction, your structural engineer can tell you what you need to do (if anything) to beef up the existing structure to the point where it can support the extra weight (you will be putting tons of materials up there, and I mean literal tons). Then when all of that is in place, you need to get the building permits in order to do that. That's the procedure you should be following. The sizes, shapes and treatment for the rooms is something that comes way down the road, after you already have the basic plan figured out, and the building permits in place. You seem to be running ahead of yourself by trying to figure out where to divide the space and put the bass traps before you even know if you can build anything up there at all! So it seems that your number one priority should be to get a structural engineer to examine your building, and tell you what can and cannot be done there. In any event, the ceiling you have in there is very low, at only 7 feet, and that is without even considering the space that will, necessarily, be taken up with the isolation, and the space that will, necessarily, be taken up with the treatment. In all honesty, your best bet is to think about how you could use part of the garage itself for your studio, rather than the attic space above. The garage has a concrete floor, which is excellent, acoustically, and has a 10' ceiling, which is also excellent. Even if the room has to be smaller, to still leave some space for parking cars in the garage, that would still be better than trying to do what you are planning right now. - Stuart -
