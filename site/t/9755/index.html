mingo115 November 2007#1
Hey everyone, I'm a long time visitor and just finally jumped in and signed up to share a couple projects I've got planned. I'm more a musician and producer than I am studio owner, but I figured I'd take this winter as a chance to improve my setup (and hopefully save myself some money in the long run). This site has been an incredible resource, and hopefully I can give a little back while I do my best to soak up the expertise that so many here graciously give away! My control room is in the master bedroom of a rental house. I'll get some photos and diminensions ASAP. We're setup around a MCI 636 console. We're lucky enough to have a MCI JH-24 2" (that doesn't get enough love lately) and she lives in another room. The room actually sounds pretty decent and fares pretty well with a pink noise shoot. As always, room for improvement. Pretty much, I'm trying to build John's wall units as close to his designs as my feable carpentry skills will get me! Today I started on the slot absorbers. I'm building two 7' X 4' units. My plan is for them to be free standing with some anchor hooks/eye hooks and twine attaching them to the wall (just to keep them from tipping). I'm trying to keep all the treatments fairly movable as I'm am looking at moving in the next 12-18 months. Here's a couple pics after today's first trip to Lowes. I had them cut the two 4' X 8' x 3/4" (no 1" to be found) MDF boards into 8' X 8" strips. Perfectly straight and clean cuts (and no clean up!). Enough to frame out the two units plus a couple left over for the next. Tried to make notes in the descriptions but they didn't get posted. Sorry 'bout that! I made the remaining cuts to the MDF, mocked up the frame and applied a bead of construction adhesive anywhere two boards met, then 2" brads (and a pneumatc nailer) sealed the deal. I only have a circular saw, so I had to experiment with the angle of the cuts on the slats (as well as the length with those angles!). Came out pretty flush with the sides of the frame. I'll make it airtight with caulk and maybe some rubber tubing. The front of the slat braces are 3/4" from the frames front edge, sloping to 2" inside the frame (hopefully as per John's specs). After I had mocked up some slats, we flipped the frame, glued and nailed the 1/4" plywood back. Tomorrow we'll finish this one out. Not sure whether to use 703 or fluffy pink stuff. Hoping to save the 703 for the rear wall absorbers. Any suggestions? Couldn't seem to find if it really matter for these boxes. More pics soon!

Got a bit more done today in between trying to make the G5 be friends with Logic Studio! Hoping to finish these two tommorow and move on the the back wall bass panel. I used the pink stuff on these, figured I'd save the 703 for doubling up and taming the lower octaves. The insulation just barely touches the slats at their most narrow depth. I know there's some debate as to whether it should touch at all, but I'm not aiming for a specific feq, so I figured I'd leave a little damping, and I think it'll work well. I used twist wire and staples to keep the insulation in the back of the box. Next post should see these two boxes in the control room!

mingo117 November 2007#7
Gareth, I caulked (Liquid Nails, I think) the out side edges of the slats (the short edge that meets the frame wall) and used a couple 2" brads. I was worried about this, and how to make it airtight. Because the fabric (black quilters cotton) is between the slats and the bracing, I couldn't just draw a bead of caulk on the braces and nail them. I'll include a pic. Not to scale or anything. If you had the right tool (table saw?) to bevel the supports, it'd be a LOT easier. Cutting those slats one by one with beveled edges (with a circular saw) is a pain! Plus, I'm sure that the way I'm having to attach them has an affect on the freq., Q, and efficiency. But as I'm looking for broadband coverage, I didn't worry too much. The boards still resonate when tapped over a big range of frequencies (not so much as to be a problem - though I'm glad I left a little insulation touching to dampen a bit). It's pretty amazing to hear the difference in freq the same size slats cover with the other variations in place. I mean, it actually works! Gotta love physics! I'll keep looking at this http://www.avatarstudios.net/ for inspiration... BTW, I don't think I'd be doing this without that Pneumatic nailer! Rog http://www.mingofishtrap.com

mingo119 November 2007#10
BradJacob, John's design for corner traps is an airtight slot resonator with a couple layers of insulation, so I'd think if you made some super chunks inside an airtight "box" with slats on the front (leaving some air between the slats and the chunks) it would work. Though it doesn't seem like it'd be that much more effective in the lower octaves. And with so many variables, it doesn't seem like the corners would be the place to address particular freq. issues. Guess you could always just drop the super chunks into place and see if it takes care of your problems before you finish them out. rog
mingo110 December 2007#11
I figured I'd post a shot of these portable 4' X 2' slot resonators. The boxes are only 4" deep, so not meant for low absorption, but they've come in really handy for changing the sound of our little drum room. They're on a swivel (threaded rod running through the box with washers and wing-nuts on end), so they can break up flutter pretty well too. They stand about 7' high. I think I'm going to build another four that are 8" deep on stands made of angle iron (or something metal) with the small holes in it so the height could be adjusted. Also considered putting two 3'6" boxes stacked on one stand so they could be swiveled independently of one another. rog

Thanks for the encouraging words guys! Basically, my drum room is fairly small, about 18' X 12' X 8' 6" high. It's a little room within a room inside a garage. I went way overboard with high end absorption when we first put it in. Aside from an extremely dry recording (which I could deal with), the main problem to me is that it hasn't been very fun to make music in.
