Soundman20201 June 2010#2
For bass absorption, I was contemplating sand bags in the corners or bean bag chairs behind chicken wire . . .
Neither bean bags nor sand bags are going to do much to help you with bass trapping. Bean bags are commonly filled with polystyrene beads, as far as I know, and that is basically useless acoustically, since it is closed-cell. Sand has plenty of mass but practically no absorption, so it would help with isolation... (if you could figure a way to surround your entire room with it!) but is pretty useless as bass trapping. Bass trapping is usually accomplished with thick mineral wool or fiberglass absorption, placed in the corners of the room. All 12 of them, if possible. But your booth is way too small for that. Take a look at some of the vocal booth designs on this forum for ideas on how to build and treat a vocal booth.
I know that low frequency is my battle. I plan to put as many angles in the room as I can.
Angles won't help much with low frequencies. Angled walls help stop flutter echo, but don't do much to treat lows, and do even less to isolate. It seems like you might be mixing up "isolation" and "treatment" all at once: they are tow different things, really. And the are accomplished with two different types of materials. Mass for isolation, and absorption for treatment. Basically. But it's much more complicated than just that. - Stuart -
