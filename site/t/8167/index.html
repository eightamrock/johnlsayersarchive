1. You're in the ballpark. The desk should get pulled out a bit, see #2. 2. You're going to want to pull the desk out from the wall a bit . One rule of thumb: calculate 38% of the length from the front wall to the rear wall along the center axis. Put an "X" in that spot. That's your listening spot, your chair should be directly over that spot. Then, pull your desk out from the wall until the 2 speakers make an equilateral triangle with your head. 3. What kind of foam blocks? Are they Auralex sheets? What are their absorption properties, do you know? Let's assume these sheets are effective absorbers. You'll want to use them to make a reflection free zone. Put them on the ceiling above you, and on the side walls, such that the first reflections from your speakers will be absorbed. This will improve your stereo imaging. Once you have a solid RFZ, I'd probably put the rest of the panels equally distributed on the back wall to absorb some rear reflections. 4. In small rooms like this, I'm a big fan of superchunks. This is where much of your budget should go, imho. I'd put superchunks on the wall/ceiling corner above the 3 windows, and on the wall/floor corner below the windows. I'd also put them on pretty much all the wall/ceiling corners in the room. You're kinda limited with your wall/wall corners. 5. I'd guess that the absorptive ones would sound better. But that's just a guess. 6. The biggest factor in the way you use this room is to make it an accurate listening environment. Design the room so that it sounds good, then make room for your electronic kit. That closet just feels.... in the way to me. If it were my room, I'd either build it out with isolation in mind (make a soundproof machine room/iso booth) or just take the closet out entirely. Good luck!
