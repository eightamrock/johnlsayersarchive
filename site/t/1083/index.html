knightfly9 December 2003#6
Hey Chung, first of all welcome to the board - you've obviously done quite a bit of homework (beyond your obvious architectural training, unless you trained at "studios R us" - One thing that will help keep the "air" in your sound - those side splayed walls, if they keep reflected sound from either speaker from being reflected to the mix position, can be live and don't have to be balanced at lower frequencies - in other words, you could leave the one to your left the way it is for bass trapping and (if you want to keep the window) replace the one to your right with glass - the large air space depth and the splaying should put any early reflections above maybe 300 hZ to the rear wall, which should be heavy absorption (like 3-4" 703 spaced a few inches off the wall) - you'd want to do a crude ray trace from each speaker to the splay and back to ensure this is true, but it looks like it would work. The 10 x 10 isn't ideal - and if your ceiling is ALSO 10', I'd recommend either making two assymetrical iso-boths out of that space and looking elsewhere. Otherwise, as long as you're aware of the problems they can most likely be dealt with. Your splays on the sides will go a long way toward solving most of the problems, with the possible exception of the lowest modes - that would depend on the stiffness of the splayed sections as to whether the splays become the new boundaries or not at low frequencies. to fix the parallel floor-ceiling thing, unless you're able to angle a false ceiling you'd need a reflective floor (brightness) and an absorbed ceiling (flutter control mainly) Is that room on its own separate level (I'm NOT an architect, I just "play one on TV") if so, the video thing probably makes the most sense. Great start, and again welcome... Steve

knightfly10 December 2003#11
"for proper stereo imaging, I needed to keep my treatments symetrical" - That IS the absolute SAFEST way - however, lower bass frequencies are so long wavelength that they start causing a small room to act as a Pressure Room instead of sticking to wave theory - for example, it takes almost 10 FEET of path length DIFFERENCE for a 30 hZ wave to completely cancel itself out (1/4 wavelength), and that's only if there are no other boundaries to confuse the issue - Plus, with the side walls splayed like that any early reflections will end up at the rear of the room, where they can be absorbed, trapped, maimed or mutilated... In addition to that, flush mounting your speakers will lessen the low frequency chaos in the room too - the combination of all that should allow you to enjoy that window if you want to - just make that portion of the splayed wall glass, and only use one other layer of glass at the outer perimiter of the outer wall, that will give you maximum sound isolation AND a view. Here's an approximate performance graph for a 40 x 80 window with closest spacing of 12", using 1/4" and 3/8" glass - Of course, if you can "steal" a bigger control room with better ratios, far be it from me to suggest otherwise :wink: ... Steve BTW, you can always use the REST of that wall for bass traps...
