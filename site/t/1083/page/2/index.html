knightfly19 January 2004#28
Hey Chung - "by hand" is kinda what I do also - I use an old DOS based pgm called Generic Cadd - I've not found an affordable proggie that will Ray trace, plus I hate learning a new program for one little feature, got more things to do with the time. What I do in my Cad program is to draw a line from speaker to one end of a wall section (like the rear door planes in your case) - then, I set the ortho angle to be equal to the plane in question (in your case, that same door plane) - then I snap to the intersection of the new line (speaker to plane) and draw a short ray perpendicular to the plane being checked (the door) - with the ortho angle still set same as the plane, I draw a line from the end of the short, perpendicular line last drawn, over to the original "ray", snapping to that ray. Now I copy that short perpendicular, using the end of it that snaps to the original ray as a reference, and "slide" the new copy to the other end of the newest short line I'm copying - this gives me a line that is twice as long as the first one, which makes a point that, with the intersection point at the plane (your door), you can now draw the identical angle coming AWAY from your door as the angle going TO your door. I then repeat this process for the OTHER end of the plane in question, so now I have a range of where all reflections from that plane will reflect. This is more difficult to explain than it is to do, it's just basic geometry in actual practice. I wouldn't want to "ray trace" an entire setup with this method though - I did a nearly square bedroom that way, trying to find out what needed absorbing if the mix position was in a corner - took a while... If you have as hard a time understanding my description as I did describing it, let me know and I'll try to do a drawing example and label the steps. May take a while though, I'm so far behind in home stuff I'm starting to look like I'm ahead... Steve

knightfly21 January 2004#30
EPDM is reported to be longer lived than neoprene by about double - approximately 10 years for neoprene vs. maybe 25 or more for EPDM - Auralex U-boats are (according to Jeff Szymanski, their head engineer) made of EPDM just because of that lifespan difference. However, if Im not mistaken they are 60 Duro instead of 40, so loading would need recalculation. Most neoprene I've seen available is 60 duro. I didn't notice what you're using for joists and whether vertical or laid flat - you'd need to be careful of spans and point loading depending on those parameters as well as subfloor construction/support capability - Have you checked with kineticsnoise.com to see if they can supply what you need? They have reps in Portland, hard to believe they wouldn't also have them in the Bay area... Steve
