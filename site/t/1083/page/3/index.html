Here's how I think I might approach treating the room. Please let me know if I'm understanding things right. I should try to achieve at balanced RT60 over all frequencies in order to have a balanced room. What I do not know is what range that number should sit in for a live room/alternate listening room. Assuming I'm at least going to have to knock everything down to the lowest response time which was .52 sec, I'd need to have some high frequency absorbtion on a substancial surface area. I would also need to provide some trapping from 250-500Hz which could be achieved with slat resonators covering much of the side walls. And get some broadband bass trapping and hangers going for underneath ~250 Hz. . . When i look at the room modes is this gives me the points in my room where there will be spikes and dips in my frequency response due to sound behaving like pressure zones or something like that. Anyways, if I want to minimize this effect, I should attenuate the peak frequencies, and pay particular attention to where the modes overlap. So, for my room in the model, 51 Hz and 103 Hz will be of particular concern, so I should tune my slot resonators and hangers to hit those mode frequencies. . . or even build some panel absorbers. The way I understand it, all rooms including mine benefit from broadband absorbers along the corners, and I'll try to do that. Also, I assume from what I see in the forum that it's safer to build rather broadband absorbers as opposed to multiple absorbers targetting specific frequencies. . . you want to kinda center an absorber around a frequency, but spread it's range out a bit to get a more even effect. Lemme know if I'm on the right track. If so, I'll proceed w/ designs and plans and letyouall know when I have a model or drawing. . . otherwise, please let me know if I'm not understanding something correctly. . . well, before I build something that won't work :) Before I build anything, I'll go ahead and take some measurements of the pre-treated room and post them to verify and adjust details like slot spacing, hanger size, and such, but I wanted to have a game plan in mind. chunger22 April 2006#72
Hello, I just finished the concrete floor in one of my rooms. I thought I'd share some info because I think it turned out great and the products I used worked better than expected. The color is acid stain from www.scofield.com . . . I used a combination of Padre brown, faded Terra Cotta, and Copper Patina. Application was simple. . . the contractor I originially hired from craigslist applied with a brush, but I DO NOT suggest doing this. After she botched the 1st stain application, I didn't want her back. There were annoying visible brush marks on the floor. I had to go back over it 2 times with a spray bottle, and that did the trick. Oh, mask your walls so you don't get acid all over your walls like I did :) Apply the acid stain, and let it sit for 4 hours. Then, mop with water to neutralize the reaction. Then mop and mop and mop until water is clean. I diluted some baking soda in the water to help neutralize the acid. The acid stain can be re-applied as many times as you like prior to sealing until you get the color you want, so it's forgiving. It was difficult to find a strong sealer that was maintinence free, so after much searching, I found a 2 component Aliphatic Polyurea sealer. It's expensive. 2 gallons costed me $200. Coverage is 225-250 square feet per gallon 1st coat and 325-350 square feet 2nd coat. This stuff is strong enough to use in industrial applications with forklift traffic and is very resistant to chemicals/oils, etc. The amazing thing was it is dry enough to move furniture onto in 2 hours. . .and smells horrible for those 2 hours. The stuff kicks pretty fast (useless in 30 min), so it was best for me to mix 1 or 2 quart batches throwing away the mixing containers after use. I applied it by pouring directly on the ground and spreading with a paint roller on a stick. It can go 10 mils thick at a time. The 1st coat went on real easy, but absorbsion was a bit weird in 3 or 4 spots. 2nd coat no problems. I should have chemically de-greased my concrete prior to application. I sanded the surface to remove paint and thought that would be good enough. You can get the product from a nice lady named Lindy in Texas. She's been in the industry for years and can answer any questions about prep, applications, product, alternate products, etc. Lindy A Lindy (Lynn) Ausburne GoldenLook International, Inc.
