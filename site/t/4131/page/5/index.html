sharward10 November 2005#106
Perhaps I can clarify that for you... And I hope I'm correct about this. Codes are "fluid" -- they change over time, typically to be more restrictive. Whether or not your building was built to code back in 1969 isn't as relevant as what code says today about what you are going to build today. You will not be expected to bring the entire building up to today's codes. You will, however, be expected to bring all of your new construction up to today's codes -- and there might be a few things in the existing construction that will need to be addressed. A good example of this is residential construction in my area -- any construction permit (even a reroofing permit) requires that smoke alarms be installed in all sleeping areas, even though they may not have been required when the house was built. It seems you're in a classic "egg vs. chicken" scenario. :( Perhaps your engineer will will sign and seal for your plans that you will then submit to the city to get a permit...? :roll:

knightfly22 November 2005#112
When used that way, you don't have to worry about durometer, etc - it's merely another layer of mass, with (as Bryan said) different properties. Not that it's available (or reasonably priced) but an even better choice for this might be some sort of BUTYL sheeting; has a LOT more damping capability than neoprene or rubber. If this is a "sorta" floated floor, (any air space between it an main floor), be sure to put rockwool and/or fiberglass in the cavities, enough for medium compression when the first upper layer is laid; otherwise, major boomy...
