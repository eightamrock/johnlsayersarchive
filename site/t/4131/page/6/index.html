sharward28 November 2005#126
Yes, indeed, there are code constraints on this... This is one of the areas that I had to educate myself quite thoroughly! Each side of a door must have its own level clearance with no step up or down -- typically 36" in residential and I think 43" or something in commercial. The only exception is at the top of stairs -- a door can swing in, away from the stairs. This was a surprise to me -- I had always figured there'd be an exception at the bottom of stairs as long as the door swung away from the stairs. (It would have to, otherwise the door would bang into the stairs! :P) But, as I interpreted what I learned, there was no such exception. But please don't take my word for it -- you need to check with your building department so they can give you an answer based on the codes in effect in your municipality. Not only are there different model codes, but every few years a new version of those codes comes out, and not all municipalities adopt the new version right away. In other words, here in California, there can be two cities, each adopting California Building Code (CBC), but the codes could differ because they may be on different years of publication. Plus, government officials can amend codes to maike things more strict and/or spell out alternatives... So, even if I'm totally correct in my case, it may not be correct in yours.

Any way, and I quote: "K.ENG's analysis has determined that the floor system is capable of supporting the minimum required floor live load of 50lbs per square foot throughout but has limited additional capacity. The minimum floor loading requirement is based on the mezzanine floor being used as an office and not as storage and is based on the IBC 2003 building code. However, one area, west of the stairs, is capable of supporting an additional superimposed dead load. If you have enough floor space to leave wider air gaps in walls, and enough headroom to use a bit taller floor spacers, you can still do a good job of isolating. sharward21 December 2005#141
I know you're still understandably frustrated about their not having the design documents for your building after all those years. In truth, they probably have minimal documentation for it someplace, perhaps on microfiche... but surely not all the blueprints and stuff. Many municipalities' building departments have Web sites with content that describes when permits are required and when they are not. If they don't, they have phones, and one of their responsibilities is to answer an endless plethora of "Does this require a permit?" calls. I actually think there's more consistency across the country in terms of what does and does not require a permit. There may be variations in how seriously those requirements are followed or enforced. Even here in California, I am one of the few homeowners who takes the requirement seriously. Adding foam insulation to some hollow blocks may not require a permit. It's best to ask so that you're sure -- especially since you know you'll have inspectors coming onto the property in the not-too-distant future and you wouldn't want them to see evidence of work done without a permit that required one. That's just asking for trouble! :-) --Keith :mrgreen:
