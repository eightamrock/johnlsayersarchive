cyeazel wrote:
I know there are no official test results on them but I feel like they must work, at least somewhat.
There are a lot of bogus nutritional supplements and health aides on the market that rely on just that kind of thinking... Or, rather, "feeling." If someone spends money on a product with a promise of a certain effect, and then they use that product, they're going to expect a certain effect, and may even think they are experiencing that effect, whether or not they actually are. Power of suggestion. Placebo effect. Wishful thinking. Unwillingness to admit that maybe we were suckered. I don't know of anyone who, in the course of building their own project, made two builds equal to each other, with the exception being the U-boats or any other untested, undocumented elastomer, with details of the difference between the two. Unfortunately, floated floor/room elastomers can't be added or removed after the fact for an effective "before and after" comparison. That's why testing is important, and in my opinion, unwaveringly crtitical and necessary, when it comes to floated floor/room systems. To everyone who has said, "I used U-boats and they worked great!" I ask them: "How do you know if the results would have been the same, or even worse, if you had not used them?" In my opinion, we need to get beyond "feeling" that a product will work as we "hope" it will, and that if a manufacturer is unwilling or unable to substantiate the claims with hard evidence from an independent third party (something Audio Alloy / Green Glue has done extremely well -- kudos to them!! 8)), then -- again, in my opinion -- it shouldn't be worth our consideration. In closing, I'll just say that U-boats may be a fine product, and may very well stand to the scrutiny of independent lab testing. I actually hope that they do someday. However, until they do, and until its manufacturer publishes credible specifications regarding weight limits, optimal loads, related longevity, flame tests, etc., I will continue to discourage their use. Finally, remember that building codes probably prohibit their use, and everyone knows my sentiments on the importance of following codes and doing construction above board with the municipal building authorities. --Keith :mrgreen:

Dear Eric, Thanks for your email, and good questions. It sounds like you are definitely going about your home studio construction the right way, excellent construction methods and application of materials. For additional info on this, check out www.acoustics101.com , a website completely dedicated to studio construction. The spacing of U-Boats 16" OC is recommend for almost every installation, including across your 2x3 studs. The only reason for the 0.01% disparity is more of a structural issue than an acoustical one. Research shows that you can actually improve the decoupling of your floor with >16" OC spacing (fewer contact points, greater distance between couplers), however not having enough support can lead to floor joist sagging over time creating uneven floors that would reduce the decoupling effect. 16" OC is a happy medium that should offer great decoupling while keeping the structural load-bearing requirements intact. Unfortunately the durometer of U-Boats is a trade secret that we can not disclose - it would then make them easy to reproduce by an imitator or competitor - however please rest assured that we have designed these with the optimal balance between acoustical performance and structural integrity based on what we consider to be an average studio floor weight. Let me know if there are any other ways we can help. Thanks again, have a great day!
