gullfo13 November 2007#259
technically, the slot resonator has to act as a third leaf because it has some amount of trapped air volume (even with the openings) which transfers energy to the isolation wall at frequencies not neutralized by the Helmholtz effect or dampened by the rigid insulation inside) but i believe its a marginal amount of third leaf effect. the dividing panel separates the air cavities and improves the efficiency of the absorber. if you're worried, you could choose another set of treatments... either way, having your floor coupled to the exterior walls will eventually be the bigger issue for isolation than the slot resonators...

Soundman202024 November 2008#267
Personally, I think I'd render the entire wall. You might call it different things ("plaster", "stucco", etc.), but basically it means covering the entire surface with a thick layer of mortar, plaster or some other compound, using an exterior grade product that has water-proofing included in the mixture, or that you put in as an additive. Then when its dry, seal it and paint it. It might sound like overkill, but that way you are absolutely guaranteed that you have it sealed tight. I have a very similar situation with my build, except that is is a brick wall, and I'm planning to do exactly that. It will cost a bit more and take longer, but I think it's worth it to get it right. It also adds mass to the wall, which can only help.
