sharward27 September 2005#37
I hate to be the bearer of bad news, but that door installation won't meet code. I see two problems right off the bat -- the bottom of the door will be too high, and there's not enough clearance (zero, or actually less than zero) between the bottom of the stairs and the door (i.e., a "landing" at the bottom of the stairwell). There may be other issues too, but these are the two that jump out at me after wrangling with this issue myself for quite some time. Those who were tuned closely to my project thread know that these landing requirements are one of the main reasons I decided against floating a floor in my space. Visit your local building officials to determine what model codes they use. If UBC, you'll find the relevant code at:
  • 1003.3.1.6 Floor level at doors
  • 1003.3.3.5 Stairway landings
I urge you not build anything more without a permit -- this is especially true in your case, since it's a commercial building. Most likely you will not be granted a permit if there are obvious code violations in your plans, so you'll keep yourself from making any mistakes that will be difficult/expensive to undo later. I realize there's a strong urge to do something on our projects, and that "doing" usually means "building." But as has been said time after time on this forum, our projects are 90% planning and 10% building. Believe me -- I understand the magnetic pull towards wanting to do -- but the more I learned in my planning stages, the more I appreciated the fact that I didn't "put the cart before the horse." Transfer all that do something energy to learning the building codes, learning acoustics, drawing plans, posting them here, getting criticized, drawing better plans, getting criticized again, drawing yet better plans, ad nauseum, until you have something that you're sure will hit the ball out of the park at the building department. Then be prepared to get criticized some more ;-) -- but you'll be in good practice by then! :lol: Ultimately you'll have a plan that everyone loves -- us, the building department, and most importantly, you.

Figure on at least a 44" unobstructed area at the bottom of those stairs -- no walls, no doors, and no door swing paths (i.e., the swing path in your drawing wouldn't meet code either).
Where did you find those code numbers?
I found them by some creative Googling. I translated my concern into keywords that I knew would fetch some relevant results. But I should point out that I wouldn't have even known about the issue had I not sat down at my city's building permit office back across from their senior inspector back in April with a stupified look upon my face when he asked, "Where's your landing?" See my post about that experience. (Note that you will probably have a 44" clearance requirement, not the 36" required in residential construction... But don't take my word for that.) Unfortunately, I have not found it possible to access code books via the Internet. They're copyrighted publications, so you'd need to either purchase a set for yourself (very expensive), study them at a local library, or just try to "wing it" with your plans and be prepared to need to make corrections in the permit application/approval process. Note that California Building Code (CBC) and Universal Building Code (UBC) use the same section numbering scheme -- as best I can determine, CBC is a derivative of UBC -- just more explicit and more restrictive in some cases, mostly due to our seismic challenges. One more thing... Just because you've been inside a building done a certain way doesn't mean you'll be able to legally duplicate it. The building may have been originally built under older, less restrictive codes, or it may have been modified after the fact without permits and such modifications would have been out of compliance with codes in effect at the time the modifications were made. :roll: I think it's time you get very closely acquainted with your drawing program so that you can post some plan images! Hang in there, my friend! We'll help get you through this. --Keith :mrgreen:

You know, there is another option that I intended to make clear but didn't. That is, sketch something fairly basic and pay your city's building department a visit. Tell them what you're planning to do, make it clear that you intend to pull permits and follow code, and ask for some advice. They get paid to help you get things right -- at least how it's supposed to be. They should be able to spot obvious things (like that door issue), call you on it, and show you the relevant codes that are in your way. They can then advise you on ways to get around the obstacles.
