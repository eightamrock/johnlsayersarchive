Hi, I'll just post some pics and comments of the construction of the studio I'm currently building, some earlier posts to this is http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5407 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5463 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5565 first image was shot when I was a bit underway, so some explanations might be necessary; the floor was first covered with Platon, that will keep moisture away from the construction, the Platon goes about 10cm up on the walls. On top of that is 12mm thick particle board. Then comes the Vibratec Pucks which is the same as the ones which comes with the CDM system, they're glued onto 45mm thick studs and then it's filled with low desnity insulation. on this second shot you see the whole floor from inside the "office". You also see the first layer of 22mm particle board. Then we started with the Noisekiller Yellow..and it sure was yellow..glueing a second layer of 22mm particle board making a big fat sandwhich of it.

AndreasB3 April 2006#6
Check my other threads in the beginning of this one or just go to http://www.vibratec.se/ and download a catalog. I got the impression that the system called CDM is sold in many countries so you could probably get hold of them locally..(don't know where you from since you haven't posted your location). I got the 30mm LAT30, which is the softest one.. you should calculate the linear weight of your structure to get the number of pucks correct and of course you have to know what CC you should put them in. mine are 200mm cc under the walls and 400cc under the rest of the floor, so about 160 pucks for that room. best andreas

Such stiff materials require very little excitation to produce resonant vibration and it is quite easy to produce structure borne vibration which is reradiated as noise in other parts of a building construction. Just how big a problem the noise becomes is dependent on the overall Loss Factor of the construction. Typical construction materials such as plywood, chipboard and plasterboard have very small loss factors in the region of 0.01 to 0.03 - that is to say, very little internal damping. By means of Swedac DG A2 Damping glue, the loss factor can be raised to 0.2 to 0.4 - the effect of which is to provide a noise reduction of 8 to 16 dB. Use: Control of structure borne noise and vibration in sheet material and wood products." Would that be eqvivalent to greenglue? It's more than half the price of Noisekiller!! a
