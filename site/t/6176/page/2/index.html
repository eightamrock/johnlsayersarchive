Just 10' back home from visiting this studio. (> 4400 miles) I spend the whole Sunday with George Massenburg in this studio, with the owner of another huge studio complex, listening, experimenting. Recordings made by George himself, and others by this other studio, mixed and very well known by the this other studio owner (audio engineer and pro musician) as well in his control as mastering room. Quiet amazing ...... Even more amazing is George Massenburg himself. A gentle, warm extremely intelligent individual I can only respect a lot, a very great lot. A real and genuine person ...... I never could expect that such a large collection of mics and gear could exist. One can really start a museum with everything available in the Blackbird Studios. It seems endless. It goes from top gear/mics many decades back (real rare collectors items) to the most recent stuff. I also underestimated George's part in the design of this studio. He specially studied numbers theory and primes in function of diffision and experimented with it on smaller scales. He calculated this whole stuff (saw the Excel files), and checked/discussed it with Dr. Peter D'Antonio. Special software (making AutoCAD a cheap program) was needed and purchased to convert this Excel data to Cad-Cam software for a numerical milling machine to convert this Excel data into production software. Not 1 of the 1532 milled 1" MDF boards is equal to one another. (and that's only for those wall diffusers/absorbers). Hence that means 1532 boards had to be programmed for production (done by this special software). Starting from 90 tons of MDF, it ended with 40 tons finished (didn't check those numbers). As one could expected detailed data will not be made public (at least for now and is certainly not up to me). Hence I can't share my real experiences. Still trouble with Jet lag (7 hours time diff.) and very tired. Must sleep now. In function of the title of this topic. :) One can hardly call this a DIY studio.
