knightfly1 July 2005#7
Wes and Rod over at RO won't steer you wrong; one thing to consider, aside from whether your ceiling can "keep up with" your walls for sound isolation, is the idea of using "soft" inner walls a couple of feet in from the concrete, to allow more of the "brutal bass" to be trapped. Even a single layer of drywall on studs with that large an air gap can help quite a bit. Room ratios, as Wes mentioned, will be really important here - even if using splayed walls, taking the average of each dimension and keeping ratios near "ideals" can smooth out the room quite a bit. Might try a google search using "louden", "bolt", and "seppmeyer" along with the words "ratio", "acoustic"... I know that's a LOT of space to give up; but a small space you can actually USE can beat a larger space that never stops irritating you (and your neighbors or family) and can't even be USED when you want to... Steve
