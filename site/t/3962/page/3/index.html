DDev29 July 2005#62
The way I look at it there are 2 primary issues with windows in a studio setting. 1. They are a hard, reflective surface so you need to be sure they are not designed in a location which will create an acoustical problem with whatever sound reflects off of them. Just my amateur look at your desing tells me that you don't have any parallel surfaces so I think you are okay from that standpoint (again, John or someone else more knowledgable really should address this). 2. Windows are prone to be a weak link in the containment of sound within any given room (leakage from either side). Sound leaking from any of your rooms into the hallway probably isn't much of an issue but sound from the hallway into your recording spaces would be an issue if you pick up someone laughing in the hallway during that perfect piano take. There are tried and true ways of installing double windows around here that help minimize this leakage. With separated wall frames, again, if installed properly I don't think it will be much of an issue, but, as usual, my caveat is that a REAL expert should give an opinion on that one. On the positive side, as Drew mentioned, the windows you've got will provide good sight lines which are both functional and aesthetically pleasing. My 2 cents for the day, Darryl.....
