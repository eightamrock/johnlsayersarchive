Ro17 March 2009#12
Wasn't aware of the topic being about creating a speaker soffit wall. But same principles apply here. A stiff material is easier to resonate. A stiff material will give back more energy on impact. But (sound)waves travel much easier through a hard material than a soft one (aka flanking) Tho, at low frq there's no stopping the waves. Stiffness is effective at low frequencies due the fact that the (sound)wave must push against the stiffness of the material. We're talking stifness controlled isolation at this point. As frq rises, and nearing it's own resonance, the stiffness becomes less effective since there's less distance needed (shorter waves) to move the material and radiate sound. At resonance the MASS law steps in, taking over the stiffness controled isolation. More "force" is needed to move that same material at a same distance. To avoid "flanking" you can use less stiff material (since stiffness is no longer a factor). With a soffit wall you want your wall resonance as low as possible (less obvious to hear the buzz..) so you want mass. doubling the mass will add about 3dB of TL but the resonance will drop too. If you want it really low, use a concrete wall (mass). Is that practicall? dunno, we have to wait and see what Stuart makes of it (still planning on doing a concrete soffit wall, Stu?) Making a heavy wall resonate, it needs force. Low frequency for example. If we don't need it for isolation we don't need the stiffness controlled isolation region and we don't want the wall to resonant easy, so why not make the wall as heavy and flexible as we can? I'm sure ROD or Eric has got a thing or two to say on this matter as well, I'm just an "overnight expert" as John pointed out so elegantly.

Soundman202017 February 2010#21
My big question: The wall around the speakers must be from drywall or from fabric? If its drywall, is there a need for hangers, wont they be useless behind the drywall? How can sound reach the hangers then?
I'm looking at your question and also at your model, but the two don't match! Your arrow on the diagram, labeled "Fabric or Drywall" is NOT pointing to the soffits! It is pointing to the center panel BETWEEN the soffits. So I'm not sure what you wanted to know... But anyway: in your diagram, your soffits (the panels with the speakers in them) do not seem to be wide enough to be very effective. They need to be as wide as possible, 48" being ideal. Yours don't seem the be more than a few inches wider than the speakers themselves. You should probably fix that. Regarding the material for the soffit panels (the panels with the speakers in them). IT needs to be solid, dense and massive. Something like thick plywood or MDF is fine. I am planning to cast mine in concrete. So cloth is definitely out of the question! Having said that, as you move further away from the center of the speaker on teh soffit panel, the solidity and density becomes less important, so theoretically you could change to another material far away from the speaker, but it does no harm to keep it all solid and massive. Finally, regarding your center panel question: In fact, that can be whatever you need it to be, in order to correctly treat the acoustics in your room! Not much of an answer, I know. Most likely you'll want to cover that area with absorption, to deal with reflections coming off the back of the desk and console. But you can also use the SAME area as a bass trap with hangers, like John does. To do that, you'd have a panel of plywood / MDF or something like that in front of the hanger chamber, you'd leav a one foot gap at the bottom of the panel so the sound can get into the hangers (in other words, the panel does not go all the way to the floor). Then you'd cover the front of that panel with absorption. That way, you kill two birds with one stone! (If you don't want to see the gap at the bottom, you could build a frame around it and put cloth over it.) You can also do the same in the bottom section of the actual speaker soffits: create hanger space below, leave a gap at the floor, and cover the lower front of the panel with absorption. But once again, don't take the absorption too close to the speakers themselves: You do want at least a couple of feet of clear hard, massive surface around the speakers. And take a look around the forum: you'll find several designs for speaker soffits, most of them based on John's designs. You can most likely adapt some of those to your situation. - Stuart -
