lilith_envy17 October 2008#1
Well, I'm finally here! After a rush (24hrs) to finalise the design, get the conduit down for the LV wiring and demolish a 100 year old shed, I finally have the beginnings of my studio. Concrete Slab is set. Design: 3 room studio- control room, drum/amp room and medium size vox booth Aim: Multi purpose studio for bands, vox overs, film sound tracks, video editing and song writing. Gear:DAW protools, Mac G5 budget: I have just spent$12000 on the slab and outer shell and figure each room will cost me between $3000-6000. I will be building each room in stages as the money replenishes. 1st the control room..... I'm using an older room ratio 1 1.24 1.54 Which equates too 3m ceiling, 3.84m w and 4.62m length. I will "soffit mount" a set of home made passive speakers with a pair of Fostex and yorkville nearfields. My wall beams will be at 600mm spacing with the ceiling joist identical so the weight of the ceiling will sit evenly on each wall beam. For the ceiling I'm using a product called Hyspan which is simply multiple ply boards glued together vertically. You can get a far greater span with these over other timbers. Walls will be the inside out design pictured. I have finally found rigid fiberglass! at home hardware. Looks like it's been sitting there for about 8 years but I'm getting a good price on it. The front and back of the room willl start at 2.6m height, angling up for a metre or so then platueing at 3.1m in the middle. I'm still working on this design.

Soundman202025 November 2008#13
using green glue/no screws?
I don't know if that would work: the "glue" part of "green glue" is just a name. It isn't really meant to be used as glue. Plus, even if it did work, it looks like you are creating a seal in part of your "air". I have no idea what that would do acoustically, but it might not be beneficial... How about if you just stretch some acoustic fabric across the gap, staple it place, and cover the staples with molding?
SquarePants25 November 2008#14
Hi Lil, Just make it so the top piece of plasterboard covers the gap, to within 5-10mm. Use standard jointing tape to cover the corner, and remaining gap. This will not be a major path for sound to travel from one frame to the other, and will have a nice "finished" appearance... There should be a guide on how to "mud and tape" corners (and all things plastering), maybe on the CSR site? Hope this helps, Geoff
