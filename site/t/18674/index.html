Soundman20204 December 2013#3
Hi there Axel and welcome! :) You didn't mention the dimensions of your room, but very likely the issue at around 100 Hz is one of several issues that are very common in small rooms. For example, it could be modal (corresponding to a null in one of the natural modes of the room), or it could be SBIR, due to the location of the speakers and walls. Plus, you only mentioned a frequency-domain response issue, but very probably what you are dealing with is also a time-domain problem. We'd need a lot more information on the room to be able to help you pin-point the issue, and then decide how to treat it. Please provide an accurate diagram of the room, done in SketchUp, and also run a REW analysis of the room as it is right now, then post the MDAT file from that here, so we can take a look at it. Photos of the room would help a lot too. About those Helmholtz devices you mentioned: You say they are "closed" right now, which implies that you have resonant boxes inside the room, and that could also be part of the problem. Helmholtz resonators are not generally used in studios, despite the hype you see about them all over the Internet: they are extremely hard to tune accurately, and even harder to place correctly in the room. They also have to be very large to be able to deal with the typical problems found in most small rooms, and that in itself defeats the purpose of having one: just by being there, it makes the room even smaller! So it would probably be in your best interest to just get rid of them completely. They are taking up valuable space that could be used to better effect by other treatment, and they might be causing one or more problems just by being inside the room, even though they are "closed".
Conclusion: I damped the high frequencies too much.
Quite possible, but I'm not sure how you arrived at that conclusion since you don't mention anything at all about high frequencies! :) You only mentioned lows and low-mids. If you did in fact over-damp the highs, that can be fixed easily, so I wouldn't worry about that too much. I would first figure out what might be causing the issues with the lows and low-mids, fix that, then see if the highs need dealing with too.
My major question is: is there any way to raise specific frequencies up again in a room (100Hz, pretty narrow ...
Yes, assuming that it is a modal issue, and that you have enough space in the room. You would deal with that by putting very large bass traps in the room, and/or by correcting your speaker/boundary geometry, if possible. However, after having said all that, there are certain limitations imposed on what can be accomplished in any give room, simply due to the size and layout. It is impossible to get flat response in a small room (or any reasonably sized room, for that matter), simply due to the laws of physics. Sound waves interact with the walls, floor, ceiling, and other objects in the room in such manner as to create a non-flat response, and there is nothing at all that can be done to change that: It's just a fact of life that you have to live with. The larger the room, the less terrible the artifacts are, but any room that is small with respect to the dimensions of the wavelengths of the sound inside it, will have non-flat response. If your room response is a long way from the theoretical limit, then yes, you can do things to improve that, but the closer you get to the limits, the harder it is to improve things.
if I should add some more diffusor ,...
Diffusers? What types of diffuser do you already have in the room, what frequencies are they tuned to, and how big is the room? Many types of diffuser cannot be used in small rooms, especially if they are tuned low, due to the lobing artifacts. The problems you mention are probably not related to that, but you might have other problems that are related to that, and once again if you do have diffusers in a small room, then they are probably taking up space that would be used better for other treatment. So please provide as much information as possible on your room, but an especially accurate diagram with dimensions, a full REW test, and some photos. Those will allow us to analyze your room and see what is wrong with it, then figure out hoe to fix it. - Stuart -
