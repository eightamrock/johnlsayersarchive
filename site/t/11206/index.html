Filling Joists with drywall strips : why the 1/4" space

Started by shamca on 12 September 2008. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 11206.

shamca12 September 2008#1
This is a duplicate to a post I left on Ethan's site. I'm getting very close to doing this work, so I thought I would spread the joy :) ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ I'm about to start filling the joists above my studio with drywall strips as suggested in Rod's book. The section shows a 1/4" air gap between the first strip of drywall and the subfloor above. Considering the two layers of drywall that form the ceiling below, doesn't the 1/4" air gap create in effect a 3 leaf system : ceiling/air/drywall strips/air/floor ? I was thinking that I should snug the strips right up against the floor? Appreciate any thoughts. Regards, Shaun
Ro12 September 2008#2
Yes, that would create a multiple leaf. Which page in Rod's book is that?
snowdog9912 September 2008#3
I'm looking at pgs 66-68 of Rod's book. Figs 4.19 and 4.20 clearly show that the drywall is tight to the deck above. I think what may have caused your confusion is this: "Fit the board so that it is about 1/4" from your existing floor joist, hold it in place temporarily..." pg 66. However, if you look at the sentence just before that- "... you can begin by installing gypsum board directly against the bottom of your existing deck assembly. Hope that clears things up a little. Andy
