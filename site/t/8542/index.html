milestone3 April 2007#1
Hello from Cape Town We're in the process of building a new commercial studio in central Cape Town, South Africa. It's in a new building, which has been built on the site where we operated a studio for 20 years before the building we were in was demolished. The new building is 9 storeys high, has 45 apartments on the top 5 floors, three floors of parking, and we are in the basement and 1st floor. The studio was designed by an architect friend in Los Angeles, Ivan Kadey, who previously worked with Vincent van Haaff at Waterland. I've attached a pdf of the ground floor design (upstairs just has two ProTools booths, similar to the one in the attached drawing). The plans specify floating floors, dry-wall sound treatment, and suspended ceilings. We are supervising most of this ourselves, as there's not much call for professional studio-builders around here. We are finding that some of the items specified are not available at all here, so we are adapting as we go along, as our budget doesn't allow for importing lots of expensive materials. The main recording area is double-volume (around 6.5 m high - it extends up to the roof of the next floor), and all the other rooms are regular height (around 3.5m). The construction of the structural walls is complete according to the plan. We have floated floors in all the rooms (except the reception area!). The electrical conduits are in place, ready for the wiring to come through the dry-walling into ducting in the rooms. We are now getting ready for the dry-walling to be installed, and this is where our first question comes up: We plan to put it up using the steel track method, and our architect specified that the track be attached to the floor (resting on a 10mm layer of Machine rubber), using Hilti pins with a special rubber washer around them, to de-couple the track from the floating floor. We can't find a washer that looks anything like what is shown on the diagram, and we want to know how much we will be compromising the design if we just rest the track on the rubber, and attach it with the Hilti nail directly (no washer). I'm sure we will have many more questions over the next few months, but any advice on this would be appreciated. Alternatively, does anyone know of a substitute washer that could be used to isolate the Hilti pin from the track? Many thanks in advance. Murray Anderson

sharward6 April 2007#3
Who did the engineering calculations for the floating floor -- resonance frequencies, elastomer point loads, dead load and live load limits, etc.? Have you seen the "Is a Floating Floor Right For You? Answer: Probably NOT" thread? I'm not saying that floating floors aren't necessary in your case -- they may very well be -- but if they aren't engineered correctly, you run the risk of having results that are worse than if you had not floated. In the case of non-slab-on-grade installations, the results could be catastrophic (i.e., collapse, injury, death...) :roll: I also see quite a few parallel wall surfaces (e.g., iso1 and iso2)... --Keith :mrgreen:
