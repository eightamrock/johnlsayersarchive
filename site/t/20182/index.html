Hi there Stuart! Meet your namesake! :) And welcome to the forum.
The walls are build with solid block (laid flat side down creating a wider wall). Then studded, insulated and with 2 layers of plasterboard on top.
When you say "studded", do you mean that you attached stud framing directly to the outer concrete block wall? Or did you leave a gap between the concrete block wall and the stud frame? If you did not leave a gap, then there's your problem... Also, did you fill the air cavity between the concrete block wall and the plasterboard wall with acoustically suitable porous absorption, such as fiberglass insulation with a density of around 30kg/m3, or mineral wool insulation with a density of around 50 kg/m3? If not, then there's your problem... Also, did you seal the surface of that concrete block wall with some some type of masonry sealant, or at least with a good coat of paint, before you put up the plasterboard inner-leaf? If not, then there's your problem...
The internal dividing wall is the same.
How was that built? Is it done as a proper fully-decoupled two-leaf MSM system, or is that dividing wall also connected to the outer-leaf concrete block wall? If it is mechanically commected to the block wall, then there's your problem...
The ceiling is a flat roof on timber joists. Two layers of insulation and 2 layers of plasterboard.
Are those joists resting ONLY on the inner framing, or are the also attached to the outer concrete block wall? If they are attached to the block, then there's your problem... Also, you mention the ceiling but didn't mention the roof? how is the roof built?
so you can probably see straight off where the problems are or will arise.
I do have my suspicions, yes! :)
It was an overkill build,
It doesn't sound like overkill: it sounds about right for drum isolation... assuming that the inner-leaf walls are fully decoupled form the out-leaf walls, that the inner-leaf ceiling rests only on the inner-leaf walls, and that that outer leaf roof has similar mass to the outer leaf walls. If that's not the case, then it is "under-kill", not overkill.
The only thing it doesn't do is soundproof the live drums,
Measurements would be very useful here! How much isolation are you getting right now, in decibels? In other words, get someone to play your drums for you, loud and hard, while you measure that level inside the room (Use "C" weighting, slow response"), then go outside, close the door, and measure the level outside in several locations, all around the shed. Let us know what the results are. And do make sure that you use a decent sound level meter, not just an app in a smart phone! Or even worse, a cheap Chinese "toy" meter... With that info we can help figure out what is wrong, and what to do about it.
The doors/seals straight away are a weak point. Would a double internal door system be the way to go and a better external door, or just use single thicker doors for the external and internal entrances?
Assuming that you have a proper 2-leaf isolation system in place (inner-leaf stud framed walls mechanically decoupled from outer-leaf concrete block walls), then you most definitely need a pair of back-to-back doors there: one door goes in the outer-leaf, and the other door goes in the inner-leaf. Both doors must be massive (very heavy) and have at least two separate and independent full-perimeter seals (in other words, seals that run all the way around the edge: top, both sides, and bottom).
Air vent - This is something I initially asked the builder to leave out
Big mistake! But don't feel bad: most first-time studio builders make the exact same mistake. It's really hard to retrofit a proper acoustically isolating HVAC system into an existing MSM wall and still retain the correct decoupling....

xSpace3 October 2015#6
" but my fear is that i'd still have noise escaping and then be back to square one. " It will still be a requirement in the next phase. Your goal is to create mass that is consistent as possible to maintain as much of the overall frequency that you require to meet your objective. Windows and doors are the most common weak points in any build...any build anywhere in the world yours included. Now you can do other things that it might need to make a more consistent mass assembly but until you correct the failing of the weak point/leak that is that door, you will not hit your requirement. Ever.

Here are some research documents I have stored: http://1drv.ms/1DFGpr9 Select the "BBC" folder and read or print the (2)two documents, one titled: "The sound insulation of studio doors part 1:Door blanks and the other titled "The sound insulation of studio doors part 2:Door seals I don't know how to get my point across about how important proper mass and sealing is to isolate sound. Where ever the door is that actually gets you directly into the drum room needs mass and better sealing. There are some things to consider. Three things should always be in your mind when developing high isolation: 1. High Mass 2. Low rigidity 3.
