xSpace9 April 2010#10
Hally, The issue with moisture and block walls is that it is just as often coming in from the outside. But since we tend to look at our rooms from the inside, we forget about that. You can attempt to slow the moisture down by coating the interior of the room, but my guess is that, especially with a porous product like concrete block is, it is migrating from the outside to the inside. The separation that you point to in the concrete, is most likely where a footer was placed for the block and then some where down the road in time, a floor was poured on the interior side of this room. Pointing it up with concrete is not as good a barrier as you would want so you should get a elastomeric type specific for concrete joints. You want to seal that up to ward off possible moisture creeping into your room and you do not want to add the rigidity of a hard connection that can stop the cycle of expansion/contraction.
