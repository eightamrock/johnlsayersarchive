knightfly13 February 2007#4
ETF uses ONE of your line in/out channels to "characterize" the sound card - this is how it can use the "cheap" sound cards instead of high dollar ones. The other channel is the actual measurement channel, which ETF "tweaks" according to what it sees in the "calibrate" channel - this is why you have to connect the line out of one channel into it's own line in. The Rat Shack SLM's put out approximately (consumer) line level, so you shouldn't need a preamp when using that as your test mic - but be aware that the SLM doesn't come close to being "flat", and should only be used for the lower frequency measurements available in ETF - don't remember exactly where the SLM falls off, somewhere in the 4-8 kHz range IIRC. Still, it's fine for all modal concerns since they rarely exceed 400 hZ or thereabouts. Speakers - if you want to actually know what your entire ROOM is doing, then you SHOULD only use one speaker. In the case of an actual, closed, rectangular room, a single speaker should be placed as close to (for example) the lower, left, front corner - in that example, the measurement mic would be placed in the upper, right, REAR corner of the room. That way, ALL the room modes will be excited and ALL the results should be evident at the mic location. For a more accurate idea of what your speakers are doing in their chosen location, measured at the mix position, the "Y" adapter Ethan mentioned would be used so the same test signal is emanating from both speakers at equal SPL's - Even in THAT scenario, though, there are situations (like non-symmetrical object positioning in the room) where you may want to just check one speaker or the other, to see where a specific problem is coming from. Beyond that, I think Ethan's used the software a lot more than I have (I tend more toward gut feel and practice, plus haven't had enough time lately, starting a new biz doing corporate training videos) so Ethan is more likely to be able to help you with specifics of the software... Steve
