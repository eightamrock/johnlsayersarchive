knightfly24 February 2006#28
m-a-m is the derivation of the mass-spring-mass equation - air is a spring, in fact a fairly STIFF one; so for walls, we refer to it as m-a-m - in any OTHER resonant system, whatever is being used as the Spring is the "Meat" of the sandwich, so to speak. IF you rest a speaker on a foam or other flexible pad, the speaker mass and the mass of the support are separated by the spring that is the rubber or foam, etc - so this is your m-s-m combination. calculating the resonance of this involves the two masses and the elasticity of the "spring" material. Damping is yet another factor, and the higher the damping the less problems at resonance. There is always more than one way to do things, and most speakers aren't heavy enough by themselves to make a large enough mass to get resonance below audible; so two ways around this are to (1) mount the speaker SOLID to the "pillar", and float the entire pillar/speaker combination - this will lower the resonant frequency a LOT. (2) solid mount the pillar, float the speaker on the pillar thru rubber/elastomer, and add weight to the TOP of the speaker to lower the resonant frequency. knightfly12 March 2006#37
The bench should NOT intrude into the cavity behind the slats; it won't make any difference if you mount a bench right to the slats themselves, if they're strong enough; it isn't the slats that "resonate", it's the air column that's contained between the slats and behind them. That's one reason why you need cloth and absorbent right up against the slats inside, because you don't WANT the slats to vibrate if possible - it would color the room sound. You can go either way with internal dividers; using them would actually improve the range of the trap, since you'd be using several separate chambers behind same-spaced slats, each with a different average depth (therefore a different frequency range) - They've been done both ways; if you're planning on that room being your drum room, I'd consider leaving the dividers out and centering the trap for 300 hZ, or you could put dividers in maybe HALF the cavities and leave the other half as one larger trap, and use that portion's AVERAGE depth in calculations for your 300 hZ absorber. I've been working on your CR but find that I need a detail of your front CR window as it is now; exactly how you have (or will) frame your window, mount both glasses, frame the wall (single or double frame, ??) I'm looking at using 1/2" plexiglas extensions to the inside of the speakers, so they will make the baffles large enough without blocking the view, and I need to know where each component of your front wall is and how it's connected to what, etc - a DETAILED drawing or thorough explanation of how that part is done. For your rear trap, what seems to work out is to build a frame across where the little alcove is, making that entire part of the room a "non-environment" trap, and putting the couch just in front of that; this puts the couch in a good location for even modal distribution overall, even though it makes the room smaller. knightfly22 March 2006#42
Len, I got your PM; I've been lucky to get 4 hours sleep the last week or so, but plan on working on your CR front tonite. On your table scene, if you've covered those frames all over (not just the tops) with plywood, you've just built undamped, hard-to-calculate panel resonators - with at least one being in the center of a wall, it will try to affect 2nd and 4th harmonics of that wall's axial modes. This might be a good thing, but usually NOT; more likely, they will "suck out" frequencies that may already be a bit weak. Two ways I'd consider - first, fill with insulation and cover with cloth. This will be the LEAST likely to negatively affect room acoustics. Second, if you want the sides of these tables solid I would GLUE heavy rockwool to all the inside plywood surfaces to damp them. They might still act a bit as panel absorbers, but much more broadband due to the damping (therefore less detrimental) Sorry for the delay, I REALLY need a vacation about now and there's not a snowball's chance in Hell it's gonna happen... knightfly24 March 2006#46
The "non" speakers are passive radiators; they're supposed to act similar to a reflex port but without the audible "chuffing" that sometimes accompanies ports when speakers are pushed. The baffle extensions could be either triangular shaped inserts that would stand up in the corner during mix, or another way would be to use 1/2" plexi extensions across half the CR window from each side, screwed in place permanently. Too floppy and they may cause more problems than they solve. Head height is the reason for the dais under the couch, for two purposes - one is getting listener's heads out of the nulls, which wouldn't require the dais at all because they're ALREADY below center by about the right amount; but it looks to me like your CR window is kinda high, so if sitting on the couch at floor level doesn't give good line of sight into the live room, you'd need to raise the couch ABOVE the null point; this would take about 15" of rise so heads would be about 8" ABOVE the 2-4 null point instead of 8" BELOW it. You would also need to damp the decks of those two levels or you'd have MORE panel resonators mucking things up; by all means, if there's good line of site WITHOUT the riser then you shouldn't do it. Should I mount MW on the floor and ceiling of this cavity too? Same answer - although it\'ll be easier if you get your hangers done BEFORE doing the floor; they tend to be heavy, and being able to get directly under them to lift will make it easier. I\'d start at one end of the trap, and work my way to the other end with the hangers. Step 0 - set your speakers up at the front of the room and listen to a couple of your favorite CD\'s, make mental notes about the sound quality - set levels to 85 dB(c), slow response, at listening position. Step 2 - walls (this will help hold the ceiling MW in place- a couple beads of construction adhesive will hold the stuff til side walls are in place.
