knightfly20 February 2006#18
Radiusing your corners at the window will help with higher frequency diffraction effects, but won't fix the baffle size problem; I need to know which speakers you'll be using and what their published lower cutoff frequency is first, but for a lower Fo of 57 hZ, total baffle width should be 80 inches. Less than that will work, but that's optimum. The formula for finding baffle width in inches is Wb = 4560/Fo, where Wb is baffle size in inches, and Fo = the low cutoff frequency of the woofer. HTH... I'm still working on options for this... Steve

knightfly22 February 2006#22
Your speakers will need to overhang the pillar by a bit anyway, so if you build a single stack pillar, FILLED with concrete and rebar it should work. One downside is that ideally, the ENTIRE pillar and speaker should comprise the second mass in a m-s-m system in order to best isolated LF from structure - this would mean a fairly "tippy" pillar, with only an 8" wide front-to-back dimension, and could cause some problems - Speakers can move slightly when reproducing strong bass (Newton, an' all that) so might rock enough to contact the soffit and rattle. ANY speaker mount needs to isolate from structure in some way, otherwise (since speed of sound in solids is several TIMES that in air) you get a "pre-echo" radiated from surfaces close to you (like the desk) - similar to early reflections, only even EARLIER :cry: Few speakers are heavy enough by themselves to work well by placing elastomer pads right under the speakers; this usually causes the m-s-m resonance to be in the audible range. That's why it's better to "spring" the entire speaker "pillar" and hard fasten (straps or something) the speaker cabs to the pillar, so the act as one mass in the m-s-m equation. I'll try to find some time tonite (if work is slow) to play with these new dimensions, wish me luck... Steve (peaked rear absorbers aren't necessary if you don't use slats; the peak is mainly to keep rear wall reflections at higher frequencies from coming back to your ears) Side absorbers shown at the SAE site are more for portable treatments - all you should do before serious listening in your CR is to use flat, stood-off absorbers at sidewall first reflection points and cloud on ceiling, and a good deep rear absorber such as Newell uses in the Hidley-type "non-environment" rooms. In fact, you should listen FIRST so you'll appreciate each step of the treatment...

knightfly22 February 2006#25
Paul has used mostly the Sylomer for mounting floors, etc, and may have also for his speakers; it's very high damping (several times that of neoprene) so works better for all this stuff. However, in order to get m-s-m resonance low the weight needs to be high; if pillars are mounted solid to the floor, then the speaker weight should be increased, possibly by adding a thick steel plate on top. Ideally, your pillars should be in contact only with the earth; then there's more than enough damping that you can just put rubber pads under the speakers, and the pad's only function in that case would be to stop rattles, and keep the speaker from moving around on the pedestal. However this is a HUGE job to get right for a less-than-world-class million-dollar facility with REAL acousticians and engineers doing all the heavy math for location, etc - not too practical for a home/project studio (even though I've not yet ruled it out for my new build :? but probably WILL rule it out before I'm done)... Steve
