Acoustic doors and viewing window vs double sliding glass

Started by vicksburgstudio on 22 August 2007. 4 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 9294.

vicksburgstudio22 August 2007#1
1) I've been thinking about a few things for my new studio... I need about 4-5 acoustic doors... From acoustical solutions, that comes out to $15k-$20k. If that's what I gotta do, that's what I gotta do. But there has got to be a slightly cheaper alternative that is aesthetically pleasing while providing a nice STC. So my first question is: Are there any cheaper high stc doors? 2)I have been debating on the viewing window from control to live and from control to iso. I could kill 2 birds with one stone by using 2 double sliding doors. One for control to live, one for control to iso. I get the feeling that may be too much glass though. It seems as though a home depot variety sliding door has a stc33... And 2 of them will have an stc 40 or so. This isn't bad... But I am shooting for STC 55... This leads me to a thought though... If I use a stc 55 door and then use double paned viewing glass that only has an stc of 40... Then it was pointless to buy the stc 55 door. So my 2nd question is Can I get away with 2 double sliding glass doors from both a reflection standpoint and from an stc standpoint? Or does it make more sense to go with home depot type sliding door x 2?

John Sayers23 August 2007#4
I would suggest that you check your door manufacturers. They don't always list their doors as acoustic doors. In my area there are huge high rise buildings facing the ocean. At the moment there's gale force winds plummeting them. If I lived on the 35th floor of one of these buildings I wouldn't want the weather coming in through my sliders to my balcony. And it doesn't. When I approached the company that made these doors I got glass sliders that seal tight and double 8' opening doors that lock tight and stop a drummer. there's lottsa highrise in America, they make em - you just have to find them.
