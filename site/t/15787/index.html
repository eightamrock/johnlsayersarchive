gullfo28 March 2011#2
for a mastering space, tall ceilings would be ideal. covering the issue of first reflection points - floor, ceiling, front, back, and side walls. proper speaker placement including dual subs, very small console to avoid diffractions and reflections. good power conditioning to avoid any extraneous electrical noise.
rod gervais28 March 2011#3
You have a huge challenge ahead of you with that space to develop a mastering room for (even) simple stereo imagery - never mind surround sound. For simple video editing - you can pretty much make the room relatively dead - you'll need plenty of bass trapping in a room this small to make it work - and you can use wall to ceiling corners to help with that........ you should be fine with the budget you're talking about. Bass trapping is in addition to what gullfo mentioned above. DO a search on this site for "bass traps" and you'll find the info you need....... But for a mastering room you need very good sound control as well as imagery - and I do not think (even with all labor donated) that you can get there for 2 grand - in fact I doubt if you can get a "mastering environment" for surround in this room at any cost.... gullfo12 April 2011#6
overall it looks like a good plan. maybe take the large square absorbers and make them into smaller rectangular absorbers and spread them around more - first reflection points but also opposing parallel surfaces including the doors, behind the front speakers and overhead. one the clouds consider angles on them to increase the range of absorption as well as suspending them down from the ceiling 4"-12". putting some absorbers on the front of the desk/cabinets can be very helpful as well. to save some money, make the corner absorbers a layer of rigid insulation spanning across the front and pink insulation batts behind it. on the angled absorbers you have over the ceiling-wall corners consider moving them out and down around 4"-6" so they span the corner but have an air gap around them. more work but also more absorption.
