knightfly5 March 2005#19
You still haven't said what SIZE the roof trusses are made of; looks like 2x4, are the horizontal chords ALSO that size? You're saying that the holes in the centers of the concrete blocks are filled with insulation? If so, probably the way to get decent isolation would be to add two layers of 5/8 gypsum directly to the inside surface of the block to compensate for the extra "leaf" created by future inside room walls - I would LEAVE the wallboard on the ceiling, put a second layer up, seal all around, and let the blocks and those two layers of wallboard be your outer leaf. More unfaced insulation in the ceiling will help isolation some. Then, one more leaf of 2-3 layers of wallboard on each wall frame on each inner room, so that anywhere a sound tries to go it has to get through two mass regions separated by air/insulation. I agree you'll only need half the AC - if that place was a server farm before, it needed a LOT more AC than a studio will. Your double doors may just need to be ignored if it's not practical to seal them, I'd need a lot more info/pix/close-ups to know one way or the other. If you do a lot of keyboard work you may indeed want to consider a larger CR, so you have room for a key stack without crowding - although optimum distance for speakers for 5.1 surround is about 7 feet all around. As to layout, John is the king; maybe we can get him to step in with some ideas... Steve

knightfly5 March 2005#22
Depending on budget, for really loud tracking the CR should be floated; the best way to do that is 3.5" concrete poured on 3/4 OSB resting on 2x6's which are floated on EPDM pucks, and joists filled with rockwool; this gets the m-a-m resonance sub-audible; lighter materials will be harder to control boom, and won't isolate LF as well. With a floated CR, you should be able to set mics based on what you hear out of the speakers, not through the walls/floor... Steve
