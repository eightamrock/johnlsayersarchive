"the garage door needs to be able to open as i am only renting but it is paper thin." A typical garage door needs 8 feet of track space to open a 7 foot door...there is no modification you can do to this door short of some damping...adding mass is not part of your reality.

Soundman20201 March 2011#6
what about a dreaded triple leaf construction? if i got permission to have it not openable i could build a wall right in front of the cheap garage door and then keep my 4 feet of space between my wall that is directly in front of the garage door and the wall for the actual room within a room.
That should work fine. Triple leaf with one of the gaps being 4 feet and one leaf being low mass sounds an awful lot better than trying to beef up that door enough. A 4 foot air space is definitely going to help that 3-leaf. Just try to put plenty of mass on the other two leaves, and maybe damp the door itself a bit, if necessary. - Stuart -

the_speakeasy wrote:
what about a dreaded triple leaf construction? if i got permission to have it not openable i could build a wall right in front of the cheap garage door and then keep my 4 feet of space between my wall that is directly in front of the garage door and the wall for the actual room within a room. ??
Bad idea. Lets look at the garage door as being invisible. Building a wall in front of it is not an answer, only more cost with less results. If you are doing a room-in-a-room, establish where that is and where that footprint will be and develop a decoupled two framed assembly...never mind the wall in front of the garage door...it's not helping. But then again...we need more information, the ceiling/roof...these things have to be fleshed out as a unit not as "some parts may not be included"...since they are all included.
