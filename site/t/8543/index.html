Basics: I'm working on the live room for my studio. The total goal isn't to have complete sound isolation as building a room within a room is out of question for now and would eliminate valuable space. It's more to take noise down to a reasonable level at the street and from my neighbors acrossed the road. It will be used as a general purpose practice room, as well as for recording music. Mostly for multitracking, having as good of a sounding drum room with this is what I'm concerned with. Distances: Facing what I'd consider the rear of the room. On the far side you'd have about 100 feet before any house. To the right over 250 feet to the nearest house. Behind me (to the road) you have approximately 30 feet to the street, the road, then another 30 feet to the neighbors house. To my left is the dining room. Dimensions: The room I'm working with, with the new wall being put over the garage door (unattached and permanently fixed to the house) will be 22 foot long, 13.5 feet wide, and 8 foot high (minus flooring). The heigh of the roof above the ceiling is 4 foot at peak. The Plan: Electricity is all run as far as the wiring. The room has 3 20 Amp Circuits. Two are dedicated between the electrical outlets, split one long wall and one short wall each. The third is strictly dedicated to the lighting. The boxes are to be surface mounted so that will be done after the rock is up. Holes will be made to pop the wiring through and then caulked up. This consists of 9 metal double outlet boxes. The walls are going to have 16" wide R-13 Mineral Wool. The ceiling is going to have the same. The roof /\ above the ceiling will have 24" wide R-19 mineral wool. as well as the walls of the roof connecting to the house and the outside... Then the walls will have one layer of 5/8" gypsum board, spaced with an 1/8" gap that will be filled with acoustic caulk. These boards will be installed horizontally. Then over this there will be a layer of 1/2" gypsum board, spaced with an 1/8" gap that will also be caulked up and smoothed out with a wet putty knife. These will be installed vertically as to eliminate much of the seam overlapping. This will then be painted. Hardwood installed on the flooring over a little padding and vapor barrier (over concrete). Then I will install acoustic treatments that I'm making. About 60% coverage of broadband absorption traps on the walls which I'll make, fabric wrapped. Made from 2" rigid mineral wool. These will be mounted with a two inch air gap behind them to increase their effectiveness. Each of the corners will have an 8 foot tall bass trap made from 4" rigid mineral wool. They'll be two foot wide and straddle the corners. Then the ceiling will have 3 clouds that will be roughly 4' x 4'6". These clouds will have frames which each have 9 can lights. and two runs of 2' x 4' 2" rigid fiberglass. The frame will have 3 cans on any of the outside edges, and then one in the center frame piece. The ceiling will have a hole for access to the attic, for maintaining any cables run from inside the house (where the control room is located) and anything else needed to be done up there. The hole with have an ad-hock cover comprised of thin plywood and rigid fiberglass bound by fabric (like a little absorption panel.) that will rest over the hole. Questions: 1. I need to work on a system for delivering air in and out. Preferably heating and A/C. The room itself is only around 300 square feet, which accordingly to different sites I've looked at wouldn't require any more than a "1 Ton" unit. I am pretty clueless as far as to what I'm going to use for this. As I posted in another thread, I'd like to have this wall mounted above the ceiling, in the wall near the roof itself. I can easily make the kind of diffusor boxes to slow air down to have it not be a loud problem and can develop a sort of ISO box for the unit, even decoupling it from everything to keep vibrations down. I need advice on picking a unit. Preferably in the 500-600 USD price range for the unit. 2. Do you have any other ideas for the room as far as absorption? Should there be some diffusion? What sort? Details please. 3. Anything else you can think of would be greatly appreciated. I apologize for the lack of a sketch. My computer is being extra screwy today. The room is a fairly simple rectangle with a ceiling, and then roof over it. Hopefully I have described every well enough. Any questions, please ask.

jwl14 May 2007#24
Hi there For a live room, I'd probably approach it like this: 1. Put superchunks in the corners. This will help even out your low end. 2. Hang a cloud over where you envision the main recording area to be, esp if there is a drum riser or something. 3. Build John's angled slot resonators throughout much of the room to break up reflections. If your walls are angled you can build straight slot resonators, these will help balance out the room sound nicely.
