Regarding the 'absolute' exterior wall ... it does need to be airtight [like most everything else in studio building]. A one room studio, for recording and mixing. ... It'll be mainly for me working alone, but should be able to handle a band with drumset, or several people doing voice-over. ... The room will also be used partly as a living-room, and it should be possible to use it as a spare bed-room. ... If selling the property later, I'd like the room to be usable for other purposes -if possible.
Clearly, a one-room studio is the best option for you. No doubt about that! You only have about 25 m2 of space, so dividing that up into several rooms is not an option. Or at least, not a good one.
The goal regarding sound-isolation is 60db.
OK, it's good that you have defined that, and it is possible, but it is not cheap to get such high level of isolation. Since your budget is limited, it might take a while to be able to do this, but as long as you recognize the limitations, then it can be done. So the first order of business is to figure out how you are going to modify that building to get 60 dB of isolation. And second priority is to get the room sounding good (acoustic treatment). Right now there is an issue with the dimensions of the room: it is practically square! 5.93 x 5.94. So you will have to take that into account when you build the inner-leaf, and design it so that the dimensions of the final room are not square, and are close to a known good ratio.
This is how the walls are currently:
You are going to need to change that: the inner surface will have to be removed, and you will need to put a lot more mass on the outer leaf, to get the level of isolation that you are aiming for.
Concrete floor with drain
That's a problem! There is a reason for that drain, so you can't just close it up. You need to find out why it is there, and see if you can remove the reason so you can seal up the drain. It also implies that there is a drain pipe in your slab, which is a potential issue with attaining high levels of isolation.
How should I build my walls? Angled?
You first need to define your basic design concept. Angled walls are useful for several things: eliminating flutter echo, creating a reflection free zone, redistributing modes, etc. So for example if you chose to have an RFZ design, then yes you do need to angle the front section of the side walls. But if not, then it is not necessary.
How to deal with the quadratic shape?
You will need to build the inner-leaf so that the square shape is gone. Based on your limits of length and height, use one of the room mode calculators to find a width that gives you a good ratio.
Where should my mixing-position be?
On the center-line of the room, at roughly 38% of the distance from the front wall to the back wall: That's where your ears should be: Your speakers should then be set up such that they are symmetrical about the center line (same distance left and right), angled inwards at 30°, and aimed so that they acoustic axes intersect about 20 cm behind your head, at an angle of 60°. The speakers should be mounted on massive, heavy, robust, rigid stands, such that the acoustic axis of the speaker is 1.2m above the floor. That's the basic layout. There are more recommendations in the specifications of the ITU, EBU, and other organizations for control rooms, but those are the basics.
- Basstraps in the corners
Absolutely! The room will need a lot of bass trapping, so I would suggest superchunks in the rear vertical corners and perhaps some of the horizontal corners too.
- Soffit-mounted speakers
Excellent! Then maybe an RFZ design would be a good idea for you.
- Mid/high absorption centered on the walls.
Yes, but not too much: there will be a lot of high absorption in the bass traps, so I would concentrate the mid/high absorption around your first reflection points and on the rear wall.
- Maybe some storage/lockers/rack between speakers and basstraps in 2 corners
Not on the front wall! They can go on the sides, or at the back, but the front wall must be kept clear.
- False ceiling all over, broadband absorber (org. ceiling is 2,50m)
Not necessary. Just a large cloud over the mix position, possibly angled, and possibly hard-backed, as needed. ---
But, as I then would have to make a car-port, I'm starting to believe it's better leaving the garage as garage (it's a nice garage), and maybe build the studio from ground up -possibly as an extension of the garage-building.
If you have the budget, then that would definitely be a better option, since you would be able to design the building from the start as a proper studio, and not try to adapt something that was never meant to be a studio. ---
Would like some perspectives from non-US/ cold places, though
That won't affect the studio acoustics at all. In fact, since the studio will have to have thick two-leaf walls with a large amount of insulation all around, then thermal issues should not be a problem. - Stuart -
