RLL0524 December 2006#1
This is a terrific site! My drawing skills are limited and not on par with the other pro designs...This is my first post and I would appreciate any feedback on my plans. Primary purpose of room: Be able to play drums at any hour without disturbing my children or neighbors who live 20' away from the basement. I want to keep part of the basement for a workshop. Secondary purpose: Recording. I tried to stay close to the 1:1.5:1.2 ratio, although with a room this small, I hear the ratios are less important. Proposed room size: Ceiling: 7.5' 11'L X 7'W Budget: $1,500 - $2,000 Location: Atlanta, GA Basement construction is cinderblock with a concrete slab floor. To keep the sound in, I plan to use the room within a room design. Framing will be 2 X 6 with staggered 2 X 4s. Plan to use 3 5/8 sheets of drywall on each side of the walls with R19 isolation in the middle. Studio doors will be wood with MDF overlay treatment described on this site to give them about a 40 STC rating. For mechanical isolation, I will not attach any of the 2 X 6s to the cinderblock. I plan to keep the walls about 2" away. Also there is an exterior window in cinderblock wall now. To improve the sound retention, I don't plan to build a window in the drum room. Basically, just close off the outside window by building the wall across it. The ceiling will also have 3 5/8 sheets of drywall. I plan to use the existing concrete floor with acoustic caulk at all plates/joints. Will add padding and rug to floor. Here is a very rough sketch of the room within the 25' X 12' basement. My drum playing measured 104 - 110dB. Question: Does this look like the best orientation for the drum room, given the dimensions of the basement? Are there any other techniques I should absolutely follow to make the room as quiet as possible to contain the drum sound? Will the techniques above reduce the sound outside the room to ~ 60dB? Thanks for your input. Ron Notes on Attachments IMG_2799 View of basement from exterior door. Studio will be in far right corner. Stairs from upstairs level are on far left. RLL05 wrote:
. . . a question about whether floating a floor is needed. I want to be able to play at anytime without bothering neighbors 30' away.
With a 7' 6" ceiling, you don't have the headroom to float the room, so it's a moot point.
RLL05 wrote:
there will be 2 X10" walls with 2 X 4's on each side, 703 insulation, 2 1/2" of airspace between the 2 X 4's, RC on the inside wall (only) with 3 layers of 5/8 sheetrock on the inside and outside.
If you're planning to do separate frames, there's no need for RC and it is actually not recommended to use RC in that situation.
RLL05 wrote:
If the floor is sealed with acoustical caulk at the sole plates, do I have to float a floor to get the sound reduced to where it won't bother neighbors?
Moot. But even if it weren't, you'd have to build one heck of a bunker for it to keep up with your floor. Have you bought Rod's book yet? If not, you should! 8) --Keith :mrgreen:

RLL058 December 2007#12
Finally finished the studio. 1 year start to finish. Decibel level inside room: 112 1 foot outside the door: 63Db. A reduction of 49 decibels! 5 feet from door <60 decibels I am very pleased with the results. Thanks to Rod, Glenn, Sheward and everyone on this site who provided advice. The techniques in Rod's book really work. Building the Super Door was key. The drop seal, 2 layers of Type K gaskets, and magnetic weather striping provided a tight seal. I also routed a 1/2" steel bar into the door to improve contact with the magnetic weather-stripping. The thumb latch holds the door tight against the seals and magnetic weather striping. Fresh Air is a must - great advice. The Fantech unit does a very good job. The best part is now I can play after 10PM and not wake up my sleeping children. This is a small studio (10' X 6.5') so it is difficult to photograph. Thanks again for an informative and very helpful website. Ron

RLL055 January 2008#18
Wael, Yes, the white 4" circle vents on the back wall (behind the drums) are for ventilation. Note: nothing goes through the ceiling of the shell. I used 4" metallic venting to go straight through the double walls.. Any gaps between the sheetrock is filled in with caulking and backer rod - both the inside and outside wall. The sound doesn't travel beyond 12 feet of the flexible venting pipe. With the caulking and backer rod there is no noticeable loss in isolation - keep it tight so there are no leaks. The Fantech heat exchange unit brings in fresh air from the outside. Works great. Ron
