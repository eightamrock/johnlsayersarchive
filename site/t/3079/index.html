John Sayers26 January 2005#2
Matt - it really depends on what sort of drum room you want - should it be live, semi live or dead - those are the options. You can build a very live room and they are great but they are always live except for some high end deadening you can achieve with curtains etc. Alternatively you can build a room which is variable - by changing the wall treatments you can go from relatively live to relatively dead. Go to my site (click John Sayers Productions log top right) and check out the drum room in Cloud Studios and then check the larger room in Left Bank studios. Cloud is totally live - no traps etc. nothing but glass and lots of angled timber - it works great and has a short reverb of around 1.2 sec which is great for drums. Flutes,percussion digeridoos all sound cool in there also. Left bank on the other hand is deader and has variable treatment along one wall that changes the rooms decay. Drums sound great in there also as does the piano. Once you've sorted that out maybe then we can talk about how to build it :) cheers john

I have done mixdowns with various studios around Dallas and in Nashville. And everybody's rooms are different ( just adds to the confusion by the way ). But there is one inevitable truth. A room with good acoustics makes really good tracks. You can take cheap mics and place them in their sweet spots and you will get a sound that's what I like to call "Set in Stone". But on the other hand, I've seen the most expensive mics in a bad room and you couldn't hardly find a sweet spot. Poor guy was moving mics forever and a day. Waste of time and money if you ask me. So they need to be pretty dry/dead. And tracked right to begin with. But Don't want to completely rid it of some of the rooms natural acoustics for good stereo seasoning.
