lex1257 July 2004#32
I'm getting a very late start on this thread, but I'd like to add a general comment. I'm VERY glad to see that Lonesome Leo and his boys are fans of a very large tracking room. I am always amazed when others suggest giving a large portion of the available space to the control room??? Personally, I could work with a closet for a control room, the live room is where the music happens. Maybe if you are one of those "1 man bands" that does 90% of your work behind a computer keyboard in the control room and rarely record live instruments; but even then, why do you need so much space in the control room? I record acoustic instruments almost exclusively and I am constantly amazed when I walk into a mid to upper level studio and find a live room that looks more like a vocal booth. The largest possible live room, with the best possible natural acoustics, is more important than any control room, if you spend any time recording actual instruments. Maybe if you spend most of your time mixing music recorded elsewhere, you might want a huge control room for for your clients and their "entourage." But any studio that does tracking as a regular part of their work might be better off with as much space as possible dedicated to the live room and use what's left over for a control room. I guess it comes down to your opinion of what side of the glass has the "important" activity taking place: the knob twiddling or the playing of instruments? Sorry for rambling, but this has been one of my most frustrating issues whenever I have to work in a studio (instead of on location).
