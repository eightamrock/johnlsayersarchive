Why are you doing "staggered stud" instead of separate wall frames? There are several problem with using continuous rubber as an elastomer under your wall frames. In order for the rubber to act as a spring, it will need to be allowed to compress/deflect somewhat, preferably around 10-15% to give you elastomer properties without overloading it (thus shortening its lifespan) and without underloading it (thus not acting as a spring). If you float the walls on pucks, you have large air gaps you'll have to fill with something resilient. Then, you still have your floor that will act as a flanking path for structure borne transmission. :roll: Honestly, you haven't yet posted enough detail about your intentions. You haven't yet told us how many sheets of drywall you intend to use on your staggered stud wall -- you've only told us your method of framing. You're concerned about decoupling (which is good) but you haven't given us enough of the variables in the equation to even know if your proposed decoupling method is sufficient for the amount of mass you'll be using. It could be that the amount of mass you intend to use is way too small, such that decoupling won't be your weakest link (the lack of mass would be). What about that ceiling? What's overhead? Review the "Before You Post" announcement thoroughly and present all the information that is requested in that announcement. Don't skimp on the details -- nobody ever gets lectured on providing too much information. :) Extreme anal detail needed. Imagine you have to describe your building in such detail that it has to be recreated by someone else. That's what we're talkin' about. --Keith :mrgreen:
