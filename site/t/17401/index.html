xSpace15 February 2012#4
and....pictures, pictures, pictures :) Might be the only thing you can do is going to be a basic damped type flooring system. This would mean installing something similar to 2 inch rigid owens corning insulation on the entire floor area, and add two layers of 3/4 inch plywood over the top of the is insulation. But this method will change the door elevations...so as they say, there is the rub. Then again, you might be able to simply add mass directly to the existing floor and have that work, more understanding of how this floor leaks into the surrounding environment would help some. Still, the door elevations are going to be modified. But you did not mention the overhead, the ceiling/roof area...this is often the weak area and may still prove to be as such. xSpace29 February 2012#10
"the floor will take the weight." You do not know that...we have not the first measurement to work with, so we do not know that as well. :) I would venture to say that you currently have a mass/spring/mass system in this floor. You have the upper layer of the floor, the air space and the damped Earth. Kind of a good thing, maybe more mass on the upper side of the above floor would help, depending on HOW much leakage is available in the brick perimeter with coupling vents. But, will the floor take the weight of the sand and the additional framing has yet to be determined. With a center support under the floor joists this option could be doable...need some measurements to work with.
