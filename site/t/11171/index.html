Hola, gang--been a lurker for many months before and during the design, so thanks for all the good tips I've picked up here. I wanted to post the layout of my new studio to get your comments. It was designed by Ken Goerres, based on the way that I like to work. I thought you'd find it interesting, and I also thought I could benefit from your questions and feedback. The studio is not for public use, although I will bring in artists I am producing and record them. I am a multi-instrumentalist and engineer, and many of my projects feature me playing the lion's share of the instruments, but I am also a jazz pianist who wants to be able to record an acoustic trio, and I think all of this can be accomplished by the plans below. The studio will be adjacent to a new house we're building in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. There is very little wood used in construction here. In addition, we will be in the middle of absolutely nothing--our lot is 5 acres, we own the lot next to us, and the nearest neighbor is 10 acres away. We're on a hillside a mile from any roads save our little access road which would get no traffic other than our own. Soundproofing from the outside world is not a major consideration and barely a minor one--if a goat wanders by and bleats during a take, being a jazz musician I'll either use it as inspiration or just start over. :) Ok, first the overview: http://img.skitch.com/20080901-kbnu9ffpqih3br66e2qpbd65j8.preview.jpgClick for full size - Uploaded with plasq's Skitch Total square footage: 700sf. The materials: most of the walls, including the outer ones, will be brick. I might plaster over them, or I might leave some of them exposed for a little guerilla-style diffusion. An exploded view with the ceiling design: http://img.skitch.com/20080901-1ijjh4i2ud5717w6ccphtweag8.preview.jpg Click for full size - Uploaded with plasq's Skitch The tall, thin items in corners are bass traps. The array of clouds on the ceiling was designed by Ken. They are inside the bass and drum booths as well. The drum and bass booths will have sliding glass doors--Ken has a brand in mind that has the highest rating in terms of keeping sound on either side, but I don't have the name right now. There will be a double-paned window between the two booths as well. The idea is that I can slide over from the mixing area to the acoustic piano (in the middle of the room, what I'm calling the 'live area'), and have sight lines to the drummer and the acoustic bassist, and they can all see each other--for me, that's crucial for recording jazz. I'm coming from a one-room studio--I've recorded a trio with only gobos and baffles, and while the results were good it was too much of a challenge during mixdown for me, especially if we wanted to punch some parts in on the bass or piano. This new layout should give me a better head start. The walls at the bottom of the picture will likely be framed in wood and the areas behind them will be stuffed with absorbent material. Here is a view into the drum booth: http://img.skitch.com/20080901-r46prt21denws6kxg9sfy6tpmd.preview.jpgClick for full size - Uploaded with plasq's Skitch The windows are those horizontal slits. These are probably going to get a little bigger, but not big enough that someone could squeeze through. I will probably leave most of them open for ventilation all the time (with screens). A/C and heat don't get used in this region--most of our house is designed to be open-air most of the time. Finally, here are the plans themselves--not totally complete, and maddeningly Ken doesn't use a computer in his design work so all I have now is a photo of the plans which I gave to the architect/GC. http://img.skitch.com/20080901-e5nmg3mx4h1m9a1sndm1jqnhdq.preview.jpg Click for full size - Uploaded with plasq's Skitch I am curious to hear your thoughts and questions. Things do get done differently here, and my needs aren't the same as a commercial studio (such as needing a separate control booth). Many thanks, Doug
