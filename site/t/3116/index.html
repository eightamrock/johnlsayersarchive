knightfly3 February 2005#3
Gluing isn't a good plan for ceilings, and in most cases not even for walls - it's been found that as long as one side of a wall's cladding is resiliently mounted (or on a separate frame) that flanking from fasteners makes little difference; and insulation soaks up whatever might make it through. I'd just go with both layers 5/8 - it seems the extra mass helps more than the slight improvement in coincidence frequency caused by dissimilar thicknesses of wallboard; hope that helps... Steve

sharward3 February 2005#6
J Wah wrote:
...could you define flanking?
Flanking is the transfer of sound energy to structural components via firm connections, like fasteners or brackets. Think of the "two cans and string" experiment -- the sound flanks along the string. You are using resilient channel for decoupling, which eliminates flanking. I'm sure I won't win any contests with that definition, but I think it gets the point across. :wink: Edit: Here's a better and more official definition:
Custom Audio Designs wrote:
Flanking sound transmission occurs when the sound takes a secondary route instead of the direct noise path. Flanking transmission can be either airborne or structure borne. (Source)
So, there you go... Not just structural paths, but airborne paths as well.
