gullfo24 March 2011#3
welcome! one option, since it sounds like isolation isn't the concern, would be to replace some of the ceiling tiles with space couplers and panels with slots cut in them (alternating) and add absorption above that. this could extend the "space" and work to remove the echoes and still provide enough absorption where needed. consider polys on the walls to keep things diffusive and add some bass absorption. perhaps some deep cloth cover absorbers between the polys. you might need some panel traps as well depending on what the acoustic measurements tell us. if you're not going to encase the "control room", you might consider some free-standing treatment cabinets to form a symmetrical area on the floor for your desk. use gobos to separate the instruments and adjust the mix space when needed.

Soundman202025 March 2011#7
I could close of the laundry/kitchen area and leave an opening which i could slide a gobo to cover when recording.
For what purpose? If you are planning to use them to isolate your room, then that won't work at all. Isolation requires abundant mass and totally hermetic, airtight seals. That's practically impossible to get with mobile gobos. But if your plan is to use them for treatment inside the room, then that's a possibility. On the other hand, if they are only for treatment (not isolation), then you probably don't need them anyway, or at least not in those positions.
Will i find solutions for this by searching the forum ...
You bet! :) This forum is a gold mine of very solid, sound (excuse the pun) detailed and correct information on acoustics. It's one of the best places on the internet, in fact. The only problem is that it is not really organized in a manner that allows you to find things easily. The search function helps you there, or you can do what many of us do: just jump in and wade around, reading through the threads of some of the many builds here, following the links, etc. That could keep you occupied for years, but when you finish, you'll be an expert.
or other you can point me in the direction of that will assist me in designing my way around this?
The two best books that are often recommended here are "Master Handbook of Acoustics" by F. Alton Everest, and "Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros", by Rod Gervais. Between those two, you'll learn all you need to know to understand the basics of acoustics and studio design. Or you can just start posting your own designs, based on what you already know, and asking questions about them. That kind of is how things work here. This is sort of a "self service" forum, where you learn what you can until you get stuck, then post your design so far and explain why you are stuck, so someone can help you get over that, and you can carry on until you get stuck again.
I have two support iron beams encased in bulk heads down there as well and i think that is probably also an acoustic disaster waiting to happen is it not?
