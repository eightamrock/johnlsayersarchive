Building cloud: twice as much is twice as good?

Started by Lasso on 3 July 2007. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 9063.

Lasso3 July 2007#1
AS the title indicates, I'm building a cloud in my tracking room to reduce floor to ceiling flutter. I came to wonder: the standard fiber glass (rockwool) is 45 mm thick here in Denmark. Would putting up two layers of fiber glass make the cloud double as effective or would one layer actually be enough? Two layers would of course be better to some degree, but enough to justify the extra weight, labor and money?
lovecow3 July 2007#2
No. Assuming the rockwool is exposed to the sound waves on the room side, then the double thickness will only improve the low frequency absorption (relative to the single thickness). How much depends on frequency. At higher frequencies, the improvement will be negligible.
Lasso3 July 2007#3
Ah, yes. That makes sence. Thanks for putting that in perspective!
Ethan Winer4 July 2007#4
Lasso wrote:
Would putting up two layers of fiber glass make the cloud double as effective or would one layer actually be enough?
Jeff is correct (as usual), but don't underestimate the value in having a cloud absorb bass frequencies. The more bass absorption you have in a room, the closer you'll get to a flat response. If the cloud is four inches thick and spaced four inches below the ceiling, you'll get much better results overall. --Ethan
