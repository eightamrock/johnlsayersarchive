Soundman202012 November 2008#28
Actually, those links were much more about your first question than your second question: "1) how do I soundproof the inside of the duct that is going to the studio to pump air? I would assume the sound would travel right through the vent to the rest of the house, thus making isolation created rather pointless. " They cover all of that pretty well: Lining your ducts and building silencers is the way to go. Lots of examples there. I don't think those threads deal much with code. I think the only person who can determine if your fresh-air plan is good enough and meets code, is a qualified HVAC contractor. I would imagine that it depends on the maximum number of people you will have in your room at the same time, and the flow rate, and the amount of fresh air mixed into the system, and the total number of outlets, and a whole bunch of other things. I'd really suggest that you get an HVAC guy to look at your plans, and tell you. Personally, for my room I'm planning to build a separate ventilation system, not connected to the house at all. The last thing I want is cooking smells getting into my studio from the kitchen, or the smell of popcorn from someone watching a movie, or even worse, cigarette smoke getting drawn in. Nobody in the family smokes, but guests sometimes do, and I'd really hate to have smoke in my room: So I'm building a separate system, for those reasons, but they might not apply to your situation. That's just I myself me personally! - Stuart -
