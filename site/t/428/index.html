knightfly12 July 2003#3
Hey xP, as far as the "office" partitions go, I'd need to know more about the way they're built - some of those are just a single absorbent panel covered with a durable, unobtrusive cloth and framed with feet so they stand up - others are thicker, with an air space between outer layers, and there are undoubtedly still others I'm not familiar with. The main thing you need to keep in mind (think "mantra" here) is that ANY deviation from a "double leaf" wall (or ceiling, for that matter) will almost ALWAYS make isolation WORSE instead of better. So, if your "office" partitions already have an air space and you put a third "center of mass" in a wall made with them, you could actually LOWER the Transmission Loss of that wall. Unless those partitions are huge (full size, ceilng height) you'd probably be better off using the partitions for Gobo's inside the studio and building your walls from scratch with a method you KNOW will work. ".36 NRC rating.. " - all this means, is that the panels are 36 % absorbent in the range of 250 hz to 2000 hZ - not too practical a criteria for a recording studio. The NRC system only looks at 250, 500, 1000 and 2000 hZ absorption. This is OK for office noise, but says nothing about the performance in stopping bulldozer noise, kick drum noise, 6-string bass noise, etc. - in order to find out what these panels do at other frequencies, you would need to test them yourself. Kind of a daunting task... If you intend to use ALL the space in that 25 x 25 x 10 area, you'll need to consider modal response - it won't be good at all with those dimensions, hopefully you intend to break it up into a control room/tracking room/iso booth kind of deal. The higher ceilings are good, the brick is good for low freq isolation (with some help from a secondary wall) - this is where you need to figure out just what you want to do, and how far you want to go with construction/design, etc. You didn't mention ceiling/roof construction, that will also need to be looked at closely if you need good isolation. Note that I try not to use the term "Sound PROOF" - that is a term that was "supposedly" coined in the 50's by the TV show, "The $64,000 Question", in order to convince people that they weren't cheating. Apparently, they WERE cheating, but the term "sound proof booth" stuck. The reality is the same as for the term, "bullet-proof vest", as well as others. Hint - you got a bullet-proof vest? I'll just get a bigger, faster bullet... Anyway, you should work on figuring out just what you want to do with your space, how much noise there is in the neighborhood during the times you would want to record, how much noise you intend to MAKE, etc - that's the only way you'll know how much isolation you need at what frequencies, and what it will take to get there from what you already have... Steve
