Soundman202015 September 2009#2
I run live sound a lot for churches, and what you describe is a universal problem: acoustic drums in a small hall usually sound lousy, not to mention overwhelming. Usually the problem isn't just the wall behind the drums, or even just the stage area, but the entire hall! I rarely come across small church halls with good enough acoustics that an acoustic kit can be made to sound really good, and in my experience most churches just aren't willing to invest the money needed to treat to hall properly. I hate to admit it, but in those cases what I usually recommend for small churches is to forget acoustic drums, and go with electronic drums. That helps in several ways, but of course can never give the same sound as a great acoustic kit in a well treated hall. The treatment, of course, depends on the hall! There is no such thing as "one size fits all" in acoustic treatment. So to answer your question: putting a thick layer of 703 over a large surface area behind the drum kit might help somewhat, but how much it helps depends on the rest of the hall: It might make a big difference if that wall was the only remaining culprit in an otherwise reasonable room. Or it might make practically no difference at all if the room is basically a concrete cave with the drum kit sitting on a hollow stage with a thin wooden floor. Shape, dimensions and construction materials are the issues here. ( Of course, having the right drummer can go a long way towards improving the sound of the drum kit! :) ) If you describe better exactly what you have around your drum kit, in terms of construction, and more about the hall itself, then folks here might be able to give you some suggestions to improve the situation. And hopefully you'll be able to get the pastor / elders / deacons / musicians / congregation on board with whatever plan you come up with. That will probably be just as hard as coming up with a good plan in the first place, if not harder! - Stuart -
