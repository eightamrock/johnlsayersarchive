John, welcome to the board; if isolation isn't your main concern, then just filling the block with sand may be enough; that would leave you more room inside for acoustic treatment, which you'll definitely need with concrete walls. Tin for roof - is this corrugated, or flat with ridges every 200mm, or what? I'm asking because this may make it necessary to put more layers of drywall on the inside to combat the multi-leaf situation you're getting yourself into with the ceiling. If you want really good isolation between your Control Room (CPU room) and the live room, you'll need a double framed, well-insulated and caulked wall between them; a window makes it harder to get good isolation, but with heavy enough glass and a wide enough air space it can be done. A sketch of what you plan for a roof, starting with the tin and including every detail through the roof into the innermost ceiling layer, will be necessary to see if you're on the right track there. knightfly11 June 2005#13
John, you are about as far from "rude" as anyone could get and still be alive, no need whatever for apology. I've been so tied up trying to make sure the opportunity of a lifetime doesn't get away from me, I've missed more than a few members' needs - hopefully I can stay up on this better now (at least for a while) - Your drawings, as Keith so jealously :wink: pointed out, are wonderful; they show everything needed, except an answer that will work for you :cry: By that, I mean that if you just put mass on the bottom of your roof rafters, and only have the light corrugated metal for the upper mass, then your mass-air-mass situation for the roof won't stop much of anything in the way of sound - Ventilation-wise, that's the way to build it - it allows the metal to breathe, and will therefore keep your framing from water/moisture/mold damage. Unfortunately, that's not what works for sound isolation - for that, you need as heavy a mass as can be supported, then an insulated air gap as wide as you can get, then ANOTHER mass, so that anywhere you don't want sound to go there is a mass-air-mass barrier in the way. The corrugated shape of the metal makes it nearly impossible to put MORE mass at that position, because the air spaces caused by the corrugations make almost all of the outer skin act as a THIRD LEAF of mass - this can (in a limited frequency band) make mids to high mid frequencies better isolated, but it raises hell with low frequency isolation by creating resonances which allow those frequencies to pass through the "barrier" too easily. IF this type of roof is necessary, the only way I know to "compensate" for its weak sound isolation is to put EVEN HEAVIER MASS right below the 2x2 stringers, using heavier framing for support, then insulate the cavities between these heavier rafters, then put another (preferably decoupled) heavy mass UNDER these heavier rafters - this gives still a 3-leaf construction, but the heavier masses will (somewhat) make up for the extra (corrugated) light leaf. I didn't mention air/vapor barriers in this explanation yet; I would need to know more about your climate and whether you heat the space or cool the space more of the time. If sound isolation is AT ALL important to you, please wait to build until we've covered this in more depth... Steve

knightfly22 June 2005#21
Agreed; that is a good plan for a mass-air-mass system, the deep air space should be at least half-filled with fiberglass insulation, NO BACKING of any kind, and preferably two layers of 15mm drywall (gypsum) for the inner drop ceiling. Be carefull NOT to put anything that can act as a vapor barrier, in any location other than required by your local codes; with your "half and half" climate, I'm not sure where your building people requre this but the MAIN thing is to NOT put TWO such barriers; the construction needs to be able to "breathe" to one side or the other... BTW, foam and the like is 99.9% WORTHLESS for soundproofing or insulation in this application; I'm glad you saved me the trouble of convincing you... :) Steve
