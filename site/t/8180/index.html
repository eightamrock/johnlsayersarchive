knightfly12 February 2007#2
Wes is well known to me, and we usually agree on most things - his idea of a good, simple 1-room setup among them. If you build a rectangular room around established LWH ratios, it will still have a LOT of flutter and some modal issues - by the time you get rid of the flutter, the room may well be too dead for your tastes. So your choice may end up between "live enough" and "no flutter echo" - If instead you splay your walls and ceiling so that they are at least 12 degrees (total) out of parallel, you kill the flutter without killing the highs/high mids - that way, you still have the option of just how reverberant you want the room to be. I generally try to keep to one or another set of "ideal" ratios, taking the AVERAGE dimensions for width and for ceiling height - the front and rear walls may still end up parallel, but you will typically have quite a bit of absorption on those surfaces to keep flutter at bay. Generally, a "live" room should have a longer decay time than a CR, so for a 1-room setup I'd always build some 2-sided gobo's - absorbent on one side, reflective on the other - then you can reverse them if you want an area brighter for recording. The main reason for the difference is so that you don't mix in an overly live room and think you're actually putting that extra 'verb on the recording media - so if you keep the mix area drier, then the "wet" you hear is much more likely to be "on tape". If you stick with a single room, you're gonna want some sort of iso booth or closet for computers; otherwise, you'll find yourself looking for a "bandaid" to get rid of the white noise that's on every track you lay down. That's just a quick run-down, keep asking if you still have questions - that's what we're here for (and you thought it was just the dancing girls :wink: ) Steve
