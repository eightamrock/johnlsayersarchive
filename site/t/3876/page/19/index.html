Dan Fitzpatrick27 January 2008#460
ok, i've finally got some pictures. as of about a week ago i have really and truly completed the outer leaf. the last thing i did was the door closer and the doorjamb and seal. there is nothing else to do but start building the inner leaf. for the door seal i was going to go with Rod's K seal recommendation, and i even found a source for it online, but then i decided to try some stuff i found at home depot instead, which probably isnt' as good, but the way my jamb is done it is easy to change if i decide to. today i bought half of the framing lumber for the inner leaf walls, i'll do half first, move all the crap, and then do the other half (the half that includes the door will be done second). then the ceiling joists. the only remaining head-scratcher is how to run all of the computer cables thru the wall. i want the computer to be outside the room, and just the monitor, keyboard etc inside. i probably will make a small removable panel in the inner and outer leaves (very far from each other) and make keyholes big enough to fit the required cables. and then caulk. should be good. i did some sound testing with the complete outer leaf, just for fun. i created some tone tracks at 16hz, 32hz, 64hz (more or less anyway) and blasted the tones as loud as my little computer speakers w/subwoofer would go (not very loud, only about 85db according to my radio shack meter). then i went outside to hear what i could hear. isolation, masked by background noise, was surprisingly good for one leaf. but i could hear particularly the 16hz tone fairly easily even quite a ways from the garage. When i did the tones at 75db i really couldn't hear anything. i'm very confident that once i get the second leaf done i'll have excellent isolation. the weakest point was my door, of course. but that is ok since it is only bad when you are in the other part of the garage. outside or in the house, there is another door that stops the noise. but anyway once i have the second door from the second leaf everything should be peachy. but i don't think it's really the seal that's the problem, since even when i put my whole weight against the door to seal it better, there is hardly any difference, except in the high frequencies, which you would expect would suffer most from a bad seal. but i'm not worried about highs. the problem with doors i think is that they are movable. they may have as good or better density than the wall right adjacent to them, but the wall is attached to more wall and the floor, etc, and isn't going to move as much. the door is going to vibrate and transmit sound no matter how good the seal is. you have to add a lot more mass to improve it. which i could do. anyway, here are the pix.

Dan Fitzpatrick27 January 2008#461
oh yeah, don't forget to check out the kick ass shelves i made in the first picture, attached to the new outer leaf wall. lots of good storage space there. also, one note about the door seal. i was pretty sure that the adhesive on that seal wasn't going to stick worth a damn on the bare wood of the jamb, so i put contact cement on that surface, and on the seal. it sticks great. it actually sticks pretty good with just contact cement on the wood and the adhesive that comes already on the seal, but i went for the overkill. i attached the seal to the wood strip before i attached it to the door frame. i had the closer installed already and had wood blocks set up to keep it closed to the point i wanted it, then i snugged up the wood strip with seal up to the door all the way around. on hanging the door, i just put the door in place and framed around it. i actually made it a little tight and had to trim the door a little. then i chiseled out the recesses for the hinges by hand, both on the door and the frame. i actually went too deep and then i shimmed the hinges with some cardboard to get it less deep. but all things considered the door hanging was not that bad. i'm glad that i didn't use a pre-hung, and i'm going to do the inner leaf the same way.

Dan, Don't put the wires through the Gypsum. Put the conduit pipe through the gypsum. You'll have to get conduit pipe big enough so the end of the largest cable will fit. Pull the cables through conduit after your done building. I've got through more thought on mine. I bought some 1" and 1 1/2" 90 degree turning conduit. I'm going to make my run turn 90 one way, turn 90 another way, go straight a ways and then turn 90 into the other wall. I'm not going to exceed a 5' or 6' run. I'm taking the 1" and 1 1/2" conduit back. I think I'm going to go with 3" or bigger. Big whole in the wall to deal with. At least it will be decoupled by leaving a 1/4" gap in the run somewheres. Aaron described that too somewheres. On the electric thread Aaron made a sticky with a bunch of links for parts. Included is some places that make custom connector panels. I don't know if I'm going to bother with a connector panel yet BUT I do know on the family room side of my hole in the wall it will either have to look nice or be out of sight. Also still up for debate is how to plug either end of the conduit once cables are installed. I do recall reading on the forum stuffing fiberglass or a towel or something into the hole around wires... also a pretty box with a custom connector panel would be nice... but USB has a 15' max run and some devices don't work USB extension. Only use High Grade SVGA cable or you'll get ghosting on the monitor screen. PS/2 (mouse and keyboard) can run 25'. Firewire I think is 10' or 12'... can't remember. Both Firewire and USB booster boxes are available but add to project cost, and I'd rather keep to manufacture if original device suggestions. Marc

Guys, be aware that a rigid conduit firmly connected to both leaves will probably form a flanking path. :? You may need to do some kind of rubber coupling in the conduit between the leaves to break the firm connection. Either that or check out the Mason Industries site for isolation collars of some sort. --Keith :mrgreen:

Keith, The suggestion somewhere on this Forum that I think Aaron made is the leave a 1/4" break in the conduit run to avoid flanking. Conduit is actually in two parts, one attached to inner wall and the other part attached to the outer wall. "Outer" and "inner" part of conduit is lined up exact at the break. The only flanking would happen through the cables which will be impossible to avoid. Only thing to remember when fishing the cables through is there is a break somewhere that the cables may snag on. it depends on how much isolation you have as to the amount that a small hole or physical connection between layers will reduce it. if you have only 29db of isolation then flanking or small holes probably isn't your main concern. if you have 60db of isolation, then flanking and air leaks (even small ones) will be a bigger problem. and yes, low frequencies will pass through very small holes... :wink:
