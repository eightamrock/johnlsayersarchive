thanks keith! here are a couple of pictures from about a week ago, just before the baby was born. never got a chance to post them. as you can see, almost all of the building paper has been closed up again. beneath the closed up, old paper is a layer of new paper. the old paper was glued down with a liquid nails type product. i decided not to continue using the roofing cement to seal any of the paper edges, i just used acoustic caulk which hopefully will do the job. the vapors from the roof cement i used are extremely noxious and also carcinogenic, and after i realized how much smell got into the house i decided it wasn't worth the health risks. on the roof, where this stuff is meant to be used, that is not much of an issue. you also can see in the first photo a small triangle at the bottom has been filled with mortar, rather than wallboard. in another, larger triangle i used a "buttered" piece of drywall as an experiment. i may use mortar to fill other odd gaps that crop up, like the unevenness that's sure to occur between the layers of sheetrock and the diagonal brace. it's dense and, unlike wallboard, can be easily be applied to fairly large gaps in a custom thickness. it's also much cheaper than drywall mud. also, my garage is now full of insulation. i don't really need it yet, but a couple of fellow forum-ites wanted to get rid of it cheap and i happily obliged them! :) d

time for some caulk talk :) by the way i did have a chance to try out the USG vs. the Miracle acoustic sealant. the usg seems to have more mass, although i haven't actually checked that. it's more viscous and a bit harder to squeeze out. these two properties make it seem "better" than the Miracle, which seems less robust. knightfly1 October 2005#172
IF you envision having a LOT of ripping to do with large panels, I'd consider seeing if that saw will accept a table extension wide enough to allow 24" or more between the edge of the table and the blade; if so, you can then clamp a 4' length of straight 2x4 parallel to the blade to use as a rip guide. If not, you may also be able to MAKE an extension table from a piece of OSB and some 2x framing - although this can rapidly become a serious project in its own right. For skil saw users, there are combination clamp/edge guides available up to 8' long; I have the 3' and 4' versions of this, two each. They really come in handy. you just measure from the nearest and furthest tooth of your blade to the edge of the saw's shoe, and offset your measurements by that amount when you clamp the edge guide. http://woodbutcher.net/images/normstools/tru-grip.htm http://www.internationaltool.com/trugrip.htm Or, you can just cover up the wavy edges with caulk and cleats :wink:

ok, here's a quickie ... somewhat interesting. i've been pluggin away at that same wall, putting up an OSB panel here and there, and caulking. but i haven't had much time. i did, however start in on that electrical box problem area. i debated what to do here, there is a gas pipe, the main electrical conduits, etc. was i going to fill this in with mortar, OSB and drywall? No way. Forget it. So I decided to try that foam filler. This is a closed cell urethane that is very lightweight, so it doesn't have any isolating ability, and since it is closed cell it doesn't offer any dampening ability either. What it DOES do (i hope) is prevent a resonance in the space, in other words prevent a triple leaf. It seemed to work pretty well. I was afraid of it at first but actually it turned out to be fun to work with. It expands very rapidly at first and then seems to stop but in reality it keeps expanding for maybe a half hour before it dries. It also fills up more space that you would expect. It is expensive, maybe about 4 bucks a can or maybe more, but i think i can finish this problem area with 3 cans. then i'll close the space up with OSB and then go with mulitple layers of drywall. the one problem is that this will drastically affect my airgap, but maybe i'll throw some extra mass in there and maybe it won't be too big a deal. hopefully there aren't any safety issues with this. i'm pretty sure this stuff doesn't conduct electricity. it is a bit flammable but not too bad (it's flame retardant), and there won't be any air available for it to burn so it shouldn't be a problem either. if i have to remove the foam it actually can be chiseled away fairly easily. one thing DO NOT get this stuff on your hands, it does not come off. it will "wear off" in a couple days though. any comments?
