Let's talk about backing rod, shall we? I really wonder how many people use backing rod, because I hardly ever see people talk about using it or where to get a good deal on it. Maybe people just don't understand how awesome backing rod is. First of all backing rod is a good idea from a "doing things right" perspective. As you know from my previous posts today this is an argument that doesnt' fly to well month six into the project. But let me mention it anyway. The idea on caulk is that it should be stretching between the two sides of your joint, not that it should fill the joint. If it fills the joint it can't stretch as easily, because it might be stuck to the back of the joint. If it is only stuck to the sides, it is more free to stretch and not break. Backing rod does stick to the caulk but it doesn't stick to the back of the joint, resulting in a much stretchier joint. With me so far? See here: http://www.loghelp.com/info/brodtips.html Man, this is even boring me. Sorry, but i must ... keep ... typing ... Here's a really compelling reason to use the rod. It saves caulk. It saves A LOT of caulk. Which saves money and time, and sore hands. Ok, it takes time to put the backer rod in. But not that much if you have the right tool. Do you really want to fill up that 3/8 by 5/8 inch gap with precious caulk? NO! Put in some 3/8 backing rod and save tons of caulk. Sometimes your gap could be even bigger than 3/8, you might need to double up the rod. Think how much caulk you'd be wasting!! Tragic. 3/8 is bigger than you will usually need in my experience. That is why I got a big spool of 1/4 inch that you see below. That is my main rod. But 3/8 is good to have too. I just ran out of the 3/8 i bought from home depot, and am about to order about 500 feet from the same people i got the 1/4 from these guys: http://www.loghelp.com/backerod.html They are way cheaper than HD if you want any kind of quantity, I think I paid about $40 for 1000 feet of 1/4 you see here. HD doesn't have 1/4, but 30 feet of 3/8 costs around $4 there, and this log cabin place sells 100 feet of 3/8 for $5. They actually have sizes up to 4 inches if you can figure out a use for that. Anyway, if you use backer rod you will use less caulk and get a more credible fill more quickly and easily. Also the nice thing about it is that it gets stuffed in there pretty good and will provide some sound sealing in and of itself, as a backup. But the bottom line is ... I like using backing rod, and yet I am lazy and cheap. Hmmm. How can this be? Backing rod costs money and effort to obtain, and takes time to use. And yet, and yet ... I am still using it. That just about says it all. Mmmmmm ... backing rod .....
