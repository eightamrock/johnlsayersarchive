Stan, in general I used backer rod. In some cases the fit was too tight, so I couldn't even fit in 1/4 inch rod. But in most cases i had a gap of at least 3/8 inch, or even more. That's what prompted me to order some fatter backing rod today. I found i needed some 1/2 and 5/8, which i had some from Home depot but it's too expensive there. I do believe that the seal will hold up much better over time with backer rod. also, without the rod you will end up using much MUCH more caulk. i realize that you and others may be thinking of the mass differential between caulk and backer rod (which has almost no mass by comparison) ... but i think in this case the integrity of the seal is much more important than the mass. it's my view that the overall, average mass of your construction, plus the airtightness, that determines the TL. not the mass (or density) of any one small area. the backer rod helps the seal to stretch and move without breaking. i will try to get some pictures of my seams up, you will laugh when you see them.

1/2" OSB secured with cleats, three layers of 1/2" drywall secured to the OSB between the cleats, then one layer of 5/8" drywall covering the cleats with screws driven at a 45 degree angle into the cleats and studs. There's Green Glue behind the final layer of drywall applied at about 75% the recommended amount. All of my studs were warped, twisted, or out of plumb. :evil: My gaps ranged from wedged tightly (no gap at all) to 1/2" and perhaps even 5/8" in some spots, even along the same stud. :( I'm hoping the pattern of the OSB (one width), 3 layers of 1/2" drywall (all the same width, slightly narrower to accommodate the cleats), and then the width of the 5/8" drywall over the top (same width as OSB) will make the sealant somewhat redundant. My goal was part seal, part make-up for lack of mass. All of this is covered in detail in my marathon project thread. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward6 March 2007#417
This is a really interesting point of discussion... It's not so much a "to use or not to use backer rod," but rather, "to leave or not to leave a gap that would then require backer rod." It seems to me that a tight-fitting piece of beef-up drywall against a stud or a joist with a bead of acoustic sealant where the two meet would create a two-sided bond -- definitely not the dreaded three-sided bond that backer rod is supposed to help be maintained over time. While I'm inclined to think that's acceptable, and perhaps even superior to a joint that requires backer rod, I'm left wondering if there may be a risk of further warpage or twisting, or even seasonal swelling, of the framing members that would cause the edges of the gypsum strips to be compressed, or worse, parts of them to buckle, under the lateral forces. If that were to happen, then I would think the seal would be the first to go. That does double or even triple for an earthquake event -- even a minor one that causes no noticable structural damage anywhere. This is all just theoretical, just as so many other ccpncepts described here... I'm not claiming expertise on this, but I do want to at least provide food for thought and/or further discussion. It makes some sense having this all taking place in The Backer Rod Czar's thread... ;-) ...But I may exercise my awesome moderator powers at some point by moving this series of posts to the aforementioned thread, or perhaps splitting it off into a new thread entirely... Meanwhile, assuming Dan will indulge us, carry on!!! :-D --Keith :mrgreen:

n8nod9it, in my small experience you need to cut drywall about 1/2 inch shorter than your gap, so you have 1/4 inch gap all around. this is becuase if you cut it exact, you will (as you found out) have a tough time fitting the piece in. your stud bays are not going to be perfectly square of course. but, i think the way you did it is probably fine, although a lot more work. but i will say that in my mind, i have been thinking that the areas where i cannot fit backer rod, i was a little disappointed. my feeling is that the caulk will not penetrate a small crack very well, so you will get an apparent seal on the surface that may not be that great. keep in mind, soundproofing is done all the time in the real world by real contractors under real world conditions. i guarantee you they are not micro-fitting each piece of drywall, they are leaving a gap and then filling it with backer rod. now, maybe the way you did it is better, but i'm perfectly satisfied with following industry practice. in general, standard practices i believe have evolved to provide the best effectiveness at the lowest possible cost (most of that cost being TIME, not materials). personally i'm trying to get my project done this century :) all that being said, i get really annoyed when my gaps are bigger than 3/8 inch. but sometimes it just happens. the good news is since i'm doing two layers, hopefully i won't have two layers with huge gaps right in line with each other.
