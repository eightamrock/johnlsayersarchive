knightfly3 February 2006#240
IF this is a typical segmented door, there is a helical spring wound around a heavy shaft at the top of the door - this spring is preloaded by the installer so that the (effective) weight of the door when closed, is close to zero pounds. This is done by adjusting a heavy collar at one end of the spring, literally "winding up" the door spring til it balances the door weight. The collar typically has at least 4 holes in it, and the installer uses two steel rods inserted in these holes alternately so he can increase the spring tension by 1/4 turns. Once the required "preload" has been reached, there are LARGE set screws which are tightened on the shaft to maintain this tension. This tension has another name - stored energy. Unleashing this energy without proper tools and knowledge can KILL you, or at least make it difficult to play any instrument that requires both hands... :cry: I'm aware of how these work, and I STILL called a door guy when one of my springs broke a few years back. He made it look easy, but IT'S NOT. One slip can mean broken bones or WORSE. BTW, it's a good thing I had a couple of pry bars on MY side of the door when the spring went; mine's heavy wood, and I doubt I could have lifted it without. Glad your door was the only thing dented, and thanks for the reminder. I'd bet that somewhere near HALF the membership is working on garage re-do's, so this is really important to know... Steve

Dan Fitzpatrick wrote:
i bought the cheapest powder-actuated hammer tool HD carries, to attach the walls to the concrete floor. anybody have any expereince with these? can it really pound a nail into concrete? i can't wait to see that.
Are you talking about the ones that use .22 caliber blanks to shoot in the nails? I'd have two words of advice: 1) Wear ear protection - They are LOUD. Especially if you are in a confined space like a garage. 2) Wear foot (and calf, arm, leg, eye,...) protection. To be honest with you, if you haven't used it yet, I'd recomend returning it now and using anchor bolts instead. You only need a hammer drill (not strictly necessary but goes a little faster), masonary bit, and a wrench. That is how I put up all of my walls. I was advised against using the "concrete bullets" (I was going to rent a gun instead of buying one), because depending on the age and composition of the concrete you are shooting into, you can blow a nail into it and have it completely shatter or create cracks from the point of impact to several feet away. Using the anchor bolts, you drill a hole through your bottom plate into the concrete, then just lightly hammer in the anchor bolt with a washer and nut on the end (both supplied in the box). Then you tighten the nut with a wrench and it locks everything down. One big advantage (that I hope to never have to use) is if you need/want to take the wall out, you only have to loosen the nut and slide the bottom plate off. You then either cut off or pull out the bolt and you're left with just a hole to fill. Just my 2 cents worth. len
