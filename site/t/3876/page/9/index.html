len-morgan16 January 2006#212
Dan, If you've still got a lot of dry wall cutting to go, you might think about doing what I (and my helper) did after both of our arms got too tired to even cut through the first layer of paper. I bought a long straight edge (I think Kieth has one of these for cutting OSB). It's about 9 or 10' long and has clamps on each end that are slideable. We used a Dremel tool with a dry wall bit (normally used for cutting out vent and door openings (no comments, Kieth!!! :-) ). The round "ring" around the base of the tool is exactly 1" from the bit edge so we marked our pieces and "extra" inch wide, clamped the straight edge done, then slid the Dremel down the entire length of a sheet of DW in about 15 seconds. No more strained muscles (or cut thighs, fingers, etc [it was the "etc" that hurt the most! I now wear a cup when drywalling]). The bits last a long time if you don't try and pull/push too hard on them (I think we used two bits over the course of 170 sheets of drywall (and the second one is still in pretty good shape). Just a little something I thought I'd pass on. len

knightfly26 January 2006#221
Dan, in framing you should NEVER rely on just nails to support anything - you definitely want solid blocking UNDER your joists. You might be able to use hangers, but I've never liked the fact that you have to use those special, fat short nails with them. They tend to split the wood if it's very dry in my experience. Simple test for framing - theoretically remove the nails - would it fall down even if there's no wind, and nothing bumps it? If so, use blocking... Steve
