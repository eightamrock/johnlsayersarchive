here is a view of the inside of a silencer, with some rockwool in. the silencer was built taking advantage of the space between the joists, so the joists are the "walls" of the thing. then i put down osb on the bottom and filled the thing with rockwool until i had 3 inches of clear space left. i figured out how much space to leave to be equivalent to the 8 inch round ductwork i'm using (and fan). then i coverd it with the polyester. i though i had a picture of it covered but can't find it. another thing i was too lazy to photograph. anyway what i did was carefully lay down the polyester that was cut to fit with generous amount left to staple to the sides. then i cut with my table saw some very thin wood that i nailed on top of the fabric to make it really nice and sealed (on top of the staples). maybe unnecessary but i liked the way the fabric tightened up when i pressed the wood down. you could bounce a quarter on it. so here are some pictures of the silencers. one shows the rockwool in the silencer. also shown is the intake silencer, which has no fan between itself and the roof, and the outtake silencer, which has the fan. the top of the silencer has 1/2 inch osb and then a layer of 5/8 drywall. of course my pictures are out of order because i'm too lame to remember how to do it right. but check out that fan, all wired in and everything. that is the thing that really inspired me to get on here and show off my work. the fan install is cool :) also here's a view of the inside of a silencer

Dan Fitzpatrick23 February 2007#395
ok, here's something that might really be worth something to someone. if you are "beefing up" between your ceiling joists and you need something to help you hold up one or both ends of the drywall until you screw it in, you can use a couple of these things available at your local hardware store. this is a ceiling fan or heavy light fixture anchor you use in your already finished ceiling. i've taken off the junction box. the idea is that you can cut a small hole in your ceiling, put in this expanding rod that will solidly anchor between your joists, and hold up your fan or heavy fixture. so, it works well for holding up one or both ends of a piece of sheet rock being used for beef up. when my dad and i did the ceiling, we used these even though there were two of us. i think using these i can do some beef by myself too, although i haven't gotten there yet.
