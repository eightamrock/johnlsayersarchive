well time for my monthly update. I have to admit I've been a little hesitant to post lately because I'm a little embarassed about my shoddy construction methods and pretty crappy job i'm doing. BUT, in the interest of presenting concrete examples of actual studio construction, no matter how crappy, i'll continue. PLEASE remember, you do not necessarily want to do what i am doing. okay then. how to deal with the corner where the outer leaf walls and ceiling meet had been a huge conundrum, and i finally solved it. i wish i had figured this out before i had put up the wall blocking my garage door, since it would have been much easier to do things in a different order. obviously, the fact is that there is more than a little triple leafing going on here. i've got two leafs of ceiling, plus the roof itself becomes a third leaf. we knew that already. so it should not be surprising that we actually have a little more triple leafing going on where the roof meets the walls. i decided to make the connection between the outer leaf ceiling and wall the same way i did the corner. i framed this out on one end of the garage. in retrospect, maybe i could have done it differently, if i had decided to beef up part of the roof that runs between the outer leaf ceiling and the outer wall, then i could have minimized this third leaf area. when you add up the square footage it's a lot of area. but, i'm not too excited about the idea of beefing up the roof. the actual thing that is keeping me dry. in a few years i'll have to have a new roof put on, and maybe something could happen in this process that screws my beef up. anyway, i've done the framing and i think i'm going to go ahead with the gypsum. maybe i will do the other end of the garage differently. in any event, i'm going to insulate the area between the second and third leaves the help dampen down any resonances that might occur. the good news is i had my dad here over the weekend to position and tack in all of my outer leaf ceiling joists. you may remember that we put them up there weeks ago (months ago?) and they have just been sitting there. NOT a good idea, as a few of them got severely warped in the intervening time. oh well, i'm going to brace them so they should work ok. so remember, try to use the wood you buy soon, or at least, don't leave it someplace where it will be baked, like in a garage attic space. pix of the joists to come. here are some pix of the corner and third leaf framing i spoke of. o yeah there is one more good news to report. i have decided to spend some of my free time actually doing music instead of just working on the studio all the time, and so that has been good. kind of helps keep me motivated to complete thsi project. of course my stuff is mostly terrible (since i don't play any instruments or sing, and know very little about writing music) but i'm having fun anyway. oh yeah, and metal angle brackets rule. remember to keep plenty of them on hand and lots of little nails for them

a few updates. have been working steadily toward finishing the outer leaf ceiling structure. am within striking distance of sheetrocking the outer leaf, but am not sure yet whether i need to put ventilation up above the c ceiling yet or not. i probably will, if it is practical. it's just wasted space otherwise. but it may be difficult or may compromise the airgap, so still pondering. i had this 1 1/2 inch conduit supplying power to the house, that was in my way ... in the way of the new joists. rather than notch the new joists and weaken them, i raised the two problem joists 1 1/2 inches and added 1 1/2 inches of wood on the bottom of them, using 3 1/2 inch screws every 6-8 inches plus liquid nails to attach the new material. actually i had to add material to just about all of the new joists, in varying thicknesses, because the old joists were so uneven. but that's finished now so no worries.

Dan Fitzpatrick7 June 2006#333
by the way i used the heavy duty joist hangers here, just because why not use the heavy duty right? they are just a little more expensive. but WHOA, did you realize that each hangar required 20 nails, it took me like 3 different after-work sessions to get those nails in. what a pain. yes i know this contruction looks sketchy, but keep in mind there is a 2x10 immediately behind the truss that the nails also go into. so i feel pretty good about it being solid. also you can see i ended up jamming a shim into the hanger on the right ... if you are doing something like this where the joist is already semi supported without the hangar it might be a good idea to solidly support the hangar with a 2x4 or something, then let the joist rest on it, so that there is a solid connection between the joist and the hangar bottom. normally this would not be a problem but since my joists rested on top of existing joists there was no real weight on the hangar when i put it in place. don't know if that makes sense or not.

Dan Fitzpatrick7 June 2006#334
a couple more items. one, i like these "quick change" drill bits, i just learned about them recently and they are pretty cool, check it out if you don't know about them. but what has really impressed me is the difference in nails you can buy. this is obvious to anyone who knows construction, but to me, even though i'm reasonably handy i never realized how different the different types of nails are. you want to use "common" for joist hangars, for example, not "box" ... the common are much thicker in diameter and so are stronger. but what i have found is not only are the common stronger but they are just much easier to use, since they rarely bend on you. the "box" will bend in a heartbeat if you are the least bit off axis. but the common you can practically hit at a 45 degree angle and they will still go in. that is pretty awesome. i have a few box nails around but i just won't use them anymore because i am just so used to the non-bending indestructibility of the common. also i think i am ready to graduate from the 19 oz hammer to something more robust. but i am too cheap to go buy one ... but it is funny to go back to the "normal" hammer, with its pathetic smooth face, and try to hammer a nail with it. the waffle head rules. except i did hit my thumb with it finally, that kind of sucks. stupid 1 1/2 inch nails ... pic: box vs. common
