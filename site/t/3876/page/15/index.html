oh you guys are too funny. on another note, i had finished up my ceiling framing yesterday, or so i thought. i put up my first piece of gypsum on the ceiling, and finally the realization came over me that i had more work to do. being mildly brain-damaged that i am, due to improper ventilation most likely, i am only just beginning to realize that gypsum needs to be attached to solid wood AROUND ITS ENTIRE PERIMETER. you really can't just edges floating in space and then try to caulk between those edges and some adjacent member. i wish i had taken a picture of this, but if you look at my first sheetrock layer on the ceiling you can see right through up to the roof on the sides. i had been puzzling over how to fill this gap, and thought i could probably get away with careful backing rod and caulking. then the second layer would be easy since the gap would be against "solid" backing material. but it dawned on me finally that this is not good soundproofing procedure. so what i need to do is probably cut a million little 2x4 cleats and put them along the tops of my 2x6 (old joists) to provide backing for the outer leaf gyspum. so that will take another bunch of hours :evil: maybe this early sketch will show what i mean (i'm not planning to add as many layers of gypsum at this point) ... if you can imagine what this sketch looks like along the z-axis (going INTO the sketch) you can see that there is no way to attach the LONG edges of the drywall to the 2x6s.

a little bit of progress. decided the cheapest and most reasonable solution to my drywall backing problem was to get some OSB sheets and rip them into about 6 inch strips, then cut them to size. so that meant removing the seats from the minivan, which meant a trip to the dump since i told myself, the next time i take the seats out to get 4x8 sheets i'm going to the dump. so i got my 4x8 sheets and got enough ripped to do the backing. i removed the one narrow bit of inadequately backed drywall beef, did the backing on it (extra complicated near the wall since the blocking was in the way). and put the drywall back in place. the good news is that doing the backing is not as bad as i had thought, i can use nails and i have decent room to swing the hammer for the most part. and the 6 inch strips of OSB are JUST narrow enough to be cut by my chopsaw (phew -- should have checked that first). one thing that was cool was i finally got smart enough to get a holster for my cordless drill. this, after leaving it on the top of my ladder, moving the ladder and dropping the drill on the floor, breaking a brand new bit-holder thingy. so that has made me more efficient. and then i found some more things to put screws and nails in so i don't have to change the contents of my measly two pouches in my tool belt as much. the other thing was that i think i have figured out a way to put up the drywall beef on my own, which is to use nails to hold it up, which was suggested sort of in Rod's book. But i just used the idea to temporarily hold the drywall up so i can screw it in. you can nail nails in fairly lightly, slide the drywall in on top of them, and it will hold well enough, and then you can pull the nails out by hand with a bit of wiggling. of course, when i get to the inner leaf ceiling, i'll be borrowing Keith's, i mean stick's, drywall lifter :lol: :lol: (pretty please?)

I just noticed that i had passed the one year anniversary of starting the physical work on the studio (jun 14). overall i guess i'm pleased with the amount of progress. one regret is i haven't kept track of how much i've spent. it would have been fairly easy to keep a jar for receipts for all the HD and other receipts ... but then again maybe it's better not to know. i may go back thru my past year credit card invoices and add up the HD amounts, most of that will be studio related. what would have been even cooler would have been to actually scan or photograph all the receipts and post them here. BUT ... one only has so much energy. anyway, on to this week's progress. not as much as Sharward has done, i'm afraid to say, but progress nonetheless. i began on the dreaded ventilation. as i go through this project i find that there are particular issues of the project that worry me and bug me, and i always want to start on them so i can get a handle on it and it will stop bugging me. the ventilation is like this. so anyway after looking at HD's selection of ductwork parts and seeing how expensive they are, i've adopted a plan of building most of the ductwork out of wood, which has the added advantage of much larger cross section, and better sound isolation and damping potential than round metal duct. so yesterday i built the better part of one duct, using the new 2x8 joists as sides of the duct and using OSB for the bottom. i will layer a few inches of rockwool on the bottom of the duct to absorb sound. as long as i have at least 3.5 inches of open space in the duct i should be equal in cross section to an 8 inch round duct. so that should work. one end of the duct will have an 8" round adapter coming out from it with metal duct going straight up out my roof. the other end will turn toward the corner of the room, simultaneously dropping down as it turns to inhabit the space between the "old" 2x6 joists. this duct will have less depth, but wider, 24 inches wide so the cross sectional area is still huge, still around double that of an 8 inch duct. i could probably throw some rockwool in here just for fun since i have the room. i'm sure this is hard to follow but the most important thing to know i guess is that part of this duct is primarily outside the outer leaf (the bottom of the duct IS the outer leaf), while part of the duct is inside the outer leaf (the top of the duct IS the outer leaf. neither of these parts will touch the inner leaf, which will receive the end of the duct via a short bit of flexible duct. also i started demoing the area in the attic adjacent to my studio which will be home to the fantech fan. it needs to be in this spot so it remains accessible from outside the studio. one more thing, if anyone is wondering whether to buy a table saw, i have really found it useful, here's a picture of my setup, i think i would be having a hard time without it. it rocks. just make sure to use a push stick, i made one that is about 3 feet long :) ... my fingers never get anywhere near the blade. just the amount of money saved by ripping your own smaller pieces out of larger pieces is huge. like did you ever notice a 1x4 costs the same or more than a 2x4, even though it is half as much wood.
