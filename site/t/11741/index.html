Soundman202017 March 2009#7
Stuart, I undestand soffits are not part of the inner leaf, but how come in bassman's build, it appears as if he used drywall on the soffit wall?
Well, you need mass on your soffit panel. Lots of it. Drywall is reasonably good as mass(!! :) ) MDF, HDF, concrete, steel plate, lead sheet, etc. are also good for soffit panels. The denser the better. The soffit panel is doing its best to pretend that it is an infinite baffle, so the more mass you can put into it, the better. Occasionally you see folks here proudly show of their soffit walls that are just speakers stuck in the middle of a huge pink fluffy bass trap, with only acoustic cloth over it: that won't work! I have no idea what it will do, but I do know that it most certainly will NOT be an infinite baffle, which is the entire purpose of a soffit. So why is it not a third leaf? Personally, I think it is! I would imagine that, to a certain extent a soffit actually IS a third leaf, just like a panel absorber is a third leaf, to a certain extent. But consider the following: 1. It does not fill up the entire wall: Worst case, it only covers maybe a quarter of the wall, left to right, and maybe half or so vertically. Maybe more. But never the entire wall. 2. It is not over a sealed cavity. You don't want your soffit to be sealed! Yeah, it certainly is true that you can still have a third leaf without it being sealed, but see (1) above: The soffit panel isn't very big compared to the entire wall, so the effect is not that great Theoretically, if you hold a large book (such as an atlas) close to the wall, you create a third leaf, but obviously the effect is minimal. (OK, soffits are bigger than atlases, but you get the point.) 3. The soffit is not in front of the speaker: it IS the speaker! I'm still trying to think this one through, but it seems to me that waves on the soffit panel are traveling across its surface, spreading out like ripples on a pond, and are not hitting it head-on, as they would if the same panel were at the back of the room. That must make a difference. Of course, it still gets hit with sound coming back from other places in the room, but that will hopefully already be several dB down, and it never gets hit with direct sound from the speaker. Like I say, I'm trying to check this and see if I'm on the right track here, but I'm not having much success. It just seems logical. But take that with a pinch of salt: Lots of stuff that seems logical in acoustics, actually isn't logical at all! :) I'm hoping one of the acoustic experts here can either confirm this, or shoot me down in glorious technicolor flames, and make me feel like a dunce.... 4. Other minor stuff: the soffit is angled steeply (at least 30 degrees, maybe more) to the second leaf, not parallel to it, so I reckon that must have some effect too. Maybe you could say that it will be a "broadband" third leaf, if there is such a thing! Also, it is massive, and held rigidly in place. So my take is that the third-leaf effect is going to be small, at best, and that the big advantages to be gained by soffit mounting strongly outweigh the third-leaf disadvantages. Anyway, that's my line of reasoning: It actually is a third leaf, but the effect is minimal. Of course, I might be totally wrong on that! It wouldn't be the first time.... - Stuart -
