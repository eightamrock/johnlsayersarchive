Hi DLee, and welcome!
I have a preliminary design I would like looked over for mistakes and criticism.
I prefer that first design, since the second one kills the symmetry in your room. Symmetry is very important, so you don't want to lose that. Have you considered other layouts? It seems to me that there is a lot of wasted space in that "airlock" between the living room door and the two rooms. Maybe if you re-arrange things, you could use that space for a larger booth, a larger CR, or maybe even a second booth, or storage.... Is it possible to move that living room door, or is that pretty much fixed? Regarding the booth: You don't need to angle the left wall, since the right wall is already angled (don't worry about symmetry in there: symmetry is not important for the booth, but it is fundamental for the CR). Your booth would be a bit bigger if you didn't angle that wall. You could maybe angle the front wall of your booth a bit (the one with the door in it), which would make your booth even bigger, and would help control flutter between front and back walls, as they are parallel at present. Apart from that, I think your basic layout is fine! My only suggestion would be to do the design in SketchUp and post it here, so that others can maybe play around with it a bit. - Stuart -
xSpace28 May 2009#5
As an aside, for double framed walls, the air is often thought of as sealed. I realize that yours is, so long as the main entry door is shut. What kind of access/traffic does that MainDoor get, is what I would be thinking. If, in the middle of tracking, your spring is modulated by the release of the compressed air just from simply opening this door, it changes the effective ability of your isolation, does it not? Other things that happen is the mass-spring-mass of a double wall is not where your control room wall and booth wall are out in the "main entry" way. These will either be open and exposed insulation or you will have already reduced your isolation from installing sheetrock directly to these areas. Essentially you will undo all your good work of decoupling by coupling this area back together. Still, if you have thought this area through then you are one step ahead of the game and already aware of the potential weakening and are devising remedies of which several exist. In any event, if this were my build, I would have to go ahead and do the interior wall all the way around the perimeter, not just part of the way. P.S. Which introduces the ceiling and how this will add to the potential reduction in isolation.
