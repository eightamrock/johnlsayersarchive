John Sayers8 September 2006#7
APR wrote:
:) :) :) Are the the wooden slots ment to act as a slot resonator, and do they need to be tuned specially for the room?
Yes - you tune them to the low mid frquencies around 250 - 350 hz.
By constructing the wall with only one layer of gypsum, do you lose the mass-air cavity-mass benefit? Or is it not significant enough to worry about?
You retain the mass - air - mass, it's just a narrower airgap.
Your point on using the high ceiling as to my benefit on bass trapping has been taken in consideration on the new plan for the studio ( see .skp file) I have two possibilities for the ceiling: Sloping or even. I'm wondering is the sloping ceiling would be worth the construction trouble so that the ceiling basstrap would get a wider band of absorbion??
Yes - aim for the max absorption in the upper corners, i.e above the door to the studio and above the door into the control room.
Also I was left wondering what is the reason why my room is too short to support diffusion. Doesn't diffusers work on all ranges?
The aim of a mix room is to have a clean signal at the mix position. The sound from the speakers that passes you will reflect off the diffuser and come back at you. In your case it's only 2.4m (8ft) from the mix position to the rear wall so the sound will travel 4.8m (16ft) before you hear it again from behind 16msec later. The human ear can't determine the source until the two sounds are greater than 20msec of each other so in your case the two sounds are merged and create aural confusion. If you check out the control rooms with rear diffusers you will see that they are big rooms with more tha 10' (3m) from the console to the rear wall.
