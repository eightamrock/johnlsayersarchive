So I'm figuring at least 1/2" glass for one side... maybe more? I couldn't get a solid density spec for the Celotex. That would mean I need 3/4" of glass on the other side, correct? It seems high, but then again I've never done this before.
Glass is roughly 3 time heavier than GB (Gypsum Board), which is about 700kg/m3. So glass has a mass of about 2000-2500 kg/m3. 3 times heavier means 3 times less thickness needed (theoretically) to match GB. So you should need, atleast, 2x15mm/3=10mm of glass. (5/8" is about 15mm). Let's make it a 11mm thick glasspane, shall we.Depending on your wall structure you need 1 or 2 panes of glass. A pane of glass should be (advise) laminated; laminated glass are 2 pieces of floatglass pressed against each other with a PVB layer in between. The layer of PVB will help dampening. When each sheet of glass will be different in thickness you'll "mask" the coincident effect of the other layer. Glass, or any other material, has a so called critical frequency where waves penetrate much easier. This frq is easy calculated with this formula: f(Hz)=12.000/d(mm) f=frequency d=diameter (in mm) The frq is your critical frq. a 11m will pass have a cr.frq of about 1100Hz. (roughly). Add another piece of glas with a thickness of, say, 13mm, and you'll end up with a cf @ 900Hz. Therefor it's better to have a slighly thicker glass than you would calculate (the CFs are the weakpoints, better add some more mass to compensate a bit) Take that in consideration and take your plans to a local glass dealer. While your there, ask for glazing tape (a rubber band a few mm thick, costs you only a few bucks), setting blocks (EPM or any other rubber). Follow Rod's advice (read the chapter again) and you'll be in the save zone!
