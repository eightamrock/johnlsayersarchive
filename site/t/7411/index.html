knightfly12 November 2006#4
Looks like you're on the right track - although you CAN soffit your mackies, just use John's vented soffit plan http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=66 Scroll down a ways on the first page Sloping your ceiling is a good idea if you have the headroom, otherwise a 2" or thicker MW or rigid fiberglass cloud, centered between speakers and mix position, and hung (without backing) a few inches off the ceiling will take care of early reflections from the ceiling. IF you slope, you'll want 12 degrees - this is roughly 2 feet in 10, and you'd want this back to at least a couple feet PAST the mix position. You will likely STILL need a "cloud", but the slope will kill any flutter and the cloud could probably be smaller this way. IF you soffit, you'll want to keep the flat surfaces around your speakers as large as practical to avoid "horn loading" which would narrow your sweet spot somewhat - see this for more http://www.quarter-wave.com/General/BSC_Sizing.pdf page 3 has the formula you want to use to see optimum baffle sizing - don't be alarmed if you can't get there, this is mainly to illustrate why NOT to build your extended baffles only a foot wide if there's 2 or 3 feet each side you COULD have used :? This http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2441 can help you figure out good locations/widths, etc, for speakers and your head - when using soffits, you can ignore the bevels created by soffits and consider your woofer center-to-center distance the same as if the speakers were free-standing - you'll still want to maintain close to an equilateral triangle between head and speakers, with your head just forward of the point of the triangle - and you can adjust speaker positioning along the baffle until you get the speakers NOT in a 1/3 2/3 location (see the link above for why) Hopefully this will cause you to think, plan, and ask MORE questions :wink: ... Yes, pretty much true - moreso for bass frequencies. Caulking fixes small holes, mortar/drywall mud/fixall fixes medium holes, and drywall/mdf/lumber fixes larger ones. If you use a hollow core door, you may as well leave it OPEN. knightfly18 November 2006#9
Either the beefed hollow core or a full slab (not raised panel) solid core will give similar results, in this case I'd go with the easier of the two. Understand, when I say "solid core" I mean an exterior, 1-3/4" thick, solid slab door of MDF with veneer skin - essentially, a constant-thickness, nearly 2" thick slab of wood/wood products, typically weighing around 125-140 pounds for a 36" wide (3/0) door WITHOUT the frame. Without a correct seal kit, it won't make much difference WHAT door you use. Circuit breaker - IIRC, any covered access door needs to be clearly labeled to be code-legal (wrong computer, no codes installed on this one, sorry) Acoustically, sure - just build the inside with a constant depth for that section so there's air space behind it...
