guitardad729 April 2007#4
1st if you haven't already been check out Johns STC Chart Note: 8" Concrete Block, hollow core, no wall finish = STC of 45 by itself. Standard house brick unrendered. = STC 39, 45 if you render/stucco Brick Veneer construction with a layer of 16mm (5/8") plasterboard. That's one layer of 5/8" gypsum board attached to 2x4 frame, with insulation) = STC 56. All of the above will also have different TL numbers not necessarily corresponding to the difference in STC. Presuming by "cavity brick" you are referring to standard 8" x 8" x 16" (well really 7.5x7.5x15.5") concrete block or commonly referred to as cinder block (CB), I would say NO for that option, because of lower STC and the amount of real estate the CBs take up especially if you were to consider adding a 2x4 wall for inner frame. As Sharward noted hollow CB are (now, may always have been) considered 1 leaf. I am very guilty of advising and posting about CB and 2x4 wall system making 3 leafs, Knightfly told me this, I believe Eric Desert corrected us both on this... basically the concrete is to stiff to act as 2 leafs, bounce air and amplify sound as 3 leafs would. CB's should equal the mass of 4 layers of 5/8" drywall/gypsum or a number real close. If I had the option between 2x4 outer/exterior wall and regular brick, for sound isolation I would choose brick, IF I could afford it. The 2x4 wall is going to require a lot of beefing up to get it's mass equal to that of standard brick. Also in comparison to footing required for brick or 2x4 wall I don't think it's that big of a difference. Your still going to need a footing regardless. Here in Florida the same footing (basically) is required for 2x4 walls as CB because of wind sheer, the footing needs to hold the wall and house down/not let it blow away during a hurricane... I'm sure you won't have as strict wind codes, just thought I'd mention it. My wild A$$ guess is the only additional cost for footing would be a tiny amount more concrete and maybe re-bar. The real big difference in cost would the wall BUT with 2x4 you're going to have to factor in the "beef" of additional layers of sheathing, gypsum in stud bays, adding render/stucco to the exterior AND/OR another method of getting the mass of the 2x4 exterior wall equal to brick. SO if I could afford it, in your situation I would go with brick AND when making those footings I would make them wide enough so that new inner 2x4 wall rest on them. I'd make a monolithic slab (whole slab and footing= 1 pour), not pour the slab separate from footings. Hope this helps, and remember I am not an expert :D Marc

guitardad72 wrote:
SO if I could afford it, in your situation I would go with brick AND when making those footings
Marc, If it were me - I would avoid this - although it has a good STC rating - in order to install a brick veneer - it is required that the brick is bonded back to the structure behind it - the brick itself is not structurally capable of standing up all by itself...... Thus - for frequencies in the STC range it's fine - BUT - for low frequencies you loose a lot due to the coupling between the wall assemblies - flanking takes over. If it were a perfect world - I would use hollow core 8x8x16 (nominal) blocks - install rebar and grout as required for stability - and then fill the remaining cores with dry sand - prior to installing my bond beam to finish the top of the wall......... dry sand is an excellent damping agent - and raises the LF value of the assembly tremendously. I would then build my room within a room without ever connecting the 2 structures. In new construction - I would ALWAYS opt to construct in such a manner as to uncouple the slabs from the outer walls and foundation - thus i would not install a monolithic slab/foundation. Just my .02 cents......... SIncerely, Rod
