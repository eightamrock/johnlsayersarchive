Steeeve wrote:
That’s quite an increase for just a 40mm wider gap, and staggered studwork - so staggered studs offer greater isolation than a shared frame assemly, but why ?
You said it with the word "shared". Sound is like any thing you may have heard about in that it will take the path of least resistance. If you give it a path, like a shared vertical frame, it (sound) will use it like a bridge to continue until the path outward or inward depending on where it is coming from changes. Breaking the path is what we are always trying to achieve in relation to sound isolation. Even more then just staggered studs, it is common to cut sheetrock just short of hitting any other boundary for this same reason, break that connection or decouple the members. This is also the reason a room-in-a-room design is the most sought after sound related environment. Every connection that can be broken IS broken, in the highest level of isolation, slabs are decoupled that only have frames for walls resting on one slab with the ceiling frame resting on that wall frame that rests on the one slab. Air/distance is another factor after breaking the connection with decoupling via staggered studs but more often, a double framed enclosure with no exterior wall touching the interior wall, ceilings the same, not one touching the other. A lot of weight is already in a concrete slab due to the concrete, add to that the compounding effect of more walls and more mass and you have a boundary that will be difficult to shake, your floor that is. So even with staggered studs making a connection at the floor, this connection has to move a very heavy object usually damped by the earth below it, so if it does it is in the low frequencies.

Ted White14 July 2009#6
Less contact surface area for conduction is one component of decoupling. The other is that each side (leaf) can move in and out independent of the other side. A single stud wall with razor thin studs may have reduced contact area, but it's still coupled. Once decoupled, a partition will have a lower low frequency resonance point, as a side note. The correspondingly deeper cavity will also lower that resonance point, as will the addition of absorption and mass.
