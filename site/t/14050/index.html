I was planning on doing an extra layer of gypsum board on the outside walls anyways, I do not want any neighbours complaining :) So the Isolation should be fine, right?
One extra layer of drywall over 45cm of solid brick is not going to make any appreciable difference at all. Not even a measurable fraction of one dB! Brick is roughly twice as dense as drywall, so just to double the mass of that wall you'd need to put about SIXTY layers of 5/8" drywall on there, and all of that would only gain you 6 dB of isolation, at the very best, assuming that there are no flanking paths at all. So don't waste your money with that plan If you really do need better isolation, then your best bet would be to build a stud frame about 20cm inside of the existing brick wall, totally decoupled from it, put at least 3 layers of 5/8" drywall on only ONE side of that frame, with Green Glue between the layers, and fill the entire gap between that and the brick wall with mineral wool or fiberglass. Do the same on the ceiling, with the new ceiling resting ONLY on the new stud frame walls. No mechanical contact at all between the new wall and the existing wall. That would make a worthwhile difference in isolation. However, you did not say how loud you are nor how quite you have to be (in real numbers), so it's really hard to say if you already have enough isolation, or if you will need more. You say you have someone living directly above you, so that will be the limiting factor in how quite you have to be, and since you share walls and his floor (your ceiling) there will be flanking going on, which you will need to deal with through decoupling.
Now I am thinking of the best geometry. I know of trying to avoid paralell walls. I tried to calculate a couple of wave paths to have an area of no reflections on the mixing position.
For a reflection free zone (RFZ) you need to splay your inner-leaf side walls by at 6 degrees each (total of 12°) and tilt your inner-leaf ceiling by 12° (lowest at the front of the room, highest at the back). That is assuming that you will arrange your speakers in the "usual" geometry, being an equilateral triangle with a 60° intercept of the acoustic axis a few inches behind your head. However, based on your current design, it looks that would put the listening position at roughly 50% of the room depth, which is a bad idea. You will probably have to tighten the angles on your speakers, perhaps to as much as 90° intercept angle, to get your listening position closer to the 38% general guideline. (It doesn't matter if you can't hit 38% exactly, but as log as you are close to 38% and far away from 50% and 25% then you should be OK.): So if you were to change your speaker angles like that, then you'd need to increase your wall and ceiling splay angles accordingly, to still have a good RFZ around your mix position. I'd also suggest getting rid od most of that glass up front: bad idea. Way too much reflective surface, and it takes up the exact space where you could be soffit mounting your speakers.
Another consideration I am not exactly sure about is floating the floor. How do I do it?
with great care and a LOT of money! :) Seriously, correctly floating a floor isn't that easy, and is expensive. Unless you absolutely determine that you must do it in order to get the isolation you need (calculated from your numbers) then it isn't needed. You say that there is only an empty basement below you, so it sounds like it won't be necessary. The only reason you MIGHT want to consider it is if you figure out that you will have too much flanking through the floor and into the walls.
Or do I even have to do it?
Most likely not!
I do not want any neighbours complaining :) So the Isolation should be fine, right?
That depends on how loud you will be, how quiet the neighbors want you to be, and how much isolation you already have. You need to measure those with a sound level meter, to get real numbers. You can't guess.
I do not know what kind of music will be recorded in it. I guess mostly rock/metal.
Well, that will give you an idea of how loud it might get in there: Assuming acoustic drums, bass amp, etc. you'll be hitting around 115 dB or even more. so now you only have to figure out how quiet the neighbors will want you to be, and/or how quite your municipal regulations require you to be. With those two numbers you can figure out how much total isolation you will need, and after measuring how much you already have, yo can then design your structure to give you the additional isolation that you need to reach your goal. In other words, your very next step is to research the sound levels. You cannot design your isolation until you have accurate numbers. If you try to guess then either you will screw up and not have enough isolation, meaning visits from the police (or from the neighbor, with a baseball bat in his hands!), or (much less likely) you will go too far, install too much isolation that you don't need, and waste lots of money.
