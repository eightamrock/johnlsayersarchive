Scot30 March 2005#13
Hey vair, I just wanted to jump in and add my $.02 (USD) because I'm sort of in a very similar circumstance as you. I am in the planning stages of building a studio in a one-car garage with around the same dimensions as your space. I'm considering a design very similar to the one John proposed for you. My problem, which is also similar to yours, is my wife (drummer) wants to have a "live" room that's large enough for her to teach ensemble classes, so it would have to be large enough to have her plus 4 or 5 others along with a drum set, bass amp, guitar amp(s), keyboard, keyboard amp, etc. Sure, it would be nice to have a large live room for band rehearsals and such but this whole large live room idea is really starting to ruin my plans as far as what I'm really trying to accomplish with this space, which is to make a recording studio, not a rehearsal studio. It's also been pointed out here and elsewhere that a common pitfall with studio design is to build a studio with a large live room and too small of a control room. During a recording session, the control room usually turns out to be the "hang" room and many (bass players, guitar players, etc.) prefer to be in the control room while recording so they can listen to the monitoring system available in there instead of headphones. So, in my case, what I'm really trying to do is weigh everything out by calculating the percentage that the space will be used for different activities and then will design to suit my needs. I don't want to end up with a control room that's too small, a live space that doesn't work for band rehearsals and other rooms that nobody wants to be in because they're too small. One thing I have been considering is to create a two-room studio (control and live) where it's possible to open the isolation (sliding glass door, for example) between the two to widen the live space for rehersals. The issue I'm having with this type of design is that my neighbors are less than 15 feet away, so sound isolation is VERY important. I would have to build 2 rooms withing 2 rooms so that proper isolation can be obtained and that would lessen the overall available space. This post is probably not helpful to you at all :) but I just wanted to post to let you know that there's someone else out here with similar concerns (maybe) and also to point out that pitfall of building a control room that you will wish you would've built larger when all is said and done. Good luck with your studio and I hope it turns out exactly how you want it! -Scot
