Hey, I gotta agree with you there! :D Isolation from sound coming in/going out requires a principle called mass-air-mass. One of the side effects of m-a-m is $$$. Another is weight, and LOTS of it. Being that you're on an attic level, you might want to consider checking to see if the structure can support the added load. My understanding of small vocal booth/voice-over treatments tend to lean toward the "dead" side. During the insulating phase of my front soffits, I noticed how nice and quiet it got with each 4' x 2' x 3" section of rockwool that was installed. It occured to me that this approach would make a nice vocal booth environment given a little more attention to inside dimensions and air circulation. In your case, several well placed absorbers, whether free standing or wall/ceiling mounted depending on your work area, should help things quite a bit. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

In light of the challenging architecture and quest for the "ideal situation regarding the furniture," would it be viable to build several of John's "rear absorbers" in a more portable size (I say that loosely - mine weighed in at about 60lbs each, but had the added weight of wooden slats), stack and arrange them in a non-square configuration, capping the whole assembly off with a cloud unit (either hung from the ceiling or just placed as a cover on the wall unit assembly? I approached the side absorber treatments in my room in the same way (stackable, portable) and the difference they made in the room (in addition to other treatments) is amazing. What's nice is that after you're done recording, you can take them down and store them in a corner, closet or against the wall and have your living space back all without having to move your furniture. There are commercially available units like these which look great, but are pretty expensive. Depending on the cost of materials in your area, you should be able to construct this much more affordably than the commercial products. Hope this helps.

Aloha Richard,
Will it make a difference how tall the absorbers are? If so, what height would you recommend given the configuration you suggested? (My ceiling is 7'10")
I would imagine that at least as high as a foot above the level of your microphone would be good, but if you're planning to place a "cloud cover" over the walls, then I'd plan for as much extra room above your head as you can spare.
Also, I've searched but haven't found this answer: Is it important what kind of wood I should use for the slats?
I remember reading about that somewhere around here, but the gist of it the answer was, whatever's available/affordable: pine, hemlock, cedar, birch. I used hemlock on mine; it was affordable and looked good to me. There was even a couple of posts of someone routing out the variable sized slots into a sheet of plywood. One thought though, my understanding of very small room acoustics (as in vocal booth size) is that it might be better to have it "dead" than "live." I saw that the plans for the vocal booth called for slats, but there were several posts around here lately that recommended a "dead as possible sound." Perhaps you could try a couple of recordings without the slats and see if you like it that way or prefer a more live sound and add the slats later.
And finally, you mentioned commercially available units like this: Are you referring to those super expensive all-in-one IsoBooths & WhisperRooms, or are there actually commercial free-standing absorbers/walls out there?
