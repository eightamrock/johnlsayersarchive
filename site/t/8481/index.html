Hi All, I was going to post this in the silencer thread to try and steer it back to a focused discussion, but it's been locked so here I go with a new thread. I've got my return and send HVAC lines going into my room, and back to my furnace, but I'm finding that the return is too strong....lots of air motion, and naturally air noise. The problem, I beleive stems from the return for my studio being mounted right at the root of the main return. I haven't built a silencer box for this line yet (I was going to wait since I have easy access to theis line in my furnace room). My plan is to build a baffle box using Johns Manville superduct. Now, what I'd like to know is how can I lower the air handling noise, reduce the bleed thru of studio generated noise into the HVAC return, and better balance the return flow? Seems like these are all sort of contrary objectives... I understand that for air rush noise I need to work at large opening sizes to slow the flow, but how do I control the return air pressure (I guess with maybe a mechanically adjustable baffle at the connection to the return ait main trunc)? Looking for help here....and trying to keep any discussion 'on topic'.. Cheers Kris

The 2 supply lines are 5" diameter straight steel runs, with the baffles that I built in-line. They come into the room via standard registers (4X12"). Cheers, Kris
OK- that makes sense- you have a total of 36.27 si of supply running free and full - and then you have a return of 50.27si free air trying to return to your system........ so what happens is you create a build up of static pressure in the supply - which increases the retun air rate even more....... See - this is why I always over size evenrything for studios- you won't see anything less than a 10" round feeding a little room that is only getting 100 cfm if Iminvolved in the project.... and the supply and returns should alwys be the same size...... Yes - a balancing damper on the return (although odd - they usually always go on the supply) can be used to slow the rate down to match......... you should try to place it close to the unit - and let any air noise it makes dissapear with the baffles you built. Rod
