bpape30 December 2005#2
Move your measuring position to determine which dimension or dimensions are causing the issue. Then you can put up some targeted Helmholz or sealed membrane absorbers that will get down that low. Just don't try to target 40Hz as a center frequency or you can actually cause more issues than you fix.

Aloha Ermi, Have you tried calculating the room modes? (requires Excel) http://www.harman.com/wp/index.jsp?articleId=131 If I've applied your measurements correctly (L x W x H): Length: 4 m = 13.1232 ft (decimal) 13' - 1 5/10" (feet, inches and tenths-of-an-inch) Width: 3 m = 9.8424 ft (decimal) 9' - 10 1/10" (feet, inches and tenths-of-an-inch Height: 2.4 m = 7.87392 ft (decimal) 7' - 10 5/10" (feet, inches and tenths-of-an-inch) then, it would seem that you do have standing wave a @ 43Hz. 1st 2nd 3rd 4th 72Hz 144Hz 216Hz 289Hz 57Hz 115Hz 172Hz 230Hz 43Hz 86Hz 130Hz 173Hz Attached are the results of the info entered from above. In my first thread: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic ... ght=#15644 Steve (Knightfly) replied to a similar question I had with:
Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips; here's a useful calculator for figuring this. http://www.harman.com/wp/index.jsp?articleId=131 I cut and paste the length and width graphs alongside a scaled room diagram (after scaling them to the same size as the walls) and draw red lines across the room where peaks and nulls are, then green lines where there are NO peaks or nulls - anywhere there's a green intersection is a candidate for speaker or head placement, keeping in mind you need plane symmetry in a mix room (head in center side to side) and an equilateral triangle for speaker/head placement (or at least an Isoscelese triangle)
If I understand this correctly, we want to position ourselves where there is: - no peaks or nulls - front to back - a plane symmetry in mix position (head in center, side to side) - an equilateral triangle for speaker/head placement (or at least an Isoscelese triangle - no peaks or nulls - ceiling to floor If you could post: - a drawing or pic of your room - measurements of your ears at listening/mix position from the front wall - measurements of your ears at listening/mix position from floor to ceiling it would be helpful to further determine any possible courses of action. Hope this helps. Aloha 8)

bpape31 December 2005#6
If you're getting an issue tied to room length, then the measurment/seating position should be moved to minimize interaction with the modes. Generally, 38% is considered the best place to minimize modal interactions for the length dimension. While this may not be the whole answer, it's a start to verify that this is actually the issue.
