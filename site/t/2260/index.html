dymaxian5 September 2004#2
In your situation, a one-room studio is probably the best thing for you. It's basically what I'm doing, and while I do a lot of recording for my band and other people who come thru, I do a lot of my own recordings with me playing everything, and it's always been preferable to have it all in one room. Portable treatment may leave you with a somewhat lacking sound treatment system, but if you can do semi-portable stuff you'll be just fine. When I use the term portable vs semi-portable, what I mean is this; to me, portable means not attached very tightly and can be picked up and moved around whenever you want at a moments notice. Semi portable means it's screwed into the walls or ceiling, and while you won't want to be shifting it around every weekend, it won't be too much trouble to pull it off the wall when you move, and put it up at the next house. The other thing you might want to try is adjustable absorbers. Take a look at John's work at Left Bank studio on the "recording studios under construction" page. On update 3 he has a simple plan for a 4' wide panel system that amounts to a 2' wide door, and a 2' wide space next to it for that door to swing into. The door frame is filled with rigid fiberglass insulation and covered in fabric, as is the space for the door to swing into and the frame behind the door. On one side of the door, there are wood slats over the fabric. When you open the doors, you get wood slats for a more "live" sound. When you close them, you get just fabric and rigid insulation for a more "dead" sound. Either way, they're deep enough off the walls to give a deep absorber and soak up a lot of low freq sound. I'm building a few of these, and they look promising. This way you could have a variety of room reverb times to play with. Anyway, yeah, you can do a search for all-in-one-room ideas and you'll get a couple responses. Most people here prefer to have separate control and tracking rooms, so it'll be a short list. ;) Good luck!
