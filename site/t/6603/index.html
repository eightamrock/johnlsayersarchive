John Sayers11 July 2006#4
jnrhacksaw, could you please follow forum rules and complete your profile showing your location - you could be at the north pole for all we now. I'm aware of straw-bale construction and I see the following problems. 1 - it takes up precious floor space as they are almost 2 feet thick. 2 - you still end up with a plaster wall that needs additional acoustic treatment. 3 - inundation with vermin, they love straw nests. 4 - they do Not save on labour as they are hard to surface, I've seen it done with a spray gun but it still takes as much labour as a standard stud wall. 5 - they must be used in conjunction with either a veranda or wide eaves as they don't like rain. 6 - they are non load bearing so additional structure is needed to support any ceiling system. cheers john

on the points raised above: my intention is to build rendered straw walls within an existing brick structure. the bales will be played on the side giving a 12-14 inch thickness. The walls will not be load bearing. Although straw bales have excellent load bearing proprties and many houses are built without frames. The builder just waits for the weight of the roof to compress the bales and leaves the necesary tolerances. I've looked into vermin problems and builders of straw buildings report no problems with infestation. The bales have no spaces inside them and when rendered will be as diffcult to knaw into as a plaster wall. Stud walls on the other hand have voids within them than provide excellent rat hotels. I'm guessing that a rat would like to have a nice rockwool bed especially as they are oblivious to the health risks. I'm sure plastering the bales willl be a tedious process, covering them with chicken wire will help the plaster to key. I'm not going to need a building permit as I am using an existing structure. So long as I leave the doors on it is still classed as a garage in the UK. I'm sure a lot of the people on the forum find the idea of a straw bale studio amusing. But I am serious about doing this. The low costs and Idea of using an organic waste material appeal to me. Personally I'd rather be in an environment free of toxic materials.
