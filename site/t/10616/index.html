Hey Matt, not really a lot of information to go on, well, except for "without spending thousands of dollars". Since you just want opinions, and I have one, maybe I can help. "... which is the one shared wall with our neighbors. Right now, the concrete wall, which I beleive is 8" thick, is finished on both sides (my house and his house). Both finishing is done with Single Drywall over 2x4 studs." The shared wall is going to be the most difficult area to address. Not the wall but the airborne sound that gets over the top via whatever flimsy situation is currently installed. The ceiling needs to be addressed. Not a simple thing like take away the ceiling panels, which you may wind up doing, but to add density to that overhead area. If I were in your space, reading further on this topic would be a priority. If you don't want to spend the money for materials, your going to really need to know more then just a general laypersons words to correct some of the deficiencies.

Cram17 May 2008#4
You've really got to think of what level of sound and what frequencies of sound you want to prevent from transmitting through the structure. You replied to xSpace with a point about the sound travelling over the wall and while that is true it's not a complete picture of what would be happenning if you had 4 walls of mass installed with a suspended ceiling. A suspended ceiling wouldn't address the lower frequencies that the other walls would be effective with. Your sound wouldn't just be going over that wall you mention, but vibrate the floor above the room. The best thing you can do is find literature either through this site in the reference section or as many have done here - get rod's book. For the maximum effectiveness, you'll want to get as close to a room within a room as you can with as little contact points as possible to transmit sound to the walls and ceilings surrounding your room. Find information about rc clips, risc, wic, hat/furringchannels to buffer frames and even independantly framed ceilings and how those can be done. Then move on to the subtleties of every piece of the puzzle. Trust me there are tons of subtleties to be dealt with in overcomming unique needs of your room. best of luck. If you spend time now learning, you're going to save and do it effectively with a better informed mind.
