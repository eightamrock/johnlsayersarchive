blue_thunder18 January 2011#1
My brother-in-law recently renovated a building that he's using for party rentals and a portion of it has a hair salon in it. There are a couple of rooms in the back of the building that are not being used, so he told me that I could use it for free for a practice/recording space in the evenings and on weekends, when the building is not in use. The walls are already insulated and I added a second layer of drywall and a solidcore door between the 2 rooms, with plans to add a second layer of drywall to the ceilings when time/money permits. Up until last week, the building did not have heating or cooling. So, he hired a local company to come in and install a HVAC unit and ducting and such. Without consulting me, he decided to have them build a closet and put the HVAC unit in one of the rooms I'm going to be using. I went up there to see how loud it was going to be and it turns out there is so much noise coming from the return air vents that the rooms are unusable while the unit is running, and since there is a return air vent in both rooms there is now an opening for sound to pass from one room to the other. The HVAC unit is inside a closet on a platform about 30 inches off the ground. There are vents on the walls below the platform for the return air. I took the vent covers off to see if that was part of the noise, but it didn't make a noticable difference. It seems like a good deal of the noise is being created by the fan itself, and not just the air rushing through the vents. What I'm needing to know is, can I build silencers for these return air vents to quiet the HVAC enough to be able to record and mix in these rooms? If so, how big should I make them? What thickness of duct liner should I use in the silencers? Is there something cheaper that I can use, instead of duct liner? Is there anything else I can do to make this work? I've already spent a lot of time and money on these rooms, so I'm hoping I can still make them work. Thanks for your help, Joe
gullfo18 January 2011#2
in general, 2x the area of the duct for the silencer path e.g. if you have an 4"x12" duct then you want 8"x12" (or equiv) for your silencer. this will slow the air flow down by about 50% which will help with air flow noise. use at least 3 turns in the silencer. you also want to check for vibrations (fan unit and ducts), you'll want to seal things up again, and use duct liner in the box to absorb and buffer. you might also want to check with the HVAC company that installed it if they have any recommendations on making the machinery/ducting quieter.

since that closet is sitting in the live room you should check for vibrations and and such since your mics could pick that up and in which case you'd want to put that unit on isolator mounts. it might not be an option to reduce the fan speed on the unit and since the returns are right in your room, you're basically listening to the fans almost directly. given size constraints, you might just make the baffle boxes the same size as the returns and live with any air flow noise. where are the returns positioned on that closet? near the top? you might create the baffles as soffits on the ceiling around it to avoid major intrusions into the space where people are walking around.

xSpace19 January 2011#8
"What I'm needing to know is, can I build silencers for these return air vents to quiet the HVAC enough to be able to record and mix in these rooms?" Any restriction you place on return air modifies the way the system is designed to work and that most often means reduced performance and reduced life expectancy on the machine. As another downer, you essentially have a three way bridge from the control room, live room, and the rest of the building. There are only a few ways out of this and either one is going to require walls to be built to isolate the path. With hvac guy in tow, then you develop where in the wall your returns will go (room returns as opposed to a system return) that will give you the servive you require and keep the system balanced as needed. You and your brother-in-law should have a talk first...
