camistan23 June 2007#14
I would compare that Rwdb (Airborn sound measurement) rating from your brace with a brace that has neoprene. I would also call each company that manufactures the brace and explain what I wanted to use the product for and make sure the brace you have would give you the best isolation the way you have it attatched. On the Lafarge web site, the info they give pretty much lines up with a lot of the info given here, but I'm just not sure as far as the product in question. I've never seen any type of recommended brace on here that supports a frame (to ceiling joists) or connects to another frame for support where some type of decoupling wasn't recommended. And neoprene was one of the primary items listed to help for this. I'm not saying this type brace is incorrect or wouldn't work, mainly I've never seen one like this. I would think some investigation into this is worth your time. Hey, I'm no Pro, so take my thoughts for what it's worth. However, ESPECIALLY since you have steel frames I really question connecting 2 steel frames with a metal brace. Seems like flanking noises transmissions are inevitable.
