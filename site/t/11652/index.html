I don't know much about HVAC, but it seems to me that you could probably divert that ducting over against the wall, to free up the middle of the room. I'm thinking that it might be possible to just install "L" shape angles at each end of the room, so that the duct ends up along the top of the wall. xSpace31 January 2009#3
A lot of things happen when you re-route hvac. Moving it, as you want, will create open holes in the floor finish where once an opening was not and create new openings where once there was none. How that fits into your up stairs environment, only you know for certain. Floor finish will have to be considered, tile, wood or what have you. Can it be done? Sure. You need to ask someone closer to you, someone more inclined to know the dollar figures. But even moving the send/return only exposes the steel beam. I guess it is smaller and easier to deal with. It may be an area that you have to attempt to incorporate a sloped ceiling to hide the ductwork, or some such ceiling configuration. "I'm a pharmacist (not really familiar with construction)." Funny, I'm a carpenter not really familiar with pharmaceuticals! Good luck, Brien

Soundman20205 February 2009#5
If I use a two leaf system to isolate with 2 or 3 layers of 5/8 gypsum, do I need the same 2 or 3 layers of gypsum where isolation is not necessary?
Unfortunately, yes you do. You need the same amount all around. Your wall is only as strong as the weakest point. So if you need 3 layers in the "worst" spot, then you need 3 layers everywhere, including the ceiling. The inner leaf needs to be consistent on all 5 sides. - Stuart -
