Soundman202021 March 2009#2
So, we have a basement (4x4 m) and we built a booth (2,20 x 2,10 m) out of double drywall (knauf) panels and fill the middle with rockwool
When you say "double drywall panels", do you mean that you put two layers of drywall on one side if the studs, or that you put one layer on each side? One layer on each side is not good, since your house walls would make a third leaf. Also, how high is the ceiling?
The ceiling will be lowered and insulated with rockwool, and carpet flooring.
Why do you want to put carpet on the ceiling, if you you already have rockwool there? Do you mean special acoustically transparent carpet, that has no effect on sound? Or do you mean just any old piece of ordinary carpet? Acoustic carpet would work fine on the ceiling. Ordinary carpet would probably not be much use.
So I guess that the space between the concrete house wall and the slats need to be airtight in order for the resonator to work properly...
The entire BOOTH needs to be airtight! All four walls, and the ceiling, and the floor, all need to be sealed. If not, then you won't have decent isolation. In any event, you cannot use the space OUTSIDE the booth for acoustic treatment! Treatment goes INSIDE the booth. The air gap between the house wall (outer leaf) and the booth wall (inner leaf) is part of your isolation, not part of your treatment. Two different things. If you build the booth wall "inside-out", then you can put slot resonators on the walls, and use the stud space for absorption, as needed. Of if you build the wall normally, then you can build frames to hang on the wall, with your slot resonators over those frames.
Please, if you have any more ideas for acoustics of the booth...
Try using the "search" feature of the forum, for terms like "vocal booth". I'm sure it will give you lots of useful information. - Stuart -

Soundman202023 March 2009#4
About the resonator, I just wasn't sure that the cavity between the outer leaf and the booth wall needed to be airtight in order for the resonator to work properly.
The "cavity" between the outer leaf and the wall is not part of the treatment: It is part of the isolation. In order to avoid the drop in isolation that this third-leaf will cause, you should in fact make that gap as large as you possibly can. If you put the booth close to a wall, that will create a third leaf, which of course reduce isolation at the low end of the spectrum. But that "cavity" will not have any noticeable effect on the slot resonator, as far as I can see, since it is totally outside both leaves: There is a full two-leaf MSM system between the "cavity" and the slot resonator, so any effect would be negligible, IMHO.
The treatment inside the booth will be made of acoustic panels all over the walls (I copied the design of a friend - profesional musician).
You copied the design of the entire booth, or just of the treatment? If the booths are identical, then of course the treatment should work the same. But if there are appreciable differences in the booths, then the same treatment might work differently, depending on what it is. For example, if his slots were designed to deal with specific frequencies that are known to be a problem in that specific booth, but your booth has different dimensions, then it will be treating the wrong frequencies, and not treating the right ones... - Stuart -
