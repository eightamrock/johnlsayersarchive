Started by Randy K on 24 April 2010. 9 replies.

Randy K24 April 2010#1
I'm using whisper clips with hat track and two layers of sheetrock with GG in between to soundproof a ceiling in a basement room. The room has two windows. I'm not worried at all about sound escaping through the windows to the outside - there are no houses anywhere near us. My only concern is in blocking noise from going up through the floor of the room above. When I combine the height of the clips, hat track and sheet rock, my ceiling will end up being at the exact level of the inside of the top of the windows. I plan on sealing the joint with sound caulk. My fear is that noise will leak up through the window frame, which is plastic. The frame itself is isolated from the rafters, so there is no direct connection there, but the thin plastic doesn't have much mass. I've got 1 1/2" between the top of the window frame and the rafters above. Any ideas on how I can prevent noise from leaking through the frame? I was thinking of just laying 2 layers of sheet rock with GG over the top of the frame, but I'm not sure if that will do any good... :?:

Soundman202025 April 2010#4
Once again, it looks like you missed the point. You are wasting a huge amount of money by putting all that sheetrock and green glue on the CEILING, when sound can simply flank AROUND it, out through the window... Think about it a little bit: Low frequency sound is not directional: it will get out through that glass EASILY, it will wrap around the entire house, and it will get back in again through another window, a door, the walls, the roof, etc. You cannot stop sound by only treating one wall of a room, any more than you can build an aquarium by putting glass in only one side of it... In other words, unless you do ALL the walls of your room, you will not succeed in stopping sound from getting up into the room above.
The room has two windows. I'm not worried at all about sound escaping through the windows to the outside ... My only concern is in blocking noise from going up through the floor of the room above.
The floor is not the point. The floor is only PART of the issue. The windows and walls are the rest of the issue. No matter how good you make the ceiling, it WILL NOT DO WHAT YOU WANT. Sound will get around it ("flank") through any number of other paths that you say you "are not worried about", but in fact you should be very worried about. Once again: What are you trying to achieve? (in terms of numbers). How loud are you? How quite do you want to be? - Stuart -

Get ye some laminate glass and replace the existing glass in that window. The issue is air-borne not so much flanking once you decouple the ceiling since the window is basically a hole in the wall. Once sound gets out of that hole it acts pretty much the same way a monitor baffle does, sound spreads like pouring rain on the wall taking the path of least resistance. Things that can make your situation better are that the openings in this exterior wall be far away from the original window currently in question.
