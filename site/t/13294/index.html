rnelson wrote:
3) Still not completely sure what I'm going to do to the floor....thinking about foam carpet underlay, then carpet, then 1" plywood, then regular laminate underlay, then laminate....I can always find the carpet for free so this would be the cheapest solution.
I have seen the use of rigid insulation, owens corning 703 and comparables, as being a way to decouple a floor from the structure. This isn't something I have tried so I can't speak to it directly, but the idea is solid, so to speak. I have some 1" 703 that I placed on the floor and was able to stand on top of without it crunching down. I have 2" 703 as well but it is installed in corners and completed so I cannot check that. But the practice is becoming to lay sheets of this type of insulation butted together with no space on the entire floor area of the upper build, or a drum room /vocal booth, etc. Then lay the heavest sheetgoods you can physically handle over the top of the rigid insulation. Laying one layer then the next being perpendicular to the first, screwing the sheets together top to bottom. Whatever you do consider, the carpet and matting is not of any value to your build due to no real thickness of either to allow for any good seperation of the boundaries and also the compression factor of either. The high quality carpet matting is a real heavy closed cell foam that could be used and may help but if you do not have it I wouldn't run out and get it:)
