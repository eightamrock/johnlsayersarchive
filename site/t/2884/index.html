what is brick veneer? How heavy is it? sand between two leafs of gyprock is difficult to construct. You would get massive side force from the sand on the sheets. It would probably be a leaky construction because the sand won't fill the construction completely. Why not built a plastered 10 cm brick construction with a heavy ceiling?
z6061129 December 2004#3
bert Stoltenborg:
sand between two leafs of gyprock is difficult to construct.
It's good to put sand inside concrete block. But never put it in a gypsum/drywall wall. At one time or another lots of people seem to have this idea about putting sand behind gypsum, including me. :oops: Rod Gervias once wrote somewhere that it's impossible to construct. There's like a couple hundred pounds of force on the bottom of the wall against the gypsum, and it's not strong enough to hold it back, so the wall eventually 'explodes' (my word). Ahh, here's the link with comments by Eric and Rod: http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=397
knightfly29 December 2004#4
Wolf-man, your proposed construction (two 13mm gyprock on 70mm studs) would only give you about 20 dB of isolation at lower drum frequencies, with about 40 db at snare and cymbal ranges; and this is WITHOUT subtracting the negative effect of your existing construction, which would cause other problems due to multiple leaf effects - This would mean that (at kick drum frequencies) you could start hearing the drums outside when you were playing at around 70-75 dB; typical levels for a drum kit are more in the order of 100-110 dB, so they would be pretty loud outside. For Bert; scroll down to "Brick veneer" section, here http://www.saecollege.de/reference_mate ... 0Chart.htm I'm not positive, since I've not had first-hand experience with this type construction; but I think the brick is laid on edge, and ties placed every so often, mortared in place, and tied to the stud frame - I'm not sure how it rates so high on STC, because even with the mass all brick construction I've seen uses "weep holes" at the bottom to allow drainage of all the water brick is known to absorb - maybe John can shed more light on this, being it's his "stomping grounds", and all... Steve Wolf, if your construction is like the example at the above link, your walls are already about 13 dB BETTER than your proposed additional at low frequencies; you may be better off just adding more mass to the inner leaf of the existing wall. The garage door is another PITA to consider...

knightfly29 December 2004#6
A lot of them seem to bite the bullet and get electronic sets for practice; I have both, but definitely prefer the real thing most of the time. If you're not trying to do the "night-thrash" thing, you might be able to use the existing brick for at least one wall - if there are weep holes along the bottom, they will reduce the effectiveness by at least 10 dB though. For that portion of the new room, I'd go for a separate stud frame with at least 8 inches to the inside edge of the frame, then two or three layers of gyprock on that - air space should be at least partially filled with insulation, and the insulation should be in good contact with the inner surface of the gyprock to help dampen the panels. If you have enough headroom, you could put a 2x6 (140 x 35mm) ceiling frame across the tops of your inner walls, and hang at least two layers of 15mm rock on that; fill above with heavy insulation. It's possible that leaving your garage door OPEN could actually work better than closed, depending on too many variables for my brain; thing to do is build it as heavy as you can afford, then try both ways... Steve
