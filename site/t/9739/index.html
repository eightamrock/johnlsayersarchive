gullfo12 November 2007#4
sounds like the Quiet Solution THX certified wall paneling...
The patent-pending CPG composite technology in QuietRock QR-545 uses advanced viscoelastic polymers, ceramics and gypsum in a constrained 11-layer fabrication. The standard size 4' x 8' panels are manufactured by Quiet Solution at its Silicon Valley factory
the thing is THX "certified" is for a certain type of usage and construction and might not be as [cost] effective for a recording studio...
In addition to being tested by technicians at THX, the 1-3/8” panels were tested by globally recognized labs at the National Research Council of Canada (NRC), which showed the industry’s highest ratings, approaching STC 80, for QuietRock QR-545. This level of acoustic isolation performance is unprecedented, exceeding other techniques by 20 decibels or more. Low frequency performance exceeds 50dB in the highest-rated assemblies. This performance is achieved in a drywall-like panel that hangs and finishes just like standard drywall.
that said, it sounds impressive and it would be great to actually see the report they are talking about... given nothing shows up when you search the www.nrc.org site... one concern is that once you reach a certain level in wall isolation, then structure borne sound transmission (flanking) becomes the prevalent source. so its good to consider expensive panels but also consider the structure, doors, windows, lighting, wires, etc that will penetrate the walls and how much it would cost to have proper isolation there... it may be less expensive to use several layers of 5/8" (16mm) drywall over metal or wood framing and use the saving to buy better quality doors and windows... i believe the QR is about $110 (in the US) per sheet whereas 5/8" drywall (in the US) is about $9 per sheet. you could easily get STC-63 walls using 5/8 drywall and its still less than 50% of the QR... just some thoughts...
