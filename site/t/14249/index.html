Hai Max, First advice: take your time to develop a good plan. Read this forum as it can save you serious buckaroons by building it the right way. We're here to help, ask specific questions. There's much info on this forum that will help you on your quest. Right, having said that. Here's an attempt to answer your questions;
1. As the diagram shows, I plan to build a fake chimney breast to keep things symmetrical. The extra corners this will create will have bass trap-type traps too. I'm thinking they are likely to have little effect on bass, but thought that if nothing else they should help tighten the stereo image by preventing sound bouncing in the corner directly back to my ears. So is the fake chimney breast a good idea and if so is my plan to treat the corners it creates a good idea?
It makes the room more symmetrical. It's at the end/near your listening point so, yeah good idea.
2. How much of a detrimental effect will the chimney breasts have, seeing as they make the room non-rectangular?
Detrimental effect on what? But, see Q1 :)
3. Is the back wall superchunk a good idea seeing as I cannot do things symmetrically with a bass trap in the opposite corner (because of the door)?
You mean the backCORNER, right? So, why indead not make a bassabsorber on the BACK WALL (where the sofa is). Remember, you can't have too many basstraps. Live with what you can do. . .
4. Are the wall/ceiling bass traps I plan to put in (i.e. horizontal above the windows) worth doing seeing as they 'll be so small? I haven't measured up there but I don't think there's any more than 30cm between the top of the window and the ceiling, so the "superchunk" would be 30 x 30 x 41cm.
30x30 is not that big no...... there's more than 4 corners in a room tho. sound is 3-dimensional, so why isn't your drawing?
5. RE: ceiling traps. Are the two extra ones at the back of the room (see above) a good idea or overkill? Could too much trapping on the ceiling cause problems?
If this is a mix room, I'd concentrate on the listening position and create a (near) RFZ (Reflection Free Zone). Best practice is (overall) to have hard vs soft area (eg. hard floor, soft ceiling). So, no it won't be overkill.
6. Would also be good to know if my room ratio of 1 : 1.25 : 1.85 is good or disastrous (those dimensions are as if the chimney breasts aren't there).
It's an existing room so it won't matter... can't change it anyway.
