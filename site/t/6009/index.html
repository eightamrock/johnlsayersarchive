Joji wrote:
I am buying a new house and want to build a recording studio sound proof room above the garage. . .
Are you sure about that plan? :shock: First of all, there's no such thing as a "sound proof room." Noise will escape -- it's just a matter of how much (or little) of it does. Secondly, such spaces are extraordinarily heavy, such that they're almost always best suited on the ground floor... Unless you don't mind the room collapsing into the garage and smashing everything (and everyone) in its path! :!: (No, I'm not kidding!) Perhaps you can reverse your plan and put the guest quarters above the garage instead... :roll: I realize you're asking about available contractors, but I suggest you spend some more time here and learn as much as you can about what you're getting into so that you can effectively plan the project and (very important!) supervise the construction of your space. A tiny tiny tiny fraction of contractors know how to build studio spaces properly, yet only a tiny tiny tiny fraction of them even realize this. Welcome to the forum -- we'll be interested in seeing this plan of yours develop! :) --Keith :mrgreen:

Joji wrote:
Which is more of a pain is that it ia gated community so the plans have to get passed by management. How many sqft would be good for a mix room and vocal booth thanks
By gated community I presume you mean home owners association with deed restriction on your property. They typically take a vote whether or not to allow a resident to do renovations not already within "the rule book". If I were you I'd be asking them questions ASAP. Especially if the commuity has a lot of older residents, no offence to any age range but hear in Florida there are a lot of older residents and there are numerious news stories of home owners associations not allowing minor things, let alone a studio for hip-hop music. :D How many sqft? Vocal booth's can be smaller. For a mix room the general advice is make it as big as you can. Reason being the smaller the room the more acoustic absorbers (& other) needed to correct room modes, especially in the bass end. Bass traps (Low Frequency Absorbers) take up a lot of room, the smaller room needs more bass trapping. :shock: John Sayers suggestion is: 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m or (about) 19' 6" x 16' 6" with a minimum ceiling height of 7' 10" I'll suggest you read through (where above info came from): http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html For most of us it seems "how many sqft" is determined by budget, existing structure conditions and available space. Marc
