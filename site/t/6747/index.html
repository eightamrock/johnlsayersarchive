sharward1 August 2006#2
Not much time right now, but you need to learn the difference between a vapor barrier and a vapor retarder. Most people say barrier when they mean retarder. You most likely want a retarder. Kraft paper is a retarder. Plastic sheeting is a barrier. And be sure it doesn't "split your air gap," in which case you'd end up severely compromising the benefit of your air gap (third leaf effect). On the stud spacing, in general, "floppier" is better for noise control, so go for 24" if you can do so legally. Verify with your local building department. --Keith :mrgreen:

Stan, on the floor thing - I think you're over-thinking and over-spending to buy separate butyl sheeting - in your case (MIDI studio, non-metal volume levels) it's not necessary or even desirable (cost-wise) - instead, I would go for one of the commercially available "floated" floor systems that include a soft sheeting layer, and follow their directions EXACTLY (along with taking pix during install) That way, if there's ever a problem you'll have a better chance of warranty doing some good - I would NOT support your inner stud frames on this, but simply use 2 layers of 30# roofing felt under the frame plates. See attached crude drawing. Your ceiling should be resiliently floated INSIDE these walls and caulked using backer rod and caulk (see reference section under caulk, there are about 3 threads) According to this http://www.buildingscience.com/resource ... 004_08.pdf you're in Zone 4, non-maritime - assembly #13 appears to be closest to your wall construction so I would use ONLY latex paint on your inner layer of wallboard with NO OTHER barriers.
