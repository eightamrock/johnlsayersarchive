luvshinerbock11 November 2003#1
The U-boats from Auralex don't need mounting by their design, however, I've purchased a 1/2" 60 durometer neoprene sheet that I've cut up into strips 2" wide and 6" long to place under my 2x4 floor joists. Do I need to somehow "mount" the pads to the joists, or will careful placement suffice? I'm assuming the weight of the flooring on top of the joists will hold everything in place, but I want to hear your experienced opinions please. Additionally I've placed GAF Tri-Ply rubber roofing roll on the wooden floor beneath the floating floor...there are some creaks I couldn't repair without going crazy with costs. I intend on placing strips of the GAF on top of the floating floors' joists for additional dampening. I'm really open to hearing your feedback. Thanks.
cfuehrer12 November 2003#2
For the creaks, get some teflon powder and dust in around to help eliminate some of them.
luvshinerbock12 November 2003#3
Teflon powder? Dusting powder on top of the wooden subfloor will help eliminate the creaks? Really? How does that work? :?: :?:
cfuehrer12 November 2003#4
It's an old carpenter thing used to quiet creaky stairs. The teflon powder helps to reduce the friction between boards.
giles11712 November 2003#5
2x6. Seems kinda big, I'd cut them into 2x3 strips. I have always used drywall screws to screw them to the stud being careful to sink the screw in halfway so when the neoprene pad compresses the screws dont touch the surface below. Bryan Giles I think it's over killl to place the GAF on the studs, but hey every little bit helps. The material is so cheap it can't hurt your pocket too much to experiment.
