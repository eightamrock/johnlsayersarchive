knightfly28 February 2005#4
OK, knowing you're on the 16th floor helps somewhat - sorry about the "technical stuff", but there is no UN-technical way to make sure you get any SERIOUS degree of isolation. You either need to learn enough about the physics of sound proofing to do it yourself, or spend enough money to HIRE it done by a knowledgeable pro. The link I posted is an attempt to help people "get up to speed" enough to not force me to re-type an entire acoustics book or two twice a day - if you follow the suggestions, you will learn enough to understand what some of the problems are with non ground-floor rooms and isolation, as well as ways to get as much iso as you can under a given set of circumstances. Before you can begin to get your questions answered with any accuracy, we will need to know how your floor has been built, EXACTLY - this info is very likely only available through the engineering firm that designed the building but if you DON'T find that info, there is NO WAY you will know if you are safe in adding the needed construction weight that it will take in order to isolate yourself from the neighbors. Same with wall construction. Please read the requested material so you can understand why I'm asking for all this "technical stuff"... Steve
