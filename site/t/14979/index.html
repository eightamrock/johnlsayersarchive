Or is rectangular the way to go?
Always go with a rectangle. The modes are predictable and correctable. The construction is cheaper. The isolation is predictable. Philip Newell in 'Recording Spaces' has said, "the angling of the walls has produced a very different path for the hand-clap echoes, and will be quite dispersive at high frequencies, at low frequencies very little has changed." and "It can thus be seen that whilst the angling of walls can have a very worthwhile effect at frequencies above those which possess a wavelength which will be subsequently shifted in position by a half wavelength or more on their return to the source wall, at frequencies below these, the effect will simply be that of a comb filter... Strong comb filtering at low frequencies is usually musically disruptive and very undesirable in recording studios and music rooms in general, though it exists to some degree in all reflective spaces." F. Alton Everest also states; "The acoustical benefit to be derived from the use of non-rectangular shapes in audio rooms is rather controversial. Gilford10 states, “...slanting the walls to avoid parallel surfaces.... does not remove colorations; it only makes them more difficult to predict.” and "The proportions of a rectangular room can be selected to eliminate, or at least greatly reduce, degeneracies, while in the case of the non-rectangular room, such a prior examination of degeneracies is completely impractical." and "If the decision is made to splay walls in an audio room, say 5%, a reasonable approximation would be to analyze the equivalent rectangular room having the same volume." - emphasis added. I hope this helps with your decision. ;)

Position your speakers so that they are equidistant from you and each other... be aware of SBIR - speakers not too close to the wall :cop: since, you will be making the ceiling absorbent, a cloud trap is unnecessary. Corner trap in all vertical corners - as possible... Be sure to maintain symmetry in front and to the sides of the mix position. Place reflection point traps to the left and right & behind the speakers. My First Reflection Calculator can help you there... http://www.jhbrandt.net/pubs.html Absorption/diffusion on the wall behind the mix position - you will need about 3 meters behind you for diffusion to be effective.

isolation brackets shouldn't negatively impact your room response. getting the proper ratio and treatments defined will make sure the room works as expected. from a room ratio perspective, you could start with Louden's best ratio: ratio size 1.00 7.58 h 1.40 10.61 w 1.90 14.40 l and adjust your isolation walls to match these. stripping your existing walls to the frame and then beefing up where needed, closing off some windows, planning for ventilation and cooling/heating. then adding an inner isolation wall or using isolation mounts to decouple.
