carsten_d7 February 2010#47
now to the plan: actually i looked for a design facing to the big window (reasons see above). I changed my mind again and this is where I'm at right now: speaker (ADAM ANF 10) will be flush mount. due to axial room modes (heigthwise) the speaker will be mounted 20cm higher than the proposed ear level (~120cm). therefore speaker heights ~ 140cm. facing roughly 7 degrees down. modal problems (heigthwise) @ listening position will hopefully reduced by a cloud with a solid back tilted @ 12 degrees. listening triangle will be 60 degrees. speaker to speaker distance ~200cm. listening position will be roughly 44% from front. "speaker-beam-crossing-point" will be 20cm behind listening position. the front will be absorbing (except the speaker soffits). no slat resonators @ sides of listening position due to lack of depth to the left (there will be the fresh air intake) the "real" backwall will be slanted (hopefully to reduce the axial mode lengthwise) slat resonator @ backwall will have a "pseudo calculated" center frequency of 80Hz (2nd axial length mode) - "center frequency" isn't the right term 'cause the depth of the box will vary from 10 to 20 cm. so: more broadband than tuned. the rest will be real broadband absorption. still playing with small QRD diffusors (like in the front e.g.). BUT this will be stuff I'd like to experiment with when the main treatment is done. aaahhh...hmmm...yes...the floor: I need a floor. impact noise between ctrl and live room is unbearable: 30mm compressed rockwool. 1 layer 22mm particle board. finish will be 22mm real massive spruce deal board - no laminate. no parquet. these are (again) rough sketches - to scale but not drawn out exactly. cheers, carsten

mika7 February 2010#48
Hi Carsten Please skip the idea with roomwool. it will make isolation much more worse. I had the same situation, first floor of an industrial building (warehouse) , and i build a floor like you want to do. at the end i had a lot of extra work to remove this floor again, since isolation was bad, the floor make acoustics inside the room worse, and this kind of floor ist not very stiff, which gives you some holes with the time. Save your money and live with your situation, or build a real floating floor (concrete) with sylomer or something similar. Don´t make the same mistake i did. cheers Mika

Phybeau7 February 2010#50
Let me start by saying, you've done a wonderful job up to now. You're clearly NOT without DIY skills :thu: About the floating floor. I have to agree with Mika. In order to make it work, the topfloor will have to weigh 'tons' in order to make it's own (resonance) frequency very low. Paul Woodlock had some excellent research on this in his build diary, if I'm not mistaken. And it would work best if the separate walls were attached to those separate floors. Is it transmission of kick drum etc. you're trying to reduce? Maybe a thouroughly designed (dampening) drum- and or bassamp riser could help you reduce the impact noise on the floor? I found this, which is a simple idea, but might be effective: (it's cheap, so you could try it, without breaking the bank ...) http://www.dtxpressions.com/platform/ I've never seen anybody make a 'floating floor' with just a couple of layers of mdf (or similar) and GG in between. It might be that GG doesn't work very well on these low frequencies, but you could try a search....
:?: apart from that: I wonder if this kind of transmission would also happen if the concrete would be directly on solid earth (ground level)?
I'm inclined to say NO, since it is damped on one side. But that's just a hunch. Just a quick question: why are you planning your listening position at 44% instead of 38% from the front as recommended by Wes Lachot? ( I'm aware it' s NOT a RULE.. :mrgreen: ) Do you expect your speakers to get too close together, if you stick to the equilateral triangle?
