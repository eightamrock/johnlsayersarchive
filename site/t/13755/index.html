I hope I have given enough detail in this initial post to create a decent "design" thread that will continue on to a construction thread that will hopefully prove to help myself and others. I guess I would be able to get more interested if the demo had been completed. Like those overhead soffits...doesn't read like you think you need them, I would get rid of them. Then most definitely get the paneling off the walls to evaluate the situation behind the paneling. Is there framing that is reusable as is or do I need to demo that as well? Whatever it is you are wanting to do here, you have to demo this place to some extent in order to get an area that can be built into a well isolated basement. Read on my brother, read on... EDIT: A word of caution! When I use words like demo and extract and tear out walls and ceilings, etc. it is because I am aware of how the structure is put together and what NOT to knock out or down. Your experience may not be as developed. Be careful and hire a professional if in doubt.

"And it is fir 2x4 framing with insulation "bags". In the old days, these were in theory used for insulation and vapour barrier. I plan on basically replacing everything. The overhead "soffit" is actually the main heating duct (supply), see the sketchup drawing. The first step in the building proccess is going to be redoing the wall insulation against the wall foundation concrete. I have not fully decided if I'm going to use fiberglass or rigid Celfort. If I use Fiberglass I will of course need to install a vapor barrier." Actually in the old days it was paper, it's in the "new days" that plastic has emerged. In any event, no mater what insulation you will use, the vapor barrier is most often not something you do or do not do. You install a vapor barrier, period. On the interior side of the build, the last wall that is framed, as an on going rule for existing basements. Celfort is not insulation as it pertains to the interior of an acoustical build. It is a rigid polystrene barrier designed for exterior applications OR as part of a thermal environment. But there are many things that need addressing, like getting the actual footprint of the area which most likely requires all the interior waste to be removed, or you can guess at it :) I recognize that everyone in the world wants a pretty design to coo over and no one wants to get dirty :) Getting dirty is part of my job, so to speak. And the more information you can give to a potential designer, the more accurate a build he or she can design for you. "I would, however, like to do my best to isolate the studio from the rest of the house." This is where you have to open the overhead ceiling area including that existing vent to add mass if at all possible. The vent would have to be reconfigured/redesigned and massed up in order to meet this desire of separation from the house. That said, if the neighbors can hear you and you can hear the neighbors then your band playing louder will only mask the potential for the neighbors, traffic and what have you outside of those above ground windows to be recorded. You may require window plugs for these windows. I'm pretty certain, that or heavier glass. You may find that you can re-route the heating vents to run parallel in the overhead joist bays, gaining you the additional head room. But you have to get dirty :horse: :yahoo:
