knightfly20 July 2005#4
No, using absorbent across the room corners will get you down to maybe 200 hZ or so; but for best absorption the absorbent needs to be 1/4 wavelength of the sound being trapped as the distance away from the wall; the 1/4 wavelength of 30 hZ is nearly 9 FEET, so for this low a frequencies you need panel traps, which work on a totally different principle than absorbent traps. Bridging the room corners will smooth out most of the room modes, positioning of your head and speakers (in all 3 axes) will help (should be done first) and if your room has serious modal problems (wrong ratios of width to length to height) then you may need to consider a few panel traps to smooth out those particular lower frequencies...
