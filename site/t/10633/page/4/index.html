xSpace12 May 2010#94
Riff wrote:
" Do you really want to pump a lot of warm moist air into your attic space? " Attics in south Louisiana rarely get below 100 degrees and are probably 120+ degrees from May to September. I'd be pumping dehumidified (by the air conditioning unit), COOL air in there which would help the attic move air out through the ridge vents. I would think, anyway. Am I missing something?
And sometimes 140+ degrees, but pushing it into a residential attic that by design depends on convection to work doesn't increase the ability of the flow of air. You are most likely aware that hot air rises and cool air falls, simple enough right? As the accumulation of hot air gathers in an attic the cooler air is, by vent design, allowed to flow out the eave and the hot air has gable vents to allow it to escape from the expansion. What happens if you push more air into this cavity is that the building materials will take the brunt of the stress, e.g. pressure will be placed on any material that is accessible in the attic, ceiling sheetrock, insulation, the plywood on the roof, etc. The potential to increase the temperature/moisture in the attic, which is a legitimate concern that Stuart brought up, escalates beyond the ability of a simple roof venting system to recover from. "I'm totally sealed in the attic," I still have my own concerns about encasing a room, including the ceiling, with a vapor barrier with an attic space that has no outlet/inlet for air. I understand using a vapor barrier in this fashion does happen, it is after all the ability of the HVAC to extract the moisture from the room. What I don't understand, yet is how do you do that with an air tight attic that will get upwards of 120+ degrees just sitting in the sun so how does the moisture move when the warm air is moving towards the cooler air? My experience? Read paragraph 2 :) Not that this needs to be said, but what you are suggesting is a code violation and I do not even know that for a fact...I just know ;)
