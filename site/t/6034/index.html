David French18 April 2006#2
In a room that size, you can pretty much kill the modal ringing above about 60 Hz with eight 4" thick panels in the right spots. Those spots will likely be the front two wall-meets-wall edges, and a panel across each wall-meets-ceiling edge. You may have to treat that little corner to your back right, so that woudl make two more panels for a total of ten 4" panels. This leaves early reflections and boundary interference. You will have to have your speakers very close to the wall behind them, so I would recommend placing a large absorber in this wall, as thick as you can and with as thick of an airspace as you can... I'm thinking nine 2" panels here, stacked three thick and side-by-side. That leaves the side walls and the ceiling; two panels on each side wall and three on the ceiling with a generous airspace should do the trick. So, we have eight 4" thick panels and 16 2" thick panels... I think you can get this for within your pricerange if you get a good deal.

David French18 April 2006#6
If you're talking corner placement for bass absorption, a panel near a corner but flat against the wall is a waste. Always across the corners. A panel on the wall above the pillows would be a good idea. A panel practically anywhere is a good idea since your goal in a room that small is dead dead dead. The solution I outlined was designed to be the minimum required to get the job done, or as they say in my neck of the woods, git'er dun.
