knightfly27 May 2004#5
Hey Casey - didn't notice you weren't getting response - possibly others were as aghast at the mess as I was - I finally found someone even less organized than I am :? - The first thing to do is support your local dumpster - all that clutter, empty bottles, dead pizza boxes, etc, may be the thing for college dorms but will cause problems in a room used for sound - buzzes, rattles, wierd frequency response, etc, not to mention health hazards from mold, dust, mildew... Beyond that, whatever monitors you get should NOT have larger than about 6" woofers for 2-way speakers, it leaves too much of a hole in the frequency response between woofer and tweeter - they need to be placed symmetrically in the room - if there's a wall within 2 feet of one, there should be a wall within 2 feet of the other, etc - you want PLANE symmetry, with that plane being VERTICAL and drawn equidistant between the two speakers and through the center of your head - imagine a large sheet of plywood bisecting your head and passing between your speakers - everything on one side of that piece of plywood should have a counterpart on the other side of the plywood. Blankets in the closet are the least of your worries, and will likely help absorb lower frequencies to help balance out the room response. Check around this site, you'll notice that most pix of rooms have the mixer on a desk centered between the speakers - this is because you don't want your head pulled out of the "sweet spot" when making adjustments on the mixer, or you'll either make wrong choices or have to make several changes and then put your head back centered between speakers to hear what you did - Blankets hung here and there will mainly just deaden the highs too much and cause your room sound to be too "boxy" - too much midrange - sound-wise, you'd be better off using the entire room's volume to even out response. Is your bed really that large, or could it possibly go in the alcove where your desk is shown? You only need access to one side to keep it made - that way, you could put your desk on the wall on the left side of the plan, centered on that wall - the bed would act a bit as a bass trap if it were in the alcove to your rear (as you sit at the desk) - Just a start, I'm pretty buried right now with project stuff; you can get a lot of education in this stuff at the SAE site, and the more you learn the more questions it will bring up (but they'll be informed questions) - I would concentrate on filling a dumpster with all the crap that will vibrate, trip you, or give you asthma - (not including the guitar, of course :wink: ) and then, spend some time reading the tons of useful stuff here and at the SAE site - it's all good, and any day you learn something is a GOOD day (Even at my age there are no BAD days :D ) I'll try to not let you fall through the cracks, but don't get all "frowny" if it takes a couple-three days, I work some pretty long hours and generally give up sleep to get more than a couple hours a day for this - all the study you do here and at SAE will pay off in improved understanding of this complex subject though, which will help you make better and better recordings with the gear you have available... Steve

knightfly28 May 2004#7
No problem; and, if you're not using the mixer that much then you're right about placement - instead, since you're doing most things on the 'puter that's what should be centered between your speakers (try to keep the monitor as far out of the audio path as possible, though) Also, watch that you don't place speakers or your head at exact fractions of any dimension of the room, such as 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, etc - (you DO need to be centered and symmetrical left-to-right for stereo imaging, but don't put your speakers so the woofer sits at half your ceiling height, for example) Good luck with your dumpster campaign...
