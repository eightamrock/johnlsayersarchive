Hi there, before you do anything I'd recommend reading more about acoustics and room treatment. My favorite place to direct newbies is to Ethan Winer's acoustics FAQ: http://www.ethanwiner.com/acoustics.html That's the single best introduction I know of. Though I have seen some experts dispute some of the details, reading that thoroughly will certainly get you thinking in the right way. There are other places to read, on this forum, the studiotips forum, etc. There are 2 first steps to good acoustics treatment. Step 1 is to install some bass trapping, ie, get your low end under control. Step 2 is to create a Reflection-Free Zone at your mix position, to tighten your stereo image so you can hear where you pan things while mixing. For bass trapping, the most cost-effective solution is probably to get 4" thick panels of rigid fiberglass or rockwool, and straddle as many room corners as you can. This will help get your bass under control. There are other options, which may or may not be more effective than this. To create a RFZ, have a helper hold a mirror up to your walls while you sit at your mix position. Anywhere you can see your speakers reflection in the mirror, put some absorption on the walls in that spot. This includes the side walls, the rear wall, and the ceiling. A third step that would be useful for your room would be to have some freestanding absorbers. I'd probably use those for the RFZ, actually, at least for the side walls. If it were my room I probably wouldn't bother with diffusion. Maybe, once I had it sounding good with well-placed absorption. Good luck!
