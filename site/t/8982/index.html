jwl22 June 2007#6
staggered studs are a bit better than normal walls, but won't give near the performance of a well-executed 2-leaf room-in-room construction. The budgets of the 2 probably aren't hugely different. If you're building from scratch, you'd be better using the alternate design, imho. Generally speaking of course.
sharward22 June 2007#7
Using double 2x4s instead would be, as Jwl said, not much more expensive, but would be superior, isolationwise. It would also be, I think, easier to frame: the frames will be much lighter and easier to work with. Have you bought Rod's book? If not, I recommend it. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward23 June 2007#12
Don't worry too much about slight imperfections in thr surface of the slab as far as air-tightness goes. In theory, your soleplates could be an inch above the slab, but it wouldn't affect the air-tightness because you'll be using a resilient acoustic sealant (and backing rod) where the drywall meets the slab. That being said, Sillseal is good to use as a capillary break to ensure your soleplates don't get wet from the concrete. --Keith :mrgreen:
