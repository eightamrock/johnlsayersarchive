My "home studio" with a "pro theme" is most of a garage loft, which is about 23'x13' give or take. The garage loft was "finished", but not really well. Most of the old 1940's insulation was "stolen" by mice that the prior/original owner of the house ignored for decades, and the walls/ceilings were covered in broken, cracked, rotting, falling down tongue and groove cedar panels. I like cedar, but not when it's in such horrible condition. A good loud sneeze in this place would result in paneling falling from the ceiling, or worse - mouse poop falling through the gaps between the boards, into your coffee. And there were some structural issues I wanted to address as well. The ceiling in some areas was only 7' high, and in other places, a mere 6'4" or so. I'm 5'11" barefoot, so I found it a bit claustraphobic. Any, here is the initial design, which I strayed off quite a bit. The sofa in the picture isn't there, and instead that's the area I placed all my synthesizers, on stands.

frederic8 December 2005#9
Heh-heh. The basement is nice and toasty, even with an open window on the other side of the basement. This is why I like compaq equipment... it's been in those racks running for almost four years, never failed, dispite it being about 80 degrees in the basement right now (it's 35 outside), and during the summer even with the four basement windows open, it approaches 100 degrees very quickly. Yet the server farm seemingly is happy. Aside from web, mail and other typical internet-related services for myself, this server farm is what I store all my music/video data on. 1.97TB of contiguous storage available for recording. Makes shuffling data between PC's, laptops, and external drives a thing of the past. And since it's a rock solid raid array, with two automatically consumed "spare" drives, I can lose three hard drives of the array without losing one byte of data. And, if a drive does fail, I simply yank it, plug in a new one off the "spare drive shelf", and it rebuilds. Don't have to reboot, don't have to run any configuration utilities, and it's totally transparent to Linux. And, completely constructed of use bits and pieces off e-bay for about $2k. Best $2k I've spent in years. Anyway, that's what is in the left rack. The right rack contains two servers - one is a game server/backup server, and the other server in the rack is an internet radio station which I haven't streamed to in years. I used to stream stereo digital audio right from my console, through ethernet, right to the internet station for people to listen to live mixes.
