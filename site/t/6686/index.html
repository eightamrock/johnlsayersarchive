[quote='oakdown']finally (and i'm sure it's been asked so i'll do a search too) much benefit to roxul safe n' sound, over regular insulation? i'm thinking of going with the roxul just because of the ease of handling (no itchiness). [/quote] Two points here: 1) There IS itchiness with Roxul!!! I know, I put up two pallet loads of it. I'd use the same precautions with Roxul (or any mineral wool product) as I would for regular FG insulation (long sleeve shirts, dust mask, goggles. At a minimum, put on a lot of baby powder before starting. It fills your pores and prevents the little "hairs" from getting in. 2) As I understand it, if your insulation's purpose is for heat/cold insulation or for damping, standard "pink fluffy" insulation is fine (preferred?). When you are making acoustic treatments (wall absorbers, ceiling clouds, etc) mineral wool or RIGID fiberglass is what to use. len

knightfly28 August 2006#12
Using 3 layers of 5/8 wallboard each side on double frames as an example, air gap of 8" (1" between frames) STC 73, 34 dB TL @ 50 hz, mam 34 hZ air gap of 10", STC74, 35 dB@ 50 hZ, mam 31 hZ Air gap of 12", STC 74, 37 dB@ 50 hZ, mam 28 hZ Air gap of 16", STC 74, 39 dB @ 50 hZ, mam 24 hZ Air gap of 24", STC 73, 42 dB @ 50 hZ, mam 20 hZ Air gap of 36", STC 73, 46 dB @ 50 hZ, mam 16 hZ. All from Insul's old demo, no longer available. AS you can see, LF TL continues to improve mainly due to lower mam frequency; so if you're wanting better drum iso, wider air gaps can be useful if you have the space to give up. According to the same calculator (typically somewhat optimistic) replacing the outer 3 layers of drywall with 8" sand-filled concrete block raises these numbers to STC 94, 59 dB @ 50 hZ, mam of 12 hZ. THis is with 36" air gap. Drums would have to be louder than about 115-120 dB in order to be audible outside such a wall, assuming all other paths as well built... Steve

sharward7 September 2006#24
Looking fabulous, Oakdown!!! 8) I'm by no means an expert on concrete foundations... But here's what I do know.
  • "Frost line" is defined as the level down to which the soil will normally freeze each winter in a given area.
  • "Permafrost" is defined as a thermal condition where ground material stays at or below 0°C for two or more years. (You definitely do not have permafrost.)
  • Failure to dig foundations down below the frost line can cause frost heaving, dislodging the building at least slightly and causing damage which may become a serious threat to the building's structural integrity.
  • From what I can gather in my Google searches, Vancouver Island, Canada, has a high frost line, so I'm guessing that a foundation depth of one foot (30.5 cm) below grade would suffice.
