Ok, so I'm reeling at the amount of information on this site. I've been reading for a few months and still don't feel prepared to post. My family has lived on a farm in NC since 1833. We are just artists now, so the buildings aren't being used. Developments are springing up all over the old countryside around us and people are moving to the area en masse. They are building a 200 acre development twenty feet behind my mom's painting studio. It's depressing and we could either sell the old farm and get the hell out or try to live up to the changes and turn our piece of heaven into an oasis of creativity and... trees. You can assume that we prefer to do the latter. So anyway I have chosed to take one of the old barns and turn it into a recording studio. I'm eighteen years old, a freshman at Vassar College in Poughkeepsie, NY. I've been recording since eighth grade, beginning with hardcore and punk bands. My current projects include the New York Guitar Quartet, my teacher Terry Champlain's next two classical guitar albums, students from Mannes Conservatory, and folk rockers here on campus. I've got Terry to thank for most of this recent work. I'm trying to do this barn for about $5000 (paltry, I know). Keeping the barn feel is a must. Perfection is not, I would love for this place to have it's own quirky (but obviously usable) sound. Here is a link to the plans as they stand now: www.geocities.com/thebarnstudio I just made this site quickly so I wouldn't have to post pictures and take up space on Mr. Sayers' site. All of the wonderfully imaginative designs that I've seen so far on this site have me reconsidering my design (obviously total symmetry is not necessary). Basically I'm inviting commentary and design alterations from all of you far more experienced studio nuts. Thanks so much, I'm looking forward to working out a much stronger design with you folks! Also, are you guys using CAD? MS Paint just isn't cutting it, but I can't afford CAD.

John Sayers30 March 2005#7
Your new structure isn't going to hold up a second floor. A rough calc estimates you could replace the vertical 4 x 4 posts with horizontal 10" x 3" beams running down the building from left wall to right wall in their place. Then your studio will be able to be built disconnected from the rest of the building which will give you way better isolation and you won't hear the studio upstairs and all rooms are free of pillars. cheers john
