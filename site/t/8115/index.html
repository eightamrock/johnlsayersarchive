Ross H2 February 2007#1
Hello all... I have a basement studio I am building. I have purchased an 8'x9' isolation booth that will be used for vocals, amps, and maybe drums. The ceiling is low and the top of the booth will almost touch the ceiling so basically all I can do is insulate and put up one layer of ceiling material. The floor above the studio (1st floor of house) are hardwood over a particle board subfloor on top of typical floor joists. I would like to reduce the floor bourne noise from family walking and "living" on the floor above to the best of my ability as well as reduce the studio noise to the living area of the home. What is the best insulation to use to fill in between the joists? would 5/8" drywall or 5/8" MDF be a better ceiling? Last, a gentleman I know builds hotels and said they often install a thin lead sheeting between the studs and drywall to reduce noise between rooms...Has anyone ever heard of such a thing in a studio and would anyone recommend this in the ceiling? Thanks in advance, Ross
Ro2 February 2007#2
Lead=heavy (mass) about 12250kg/m3 (about 15 times as heavy as drywall), so yeah good isolator. but it's not cheap ya know. You talk about hardboard, is it real hardboard? since hardboard has great mass. about 1000kg/m3 (gypsum is about 700/800, go figure). But that only works if installed properly.
What is the best insulation to use to fill in between the joists? would 5/8" drywall or 5/8" MDF be a better ceiling?
They both have the same mass, so either will do just fine. Drywall is cheaper tho...
