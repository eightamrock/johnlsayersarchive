Soffit angle for wide dispersion monitor

Started by drfrankencopter on 16 April 2005. 2 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 3588.

drfrankencopter16 April 2005#1
I'm in the middle of designing my one room (plus amp iso closets) studio, and am at the point where I'm placing the monitors in the design. I am using ATC SCM-50a (active triamp) monitors and intend on mounting them on filled concrete blocks, and constructing a false wall in front out of MDF(and framing) to act as an extension of the baffle. I have already determined the optimum listening position of my room based on mode analysis, however if I aim my speakers at the classic 30 degree angle (for an equilateral triangle between listening posn and the monitors) I end up chewing up a liite too much floor space at the front of my room. If I get the monitors as close to the corners as I can I and keep them aimed at the optimum listening position I end up with a 37 degree angle. I've attached a sketch with the monitors in the optimum 30 degree angle and equilateral setup...basically I'm trying to get the monitors into the corners to optimize floorspace. This is a control room and rehearsal/tracking room (I will put my console desk on wheels and roll it up against the front wall while tracking/rehearsing, and roll it to the ideal listening posn for mixing). SO here's the question...the ATC's a a wide dispersion monitor, so they sound really good and coherent even well off axis. After getting the monitors into the corners, should I keep the angle at 30 degrees, and just listen 7 degrees off axis, or should I try to make my soffits at a 37 degree angle (might be tough, I'm not certain what accuracy mitre saws are capable of).. Currently, I'm leaning towards keeping the angle at 30 deg and listening a little off axis. Anyone think this is a bad idea? I checked the 2D wall bounce calculator and the 37 deree angle gives a little bit (1dB) of a bump in the bass, but its a broad bump, and I doubt it would really be audible. Cheers, Kris
