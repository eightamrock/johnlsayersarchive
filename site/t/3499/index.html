John Sayers30 March 2005#4
Yes - use lottsa 703 in the rear wall - there's no room for hangers - yes make the slots cover the low mids. The top slot is deeper so make it the lower end of the mids and the bottom resonator the higher low mids. Go with what I drew and if you need additional treatment you can always put it in the ceiling. The slots are bright to the high end, so are the soffits - the rear wall is your only heavy absorption. The room won't sound dull. cheers john

drfrankencopter7 April 2005#6
Well, the biggest problem with your room, in my opinion is that with the length and width being almost the same value you may have some mode buildup to contend with. Bass trapping will go a long way towards mitigating the problem, however a pro desgin done from the ground up would likely have golden ratio type proportions (I forget the numbers but they're in Everest and probably all over the 'net) to minimize the requirement for treatment. Also, ideally, from an early reflections standpoint you'd prefer to see a distance of around 10-12 feet from your listening position to the rear wall, and would like to have your listening position at around 1/3rd the length of the room. However these things are again best left to a 'from the ground up' construction. The design looks pretty cool to me. I'm curious why the rear wall will be dead as opposed to the front though....is this the new trend in control room design? Is this something that works better for small rooms? Sounds kindof like a Moulton room, but with elements of RFZ. At any rate, good luck with it! Kris
