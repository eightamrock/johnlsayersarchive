Mix room design overview Hey Everyone Thanks heaps for all of the great info on the site. It gives me enough confidence to attack a project like this. I have been trundling around the web on sites looking for information on studio design for about 2 months now in preparation for my own project. I have found plenty of great information and am now ready to put it together for my specific situation and see how I go. I am still at the planning stage (guess I have been for a couple of months now) and hope to begin construction at the start of September. My budget is around A$4500 for materials and I will be building the room with some help from a few mates. The room will be constructed to suit 5.1 surround mixing, although initially it will be setup for stereo mixing only. The loudest I would be mixing would be 90dB. Generally I mix at a level around 83-85dB. The neighbours are close because I live in a residential area of Brisbane. The night time ambient SPL is 50dB slow response C weighted . Ideally, I would want to isolate the room enough so that any sound escaping from the studio is below the ambient level and not noticeable by the neighbours. I have access to another local studio where I can do my main tracking (drums, guit etc) but I imagine on occasion I will be tracking some instruments in the mix room. My monitors are Adam A5’s which I am initially going to mount on speaker stands that have a sand filled cavity (increase the mass). They have 2 x 25Watt amplifiers in each speaker and a rated 101dB SPL at 1m. I have a 2 bay 6m x 6m shed (that has steel C purlins with ceiling battens between them and some tin sheets screwed to the outside of that) that I am converting to a mix room with space left over for household storage. The room I will build at the back wall of the shed allowing the storage to be close to the roller doors for easy access. The ceiling height at the walls is 2.4m and 2.9m at the peak in the centre of the shed. I am considering a room size 3.19m wide by 4.66m long by 2.45m high (average). There are structural steel ‘C’ channels that reduce the allowable studio ceiling height to 2.2m at the walls and 2.7m at the peak. By reducing the room width to less than 3m (to fit the ceiling structure in between structural C Channels) I can get an extra 200mm ceiling height but the room dimensions would look more like 3m wide by 3.7m long by 2.65m high (average). After a little more reading I’m going to keep the width and not worry about the extra ceiling height. I can get some extra height by building the ceiling just under the Structural C Purlin. I am getting these room sizes from recommended room ratios from ‘RealTraps mode calc’ Materials and design I am thinking of building the outer leaf for the ceiling, east and north wall on the inside of the shed by screwing 19mm yellow tongue directly to the ceiling batten. The other 2 outer leafs will need to be constructed using 70mm x 35mm pine frames. The outer leaf will have standard 90mm ‘pink batts’ glued to it. Make the internal ceiling construction modular. The required roof truss beams will go in and then the 2 layers of gyprock will be fixed to the pine frame and screwed to the roof beams. Once screwed in 2” Rockwool will be fixed into the frame in line with the bottom of the roof frame allowing an air gap of approx 40mm between the insulation and the gyprock. Where the roof frame contains the shed structural C Purlin the 2 layers of gyprock will be fixed in line with the bottom of the roofing purlin and 2” insulation will be installed above the gyprock. This will be replicated on the opposite side of the ceiling so that it remains symmetrical. I am looking at a false floor not so much for the isolation but so that I can run cables under the floor to improve the appearance of the studio. Acoustic treatment At the moment I want to put I 2 slat resonators/diffusers alongside the mix position at a 6 degree angle. Superchunk bass traps in the 4 corners of the room from floor to ceiling. The north wall (behind the speakers) will be an inside out wall with insulation to absorb as many reflections from the rear of the speakers. I’m not sure what to do on the rear wall yet. Perhaps some curved reflectors? I've attached drawing that I have so far and (once I have worked out how to get my photos down to 700 pixels wide) will attach some photos so you can see what I can see. QUESTIONS (for now anyway) 1. Will standard Pink Batts be adequate insulation for in between the two wall leafs? 2. Should I put insulation between the yellow tongue and the tin wall and roof of the shed? 3. Should I build a fully floated floor design to help with isolation? If I don’t need a full floated floor I will just create a false floor to allow for cable access. 4. Will the Adam A5 monitors be powerful enough for the room size? 5. I have my speakers focussed at 60 degrees just behind where my chair would be. This makes the speakers (being 600mm off the wall) 1.84m apart. Will that be too wide and mess with the imaging?

"I am getting these room sizes from recommended room ratios..." Thing about ratios is that all surfaces are assumed to be flat as in not splayed at wall level or slanted at ceiling level like your ceiling is, so all bets are off ;) To do a false floor you have to take into account that the plywood will have it's on resonant frequency so effectively it will vibrate if care is not taken. Some have attempted this process using dry sand in-between the floor joists to damp the cavity/plywood. Others have used full sheets of Owens Corning 703 (or a comparable rigid insulation) to do the same thing, either one will work but the application is different for either method. You PDF file looks like the ceiling will be slanted from the left to right. If you can it would be beneficial to either change the sitting position to a wall that places the low side of the ceiling over you head, basically you want the ceiling, if not flat, to go up (higher) as the distance from the back of your head increases, to push sound equally away from you. Or to re-design the ceiling so that you have it slanted up and away from you rather than falling off from left to right or right to left, as it seems to be now. I don't quit understand your isolation methods at this point, how you are going to add mass to the thin metal exterior. I read it, but I am not on it yet. The exterior is going to be the hardest part to get mass added to, and it will need it. But, funny thing about metal buildings is they tend to sweat so you have to think about that as well as anything. You will notice that there is not venting anywhere on a typical metal shed/garage. Reason being that the design uses the door openings and the metal corrugation as a means to get some unrestricted air flow. So if you restrict these areas, you have to take your materials into consideration. Also, that metal shed will not support as much as you might think it will and it >reads< like you are well on your way to overloading the ability of the shed to support itself by attaching more weighted material.
