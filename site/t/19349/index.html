Studio: attached to house, or not?

Started by Rigby on 20 March 2014. 11 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 19349.

Rigby20 March 2014#1
We are in the process of planning some building work that will include a single-storey extension to our house and a music studio. The original plan was that the studio would be a separate outbuilding dedicated solely to that purpose. However, the architect we are using has given us some fairly convincing reasons why it might be better to incorporate the music studio into the house extension instead. I'm wondering if anyone here has any opinions as to which option is preferable. I will try to provide some more details: In either case, the music studio would be a single room (ie. no separate control room) on a concrete floor, with inner walls and ceiling fully separate from the outer walls and roof to provide a two-leaf structure. The dimensions would be such that the inner walls would form a rectangle measuring 5.2 x 3.2 metres (17' x 10'6"). One of the reasons the architect suggests building it as part of the house is that more height would be available. Apparently the floor-to-roof height can be up to 3 metres in this context, so a floor-to-ceiling height of perhaps 2.7 or 2.8 metres could hopefully be achieved, which would make the room modes reasonably benign as far as I can tell. If we were to build a separate studio building she thinks we would be unlikely to get permission for anything higher than a 2.5 metre roof. If built as part of the house extension, the music room would have structural block or brick walls on three sides -- two of them external -- with one of the long walls being non-structural. So far, so good. But I guess what concerns me is that if it's to form part of the house, there are some aspects of the design which need to be more 'housey' than might be necessary in a separate building. This is partly to do with how the whole thing looks, and partly to do with the possibility that some future owner might want to repurpose the studio space if we ever sell the house. For instance, the house has gas central heating, so it would make sense to fit a radiator in the studio and attach this to the heating system -- but can this easily be done without compromising isolation? And one of the end (short) walls of the studio faces our long garden... for any normal purpose, you'd want to have a largeish window in that wall, but can that be done affordably in a studio without creating insurmountable problems? Finally, all other things being equal, I'm not sure how bringing the studio into the house will affect the degree of isolation that can be achieved. The house is semi-detached, and the music studio will be on the side that is not adjacent to the neighbouring house. In other words, our new kitchen would be between the studio and the next-door house, but there would be potential transmission paths along the outer wall of the studio. I will ask the architect if she is OK with me posting her drawings here...

I might not be the right person to answer this, but since Stuart seems to be the only active person here these day, I'll give it a try: If you do build a room in room, you would need to decouple the slab of the area, where your studs stand on from the rest of the foundation. I think there was a passage in Rod Gervais book about laying foundations, if you are able to do that, but I don't have the book here right now. You might check that out. Of course you cannot go wrong building a separate house for the studio. If you are afraid of not getting a permission to the required height of your building, you might also dig deeper by the way. :-) Cheers!

Eric Best9 April 2014#11
OK, this falls into the "for what it's worth" category. I would have it attached to the house. The extra ceiling height is worth it alone. You wouldn't have to worry about running separate electrical or heating. What you would need to do treatment wise is easy to redo into a standard rectangular room and I think that an extra attached room would have more value in resale than a separate room. I also think that sometimes the subject of isolation is overblown around here. If you are going to record drums occasionally, all you need to do is pick your times appropriately, and it won't bother anyone (unless you have crazy neighbors), but mixing should be done at most about 85dB (maybe a bit more just for fun), it is pretty easy to knock of 25 to 30dB with basic isolation, and that will get you down to about the standard sound of a library. A standard exterior wall in a home will knock off about 30dB. With a little extra effort 40dB is very reasonable. I have recorded 6 CDs for people just using my house for recording rooms, and I live about 200ft from a major freeway and have never had traffic noise ruin a recording.
