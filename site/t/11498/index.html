xSpace26 December 2008#3
"Now i wanted to ask if building the second inside wall which would be my second Leaf could also be made of brick and concrete as for me over here it is the cheapest option and i suppose it would provide a good isolation and i dont plan on using any Insulation in between the two walls so basically heavy mass - air - and Mass . Do u think i would be running into any problems with this choice ." This is a tricky area. What can happen is the mortar creeps out of the joint, builds up and either piles up at the bottom and makes a hard connection, or it piles up until it creates a bridge that allows a hard connection between the two walls. OR Both! This is what the folks call a short circuit and makes the wall one leaf. The problem with building brick walls is they have to have something to attache to to keep them vertical. The only way around that is to build the wall so thick that it becomes a support to itself. As to the insulation in the air gap; you gain several points (STC) so it is always recommended. http://www.saecollege.de/reference_mate ... 0Chart.htm

Soundman202027 December 2008#5
Your link led me into doing a lil more research on google and i found some interesting info here http://www.fhwa.dot.gov/environment/audible/al4.htm
Interesting link, but curiously it does not show the best wall of all, and the one that is recommended on this forum tirelessly: double stud wall, with independant stud frames separated by an air gap of at least a couple of inches, two (or more) layers of sheetrock on the OUTSIDES of the stud frames, and the air cavity packed full of rock wool or fiberglass insulation.
ANd also if i plan on building a concrete floor about 10" thick is there any particular way of isolating it that i should keep in mind for when i meet the contractor.
Ten inches of solid reinforced concrete? That's going to give you pretty decent isolation all by itself. But if you do want to isolate even more, then just make sure that your contractor does not connect the slabe to anything else! Pour it directly on grade (plus usual code requirements), build your inner walls ONLY on top of that slab, and your outer wall on a SEPARATE foundation that is not connected to the floor slab at all. In other words, leave a gap between them, and make sure that nothing at all bridges that gap. Not even a nail. But the best advise of all is to buy Rod's book, read it carefully, and take into account everything he says. In his book, he shows you exactly how to build the floor you are talking about, among many other things. - Stuart -

Soundman20202 January 2009#7
Hi Ankit. Happy New Year to you too! Those are really small rooms you have there. I'm not sure how functional they'll be, and you don't even show the positions and swing of the doors. Would it not be better to forget one of those rooms, and just build two that are slightly larger? Even better would be to use that other room as well. Make the garage your control room, and the other room your live room. Or vice versa. Don't forget that your walls are going to be many inches thick, taking out even more space and once you allow or doors swinging open and closed, you are left with really tiny usable space. Plus, your mix positions seems to be at very nearly 50% of your room. OK, your rear wall is actually a corner and two diagonals, so it's hard to tell for sure.
Also another question i couldnt quite find an adequate answer to is if i have my existing walls made of brick and plaster as i mentioned earlier and i build my internal wallls with sheetrock would one layer of 1/2" gypsum with mineral wool filling with a 4" air gap between walls all around give me a good isolation??
That depends! How much isolation do you need? How loud are you? How quite does it have to be outside your room? Are there any exterior noise sources that you need to block, such as roads, airports, railway lines, noisy neighbors, etc.? Sound goes both ways: you want to stop bad stuff getting in, not just your good stuff going out. Personally, I'd go with at least two layers of 5/8 gypsum board on the inner leaf. And make sure that your outer leaf brick and plaster wall is well sealed.
or does it make more sense for me to leave a two inch air gap with mineral wool and build a 3" brick wall all around ?
If you have the extra money, and can build that brick wall VERY CAREFULLY, so that nothing falls inside the cavity that would bridge the gap and causing flanking, then sure, brick would be good too. It's much denser than gypsum board. But it all depends on what you need, in terms of isolation. How loud are you? How much do you need to block? That means getting out your sound level meter, playing loud music and loud pink noise on the room, and taking real measurements inside and outside your room, to see how loud you are, and how much you need to isolate. You need real numbers. Designing a room by guesswork is not a good idea. Either you won't have enough isolation, and will need to tear it down and re-build, or you'll have too much isolation, which is good but it means you spent much more money than you needed to. Take real measurements, and then start designing. And only when your design is totally complete and triple-checked, only then start building. Not before, no matter how great the temptation... Nobody can help answer your questions above without having real decibel numbers to work with.

You need to be VERY careful with building those brick walls, to make CERTAIN that no mortar leaks out the back of the bricks and falls, to cause "bridges" between the two layers. Very important. and the dividing wall for the studio and control room is basically a wall with two layers of gypsum on either side with a 3 " gap in between ( So i suppose i have to use 2 x 3 studs [wooden] to build this wall ).
Doing it the way you describe is a bad idea. You MUST decouple the two sides of the wall. If not, then putting two layers of sheetrock on each side is pointless, and a waste of money. I'd suggest that you make the wall much thicker. I'd go for two sets of studs, each 2x4, with a 2 inch gap between them. Just using a single set of 2x3 studs will NOT decouple your wall. In other words, it won't work! - Stuart -

Soundman202012 January 2009#12
Ankit, your second best possibility is staggered studs on common bottom and top plates. For example, you could do 2x8 bottom / top plates with staggered 2x4 studs on them, or if you are REALLY short on space, then you could go to 2x6 plates with 2x4 studs on them. But I wouldn't go less than that. 2x6 top and bottom, plus two layers of 5/8 sheetrock on each side will make your wall roughly 8" thick. I don't think you can go less than that and still keep good isolation. Your third choice would be a 2x4 stud wall with resilient channel on one side, but that is only going to save you about an inch, and will cost you several dB in isolation.
a) i dont know for sure but i will find out if possible . but if it is does it make sense to build a concrete reinforced wall about 2" thick on either side of a 2" airgap
That would work, but the expense is pretty high, and it will still be six inches thick, overall. That's about the same thickness as the "third choice" wall above, but much more expensive to build. Of course, it would also be a better isolator. Maybe someone else here has some better advice to give you.
on another note i was also wondring if it makes sense to lay the pies for the wiring of the room in the floor that is being built or does it make more sense to run it in between the walls.
If you have all that concrete in your floor, then sure! Put your cable ducts in the concrete! Just make them big, and figure out how to seal them, so sound doesn't travel down them. - Stuart -
