I think I may also put rockwool in the original walls of the garage (covered by burlap) to help matters a bit further.
Well, you'll need the rockwool certainly, as that will be part of the "spring" in the MSM equation, but you will also need more mass on those existing walls. I assume that right now you just have studs with OSB and siding on the outside, and nothing else? Or do you have something else? If that's what you have, then you'll need more mass on that wall, best achieved by "beefing it up" with pieces of drywall carefully cut to fit between the studs, up against the OSB, siding, or whatever you have in there. Since you want to play drums, you might want to consider going for three or even four layers of 5/8 drywall in there, for lots of mass. Then your rockwool (probabaly about 8 inches or so thick). Then another three or four layers of drywall on one side of the inner-leaf frame. Same for the ceiling. Then a pair of really massive, well sealed, back-to-back doors, and don't forget HVAC to complete the picture. That should pretty much do it. - Stuart -

Soundman202030 May 2009#4
Hey there CSMR! A drum riser won't help much to "keep sound in", but it might help a bit to prevent impact noise from getting into the structure, through your slab. But depending on your slab, that may or may not be an issue. If your slab is already separate from the house slab, is thick and dense, and 100% on grade, then a drum riser probably won't make any real difference to the amount of sound that "gets out" through the slab. But if your slab is thin, coupled to the house slab, or has some kind of air space under it, then I guess a drum riser could help. On the other hand, if it is a resonant riser, then it might also add some character to the drum sound (John recently posted a photo here of such a riser).

Soundman20204 July 2009#10
Yeah, I'm with Ro on this. See if you can get more mass up there (safely!), above the "acoustic" tiles, then pack the cavity with insulation. I guess you can at least take the tiles out temporarily, in order to do that? That will help a bit, but see below: Like Ro said, if you need isolation then you have no choice but MSM construction. You could build them inside-out to save as much space as you can. But the problem here is that you can't put an MSM ceiling on top of the walls, due to the height issue and the drop-ceiling restriction, so I don't know if it is worth doing the walls at all. Personally, I don't think the walls will do much at all without the ceiling. It would be like an aquarium with one side missing. I'm not sure there is much you can do at all, to be honest. That drop-ceiling restriction is the deal-breaker. If you can't move that, then you can't build a complete sealed inner leaf, which means you can't get good isolation. I'm assuming that your basement is not entirely below ground level, and some portion of it sticks up above ground. Is that the case? If not, and it really is completely below ground, then perhaps just doing the ceiling might be enough! - Stuart -
