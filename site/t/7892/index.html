sharward9 January 2007#2
The more I learn about this stuff, the more I conclude that a slab that has firm contact with the ground is not a very good conductor of airborne noise. It's dampened by the earth it sits on. The walls have to be amazingly thick and heavy with a very wide air gap before such a floor can become a "weakest link" in the transmission loss. I'd say the bigger concerns are water intrusion and structural integrity in general. The only reason I personally did an isolated slab was that I had to pour a new slab anyway, so the thought was, why not isolate it at the same time? Knowing what I know now, I'm not sure I'd have done it the same way. I probably would have just poured a new slab and made sure it was good, strong, thick, reinforced, level, and smooth, forgetting about isolating it. But, who knows... ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:

knightfly11 January 2007#4
The sand is to damp the floor and add mass - if you're gonna do a wood framed floor you want to use the tallest frame you can to increase the air gap, lowering m-a-m resonance frequency use the heaviest boards you can for the floor deck, for the same reason use GG between layers for even better damping use 3 to 6 PCF rockwool or fiberglass (not regular house insulation) under the floor, and fill it enough so your first layer of decking just BARELY compresses the insulation enough to fasten the panels to the frame; this also for damping. Anything less and I hope you like the "boom-boom;s"...
