I would rather soffit mount our JBL 4311b speakers
A word of caution about those: I just did some research on them, and it seems they are designed to be mounted "upside down", meaning with the woofer above the tweeter, rather than the more conventional "tweeter above woofer". That places the acoustic axis much lower than for a traditional design, so the speaker will have to be mounted higher up in the soffit. It might also cause problems with reflections from the desk surface, and "shadowing" from things on top of the desk, such as computer screens, etc.
but am wondering if I can make the soffit wide enough to accommodate them.
IT should be possible: You might have to fiddle with the angles, make the front section between the soffits narrower, adjust the design of your slot walls, etc., but it should be possible to make it work, with some trial and error. Speaking of he slot walls: them see to be at the first reflection points! You should probably fix that.
I would appreciate some help calculating the proper angles for the soffits.
The general rule (which can be broken!) is to set up the room with your ears at 38% of the room depth (front wall to back wall), and work from there. The acoustic axes of the speakers should intersect at an angle of 60 degrees, a few inches behind your head, and the distance from your head to each speaker should be about the same as the distance between the speakers. (Actually, it will be a bit less.) If you can't make that work, then try adjusting the intersect angle: you can go up to as much as 90° if necessary, but there are consequences...

You'd have to check with John, but I'm not sure that that is actually a design for a room, but rather just a set of "things" that people might find useful in their rooms: The file is called "components"...
I use the JBLs to check bass levels or to blend in to add bass when I am playing music to listen to or for visitors, so I am wondering if I can get away with hanging them up near the ceiling or even putting them in the back of the room?
I wouldn't put them behind you, unless they are actually part of a 5.1 setup (or other surround setup). You might be able to get away with mounting them up high and angled down steeply, as long as you don't plan to use them for critical listening. If you put them up near the ceiling, for example, you'll be getting additional bass boost from the corner (radiating into quarter space, instead of half space), plus the tilt angle will have to be well beyond the recommendations, with all the accompanying issues. But if it is just for adding some bass and occasional casual listening, that might work. But I wouldn't try to mix on those.
If you were looking for speakers in that range specifically to soffit mount them, which ones would you buy. I want more detail than I get with the M1s.
Hand's down, no doubt about it, if I were in your position I'd sell one set of JBLs and use the money to buy a nice pair of Adams with a good sub. Maybe a pair of A7x or A8x plus a Sub-10 for a room like that. You'd have change left over from that deal too, I reckon, and really great speakers, with all the detail and clarity you could ever ask for. There's a reason the Adam's are fast becoming the de-facto new standard. Soffit mount the Adams, and keep the M1's for checking your mixes. But that's just me: others might have different opinions. I'm not telling you what to buy! Just saying that that's what I would do, in a similar situation. Another option might be a nice set of Genelec's. - Stuart -
