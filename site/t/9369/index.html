Hello from Almonte, Ontario. I'm just wondering if you can hang drywall on 32" centers. I plan on using Roxul Safe'n'Sound, which unfortunately doesn't come in 12" widths, and nobody wants to cut that stuff anymore than they have to! I'd use metal studs, but these be load bearing walls, so I can't. Anyone? Thanks in advance, Rhythmrhymer
gullfo7 September 2007#2
i think if you were to ask this in the construction forum you'll probably get a similar answer - load bearing? you'd want proper and full support. i don't see codes allowing 2x3 for load bearing purposes... Safe-n-Sound? maybe $$$ - consider using 5/8" drywall - $. for walls - don't stagger on a single plate - create 2 separate walls using 2x4 and 1" gap. thats 8" of air space between rooms... remember the insulation... if load bearing - 16" oc. if not, use 24" oc. why are you thinking you need to do a lot of cutting?
rhythmrhymer7 September 2007#3

So, I figured if drywall can hang on 32" centers, I'd stagger 2x3's on only these shared walls to control vibes transferred through studs. If I can't hang drywall on 32", then 24" it must be, and that makes for 12" Roxul which they don't make. I'd be slicing 4" off every single batt. and dat's dat. Rhythmrhymer
gullfo7 September 2007#4
still not sure of the logic... if you have vertical wall studs on 24" centers, then you'd use 24" wide insulation... if you have only a single stud-width wall, then hang RC-1 on one side of it to decouple it - keep the 2x4 and skip the 2x3. use braces between the studs (cats) to reduce resonances and put the braces at different heights to avoid common resonances. the braces also hold up the batts... check the construction thread for links to the IR-761 documentation - it is your friend when determining wall construction...

gullfo9 September 2007#9
ok, based on your drawing i'd say the center wall where the ceiling joists are supported is most likely your load bearing wall (when in doubt have a structural engineer check it out). i see now, you want to put up 2x3 staggered on a 2x4 and spaced on 32" will effectively give you 16" between each which can be spanned by the 16" insulation to dampen each wall. this could work since the walls are simply partitions but keep in mind, that they may not be able to support a lot of weight (so I'd check with your local building official to find out what applicable codes there are) and also consider that the ceiling and floor can transmit sound, or if the ceiling overhead is open, allow a free path (and the 2x3 might not be able to support a tall wall). this may defeat the staggered wall construct anyways...
