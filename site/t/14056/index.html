Hey everyone, I joined this forum about a year ago and am now ready to start my project. I have a 24x24 detached two car garage that I would like to convert into a dedicated mix room. The Auralex Acoustics 101 control room looks like it will fit nicely into my space, so I've modeled it in Sketchup and will include some pics in this post. I spend the majority of my time mixing by myself (mostly for bands that have recorded themselves at their own homes), so this room will mainly be used for that. Although, it will occasionally double as a multi-purpose room, for tracking individual instruments (electric guitars, electric bass, drums, and vocals) one at a time. It will also serve as a practice room for myself, since I play all of the instruments listed above. I mix at moderate volumes but would like to achieve as much isolation as is reasonably possible. (Ideally I'd like to be able to play drums at 3AM and not bother anyone in my house). The closest house in my own (about 40ft away), followed next by my neighbor, who is a couple of acres away. After that the neighbors are far enough away so as not to be a real issue. My garage has two existing (fixed) things that I am working my design around. First, there is a side entrance door, which I plan to keep and use after the conversion is complete. This is located in the lower right corner of my sketchup diagram. Second, my garage already has a mini-split AC unit installed. (My previous plans were to make the garage an office space, and I had installed a mini-split to use for that). It is located in the upper left corner of my sketchup diagram. Having said that, my plan is to simply position the control room diagonally and utilize the remaining space however makes the most sense. In the area with the mini-split, I'd like to make an air exchange room. In the area with the exterior door, I'd like to make a small mudroom/airlock, where people can hang their coats in winter etc. I am contracting out 100% of this work. DIY is not an option for me, for various reasons. My contractor really likes the idea of being kept in the loop this early in the game, and is open to suggestions made by the experts on this forum. He will most likely be following the replies on this thread. As I understand it, I am shooting for a 2-leaf system, where the existing garage walls form leaf #1, and the soon-to-be-constructed control room walls form leaf #2. But I have some questions before I do anything more with this design. 1. The upper-left corner of my model shows roughly where the air exchange room will go. Do I need to frame in a separate room for that, or could I just heat/cool the existing space (that is, the space between the garage walls and the control room walls), then pump that air into the control room via ductwork? 2. Same question, but applied to the side entrance. Do I need to frame in new walls to actually make a room there, or can I just use the existing open space for that purpose? 3. Due the the orientation of the control room, there is a fair amount of open space both behind and in front of it. Should I leave that as open/empty cavities, or try to utilize it for something practical (storage closets, rooms for noisy gear, extra bass trapping, etc.)? I'm asking because I don't fully understand the implications of all that air space on isolation. I *think* more air is better, but could very well be missing something obvious. The storage space would be useful, so I'm leaning in that direction unless it will really affect my overall isolation. 4. My vertical space currently measures 8' from floor to the bottom of my trusses. (I assume that the build will be confined to the space below my trusses, creating an attic space for the ductwork, like in the garage conversion thread from "on purpose productions"). If finances allow, I will be finishing with hardwood flooring over a subfloor. I don't know off hand how much that will reduce my vertical space by, but I could find out when my contractor comes over for the initial consultation. So on the topic of vertical height, can anyone tell me if this proposed room matches up with known good ratios? (I don't trust my own calculations on that one!) Or would I need to scale down the CR? This is the area I that am most unclear about. That's about it for now. Once we figure out the above, I'll proceed with my work on the sketchup model. Thanks in advance for your answers. I've read that the best results come from 90% planning and 10% action... I'm trying my best to follow that advice! - H

Soundman202013 February 2010#2
Just one quick question: Is there any reason why you have oriented the room diagonally like that? You seem to be wasting an awful lot of space like that, and cutting off the corners eliminates the very best possible locations for your main bass traps (rear) and speaker soffits (front) If you were to re-orient it either across or along the main axis of the garage, I reckon you would have much better use of space, and you could probably even fit in a small vocal booth, or storage space, or some such, as well as your exchange chamber and mud room. You have the space to do it, so I reckon I'd probably go for a more normal RFZ design, maybe using one of John's designs as the basis, then modifying it as needed. Another question: What are your plans for dealing with the garage doors? Are you planning to remove them and brick up the openings, or leave them in place and seal them / beef them up somehow? What about the existing construction? You didn't say much about it, but I'm guessing that you have a concrete slab floor, no ceiling, and bare studs on the inside? Photos of the interior would help to get a better idea of what you have, and what can be done with it. - Stuart -

xSpace13 February 2010#9
"It is solid pine and several inches thick (I don't have a detached piece to measure, but it is thickest in the middle" It is solid pine. But most likely not several inches thick even at the peak of the crown. Looking at the gable end shot you see the plywood, correct? If that is 1/2 inch plywood and your corner boards are exactly 1-1/2" then the crown of this exterior log siding that just barely projects passed the corner board, suggests the log facade is 1 inch thick at the deepest part [crown]. If the plywood is thicker, then the board is thinner than 1 inch. And if the plywood is part of a multi-layered facade, e.g. 1/2 plywood, insulation sheating THEN the log facade, the wood is even thinner. Still, I wouldn't think this is comparable to a brick facade but is better than a wood siding exterior, all things considered. End of the day? It really depends on how much isolation you require to evaluate if beefing up the interior is or is not a requirement. EDIT: A few corrections. The 1/2 inch sheathing will encompass the entire exterior. THEN the corner boards are installed. So even if there is nothing in-between the sheathing and the log facade, they are still 1-1/2 inch at the thickest. Assuming there is nothing else, thermal sheathing, fan fold, etc. in-between the log facade and the sheathing. But the thinnest point has to be taken into consideration as well. The thickest part not being contiguous and subject to change due to the roll of the log, you almost have to find the difference between the thickest and the thinnest to determine a subjective IIC or STC of this wall. The rest of what I said should remain unchanged and applicable:) Brien
