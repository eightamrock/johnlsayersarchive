sounds similar to another project in NYC... basically take a warehouse and convert it into multiple rehearsal rooms. my recommendation was to plan out a number of "large" and "small" rooms with some of the room pre-framed to allow windows to be added later to turn them into recording ready spaces. make each room fundamentally the same so its a cookie cutter approach so you know the future costs of expansion. each room is a separate unit which maximizes isolation and each has it's own venting etc so (zoned or individual units) to allow least amount of power consumption. building would need parking, security, an office, etc etc as you describe also. in fact, some rooms could be built and used as an "office" or "lounge" until you need to expand or there is pressure to rent more. given enough space, 1:1.4:1.9 room ratios could work - 10ft ceiling, 14ft wide, 19ft deep. construction wise, no reason steel framing couldn't be used all around. here's a plan view of that idea.

Soundman202022 September 2010#6
The basic idea for electrical systems is to separate your circuits, so that your sound equipment does not share the same circuit as your lights, plugs, or other "dirty" loads. So you will have one set of circuits dedicated exclusively for your equipment, another set for lights, another for sockets, another for HVAC, etc. If your building has three-phase power, then you are even better off; you can separate your circuits by phase (as long as you balance the loads), to give you even better separation. You should also use star grounding for your equipment circuits, and one single clean good-quality ground for all of that. Major important point! That's the basic idea. - Stuart -
