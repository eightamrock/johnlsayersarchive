HI, and welcome; first, you need to re-read this http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3231 and at least edit your profile to include a general location (City is close enough, we're not looking for your credit card #'s :wink: ) Second, depending on age of the building it's more typical to find a 2-frame party wall between units; but it's really good to know exactly what you're dealing with before adding layers. For this reason, it would be good to use a hole saw and cut at least a 1" diameter hole in your side of the party wall (tap around til you find a "hollow" sounding part so you don't hit a stud) - then you can look inside the wall with a flashlight, and if you drilled close enough to a stud you can find out if studs are single/deep or if it's a double 2x4 framed wall; one of the commonly available (where are you? This is the main reason for my first request) electronic stud finders will help you drill close to, but not INTO, a stud - the wall is likely insulated, so you will need to "fish around" inside the hole to see if studs are separate on each side of the wall or not - Next item - "green glue" isn't glue at all; it's a Constrained Layer Damping material that seriously improves Transmission Loss in walls, when used per directions. Because of this, you still need to use screws when fastening a second/third layer of drywall. Speaking of which, this is yet another good reason for drilling a hole in your wall - you MUST know whether your wall studs are wood or steel, because different types of screws are used for each. Next thing, but probably not AS important if you REALLY aren't gonna do drums live in there - ceiling and floor; are there upstairs or downstairs neighbors? Hopefully this will get you started; did I mention that you should allow about 10 TIMES as long for this project as you originally think it should take??!? :? Steve

knightfly13 August 2006#4
The neighbor below may complicate things a bit - even an electronic drum kit makes quite a bit of impact noise, so you may need some sort of floated riser if you use such a beast - Screws - sorry I wasn't more clear; the screws for subsequent layers of drywall DO need to go into the studs, another good reason for a good studfinder. There are two main types of drywall screws, one has relatively coarse threads and is designed to hold drywall to WOOD studs; the second type has relatively FINE threads and a self-drilling tip, and is designed to hold drywall to METAL studs. The coarse ones won't hold well in metal, and the fine ones will pull out of wood; so it's VITAL that you know which your studs are, as well as where they are, before you start to beef up your wall... Steve

sharward19 August 2006#6
Just to clarify -- you have one layer of drywall on each wall. A "leaf" can be one layer or multiple layers of something -- as long as there's no air between the layers, they all make up a single leaf. You wrote earlier about using "soundboard." Forget that (brand name "Celotex" I think). It's more expensive than drywall and performs worse than drywall. You cannot completely cover an electrical outlet. It must be kept accessible to be in compliance with code. In fact, if you add a layer of drywall, you will need to use this type of electrical box extender so that the box edge will be flush with the surface of the additional layer. You will also need to seal the outer edge with acoustic sealant -- or, better yet, use Metacaulk Putty Sticks (or equivalent). Your construction situation is pretty typical and certainly better than a "worst case" scenario of single frame. However, to be honest, it isn't terrific -- far from it actually. That wall construction will not offer much in the way of low end isolation. More than likely, your floor will be a bigger problem to address. We haven't talked about that much yet. What's the story with that? However, to be fair, your loudness levels are probably not that great, and I would imagine you could just turn it down a notch or two to satisfy the neighbors... :roll: --Keith :mrgreen:
