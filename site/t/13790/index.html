donal_finn27 November 2009#15
I Agree if its possible lose the tiles. I have been working in a room with tiles turning it into a vox booth and the reflections they give are just a nightmare, Cover em up or remove them altogether, its a bit more work but no extra cost and the end result will prove much more stable, predictable and usable. I also disagree that the rockwool panels which are covered by timber sheets will act as reliable bass traps, to use timber fronts on bass traps requires really serious thought, planning and design. Its usually done for a very very specific reason. Much better to remove the wood panels and leave the rockwool in place then build light frames and cover it all up with fabric as you see so many people doing on the site. This in effect will give you solid broadband trapping in the low end of the frequencies, which in any small room is always whats nessecary. The superchunks in the corners are great, cost effective and reliable. The room looks big enough to be of plenty use for recording to me, just slow down a bit and be sure what you spend your money on will work. Best of luck df :)

xSpace27 November 2009#17
I think the tiles may not be as bad as it seems. You have a lot of work yet to do and the tiles are, at least, some mass OVER more mass plus the tiles give lots of reflection. If you are going to do a room in room construction, the tiles are going to be behind a wall anyway. But if you are NOT doing a room in room, then you need to get some accurate measurements of the room acoustics and develop a plan on how to tame this room...if it can be tamed. Removing the ceramic tile is time consuming and the glazing as it cracks and shatters will cut your hands with small lacerations :) Safety GOGGLES [ not glasses] are highly required due to the strong chances of eyeball contact with tile debris. It may be a trade off for you, it may not. Just one man's opinion...

The hardness of the tile are going to reflect, that is the way that material is. Even if an acoustic panel is placed in front of the tile and especially if the panel is installed a distance from the tile, the tile is going to reflect...that is for certain. It is one of the reasons why a tiled bathroom has been used for vocals, because of the hard tile surface reflecting and creating a spacious type sound. So in areas that you are wanting to subdue the reflections but do not want to loose the mass you could have sheetrock installed over the tiled surface with glue or a wall type anchor system. As far as taking measurements, I'm not the guy to talk to specifically about that, but as I understand it, to start with the room should be completely empty. But you are not measuring to locate standing waves but more to get an idea of how this room is interacting with your mic/ears based on known criteria. Having this measured frequency information will give others a look at how your room is performing and then suggestions can be recommended on what it is you need to address depending on the accumulation of frequency, etc. It would be better to get some measurements first before you attempt anything else in this room.
