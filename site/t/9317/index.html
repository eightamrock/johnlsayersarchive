Question: Do you see any issues with my current design? Anything that would cause problems with reflection or 'ease of use'? The one thing that I can see would be the control room angles on the front wall. Or is it?
Establishing a room size that has the least amount of modal issues is good place to start. Next, try to establish proper wall angles, room symmetry, and strategic acoustic treatments in your layout so that the sound and reflections generated are managed and absorbed to produce a clear and defined soundstage at the mix position. For lots more great info be sure to check out: SAE Institute Reference Material Center – John Sayers http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html Be sure to click on the “acoustics”, “absorbers”, and “construction” tabs at left, and then tabs that show up at the top. gullfo29 August 2007#10
gdubs wrote:
I thought about doing that too, but almost everyone I talk to says that I should have length to the mixing room. What do you think?
if you have the room to put in a long control room then that can help with dealing with possible reflections from the back wall that confuse the mixer. however, side wall reflections can be problematic as well and in a long CR you might not have the right angles to make that happen - which means probably adding absorption at key reflection points (which you might have to do regardless depending on materials etc...) and you may end up with more combing interference effects still because of the narrow space. in the sideways CR, you're making the back wall much more dead than in a long room but you're getting better side wall angles. its really about time domain and resulting combing effects that dictate one way or another. check out Barefoot's side wall reflection Excel workbook.

I haven't been here in quite a while. Just needed to get back to where it all started. It took me 3 years, 4 jobs, a whole lot of rearranging, a small loan, a lot of hard work, and some help from some great people, BUT IT'S DONE! Here's some pics of the final project. I will update with some more details, but I figured I would show it off a little. I ended up scratching the two seperate rooms and only going for a single iso booth. I used the composite floating hardwood flooring. Double walled and each wall is stuffed with Roxul. I got a killer deal on it through a company in town called Allied Insulation. $25 for a package of 10 - 16"x48"x3". Plus the guy gave me a bunch of R38 scrap for free to fill in the gaps I had between the existing construction. I also got a sweet deal on my doors. They ended up being custom chops by the door company because my ceilings were only 79" high. $225 a piece!!! Props to that guy. I did go with the control room turned. Its kinda tight in there with a few people but it just means I wont have too many people during the mix yapping away, making my job more difficult. I custom built my desk. I'm not too entirely sure that I like it and am considering removing it and buying a new console. It only houses 14 single space rack units. Overall, I am pleased. I am getting really good results from the tracking room. It's a lively room (thankfully not dead) and I haven't come across any weird reflection problems. The control room is giving me accurate mixes FINALLY! Thanks to everyone here and all the input that I read over the years. I would have never have gotten such a sweet setup if it weren't for this place.
