knightfly19 April 2005#12
DanFanCazy, don't wanna upset a fellow "danfan", but I can't let this ride - I think "B" would work best because the frabic plate (red) resonates at a frequency depending on it's thickness an the size of the sealed room behind it (green). The bigger the room and the thicker the plate the lower the resonating frequency. The isolation glued to the fabric plate then absorbs exactly that frequency. The thicker the isolation, the more of that frequency gets absorbed. - right? The fabric is cloth; no real resonance there. This would NOT be a PANEL RESONATOR without a "panel". If this were a room, the only thing the size of the room would affect is lowest absorbed frequency and the total AMOUNT of absorption; the surface presented to the sound would determine the QUANTITY of absorption, and the DEPTH of the room (or trap) would determine the lowest usable FREQUENCY of the room(trap) - see John's drawing above, on 1/4 wave lengths. If this were an actual PANEL trap, the front panel should NOT touch the insulation; otherwise, the panel will be DAMPED and very little absorption will happen. For that type trap, there needs to be a GAP of about 1/2" between panel and the absorbent material. Ya got the LAST sentence pretty much right... :wink: Steve
