Soundman202012 June 2009#11
My general plan is to use 4" panels around the room with some 2" panels for clouds. I could use some advice for placement. I get the idea of the reflective points to the sides.
Yup. Treating first reflection points is important. There's an easy way to find them: sit at the mix position, and get someone else to hold a mirror up against the walls, and move it around. Any place where you can see either of the speakers reflected in the mirror is a first reflection point, and needs to be treated. You also have first reflection points on the ceiling, which is why you need a cloud up there. - Stuart -

tgauge9 November 2009#13
It looks like no one has answered you yet so I'll tell you what I know in regards to your issues. I have recently treated a very small room using similar materials and got very very good results. If I were you I would start by reading as much as you can in the acoustics forum. There is a wealth of knowledge in regards to all of these issues. 1) The more the better in the corners. Your earlier post talked about going only 7 feet or so. The most important areas are the corners, where all 3 walls intersect (two walls and the ceiling meet). Superchunks work, very well, everyone on here can attest to it. That is what I would shoot for. 2) Speaker height! The center of the panel should be an average between your ear heighth and the speaker height (these should be roughly the same as it is). 3) Opening the door in my opinion is actually worse than not treating that area. Sure it kills the "reflection point" but in turn you make your control room asymetrical, you only get reflections off of one side, and you will smear your stereo imaging heavily. Whatever sound comes back out of that room is going to be delayed just enough to make it even more difficult to mix. I would hang the panel directly on the door. Lastly, don't be scared about any of this. Hang them based on the answers I gave you and what you find on the acoustics forum and then adjust based on what your ears are telling you. Hope this helps a bit.

tgauge20 November 2009#15
Sorry, been on a much needed vacation. You are definitely getting there. The 2" panels should do well on the walls. I am curious to know what density the rockwool is that you purchased. That is what is going to make the difference, not necessarily the thickness but the density. As far as hanging them on the sidewalls, I've seen people use "french cleats" running the length of the room and that way they are adjustable side to side depending on what activity is going on in the room (recording vs. mixing). I used eye hooks and open hooks mounted in the walls to hang mine approximately 2" off the wall. That way whatever sound that makes it through the 2" then hits the wall and then has the chance of being absorbed by the back of the panel. That's my understanding at least. Something of note, based on your pictures, you really need to hang a few of those traps in front of you computer monitor. That is also a large reflection point that should not be missed. I see a few on the floor that I am assuming are going to be hung there.
