xSpace14 November 2012#2
" Is this curved ceiling going to be a help or a hinderence?" Curves tend to focus sound. Think of an amphitheater and how this area was set up to reach the target audience. That works fine in an open environment or for a live environment. But not in a control room or a tracking room since the sound will focus due to this shape and what sounds clear in the front will sound clear at the back. I know what you are thinking. This clearness of sound is because the waves/rays are following a path, they are not specifically engaged in the room, it is the path of least resistance. So yes, soft sided clouds always help, but how much will you need and what type of issues might exist above the cloud will take some time to figure.

xSpace16 November 2012#5
You guys are going to have to work with the room, especially with a hard curved ceiling that moves sound from the front pair of ears to the back pair of ears...quickly. This can be an advantage. You are going to have to just test your set up and see how it works...modify the instrument setup as the sound tells you. Drums in the back with the low ceiling will push the acoustical noise made by the percussion to the front and across everyone's head...essentially covering the entire space with drums. But it does not stop there. Everyone else in the room making noise gets to be on this path and has ability to disturb the other, if you will, with noise. Over head clouds could help bring the room into shape as well as side wall treatments.
