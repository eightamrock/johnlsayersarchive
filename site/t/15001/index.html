Sorry, I strongly suggest that you treat your room. or use a gobo/reflexion filter in a treated room. Their marketing for this 'reflexion' filter is bullshit. Thanks for the link, Glenn. I'm seeing but not understanding: curved perf panel with foil, foam and an air gap inside? All contained in a thin little thing like that? How on earth is it supposed to work? :!: The perf panel itself looks to be something like 80% :shock: , and I'm more than a little perplexed by the statement: "The sound waves then hit a layer of Aluminium foil which helps dissipate energy and break up the lower frequency wave-forms,"... Maybe it's just me, but I'm having a rather hard time figuring out how aluminium foil is going to "break up" low frequencies... Unless somebody change the laws of physics last night, low frequencies wont even SEE a thin sheet of aluminium foil that is maybe a couple of square feet, maximum! Then curiously they show a graph and a photo of the device being tested in an anechoic chamber, where the two lines on the graph ("with" and "without" the device in the room) are almost identically superimposed on each other, and somehow this "proves" that the device is working? The say that this shows the thing does not "color" the sound, but to my point of view, it appears to be showing that it does nothing at all! Plus, since this thing is billed as a "reflection" filter, it seems rather pointless to test it in a room which, by design, has NO REFLECTIONS AT ALL! That kind of is the purpose of an anechoic chamber, last time I checked: a room with no reflections. Why would you test a device that kills reflections in a room where there aren't any reflections that can be killed??? That would be like testing a fantastic new waterproofing product in the middle of the Atacama desert, and then when the product doesn't get wet claiming that it works really well.... :) How come they didn't test it in a reverb chamber? I wonder why.... Can you say "hype"!? If that thing works at all, it is just the little remaining metal plus the foil reflecting some of the highs, and the foam absorbing a fraction more. It might well do something for the highs, but I simply cannot see it doing a damn thing to the lows, which as we all know is where the REAL problems are in most rooms. So the only conclusion that you can come to from perusing the add, is that if you happen to have an anechoic chamber on hand, then it is the perfect device for you, since it won't change anything at all! :shock: I sure can see why every studio needs one! :) - Stuart -
