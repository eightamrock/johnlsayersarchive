len-morgan2 October 2006#4
You might want to check all of the other points around the door and make sure they are sealed as good as can be. For example, the joint between the drywall and the wood that makes up the door frame. The area around your frame should be calked on both sides and airtight. I know from my studio build that I had extrememly well sealed doors all the way around including a double sealed "bank vault" construction (except the bottom of the door) and while the sound was reduced with the door closed it really wasn't very sound proof. Just the small gap at the bottom of the door was letting all the sound out. Once I sealed that with a Pemko drop down seal, the situation improved dramatically. I'm not sure if you're ever going to get the sound of drums in the next room out completely but if you've got even little gaps between the wall and the door frame, it's going to be much worse. len

len-morgan2 October 2006#8
You might be able to use a RadioShack SPL meter (about $50) or something similar. You'd set up a speaker in the tracking room, make a measurement, then close the door and measure the level from the other side. The difference between the two readings (in dB) is the isolation the door (and walls) are providing you. The only problem with this method is that the RS meter will only go down to 50 dB so if you door is doing much of anything, it's going to be less than that (and therefore a useless measurement). If you could boost the signal in the room to around 100 dBSPL inside, you could measure at least a 50 dB isolation which would be VERY good. Expect about 40 for a well made door system. len

Probably not. If you've already got a solid core door, that's most of the isolation you're going to get. diviolin, Welcome to the forum! I'll answer your question but PLEASE update your profile so we have some idea of where you are in the world. Thanks! I'm not sure about ordering from Zero International but I can help with the other one. Your first problem is that it's Pemko. It through me too for a long time (and I had been to their site before). pemko.com will get you started. I found that you cannot get order from them though and have to go through a local rep to get pricing and place your order. I bought 5 of their drop down door seals (4301 model I think), and they custom cut each one for you. You can cut them up to two inches yourself but I'd recomend letting them do it for you. There is no charge and it doesn't take them any longer. My local rep did not stock these locally so they had to come from the factory in Tn, but total time from order to arrival was about 10 days. I got 4 that were 36" or less and one that was 48" or less and they cut them down to the right size. It is very important to note that if you are going to semi-mortise this model, you measure EXACTLY the width of the door. If you are going to surface mount them (like I did) you have to specify them 1/8" LESS than the distance between the door stops. FWIW, my 36" inchers were $57/each and the 48" was $75 all of which included the custom cutting. As I recall, Zero International was about 5 times this much. len
