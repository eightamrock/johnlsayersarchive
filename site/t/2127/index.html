No insult intended, but tell me your joking. You want to make a purchase in 12 hours and are now asking for research material from a forum where people may or not be around to answer your questions, and you want (maybe?) back up data or reference data to study/analyze?
I'm trying to decide what insulation to buy, and their differences
Ignoring for a moment the differences, what you want is the type of insulation generally designed for in wall installation for for sound and fire rating improvement. These materials are approximately 2.5 lb/ft^3. Quiet zone is one of them, Roxul's "Safe N Sound" is another. Also Thermafiber SAFB and Fibrex SAFB. Just about every insulation company has a product in this category. For a detailed explanation of why this density: http://www.usg.com/Design_Solutions/2_3_7_insulationperf.asp The NRC tested 350 walls including variations in insulation. The report IR-761 (huge!) showed that rock wool (mineral fibre in the report) was slightly better, in the densities they tested. http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/fulltext/ir761/ This summary on the insulation types based on IR-761is from CMHC 02-108 (sorry no url, trying to get this to you ASAP)
• Generally, at low frequencies, the increase in the transmission loss of a partition obtained by adding a sound absorptive material inside its cavity is equivalent regardless of the material used. Above 250 Hz, mineral fiber and blown cellulose give the best results; mineral fiber insulation provides slightly better transmission losses than glass fiber, especially around the critical frequency. Also, in the case of glass fiber, a greater transmission loss can be achieved by using denser batts.
So what should I go with, and how much of a price difference is there in these products?
You have the criteria; 2.5lb/ft^3 insulation. Prices are impossible to detail. They vary wildly from region to region depending on location of factories, local raw material (for the insulation near the factory), amount of competition, and the housing market activity in an area. You have several leads in this post. Call the building suppliers and ask in general terms (IE. fire rated sound absorption insulation around 2.5 lb/ft^3) and note the prices. Some companies get a large premium for their product because of their company's name brand recognition.
