Ro5 April 2009#5
Measuring a room, acoustically, isn't the easiest job; reading them plots needs some study. How to adjust your room accordingly is another matter. Let's start with the beginning. To get a "baseline" (the starting point of your acoustical adventure) of the room, remove EVERYING from the room except for the speaker and the measuring mic. Do the test(s) and save the plots. You need to do more than just 1 test, take a few snapshots and overlay them to get a average of the plots. Then you need to dress your room with absorbing materials like basstraps, broadband panels and alike. But before you do any construction; read this forum and follow all the links. Pay attention to Ethan's site as well. (you'll find it while reading the acoustics section for example). Use the equipment you have OR you're gonna end-up with. So, if yer going to spend some money on a descent soundcart, do it prior to the baseline test. Same goes for speaker (that is: if you were planning on buying new speakers anyway) Thaz all, folks. Read and learn. happy reading :)

Soundman202013 April 2009#13
4" Rock wool, Muslin fabric, wood frames.
That should work OK. I just noticed in your photos that your speakers are sitting ON your desk. That might be an issue! The best place is to put them on really massive decoupled stands just behind the desk, rather than on the desk itself, to avoid both resonance issues in the desk, and also to reduce comb filtering from the desk surface itslef. Do you have some kind of decoupling treatment underneath those speakers? In those photos it looks like there might be something, but it's hard to see what. - Stuart -
