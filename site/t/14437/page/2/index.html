To make it simple if you looked at the sine wave of the two power legs they are out of phase in the case of 220-240.
Out of phase as compared with what??? Neutral? Ground? Each other? That would only be the case if you are talking about three-phase power, which is unlikely to be the situation in a home studio. In normal single-phase 220 volt systems, such as those used in South Africa, Chile, and most likely India too, you have a "live" or "hot" or "phase" conductor, and you have a neutral conductor, which is usually tied to ground at the distribution panel. So there is no "out of phase" situation across live and neutral, nor from live to ground.
You need a line conditioner to correct this if it is coming in that way.
A line conditioner is not necessarily going to fix phasing issues when deriving single phase from 3-phase. A simple isolation transformer can do the job just as well, and probably much cheaper.
there is a certain amount of distortion that is "to spec" per the power company so they won't do anything about it so that's why we are installing a HUGE conditioner where I work.
If you have serious waveform distortion problems with your mains supply, then probably the best you can do is to go for a true on-line dual-conversion UPS. It takes in pretty much any garbage you feed it, over a very broad range, and simply uses that to charge the batteries. Then the battery power is used to run an inverter that creates a brand new pure clean sine wave for the output. Nothing better. Cleans up power like you wouldn't believe. Beats a simple conditioner hands down, and they aren't all that expensive at all. A decent one for an average home studio shouldn't cost no more than a thousand dollars or so. (Or maybe around two grand, if you have a lot of heavy equipment). I use a simple 1 kVA or 3 kVA UPS for live sound gigs regularly, if I have any suspicions about the feed I'm getting.
