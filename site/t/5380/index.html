Hi, I recently moved into a house on a large corner lot. One of the reasons I chose it was becuase I wanted to build a stand-alone studio on it. In discussing design ideas with friends one suggested this site, which has turned out to be informative to say the least. Because of the way my lot is laid out, the easiest place for me to locate it is in the rear corner. One of my issues I'm trying to deal with is control room symmetry. From the reading I've done here over a few days it seems pretty important, but I'm just trying to figure out how to place things and thought I'd seek fresh opinions. Any input would be appreciated! knightfly8 January 2006#10
It's not so much the parallel walls that will make the difference (you'll have standing waves no matter WHAT the angle of your walls, they're just harder to figure out ahead of building it) - but you need to reconsider the ratio of length/width/height in your CR to keep modal buildups from being so unbalanced that they color the room sound. Try a google search on Louden, Bolt, and Sepmeier to get some charts on "ideal" room ratios - when these are right, the room will still have modes but they will be balanced enough NOT to cause serious problems with response in the room. When figuring room ratios for a room with splayed walls, use the average wass-wall distance - I.E., the rear of your CR is 14 feet and the front is 10 feet wide; use 12 feet as the width for ratio calculations. Drums - unless you're willing to give up at least 12" for total wall thickness, don't deceive yourself into thinking you can play whenever you want to - the same goes for just using the wall construction you outlined. You'll need more mass than that for your outer layer, no extra air spaces, separate frames for the inner walls, and 3 layers of 5/8" gypsum wallboard on the inner frame. Your outside door should NOT enter directly into the drum room because doors are one of your weakest points in ANY sound-isolated space. Stucco, most of which is gypsum and cement, has almost the same mass as gypsum wallboard and so about the same isolative qualities. If you use specs for gypsum, you'll be safe. Because you're building from scratch, you can pretty easily make the building LOOK like your house without it being as poorly isolated as your house; you just need to know ahead of time that everything UNDER the skin needs to be build DIFFERENT. If you take your time and don't rush it, read as much here as you can CONTINUALLY before, during and after your project, wait for answers before committing to doing something a particular way, you can reach your "drum anytime" goal, especially if you're NOT an "I kin beat these things harder than Metallica" kind of guy - the louder you play (and the pickier your neighbors) the more time and money it will take... Steve
