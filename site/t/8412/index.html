gullfo17 March 2007#2
maybe you can do it in stages - try the across the corner absorber first - if it gives you the desired result - you're done. if you find you need more absorption - stack the corner frame with enough insulation to make it a superchunks. then again maybe the resonator will be good from a function and form pov and you won't have to pull out the superchunks to reclaim some air space, but it would require more work. you could use a 19 mm (3/4") plywood or 25mm (1") MDF routed with the slats needed. stain as needed.

drfrankencopter18 March 2007#4
I wouldn't assume that there is no HF in the corners behind your monitors....what about bounce off the rear wall, and then again off the front wall? What's the length of the room in question? If the net acoustic distance (i.e. round trip length from your monitors to the rear wall and back to the front wall and then to the mix position) is greater than say 30 feet you're probably ok with a hard faced trap. Otherwise, go for a soft faced trap. My experience in small rooms has been that imaging is greatly improved by doing broadband (i.e. thick) treatments on the front wall. Cheers, Kris

If it were my room, I would probably start with superchunks in all corners, assuming you have a good RFZ. Once that was done, I'd see where the room still needed help, and design slat resonators around those specific frequencies.
gullfo19 March 2007#7
the form and function pov (point of view) - function meaning design and building the resonator to address the trapping and any problem frequencies - and form = how it looks.... ;-) if you find too much HF bounce off the resonator you can cover a portion of it with some cloth or cloth wrapped cotton batting (thin).
