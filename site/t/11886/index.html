There's nothing wrong with using wood for building an isolated room. It has mass, just as gypsum.
Soundman20206 April 2009#3
Wood is good too, provided that you make it thick enough. It will still stop sound fine, if there is plenty of mass. The reason that gypsum board (drywall) is recommended so much here, is because it is usually cheaper (per kilogram) than wood in most places. Perhaps where you live wood is cheaper, and in that case, use wood, sure! Plywood, MDF, OSB will all work, if there is enough mass. Your wood will probably need to be a bit thicker than gypsum board, to get the same amount of mass, since wood is not quite as dense as gypsum board, but it will still work, and the same basic principles still apply: Just build your room as two-leaf "Mass-Air-Mass" system, sealed airtight, following all the other usual precautions, and it will be fine. - Stuart -

Soundman20206 April 2009#5
A ready-to-assemble kit might not be good enough. The floor probably will not be able to take the weight that you need to put on it to get decent isolation. Also, thin wooden panels will probably resonate and vibrate loudly, in sympathy with some frequencies in your music, causing problems both inside and outside. A wooden shed will work, but it needs to be very solid, very tough, built on solid foundations that can support the weight. I wish there was a way to bypass the laws of physics and build a good studio very cheap, but the laws of physics are fixed, and you cannot get round them. They wont go away, no matter how hard you wish! For good isolation, you need mass. There are no shortcuts. Mass needs support, lots of mass needs lots of support, so you cannot put it on a thin floor or skimpy foundation. It needs a TOUGH floor, with hefty, solid foundations. etc. - Stuart -

Ro7 April 2009#10
Buying a shed-kit might be something to consider for a basis. From there you can isolate the wood-shed. Mass is needed. So adding GB on the inside is a good plan. Caulking all gaps is another. If you've got the budget and know how, you might consider bricks. It's all up to you. The density of "wood" differs a lot. MDF, for example, is about the same as GB (mass wise). Your average wood will prolly be a bit lower in mass (kg/m3). It's all about mass. GB is heavy and cheap.
