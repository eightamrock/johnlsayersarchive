I'm using this space to think out loud for now. Feel free to offer advice but I'm still in the info gathering phase and wanted to put stuff in one place. I am only able to go out to the new country house on the weekends currently (work in a different city) but I have all my construction friends coming up the last week of December to implement all the plans I'll be working on until then. I've gone through three apartments in the last 5 years.... each had it's only bedroom converted to a upper class ghetto studio (auralex but no isolation). It was largely for my own writing and production but I badly want to sell services from mixing to scoring. The room does have 2 external walls of the prefab house. The entire ceiling mimics the slope of the roof. The bad stuff... -Both the outer walls are thin paneling trim nailed right to the studs. I can hear/feel engine rumble from the sparse residential (gravel) road. -The house is on stands not directly on a poured foundation. (I will get under it this weekend with a flashlight.) -The closet messes up the back wall. The good stuff... -The shape of the room! Slopes away from front wall mixing position.. yay! -It's mine! What I really want... to be able to record vocals and mix (DAW) in both stereo and surround. I would give up on the surround if it compromised stereo mixing by a large amount. It going to be me alone most of the time so if I were to build a vocal booth it would be me running back and forth (or shooting my mac remote through a window :D). My budget to fix up this room is around $2,000USD I could stretch it hard and put off other upgrades and possibly go to 2500. I have a positive ton of auralex foam 2"wedges and pyramids as well as 8 2' bass traps. I understand that wall surface treatment is absorptive and diffusive but does nothing for isolation and I will have to mount new walls on the studs. Right now I'm leaning towards 2 layers of 5/8 drywall on a resilient channel. I would keep all four walls separate from each other with acoustic caulk. I'm still researching ceiling options.

j.hines15 January 2008#8
Well, I've been laboring on this room for a while now. I decided to leave the 3/8" up and put a layer of 1/2" on top of it on every wall but the back one. The back wall (tallest one) has been insulated and covered with 5/8" drywall. I bought stopgap for all the drywall seams and put fresh sealant on the window. The room is MUCH improved. The reasoning to not gut and rewall was to keep the house in salable and livable condition. Still transmission has not been a problem and I don't foresee it coming up. My next step (hopefully next week) will be to figure out what sort of absorption/diffusion treatment needs to go where. I know the general layout of foam (heavy toward the mix position and lighter as you move back or down) but I want to tune this small room though as best I can. The slope makes for some tricky placement if I buy premade stuff.
