knightfly16 March 2005#2
So, you're ending up with a single pane of glass sandwiched between two wood frames, with a wood filler around the perimeter of the glass, is that right? Is that ALL you will have in your window? How thick is the actual glass? Laminating MDF to a hollow core door - first off, these typically only have maybe 1/8" thick skins on either side of a cardboard honeycomb inside, with solid wood around the perimeter to fasten hinges, locksets, etc, to; so nailing or screwing the MDF wouldn't work too well from a physical standpoint; from an acoustic standpoint, you don't want metal fasteners running through the multiple layers of a sound barrier if you can help it, they flank noise through otherwise decent material. Probably one of the better ways of laminating MDF onto a door is to use Resorcinol glue; it's a 2-part glue made up of a liquid and a powder, and the stuff is amazing. http://www.dap.com/techbulletins/00030205.pdf The downside is that it takes quite a lot of pressure and at least 70 degrees; I would get some large C clamps and clamp the edges of the door HARD using long 2x4's on edge for stiffness, and put C clamps every foot along the edges; doing a door would require about 16 large C clamps, which very few dedicated wood workers even own. An alternative is, if you have some heavy saw horses and/or a big stack of gypsum wallboard around, you could stack about 20 sheets of wallboard on top of the door til it's dry. Or, you could use contact cement... Steve

knightfly18 March 2005#4
Solid doors, even with perfect seals, only end up at maybe STC 32 for a 1-3/4" exterior, SLAB type, solid core door; since it is solid, for a single door you're looking at mass law for the TL values; so increasing the door from 1-3/4" to 2-3/4" will only increase the performance by about 2-3 dB - generally not worth the effort of adding the MDF. If you have the space, you'd get a LOT better performance by adding an air lock and a second door... Steve
