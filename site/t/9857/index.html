Rob James wrote:
1. Is it possible to deaden a room too much? Because my intention is to completely cover the Control Room in Acoustic foam and place foam bass traps behind me.
it is defintely possible to kill off the room high frequency content and end up with mud. http://primacoustic.com/pics/australis_3D.gif shows the manuf recommended installation. put the bass traps in the front corners first. use the foam sparingly to break up echos between the walls and floor-ceiling (use a checkerboard pattern when possible).
Rob James wrote:
2. Is the proper placement of the bass traps, as I envision it in my room schematic? (See Control Room pic below) Or is it as this local manufacturer's diagram shows? (scroll down to see it) I always thought they were supposed to be behind me?
put the traps in the front corners. put foam on the first reflection points on the side walls, ceiling, and back walls. since there are no real published specs ontheir site its hard to even suggest that this product works at all but try to make the best of it
Rob James wrote:
3. How do I proceed? Do I measure my room first, with Roger's Digital Inspector, then only apply the acoustic foam in places? Or just completely cover my Control Room, and analyze the room after?
do not completely cover your room! start out with the bare minimum. 2 traps in the front corners, a few squares on the side walls and ceiling at the first reflection points. and use a checkerboard pattern of squares and wall on the back wall. how does it sound? still too much reverb? well, its partly due to the wrong materials... but since it is what you have, add another checkerboard set of squares on the back part of the ceiling. still too much reverb? try some on the back side walls as well. for the back corners - get some rockwool or other heavy rigid insulation and trap them more properly than the foam will.
