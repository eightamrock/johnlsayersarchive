We can handle specific questions but multiple questions suggest that you have not read near as much as you will have to in order to get to a point were you ask, specific questions ;) EDIT: Call me a glutton for punishment: "3.) Unfortunately I didn't find the STC rating of John's inside-out wall here." A walls rating on the STC point system is dependant on the products that it is made of. The inside out wall is not a static building appliance. It can have 2X4 wood framing or 2X6 wood framing and in some cases it can have metal framing. It can have a 16 inch layout or a 24 inch layout. The panels could be 1/2 inch, 5/8inch it could be either of those X2 or it could be a variation of the three. Specific questions get specific answers...you have to know what it is you are going for, in a very educated method, in order for any one to make an educated guess. That said, the STC rating on John's inside-out wall can go anywhere from 20 to 47 (by it's self), 40 to 60ish in the proper assembly, the transmission loss will increase with the higher STC rating often but the LF has been known to suffer >if< the air space is particularly small due to a rigid air spring. If that doesn't help, I am not surprised :) It didn't help me the first time I read something similar either.

130 dB(C) :shock: !!! :!: That would make you the loudest drummer on the entire planet! 130 dB(C) is roughly the loudest sound you can expect to get from a large tower of speakers at a rock concert, measured 1 meter away. As a drummer, you might well be putting out 115 dB(C), but I totally doubt you will ever managed to get 130 dB(C) out of drum kit (unless maybe you put a stick of dynamite inside it...). At 130 dB(C) you'd be in pain over most of your body, and you'd suffer irreversible hearing damage in about 22 seconds. Give or take a second or two...
I would like to use them for the ventilation of the studio rooms, but I have to do some sort of acoustical isolation.
Search the forum for "silencer" and you will find a lot of information on how to do that.

5cm is very thin for your air gap. It should be at least 10cm, preferably more.

2 cm fiberglass is also nowhere near enough. You should fill the entire cavity between the outer leaf and inner leaf.
double layer gypsum (12mm & 16mm)
Forget the thin (12mm) drywall. Use only 16mm. Also consider using Green Glue in between the layers.
-> 10cm timber frame
so you are planning to build inside-out walls? Good!
If floor has to be floated, then 5cm timber frame on neoprene (filled with RockWool) -> 2 layers of particle board -> laminate floor (or carpet).
Read this: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 As you can see, what you propose for "floating" your floor simply will not work. Wast of time, money, space, and will most likely make things worse, not better.
3.) Unfortunately I didn't find the STC rating of John's inside-out wall here. What will it be when everything is sealed to air-tight?
Like Brien said: That all depends on how you build it! Build it wrong, you will only get maybe STC-30 out of it. Build it right and you can get well over STC-60. By the way, STC is a lousy way of specifying isolation for studios: it tells you nothing at all about low frequency isolation, which is where all your problems will be.
4.) Am I right, that each room must have it's own ceiling
Yes, if you want good isolation. And each ceiling must rest ONLY on its own inner-leaf walls, NOT touching any outer leaf walls at all, nor the outer-leaf ceiling.
6.) Am I right, that the outer insulation (the one on the gypsum situated to the concrete wall) must have a low density (e.g. 20kg / m³) whilst the inner one must have a higher one (e.g. 60kg / m³)
No. Use 48 kg/m3 in the cavity, and whatever density is needed for each type of treatment inside the room, which might or might not be the 48, or 20. But 60 is probably too high.
8.) How can I build a silencer to distribute one outlet on two studio rooms, without distribution sound waves between the studio rooms and the rest of the house?
With very long, very convoluted ducting, with multiple 90° bends and duct liner on the inside! It would be much easier and cheaper to build two separate silencers, one for each room outlet. And another two separate silencers, one for each room inlet. Four in total. Or rather, six in total, since you have three rooms, not two. I think your "footprint 2" looks like a good start, except that I think you are wasting space with the soundlock. Maybe if you were to move your CR to the middle, so that the main door is in the middle of your CR rear wall, you'd be able to put your live room on one side and your drum booth on the other. - Stuart -

"but shouldn't each layer of one leaf have a different density, " In theory. Refraction (changing the direction of travel) is what is being thought of when layers of different density are suggested. Since it is two fold, small in how it helps us and adds an un-needed level of difficulty in trying to establish just how it helps us, it is most always recommended to just use the amount of mass required to reach your isolation goal.
Soundman202021 February 2010#6
So when the concrete wall is the outer leaf and the gypsum is the inner leaf, I should fill the whole space between with insulation? Will this still count as an air-gap?
For any MSM you need to fill the air gap with insulation, not just concrete and drywall. If you do not fill the air with insulation, you could be losing anywhere up to about 16 dB of isolation. The insulation serves several purposes, but mostly it forces down the resonant frequency of the MSM system, and damps resonance in the cavity. Without it, the cavity itself resonates, and the MSM frequency stays high, both of which reduce isolation. Don't think of it as an air-gap (although it is often called just that). Technically, the "gap" between the leaves is a damped spring. The air is the spring, and the insulation is the damping. That's why the more correct term for this type of wall is MSM (mass-spring-mass) rather than MAM (mass-air-mass).
What is "Green Glue"? Could you explain it or name a manufacturer? I don't know what I have to search for here in Austria.
It is made by the "Green Glue" company http://www.greengluecompany.com/index.php Despite the name it is NOT actually glue: it never dries. It's only purpose is to create a damping layer inside the wall, between the layers of drywall, which greatly increases isolation in the low frequency region. There are no substitutes. This is the only company that makes it and also provide independent laboratory test reports. A couple of other companies claim to make something similar, but they never seem to be able to provide any independent technical data on their products... It is expensive, but well worth the cost when you need excellent isolation in low frequencies. which is what you will need for your drums. Call them or e-mail them to find out about distribution in Austria. - Stuart -
