kendale3 June 2006#18
Aloha valgaer, Here's a couple of thoughts: - extend the side wall slots to past the mix position - add rear corner absorber to right side rear corner - extend rear side wall absorbers to meet rear corner absorbers As far as setting up your monitor situation, here's a few bits of info I've collected from this forum: - Monitors placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. - Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) - Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. - Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. - Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position - Monitor angles 60 degrees (30 degrees each) - Monitor height – woofer at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement: either sit higher or lower if necessary) Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

kendale3 June 2006#20
Aloha valgaer,
My main concern are the panels that are behind the speakers, the ones angled, what kind of bass trap do they have to be?, searching the forum I`ve come across with some designs, the ones that John has on this site as corner panels and the ones for sofit the monitors. Which one of them are best for my studio layout?
That depends. Here's a couple of things (of perhaps several) to consider: - What's your preferance - soffit or not? - Depending on how much room you have to work with, how wide can you make those panels while still maintaining a 30 degree angle? The baffle extension (the face of the soffit) should be as large as you can make it, up to about 45" in each direction from the woofer in a system with a LF cutoff of around 45 hZ - There's a formula based on LF cutoff that ensures full 2 pi response for the woofer if the baffle is at least the size obtained in the formula's solution. Perhaps a search would prove fruitful for you. In my case, there were several compromises along the way, but all in all, the improvements to the sound of the studio are alot better than what I had prior. As only a personal opinion, after working for five years without soffiting my speakers, the difference it makes soffited is quite amazing. If you care to check out the threads try here: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic ... ght=#15598 and here: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4091 Aloha 8)
