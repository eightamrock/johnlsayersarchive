I think it's a good plan. The thicker the absorptive material, the lower the frequency to which it will absorb. Thicker is probably better if you can afford it. Also, I'd space it flush with the bottom of the joists, ie, leave an airgap between the insulation and the top of the ceiling. This will also increase the frequencies to which it absorbs. As far as plastic bags, you can do that if you are worried about this treatment making the room too dead. Though I do recommend leaving certain useful parts of the room fully absorptive, ie, a cloud above the mix position (to absorb first reflections), as well as above any choice recording spots (ie, above where you will be recording drum kits, acoustic guitars, vocals, etc). If you add the plastic it will start to reflect some highs, and it could possibly increase bass absorption a bit depending. Any fabric that is breatheable will work. A lot of people like Guilford of Maine fabric, it's sort of the industry standard. Expensive, but quite nice.
