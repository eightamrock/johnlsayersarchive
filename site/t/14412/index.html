rod gervais24 May 2010#3
I would not worry about this - first off - the slab is sitting on a surface of earth - which tends to act to damp it - 2nd - the mass itself, there the is the fact that you are going have to penetrate your rubber isolator to attach the bottom plate to hold it in place....... That aside - yes it take a certain material - with a certain density - which has to be calculated according to the weight of your assembly - and I have done this for studios sitting on the 1st floor of a building - not using rubber but using special isolators made specifically for this purpose - and believe me when I tell you that it is not what you think it is - the materials have to be sized (by this I mean the hardness of the materials) for all of the various loads imposed on them to work - if you use super doors as a part of your construction - then the loads can run anywhere from almost 300 pounds to as little as 60 pound in corners - and there is no one material that will cover that wide a range of loads... critical for is to work is that the compression of the should be more than 25% and less than 75% in order to reach the materials center frequency (which should be 10Hz or less) if you over compress you have a transmitter instead of an isolator - and if you under compress you (again) have a transmitter........ Save yourself a bunch of aggravation and a whole lot of money (to get it right) and forget trying to solve a problem that really isn't going to be a problem........

Ted White25 May 2010#8
There's two aspects of this situation. #1 Sealing. There is the need to seal the underside of the bottom plate to guard against flanking. If the floor is smooth, sealant as previously described will work great. If the floor is terribly uneven, perhaps it should be leveled first, then sealed. #2 Decoupling. As Rod clarified, this isn't worth considering. It's intuitive that there may be some goodness to be had, and it's in our human nature to want to deploy more products, but in reality, there will be no practical benefit.
