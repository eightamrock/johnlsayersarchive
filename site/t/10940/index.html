John Hile2 September 2008#19
Hey Matt, That is a good question. sound works like water in an aquarium in that if there is a leak (flanking) the water (noise) will find its way out. Coupling allows a direct path for sound to get into other parts of the structure. Of course in the real world some things can not be avoided and if most of the flanking can be reduced, there would be gains in performance. If the corners are the tough spots and they end up being a coupling point, at least you have taken a majority of the contact out by cutting. Once you load your wall with drywall and damping (Green Glue) the amount of noise getting to those coupling points would be reduced as well. John

John Hile15 September 2008#22
Earshot wrote:
As a somewhat DIY alternative to 'green glue', i used an rubber acoustic underlay on the walls, ceiling and floor, before adding a layer of triboard.
Hi Matt, Your room looks good, and should be OK for sound isolation in that you did use 2 panels. I just wanted to mention that rubber underlay is not a DIY alternative for Green Glue. The rubber underlay you put up added a bit of mass but does not accomplish damping, and sound will go through the rubber. Adding damping would have helped control low end frequency and smooth out the coincidence dip far far better.
