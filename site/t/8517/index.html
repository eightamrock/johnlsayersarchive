gullfo1 April 2007#4
http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8410 you'll need fresh air in order to breath... :shock: so you'll definitely need to include this plus calculate any load change due to fresh air. although you may not have this issue with the oversized unit, too large a unit can result in short run times and therefore possible wide swings in temp and humidity. I'm working on correcting this in my home because someone thought that 7 tons of AC would be better than 3.5 tons and we only needed 3.5 tons... go figure. you'd think a highly reputable HVAC company would know better. but back in the 80's (before we bought the house) this was the thinking...) I'm spec'ing a new variable speed multiple zone system to keep the refresh, static pressure, air velocity, run time, and economy aspects in balance. there's no single "just use this plan and it will work for you", you have to look at the situation and decide on your budget, comfort, and whatever your HVAC engineer recommends, as well as some of the links provided (and Rod's book), to get a sense of what will work for you.
sharward1 April 2007#5
Agreed... Oversized units not only have those disadvantages, but the uncontrolled humidity can cause mold, and the unit would be very inefficient because it's never given the opportunity to "warm up" and hit its stride. Every HVAC design needs to be totally customized to the unique requirements of the situation. Consider a heat recovery ventilator (HRV) for fresh air.
