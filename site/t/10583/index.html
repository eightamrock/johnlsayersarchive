xSpace7 May 2008#2
A room-in-a-room would be creating a four leaf wall system. If temporary is what you want, why not use plywood instead of sheetrock? I would also look deeper into your design. if you do not know what a leaf is, then you have work to do. Also, if you build this wall, any area that you cut it, like where the electrical panel is, will reduce the walls ability to contain sound. Good luck.

gullfo30 May 2008#12
if the drywall is on the inside (of say a 2x4 frame (3.5"), and the outside wall of the garage is on a 2x4 frame (3.5"), and you have a 1" gap between frames, you have a ~8" deep airspace. if you put the drywall (2x 5/8") on the outside of the inner frame (to create say inside-out walls for treatments - and a 1.5" gap between frames) then you have ~3.75" of air. having drywall on both sides of the inner frame and a layer on the outside of the outer frame means you have a 3 leaf system which will have reduced isolation value at some frequencies when compared to a 2 leaf system.
AVare30 May 2008#13
The big issue with sound transmission for music is low frequencies. Have a look at fig. 5, pdf page 10 in NRC IR 586 The walls with two layers of drywall added (which means greatest mass also) have the worst low frequency isolation. Andre

chamfer11 July 2008#19
Ok got some updates for you guys. I've been building up the 2x4 stud framing with zero carpentry skills haha. But I've followed all the construction advice I've found here, online and with a couple of construction books. I used pressure treated sole plates, double pt top plates, 16 o.c. with fire blocking about 48" a.s.f. (above sub floor, lol, that's a new one)I used white pine for the long walls and yellow pine for the short walls. Since the heavy door will be on one of the shorter walls, I used this yellow pine for more strength. The very top plates of the long walls extend 3-1/2" beyond the lower top plate on either sides to interlock with the short walls for more stability. I used 3/4" mdf to sandwich the thick mineral wool (8 lbs./ft) I placed in the garage door recesses. Against the better judgment of most of you on here (as I was creating a third leaf, yet I would still argue that flimsy door could actually even be considered a leaf) This garage door sucked and had nooks and crevices all over the place and was super thin. Even now before the buildout of my room, , I can go outside now and hardly hear a thing when I have someone play the percussion inside. I sealed all the gaps around the mdf with "stopgap". I'm building the door frame with the double header construction (long side up) with 1/2" plywood sandwiched in between for increased strength for the heavy door and for more load bearing of the ceiling. The rough opening is 38" width with 82-1/4" height from floor. I got a quote from the depot for a pre-hung flush solid core 3'x6'8 door with 5-1/4" flat jamb for $174. The solid core slab of the same size was $46 but I am a bit worried about building a frame correctly and chiseling of the hinge recesses and door lock. I figured I'd just pay the difference. will lay the finished floor first though. Any suggestions are welcome.
