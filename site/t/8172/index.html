knightfly11 February 2007#6
I'm not that certain this is true - the best way to know if you need to cut your slab (and if you do, you'll need to do a LOT more than just cut it, you'll need to cut a section out and pour a new footing) is to lay down on the concrete at the neighbor's area, and put your ear against the concrete - then have a helper bang on the concrete where your studio will go with a hammer - preferably use TWO types of hammer, one a normal metal one and the other a rubber hammer. If you hear the hammer loud and clear, then whatever is beneath the slab is NOT damping the concrete enough, and your neighbor will NOT be happy with your studio unless he/she LOVES your music.... Steve

sharward7 March 2007#23
Honestly, I'm thinking that you probably do not need to worry about the shared slab being a significant structure-borne flanking source of airborne sound. We've seen hammer tests before, but they really don't apply in my opinion because a hammer strike is impact noise which is entirely different. Think: knocking on a door is going to be a lot louder to the occupants of a house than an airborne sound of equal intensity. Also, hollow concrete block walls are not two leaf as previously thought. They are considered as one leaf. Filled concrete block walls are in fact better because they're heavier and more massive. --Keith :mrgreen:
