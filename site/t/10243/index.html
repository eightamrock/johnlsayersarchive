I wanted to share some pics of the studio desk I just finished. I have been working on it for several months now when I could find the time. I designed it on Google Sketchup. I got the design idea from an Omnirax desk I saw on their site. It is made out of cabinet grade birch plywood. The top is made from ¾” birch with a 1/4” layer of cherry on top (a solid inch of cherry would have been way too cost prohibitive). The speaker stands are made from birch with a cherry vaneer on top. I used some ebony aniline dye for the bottom half and the sides of the speaker stands. The top and top of the speaker stands are all natural. I finished the top and the speaker stands with a diluted gloss polyurethane (I added a 50% mixture of mineral spirits so I could wipe it on with a cloth versus a sponge brush – it took much more time and many more coats, but it came out like glass). The bottom half was finished with a semi-gloss polyurethane and a final wipe of the gloss mixture I created. I added a small shelf in the back to hold all the cables, power supplies, etc. The desk is about 8 feet wide to give you an idea of dimensions. The boxes below and the speaker stands are all 19.125" wide thus allowing me to rack equipment as I get it. Once I have everything set up on it, build some bass traps, diffusors, and mid/high freq absorbers, I'll post a pic in the studio pic thread. For now, I just wanted to share the desk itself....

BradJacob wrote:

Here's the breakdown - roughly... 5 - 3/4 x 4x8 birch plywood = $250 1 - 1/4" Cherry plywood = $50 Cherry Vaneer and vaneer glue - $25 Aniline dye (ebony and cherry - the bottom of the top was stained a dark cherry color) - $20 Polyurethane - $40 Edge banding - $20 Bolts - $10 Figure 8 fasteners - $15 Sandpaper, glue, biscuits, steel wool, cotton wipes, gloves - $50 Total = $480....this is a rough guess I did this all in my head last night. I was way off. My apologies. Oh, and I take offense to you saying those are painted black :lol: Those are 'ebonized' with aniline dye. You can still see the grain in the wood. And, its a cherry top. Oh, and did I mention I spent $500 on a tablesaw and $200 on a biscuit joiner? Of course, those cannot be included in the cost as they will be used for many projects to come. The time was the most expensive thing here. The whole staining and finishing process seemed to take forever. For that glossy coat on top, I literally put about 7-8 coats of mineral spirit diluted poly on it. When I first started finishing the boxes, I used full strength poly with a foam brush. I now know this is a mistake. Air bubbles formed this way. The diluted poly takes more coats, but it goes on much smoother and no air bubbles. I would also recommend a rub down using #0000 steel wool between every other coat. Here's a pic of all the pieces that it took to build this thing.
