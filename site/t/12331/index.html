What do you think would be the ideal setup having these rooms to work with?
To start with, you should place apropiately your mixing position, which should be simetrical to the room. Also you'd want to face the shorter wall. In other words you should face the RED wall. I could go and go about where to put speakers, at what angle, that distance of your front wall, but seriously, where to put the bass traps, whats RFZ, etc. this has been covered A LOT on this forum. I suggest you make a search on mixing setup.
Our loudness factor isn't really an issue since the only time we're LOUD is on weekends and I have a deal with the other residents in the cul-de-sac and all are ok with it and a majority of the recording is done digitally. ( a reason why I want this iso booth so I can mic some REAL amps! )
Well, a guitar amp fully cranked could go up to 100 db easily, so you need isolation somehow. Im pretty sure that 8 hours of recording loud guitar amps in a weeked could make even you go nuts. Also you need isolation BETWEEN your rooms, otherwise you will listening to nothing but loud noise. I would suggest constructing a stud wall/drywall room on all the iso room perimeter, and hopefully you could build as well a second stud wall in your control room perimeter, making a decoupled two wall system. If you are not THAT anal about noise leakage between rooms, then maybe, just one room system (in your iso room) could isolate it decently, you could probably use at least 2 if not 3 5/8" drywalls. Yeah, its going to be just a 1 leaf systen between the rooms but thats better than nothing ! Actually, in my actual setup (which i'm about to change soon to a new place) I have a DIY vocal booth, made by the previous owner of the room I'm renting. It is a poorly made 1 leaf vocal booth, but a loud guitar amp doesnt leak THAT much to my control room, I can actually turn my monitors up a bit and I'm good. Its not perfect, and I can hear the real amp, but it works!
$300-$500 since we already have some material and I get free stuff from pops.
Thats not enough really for what you are telling us to achieve. What other materials you have? If you don't care about not isolating properly a iso booth, and having to record loud guitars with headphones, probably with a LOT of leakage from the noise source, thats ok with you, and you could probably spend those $300 on some DIY bass trap. However, this is an studio design forum, so its true that we are going to encourage you to do it the right way. Like I suggested before, because of your budget, you should look into building a simple wall room inside the iso room. With 2 5/8" sheets of drywall on each side, filling the inside with insulation. But thats gonna cost you more than 500 for sure, and you have to decide whether or not you want a double glass window etc.

Well, a guitar amp fully cranked could go up to 100 db easily, so you need isolation somehow. Im pretty sure that 8 hours of recording loud guitar amps in a weeked could make even you go nuts.
Very true! In fact, at a level of 100 dB, maximum safe exposure time is one hour. Beyond that, you are pretty much guaranteed of irreversible damage to your inner ear, with selective hearing loss. Most musicians don't realize this: Which is why most musicians are partially deaf. I don't mean that as an insult or a joke: I mean it absolutely seriously. I did some research on this a couple of years ago for a course I was teaching on sound systems, and came across numerous papers documenting partial deafness in musicians. One that I recall was a study done on a symphony orchestra, (in Boston, if I recall correctly) where it was found that all the violists suffer something like 30% hearing loss in their left ears. It isn't hard to figure out why... In any event, a big guitar amp cranked up high sure can produce 100 dB, and more too. An average drum kit played hard can easily put out 115 dB. Maximum exposure time before permanent irreversible hearing damage at a level of 115 dB, is seven minutes.... How many drummers know this? And of those who know it, how many take precautions? Javier is right: You need to isolate your rooms, all around. - stuart -
