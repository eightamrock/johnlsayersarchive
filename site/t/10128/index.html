Hello, I just discovered this site a few days ago and have spent many hours reading the various threads. I still have a few unanswered questions and am curious to see if anyone has any advice for the space I am moving into. SPACE (see attached) It is a 200sq' space on the 6th floor of an office building in Brooklyn, NY (see diagram). I have a hallway on one side, bathrooms on one side (right side on diagram), and people working in offices on the other 2 sides. The walls seem like they are standard office drywall. Speedo, Welcome to the forum. I've not posted advice on here in a while but here I go. 1. $2,500 to $3,500 seems like a real low number. I doubt you can get it done in that price range. 2. Please spend a few hours going through the forum and the SAE site. Please buy Rod's book available in the Stickies. Most of your questions have already been asked and answered many, many times. 3. Your not really giving us enough information to go on. As to your "design so far". 1) Leave the current walls untouched Most likely NO that will not be the recommendation 2) Build a standalone structure a few inches in from the original wall Maybe 3) Build an outer wall 2 layers of drywall - staggered By suggesting staggered stud I can only guess you are missing the MAM concept Mass Air Mass 4) 6" gap You mean AIR or space between Mass's or what? 5) Inner wall with 2 layers drywall - staggered studs It would be absolutely pointless to build a staggered stud walls on the outside walls of a new studio inside an existing structure. Staggered Stud could be used to divide rooms with in the studio space however most go for double non attaching walls where the studs are 1" apart. Then the Air space = that 1" plus the width of stud bays of both walls 3.5" X 2 ( a 2x4 is actually 1.5" x 3.5" so... you'd have an 8" air space. Insulation is included as air in the calculation. Again please take the time to read and study. Go find out specific things about the structure I'm sure you will wonder about as you study. Then come back with questions for which you can not find answers. I've not been on here for a while.
