rod gervais1 February 2005#9
Caulk - including acoustic caulk - is not intended to be installed with 3 point contact. This means (for example) the floor - the bottom of the drywall and the framing behind. For caulk to work properly - 2 point contact is required - this means just the floor and the drywall.......... here's a link to a manufacturer who makes backer rod as small as 3/16". for 1/4" joints you want a 3/8" backer rod. http://www.sandellmfg.com/SPEC%20DATA%2 ... %20Rod.pdf This way you don't have this large mass of limp caulk that wants to keep migrating "south". As far as the smell goes - it will eventually go away - just make certain the room is well ventilated or a while. If you did not use a backer rod - this problem won't just go away - you can always try to block it in with baseboard - but then the base has to run tight to the floor and you may introduce a flanking problem you didn't want. Rod

z60611 wrote:
Here's the new one. Any comments, change recommendations, better alternatives?
Z, Looks good - the only thing I might consider would be creating a shadow line with the molding. By that I mean - you tape out the walls/ceiling - paint them - and then drop the molding down 1/4" to 3/8" (more for ceilings higher than 8'. What this does is create a finished "shadow line" at the ceiling edge without pinning the wall to the ceiling with the molding. The idea of the caulk joints at the edges is 2-fold.......... 1) seal a sound path,,,,,,,,, 2) seperate the ceiling membrane from the wall membrane - which also helps to stop the transfer of flanking sound between the 2. (And - least anyone asks - yes - even if the wall/ceiling framing connects - this does help stop flanking transmission - at least everything that would transfer through the bodies of the membranes themselves. Rod
