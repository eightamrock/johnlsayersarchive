Jas6 February 2005#31
z60611 If you don't use plastic or metal (gal) corner strips under your plaster mud in the corners the joint will crack. If you must avoid flanking noise between the wall and ceiling (de-coupled) don't mud - use caulk. If you have already used mud, I would cover the joint completely with a moulding and caulk or gapseal the crack top and bottom. Air leakage due to a crack could provide a much more efficient transmission path for airborne noise than the outer wall and ceiling layers semi-coupled. I've been building plaster/timber walls for a long time. Make no mistake, this joint WILL crack unless you use some angle under the mud. Cheers Jas

Les Ismore10 February 2005#33
What we've finally ended up doing to fix this mess, (and it truly is a mess) is cut pieces of cardboard just a little wider than the gap between the floor and walls. We have pushed this cardboard into the gap thereby pushing the acoustic sealant goo backward to create a space to fill with sillicone. We will next put on a border trim and finish the room. This idea came from a carpenter friend and it has worked well and saved the day. To be honest with you even if I had seen the instructions for the backing plate which I'm guessing is somewhere around this site, if I was to do it again there is no way I would ever use this stuff anywhere near a studio. If some of the experts here have had actual success with this stuff, I respect that. However I think that to recomend that people who are building home studios use this goo to seal walls to floors and / or ceilings is a very big mistake. This stuff was never designed for this application and unless you are an expert and experienced with this, one should use a sealant that's actually designed for the application. In my opinion silicone does a much better job as long as you don't need to paint over it. It actually dries, doesn't make a huge mess, and will stay put. In your earlier description you talk about mudding over the acoustic sealant at joints. I don't see any way you can mud over this goo. But that's just me. Your mileage may vary. This is a great site and I have learned a lot of stuff here. I think for the amature builder though that this stuff should NOT be recomended. Thanks very much for all your efforts in trying to help me out of this. I really appreciate it.

"Another option would be to shove the acoustic sealant in a bit deeper and then seal the outside drywall with a layer of regular caulking" Is this the stuff you have been using: http://www.lepageproducts.com/products/ ... 9&plid=260 If so it will eventually dry and the smell should stop. The smell is caused by a solvent in the caulk to help it remain very flexible during application. It does dry and become less flexible(but still maintains much flexibility). It does take a long time to FULLY dry - if I had to guess I would say several weeks. I am not sure about it settling and separating from one surface or another but as you have noticed the stuff is very sticky and basically stays put. Now if a wall experiences shaking .... will it settle over time? Perhaps? One of this products main uses is with a vapour barrier that is applied over say 2x4's. This is a two surface contact. I do not think this material will seep out or it would not fulfil its purpose. For certainty you can call the company direct. I had called them several months back regarding this product and they seemed very helpful. I believe that this product should not be left exposed. Therefore your comment above regarding the application of regular caulking would seem appropriate. For cleaning purposes mineral spirits will clean it up excellently. I have spoken to one custom installer who recommended applying gobs of this stuff to the face of 2x4 studs just before applying drywall to create less contact (i.e. drywall screws will still provide contact). Did you only use this stuff at the bottom of your wall? Did you apply any to the upper corners between walls and ceiling or corners where your walls meet? If yes how did that work out for you? For what its worth I did use this stuff several months ago - not sure that it will get much dryer than this. The area was still exposed as it has not been closed off yet so I touched it and no material stuck to my finger. I pushed it much harder and yes some did stick to my finger. It appears to dry to a gummy like texture. Seams reliable.
