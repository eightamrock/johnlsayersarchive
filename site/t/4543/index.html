sharward22 October 2005#8
Great question about whether or not that extra wall is necessary. I predict that it is not, because you don't care about isolation in the hallway/bathroom area... But you probably do care along the stairwell, so maybe some of that red line would be beneficial. We'll see what John has to say about that. Anyway, I don't think the "control room and isolation booth doors banging into each other" situation will pass code. :roll: Here are some alternative ideas:
  1. Move the iso booth doors to the right and shrink the booth by changing the angle of its bottom wall to allow a swinging outer door to clear the cabinet.
  2. Move the iso booth doors to the right and rotate the cabinet so that it is flush against the right wall.
  3. Use dual sliding glass doors like the drum booth has.
Personally, I think option #2 would be best. Using sliding glass doors is probably a waste of money, unless there's value in the booth occupant being able to see the cabinet... :lol: ...And leaving the cabinet where it is may cause collisions between door and coffee-fetching humans. In any event, I would change the orientation of the control room outer door swing so that the hinges are in the corner. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward25 October 2005#16
I can't think of anything offhand that would be much better than the compromises you've already thought of... :roll: ...but, then again, my name isn't John and we all know I'm not as :idea: brilliant :idea: as he is -- perhaps he'll find a nearby phone booth, do a quick change of clothes, and then whip something up that will knock your socks off! :twisted: Meanwhile, perhaps you can do some more research into the code restriction that you've come up against. It would be good to learn what model codes your municipality is using, the section number, and the precise language. Sometimes there's the "quick answer" that says you can't do something, but then there's the "long answer" that says how you might be able to get around it. I don't hold a lot of hope for being able to wiggle out of it, but it's a worthwhile exercise, even if only just to learn the details of why it's not allowed. I'm sure it's egress in case of fire... Here's an example of "short answer" vs. "long answer." In my city, the "short answer" codes say habitable rooms require windows. However, the "long answer" codes say that as long as the room doesn't contain a closet and isn't used for sleeping, mechanical ventilation that does a full air exchange at least once every thirty minutes can be used instead. In any event, be very glad that you're going about this build legitimately... Imagine the horror if building it all, and even having use of it, only to find out later that it all has to be torn down to comply with an order from a government official! :shock: You're doing the right thing... And you will get through this episode of frustration. I speak from experience! 8) --Keith :mrgreen:
