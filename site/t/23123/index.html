Sometimes the small gaps are left on purpose, in order to vent the cavity between the drywall and concrete blocks. If you fill in this gap then you risk moisture being trapped inside the cavity forming condensation on the inner face of the concrete blocks. The gaps provide venting to help the moisture escape. Exterior walls and roofs are complicated when it comes to sound isolation since they either need to be sufficiently insulated so that the dew point happens way before reaching any interior surface, or in the case of cold assemblies they need sufficient venting so that any moisture/vapour that does either get into a cavity or condenses has a chance to escape to the outside world. Obviously, holes are not ideal although if small enough they become much less of an issue IF you have an inner shell which is completely air tight. I think before going forward we need to study exactly how your room is constructed in regards to its wall and roof assemblies otherwise you'll just be ripping it all out again if you get moisture problems in the future
