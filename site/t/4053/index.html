Hi Nick - welcome to the site - you guys amaze me with your command of English ;) Look - for a vox booth you want a room with a short reverbtime as you don't want room colouration added if you want an upfont, close vocal sound. therefore you need to treat the room so ALL frequencies reverbtime are lowered. For the high end - that's easy - egg cartons will do it :):) but to get the lower frequencies down is harder. the hardest frequencies to tame are those below 100hz - yet the human voice doesn't produce those frequencies - hence the LF rolloff switch on most quality mikes. So you'd be best to work on lowering from 100hz - 20khz region. Quality insulation like 703 will absorb well down to 500hz and further so your problem area is 100hz - 500hz. For these frequencies I use slot resonators as they not only absorb the low-mids they also reflect the high end so it's a matter of balancing these two absorbers in your room so your highs aren't wiped out totally (what people refer to as a DEAD room). Your low ceiling will be the closest wall to the singer so in a small room like yours it's best to wipe it out totally with a layer of 703. this leaves your other walls for treatment. the other advantage of slots is that they can be angled to break up the parallel walls in your room - slots on two of the walls will do that and if you have a timber floor you will have a balance between the absorbing surfaces (703) and the reflecting surfaces - timber floor and slots. Check out these slots I designed for a Van in Canada - They are made using veneer plywood and the slots are routed out with a router. the areas behind the slots are divided into separate chambers for each slot width. I hope this helps. cheers john
