Started laying out the actual framing plan this week. I'm still mulling over the double walls: that's quite a bit of space they take up in an already small space. They may be overkill for my intended purpose, still, I'd rather err on the side of quiet than make a nuisance of myself. I have some cool screenshots from my design file, but I need to resize them for the web before I can post them.
Image not preserved: GarageFraming.jpg
Bear in mind I haven't shown the outer shell or exterior doors in the pic - only the interior walls. I decided to add a door between the iso and mix room. I hated to poke a hole in that wall, but I didn't like the idea of only one exit from the mix room. In the event of a fire or other emergency, I'd rather go thru a door than thru the wall. I also considered a door with a window in lieu of the mix room window, but I don't want to be walking between the desk and the window wall because the cables from the mix desk would be a trip hazard. The diamond on the floor of the mix room is a reflection-free zone (I left the guide lines in for reference). The bass traps are obvious and the flat walls are slat resonators with 2'x2' absorbers strategically placed to maintain the RFZ. In the first set of design pics, I used angled walls in an attempt to direct reflections off the side/rear walls, but I couldn't find a solution that would seem to offer any advantage acoustically, although aesthetically I find them visually interesting. I also tried convex diffusers, but judging the ray tracing I deemed they might actually contribute secondary reflections, so I nixed that idea in the mix room, but I think I'll use them in the practice room on the common wall. Also, I'll probably start with double doors between the iso/mix room, but single doors elsewhere. I can always add them later if I find them necessary. I think this layout would also lend itself to surround production also, but I'm not into that yet. Comments, observations, or suggestions are welcome.

Soundman202014 March 2009#14
That puts me in a chair anywhere from 6-7 feet from the window wall - roughly in the center.
Exactly. The center of the room is the worst possible place for your ears. It is right in the center of all the first order nulls, and all odd harmonics too. You don't want your ears positioned in nulls! Your room is non-rectangular, which makes it much harder to predict the acoustics, but the 50% spot is bad pretty much always. Theoretically, the best place for your ears is at 38% of the room depth (front to back). What ratio is your room based on? - Stuart -
