Hi All, I currently have my project studio in an NYC office building. I built it myself a year and a half ago using this forum as a reference. It came out great, cost me only $600 and has stopped the transference of sound much better than my expectations. See pic attached.
Image not preserved: studio1.jpg
Anyway, the building is going under new management and I am being forced to relocate to probably another office. This will now be the 3rd studio "room within a room" setup that I've built in 3 years! I want to find an easier solution than 2x4s and drywall and weeks of labor that cut into my work schedule. I've looked at whisperrooms but they are too expensive for a studio sized one. I would gladly pay $5k for a solution that I can easily take apart and bring with me when/if I have to relocate again. My sound isolation requirements are not that demanding. I monitor at a moderate (70db?) listening level and never have drums or loud instruments in the room. Occasionally, when mixing I will crank the volume to see how it sounds LOUD. However, there is music constantly and I don't want to disturb the neighbors. That being said, I would need to treat mostly for moderate listening level music. The spaces I'm looking at all have concrete slab floor and drywall separating the offices. So my question is: Does anybody know of any "modular" rooms or materials that I can box together to create a ~200sq' space that might be suitable to my needs. Something similar to the WhisperRoom concept, but DIY to save cost. Thanks.

specialkm19 June 2010#10
I built my first studio in sections to be modular in fashion so I could take it with me when I moved. It survived a move but barely. it was really heavy, needed a lot of drywall repair and in general was a real PITA to deal with. The only thing that might work is to substitute MDF for the drywall. I built my 3rd studio with it and screwed it all together. It was a lot of work to disassemble, but it all came apart in good shape. Constructing something like this will still take some time to reassemble, but at least you save on the material and time spent making cuts. Of course you have to weigh that against the fact that MDF is much more expensive than drywall so bigger initial cost. Also, it's much harder cutting MDF and I wouldn't recommend using it unless you can keep dimensions utilizing full 4 x 8 foot sheets.
