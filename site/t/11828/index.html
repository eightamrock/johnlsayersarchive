xSpace23 March 2009#3
"Is there anything I should know before the new roof is put on?" What style of roof framing are you talking about? Typical code approved venting for a roof in general requires soffit (overhang) venting, gable end or hip venting, depending on the type of framing your build has. Ridge venting may not be part of code but is a good idea if you have a situation that you cannot get a good flow of air to the entire attic area. Ask your roofers what is most required from them to get a job completed in your area.

xSpace23 March 2009#7
That design will work, but the roof will still have to be vented and your interior leaf is now closer to the openings that let sound in. It is one of the places folks agonize here about. Often it is one of the areas you live with. If your interior leaf and your second leaf are of a substantial mass, then the thinking is that the exterior roof decking and the distance allow the room to minimize the three leaf effect.

xSpace26 March 2009#9
You have to look around and get a feel as to how nuts someone may go. It is common to apply two 5/8 inch sheetrock on a ceiling, with an approved way to decouple the mass from the existing framing member and carry the load. But it is not uncommon to live with what you have. Walls stood up and joists placed directly on these walls that can carry the load is another option. This is the three leaf with a room in a room design. Usually the issues are multiple when constructing room in a room in a residential environment as it applies to code, home owners insurance, fire safety, etc. This is why the best builds get designed by those that can run interference on these issues and address them before they become a problem to you.
