xSpace28 February 2009#11
Your room dininsions are short even if it was a square or rectangular, it would be a super small room. So you have two small rooms. Your expectations have to really be brought down as to what you can actually get out of the two areas. Your selection of which room to use for recording and which one to use for a"control" room will go a long way in establishing how successful this situation really works. Most here will agree that putting a room upstairs is not always the best option, even if it is the only option. But to place a room used for making sound over top of a room designed to capture this sound(or vice versa) is at best a series of compromises that have not been addressed, at worse, an exercise in futility. The ceiling/floor between the two rooms is your area to be concerned with. This is where most of your sound transfer is going to be taking place and possible where a large part of your money and energy will go. You mention 9” thick cavity block walls so is it safe to think these block are, overall, not filled? Not having anything to go on towards what kind/type or loudness levels you create or expect to generate I can only guess at most of this. But with small rooms, anything that begins to get loud is going to give the listener some real hearing problems. Any type of absorption will most likely have to go directly on the walls or you run the risk of having it right in your face. Knocking the acute angles out of the room that the walls create by the triangular design using bass trapping is an area you have to work on. Those angles are going to be an issue.
