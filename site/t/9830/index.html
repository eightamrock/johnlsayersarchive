Andre, With all due respect - when it comes to the point of fine tuning - you cannot EVER pick up with any mic that you will find in any recording studio in the world - the sound levels you can with a stethescope. When you purchase sound iso doors from the real deals (picture companies like Overly) their specs called out requirements for field testing using certified techs =- hwever = when I informed them that I would be performning the testing myself (I wanted it done right the first time) they provided me with a copy of their test casette tape (white noise) and insstructions to set up sound source on one side of door (85 dB levels) and use the stethescope to travel the perimiter of the door seals looking for any point where the sound could be heard - - making adsjustments as necessary until no noise could be heard. You don't need to measure the sound levels - just make them go away........ BTW - I used this method to tests the entire perimiter of Studio 15 @ Sonalysts Studios in Waterford CT (15,00 sf - 750,000 cf) including any and all penetrations (cable - HVAC - as well as caulked corners, wall to floor junctions, etc.) and found it to be very effective in taking that last final step in sound isolation. Listening at that level shows up even the smallest imperfection - allowing you to correct as you go. I'll take that over a studio microphone any day of the week. Sincerely, Rod
