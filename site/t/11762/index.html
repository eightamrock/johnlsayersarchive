This is close to what I am thinking. Only different. At the specific joist/duct area you could fur the joist down a specific dimension. Any space you can develop to keep from binding against the duct metal. You "could" insulate but you really do not have the room. So I would install mass here, multiple layers of rock. If the span is so great that you have concerns of eventual sagging of the rock, use construction glue and adhere the panels together. Or use plywood as the base and apply the rock to that. Watch your depths and screw lengths!!! You do not want to pierce this metal and it can happen faster then you can say, aw hell!

"It would only be a question of doing the math so the 1/8's of inches matched up." And that is exactly where I was at. Furring the thing down was/is only to keep the sheetrock from being pinched up against the duct metal and allow some type of damping to be placed in between the two. xSpace9 March 2009#11
No apologies needed. Your doing fine on your own ;) However you do it, because either one will work, the goal is not to bind one piece of material against the other. So you can fur it down, but you should fill the void created by this method with a suitable insulation type of material. This would be the better of the two but will most likely interfere with the line being straight on your ceiling. OR, you can simply install the sheetrock directly to the joists and over the metal. But you should place something between these two pieces of material[duct metal and sheetrock] as well, and for the same reason. You can glue the metal and rock with construction glue OR you could even place a piece of exterior builders house wrap in between the two. What you are trying to do is cover/add density and dampen an area that has potential to move everytime air moves thru this duct. You'll get it.
