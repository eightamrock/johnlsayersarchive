Louis19 February 2007#1
My aim is to create a commercial recording facility, that would appeal to the semi-professional to cover both recording and audio visual markets. The total floor space is 1600 sq. ft., and after some thoughts, I have come to the basic layout below :- See studio layout below. Also upadated design included (Version 2). The materials budget is 10k approx, excluding all equipment. Some savings will be made where possible by self building with appropriate materials. For ease of our discussions I have split the design into the following areas, and I will try to be brief. Space Plan ---------- Given the shape of the space with no load bearing walls in the way, I have placed the control room with easy access to toilet / kitchen. In my past experience, this is the area most visited, so recording can continue uninterruped if visitors arrive. There is also a back entrance, doubling as a fire exit, so that both studios can be accessed while not disturbing the control room. The existing roof structure is a corrugated steel constrution on wooden joists, sloped to one side, so there is a head height issue once build has been taken into account. I have created a service corridor that will be useful for maintainance, and will use up a little of the less useful space, as shown on the diagram. The service corridor will be used to maintain ventilation and useful for cable runs between areas. Given my layout, this does mean that the control room cannot (easily) be symmetrical to the ceiling height, this is a compromise that I think I will have to live with, to ensure that the control room faces towards the studio. Any readers have a different layout, I would consider. The control room is ratio 1.28:1.54 (length to width), for Sepmeyer 2, but the height is variable, so there is a certain amount of correction to be considered when built (but not too much, I hope). The drum booth also acts as a practice room. I am hoping to achieve isolation so that the practice room can be used at the same time as studio 1A/B for recording. Can this be practically achieved ? If not then the back studio would be a drum booth only, visible to the control room via a camera system. Basic build ----------- The existing building is very leaky, both for sound and air, so I am assuming that the building is providing shelter from the elements only. The interior build is (at current thinking) based on a concrete blockwork outer shell, with a studwork interior shell. I have taken the decision not to float the floor. I have no party walls to consider, other than soundproofing the building itself, i.e. nothing below, above or to sides. Note that the area is a mixed business residential, so I should allow for working to evening night without significant sound spillage to neighbours gardens. The road is offset from the building some 20 metres or so. I have decided not to cut the concrete between control rooms and studios, as I suspect that the concrete base is quite thick. Thus I am relying on the weight of the flooring to as floor isolation between rooms. See basic layout below. Each doorway has 2 doors, one set in each shell. I intend to carefully build fire doors into each structure, rather than purchase pre-built units. As good as these are, I think pre-build units would push the materials budget out of range. Base traps as shown (T) in each area. Ventilation ----------- I am assuming that fresh air is available within the basic building structure, so no external vents will be required. However, vents will be required through my internal structures, so I have taken some inspiration from a small cinema I recently visited. One side of the room provides air intake, while the other side sucks out the air. This provides a flow of fresh air through the structure. To maintain the sound insulation, the air intakes are through 4 inch flexible ducting, with two 90 degree bends in each leg. Each fan is build into an acoustic box. These are expensive, but maybe I will purchase one and use this as a template to build my own. See ventilation concept below Air conditioning ---------------- I am considering a through wall unit, split system. Externally mounted is the condenser, while the air handling unit is on the inside wall. Speaking with some air conditioning installers at work, Dakein are the quietest. Flexible rubber couple between internal and external units is not easy, as the refriderant corrodes the rubber, so a certain amount of noise transmitted from the external unit is inevitable here. I am taking the pragmatic approach that cooling will only be required in the control room. The other rooms will have to do with ventilation only. Given that the lowest noise floor is required in the studios, I am hoping that this approach will work. Heating ------- Panel electric radiation heaters. These are inexpensive as well as providing silent operation. Lighting -------- Probably track, incandescent, no dimming. Electrics --------- Three phase electrics will be installed :- Phase 1 - HVAC Phase 2 - Studio / control rooms + their lighting Phase 3 - All other non studio power and lighting All cables to be screened, and earthed from distribution end only. All power cables to be run 100mm minimum from non power cables, radial circuits installed only. All boxes / face plates screened. All mic/midi/audio connections panels to be screened. I intend to install screened connections to broadband, and a hub for computer interconnect between studios. Add to this a fire alarm system, security system, video cabling, and this adds up to a fair amount of wires. All rack mounted equipment to be installed in the control room bay, with heat sinking out at the back. This outlines the basic build. Any comments welcome. Thank you.

Aloha,
The down side, is that the CR is then faced across the building, but I believe that I can make this symmetrical to ceiling by false sloping ceiling structure.
1745 Millimetre(s) = 5.7250656168 Feet. Whew, that's pretty tight. Also, having a / \ shaped ceiling centered above the mix position may introduce reflections, cluttering up your imaging.
The other issue is that the CR is shaped like a coffin, with the walls sloping inwards towards the engineer, not away as seems in most design. Is this an issue, and if so is there a solution to this?
The main reason for having angled walls in a control room is because of reflection control of the high frequencies for true imaging from your speakers. The side walls are angled away from the mix position to help direct reflections behind you, to be absorbed at the rear of room. Perhaps you could flip the shape of the room to where the narrower end is where your window is. One other point of note, in your drawing it appears that there is a gap between the insulation and plasterboard. Is this the case or are you planning on having the insulation lightly pressing against the plasterboard to dampen any resonances? Aloha 8)

gullfo18 March 2007#19
the main downside to the racks positioned like that would be the rear reflections from them. given how small the CR is, this could be a problem. one option would be to put a drape or soft panel you could slide over the front of the rack - assuming you have other controls for the effects. if not, angle the racks vertically to alter the reflection path. also, I'd put the "producer" couch up on a platform - 6-8". the doors - if the sliders are out of range, consider doors with windows in them as an option. generally speaking though, sliders tend to be fairly welled sealed and plenty of visibility, but can be costly. here in the US they could easily be $600-700 each (and high end ones $2000+). whereas a glass exterior-capable (heavier, better seals) door is less but still (at the low end) $350-500. you could also consider fixed windows (small enough for the budget and large enough for view) and a separate solid door. I think this adds to the construction complexity (seals, gaps, framing, finish, etc) but may be less expensive for parts and may even provide more isolation (done correctly).
