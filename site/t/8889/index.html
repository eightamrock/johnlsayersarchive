sharward7 June 2007#8
The benefits of rockwool (a.k.a. "mineral wool") insulation vs. standard fiberglass batts has been discussed here a few times over the years. Some very credible gents (Rod being one of them) believe that the use of mineral wool products inside of walls is overkill as it is more expensive than standard fiberglass batts and offers little, if any, tangible benefit in terms of additional isolation. As long as there's enough fill or something like nylon straps to keep the insulation lightly compressed against the back side of the drywall, it should work fine. Mineral wool (namely Thermafiber Sound Attenuation Fire Blankets (SAFB) - Nominal Density (2.5 pcf) and Roxul Acoustical Fire Batt (AFB) - ≥2" (2.5 pcf)) is a favorite for do-it-yourself acoustic treatments and is far superior to fiberglass batts in this context -- so much so that I'm pretty sure it's a "don't even bother with fiberglass" when it comes to acoustic treatments. Also, mineral wool has far superior fire resistance properties, which is especially good when you consider that many do-it-yourself acoustic treatments are made to hang on the wall like picture frames -- use of a fire resistant fabric completes the job of a nice acoustic treatment that doesn't attract fire like a magnet. That being said, I did order 19 bags of mineral wool insulation for use between the walls and above the ceiling in the space I am building. I decided I wanted to err on the side of overkill, but what really clinched the deal for me was seeing videos of two test structures, one insulated with standard fiberglass batts and the other with mineral wool, and seeing how they reacted to fire. Hands down, the mineral wool kicked ass. I also recently reviewed video of the infamous night club fire in West Warwick, Rhode Island, which reminded me of the destructive and deadly power of structure fires, especially when safety regulations are not followed and when venue owners don't take seriously their responsibility to only use fire resistant acoustic treatment products. The owners of the station used non-fire resistant packing foam as a cheap alternative to proper materials, and that was one of several key factors that cost one hundred innocent lives on that fateful night of February 20, 2003. Unfortunately, mineral wool insulation is often more difficult to obtain. Home center stores tend not to stock it, and even specialty sellers don't carry it in a good variety of sizes. I had to settle for 24" wide batts, even though my studs and ceiling joists are 16" o.c.! :x I'll be doing a lot of cutting... :roll: ...I was also unable to get the 6" thick batts, so I ended up getting 3" batts with the intent to double up on them... :evil: In my case I've been toying around with the idea of using both fiberglass and mineral wool as my insulation -- mineral wool in the ceiling, mineral wool in the corners and at the halfway point along my long walls in complete fill fashion so as to make a very good fire break, which will use up what I have already purchased -- and then the complete fill cheap fluffy stuff the rest of the voids. I hope this information is helpful to you in deciding between the two types of products. --Keith :mrgreen:
