Hey there... First off, English is not my first language, so I could say weird stuff... Well for some background I'm a 26 years old software developper who has been into music for about 8 years now. I've been recording myself for the last 4 years, upgrading my setup as I went. From computer microphones and soundblaster cards to a Firewire card, monitors and condenser microphones. I'm into a wide range of music and what I record reflects that. It's really the best thing I've ever done, if I only knew I would have started earlier ;) My stuff sounds ok, but not great, and I found it really frustrating to try to have a good sound. So I figured I needed some acoustic help. At first it all seemed way too complicated. I know a lot of musician and none of them really know what they are doing in term of acoustics. I really have to meet some of their engineers... Anyway instead of covering everything in egg-case, I started to read and learn. I've read a lot, but I have absolutely no experience. Well I've been lurking a lot around here lately, (very nice forum and incredibly smart people here by the way!) and I think it's time to make some improvements to my room. I've looked at a couple of designs and I've read Rod Gervais's book a couple of times... And what seemed most practical for me was to DIY some traps based on the realtraps "mini-traps". I'm only renting so all I build has to be easy to move. So I looked around and found some OFI 48 (Owens-Corming 703 equivalent) locally (www.isofab.com for anybody in Montréal looking for some). They cost 1.84$ for a square meter. I'll look around for better price but it seems alright. So... I'll go ahead with the room dimensions 10'W * 12'6"L * 7'10"3/16H Like you can see in the picture, it's not a complete rectangle, because of the slanted wall where the door is. I've looked up those dimensions in ModeCalc, I don't know how the slanted wall affects those results in reality. My room's ratio is not far from one of the recommended ones... The treatment I was thinking of implementing was to install 3 2'*2' minitraps in the 3 upper room corners. a 2*4 on the wall/ceiling corner above my desk, the same thing at the back of the room. 2 other, on in each wall/wall corner on my right and left. 2 on the ceiling, on either side of my ceiling lamp. I'd be left with a 2'*2' piece of insulation which I would use to dampen the sound of my computer. I'll build it in sketchup so it's easier to see. I really have to buy myself a sound level meter, and find a way to test my room with a software like ETF and those flat mikes... So, on with the questions... 1- On the picture of my room, my desk is on the smallest wall. Right now it's on the wall beside the closet. From what I've read the position in the picture would be better right? 2- Since my room is not entirely symetrical because of the slanted wall, is it better to have symetry in front or at the back? 3- Would the insulation I want to install be sufficient? 4- Should I use the OFI 48 or the SFK version? I read it reflected mids and high. Would it be worth it? Acording to the stats they do absorb less hight and mids than the plain version. I don't know how "dead" my room would sound with all those traps and no reflection... Hum... well I guess that's it for now Thanks in advance for your time!

the position looks good. if you could move the dress away from you desk that would be best as its likely to reflect something right back at you or add some resonances to that side. the slanted door is ok in the rear of the room. i'd hang an absorber (50mm) on it. just to reduce the reflection coming back to the desk. the rest of the treatments sound fine and if you find you need more you can add more. the room shouldn't be too dead with either product. since its a tight space i'd go with the less reflective product.
