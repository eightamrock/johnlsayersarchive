Hello all! First of all I must say I'm in agreement with all who say what a great forum this is. I've been studying it on and off over the last few months in preparation for my project of converting part of an old livestock shed into a studio. The shed is timber construction with a metal roof. At a later stage a floor of about 10cm (4in) concrete was laid and the roof and walls were insulated with polystyrene with an inner layer of plywood. My Dad uses part of the shed for guitar making with the occasional use of power tools. There is a room in between his workshop and the area I'm interested in – so not too much sound comes through. We are in a rural location so there is very little traffic – an occasional tractor perhaps and we don't have to worry too much about disturbing neighbours – the nearest is a house 50m away with tolerant occupants! The space is about 6m x 6m (or 20ft x 20ft). The ceiling starts off at 1.9m (6ft 3in) at the side walls and slopes up to 2.8m (9ft 3in) along the centre. There are 2 big wooden beams and 2 support posts in the middle of the area. There are windows on two walls. I've partitioned off the area I can use with a one leaf wall of plasterboard I've atached a sketchup of the empty area and one of a proposed plan – apologies for my rudimentary sketchup skills. My aims are to be able to record bands on a semi-professional basis. Most of the bands and musicians I am involved with play fairly gentle songs with acoustic instruments such as this: http://www.myspace.com/fursdonmusic My requirements for the studio would be to have a live room which was fairly well isolated from the control room – so that drums could be recorded in the live room and acoustic guitar in the control room without too much spill. I also need to make sure that the sound of heavy rain on the metal roof does not come in too much (The polystyrene and plywood has already done quite a good job here). It would be good to have good shaped rooms for recording and mixing. My budget is pretty modest – say £2000 at the moment – hopefully more in subsequent years. Due to the low budget my plan for ventilation is to use the existing windows and put windows in the inner walls and open them every now and then! My questions at the moment are: 1) What do you think of the proposed plan generally? 2) What about the control room? At the moment it will have a ceiling that slopes up from either side wall to central ridge running the length of it. This ridge will slope slightly up as it gets to the back of the room. This is to try and get as high a roof as possible in a cramped area. Do you think this could be problematic acoustically? Any alternative suggestions? Any comments will be gratefully received. Jake

Soundman202017 April 2009#2
Hi Jake, and welcome! Looks like you've been waiting patiently for comments! So here are my comments:
and the roof and walls were insulated with polystyrene with an inner layer of plywood.
So you already have two leaves? Anything that you do in addition is going to add a third leaf. That might not be an issue, though, since you did say that isolation isn't that important.
My Dad uses part of the shed for guitar making with the occasional use of power tools.
What type of power tools? A rotary hammer is rather different from a Dremel hobby drill... Have you checked the sound levels and spectrums ("spectra"?) that your dad produces with those tools?
The ceiling starts off at 1.9m (6ft 3in) at the side walls and slopes up to 2.8m (9ft 3in) along the centre.
That's pretty low. Yes, it will cause a problem for you with your proposed layout, since your CR ceiling will either be very low on the sides with your plan, or it will have to angle up from left to right across the room. Neither is good. can you re-position your layout so that the front of the control room is on one of those outside walls that are only 1.9m high? That would make the ceiling slope up and away from the speakers, until it gets well past your head, which is much better. Rotate your entire design 90° clockwise, then re-jig the rooms to fit again. I think that should work. Also, your control room looks rather long and narrow: what ratio are you using for it? Maybe you could try a different ratio that makes it shorter and fatter? That way, you might be able to fit in almost all of it between the outside wall and the room centerline.
Due to the low budget my plan for ventilation is to use the existing windows and put windows in the inner walls and open them every now and then!
Well, you can have operable windows, of you can have good isolation. But I don't think you can have both. At least not easily, and not cheaply! - Stuart -

Soundman202017 April 2009#4
can you spin you whole design to the left 90 dec?
I think you mean "90° to the right", Lil! Spinning it 90° left would place the rear wall of the CR at a location where the ceiling height can be a maximum of 1.9 m, and the front wall where the ceiling height can hit 2.8 m! Reverse-ceiling angle, anyone? Well, I guess you could spin it 90° left, then flip the CR end-to-end (mirror-image). That would solve both problems! - Stuart -

John Sayers16 July 2009#10
Jake - unfortunately polystyrene is good for thermal insulation but not for sound. I would not class the corrugated aluminium roof as a leaf as it has basically no mass and is not airtight so your suggestion of building a new ceiling below it is the best way to go. For the area of exposed iron I would just line it with insulation and plaster and then build the new ceiling under it and decoupled from it. Yeah - that must be one big ridge cap Deanne, :)
