Dan Fitzpatrick3 August 2005#14
I Annoy, I have a couple of concerns with the plastic over your insulation. I'm not an expert so take this for what you think it's worth. First, the plastic will I believe act as a third leaf. At least at some frequencies. I've heard that plastic is acoustically transparent to lows, but not to highs. So maybe the third leaf effect only applies to the higher frequencies. I'm not exactly sure. but I know Knightfly always warns against having kraft-faced paper with the kraft paper in the middle of a wall, and that's pretty thin too. I'm pretty sure he would warn about this plastic. I'm sure he'll correct me if i'm wrong though. :) to avoid this you would put the plastic, if it is needed, directly against the inner leaf, on the inside of the wall of course. The second thing is, I know you're in a northern climate so maybe it is appropriate to have a vapor barrier on the inner leaf of your wall. But this is not on the inner leaf, it is in the middle. So you may have a situation where moisture is traveling into your wall until it hits the barrier, and then it can't escape. it may be that plastic over insulation is normal and correct construction for your area. but this is for single stud walls. since you are doing a double stud wall this changes things. from what i have read you would never put a moisture barrier between two airspaces in a wall, it has to be against one leaf (outer in warm climate, inner in cold, no barrier in mixed climate). the moisture will try to travel from warm inside to cold outside. a vapor barrier will prevent this IF it is right up against your inner leaf. but that is not where the plastic is going to end up as you have it. it will be halfway in the middle of your wall. so you may create moisture problems, perhaps mold or even rot inside your wall. again, i'm no expert and i've just done very little reading on this for my own project. but i wanted to give you a heads up in case you hadn't already been over this. my best guess is you should take off the plastic, hold it in place with wire instead. and perhaps put plastic on the inside of your inner stud wall before the gypsum if that's appropriate for your climate. dan :)
