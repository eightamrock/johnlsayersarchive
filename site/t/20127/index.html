Soundman202015 September 2015#8
Yeah this kind of thing
That's Tyvek. Tyvek is not a vapor barrier: it is a water barrier, and also an air barrier. It stops water getting in and out, but still allows the wall to breathe. That's what it is there for: so that any water vapor trapped inside the wall cavity can escape outwards, to the outside world, while also not allowing any liquid water to get in. The actual vapor barrier goes on the inside of the wall cavity, up against the warmer surface, which in a cool climate is normally the inner-leaf. You should never have more than one vapor barrier in a wall assembly. There's a lot of confusion about vapor barriers, vapor retarders, water barriers, and air barriers: they are not the same thing. Some are always needed in a wall, some are not. And they all need to go in the right place in the wall, depending on climate. Since the UK is a cool climate, I would think that the vapor barrier (if there is one) should go on the warmer side of the wall cavity, facing the interior of the building, and the Tyvek should go on the outside, where it does it's job of preventing water getting in. - Stuart -
