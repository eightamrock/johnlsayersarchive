Outside dimensions are 130m2, walls are 5.4m high on the high side and 2.8m on the low side. That's with just me & my Dad doing everything ourselves, with the following exceptions: (1) Footings dug by a digger, (2) footings & slab pours, (3) Concrete pumps to core-fill the blocks, (4) rendering, and (5) a crane to lift the rafters. As I think I mentioned, I used dry-stack blocks which are a bit more expensive but laying blocks the traditional way is pretty difficult for non-pros (we had to lay 3 courses of them for the foundations).

adam21 February 2012#38
Hi Anthony, I'm no expert, but from what I've read, sand-filled blocks might even give better transmission loss at lower frequency than grout-filled (core filled) blocks. The reason for this is that sand may provide a dampening effect. Search the forum for "sand" to find out more and make your own mind up. Your mention of the word "veneer" is a concern - that usually describes a very common building method in Australia where the timber frame is built first then a masonry wall is built next to it, and tied to it, so the timber frame actually supports the masonry wall. Works for houses, but it's a no-no for sound isolation. For better isolation, you need the walls (and ceilings) to be independently freestanding and not connected to each other. Free standing block walls require steel reinforcement and at least some of the piers/courses do need to be core-filled for the steel to work. You could fill the rest with sand I suppose. My walls had to be spec'd by a structural engineer who specified how much steel and where to put it. To understand the standard building techniques, go to bunnings and flick through the first few pages of the Alan Staines House Building book which really spells out the standard aussie building methods nicely. BTW, Oasis Studio which John designed has a split face block exterior, the only noise issues they have are when musos use the BBQ outside on the deck!
