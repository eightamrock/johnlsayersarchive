Philip16 July 2007#2
G'day from Oz, I hope this is some helpful advice. I began in 2004 with the excavation of some 250 cubic meters of sandstone under my house. (part of this was originally the garage to the house.) We ended up going nuts and excavating more than we needed so that we could add down the track. I live near a railway and a major freeway - so far there are no problems with low end rumbles coming through. After excavation I then laid a tonne of ag line (drainage) then a unreinforced slab (200 mm thick) then covered with waterproof sheeting - this was a leveling slab! We then laid one reinforced slab (again 200 mm thick) on top of this for the control room, machine room, office, and bathroom. Another reinforced slab was laid next to this also on the leveling slab for the control room. We kept the slabs around 4 inches away from sandstone walls and formed channels for any water to move. We also applied waterproof sheeting to the sandstone walls as a protective barrier, then the walls were also painted as they were laid with a waterproof adhesive, especially designed for Hebel. We had a gap between slabs of 100 mm (4 inches) For all walls I've used 150 mm Hebel (aerated concrete block) It's great! It's light and easy to cut and actually worked out to be really soundproof from the house above. I only needed to stick one layer of gyprock on any walls I wanted to have live. The others have just been a combination of panels. Hebel also make ceiling panels of 150mm thick - they weigh a fair bit so you may need some machinery for this. With windows. The windows to the out side I tripled up. One 6 mm laminated for the external of the house and 2 (10 and 12 mm laminated) for the sound that could transfer into the air cavity between the house and the studio ceiling. Seems like over kill but I play percussion and tend to think late at night! For flooring all the floors are just floating floors on the concrete slabs. Hebel makes an amazing difference. To save as many dollars as possible - do most of it yourself but hire a builder as an adviser. Also find a builder who has knowledge of studios and hebel. I'm sorry but I don't know what the name is for aerated concrete anywhere else on this planet. The Hebel is dead easy but you'll go through lots of saws. I hope this has helped. P
