I'm not experienced in building vox booths, but going from what i've learnt on this forum, then it's a small room. A small room is going to have higher freq room modes due to the smaller size. How much of a difference changing angles of walls will make could be a bit of a moot point due to size. Apart from not having a symmetrical box I think your okay. Have you had a search around the forum to see if anyone else has built a booth that they're happy with? In my experience, getting a good vocal is about choosing the right mic for the voice. (and having a good voice to start with!!) Be wary of over deadening the booth as it's a very uncomfortable space to sing in, and can be quite tiring for an artist.

2) For the floor of the booth: I am thinking of using U-boats underneath 2x4 joists (my thinking is this will protect the apartment floor and give me some isolation from the squeakiness of the old wood floor) and cover that with with 1 or 2 layers plywood or MDF, followed by some engineered hardwood. I'm not sure what U-boats are, but i think i'm getting the general idea. I think MDF has a higher density than ply so I'd go with that. use whatever is heavier. Green glue could help you with isolation in the booth if you're doing double layers.
mr.john711 wrote:
3) Does the overall design of the control room look like it could work as a room to mix in? I'm sure I will need to play with the treatment of the room big time to get things sounding right, but do you foresee any major problems or have any immediate suggestions based on this layout and treatment scheme?
well, to be honest, you're a bit stuck here. With a vocal booth in the room you won't be able to get symmetry for a listening position no matter how you orient it. Ideally as a mix room, you'd rotate the listening position 90deg to the left and fire the monitors down the long axis of the room, but with the vox booth behind you you'll lose symmetry and get some odd reflections. as you are you've got the back wall close to you and an odd shape off to the right which could be problematic. I'd be inclined to turn the listening position 90 deg left and mirror the vox booth with a false wall on the opposite side of the room to keep the symmetry right behind you.
mr.john711 wrote:
4) Do you have any suggestions for helping with the noise problem (for both sides) of my "green wall", floor, and ceiling? My thinking is that theres not much I can do with the floor other than maybe putting a rug down...oh well.
without being able to do some serious construction I'd say you're stuck with this. To increase transmission loss you need one of two things. more mass, ie: adding layers to the wall. Or building a completely detached room within a room contruction. It doesn't sound like either of these will be possible unfortunately. If you double layer your vox booth with plasterboard/mdf etc and use green glue between the layers you should get a good level of isolation for recording vocals. This won't help with your mix situation, but at least you'll have a clean signal. Before you go building anything though, you need to get a definite idea of how much isolation you need for your booth so you don't get any bleed from the neighbours. A sound level meter of good quality is what you need for the job. Say you want 35db in the booth and you're getting a reading of 75-80db when the neighbours are watching TV, then you need 40-45db isolation in your booth. Should be doable with a couple of layers of 16mmplaster/mdf with green glue. hope that gives you somewhere to start :) steve
