sharward24 September 2006#2
Hi Mike, welcome to the forum. The walls in that room at this point may as well be made of paper towels. :roll: In fact, I wouldn't even go so far as to call them walls -- I'd call them "vertical dividers." What is your vertical clearance right now? That itself will dictate a lot of what you can and can't do, ceilingwise. Find out from your local building department what the minimum height requirement would be for a basement build-out. I suspect that the build-out that was done down there isn't to code now, so perhaps it wasn't done with a permit... :? You'll definitely want to do this one on the up-and-up. Learn more about the importance of codes and permits. Definitely buy Rod's book, as it will be your first and most important investment. You might be able to beef up the upstairs floor from between the joists (provided your joists are able to withstand the extra weight - don't assume that), then have an equally massive ceiling in your room. This is covered pretty extensively in Rod's book. Personally, I would not put 2 layers of 5/8" drywall on 2x3 studs. Besides, that would only be for one side? Did you plan on leaving the thin, flimsy, wimpy paneling on the noisy side? The infinitesimal amount of mass on the inner leaf, coupled with a mere 2 1/2" of gap in your mass-air-mass equation, would yield terrible transmission loss figures. I'm almost certain you'll need to demolish those "walls" and make new ones. Finally, be sure to review Luftweg's "Basement studio-room project in Boston area" thread and Andy Eade's Rotten Basement Conversion Thread for a rather rude awakening into the perils of basement studio builds when it comes to water intrusion. Still think your $500 to $1,000 figure is realistic? ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:
