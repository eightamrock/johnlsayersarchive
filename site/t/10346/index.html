gullfo10 May 2008#19
you'll want all you treatments to be broadband as much as possible unless you've identified specific problems. whether porous or slat absorption. so for the slats, consider building a number of cavities which then have slats which cover a broad range rather than trying to target a specific center frequency (unless you know its a real problem and the location in the room is the correct spot for the treatment).

AVare10 May 2008#21
Actualsizeaudio wrote:
I have read different opinions about the slat space, ranging from making them different, to having them be the same within 1/32 of an inch.
I don't know how to reply to "I have read" without links or references but here is an attempt. Slat space (between the slats), width, and depth behind the slats are all interrelated. You can not write about one without taking into consideration the other variables.
It was my understanding that if they were not all the same, then the frequency does not get canceled out inside the resonator.
The absorber works on absorption in the cavity between the slats. The inside air acts as a spring. What I am curious about is if the space behind the slats is variable (sloped), will that work making the resonator broadband. [/quote] No. The air in the resonator is what does the spring action. The formal equation for peak absorption uses the volume of the space. The form that we use is adapted for rectangular volumes specifically. Helmholtz used round, rectangular, square shaped vessels for his experiments. What matters is the volume, not the shape. Andre
