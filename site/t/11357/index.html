jwl1 November 2008#12
Phazinhead wrote:
1) after reading more on realtraps.com : is it better to have medium size bass trap on walls with many small traps at 45° between walls and ceiling, or to have only big wall bass trap going from the floor to the ceiling ?
Generally speaking, bass trapping using broadband absorbers is as much about coverage area as it is about individual trap performance. If you have a given material, I'd spread it out in as many corners of the room as possible, making sure each trap is 4" (100mm) thick or more. I think you'd get better results by using 1 - 2'x4' panel in each corner than by putting all of your traps in one or two corners.
Phazinhead wrote:
2) About the ceiling panel,: 2 panels of 60cm*135cm on top of the desk (mixing area) would be sufficient ?
You want to make sure the first-reflection points, both on the side walls and the ceiling, are covered with absorption. These panels don't have to be as thick (50mm will do).

Phazinhead wrote:
When you say it doesen't matter which corner, you mean it is the same effect if bass traps are placed in the 4 corners wall/ wall, or the corners wall/ ceiling, or wall/floor ? .. I'm not shure to have understood.
Theoratically, yes it's all the same. Sound is 3D so it isn't picky on which corner it will be present. Every corner matters. So if you're low on floorspace, those ceiling/wall corners are prolly your number one to use. It's better to stuff ALL corners as much as possible.
Another question about corner bass trap : if the bass trap doesn't have a plywood face, how much does it lower its absorbtion?
We're talking different kind of 'traps' here. There's (pure) absorbers (no front, just wool) and resonators (as soundman replied). The wood, in front of and not touching the wool, acts as the resonator in question. In that case, yes, you need a closed trap. I'd suggest to check Ethan's site(s) for such devices. As I said earlier, deeper trap (read: more wool volume) means more low absorption. A panel resonator will reflect much more mid/high back in the room. They do, however, work great as low frq absorber without having to use lots of wool. But its another type of trap which is mostly used on walls, not corners (but it's not impossible to place'm in corners ofcourse). Get to know the differences.
