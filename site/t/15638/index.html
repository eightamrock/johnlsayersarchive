You design the TL for what ever reading you measure on the outside vs the inside. Of course, each owner has the right to lower his expectations and compromise according to their own judgements ...ie...when will you record vs time of most noise vs client use vs TL construction costs etc. However, EVERY studio is a compromise.
I know this is the big question I might not get a response but what the heck, Is this at all possible with a 10k loan and a little room fro an additional 1 or 2k, I have all the gear for productions and monitor systems?
This is impossible to answer at this point. First off, you don't have a building yet. And to my way thinking, you don't know what the potential difficulties are yet either. HVAC, the existing building envelope, Building inspection Dept/Codes/Permits/Fees...Material/Labor Price and availability, your design ...all and more have a bearing on the final cost. Yea, DIY' is much cheaper, but you can't remove some things from the cost structure. And btw, anytime you do things in a COMMERCIAL BUILDING(especially California)...you are obliged by Code to have some things brought up to Commercial level. Like possibly Title 24 bathrooms(handicap) Time is another thing. DIY' may be what you are planning, but if you have to make a living at the same time...well, that's pretty self explanatory.
Hope to hear back from a compassionate viewer. Thanks for time if you read!
I AM a compassionate viewer and a long time DIY'r. I figure if there is a will....well, there's a way. But the most important thing is to do your homework. Which if you have Rods book, well...you're on your way. :mrgreen: However, each building has it's own specific set of conditions. Once you know these, you have a better grip on what it is going to take to reach your goal. Compassionate or not, I understand what you would like to do. But the moment you enter the "commercial building" realm, to operate a "business", at what ever level, in California you open Pandoras box. At least from my experience. And it ain't cheap. Word to the warning too. My ex boss thought he could do a quick renovation on a small suite in a strip mall building without applying for permits. BAD mistake. During the renovation, he leased it, and then got caught by the local BID. Had to tear out EVERYTHING, got sued by the client, was fined $4k AND the cost of bringing everything up to code(bathroom was $10k alone) I'm certainly no expert, but I'd suggest talking to your local BID, BEFORE you make any kind of Rent/Lease agreement..PERIOD. You might be in for a surprise. And frankly, from talking to a half dozen online inspectors, studios can be a NIGHTMARE to pass code. And VERY expensive. fitZ

However, I believe warehouses still fall under "commercial" compliance, and as such require electrical, plumbing, HVAC, structural etc to be installed by licensed contractors and must comply with commercial codes. This is an expensive proposition which can suck up your budget very quickly.

Square footage? You don't know what you have to work with yet...exactly. Untill you research the BID thing, and use that to analyze potential buildings that allow your budget to work. Untill then, you won't know anything. Who knows. You might be lucky enough to find a situation with more square footage than you thought you could get. But in my view, if you are recording "commercially", then there is no question you need a live room AND a control room. The fact is, you can't monitor over speakers in a one room scenario. You'd have to use headphones, and THAT won't allow you to hear the truth. Bleed from the live sound in the room will alter your perception of what you are hearing. Soooooo, that's my story and I'm stickin to it! Also, the monitoring issue has a lot to do with your design as well. Depending on what you are planning on recording, Transmission loss between the two rooms may be critical. 110 db drums 5 feet from the partition wall will require substantial and well planned TL construction. Not to mention a control room design which does NOT mask comb filtering in the live room. Amongst others. But those details are way to complex for me to comment on at this point.
