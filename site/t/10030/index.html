what about neighbors across the street? 130db is pretty loud... i'd say start with some measurements to determine how much isolation you need. hearing protection will definitely be needed when working more than 30 seconds with sounds levels like that... the windows will need a lot of isolation. you could put the CR between the two posts (7.5') section facing towards the bottom of the drawing. this way you can have the big live space on the left, an iso booth on the right, and plenty of space for a vox booth in from of the CR and a hall. floating a concrete floor will require input from a licensed structural engineer to determine if the structure can handle it.

knightfly27 January 2008#8
Also, for the amount of isolation you may need you should forget about staggered stud walls, and study the difference between STC (worthless for music) and individual TL (Transmission Loss) values at various (specifically LOW) frequencies. I second the need for a structural engineer, one who can get the building's design specs from local authorities and understand them - to isolate drums, you'll need a full floated room-in-room with fairly heavy materials, double framed walls, suspended or separate inner ceiling, tight double doors, the works. BTW, an acoustic drum set may range from 90-130 dB depending on who's playing them and how. Typical is probably 110 dB at lower frequencies, which are the hardest to isolate. The GOOD news is, human hearing needs about 55 dB to hear ANYTHING at 50-60 hZ, so your TL at those frequencies from inside to "irritable neighbor" would need to be around 55-65 dB, which (at that frequency) is difficult but not impossible. As to your producer's "own construction crew" - be VERY careful here - 99.9% of so-called construction "pro's" don't know their butt from a fur-lined gopher hole about sound isolation, even tho they THINK they do. I know - I was one of them 25 years ago, and still have the "soundproof" room in my barn to prove it - the one I can play acoustic piano in and my wife, in the house 100 feet away, can tell me what song I'm playing :cry: Said room is now only a storage area, so not much sound leaks out :roll: Again, find yourself a structural engineer, you're gonna need to know how much weight your space can handle...
