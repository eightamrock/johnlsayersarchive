doublehelix20 May 2008#8
Well, I am not sure that the velour that they are advertising is going to work for you, but of course, I have no idea for sure. They talk about it as used for stage curtains, not for acoustic treatment. Remember, you need to be able to breathe through it in order to use it for acoustic treatment. I have been using some Ronte Roma fabric from JoAnn's fabric. It is a bit pricey at $9.99/yd, but they have coupons in the paper every Sunday for 40% off, and sometimes they are 50% off. It looks great, and works a treat. I am using black, but they do have several other colors (although not a huge pallet of colors like Guilford).

frostgfx22 May 2008#11
Just wanted to chime in on this thread to remind others that if you are building in a commercial space, you will most likely be required by fire codes to use Flame resistant fabric... If you are building at home, you might be able to avoid this extra cost. Just remember that if you are using Non Flame Resistant cloth near ceiling light fixtures or halogen type lights, there is a good possibility of catching fire. Not something I'd want to risk my family over. Namaste...

xaMdaM22 May 2008#13
I received the samples from Sew What; Encore PFR and Avora Lining Both are black fabric samples, but the Avora is black ONLY. The Encore is fairly breathable, while the Avora has a fairly restricted air flow. Something that's bothering me is how all these fabrics are sold. I spoke to Acousticsfirst, out of Richmond, VA. They calculated the Guilford at 3 bolts. While GA Stage calculated at 4 bolts. They are both the same width, yet with the GA Stage product being less than 1/4 of the cost of the Guilford, the price comes in at 2.5x the cost of the Guilford. I'm digging into this to determine how these folks all sell their products, because it seems that things just don't add up. My suspicion is that one is a linear yard (36"x62" width) vs square yard (36"x36"). So when you go to get quotes, you have to be sure that you are speaking the same language. Oh... and as frostgfx mentioned.. IFR is Inherent Flame Resistant and FR is Flame Resistant.
