Our aim is to have a big enough space for a large group of people (7-10) to be comfortable doing live recording, mixing, and relaxing in between work sessions. The pole barn will act as a cold frame, the studio itself will be rooms within the barn, separate from pole barn walls. It will have a poured, insulated, concrete slab. Our budget after pole barn construction is $40,000. Currently we have the sand foundation prepared and are slated to begin construction of pole barn in the coming week. -We plan to have a live room, control room, and entrance/kitchenette area, with a drum room and vocal booth in the live room space, and a mechanical room and bathroom that will share the space with entrance/kitchenette area. -We are planning the live room to be 7.3152m/24' x 12.192m/40'. All rooms with exception to mechanical room and bathroom are planned to be separated by double wall construction, also with R-19 fiberglass between frames. Above trusses will have blown insulation(not sure quantity or material at this time). * -- I am researching the necessary degree of isolation we will require. Questions: 1. Is this a good option? We have a clear slate at this point, any design ideas or information is welcome, helpful and much appreciated. Thank you, Ryan

Hi Ryan, I have to admit I am jealous with the amount of space you have to work with. Couple of thoughts. You have enough space to really do this well with less compromises than many people have to make. I would do a corner control room design ( I do have a bias for them), because it would make for better sight lines from the control room to the live room. I would also try and design the vocal booth so it had doors from both the control room and the live room. I would also make sure the sight lines from the drum room are good to the control room, the live room and the vocal booth. Start with musician comfort and work your way out. Steve

It's your ears that you want at the 38% point, not the desk! In any event, that number isn't carved in stone: it's just a good starting point, and you can vary slightly.

About 1.5 to 2m in front of you, and at 30° angles to the room centerline, on the corners of an equilateral triangle that has your head as the third point (or rather, a position about 15 cm. behind your head, more or less. And do soffit mount your speakers if you possibly can! Major positive effects...

Yup! Angle at least two of the walls (more if you feel like it), and it seems like the general rule of thumb is to angle at least 6°. Although you can go more than that if you want, for aesthetics or other reasons. I'd also suggest that you soffit-mount your speakers (also called "flush mount"), since you are going to be angling your front walls for RFZ anyway, plus all the other benefits of soffit mounting.
(maybe this is another way of saying RFZ?)
Not really. RFZ only really applies to control rooms. Like the name implies, the idea is to get your ears in a Reflection Free Zone, such that they only hear direct sound from the speakers, and no first reflections within 10 dB of that level. That's exactly what you need in a control room, but in a live room you can't really do that, since you never know where things are going to be set up, and where people are going to be. And in a live room, you most likely do want some ambiance, and perhaps even some reflections, depending on what it is you are trying to achieve. So in your live room, you can just angle your walls as in that quote, to deal with flutter and make treatment easier.
