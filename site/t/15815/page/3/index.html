xSpace16 June 2011#56
HVAC is not run on the same breaker as anything else. If something requires 30 amps, then this is an expectation of a possible flow. If you couple a potential 15 amp on to that, not only is double lugging a code violation, it will most likely be a constant source of frustration since the 30 amp breaker being constantly pushed beyond it's ability will trip. Let your electrician handle those details, that is what he is getting paid for. If he is not certain of how to approach a star ground, then you guys should get educated together on this avenue since the electrician is the more qualified between the two of you, an he actually gets to become a better electrician because of your job :)

xSpace21 June 2011#61
The only thing I might add is this. Your wiring should run high/overhead for 120V and low or under slab for low voltage/audio cables. Typically an electrician will want to run wire thru the studs, horizontally across the wall assembly. You want to eliminate this process in effort to eliminate the potential for 60Hz cycling thru mics, or amp heads, anything that can pic it up really. Keeping electrical and audio cables 24 inches apart and not running parallel to each other can help to reduce this detrimental effect that is hard to correct once the build is completed:)

gullfo3 January 2012#65
i would suggest hanging some absorbers from the ceiling - not hangers per se, maybe space out 4 2'x4'x4" absorbers about 12" apart and hung down about 2'. this will provide an open space above for LF absorbtion and still be close enough to dampen the ringing. then if it still needs a bit more, add some absorption onto the surface of the ceiling. another consideration - put some wires with hooks hanging down so you can suspend a couple of polys or space coupler in the event you want some more life in there - acoustic guitar, small drum kit etc. whilst still having the overhead absorption.
