n8nod9it30 January 2007#8
benunh wrote:
One more set of questions about this step... What's the deal with the 3/8" backer rod? Just to keep the gyp from sliding back and forth? Will 3/8" fit into the a slot 1/4" wide as directed? Thanks Ben
Backer rod is used to help the caulk retain its connection between a gap. On a 1/4 " gap you would also use a ton of caulk to fill all the seams so it cuts down on the price. 3/8 will fit in a 1/4 gap, you want it to fit snug but not crushed too much. Someone mentioned using a pizza wheel type device to insert it (like window screen). You need the 1/4" gap on the drywall to decouple it from everything else, and so you need the backer rod to fill that space.
rod gervais30 January 2007#9
Ben, I thought I had explained in the book about the reason for backer rod.....and now you have me 2nd guessing myself......... so I had to check - and found that I did miss that explanation - so I'll add that to my list of things to add / modify in the 2nd edition when it comes around. For now though you get an explanation: Although you do indeed save caulk by installing backer rod - this is not the reason to do it. Rather it has to do with the joint either holding or failing. For caulk to adhere properly - and remain sound after curing (and as building movements take place over time) it has to have a joint that is 2 edged. So if you picture the caulk joints drawn in the book - you have the drywall slot - slide in the backer rod - and caulk - with the end result being the caulk attached to the edge of one sheet - and the edge of another - thus 2 points. If you remove the backer rod -= you have a joint now attached at the same 2 points - PLUS after you fill the slot full of caulk - the material at the back that joins the top and bottom - thus you now connect at 3 points. 3 point caulk connections are going to fail based on the movement that takes place over time- thus instead of just streatching the caulk - a shear force comes into play that will cause the joint to fail in it's length......... and it's a lot of money to pay to caulk everything - only to have it all be useless when the joints all fail......... So now you understand the reason why.. and yes - 3/8" backer rod is just fine for a 1/4" joint- stuff it flush to the face with your finger - and then the idea of using a pizza cutter can work - as can the setting tool for spline type screen windows. Just don't crush it in the process.... Sincerely, Rod

Velvet Elvis31 January 2007#14
Ben, Home Depot does indeed carry backer rod. Its in the aisle (at least at my store) where the door weather stripping and such is. It comes in a plastic bag and contains a coil of backer rod. It is NOT in the drywall aisles as one might expect. I can't remember the name on it, but I'm pretty sure that it actually says something like "backer rod", so I'm surprised that they didn't know what you were talking about. I managed to find acoustic caulk by calling a drywall supply house. "Gypsum Supply" is the name of the place that has it in the midwest, but not sure if they are out east at all. They carried (and stock) the OC-175 acoustic caulk. I think you will find that buying acoustic caulk (even if you mail order it) will be at least as cheap, if not cheaper, than buying enough tubes of the "big store" caulk that would work. There are only a couple of brands in the stores that are adequate (I can't remember which ones... because I didn't go that route), but they are far more expensive per ounce than actually buying acoustic caulk.
