Other with more experience then I may give a better answer BUT... Attic spaces have to be ventilated with soffit and ridge vents (or other venting systems of what type I'm not sure). We avoid triple leaf at all costs BUT we deal with them where we have to. Things like corners in rooms, adjacent sound iso rooms, places where we just can't cost effectively build only Mass Air Mass AND attic spaces is where many of us are forced to deal with triple leaf and we deal with it by over doing the parts/walls/ceilings we are building OR live with the results. The thread that comes to mind where the topic of triple leaf was beaten to a pulp is: Basement studio-room project in Boston area the beating of triple leaf topic goes on till at least page 2 where Rod kicks with some great comments. SO what I, Sharward, Dan F and many others are doing with attic space directly above the iso room(s) is: leave the attic as normal build with (in these cases pre existing vents). The Mass Air Mass is being built as a double ceiling of the iso room. The roof will act as a 3rd leaf BUT we are either compensating for it or willing to live with the result. Can we all afford the height/space to have a "decoupled" Mass Air Mass ceiling :lol: If planning a new building all together you should get as much hieght in the room as you can to have the space for double decoupled ceiling. And this is where I stop babbling and wait for someone else to kick in. Marc
