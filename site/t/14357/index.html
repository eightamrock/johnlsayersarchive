Soundman20207 May 2010#3
Hi, and please read the "sticky" posts on the first page of this forum, with the rules about posting. You probably won't get any replies if you do not follow those rules. Regarding your problem: First, to me it looks like you don't have anywhere near enough bass trapping for a room that size. I'm not sure what you mean by "resonance bass traps" (that could be a number of things), but I'd suggest you they aren't doing very much at all: I'd say replace those with proper "superchunk" bass traps in both of those rear corners, plus another one across the top of that rear wall (the ceiling-wall corner), then also in front of your desk in the floor-wall corner, plus up as high as you can get across those front corners behind the speakers, and as many other corners as you can possibly do. Second, did you actually measure the room response at your mix position? Where exactly are the nulls? (what frequencies and how deep). With those dimensions I'd expect issues at the following frequencies: 31.9, 47.8, 57.5, 63.8, 79.7, a big one at 95.7 (0:2:0 and 3:0:0 both line up there, exactly), 100.8, 107, 115, 127, and that's just up to 130 Hz. Some of those will be nulls, some will be peaks. But that's just theoretical: You'll have to actually measure the room to find out where your problems are, then you can figure out how to fix them. Third: What are the "resonant bass traps", what frequency are they tuned to, and what is their Q? Are they the red things in the corners, or the black things on the floor? Do you have specifications for them, or maybe a link to the manufacturers web site? US$ 3,000 for those seems a bit steep... Anyway, they may well be tuned to entirely the wrong frequency, so they might even be making matters worse. What exactly are they, and what principle do they work on? Fourth: Where is the mix position (position of your head)? In terms of how far from the front wall, how far from the back wall, and how high above the floor. - Stuart -
