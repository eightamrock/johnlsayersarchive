Hello - I am a film/tv composer looking at a space in Los Angeles on the second floor of a two story office/retail building to use as MIDI studio with possibly a small live room. After testing in the space the sound transmission to the bottom floor is surprisingly small and not an issue with any first floor tenants. It shares one wall with a neighboring office and the opposing wall with a bathroom with loud plumbing. The front of the office has street noise entering through the front door and windows. The rear wall is also an exterior wall and not shared with anything. I am currently waiting to hear back from the builder about the load the floor can take. The HVAC is quiet and acceptable for me. GOALS (in order of importance): 1) Achieve at least moderate isolation of : a) outgoing noise to the neighboring office to right of space b) incoming noise from the bathroom to left of space c) incoming street noise in front of space. 2) Build a control room (at least 12' x 17' or whatever the closest acoustically correct dimensions to that would be) 3) Build a small vocal booth or live room. I don't need anything bigger than 8' x 8' and can go as small as a minimal vocal booth. EXISTING BUILDING: - Floor: From what I am told it is a simple wood floor, though I am having a hard time believing it as I blasted a speaker setup with a subwoofer and very little could be heard on the first floor. Floor is topped with a wood laminate. - Walls: Simple stud walls with drywall (not sure of stud spacing, etc) - Ceiling: 103" to a drop tile ceiling with actual ceiling 21" above that. Not sure what's in the ceiling construction - all I can see is a layer of drywall. OTHER ELEMENTS: I would like to have room (probably in the front of the space) for a small office and also a storage space somewhere in the room if I can manage it. BUDGET: I am awaiting to hear from the landlord if they will chip in for tenant improvements, so depending on that I could consider different plans ranging from $10k to $25k. I've included a pic of the floorplan (my first time using sketchup so hopefully it's usable). The measurements were done quickly so may not be 100% accurate, which I know will be required. I will work on some ideas myself and post them here, and I have an expert coming to look at the space soon, but I thought I could ask the insanely generous members on this forum their opinion on how to configure the space. Thank you!!

xSpace6 February 2011#7
rubinomusic wrote:
Another question if you please... It's a drop tile ceiling under a drywalled ceiling. If I build the walls only up to the drop ceiling, then fill the cavity above with insulation, can I still expect a reasonable amount of isolation from the neighbor and in between the live room and cr if they are 2 leaf systems otherwise?
No...you have to maintain separation between all rooms to avoid any flanking or air-borne paths that need to be eliminated....or at least reduced to an acceptable level. Since you are building walls anyway, in an attempt to establish the room-in-a-room design, you may as well learn the whole system and get that ceiling framed in as well. If at all possible you want to "consider" removal of the existing grid/tile ceiling...existing hvac/sprinkler/ hardware permitting...to establish the highest possible ceiling height that you can reach.
xSpace6 February 2011#8
"Floor: From what I am told it is a simple wood floor" Nevermind what I said before...you need to get an "expert" in to establish the deadload limits of an elevated wooden floor since it will be much reduced (permissible load limits) than if it were a concrete floor. You should get that checked first since you may get the bad news first and not be able to do anything...your landlord doesn't have this information readily available, trust that :)
