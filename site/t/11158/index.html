...would you recommend RC channel between the frame and plasterboad for max isolation? thanks. Jake.
Hi Jake, I'm glad to see the pallet debate is over, and the proper call was made. I would not recommend RC for decoupling in that I've never seen it properly installed and it is really, really easy to short circuit. I would suggest thinking about using Clips and Hat Channel for decoupling if constructing a single stud wall. If you are doing a double stud wall (room within a room) then you will already have decoupling.

John Hile4 September 2008#13
Jake, Decoupling is a very important element in soundproofing or sound isolation. The benefit of decoupling is that the sound energy is thwarted from going from one side of the wall to the other due to mechanical separation. This in turn will drive down the low end resonance point which is key when dealing with situations that produce a lot of bass like home theaters, band practice rooms, and recording studios. A typical 2x4 wood stud wall with 1 layer of 5/8" plasterboard will be somewhere in the neighborhood of 40-42 STC. Not that good. Keep in mind that when you do your tests that your dB meter will read a single numbered A-Weighted dB number. What is more important is at what frequency bands the sound produced is the loudest. For bands it will be in the 70-120 Hz area which is below the STC band. I have attached a picture of the 3 basic wall types that may be a bit easier to understand. I hope this helped John
