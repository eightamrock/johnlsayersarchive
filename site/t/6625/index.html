New house, help with basement studio

Started by Mark Blasco on 14 July 2006. 12 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6625.

Mark Blasco14 July 2006#1
First of all, a little background: I have been recording my own music for years, and have worked with quite a few bands on their own projects. For the last 4 years I have been working out of a 9X12 basement, and now I am moving to a house, and the wife has agreed to let me take over part of the bottom floor for a studio. I will mainly be recording my own music, although I will be recording some rock bands as well. Type of music: Lots of different types, but I will be doing rock music, so as much isolation as possible would be great. What I want/need: With the space I have, my guess is that I will be better off with as big of a control room as possible (big enough to have a jazz quartet if possible). I need at least one isolation room big enough for someone with an acoustic guitar, and if it is big enough for a drum kit that is even better, although not a requirement. A second isolation area for a guitar amp would be ideal, although again not a requirement. Limitations of the space: -The ceiling height is about 7'6 throughout most of the space, except where the air ducts for the upper floor are located. They go down to about 6'8. Fortunately, there are no open vents in the areas I have allocated to use the studio. I have already accepted the fact that I am going to have to use RC for the ceiling, and that the isolation between the lower and upper floors will be limited because of that. My wife is understanding, however, and is fine with hearing noise upstairs as long as it is reduced as much as possible -At least one of the hallway walls is structural, and the other one may be as well (I can't tell because it is fully finished, and since we don't own the house yet, I can't just start ripping out drywall to check) My Limitations: I am somewhat handy, but have never done any construction before. I have built a few bookshelves, and have some tools, but this is all new to me. Wherever possible I will be pulling in help from wherever I can. I am friends with a manager at a lumber yard, so I should be able to find people to help me out. Budget: I will have around $4-5,000 to start, and can put more into it over time. What I need here: I am looking for ideas for the design based on the space I have to work with. Notes on the drawing: - It is as close to scale as I can get it. I took measurements during the house inspection, and we won't be seeing the space again until we move in. The only thing that I had to guess on was the location of the doorways for the rooms, but those will be torn out, so I am not sure that it matters if they are slightly off. I also did not put in the locations of the closets in each of the rooms, as those will be ripped out for sure. - The water shutoff valve and the electrical panel are inside, so I will need to be able to access those. - the areas in blue are the areas that I am NOT going to be using. I will, however, need to have a door to get to there from the studio. Any help you guys or gals can give me is appreciated!

sharward25 July 2006#6
That's really clever -- you have something like the "service corridor" I described earlier, yet it doesn't rob you of nearly as much square footage. 8) You might also be able to use that closet for a little bit of storage... Mics and stuff, perhaps? As long as you can easily walk around them... :roll: ...I'd definitely plan on having a battery-operated backup light in there. I assume those are sliding glass doors that separate the live room from the control room. It's a good thing you have that exterior door to/from the live room. Otherwise it would be a death trap if there were a fire in the control room -- a problem that came up once before in a basement studio design that was rejected by the building department of a city in the U.K. if memory serves. Assuming you get a blessing from John and/or others, I would suggest taking the drawing down to your building department to see if they see any issues with it. If they don't, then you can make more detailed plans for the permit application. :) --Keith :mrgreen:
Mark Blasco25 July 2006#7
After looking at my original design, I realized that the control room only had one entrance/exit, and that wouldn't be safe if there was a fire. I also realized that if someone wanted to go to the bathroom, they would have to go through the live room. The new plan allows for people to go in and out of the control room while someone is recording in the live room. As for storage in the electrical closet, I am thinking that if the area is 3'6 wide, that gives me enough space to put up a few shelves on one side of the wall to store misc. stuff. I was also thinking that I might be able to run some cables for the computer to this area, and stick the computer in there, right behind the wall. This would get rid of any noise problems. I think it might be worth the inconvenience of having to walk back around to the computer if it means a silent control room. I am assuming that I will need a battery powered backup light in all of the rooms, considering that there are no windows, and I will definitely be putting one in the electrical closet. We are moving this Sunday, and my plan is to give my wife a month of working on the stuff in the house she wants to change. After that, it's time to start working on the studio!
