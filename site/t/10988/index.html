andrebrito25 July 2008#7
Once again, since I am not an experienced builder my only comment would be in the form of another question. - "What level of isolation would you like to achieve?"
That is basically the most important question, one I find rarely asked in this forum. There's the established idea that you need soundproof. It is correct and you probably need. The question after is how much soundproof do you really need and what are you trying to achieve. Helicopters like airplanes make a lot of noise. But the perceived noise depends on the distance to the listener. I can say that on several acoustical measurements I did on the countryside, regarding environmental acoustics, dogs at 100 m had an higher SPL at my sound level meter than airplanes, since they were quite high in the skies.

Soundman202025 October 2008#17
That's what I figured. Whatever you do to fix it, you need to be absolutely certain that no part of the framing of the inner leaf has any contact at all with the outer leaf. Not even one single nail or screw that got driven too far and touches the other leaf. That's all you need in order to negate all of the hard work that you put into the rest of it. They must be totally separate, isolated, independent structures, all around, all four walls and the ceiling too, and absolutely hermetically sealed. The concept of a "room within a room" is just that: no connection between the two. If there is any connection at all, then the cost and effort of building two walls is just a waste of time and money. Posting more photos of the construction details would be useful for the experts here to be able to see if there are problems, and to suggest how they can be fixed.
