xSpace8 July 2009#2
Actually the information here isn't overkill, per se, you just haven't been reading long enough to get the correct combination for your build. A few weeks here would let you know that a square room, like your 7 X 7 is problematic. A few more weeks and you would find that you can apply acoustic treatments that can kill the problem reflections at the center points of the walls. Mass is one of the componants required to isolate sound, so yes, adding something of a dense composition to either side of the door will help. Door seals are a requirement as is a threshold. Do not cut holes in the door for a knob but install a pull handle. The reason is that you do not want to penetrate the door and cause a decrease in your soundproofing. Good luck

Traditionally Dynamat was an asphalt mat used for sound deadening the interior of vehicles. It does a great job at that. It is great at dampening, but has very little mass since it is so thin. I imagine it would work similar to green glue if you used it between layers of drywall. But not as good and probably more expensive. Dynamat is not cheap. I just looked at their website and they do have architectural acoustical products. http://www.dynamat.com/products_archite ... ction.html Dynamat Extreme: Deadens sheetmetal (ducting, metal panels, etc.) $60 for 6.67sqft Dynamat EnWall: Maybe for soffit speaker isolation/deadening. But probably useless for studio application. Dynabox: Looks pretty useless for studio applications. Dynil: MLV $669 for 112sqft Dynapad: Floor sound barrier $940 for 112sqft Might be worth looking into but it's all way too expensive.
