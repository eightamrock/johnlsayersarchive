Hi there, been running round here for a few months now and love this site, full of excellent info. i have a small studio which originated as a rehearsal space and teaching studio and then morphed into a recording studio. as a result the layout of the original space was not intended for, nor is it ideal for recording. Square booths and odd shaped control room area (as also used for rehearsal). Anyway, we are taking over the big room next door to us which was a joinery workshop for a live room/rehearsal space and re modeling our existing space to better suit studio use. image 'current fusebox' shows are current setup. as we are already starting the conversion of the workshop next door i'd like some of your suggestions as to what to do with the walls in this space. we have the area cleared and are building up openings/fitting new external doors/strengthening roof purlins at this stage. image 'live room bare floor' is what we'll be starting from image 'live room section 1 NB' shows a section through the building. all walls are currently 100 mm bare concrete block and i intend to come out off the walls 100mm and stud the entire room free from the outside walls. this building is located in a rural setting. sound proofing is not that important as no neighbors very close. i read on here that soundproofing when unnecessary only makes internal room treatment more challenging so i was thinking of pump filling the studs and the cavity to outside wall with cavity insulation beads. (have yet to find out the accoustic value of these beads, but should provide 200mm of solid polystyrene beads. this is a very quick easy way to insulate but not sure if practical for sound?) the only exception is the 'top'wall on the floorplan which is about 3m off a road which although not busy does have a lot of agricultural traffic at certain times of year. the current drum booth also shares this wall (the sofa is against it in the pic) and it only has an insulated plasterboard skin (12.5mm board on 30mm rigid polyurethane) on batons fixed to the exterior block wall, thus far we havn't had any major issues with road noise when recording drums in there. i read on hear that a stone wall is a great thing to have and i have in our yard (i'm also a builder, and as such will be getting all the labour free and materials at a big discount.) a load of sandstone. i was planning on cladding the stud wall on the right hand side of the new live room in split sandstone both for looks and to give the wall a rough texture and a good dense surface. i plan to fill the room corners with those solid bass traps i've seen on here and the ceiling corners, mid ceiling and walls will have some absorber panels fixed out off the wall to help the sound. so, questions for the collective brain: 1. will the stone clad wall be a good addition sound wise? will the rough stone finish be enough to help reduce reflections or should the wall be angled too? 2. similarly is it enough to angle the absorber panels on the walls or should i angle the walls and fix the panels parallel with the walls? 3. i plan to simply paint the exposed steel bean as a feature, is it likely to cause any strange sound reflections, or am i over thinking? as far as budget goes, mostly all i need is materials as i'll be getting the labour for free but i'm aiming at £2000-£3000 as after the room is finished i'll need light fittings, floor finishes etc, etc, the initial budget is just to get walls and ceiling finished and panels in place. thanks for taking the time to read this and sorry the images aren't in sketchup, but i havn't had the chance to get the hang of it yet. dave. fuseboxstudios :horse: (love that smiley!!!)

Soundman20202 May 2010#5
Hi Dave, and welcome! First question: can the existing interior walls all come out, or are some of them structural, or cannot be moved for other really good reasons? The reason I ask is because you seem to be wasting an awful lot of space, the shape of your control room leaves much to be desired, and I think I can see ways for you to do a much better layout. Also, I may be wrong, but reckon you can probably forget the "polystyrene bead" idea: polystyrene is normally closed-cell insulation, which is practically worthless, acoustically. - Stuart -
