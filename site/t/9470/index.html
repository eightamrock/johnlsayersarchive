xSpace26 September 2007#4
As standard building practice goes (USA) MDF is not considered a good product in any area that has potential to encounter dampness or moisture. By design MDF is little more then sawdust and glue. If your roof ever has a leak the affected MDF will simply deteriorate and become a weak area in the roof having potential to cause harm to those that may have to repair it. And it swells if it gets wet...then it crumbles apart. For a roof, OSB or plywood has a better structural integrity, then maybe you could apply MDF under that? Your mileage may vary,

how dense is the top layer? if its not too dense then it shouldn't be too much of a problem - is the air gap between them sealed or open (to allow air flow and avoid moisture build up)? if its open, you won't have a significant spring there so it shouldn't act as a third leaf.
John Sayers3 October 2007#10
Mike - that looks correct - the colourbond is not treated as a leaf as it's open to the outside air, plus it's damn thin. From the studio upwards the layers should go: internal drywall (two layers preferably) (Ist Leaf) resilient channel smartjoist with insulation yellow tongue (two layers if possible within budget.) (second leaf) battens building blanket colourbond. which looks like what you've done. cheers john
