The Silent Coup wrote:
So once one has calculated all the modes/resonances etc, say at 41-59-65-72-81hz etc.
You're in good hands with Ro, but I'll add two relevant points: 1) The only way to know for sure where a room has problem frequencies is to measure. I've seen mode calculations be off by as much as 20 percent from what is measured. The problem is mode calculators assume perfectly rigid walls with no losses. Real-world walls (and floors and ceilings) flex some, and so absorb some, and the very act of absorbing shifts the resonant frequencies lower. 2) For most rooms, especially smaller rooms, you can toss all those frequencies aside and just use broadband absorption. --Ethan

Hey Chris. Some quick answers for your questions:
In order to measure the frequency characteristics of a room, does one require a specialist measurement microphone?
A measurement mic is best, but any mic with reasonably flat response in the range 50 Hz to about 8kHz. will work. Best kept secret: Some sound level meters have direct output jacks for their mics... You can probably pick up a good one on e-bay for a few bucks or so, with any luck.
or would a decent quality mic suffice?
If the response is reasonably flat, it will work: check the manufacturer's published response curve for the mic you have in mind. As long as the curve doesn't wiggle up and down more than around 3 dB or so for the area of the spectrum you are interested in, you should be OK.
and what analysis software is there for this task?
ETF is a good one. Costs around US$ 150, I think. Room EQ Wizard is also good, and is about US$ 150 cheaper than ETF! (Yup, it's free....) - Stuart -
