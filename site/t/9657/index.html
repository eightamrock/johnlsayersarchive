This comes up semi-regularly. Yes it is well known amongst the experienced designers/builders. My generic response is
Use whatever is the cheapest and fits what you need. 703 generically is "3.0 lb/ft^3 fiberglass". Roughly speaking acousitcally the Rockwool equivalent generically is "4.0 lb/ft^3 mineral wool" Just some of the companies that have similar productrs are: JM Roxul Knauf Delta Fibrex
and
In my neck of the woods Roxul safe n Sound is the price bargain being about one third 703 in similar thickness. The Safe N Sound is 3.5" thick, but the absorption is comparable to 703. Call your local commercial insulation companies and ask.

I know John, used them also. I just put my post as opposed to a circular saw (overkill, dusty and not safe since wool not really stable). For most boards a good knife works faster, but for a DIYer electric bread knives give easy to control cuts. I worked for an international company with several thousand people industrial isolating on a daily basis with mineral wool on a continuous basis (that means buying mineral wool of several types/brands with truckloads), and they used 2 tools in the field: 1) such knives for boards: http://www.clearskymedia.co.uk/insulati ... ckwool.htm http://www.rockwool.ee/sw42051.asp (there are different types hence don't look all the same) If these electric bread knives should be faster (costing little bought in mass versus cost man hours) they should use them or design more adapted versions. Mineral wool is produced and used with hardly imaginable quantities a day. 2) and these large manual "Hedge shears" for these chicken wire blankets: http://www.orchardsedge.com/tools.jsp?type=hedgeshears 3) And for foam we used either a knife or this Bosch foam cutter: http://www.perfectfit.com/product.asp?P ... 2&l2=15374 It's the same principle as such an electric bread knive, but the guide rolls on small minni rollers and it's finer, much sharper and complete different quality. It easily allows to make nice straight cuts. But's it's not as cheap as bread knives, far from. Phunkeman wrote:
Now I don't know off hand what kind of blade is on my saw probably something that leaves a small kerf. I don't know, but I will go outside and look and get back to you guys if your interested. What can it hurt.
Some things: 1) It isn't safe and I doubt any safety engineer should allow this method with a hand circular saw. 2) It's complete overkill. That industrial isolators, with quantities thousand times larger than yours, use a knife is not because they can't afford an electric circular hand saw. 3) That safety thing over your saw blade, is designed to encounter hard, sturdy materials, not soft boards. I can imagine that it can easily stuck into the soft board if not doing it carefully. 4) To get through thick boards the saw blade diameter becomes too large needing a large and heavy type of tool, making it unpractical to control on flexible boards and even more dangerous. 5) It's bad for the tool itself, if used a lot. Mineral wool dust/particles (whether fiberglass or rockwool) is as grinding powder/particles (made from hard volcanic rock, e.g. diabase or basalt, or sand). The tool has to be protected against it, or you must suck the dust away with a vacuum system. And with the width of the blade teeth you convert a lot of mineral wool into dust. Which is also why (among some other things) a lot of foam is still used for absorption in relative narrow industrial enclosures where absorption is used. Your tool or motor sucks in this grinding powder/particles for its ventilation/cooling. And you can hardly compare a motor and value of a good circular saw with one of an electric bread knife, creating much less dust to begin with, since it works as scissors. 6) If you made a small mis cut needing to further cut only few mm away then the bread knife as suggested by John is the perfect answer. And if you feel more comfortable with such an electric knife than a standard good knife, you can use it for all cutting work.
