John Sayers6 February 2005#5
Mike - the smaller the room the more treatment is required to deaden it at all frequencies, NOTE I said at all frequencies. I'd suggest the trouble you've had with small rooms is that they've been dead ONLY in the high end. Your small booth fully treated will work IMO - what is your ceiling height?? There is another area there for treatment. The opposing walls in the control room can actually have similar treatment if you build the right wall inside out - i.e. the drywall goes on the right side of the wall leaving an open cavity on the innerside that can also be treated as a slot resonator. The 703 goes all the way up the wall plus I'd add a ceiling cloud depending on your ceiling height.
4.....the most important one: Would it be beneficial for me to go forward with a design like the one posted rather than just leaving the room open and treating the perimeter walls as best as i can?
Most definitely - you need this much treatment to get a small room like that to work and you've got the opportunity to treat the lowmids with the slots and the low bass with the heavy trapping at the rear. The glass windows and the timber slots will also stop the room from being too dead in the high end so the room will sound tight but not dead. A timber floor would also help in the high end. cheers john
