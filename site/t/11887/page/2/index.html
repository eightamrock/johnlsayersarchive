Cold19 April 2009#42
Part of the confusion is not realizing the difference between "blown" and "sprayed". Blown cellulose has very low density. Sprayed cellulose has much higher density and a bonding agent (glue or starch). Sprayed cellulose is a mixture of blown fiber and a liquid, either glue or water depending on whether the fiber has an adhesive built in or not, that mix together in the air before hitting the surface to be sprayed. Blown fiber is dry fiber with no or very minimal liquid (used for dust reduction) that is normally blown into sealed up walls or between the rafters in attics. very low density, very fluffy. Check the dimensions of your live room, it looks close to a square to me this will give you problems. Steve

Cold20 April 2009#45
The dimensions of your live room do matter, just not as much or in the same way as your control room. Symmetry is not needed, as you are not worried about balanced imaging from a stereo source. Asymmetry is sometimes helpful as it will aid in different "sounding" areas in the same room. But a square room is going to create nulls and peaks that you will have to be aware of every time you set up a mic, position instruments, arrange musicians... It creates an avoidable headache. Why not aim for a golden ratio or something close to it if possible? You're building from scratch, make the most of it. As a side note: On a purely practical level I would not use any form of blow in as cavity fill between double stud walls. Too damn messy if you ever need to access that space. It would have to have a large benefit for me to go that route. And I spray insulation and fireproofing as a day job! Steve

Steve, The idea is not to completely fill a cavity between panels. Never is and never was. In an exterior wall, it is just the right thing to do. As to ever having to access the void in between a room-in-a-room type wall construction, it's not a place that people tend to open back up, ever ;) But whatever the OP wants to do is fine, batt or sprayed in urethane, blown in cellulose, etc. As long as he knows his options.
