That actually won't help much with isolation, since you still have to bolt/screw/nail through that to keep the studs in place. The rubber underneath helps to create a better seal, if you have an uneven floor, but doesn't do much to decouple.

It's unlikely that you'd get STC 56 just from a single layer of drywall on RC. The pamphlet probably specifies more than just that, such as an air gap of insulation fill. Also, you should be aware that STC is a lousy way of measuring isolation for a studio: STC does not cover the most important frequencies at the low end of the spectrum. You should be using TL.
The W Wall will be my super wall
Actually, trying to do one wall better than the others isn't useful. Isolation is "all or nothing", meaning that you need to isolate all sides of yoru room to th same level. Isolation is only as good as the weakest link. Think of it this way: Even the best isolated studio in the world has no isolation at all when the door is open...

that seems rather high! Green Glue is really good at improving the part of the spectrum that STC doesn't even cover, at the low end. Green Glue does not improve isolation directly, but only adds constrained layer damping to the system, so the best gains are in the low end.

A wall to give you STC-70 is also pretty hard to do! To give you an idea of what is involved here, the difference between a wall that gives you 40 db of isolation and a wall that gives you 70 dB of isolation is a factor of TEN THOUSAND PERCENT. The 70 dB wall must absorb or reflect one thousand times more energy than the 40 dB wall.
The resulting all will be d-GG-d, chan, clip, 2x4 w/ batt-ins, 1 inch space, 2x6 stud built by the house builders with batt insulation, , 1" space, 2x4 w/ batt-ins, clip, chan, d-GG-d. So this wall is going to be super soundproof and super thick...
Well, you seem to be decouple that wall more than necessary! You only need to decouple it once. Anything more is a wast of time, money ant effort. Isolation is governed by the principles of physics, and there just aren't any magical products, magical sequences, or magical incantations that will improve on the isolation that the laws of physics define, despite what some manufacturers want you to believe! Only three things matter: Mass, distance, and decoupling. Adding Genie clips to both side of the wall is not really necessary (with few exceptions). Adding resilient channel to Genie clips is no necessary. And adding both of them to an already fully decoupled wall won't gain you anything. The wall you describe will have a resonant frequency of 24 Hz. It will not isolate until 33 Hz, and will only isolate well above 72 Hz. It will give you maybe 60-something TL, if you seal it correctly and build it correctly.

The brand does not matter. What matters is the density and the gas flow resistivity. You can use mineral wool, or fiberglass, or semi-rigid fiberglass, but in each case it must have the correct characteristics.

I'm not sure about his configuration but it involves 3 panes per window (or three sections).
That sounds like a 3-leaf design to me! Not a good idea. This is not intuitive, and most people don't realize it, but when all other factors are equal, a three-leaf system will ALWAYS give you WORSE isolation than a two-leaf system.
