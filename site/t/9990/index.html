rod gervais7 January 2008#2
Tom, close........ What you want is 2 times the volume in size - and you want this to be long enough so that you can install at least 2 baffles before existing the silencer - wioth the runs through those baffles equal to the duct. So - for a 12" square duct (and I would use the same for a round duct) it would be clear inside dimensions of 2'x2' at the end - yes measured from the inside face of insulation. you would then have a baffle 2' high by 1' long running parallel to the duct inlet which would be 1' clear inside measured from the inside of the insulation in the box. Then you would install (on the opposite wall) another baffle with a 12" clear opening measured from the inside face of the 1st baffle - and then 12" clear to the endof the box. So the box would be 2'x2' clear inside - and then would measure a total of 3'-4" clear to the outlet if you were using 2" thick baffles. BUT - I don't reccomend the baffles be made out of just rigid duct board - but rather a MDF board or plywood which can be rabbetted into the box and thus give structural strength to it - these are then wrapped with the ridig fiberglass to quiet then noise down. Done in this manner you decrease the velocity by 1/2. I hope you can follow that. Rod - and then

Yes that would be fine- one comment though - the reason for the baffle is 2 fold - 1. stop the transmission of any sound that might be being carried through the duct itself. 2. slow down the velocity with the intent of quieting the ruch of air into the room. If you size the outlet the same as the inlet you will not acheive the 2nd goal - the velocity will step up to exactly the same as it was before - If you are working with inexpensive grilles and louvers this will result in a lot of extra noise....... The louvers (for example) that I specify come from a company called Nailor - have a NC (noise criterion) rating of 0 at 450 cfm - and around 15 at 650 cfm - (15 is below the level of hearing (on average) - but they also run as much as 850 dollars apiece - If you want to work with inexpensive louvers then you probably want your outlets the same size (in arera) as your box- resulting in the lower velocity....... sor for a inlet of 12 x 12 - you would want your outlet to be a full 1x2 (or adjust the box accordingly and make it 1.5 square. OK? Rod

The passive intake has to connect directly to your HVAC return duct (and close to the air handler unit at that) just in order to work. Your least expensive option would be to take the line in seperately - and add it's own inline electric heat element to temper the air.
