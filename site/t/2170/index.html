knightfly24 August 2004#3
Actually Colin, if you're referring to the hollow concrete blocks like the ones with about 25mm concrete each side, and two large holes in the middle separated by 3 webs of concrete, then that by itself is effectively a double leaf wall - consider the webs as the studs in a framed wall, and the inner and outer concrete surfaces = inner and outer sheathing. Now, add a framed wall with gypsum on both sides, separated from the blocks by an air gap, and you have a 4-leaf wall (not nearly as lucky as a 4-leafed clover :=( For a hollow block wall to work well in sound construction, two ways to go would be 1 - fill the blocks with sand or grout, making it a single leaf of high mass, then add a frame that's isolated from the blocks, and put gypsum on ONE side of that frame only. Insulation filling the gap between gypsum and blocks of course. 2 - Render both sides of the blocks, and maybe strip-glue an extra layer or two of gypsum wallboard on the inside (be careful here, moisture can make this a really bad idea) In either case, if the outside of the wall isn't dry you need to clean it, and seal with a really good water barrier, as well as making sure it's properly drained AWAY from the wall. The following PDF file covers this VERY thoroughly - http://www.buildingscience.com/resource ... ystems.pdf Hope that helped... Steve

If those are the blocks I'm thinking of, they are fairly porous - if so, a render coat (or at least a couple of wash coats) over them would improve TL by several dB - still, basically they would (if no holes for weepage, etc) be a solid outer mass leaf, requiring only an air gap (larger the better) an inner frame and insulation, and a couple layers of gyp board as your inner leaf. Any acoustic treatment/traps, etc, inside that... steve
