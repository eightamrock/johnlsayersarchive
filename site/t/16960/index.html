Soundman20205 September 2011#2
The gap isn't your problem: the entire room is. Seal the gap with acoustic caulk if you want, but it isn't going to make much difference. And neither is the sheet of drywall you added. Best case, that might increase the isolation that you had before by maybe 3 or 4 dB: barely noticeable. The issue is the room. "Soundproofing" just one wall of a room is not going to accomplish much: you have to do the other five sides as well. You cannot "soundproof" a room by just treating one wall any more than you can build an aquarium by putting glass on just one side of the frame. It is an "all-or-nothing" proposition. Your mother-in-law may well be impatient, but no amount of impatience can cause the laws of physics to change (not even for a mother-in-law!).
who has one side of the house (soon to be walled off)
Once again, building a wall across the entire house is not going to help. Airborne sounds from your studio will simply bend around that wall, and structure-borne sounds will "flank" around it. The studio has to be fully decoupled from the rest of the house to prevent flanking, and has to be fully isolated on all six sides in order to prevent low frequency airborne sound wrapping around. Obvious paths in that photo (aside from the huge gap) are the electrical box in the ceiling, the ceiling itself, and the door. All of those are big issues, without even being able to see the rest of the structure. Sorry to be the bearer of bad tidings, but what you mother-in-law wants simply cannot be accomplished overnight, and certainly not in the way this is going right now. - Stuart -

adam12 September 2011#5
Hi Fiz, Specifically for your question about the gap, if the wall extends up beyond the height of the ceiling as it appears to in the last photo you posted, you may be able to poke backing rod in there & caulk it. Otherwise, if you can get access to it from above, you might be better off beefing it up with strips of gyprock from up there. Watch out for snakes, they reckon 1 in 3 roof cavities in Queensland has a python in it :) The main point is that the part of your outer shell with the least mass determines the isolation, so make sure it's not that corner. The best supplier of backing rod (and sikaflex, caulk etc) I've found on the GC is Waterproofing Warehouse at Bundall. They sell backing rod by the meter in lots of different diameters, up to about 30mm.
