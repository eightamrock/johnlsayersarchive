While your at it I got this on another forum....... Tip #2: If using a conventional 2x4 wall (no decoupling, no double studs, just drywall screwed to studs), use wood furring strips perpendicular to the studs. If you have 16" on-center studs, put the strips 24" on-center, if you have 24" on-center studs, put them 16" on-center. This isn't documented anywhere as far as i know, but it's worth the following: -lowers resonance a bit -improves performance at resonance by a cuople dB -improves performance above resonance by several dB I would estimate that this is at least as effective as adding a third layer of drywall to a normal drywall-stud-drywall conventional wall. Although i konw it is the recommendation of this forum in the FAQ, just lobbing drywall onto a wall isn't going to get you far, and drywall isn't that cheap unless you're installing it yourself. Put 8 layers per side, that'll get you somewhere. just plain 1x3 wood furring strips of the bunkiest kind. It does alot for a wall in combinatoin with VE damping, long story. This is more useful with 16" OC studs and 24" OC furring strips than 24" OC studs and 16" OC strips. net cost: something like $1.75-$2 per 8' long strip, or ~ 15 cents/square foot. len-morgan24 April 2006#3
carpwftx wrote:
What are furring strips??
Just thin strips of wood (probably 1x2s). They can be made fairly cheaply because they "finger join" smaller pieces of wood that would otherwise be scrap wood. I think the gain you'd see by doing this is not that magical. Furing strips do two things: 1) stiffen the wall by keeping the studs from moving (left and right) and 2) since they would move the drywall out about 3/4" from the current studs, you get more "air" in your mass-air-mass equation. The advantage of going from 16 OC studs to 24 OC furring strips is that the area of drywall supported between screws is bigger. As you know, a bass drum is bigger than a snare and it has a lower fundamental frequency. Therefore, your completed wall would have a lower resonance than it would on 16 OC studs. As to the rest of your questions, I don't have a sketchup viewer anymore so I can't comment. I would guess that your first task would be to seal the building from the weather no matter what you end up doing to the inside. I don't know if plastic sheeting would cut it though. As to the corrugated roof, you might want to consider some sort of spray on stuff (either inside or outside) for insulation (from hot/cold) and to dampen the "drum head" effect of big flat sheets of metal. It would be better IMHO to keep it from making the noise than to try and isolate it from your room after the fact. len

SonicClang24 April 2006#5
Don't use firring strips to strengthen the studs and connect them together, that's what stringers between the 2 X 4's are for. Firring strips are pretty much good for one thing; if you want to put drywall up against a concrete wall and don't have a lot of floor space to put full 2 X 4's. They don't decouple anything. And plus, unless you use the metal ones, good luck finding enough good ones to use. Usually the lumber yard's stock of the wood firring strips is trash. Either way you're wasting your money.
