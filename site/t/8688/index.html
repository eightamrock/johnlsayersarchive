jwl26 April 2007#4
It's tricky to be both soundproof and portable. First, a note about terminology. There are great misconceptions out there about what "soundproofing" means. Just look at the "buy our soundproofing foam" ads to see what I mean. In general, soundproofing is related to isolation, in that a well-soundproofed room will exhibit good isolation characteristics. For your vocal booth, the key factors for isolation will be airtightness and mass, and to a lesser extent flanking noise, which I wouldn't worry too much about given your application (ie, not much low-end SPL to worry about in your recordings). You'll need a good, heavy, solid core door. From there, I'd probably build John's inside-out walls into a vocal booth. Seal the booth with plenty of caulk so that it is airtight, and use 2 layers of sheetrock on the outside. sealing the door will be the trickiest part. Then inside the booth put insulation between the joists and cover the whole inside with cloth. You'll get a nice dead vocal booth. Presuming you do the construction correctly, that should get you a pretty good performing booth; good isolation, but again making it portable will be a trick. I suppose with the inside-out walls, you could fasten them together with bolts that you can remove, but you'll still need to deal with the caulking issue.

tmix26 April 2007#6
Paul, I have 2 ideas, you can ignore both if you like. If i understand your needs correctly, you just need a non-reverberant quiet, semi-dead enviornment to record spoken word. It also needs to be free from your computer noise. If so then: a)If your room is already isolated enough where you only need to trap the noise inside the room, you can simply make / buy (3-4) free-standing absorbers that you can place around you when you record. they can be as simple as 1 x 4 wood frames with plywood etc on one side, with mineralwool or compressed fiberglass inside and material cover on the side facing you. You can mount them to a legs, stands or each other by hinges to where you can get them out of your way when necessary. just place them around you between you and your noise source (computer) b) If your noise problem is only the computer you can build a cabinet to place around you computer to quiet it, although you have to take into consideration it keeping cool enough to not overheat but if your problem is more severe than that see JWL idea about the inside out walls, which are basically all the mass (wood / sheetrock, etc.) attached to the outside of the wall with the insulation in between the studs and material only for the inside so that the insulation in the wall cavities are also your sound absorbers. I am thinking that unless it is pretty noisey in your room, you only need minimal mass, probably mostly absorption. My thoughts obviously. Tom
