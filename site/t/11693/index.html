Hello People, Firstly - thank you for all the great information on this forum :D I am located in Johannesburg - South Africa and recently moved onto a new property where I have the space to build a dedicated studio. The studio would be a new isolated structure with the nearest other structure being the family home apprx 10 meters away. I have received the go ahead from the local authorities so it is getting time to commit to the layout. I would like to record live instruments including drums. And would then also like to achieve the isolation to prevent the family getting upset as one mans music is anothers noise - In the CR normal levels would be 80-90dB SPL. Budget is around 250000ZAR which is approx 25000EUR - I would also need to furnish with this and hopefully have some left for more gear. After browsing this forum for hours and reading Rod Gervais book - I have come up with a layout of sorts and would welcome any comments/advice you wish to offer. All the below refers to the attached zipped sketchup model of my proposed layout. The structures' outer walls would need to be 9 inch brick and mortar with a pitched tiled roof at 27 degrees with the apex centered along the longest dimension. I propose then for the inside of the outer walls to have an air gap with a single stud drywall system. CR/Live room divider to be dual stud drywall system. The CR is sunken around 600mm as this allows me to use the area available for outboard gear (see file for graphic) and the Live room is at an intermediate level for better CR view of the live room. The CR and live room would be on separate haunched floor slabs. I have not yet explored all the options for interior acoustic treatment - but have opted for a slanted CR wall. The roof would use scissor trusses so for the live room I could follow the truss with the ceiling system to gain as much volume as possible. For the CR I intend for a slanted ceiling which is 8ft in front and 10ft at the rear. I use a DAW with dual displays and analogue desk with outboard mic pres etc - this may give an idea for CR ergonomics. Soffit mounted mains would be great with stand mounted nearfields. Gobos can be used in the live room but I normally track instruments individually after the guide track is down. A drum riser which is decoupled from the rest of the live room floor would be interesting. Please let me know what your thoughts are on the layout attached - I had to zip to keep within the 500k but wanted to put the model up as all the dimensions and fiddly bits are in there. Thanks for your time. Sean

NativeLuv1723 February 2009#8
Thanks Sean, It's been a long time coming. :wink: Now if I see your layout proposal correctly, your entry door between the LR and the CR is located on the far left corner of the CR correct? If this is the case then your corners are exposed, and you need to trap those. If you don't, you will be getting some major low frequency issues. And the soffits on the side are not helping you there any, because you basically have 2 tiny rooms at each corner open to the rest of the structure, which I would assume, would allocate the low frequencies to this area. Now I am far from an expert, so I would suspect someone with a bit more knowledge will poke their head in, I'm just giving you my two cents. Anyhow, the reason Lilith and I suggested a sliding door in the middle, would be so you have access to the rooms, while maintaining communication to the talent. Then, put your soffits in the corner of the room, which can double as bass traps which will take care of your corners. Does that help any?

We'll I don't mean to sound like a broken record but, I believe modes tend to meet at the corners of the room and low frequencies resonate there. So what your plan tends to show is you building those small spaces between your room corners and the back of the soffits. :shock: If you attempt to do this, i would assume most of the low end would be sucked into that area, which is very bad. What you seem to be trying to do is reposition the corners in your room through false walls, but this won't have the effect that you think it will; the corner will be a corner regardless and still needs treatment.
