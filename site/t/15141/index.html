Soundman202012 November 2010#2
2. How to fix the wall into the concrete ? I wanted some kind of decoupling from the bottom of the wall. What can I use to achieve a solid fixing, as well as vibration transmission-less ?
You can't: you live in Greece, which is very seismically active. Lots of earthquakes. You can't leave your wall frames un-attached to the floor. I don't know what your local building code requires, but most likely you will need to bolt your sole plates to the floor, maybe every 80cm or so (maybe more, maybe less; check your building code to find out). You can probably put rubber underneath your sole plate if you want (and if code allows), but it will only serve to seal the gap (which is good!). It won't help much to isolate the wall, since the bolts create a direct flanking path. You have the same problems as I do: we both live in earthquake territory, so we have to take precautions in design and construction that some others just don't need to worry about. So you have no choice here; just bolt the wall to the floor. It's a concrete floor, and it is laid directly on the ground, right? No other rooms below you? If so, then that's fine. You will still get good isolation like that. - Stuart -

using bracing to prevent lateral shifts is what the sway support does. using the WIC or kinetics braces will help on the walls. if you suspend the ceiling, you want the ceiling to remain stable (relatively) so you would add additional cables etc need to ensure the ceiling is held in place regards of the direction of lateral force. the joists as shown would possible prevent the walls from coming in. and if the air gaps, double leaf, etc is correct, could be your inner ceiling support as well.
