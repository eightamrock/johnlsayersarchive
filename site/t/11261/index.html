studio_drums24 September 2008#2
I believe the gap around a door frame is common and unavoidable. Just fill any open spaces with fiberglass and/or foam and caulk- making it airtight. If the rest of your wall construction and door seals are in good shape, it won't compromise the sound isolation that much. This is my experience, anyway... There are diagrams around that show the backer rod and caulk filling all gaps.

studio_drums24 September 2008#4
JC Whitney sells trunk rubber that is popular for door seals, but if you're using two doors, just get some exterior door weather stripping for both doors- if installed correctly that should suffice. For my build I have two doors. One is an exterior door with standard weather stripping and it's airtight. IT was the old door to the garage. The other door was a Rod Gervais "superdoor" I built (but without the sheet lead). I bought a threshold for it but didn't even have to use it since the other door was so well sealed. I'm think that two doors are better than one since it provides a bit of a safety net for us DIY'ers...between the two you should get one good seal- LOL. That's my experience. YMMV

studio_drums15 October 2008#12
I stacked 2" thick Owens-Corning 703 cut into triangular pieces from floor to ceiling(54 of them!), then built a homemade speaker grill which I nailed directly to the wall. Doesn't get any simpler. A typical 2x4 foot piece of 703 can be cut into 8 triangles. You can also do a google search for "superchunk" corner bass traps, as they're called. I have a bass trap build thread that's more popular than the stickies, according to the # of views, if you're interested: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=10297
