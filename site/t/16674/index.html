Hi "himigmusika", and welcome! Sorry nobody saw your thread before! It happens sometimes...
The last dimensions will give you a good size control room with walls using the m-a-m (mass-air-mass) principle with a two leaf system. For a two leaf system there are four common construction methods.
There isn't any relationship between how you build the room, and the room ratio. Two different things. The ratio would be just as good if you built the room as a single-leaf concrete bunker, or with 6-leaves of lead and steel plate! ratios are one thing, the method of isolating the room is another. But anyway, don't get too hung up on ratios: stay away from the bad ones, get close to a good one, and you'll be fine.
My question is; what are the angle degrees of my walls he designed for me?
Those don't look like angled walls to me: more like angled treatment! But you'd have to ask Glenn himself to confirm that. In any event, of you are planning to splay your walls to get an RFZ, then you need at least 6° splay on each side, and 12° on the ceiling. That could vary, depending on speaker axes intersect angles, and other factors.
That’s my question is, how much space is needed, or will it just follow the angle of the inner wall to determine the air gap from the wall on outer part or the wall that is sticking in the concrete wall.
I'm not sure I understand the question, but for an MSM wall you should have at least 4" or air space. It can be bigger, and it can change along the wall (if the inner leaf and outer leaf are not parallel), but try to ensure that it is always 4" or more. If you have enough space, then make it 6" or even 8". But how much isolation do you need?
is that, Rigid Fiber Glass or Fluffy Fiber Glass.
It doesn't really matter: wither will work.
And the construction, is it two boards, then stud, and fiber inside it, and then no wall next to it, and the air gap then the stud then the board on the other side.
Correct. You have two stud frames, and each frame has drywall on only ONE side of it. So normally it would be: Drywall (one of more layers) Studs with insulation Empty space (maybe also with insulation) Studs with insulation Drywall (one of more layers) However, you can also do what is called an "inside-out" wall, which would be like this: Drywall (one of more layers) Studs with insulation Empty space (maybe also with insulation) Drywall (one of more layers) Studs In that case, the second leaf is "inside out". Or you can do both leaves inside-out, like this: Studs Drywall (one of more layers) Empty space with insulation Drywall (one of more layers) Studs There are various reasons for doing that, but it is possible and will work.
I just guess the angles, and when I found out that the two front wall with soffit mount speaker should be 30 degrees,
That is the most common way of doing it, yes, but you can change the angles if you need to, in order to get a good listening position in the room. You can go up to as much as 45% if you need to.
There's really no need for heating, cause in our country, there's only two weather, sunny and rainy.
You wont need heat, but you WILL need air conditioning! The walls must be sealed air-tight, and you will have a lot of insulation around it, so it will get very hot in there, very fast, and also very humid. You need air conditioning. And you also need to get fresh air in, and take stale air out. - Stuart -
