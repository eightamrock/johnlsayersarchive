Maybe the scent of an idea for the Moderators.

Started by Lou on 14 February 2007. 11 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 8195.

Lou14 February 2007#1
Hi Guys – I hope you’re all well. I’ve been thinking on and off about this post for sometime, but if this idea has cropped up in the past and already been thrown out of court, then please accept my apologies. :oops: For those of you who have seen my design thread you’ll know that I started reading these forums around July last year. Whilst you may be aware of my overwhelming enthusiasm for the incredible level of advice and expertise they offer, I keep seeing total confusion from ‘posters’, concerning the correct interpretation of English. When I say keep seeing, I mean somewhere in just about every thread I’ve looked at. :shock: I’ve done a quick search on the member list and found 50 or so users who have listed their location as UK or United Kingdom, but I’m sure there’ll be at least a couple of hundred or so others that have put their town or county first. Some of these people that have caught my attention are Redeye from Edinburgh (loads of help from Glenn there), the chap in St neots (whose name escapes me), then there’s Ozzy over in Kent, and last night I discovered Jamie up in Buckinghamshire. As I pointed out to Jamie, in American terms at least, these Guys are all practically neighbours! (Five United Kingdoms, into one Colorado, or whatever.) When you consider that with all of us, and all of you kind fellows, having English as our first language, (sorry if that’s not the case), it seems a bit daft not to have a sort of ‘glossary of terms and definitions’ thing going on, that bridges the language barrier somewhat, not the technical stuff as this is all clearly laid out at the back of Rod’s book, and furthermore, there are already some great stickies up here. I just mean for materials and hardware. Maybe this could eventually become a sticky in itself. To give you just a couple of examples, (and there really are loads!), you Guys know what ‘caulk’ is, but if I held a ‘Mastic Gun’ to your head, would you run? laugh?, or go and get a bucket to catch the ‘caulk’? If, before I started reading here, you asked me to provide a definition of ‘Mud’, I’d have told you that is was the stuff that sticks to the soles of your shoes in a wet field! I would still struggle to accurately explain the RC’s you often refer to in your ceiling assemblies! We even have a Guy in Australia asking John what Roxul 703 is! To be honest I’d love to really be sure about this one too! :? There is further confusion when referring to a product and or, its brand name. If any of us in the UK conducted a survey in any of our home towns, and asked a hundred people to explain what a ‘Dyson’ was, I guarantee at least 80% would say that it was a ‘Hoover’ rather than say it was a ‘Vacuum Cleaner’. That’s really stupid, but it’s true. There must be many examples in the States of exactly the same scenario, but as I mentioned earlier, we all speak totally fluent English. Imagine how confusing this could become for those of us who are Dutch, Swiss, Greek, or Portuguese. I have a simple confession to make, until three weeks ago I’d have been certain that ‘Green Glue’ was what we in the UK call ‘Gripfill’, after all it’s green, in a green tube, and sticks ‘Gyproc’ to anything. Gyproc btw is a make of plasterboard! (getting’ my drift?) :lol: What threw me off this assumption was the price you Chaps kept referring to, then I thought, we must be lucky because that stuff is much cheaper here! I then googled the product and of course now know exactly what it is, and it aint ‘Gripfill’! :evil: My point then Gentlemen, is that we consider providing a quick visual reference for all to understand exactly what we’re all talking about. I’m not suggesting for one minute that MDF be translated into 20 languages but a picture or brief definition of all the main things we keep mentioning might prove very useful to many members, both new and old, and to realise that ‘Codes and Permits’ are what we call ‘Building Regulations and Planning Applications’ is bound to help our ‘European’ colleagues no end. This reference could be added to as and when necessary. One of my favourites is the ‘Fluffy Stuff’. ‘Fluff’ has never made me itch like hell for days on end! I know that this material comes in different thicknesses and densities, but what we’re referring to here (from a UK perspective), is fibre glass loft insulation. :o I sincerely hope this is not coming across as a moan, because I can assure you it isn’t. This is nothing more than an observation with a constructive suggestion. If you feel this might be worth pursuing, I’d be more than happy to take accurate descriptions from you Guys, and then go to a reputable Builder’s Merchants (sorry – hardware store!) and get the answers for the UK chaps, and I feel it would only be a matter of time before other Nationalities got on board. I guess I’m seeing a kind of excel spreadsheet/chart arrangement with the various regional areas listed across the top row – (U.S.A. – Australia – UK – Scandinavia – Central Europe – Eastern Europe – Middle East – Far East – Africa - whatever), photos and brief descriptions of what various things are for down the left hand column, and then the appropriate product/brand names plus alternatives, in the blanks. I also feel that from a Moderator’s point of view, this could save you Guys loads of time in the future. (Providing people read it of course!) :roll: To conclude in a way which I hope ‘rams this point home’, some of you state-side of the pond, might use the same slang word to describe a Woman’s bottom, as we in the UK might use to describe her ‘most private area’, now other than in an adult movie, that could lead to no end of confusion. :shock: Warm regards and thanks as always Guys, Lou. 8)
