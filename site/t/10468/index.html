xSpace18 April 2008#7
The left to right of your house measurements, 11', 4'-3", 11'=26'-3". That number divided by 2 equals(roughly) your existing room wall length(top to bottom). That would be a square, I have heard square rooms and they are square. They do not sound inviting and are not recommended by anyone who matters. I couldn't find anything about what type of exterior material this structure has. Wood, brick, vinyl? But, as it stands, you already have a basic two leaf wall system. Everybody does :) So to build a framed wall structure on the interior of this build and place 1/2" rock on it will be counter-productive and is also, not recommended.(Unless you are considering removing the existing sheetrock from the walls...not a suggestion, just an observation.)
Sound proofing is not needed to isolate sound inside the house. I only need to make sure sounds from my neighbors aren't getting in when I'm doing voice overs. The loudest noise would be them mowing their lawns with riding mowers.
This will be a challenge. Not only are the windows going to need a "plug" each, the attic over your head will let sound in rather quickly. The floor, which is probably 3/4" with something else, maybe 1/2", on top, is also a weak area. Hope that helps.

xSpace22 April 2008#9
Cat Trax wrote:
So if I remove the sheet rock from the existing walls, is that the only way I can frame in inner walls and have them work properly?
I don't believe they would work like you are wanting. Structure borne sound and airborne sound when present, are going to find a way into your overhead and floor. When they do, they come right into the air space inbetween the exterior wall and the newly framed interior wall as well as the room. Some here have added mass to the interior of above ground rooms like you have, and made use of it for their music. Your time may be best spent re-visiting the use of your basement?
