framo19 August 2007#1
Hello All, My first post, but I've been reading for a long time. This forum has been an invaluable resource for browsing and collecting information. But now I have a question that I can't find the answer to in any previous posts! First some background: I'm building an 18x13 room in my garage for recording mostly acoustic blues, with small ensembles that will include drums, bass, vocals and horns. I'm using the garage's existing concrete floor, which is a separate slab. I will build a semi-independent ceiling. I am planning two layers of drywall greenglued on the inside of the room, and just insulation on the outside. I am currently in the process of sealing off the garage door with a 2x4 frame, 5/8 drywall on the other side and mineral fiber in between. (The garage door will not be used.) This is a residential neighborhood and my garage is in the front within about 15 feet of the sidewalk. A neighbor on one side is about 20 feet from the garage. The garage already has 1/2" drywall all the way around. From this forum and Rod Gervais' book I have learned that contrary to logic, it will actually be better for me to rip out the existing drywall and insulate between the studs for the room within a room to be effective. So I hacked into the existing drywall today and found that there is already fiberglass insulation in the walls. But it's a weird kind, with an aluminum foil type layer in the front and a vinyl backer sandwiched around the fiberglass. I think it's this: http://www.insulation4less.com/highr_FfgV.asp Now my question: Would I be better off not building the room within a room and simply green gluing another layer of drywall to the existing drywall? Or, does this foil-fiberglass-vinyl insulation create a bad mulitple leaf situation, and so therefore should be completely removed? Sign me, Flummoxed

Hai and welcome to this forum :) That's thermal insulation, which you can remove. Or if the insulation is flush against the outer wall, you can leave it in place. It'll do nothing much with sound absorption. From your text I can't really make out your current set up; how are ye walls setup for now? What about your ceiling?

Yes, I do have a big job ahead of me, but it helps a lot to have the wisdom of those who've gone before. This is actually my second home studio build.
