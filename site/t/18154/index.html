Soundman202011 February 2013#3
Hi "notes2k", and welcome! :)
My design uses the 1: 1.60: 2.33 ratio giving me 8'H x 13'W x 19'L and from there I splayed my walls at 12˚.
You say you are building a live room, not a control room, so ratios are not as important here. As long as your ratio is far away from the bad ones, you'll be fine. You also don't need to splay the walls like that.
My question is: In a room this small, would it be more beneficial to keep a rectangular room with the exact recommended ratio without splaying the walls?
I would keep it rectangular, or close to rectangular, in order to not waste space.
Should the flutter echo and such be controlled post build with acoustic treatment?
Yes. You could splay one or two of the walls by a much lesser amount if you want, to help with that. For example, you could splay one wall "out then back in", in an inverted "V" shape: that wastes much less space than splaying the entire wall.
I understand that there is no calculator to determine room modes on non-rectangular rooms.
Well, it can be calculated, but not easily. It requires sophisticated FEM/FEA computer programs, and an experienced operator to set up your room model in the software, set up the boundary conditions, run the analysis, the interpret the results for you. Expensive, complicated, and absolutely not necessary.
Would it be beneficial to cover the interior of the brick wall with insulation? I'm guessing it would create a better STC rating with insulation on both sides of the air gap but I"m not 100%.
More insulation means more damping on the cavity resonance, which is good. So yes, that would help PROVIDED THAT the overall isolation system is correctly designed and properly built. In other words, that both leaves are totally sealed, hermetically, and that they are also full decoupled, with enough mass on each and a large enough air gap between them. Also, STC is a lousy way of measuring studio isolation: it does not take into account low frequencies. - Stuart -
