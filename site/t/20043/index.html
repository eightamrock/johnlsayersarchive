Well, you probably CAN do both: isolate the entire place, and still have the second level... it just won't be much use as part of the studio. Perhaps for storage, or something like that, but upper levels are notoriously problematic for studios. If you look around, you'll notice that very few professional studios have upper levels. There's a reason for that. The few that do, have invested big money in making the upper level usable. If your main priority is just to have a place where you can write, rehearse, and perform music, but not really for tracking or mixing, then I'd just go with a single room as big as possible on the ground floor, isolated as well as possible, and either blow away the second level entirely (which would give you enviable acoustics, due to the wonderfully high ceilings), or keep it just for storage. You are on a very, very tight budget, so I'd keep it all as simple as possible: just isolate your one-and-only room using the "room within a room" principle, and spend the rest of the money on treating it as well as possible. And don't forget HVAC.... that alone is going to eat a huge chunk out of your budget. (Yes, you DO need it!) - Stuart -
