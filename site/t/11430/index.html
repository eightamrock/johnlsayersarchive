Soundman202028 November 2008#8
I'm a little confused still though, If symmetry is the most important, I would probably FACE the garage door,
That would be my plan too. Which kind of sinks your idea of using the garage door as a "sound sucker"! Another reason I'd go with the room facing the main door is because of your entry door at the rear. It seems to be more or less in the center, which would be good for symmetry and rear bass trapping in both corners (not sure how to work around that water heater, though: what does your local Code say about enclosing water heaters in closets?). If you had the room facing the water-heater end, you'd end up with your door right in between your speakers. That's not really bad, but it does mean you'd have to maneuver around the desk to to get in and out of your room, and I'm betting that your desk is going to take up more space than you have planned right now. (It must be one of Murphy's laws that sound equipment always expands and reproduces itself when you are not looking, and then you have to figure out where to put all the new stuff that appeared! :) ) So I'd say that the most logical layout for easy access is with the room facing the garage door. Seal that door, build bass traps in the corners, then test the room and see what other treatment you need. However, one grand is not going to get you very far at all. It might buy you enough rock wool to build some "Superchunk" bass traps, but that's all. What is the room made of? The floor looks like concrete, but what about the rest? Do you have a sound level meter?
