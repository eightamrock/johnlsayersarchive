knightfly3 June 2005#4
It's mostly not a "common issue" because few people add layers to a finished house; Things to think about - if YOU were a contractor building houses, and your bottom line got worse by "over-building", would you do it? Meaning, before you add another layer of drywall to a ceiling, it would be a good idea to ask the builder (or his architect, better) what the design load really is for that ceiling frame; it may already be maxxed out. What kind of "texture" are we talking about here - "popcorn", or some kind of knocked down splatter, or?? Steve
bpape3 June 2005#5
IF the joists will support the weight, you might find it easier to just use a wide taping knife and knock off the texture if you can rather than re-mudding. One other thing to consider... Generally, the ceiling is put up first and the edges of the sheets are held in place by the drywall on the walls. With a second layer and not a second layer of wall, you might find it 'fun' to find sufficient area to screw the edges.
Dan Fitzpatrick3 June 2005#6

knightfly5 June 2005#10
Agreed; get the widest taping knife you can find (at least 12") make the mud slightly runny (not too, though) and hold the knife at a very shallow angle (nearly flat with the ceiling) while pulling it across the surface, using fairly high pressure against the blade. This will give the smoothest surface you can get. It's too iffy for a ceiling to try and put the second layer up with the mud still wet, so it's better to let it dry completely before hanging the additional. Have you determined for SURE that your support is adequate? Steve
