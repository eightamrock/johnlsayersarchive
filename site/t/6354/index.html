Question about building in a finished basement

Started by Mark Blasco on 2 June 2006. 1 reply.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6354.

Mark Blasco2 June 2006#1
Me and the wife are looking to buy a new house in the next few months, and one of the things we have been looking for is a space for me to have a studio. The latest place we saw had a finished basement with cement floor, and aside from the ceiling height in the middle where a large beam runs across, it looks like it might work out for me. I bought the book "Home Recording Studio: Build it like the pro's", and now I am a bit confused. It says that you will get more isolation with 2 walls that only have drywall on one side, rather than 2 walls with drywall on both sides. In the case of an already finished basement, if I am planning to build walls inside of that for a control room and tracking room, would I be better off ripping down the drywall that is already there, or is that part of the seal for the outer leaf of the 2 leaf system? I am a bit confused on this part.
sharward2 June 2006#2
Yes, I'm afraid you will be better off ripping off the existing drywall inside the "finished" basement. (They finished it alright, but you then have to "unifinish" it, else you will have a three leaf system which is not good. Sealing is still of utmost importance. Welcome to the forum. Good luck with your househunting. --Keith :mrgreen:
