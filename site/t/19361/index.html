That should be fixable: sounds like you have an earthing issue some place. Do you now how the earthing for the house is wired? Do you have only one single earthing point for the entire house?
The 2 windows have foam boards in them to block sound both ways
Ummm... foam doesn't really "block sound". Technically it absorbs a portion of the sound passing through it by converting it into low-grade heat. Another way of thinking about it, is that it damps sound in the same way as a shock absorber "damps" a bumpy ride. But it does not block sound, in the sense of isolating. I'm also wondering how you manage to see through your windows if the have foam bards in them! Foam isn't very transparent... :shock: 8)
I know the room is already not great because it's walls are parallel,
That's fine. The only real issue with parallel walls is flutter echo, but that can be dealt with in several ways. And having parallel walls makes it much easier to predict the acoustic response of the room.
1. Where do I put the console???
Right where you have it in your sketch. That's the best place for it. set it up so that your ears will be about 88 inches from the front wall when you are comfortably seated at the console..
2. I know I need bass traps, absorbers, a cloud etc and I have an idea of where they supposed to go, but I don't want to just make assumptions. Based on my layout, what would be the best to use?
The front and rear vertical corners are the usual first candidates, but in your case the rear corners look a bit "iffy" due to the doors there. You should be OK on the left side, where the iso booth is, as there's just enough space between the door and the wall there for a superchunk, but the right side (furnace door) doesn't look feasible. How about putting a trap across the top of the rear wall, in the wall/ceiling corner?
3. I want to paint the walls and ceiling for a cool vibe. Does paint type matter?
Nope: At least, not for typical house paints. There actually is an "acoustic" paint out now, that does have a minor effect, but you don't need that: just use any paint that happens to look good.
4. Wherever the console goes, should that back wall be drywall still?
Umm... the back wall has three doors in it, and there doesn't seem to be very much drywall there at all!
5. Can the vocal booth be used and/or recommended with it's current size?
It's probably a bit on the small side for vocals, but still usable. And definitely usable as an amp iso booth, albeit not ideal. But you won't get a very high level of isolation from it, since you are using only one door.
6. Really, is there anything else I am missing????
Probably about a million things.... :) A couple that you didn't ask but will need to know anyway: 1) Your room is nearly square (233" vs 230"), so the modal response is not going to be good. I would opt for additional bass trapping. 2) Speakers go on stands that produce the final correct height: the acoustic axis must be 1.2 meters above the floor, which is 47-1/4" . Do not put the on the desk, or the meter bridge: only on heavy, massive, rigid stands, behind the desk, up against the front wall. They should be about 76" from the side walls, meaning they will be about 103" apart, and about 90" from your head. Angle them inwards at about 30°, so the acoustic axes are pointing just past your ears, and meeting at an angle of about 60° roughly 18" behind your head. 3) You need acoustic absorption on your first reflection points, and on most of your ceiling, as well as most of your rear wall. That rear wall is going to be an issue... 4) HVAC: I don't see it... ?

Soundman20208 April 2014#7
Wow! Lotsa questions! :)
Should I stain the concrete or just leave it be?
That's more or an aesthetic thing than an acoustic thing: if you like the way it looks as bare concrete, then keep it like that. On the other hand, staining and polishing can also look really nice. Search the forum for "stain concrete" and see what you come up with... there are some stunning examples of what some folks have accomplished in their studios.
2. I'm looking into the power grounding and I have an electrician friend who is going to check things out when he has some time.
Great! Tell him that the most important issue for studios is to follow a "star grounding" scheme, with all plugs wired back to a single central point, which is then wired with thick copper conductor to the one and only actual grounding rod (or grounding grid, as the case may be). If you have two (or more) grounding points, then you are letting yourself in for trouble. The same applies to ground loop paths within the studio.
2' x 8' bass traps made with OC 705 2 or 4" thick (not sure).
OC-705 is too dense for bass traps. Go with OC-703. And instead of making them 4" thick, I'd go with the "superchunk" design. Use the search feature to see how to do that: there are many examples.
I was also thinking about putting "feet" on the 2 in the back so they could be moved if necessary.
Or maybe wheels instead? They won't be very heavy, since 703 is pretty light, so you could easily wheel them around as needed.
3.a: Is the OC 705 4" thick the correct product or is there a better company (and/or cheaper) that I should go with. Any suggestions where to buy???
OC-703 is pretty much the best stuff out there, but you can also use practically any type of mineral wool that has a density of around 50 kg/m3, or any type of fiberglass that has a density of about 30 kg/m3.
3.b: other than the 4 corners, what other places are imperative and how many more should I really do?
There are 12 corners in a room, not just 4... :) think about it...
3.c: Should I make them 2x8 or split them up and make two 2x4's. Ceiling is 101" so it could have an inch or 2 gap above, below and in-between the pair of them.
If you do superchunks, make them floor to ceiling. You get the best effect in the "tri-corners" (wall-wall-ceiling and wall-wall-floor). That's the place where all room modes terminate.
4. Midrange/Hi frequency absorbers: I know I'll need many of these. First reflection points. front wall/backwall. Celing etc. 4.a: How many should I have for this room???
Start by covering your two side wall first reflection points with at least 4" of 703 (6 would be better, if you can afford the space). Use the "mirror trick" to find out exactly where those first reflection points are. Also put absorption between your speakers and the front wall, and on the entire rear wall.
4.b: is OC 703, 2'x4' the correct product .
Yes, that's fine. And same answer as above also applies.
4.d: I was planing on mounting them away from the wall to allow sound to be caught behind it as well as in front. I've ready that helps with more absorption. Is this true? How far away?
People often mount them the same distance as the thickness: so 4" gap for a 4" panel, etc. However, you can make the gap as big as you want, or as small as you have to.
4.e: Should these be mounted so I can take them on and off?
If you want to, yes. For example, to clean them occasionally. French cleats is a good way of doing that.
5. Cloud: I was looking at some GIK and Steven Klein. Should I buy one? Should I just use absorbers above? Can I use tapestry's to tame some reflections as well as absorbers?
You can make your own cloud: it's just some more 703 in a frame, hung from chains. If you make it hard-backed (thick plywood panel on top) and angle it, then you get extra benefits...
6. Corners where the wall meet ceiling: Should I put anything in the corners?
Bingo! Perfect territory for bass traps....
7. The iso Booth: There is carpet in there right now and drywall everywhere else. How could I go about making this useful for vocals. Just absorption everywhere. When I used it before for vocals and amps I had put packing blankets on the wall and I do have 3 pieces of fiberglass a friend game me that I used as a cloud and on the walls in addition to the blankets.
Really small booths are best treated to death, with as much absorption as possible. Larger booths can have less absorption and even some diffusion, or maybe a slot wall.
8. You Mention the rear wall is going to be an issue. I agree. Is there anything to do with this besides put up every panel I can afford?
That's about all you can do, realistically. Hope that helps! - Stuart -

This idea came from the 25 minute, $25 acoustic panel thread on John Sayers Forum. I only made small tweaks and for 2 of them I used French Cletes and others I used/will use L brackets for mounting. MATERIALS: OC 703 2": 16 square feet (2 boards) @ $0.73 = $11.68 White Stretch Fabric: 1 yards @ $10.99 = $11.21 1x2 x 10ft pine for frame: 1 @ $3.97 = $3.97 2.5" L Brackets for wall mounting = $1.49 1/2 inch Staples: $.50 est Liquid Nails: $.15 est Hot Glue: $.10 est French Cleat: $7.97 (optional) Sub Total: $27.61 + MD State Tax 6% Total Cost for 1 Pannel = $30.76 W/ French Cleat = $37.70 Grand Total (6 reg, 2 fc) = $259.96 (My cost is slightly higher than $25 because I used a very nice fabric instead of burlap and 1x2 Pine instead of ferrying strips. cost would have been about $4 cheaper with burlap, and $2 cheaper with Strips.) TOOLS: Measuring Tape Miter Saw Power Drill Staple Gun Calk Gun Hot Glue Gun STEPS: Measured and cut pieces of 1x2 to frame out the 2x4 OC board.
Image not preserved: IMG_3100.jpg
Cut triangle corners for added support and cheaper than L brackets (could have done this for super chunks too and will in the future). I cut enough of everything so I could make an assembly line for 9 frames where I sat in the middle and made the frames one after another without having to get up.
Image not preserved: IMG_3101.jpg
Liquid Nailed and stapled the frames together on one side. Did the same with the corner pieces, then flipped the frame and stapled the other side. These are very stable for how light and simple the design it.
Image not preserved: IMG_3102.jpg
Image not preserved: IMG_3104.jpg
Placed 1 board down on the fabric. Hot Glued the next board on top of it. Hot Glued the Frame to the back of the board to create a 4" absorber and 3/4 inch frame.
Image not preserved: IMG_3129.jpg
Tightehned fabric and stapled to the back end of the frame.
Image not preserved: IMG_3131.jpg
2 are going behind my speakers so I attached pre made french cleats from Home Depot to the frame and to the wall so they sit right against the wall.
Image not preserved: IMG_3138.jpg
Finished and Mounted!!! These were quite easy and using the assembly line method you can crank these out quite quickly. After doing the mirror trick and measuring behind my speakers I now have 5 mounted and plan to have more as time/money allows. Cheers
