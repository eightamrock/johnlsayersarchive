Soundman202014 April 2009#29
Question: If I wire each outlet in the studio such that it is directly plugged into one of the Tripp Lite IS1000 outlets, will this sufficiently address potential ground loop/hum issues?
Straight answer: Yes. No. Maybe. Not trying to be funny here (OK, well, maybe just a tad!), but it really depends a lot on the rest of your wiring, and what you plug in where. It MIGHT be just fine. It might not. Are you still going to have an independent technical ground? Will it be star-wired to the isolation transformers? In that case, it might work OK, provided that the isolation transformers do their job perfectly, and there is no leakage anywhere...
Leveler VA Sentry 2400 ... Their documentation indicates that ground loops are eliminated using this device, but I cannot tell if that is only if each device is plugged directly into the outlets on the unit,
Looks like a nice unit. But your guess is correct: it can only eliminate ground loops in equipment that is directly plugged into it. There is nothing it can do to upstream loads.
or if the unit could be used to supply multiple outlets in the studio space.
Well, it could indeed supply several outlets, provided that the total current you draw does not exceed the manufacturer's specs and the capacity of your wiring.
I just don't have a way right now to experiment with daisy chained outlets and this device to look for ground loop issues.
You don't want to daisy-chain outlets! Not a good idea. If you need that unit to supply more items of equipment than it has outlets, you could probably use power strips on the outlet, once again PROVIDED THAT you take care not to exceed the manufacturers specs for EACH outlet. I would not like to do that personally, since it creates the possibility for someone to do something stupid, but in theory it should be OK, provided that you know what you are doing, and use good quality grounded power strips that do not include their own surge suppression. And you certainly do not want to daisy chain one power strip from another power strip. But wither way, you still need a good technical ground for your studio that is isolated from the ground for the rest of the building. - Stuart -
