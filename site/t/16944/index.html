xSpace3 September 2011#2
What you want to look for is RFZ (reflection free zone), then search for live end/dead end. Your thoughts and research should be placed on mixing/playing in one room mixing. Typically, you are going to be placing "broad band treatment" in at least the front vertical corners in front of the mix position, treatments at the "first reflection points" (about where you head/ears are in the mixing chair), an "over head cloud" may produce good results for you as well. All words in parenthesis should be considered good search terms as well in your research.

Hi there "basyl666 from hell" ( :) ). Welcome! Your current layout doesn't look too bad, and you didn't mention isolation ("soundproofing") of your room as being necessary, so it looks like this is just about treatment. As Brien said, your best bet is to design an RFZ (Reflection Free Zone) around your mix position. That basically means that you angle hard flat surfaces around the front half of they room such that all of the first-order reflections from your speakers go around the mix position, and not to it. For the remaining points where you can't do that, you put deep, thick absorption in the way. In other words, you set things up so that you can only hear the direct sound from the speakers, not reflections bouncing around the front half of the room. Obviously, that improves the clarity, stereo imaging, sound stage, etc. of your system, so that you can hear the real sound of the speakers more, and not the "colored" sound that the room is adding to it. In other words, you hear the mix the way it really is, not the way the room makes it sound. That's the general idea.
