I am still in the planning stage. Objective: Build a live room for tracking drums and possible jam sessions. I don't have a sound level meter yet, but to get an idea, most drummers being tracked will be heavy metal drummers. Size: approximately 9'x12'(height see below) Location: Residential neighborhood in Long Island, NY. Basement of a ranch house. Lot size is approximately 50'x100' as well as all other nearby neighbors. The spot I chose in the basement seems to be furthest from all neighboring houses. House is a 1950s era ranch. Wood frame with insulated aluminum siding Main Floor Interior walls and ceiling are all plaster Foundation is cement fill with plugs. It measures 10" thick in a crawl space opening. Floor above basement consists of the following: maple floor 3/4" planks(not sure what these are made of) placed at 45 degree angle across the joists 2x8 joists span 11ft concrete wall->steel beam they are placed 16" oc with only one solid bridging between all the joists. There is only 85" between the floor and the ceiling joists. So like everyone else I want maximum height along with maximum isolation. I was considering the following: A) Dow 1/2" Blue Board, sheetrock, greenglue, sheetrock between the joists. Fiberglass. RC channel or RISC clip sheetrock, greenglue, sheetrock B) Same as A but with 3 layers of sheetrock between the joists. C) Build it just like page 77 in Rod's book. D) Build 6" wide trusses that will fit between the cross bracing of the ceiling joists to achieve maximum ceiling height and maximum isolation. Here are my questions: 1) A and B seem easier to build but will my ceiling be able to support all the extra weight? 2) C I loose ceiling height. 85" - 3 5/8" - 5/8" - 5/8" - 5/8" = 80 1/2" Has anyone built a ceiling this low? 3) D I'm having trouble finding specs on wooden trusses 6" wide made out of 2x4, probably because no one uses them because they are too small to support anything? Will trusses like this placed over 9' span, 16" oc be able to hold 2-3 layers of sheetrock.

snowdog995 February 2009#10
Can't speak about the insulation type or the gap. However, you're incorrect in the location that you place the vapour barrier. Also, I'm pretty sure you need the vapour barrier. Your vapour barrier should be placed on the warm side of the room. So, from the inside: 1) drywall/drywall 2) vapour barrier 3) insulation 4) gap 5) concrete wall For the explanation, look at Brad Jacob's build or Cram's. Rod Gervais gives a great explanation as to where the vapour barrier should go. Fortunately those guys corrected the issue before they put up the drywall. Andy

The dreaded HVAC part of the build. YAH! I originally planned on making a sofit in the tracking room but I am now steering away from that idea because I think it will take up too much space because of my low ceiling. So after reading through the forum, round ducts are not very good for long runs and square ducts are better. I am considering rectangle shaped ducts made with duct board. I made a box that is 10" x 12" and walked around my basement in the places I thought would be the best fit. In my case the tracking room joists range from 14" to 17" center. Luckily outside the room there is one bay that will happily fit my box with the 12" side horizontally so I can sneak the box up in between the joists. When I cross under the steel beam in the basement the joists are much closer together about 12" on center, so I have to flip my box the other way(10" horizontally) so I can still sneak the box up there. Now I have to make a turn by the concrete wall and follow the next steel beam to the other side of the basement. On this run the box crosses all the joists so the box is back to the 12" side running horizontal. One more turn and flip and I'm finally in the mechanical room with it. What an adventure.
