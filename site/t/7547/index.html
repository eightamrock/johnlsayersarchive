I have access to 10 rolls of this stuff (50 ft x 6 inch) for next to nothing, and what interested me was the ability of this product to act as a decoupling layer between drywall/studs. If you're not familiar with it , its a rubberized tar like roofing sealing/flashing tape in 70mil thickness. Its quite heavy and sticks to most anything. Just tried putting some between 2 24x24" pieces of sheetrock and stuck them together to make a sandwich. Not only could i not remove the two once joined, they sounded totally deadened by it. I didn't cover the whole thing either, just applied in 2" strips in 4 places. Is it a crazy idea to use this between the sheetrock, and where studs interface with each other and the sheetrock to minimize sound transmission?? Has this been approached before anywhere in a thread i missed? If so, please direct me or give me some input to whether i should go through with the effort...i'm getting the stuff for free basically.

Comparing this the the use of RC or iso clips... RC and iso clips (with hat channel) are only useful on single frame walls or ceiling. Adding RC and iso clips to double frame wall is almost pointless... very,very,very little OR no gain in isolation. Have you thought through staggered stud construction all the way the end? Staggered stud was once in my plan but building double (decoupled) door and window frames on a staggered stud (2x6 plates w 2x4 studs) will be next to impossible. Also building the inner and outer ceiling decoupled from inner and outer staggered studs properly will be almost impossible. I don't have a lot of room for my studio but still dropped the staggered stud idea and have gone for double 2x4 frames.
