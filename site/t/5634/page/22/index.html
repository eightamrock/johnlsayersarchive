Plywood blades go fast. I have three finish work blades that I rotate through duty. The guy that sharpens my saws loves seeing my face. I think they go fast because there's no room for air in the cut as the teeth are so close [on plywood blades] and if the blade doesn't keep up to speed it can heat up, then the board starts to get a handle on the sides of the blade. Eh Ro, thanks for relating :) Yup loosing a family member can divide a family, just experienced it. Is the stick hard to play?... IMO Basic technique is not hard to get for a guitar or bass player. I've had to loose my finger picking nails. Melody side is tuned in 4th's like a guitar so that was sort of easy. Bass side however is tuned in 5th's, big low string is in the middle of the neck and strings ascend in pitch towards you. Where the notes are on the bass side is kinda mind warping but the beauty in the tuning is that this makes chord shapes and scale patterns the same on both sides. Chords don't sound like mud like chords can sound on a bass guitar because of the 5ths tuning. Typical blues walking lines make you jump strings at some point which is a PITA but the ability to chord with the right hand is awesome. The hard part is gaining independence in both hands. I used to gig playing bass and/or playing keys holding down a left hand bass. I'm not there yet on the stick after a month but am hoping to play it at all my gigs with in one more month of wood shedding. The stick is part easy for a guitar or bass player but more of challenge. Once I'm up in running I will be the only stick player on the Space Coast of Florida, the only solo act in Central Florida playing the stick and one of few stick players who lead sings.
