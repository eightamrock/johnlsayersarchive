sharward2 April 2007#469
I'm mindful that your permit is for non-habitable space, so you need to be especially careful as you move past the "official" plan and into your "under the radar" phase. Code requires habitable space to have fresh air provisioned, typically via operable windows, but in the absence of those, mechanical ventilation can be substituted. Fresh air must come from outside - not from other habitable space. Therefore, you need a balanced fan system, such as a heat recovery ventilator (HRV). I have posted a number of times about HRVs, so a search for "HRV" should turn up lots o' good stuff. In short, they cycle fresh air in and out of the room(s) and they slightly cool the incoming air by transferring some of the heat of the incoming air to the outgoing air. I believe you'll need a system capble of delivering a minimum of 15-20 cfm per room occupant whenever the room is occupied. Also, I'm concerned that your space may have different climate requirements than the rest of your house. While the 3.5 ton / 4200(?) BTU system won't be strained by the extra small load, you'll probably find yourself overcooling the studio or the house. I faced a similar situation. Marc, your build is very similar to mine in a lot of ways, and your house is even similar in terms of square footage (mine's 1973 square feet, not including about 120 square feet for the studio). The way I decided to tackle the need to control the temperature of the studio is by way of a small (9000 BTU / ¾-ton) mini split heat pump. They're not cheap (around $1700 DIY), but it fits my needs perfectly. I'm figuring the HRV plus ducting, controls, wiring, and professional charging the mini split will bring my grand total to about $3,000. I know that's a lot for such a small space, but I really didn't see any other alternative that would meet my isolation and independent control requirements. --Keith :mrgreen:

Please note- the mandatory use of the word "shall" - nothing allowing you to supply fresh air only "some of the time" when the room or space is occupied -
Section 402 entitled "NATURAL VENTILATION" reads identical to the IBC. Section 403 entitled: "MECHANICAL VENTILATION" reads as follows: 403.1 entitled "Ventilation system" reads: "Mechanical Ventilation shall be provided by a method of supply and return or exhaust air. The amount of supply air shall be approximately equal to the amount of return and exhaust air. The system shall not be prohibited from producing a negative or positive pressure. The ventilation system shall be designed in accordance with Chapter 6. The required rate of ventilation shall be continuous during the period the building is occupied except where otherwise stated. Ventilation supply systems shall deliver the required rate of supply air within the oiccupied space between 3 inches (76mm) and 72 inches (1829mm) above the floor and more than 2 feet (610mm) from the enclosing walls."
Table 403 - entitled "REQUIRED OUTDOOR VENTILATION AIR" does establish areas where constant fresh air is not required: Item "c." in the notes states: "
"Spaces unheated or maintained below 50 degrees F are not covered by these requirements unless the occupancy is continuous".
