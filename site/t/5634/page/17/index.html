knightfly11 January 2007#413
Marc, it's hard to be sure why I said something a couple hours ago, much less months :? But after re-reading thru eric's thread I think the main reason I recommended at least 6 degrees would have been lack of ceiling height - I tend to get claustrophobic in low ceilinged rooms. Also, I may have ass-u-me'd that Eric would still be using a cloud over the mix position :oops: Other reasons why this would pose less of a problem than in a live room (yes, I know you're doing a single-room setup) In a live room, typically several tracks are recorded at different times and then mixed down to a final stereo or surround mix, then (hopefully) mastered. It's not uncommon for people to stop treating the LR when they can't hear any "rainbarrel" effect when clapping hands in the room - all that's happened at that point is that flutter has been reduced to the point where it's no longer obvious to the ear using that test - it doesn't mean that flutter is GONE, just reduced in level. Now, if you record 10-15 tracks in that room at different times, each track will have some flutter component that, by itself, isn't audible - until you MIX them together, that is... :cry: This is part of why a dedicated live room needs to have even less flutter (and other bad things) than a CR - in a dedicated CR, you usually aren't recording but listening. So there's no build-up to worry about; if you can't hear it, you can't hear it. In a single-room setup, for optimum results you need some flexibility/tuning options in order to get the best of both worlds - for one thing, if you have anywhere near enough headroom at least the section of ceiling from the front to maybe 2 feet behind your head should be a full 12 degree slope upward to the rear - if there's room, the entire room should be that way. If necessary, putting a peak just behind your head and then sloping back down to the rear is semi-acceptable - but within 2-3 feet of that peak, a cloud will be needed to avoid "focussing" caused by the convex at the peak - but if you don't have the headroom to continue the ceiling's upward slope, this would be better than a flat level ceiling. Because of the multiple use required of a single room, it's more important that you do as much of the acoustic tuning with shape as possible (rather than with absorption) - this way, you can build movable absorbers to re-tune the room for each purpose - more live for recording, deader for mixdowns. One way of accomplishing part of this is a mounting method for wall absorbers I drew a few years ago - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download.php?id=1391 I don't remember what your available headroom is, but if possible I'd recommend that you shoot for the full 12 degrees up to at least a couple feet behind the mix position, then if there isn't enough headroom to continue that slope bring it back down in the rear at the same angle - I'd also go for some mounting rails around the room, maybe 5 feet off the floor, for movable wall absorbers. I'd build at least 6-8 of these (see the link above for partial detail) and then start listening/measuring the room. If you use the reverb calculator at the top of the Acoustics forum, this can get you in the ball park for how much absorption you'll need to get the room to "live" specs - shoot for around 0.4 seconds or slightly less. Once you're there, make whatever notes/marks, etc, necessary to repeat that - this will be "recording" mode for the room. Recalculate the RT/absorption requirements to reduce the room to under 0.3 seconds, and start adding treatments (extra wall panels, maybe a ceiling-mounted "poly" that, when hinged at one side could be dropped down to expose the rear, absorbent side) - when you get things to a relatively even .2-.3 seconds across the spectrum, take pix/make notes/make marks, whatever - this will be your mix setup. In case it's not been mentioned lately, the reason you want shorter RT in a mix situation is so you won't hear reverb in the mix that "isn't there" - IOW, the CR needs to be drier than the LR or you may mistake the live characteristic of the CR for something that's actually "on tape", and end up making a drier mix than you thought/wanted. Disclaimer - no, this is NOT as easy as I make it sound - but it IS what you should shoot for... Steve
orangenumerik18 January 2007#414
Hi Marc & Steve Well, i though i'd jump in. This topic obviously caught my attention. I slopped my ceiling as to Steve recomendation knowing that 6 degres is a bare minimum. See a long time ago i'v asked Steve about the best ceiling angle and his answer was this : http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... highlight= Now that being said, let's just see why i did go for a 6 degres ceiling (7degres in reality...). Well it was a matter of space and "feel" of the room. Slopping the ceiling at 15 degres would have set the front height of the CR at 6'6" wich is way too low ( buiding code thing) and at 12 degres it would have been 7'3", i could have use that but..... at 6 degres (what i have now ) the front of the room is at 8'1-1/2" wich is about the height of most ceiling houses these days. Eventhough now the front of the room is at standard height, when one stand in that space you definitely have the impression that the ceiling is only 6' high at the front, wich give the room a small visual feel. Even if the highest point and all the back of the room is 9'3" high. It's hard to describe. Now imagine a front at 7'... Steve would get very claustrophobic in that space :wink: . Even at the height i have now i had a hard time getting use to it, after all i am going to spend long hours in there. This is something that count for me as much as acoustic, for my own sanity. The other thing was room volume, how to slopped without loosing too much cubic volume. These were my considerations for choosing 6 degres. And as i wrote in my thread even that small angle does make a very noticable acoustic difference. Now it's your call, but i though you'd like to know before taking a decision. This won't apply if you have a wide room with a 14' ceiling of course, but i think it is not your case. If you already have only 9' of height or so, be carefull. You might try to hang somehow panels i the air just to give you a feel of the room and see if you can live with it.
