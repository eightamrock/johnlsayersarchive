(not intending to violate the 'don't talk negative of codes' ground-rule...): Carpwftx makes some pretty good points..... I would only want to say that codes, or any other rules, laws, etc., can only account for forseen issues, but not for all unforseen circumstances that might arise. There are alot of codes dealing specifically with and/or are crucial for safety, and yet there are other codes that don't seem to be much, if at all, involved with that. Some are very important, while others may not be at all (or at least critically so). Since codes vary by community, especially by adjacent communities under virtually the same situations, shows that they are, at least sometimes, somewhat arbitrary. I mean, there are traffic laws that are very important, and others which often become almost silly. Everyone drives on the right side of the road because it is crucial to safety. Most people don't keep both hands on the wheel (one at '9 o'clock' and one at '3 o'clock'), since virtually all cars have responsive power steering nowadays (and don't critically require 2 hands for handling); yet the law still exists. Cops never pull people over for only having one hand on the wheel (heck, they don't even pull them over for being on the phone, or eating, or shaving even... yet these aren't silly rules). It is right not to blatantly ignore or break codes. But it can get obsessive to carry things to the nth degree in losing sleep over tiny tiny tiny things... So, somewhere there has got to be a middle ground, and a balance between what is very important and what may not be very important.
