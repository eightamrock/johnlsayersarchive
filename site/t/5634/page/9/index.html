sharward21 August 2006#203
Hey Marc, I know that "hey, I just put up my top plates" feeling all too well. ;-) However, you mentioned that you used screws. I found out by calling my building department that I cannot use screws for framing -- they must be nails and there is a nailing schedule that you have to follow. I discuss this in quite a bit of detail in my thread starting here. Basically, the longer/thicker the nails, the fewer you need to use. I don't want to advise you too specifically in this area, because your codes are probably slightly different than mine. So, you may have to undo the screws and drive nails instead. I know that it seems counterintuitive (you'd think screws would be better[/i]... But when you figure that nails are generally stronger than screws in terms of shear, it actually makes sense. Be sure to get an early start on that attic work, Marc. Our bodies aren't accustomed to that kind of extreme heat up there. In my old house, the attic temperature regularly reached 130°-140°F (54°-60°C). Heatstroke is life threatening can come upon you very quickly, which can cause you such confusion that you could become trapped up there. How much time would go by before someone would know you were in trouble, and how quickly could they get you out of there? :shock: Knowing how urgently you're proceeding, I worry a bit about how you might try to push yourself and not realize in that moment the danger of doing so. --Keith :mrgreen:

That's exactly what I used on my outer leaf plate in that spot where I had to drill a hole in my new concrete. They're made like roaches in the roach motel -- "they 'check in' but they don't 'check out.'" That sleeve slides down into the "wedge" shaped area, which makes the bolt widest at the bottom and thus unable to be pulled out. That's the point of them! :) The tighter you crank down on the nut (without stripping, of course), the stiffer the bolt becomes. The trouble with them is that you have to get the hole wide enough to get the bolt in, but not too wide that they slip out. --Keith :mrgreen:

If you ever need to bang on the end of a bolt like that again, first run a nut part way onto the threads, but NOT far enough so the bolt's threads come clear through the nut; then bang on the NUT, watching to see that it doesn't rotate further onto the bolt while you're at it. Once the bolt's in as far as you want it, remove the nut. Most times, this won't damage threads AT ALL. Also, if you have access to a REAL fastener supplier, ask for "coupler nuts" in the size your bolts are - these are REALLY thick nuts (like an inch or more) designed to "couple" two bolts or pieces of allthread together. One of those coupler nuts screwed onto the bolt at least 3-4 threads and I GUARANTEE your threads will survive... Steve
