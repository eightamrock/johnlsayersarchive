If that means "putting an attic access door inside your garage," be aware that doing such a thing will probably require a permit and may be difficult to do while meeting code. Your garage is attached to the house, which means your access door would have to be at the same or better fire rating as the ceiling. Three of my immediate neighbors have done this. None of them got permits, and they all have seriously compromised the fire containment integrity of their garage ceilings! :!: Many house fires start in garages -- only kitchens outnumber them in terms of starting points. Think of the combustibles that are often stored in garages and the fact that we park cars that are full of gasoline, oil, and other combustible liquids. We've all seen car fires on the side of the freeway, right? Well, sometimes they happen after parking in the garage, like what happened to the Oyola-Hernandez family. Water heater pilot lights sometimes ignite vapors of improperly stored combustible liquids.

Are you absolutely certain that there's no access point in the house, or perhaps even outside the house, maybe at a gable vent? Here's an excerpt from this page:
    Attic accesses are required for any attic that has a clear ceiling height of 30 inches or more, and the opening needs to be a minimum of 22 inches by 30 inches in size. Note that one dimension is 22 inches instead of 24 inches – the building codes have made this adjustment so that access holes will fit between commonly used roof trusses, which are usually on 24-inch centers. Here again, the codes don't typically specify inside or outside access. Many older homes have outside access hatches placed in a gable end, but in newer homes it's much more common to find them inside – again, typically in a centrally located closet. If the access is inside, a dam must be constructed inside the attic so that it surrounds the opening and prevents blown insulation from falling into the hole. Also, the access hatch must insulated to the same level as the surrounding attic, which is usually done by attaching a batt of insulation to the attic side of the hatch cover.
Perhaps that part of your house doesn't have an access point because the height is less than 30 inches and as such one wasn't required... :roll: Again, definitely talk to your local building department to see what specific requirements apply in your jurisdiction.

sharward22 February 2006#38
Decisions, decisions... Regarding becoming an "employer" of laborers -- I would recommend avoiding it. As you've started to uncover, there are just too many potentially expensive (and possibly devastating?) pitfalls that could cost you many times what you would hope to save. Stick with licensed contractors as per your owner-builder declaration, always make sure they're insured for liability, and always make sure that if they have employees that they're covered by workers' compensation insurance. Some things to consider with your electrical panel situation... Although upgrading to 200 amps may seem like overkill, it really isn't by today's home construction standards. If you do opt to move the panel, make sure the electrician thoroughly understands your goals and provide a primer in soundproofing. I'm concerned there would be big junction box of sorts in place of the old panel, which would probably defeat the purpose of moving it in the first place. I think electricians are accustomed to moving circuit panels because they're not in a convenient location -- but in your case, you want to replace the panel with lots of mass, and that may not be doable if there's a big junction box there instead. Plus, remember that the junction box(es) must remain accessible. --Keith :mrgreen:

marc, yeh it's a waste of time to mess with different drywall thicknesses. others will confirm. check out report linked on page below for data on different wall constructions ... should help a little. there are other similar reports to check out if you like this one. http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/fulltext/ir761/ your walls sound a little on the thin side to me. certainly with walls of 1/2 + 5/8 only, the floor is not going to be the problem. slab provides good low freq TL, better than these walls would. ir761 shows few tests with 27+ db TL @ 50 hz except pg 350 that has 5/8 x2 on both sides. however theres probly a dip below 50hz ... id go 5/8 x3 if you want to get that level of iso .. but 5/8 x2 is minimum.
