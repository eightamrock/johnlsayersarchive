One thing that I've been 'finding out' about slabs is that -- at least around here -- they can be more subjected to changes caused by the elements, than say, deep foundations (i.e., basements)... Also, take for instance a tree in the yard near the house... As the tree grows, the roots spread out past the drip line of the tree's canopy... If the tree gets big enough, these roots could go UNDER a slab -- and do all the time, just take a walk down a sidewalk on a street lined with large trees.. you'll see them pushing up the walkway, cracking and heaving it... This can't happen nearly as much in a basement because the tree roots don't go down that low, and would almost never make it under the deep footings... they don't want to go that low because all the nutrients and water from the rain is in the upper 'zone of aeration' above the water table... (taproots do go down, but these only form when the tree is young, and are only directly under the tree). What would happen is that growing horizontal roots would reach the concrete wall and discover that there was no way around the foundation... They could press on the walls, but the roots while still small would be more apt to push from and grow away/around the wall... Of course, this doesn't mean having roots along a basement wall is 'okay'; there are still water issues (e.g., surface runoff could follow the roots to the walls, then travel down then to the footing and enter the basement). Btw, Keith, Building Codes -- at least around here -- are dictated/enforced with much politics in mind... For instance, the plumber who lives on the next street over was the one who connected all the sumps from the houses here, to the sewage system... The town had sent out notices, town-wide, that inspections were to be made if there were existing drainage connections to the sewer system... I just found out that this plumber is with the Inspectional Services department for the town, as the chief plumbing inspector. (although, to be fair, he did tell us that we would have to disconnect the sump discharge). Codes and their enforcement are strange around here... it seems like variances are given 'on the fly' either by the board or via individual inspectors... (of course, these things usually are not of paramount structural or safety importance -- rather, they're things like room sizes, ceiling heights, etc., particularly in very old houses that have few easy options complying precisely)...
