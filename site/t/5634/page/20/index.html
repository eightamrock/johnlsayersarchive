It is physically impossible for 30cfm of outside air to add a ton of cooling load to a home....... Heck - your bathroom exhaust is probably 50 to 75 cfm by itself - and a house built in the 1960's were not built that tight (in the 70's we used to deliberately construct homes so that they would receive at least 1 complete air exchange per hour). By todays standards- extremely tight homes are around ...6 or less.......... well kept older homes 1.0...... somewhat loose older homes 2 to 3...... Now lets put that into perspective........ .6 represents 6/10 of a complete house fresh air exchange PER HOUR....... in a 1,000 s.f. home with 8' ceilings - that is air infiltration of 4,800 cubic feet of air PER HOUR..... that's 80 cfm......... and that's in a new tight home built today in the 2000's. Let's suppose your house, that was built in the 1960's - had an exchange rate of 1.5..... which would be pretty reasonable if it's in decent shape..... if it was just 1,000 s.f with 8'ceilings, that would be complete air exchange of 12,000 cfh..... or 200 cfm....... Now let's look at this calmly for a minute- because that looks like an awful lot of air - I mean you'd feel a wind blowing on you right? Actually - if you take a 24 x 42 ranch - slab on grade........ that's 132 linear feet of outside wall------ and with just a 1/16" crack at the base for air to filter through - which is much better than average from a construction point of view..... that's the equivilent of a hole in the wall 99 inches square.......... that's almost 10 x 10 clear opening - AND THAT'S JUST THE SILL........ if you moved 200 cfm through that it would hardly feel like a soft breath.... Add to that the air infiltration straight though walls- through small cracks around the edges of windows - from opening and walking in and out through doors........ and you should quickly come to see that these numbers are not far off...... Doors - now there's a biggie...... you suddenly open up a hole in the side of your house that is 20.01 s.f. in area - and picture the air that can move in a minute through that baby........ So now you're talking about moving the air at a rate of 200 cfm and having to cool that..... let's see - at one ton per 30 cfm that would be 6.67 tons for 200 cfm. ...... and we haven't begun to talk about passive solar gain - cooking heat - showers - cleaning clothes - never mind the BTU output from people themselves- having to dehumidify the moisture from their breathing...... having to cool off the heat produced by their bodies in general........... for crying out load- by time we were done you'd be installing 10 ton units for old 1,000 s.f. homes........ Sorry - taint being done......... even when remodeling older properties - you have perhaps a ton and a 1/2 of cooling for 1,000 s.f. - maybe 2 tons if the contractor really wants to over size it............ Marc, NO NO NO NO NO......... with emphasis on the "NO"......... you do not want to be messing with the balancing once it's done- the right way to do this is with a pair of motorized dampers - placed on the main side of the fresh air in and outlets - wire those to a cooling thermostat - when your space is satisfied - the thermostat will close down the dampers - and then open again when you need more cooliing. Meanwhile - your fresh air fan can be running and providing you with fresh air.......... it will still work (and not effect your system) because once the rooms pressurizes - the barometrric damper will still open. The only other thing I would consider would be an humidistat that also tied into the motorized dampers - that could open them to say 50% if the humidity started getting high in the room - at least that would allow your fan coil the chance to dehumidify the air while not freezing you out........

Dayam, when you read the book you never really paid attention to the section on HVAC......... you should be asking him for a hard calculated number based on gear (sensible load) ..... people in the room (latent load) fresh air (sensible and latent loads) and lighting (sensible load) I just finished designing a smallish semi-pro studio in NC - and the HVAC was a part of my contract........ the control room required 3 tons of cooling....
