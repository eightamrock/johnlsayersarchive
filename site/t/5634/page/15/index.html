Sandersd14 November 2006#352
Have you ever heard of a mortar bed such as is used under tile? Masons use it to level a floor in preparation for laying tile. It can be several inches thick. My point is it a common practice and is considered by many tile men the best substrate for laying tile. If it works for tile, it'll work for other flooring also. You don't need self-leveling concrete unless you are laying a floor that requires a precisely leveled floor. Just make sure any aggregate in concrete is smaller than the thinnest areas of concrete.

sharward18 November 2006#367
Hey Marc - we've always known you were putting your blood, sweat, and tears into that framing! :lol: I don't think you need to or necessarily use backer rod OR caulk between sheathing panels. The wall will be air tight when you're done. That sheathing gap is for expansion, so just let it. And remember you'll have framing members at the seams to make up for the tiny loss of mass. Also, I wouldn't worry about the miniscule air pockets around your straps. I've got the same thing with my seismic ties and I'm not losing a moment's sleep over it. :-) But if you still care, then you can slather on some drywall joint compound to feather them out a bit. --Keith :mrgreen:
