sharward10 November 2006#337
I read that description of the puzzle piece plan, and while I do understand the challenge, I do not understand the proposed solution, although I must say some parts of the description are really bugging me. So, at the risk of dismissing that which I do not understand (something that I really dislike in others ;-)), I have another, I think simpler option for you to consider. Make a "header-like" threshold holderuppper:
  • Get a 2x4 or a 2x6 piece of pressure treated lumber.
  • Rip it so that its width is equal to the maximum abount of "boost" you need on the threshold at what will be its highest point.
  • Cut it to a little longer than the length of your rough opening along the unlevel floor.
  • Lay it on the floor on edge and mark the ends along the jack studs.
  • Use a miter saw not on the 90° angle to trim it down to fit snugly between the studs.
  • Once this first piece is fitted properly, use it as a template to make two or three more just like it.
  • Nail them all together with galvanized nails to form a solid wedge-shaped header-like thing.
  • Lay down SillSeal foam and toenail to jack studs with galvanized nails.
I'm much more comfortable with this plan because the threshold will rest on and be attached to a stout object, not a patchwork of smal pieces. Not bad for typing on a Treo, eh? ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:

Sandersd13 November 2006#346
Would I also have to cut those 2x4 forms to the proper angle so that I could screed the top of grout level both for width and depth of grout/subsill area.
No, unless you want to. Otherwise just float it to the proper level (with a concrete float or trowel). It doesn't have to be perfect since you'll want to place it in a bed of goopity-goo to seal it. Another common practice is called dry packing. Shim and install the door. Once it's attached at the sides, remove the shims under the threshold. You'll be left with a gap, 1/2" or so. Mix the grout with very little water, it should be dry and crumbly, with just enough water to hold together when it's packed tightly. Now push it under the threshold until it's full and pack it tightly with a piece of scrap wood or something. It'll fill the entire underside of the threshold without any voids. Remember, the less water you use the stronger the grout (or concrete) will be.
Grout could be poured to 1/2" (or less) thick and not crack unlike regular concrete. Hmmm... guess so... grout is used between concrete blocks in walls.
