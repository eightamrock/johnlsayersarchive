Marc: Ooops, for a second there I thought I was looking at a dusting of snow near your trench -- but just as quickly, I realized you are in Fla... I might have it out of context, but what I don't understand is why would you want clay in your soil? From what I'm led to believe, clay is not a very forgiving soil. When it gets wet, it expands and holds the water; when it dries, it gets crusty, shrinks and cracks. The expansion/contraction cycles supposedly wreak havoc on concrete foundations -- making them more prone to cracking and moving... As I think I understand it, the expansion (when wet) exerts enormous force on concrete walls, maybe causing them to crack; when the soil is dry, it pulls away from the concrete, and would then allow movement of the concrete (sinking, tilting, etc.). Of course, clay appears to be used as a waterproofing aid in certain situations for below ground concrete (e.g., the bentonite stuff), but I still don't think you'd want the entire surrounding soil to be clay. The 'ideal' soil -- if there could be any -- would seem of the sort that easily drains, but yet doesn't erode, AND can provide great support. Sandy, rocky soils over bedrock or (maybe ledge?) or lots of riprap or larger sized rocks, would seem to be good for concrete... But what do I know? Maybe TomVan, or someone else in-the-know can help dispell or confirm... As for the 'lunch debris' in between walls: I think it's one of those 'kilroy was here' graffiti-artifact things; or kinda like leaving your mark -- a time capsule as it were, lol. K
