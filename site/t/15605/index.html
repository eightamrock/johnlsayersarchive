Hello all. First, let me pay my respects to everyone contributing to this forum. I have learned so much and also realize how much I have yet to learn. After many months of researching numerous subjects on this forum, I feel that I have a reasonable understanding of studio construction basics. Let me preface where I am at in my plans. I am in the design phase of my project. It is my intention to build a studio with two control rooms, one for me, and one for my partner. The studio will also have a live room and 3 isolation booths. The studio will have 2 bathrooms and a kitchen/lounge area. The type of music my partner and I work on ranges from rock and roll to jazz and pop to hip hop and urban music. Let me also preface that any construction will be permitted and to code. I have located what I believe to be a suitable building for my needs. I am currently in negotiations to secure the lease. It is located in North Hollywood, CA. The building is 2900 square feet, in an industrial park, built in 1960, and is of concrete block construction on top of a concrete slab. There is some existing infrastructure in this building already. It has 2 air conditioners on the roof. A 2 ton unit, and a 3 ½ ton unit. There are two existing bathrooms that are both ADA compliant. The entire ceiling throughout the building is insulated. One of my main concerns is an existing wall that divides the building into two partitions. It is of wood frame and drywall construction. I have no idea what is in between the drywall and studs. I assume that it has some insulation but we all know what happens when you assume. There is a tenant on the other side of this wall. He is an HVAC contractor. I am not sure what type of noise levels will be produced by his current business? I am also concerned about disturbing him. I have many questions regarding my studio design. First and foremost on my mind is what can/should I do about the dividing wall? Also, because this dividing wall will be one of the leafs incorporated into my design, I am concerned about the best way to maximize it’s effectiveness as far as isolation is concerned. My first thought would be to make sure that it is as air tight as possible, sealing every crack and seam that I can find. Next I would think it would be extremely important to add more mass to it via green glue and another layer of drywall. I am looking for any suggestion anyone can offer. Below is a sketchup model of the building as is it will be after some minor demolition. I have photos of the building right now but I hesitate to post them because they will likely be confusing when referencing the sketchup model. I also have several more questions and concerns regarding my design but I figure I will ask things one at a time. Thanks to all in advance for your help.

gullfo12 February 2011#2
welcome! looks like a nice size space. one of the concerns you'll have with your neighbor is the flanking path along the exterior walls as well as the dividing wall. as far as noise levels, after you strip it out take some acoustic measurements to determine noise levels and evaluate the isolation - do some tap testing on the walls, floor and ceiling with metal and rubber hammers and if you can work with the neighbor to assess what kind of flanking you might have. assume (there's that word...) you have little flanking issues, then bulking up the dividing wall with additional layers of drywall would likely be a good idea. then assuming you have the rest of the shell sealed and isolation is generally acceptable so that it will work as a leaf, then a room-within-a-room approach should give you most of what you need.
