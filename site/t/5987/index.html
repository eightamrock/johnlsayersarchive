sharward16 April 2006#4
The plywood at the end is sheathing that is used to strengthen the shear walls. This is normal -- my house has it too. That's modern code for you.' Sometimes it's plywood, sometimes it's OSB -- I think it depends on availability and price at the time of construction. The metal corners are called "corner beads." Here's a cute animation that shows how they are installed (click "Video" upon arrival). --Keith :mrgreen:
len-morgan16 April 2006#5
I wonder if the top picture is a product to Celotex? (I think that's what it's called). It would be used as a sheathing product that has a little insulation to it. If you are going to mount drywall to it, you might want to put a layer of OSB down first (between the studs), so that your drywall screws have something to bite into. If it flakes as easily as you say, it's unlikely that it will hold screws for your beef up layer. As to your second picture, it you take another one, could you put something in the picture to give us a size reference? It looks like extremely thick drywall or very small wood framing holding it up. len
