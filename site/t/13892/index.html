Soundman20207 January 2010#20
Ummm... you have no acoustic treatment AT ALL in your room, and you are wondering why you have huge undamped artifacts and can't find a good subwoofer position? Like Javier said: Rotate the room 90°, set up the speakers and mix position in the CORRECT locations with the correct starting-point angles, install large bass traps in all the corners, place thick absorption at ALL of your first reflection points, THEN measure. In any case, this isn't a listening room, its a studio, so the position for the subwoofer is already set: on the floor, between your feet, up towards the front wall, and at roughly the same distance from your head as the main speakers. Put it anywhere else much further away than that, and there will be delays between the lows/mids/highs and the subwoofer reaching your ears, so the bass will be smeared slightly. Yeah, bass is not directional and sounds cool, so in a home theater you can put the sub anywhere that gets you the most possible chest-shaking "oomph" to impress your friends. But in a studio the bass has to be correctly timed and at the correct level, or else it won't be tight and easy to mix, and your mixes won't translate well. From your response graph, you clearly already have a problem in your lows, which would be the next thing you should have been aiming to fix after fixing those low-mid issues, but they will probably disappear anyway as soon as you orient your room correctly, get the mix position out of the mid-room nulls, and get the correct basic treatment in there. My bet is that one you do that and measure again, your new graph will show a rather different picture, which will reveal the REAL issues with the room itself, not the issues that you have created for yourself. Also, from your new diagram it's clear that you used only a single layer of 12mm drywall over a 5cm air gap, so it's very likely that the resonant frequency of your MSM system is way up there in the lows or mid-lows, so on your graph you are probably seeing some wall resonance issues that are getting back into your room, as well as the other issues, such as resonance of the 12mm drywall itself in the mids. Your graph doesn't go high enough to show it, but my guess is that you probably also have artifacts up around the range of the coincidence dip for that wall. The air gap should have been at least 10cm, and the inner-leaf should have been at least two layers of 15mm drywall in order to keep the MSM resonance down low enough. There isn't much you can do about that now, except to take the inner-leaf walls down, move them in 5cm, but them back up (with an extra 5cm of rockwool in the cavity). and use two layers of 15mm instead of one layer of 12mm. I guess you could also just do it by taking off the sheetrock and running hat channel across the studs, then putting the new 15mm sheetrock on again. I say "hat channel" not RC, because I'm assuming that your inner-leaf studs are already decoupled from the outer-leaf wall? Correct? If not, then you could use RC and gain much better isolation, as well as improving your acoustics. - Stuart -
