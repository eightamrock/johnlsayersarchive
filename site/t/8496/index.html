Hmmm depends on what you mean by isolation I guess. Do you mean:
    1. Sound transferred from your room to other rooms via the ductwork. 2. Sound transferred from your room to an adjacent room, not directly through the ductwork, but in the general vicinity where ductwork lies (i.e. less insulation is in the wall where the ductwork is, so what is the reduction in STC. 3. Silencers would typically handle #1, but if you make them larger than the supply ductwork (and use diffusers) you can handle #3 as well. The tradeoff is that as your silencer gets big, you have a larger cavity in your wall where ther is less insulation stuffed, so # 2 suffers. Has this been quantified somewhere....I'm not sure. camistan wrote:
1.) I've got two flexduct return lines running to my air handler unit. One return is 8" coming from my Control Room. (The supply in the Control Room is also 8") The other return is 7" and it's coming from my Vocal Booth. (The supply in the Vocal Booth however is 8"...I simply couldn't get an 8" return to fit in the pathway I took to my Vocal Booth)
1st off - I hate the use of flex duct in lieu of regular duct - to me it is a plauge on HVAC systems the wy most people use it......... especially in long runs. I might make a long lined metal duct run - and then transition to an outlet with 2 or 3 feet of flex - but I would never ever use more than 3 feet - and even then only when the transition is difficult - AND I have other means of isolating rooms from one another. SO seperate HVAC systems for live and control rooms- AND carry the walls between them all the way up so the rooms cannot directly communicate with one another.......
Below is a diagram of my HVAC set up. I would think since my air handler is a fair distance away from my Control Room & even further away from my Vocal Booth, noise would be fairly low from the air handler. I don't have the space to build one of the units I've seen on here, so I'm attempting to do the next best thing.
The first thing to do is to determine what you need - if you need anything at all....... Flex duct is good in the respect that (seeing as it's side walls have virtually no mass) it doesn't carry mechanical sounds for very long distances........ It is bad in the respect that due to it's basic design - and the fact that it can be easily deformed - even under it's own weight - it can produce it's own air noise. Buffeting air makes lots of noise all on it's own.
2.) I tried to match my supply and return size for each room. (It didn't work for my Vocal Booth as I had to use a 7" return instead of an 8" due to the "narrow pathway" to get to the Vocal Booth) Ok, let's see if I have this right... If I use a larger supply from my trunk to each room (let's say a 10" supply duct) the volume for *1 VENT* would stay the same whether I used 8" duct or 10" duct but the VELOCITY would be lower if I used 10" duct with a larger register. If I have this right shouldn't this somewhat help quiet the air flow "noise"? Once again, "IF" this is correct and the volume stays the same it shouldn't matter if I have a 7" return should it?
Nope - the volume would only stay the same if you had a balancing damper so you could make an adjustment for volume. Let's picture the extreem for a moment - suppose you have a 16" x 40" main plenum............ and once it's installed it has 1" of static pressure - and volume of 6,000 cfm - which means I am moving air at 1500 fpm. Assume for the sake of this conversation that the fan can only produce 1" of static pressure or it goes into thermal overload. So if I install a 100' run of 12" round duct - the 1" of static (back) pressure is going to send about 675 cfm down my trunk - which is going to travel about 850fpm........ now I shrink that pipe to 8"........... that same static pressure will only send around 270 cfm @ a velocity of about 800fpm. 7" pipe will be 160cfm @ a velocity of about 600 cfm. These numbers (of course) assume a balanced return - removing the same amount of air that we are supplying...... Note how the velocity drops along wih the cfm - all while maintaining the same static pressure the fan is capable of producing........ BUT IN YOUR CASE, the problem is this - you're force feeding your 7" from an 8"...... and you have the air locked inside a room - so you're telling this 7" that you want it to take everything the fan is capable of - and if't it's 1" of static - the fan trips out on thermal overload - and will continue to do so until something is repaired. But now let's assume the fan is capable of whatever we need it to do..... My setup right now is creating 1" of static pressure on the system... but when I shrink down to 7" - and move the same volume - watch what happens...... Static pressure climbs to 2.6" and velocity climbs to almost 1100 cfm..... all this while putting a greater strain on the motor....... so installing a 10" supply will increase your problem unless you install a balancing damper on the supply line and lower the flow until the return is where you want it - but then again - you could do that with the 8". Your real problem will be if you NEED all of the air that the 8" is providing......... if that's the case then the only thing you could do to quiet down the return would be to install a larger return.......
