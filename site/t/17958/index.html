Fine, but if you might do 5.1 in the future then you should design the room for 5.1 now. Trying to do 5.1 in a room that was not designed for it is not a good idea. Ordinary stereo mixing (either 2.0 or 2.1) can sound fantastic and be done easily in a 5.1 room, but not the other way around. If 5.1 is in your future, you should build the room that way now, even if you don't install the other three or four speakers yet.

that depends on what design concept you want to use for the room, and on the reason for angling the walls and ceiling. For example, if this is just about reducing flutter echo, then 12° is what you need on the ceiling, and 6° on each wall. But if you are going to do an RFZ design then the angles will probably be much greater. And if you are going to do a CID design or traditional rectangular room design, then you might not need to angle the walls or ceiling themselves, but rather angle the treatment that goes inside the room. You should first define your goals and basic design philosophy, then chose the dimensions, size, shape and angles based on that.

What is CID design?
"CID" is "Controlled Image Design". It is basically a further development of RFZ, but using angled panels around the front of the room to sort of "focus" the sound past the engineer, sort of like a Fresnel lens does, except on a huge scale. Hard to to do right! :)
but I was thinking about angeling the constructed walls of the control room to get some extra space for the live room.
Yep! That's a good way of doing RFZ. The angles have to be pretty large anyway, so it's a good idea to use the lost triangular spaces as part of another room.

Soundman202018 December 2012#13
Well, if it is already built and you can't change it, then let's just hope that it will work out OK. Where I live (Chile), walls must be firmly attached to floors, and that can still be done with acoustic isolation in mind. But it cannot be done the way your carpenter did it, since there is nothing holding the walls to the underlying structural floor. It would not pass inspection here, and I'm a little surprised it passed inspection in Sweden. But it is what it is, and if that meets your local legal and structural requirements, then that's how it will have to be. Technically, that is not a floated floor, but more like the drum-riser concept applied to the entire room. It does not truly isolate, but it sure does help for impact noise.
Lets focus on the ceiling instead. I am thinking about building some kind of cloud above the listening pos.
You definitely will need something up there! Treating your first reflection points is a key concept in acoustic treatment, and the ceiling is a very large first reflection point.
but I dont have enough ceiling height to both angle the ceiling and build a cloud, so my question is, whats the best way to go about this? The ceiling height is now approx 263 cm.
Leave the ceiling flat, and as high as possible, then hang an angled cloud below it. The cloud can just be a simple wooden frame filled with insulation, such as 10cm of OC-703 or something similar. Hang it from wires or chains (so that you can adjust the angle easily), and angle it as much as possible, within the limits of your height restriction. If you suspect that you will have modal issues in the vertical direction, then you could make it a "hard backed" cloud by attaching a thick layer of plywood, OSB or MDF on top of the frame. Ideally, you should angle it 12° or more, but that might not be possible. - Stuart -

have learned that one should try to have a two leaf construction everywhere. And in this case leaf nr 1 is the brick wall between the live room and the storage, the storage acts as a very large air gap( i guess), and the inside of the iso booth with one layer 12 mm OSB and two layers of 12 mm Gypsum board is leaf nr 2.
Well, yes, two-leaf construction does greatly increase isolation, but only if you do it on ALL sides of the room, including the ceiling. If you only do it on one wall, it won't work very well. Sound is like water? it takes the easy path out, so if you do one wall really well, the sound will just take another path out of the room, and go around that wall. So for maximum isolation, you need two leaves on every side of the room. - Stuart -
