lgagnon.rm9 May 2008#36
The only fonction of the plastic sleeve is to make the installation of the fabric easy, it is removed when the fabric is in place. I used expandable fabric so I tried to slide it without the plastic sleeve it's impossible. For the sound I never heard a real tubetrap, so I can't compare. I recorded drum tracks with the 13 traps I made sourrounding the drum and I made comparaison. The most obvious difference to me is on the snare. When the tubes were placed with the diffusive side towards the drum the snare is tigher, it has less bottom. With the absobant side towards the drum it is not what I am looking for, too muffled I don't like it. I tried them around the mixing desk and it's great! I can move them around to get what I want. In conclusion, I was looking for acoustic treatment for my studio for a long time and I read all the threads on forum explaining how to make bass traps and absorbers. All these solutions seemed to be appropriate for studios that have a separate control and tracking room, that's not my situation. I was very consious that I could not make the drum or other instrument sound as if they were recorded in Nashville but I would gladly take all the possible improvement. The tubetrap concept attracted me because of it's flexibilty, I can use them to record and I can also use them to create a neutral space for monitoring. I am very happy with the results All comment appreciated
