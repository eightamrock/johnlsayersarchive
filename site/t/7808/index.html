Howdy folks - after a few months of reading I'm hoping I've done enough homework to get some help with my plans for my new space! I've bought Rod's book, checked the FAQ's, and tried to do some research. Any design assistance would be appreciated. The Plan: I'm going to build a studio in an existing video post production facility in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. The studio will be in commercial leased space. I'd like a control room and a live room. I'd like to be able to record drums without driving everyone else crazy or making the upstairs tenants call the police and/or the landlord. The style of music is almost always alt.country, roots rock, or folk. The Budget: $10,000 or so - we're on a low budget - most clients are indie filmmakers or musicians with some broadcast TV thrown into the mix from time to time. Some of my partners in the project have lots of construction experience framing walls etc., so they'll be helping / doing most of that. We've found a consultant locally to help us with permit issues, HVAC, and the fire sprinklers. The Existing Space: I've attached a sketchup screen print, sketchup file, scans of the floor plan, and some photos of the existing space. We have the lower floor of a commercial building. The ceiling is made of 2"x11" wood joists that are 16" on centre covered with 5/8" drywall. The walls are 2"x4" metal studs covered by 5/8" drywall. The walls are built inside the original stone foundation. There's an interesting concrete room built into the northwest corner of the space. The celings are 9' 2 1/4" high. The floor is concrete. We're just going to paint it, unless someone convinces me that it's a problem acoustically. There is a closet on the east side of the room (left side of the sketchup drawing / floor plan) that will be removed, but on the other side of it is an air conditioning unit that I can't move. There is a video editing suite bordering the southeast corner of the room (lower left) that can't be moved. The entrance to that suite can't be blocked, so there will have to be a hallway along the south side of the studio I build. There is a fire exit on the west side of the drawings that can't be moved. The main entrance is on the other side of the building, as is the lobby, bathrooms, etc. Sketchup file: The sketchup file is attached - it's called "winwinexisting.skp" I've decided one of the closets is coming out, so I deleted that from the sketchup drawing. kendale3 January 2007#13
Aloha Dave,
Is that a standard template that you have or did you draw it specifically for the space I'm working with?
This was a drawing that I had been working on to improve my Sketchup skills. It is based on John's comment on the SAE website - http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m (19.68480’ x 16.40400’ x 7.87292’). John Sayers, SAE Manual This size room means a good sized working area with space for the musos, friends and hangers on. Also because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension: i.e. 6m x 5m.
The reason I'm wondering is the sliding door on the North side. Is there one on the South side as well?
Actually, yes. But you could easily take either/both of them out and put in a standard door on the south flat wall.
I like that only part of the room is angled - more space that way.
This was something I noticed John incorporates in his layouts.
You didn't draw a double wall, but I'm guessing I should still be building one?
The drawing wasn't quite done when I posted it, but I thought I'd offer it for your consideration, and yes, you would want to have a double wall for the front & angled portions of your control room. The rear portion would already have a double wall (a third leaf, if you include your exterior stone wall. Normally you would try to avoid having a third leaf when possible.) Let me make the adjustments you mentioned and then I'll post the skp. file. Aloha 8)
