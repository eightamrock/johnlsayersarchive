Cleats vs glue to external sheeting?

Started by Mastiff on 12 February 2020. 2 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 22274.

Mastiff12 February 2020#1
I need to beef up an exterior wall, so my plan is to insert drywall between the studs up against the exterior sheeting. I believe the recommendation among people around here is to only press the drywall up against the exterior sheeting, hold in place with cleats if necessary and then caulk all the seams. Is it bad, acoustically, to glue the drywall to the exterior sheeting with construction adhesive (or green glue, though I know it's not glue in the same sense)? Or is it just a practical consideration to allow exterior sheeting to be removed in the future?
Gregwor17 February 2020#2
You could totally glue it and it would work. But, it won't work as well as using Green Glue Compound and cleats. Check out Green Glue's website to see the benefits of Green Glue. Also, I recommend caulking all the joints before putting up the drywall. Then, of course, caulk the joints after the drywall has been installed. Tip: Install the cleats, then caulk the drywall but don't caulk around the cleat. Let that dry for a few days. Then, move the cleats over like 6 inches or whatever so that they are screwed in over an area that is already caulked. Then, caulk the spots where the cleats were. This will ensure a complete seal around the drywall. Greg
