Okay, guys. After doing tons of reading, analyzing many different plans, and playing with several designs over the last few months, I'm finally ready to get some input from the masters and see if you can help me work on my design. Sorry, I don't have Sketchup drawings. They don't have the free version for Mac owners, yet. I'm designing a studio for my church. It is a renovation of the old choir room and is currently finished out with standard 1/2" sheetrock on the walls and a dropped ceiling with 2 x 4' acoustic tiles. The buliding is a steelframed construction and the studio floor will be floated on existing concrete slab. The ceiling is high and sloped to one corner which I would like to take advantage of, but I don't presently know what's above - or how much space is above - the existing dropped ceiling, I just know we'll have to treat it. The biggest challenge I have is the given criteria. We have a good amount of space (35' 10.25" x 23' 6.5") which is completely accessible on 2 full walls, but have a number of goals which make our design choices a bit limited. Here's the limitations: 1) 2 Control rooms. One larger main control room and a smaller overdub/edit/mix/MIDI suite with iso booth. 2) Both control rooms must be accessible to the hallway or lounge without disturbing the other control room or recording space. 3) Needs 1 main tracking room and 2 iso booths probably sharing with the small control room but not necessary. 4) Ideally the smaller control room will have visibility to the main tracking room. (Not necessary, but desired.) 5) No direct accessibilty to outside. (Accessibe only through hallway.) 6) Ideally, main tracking room utilizes high ceilings in space by existing door. (Not necessary, but desired.) 7) Ideally, we would have a small machine room for both CRs. (Not necessary, but desired.) That's all I can think of at the moment. If I think of any other challenges I'll post them. Let me know if you have any questions. Fire away! I'm excited to see what you come up with. --------------------------------------- I'm posting multiple images of the existing space and a couple of my ideas.

well you can teach an old dog new tricks. :D They are good designs. We are now talking of a bigger area as the left hallway has now been incorporated, the high ceiling has become a booth? Old dog - you have to do some math. Bruce Blue Bear for his studio estimated in 2002 that it cost him approx $60- 80/sqft. It was built totally by contractors and aircon was an extension of an existing system. Remember, if it's sound proof, it's airtight - you'll die in there if fresh air is not supplied. Your last plan was around 960 sqft = $57,600 - $76,800. Studio construction is expensive because you can't automate it. Every wall is different and at a different angle. Hundreds of sqft of drywall and insulation are used and aircon ducting systems, power, audio wiring etc. have to be built into the design and cost. You are also trying to share recording rooms between two control rooms. The iso costs to achieve that add up and up. Time to get real mate :) I don't want to discourage you but hey, God has put me here to say it. ;) Extra cheers John
