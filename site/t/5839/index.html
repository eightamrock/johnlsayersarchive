Hello all! My dad and I are moving to a house in about 1-2 years. Next to the house, there will be a studio, wich i have to plan now :D The budget is also important, unfortunately. I've come up with some ideas, that may be good or very bad to save some costs (really as much as possible!!!), but I don't want to comprimise the sound of the studio. 1.) I don't know, if I save anything by not needing sound isolation betwen the studio and the sorroundings. The idea now is to make the studio kind of half basement. This would save costs at construction of the shell, but also 2-3m of dirt arround the studio (I think) makes a pretty good isolation with the outside world, doesn't it? I don't know if I'll even need to float the floor! This seems like a win-win solution BUT (and there's always a but...) I don't know how much isolation I lose when thinking about room-to-room isolation in the studio (no floating floor). Would i still need to isolate ceilings and floors of the rooms, or just the walls dividing them? I have a feeling that I need that isolation anyway... :? This is the first thing to think about, because I can't tell the architect the exact size of the shell, If i don't know how much room the isolation will take. And then all the carefully calculated ratios will be lost... Thank you so much! Borut

SonicClang23 March 2006#14
A TON of isolation! Dirt is so heavy, with so much mass, it'll stop a LOT. I've never run any tests on it but I'm sure someone has. There's a highway that runs parallel to my house only about 75 yards away. To keep down on the noise in my neighborhood they built an earth berm and it does wonders. We can have our windows open at night and hardly hear a thing. We'll hear police cars and ambulences and stuff like that, but that's much louder than normal. I'd have to imagine a couple feet of dirt with grass on top would stop a large amount of any sound you've got going on in your studio. Plus whatever mass and air gap you've got on the inside.
