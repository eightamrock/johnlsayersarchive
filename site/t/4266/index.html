Hello all, After 4 years of using completley unacceptably small rooms for my studio, it's time to upgrade to a larger room(s). I have a number of options, but here is an idea that I've considered. I'm very close to finalizing a deal to put a metal building up about 5 minutes out of my hometown of 35,000 in the rural area where land is cheap. The first idea was to put up a rehersal hall for bands to practice as loud as they want whenever they want as there is no service of this sort around here and I could put some nice cash flow into my pocket. Then I had the idea of trying to use the metal building for both my rehersal hall idea and for my recording studio. I'm concerned that the rehersal hall will bleed into my studio quite a bit. I'm trying to figure out if it would be better / cheaper for me to go with seperate buildings or if it I could pull off doing both in the same building.

The odds are strong that I won't be in this building forever. So with that in mind, I'm not going balls to the wall as far as sound proofing is concerned. Any money spent on sound proofing would probably end up as a loss when I end up selling the metal building to someone who just wants a barn. To top off all of this, I'm toying with the idea of putting my living quarters in between the studio and the rehersal hall. Is it reasonable to put a rehersal hall, a small home, and a recording studio all in the same big metal building from a sound proofing perspective without spending $10,000 on soundproofing?

sharward25 August 2005#4
I would recommend you start at your city's or county's Building and Planning Department (whatever it's called there) to determine whether or not you're even allowed to do what you're proposing. Zoning is key. Some of what you want to do may be taken off the table because zoning or planning ordinances prohibit it. Find out what your legal options are, and then we can discuss the pros/cons of all of your options within your budget. Better to know what you can do before getting to excited about any specific direction and then getting let down when gravity sets in. Don't let the "ruralness" of the area lull you into thinking that nobody cares what's going on out there. --Keith :mrgreen:
