thermionic4 August 2006#2
In an ideal world, there should be no difference in performance between solid-state and valve preamp designs; as Tim de Paravincini says, "electrons have no memory". You will find that top-end valve designs can sound just as analytical as pro solid-state designs. However, in the case of the Beh preamps, they are engineered to sound overtly coloured, and have only 40v Plate voltage - this is what's known as a "starved plate" design. Some people may like this kind of colouration, but it's definitely not what I would choose for a measurement preamp. For measurement I would choose a "Forensic Grade" preamp. The top units would be something along the lines of a GML or Millennia, but these are costly and probably excessive for measurement. I would expect a clean, budget solid-state design such as a Rane preamp to work very well indeed, and it shouldn't cost that much more than the Beh. In fact, the Rane preamp won a shootout a few years ago against expensive competition. Google Ebay Example Hope this helps! Justin

scottdru18 August 2006#23
Mario . . . I just happened across this thread. There are a couple of things you should be aware of here. First, ETF is designed to work with cheap consumer sound cards, and as a result it forces you to use the Windows mixer to adjust volume, etc. ETF does not like to work with professional/prosumer audio interfaces. You will be better off just using the soundbastard. I have an RME Digi96/8 PAD and an EMU 1820M installed in my computer, and I have not been able to get ETF to work with either of these. The only way I could get ETF to work was to go back and reenable the cheap sound chip built into my motherboard. ALSO, if money is tight, there is another application that does most of the same stuff that ETF does, but with a much nicer user interface, and it actually has documentation that is possible for regular humans to make sense of (unlike ETF). This application is called Room EQ Wizard, and all you have to do to download it for free is to register for the Home Theater Shack forum. Quick, easy and free to register, and well worth it. http://www.hometheatershack.com/roomeq/ Room EQ Wizard is also designed to work with cheap consumer soundcards. My suggestion would be to get either the Rane or M-Audio preamp and the measurement mic, and then just get the needed adaptors to run from the preamp output to the sound card's line input. You should be able to get these at a good music dealer, or even more likely at an electronics parts store. If you are at all handy with a soldering iron, you could make up your own lead for this, which would probably be even cheaper. There is a good guide to wiring audio interconnect cables here: http://www.warehousesound.com/ranms1.htm BTW, I agree with Thermionic that the Rane pres are very, very good preamps for the money. I've got a rack full of Rane stuff in my studio, including various mic pres, a headphone amp/distributor and a couple of line splitter/mixers, and I'm very happy with all of it. For the mic pres, you would be pretty hard pressed to find a better sounding preamp for anything even remotely close to the price you'd pay for them (even the new prices). As I think has already been mentioned, the only down side of the Rane and the M-Audio pres you are considering is that they don't have an output level adjustment. I know that the Rane preamps do tend to roll off a bit at lower frequencies when you are running them at low gain levels. Another option you might consider is just getting a little Behringer UB802 mixer (which is the smallest of their UB series mixers that has phantom power). These are solid state preamps, wouldn't have the colouring of the cheap starved plate preamps, and they have a pretty flat frequency response and an extended frequency range. I'm not a huge fan of the Behringer products, particularly their preamps (subjectively, they have a bit of a cold, metallic sound to them), and the EQ absolutely sucks, but I would think for basic acoustic testing purposes (such as you would do with ETF or Room EQ Wizard) the pres in the UB mixer would certainly be adequate. And this would also give you some control over the output volume level, so you don't overload the inputs of your sound card. Anyway . . . a few thoughts.
