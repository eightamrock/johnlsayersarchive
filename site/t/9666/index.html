The vapor barrier is in the wrong location - you never EVER place a vapor barrier up against the outside face of an outside wall in a cold climate area (which Toronto certainly is) You will have constant condesation of warm moist air taking place at the surface and it will destroy your framing in the end. if you want the true insulating value of the double wall then the drywall is a must - you have to beef up the mass of the outside wall - and 1/2" isn't really enough - you should have at least 2 layers of 5/8" to have it amount to anything. You don't need to caulk the studs/plates to the sheathing - just the perimiter of the drywall within the stud bay - and then the bottom plate to concrete. Iinsulate with any fluffy insulation you want - nothing special required there........
*2" of air space between structures*
That's fine.......
THE BUILD - Inner Structure: STEP 1: frame with 2x4 wood STEP 2: insulate with Roxul Safe n' Sound STEP 3: install Rez Channel STEP 4: install 2 layers of 1/2" sheetrock (acoustisealing like crazy)
2x4 framing is run at 16" o.c. - 2x6 is run at 24" o.c. (typ for each) BUT - you get better LF attenuation with the 2x6 than with the 2x4 - for exactly the same board feet of lumber - and thus the same cost (for all intent and purpose). Once again - any fluffy insulation is fine - so whatever your best cost is - just go with it. Never EVER use RC with a double wal assembly - you will loose LF isolation - RC is made for use in single frame construction. The splay you added to the wall of the control room now has it in a non-symmetrical shape =- symmetry is crucial in a controlr room - that needs to be fixed. BTW - as to the splayed walls them selves - what you have done has no acoustic value what so ever - it will not help with flutter echo or comb-filtering - it is not enough splay. If you can't devote the space you need to make this work acoustically - then you are better off just leaving square corners and maximizing your room volume. A splayed wall needs to be a minimum of 1 in 10 - thus 6% (actually 5.710593° rounded) to have any acoustic value - and that's for each side of a room - so you'll loose 2" for every 10" - or 18" total by time you reach the back of the control room (at 15' length) - in the tracking room that would be 2' total. In closing - I seriously suggest that you consider the one room combo for a space this small....... you'll be glad you did in the long run (it's the set up that I use in my room by the way).
