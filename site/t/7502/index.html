andy_eade wrote:
I'm shining the big "R" light into the sky...
:lol: That's hilarous! :lol: We need a graphic artist to illustrate what that would look like! :twisted: In my case, I took my cue from Paul Woodlock. I will be building large and beefy soffit-type silencer plenums for my supply and return ventilation ducts: two boxes inside the room, one inside the laundry room, and one inside the garage. These boxes will be made of two or three layers of MDF, plywood, or whatever else I decide to work with. Incoming air will get sucked from the outside, through duct #1 and into the heat recovery ventilator (HRV) where the fans are located. The air will then flow through duct #2 into the box in the laundry room, which will be lined with insulation and maybe a couple of baffles to "zig-zag" the air flow a bit. At the other end of the box will be a hole that goes through the wall and into a flex duct. The flex duct bridges the gap between outer and inner leaves. The other end of the short flex duct terminates to a similar sized and configured box on the inside of the room, with a register at the end. Outgoing air follows a similar path in reverse. I'm not saying you have to go to the same extent that I am, but hopefully this concept will help you understand the concept. I have yet to draw this plan up in detail -- and I'll need to do so soon. I don't think I'll have a chance to do so before you're ready to move forward though... :roll: In short, I think your existing plan is flawed, but I would think that even putting that box of yours inside the room, thus minimizing the size of the penetration in your wall, would be an improvement. How much of one, though, I can't say. --Keith :mrgreen:

rod gervais15 November 2006#9
Andy, buildinga 5 sided box to install the plenum in is the right way to go...... and it seemsto me that you have plenty of space to do this. I would(however) build it seperate from the duct and then slide the duct into it - this way you could set it on some neoprene spacers to keep it even that much more isolated from the wall assembly. I would (however) make the final connection through the use of 4 continuous pieces of bent metal - attached at the fron face - and installed over my last layers of drywall - This metal (made from the same gavanized duct metal) should be bent about 1 to 2 degrees past 90 - and then you can use it like a corner bead to finish tape (and seal) the opening before instaling your return air register. I hope that I am seeing what I see there because your contractor understood all of my comments regarding large volume and low velocity........ As long as noise in the adjacent space actually passing into the duct isn't a bit BIG concern - then you should be fine. I guess I am going to have to add substantially to that chapter when the 2nd edition comes down the pike. Sincerely, Rod
