Innovations15 July 2004#2
What is your business plan? I know we don't talk about business plans here much, but I am pretty certain that you aren't renting all this commercial space in a high rise building for the hobby value. So who are your customers, what are they producing there, How long will they take to do it, what are they likely to be willing to pay for it. and how many simultaneous customer projects do you need to be able to handle? If you go to a professional without those questions answered a responsible professional will first make sure that you have answers to them. Or to draw back to my days in architectural college. The customer's business (or in the case of homes, personal) needs drives the program. Then the program and the space drives the design. A smart professional won't allow this be an informal understanding either. The program will be an contract document that the customer signs off on before the designer even begins designing.

CHESKY wrote:
THIS PROJECT IS CLEARLY NOT COMMERCIAL. IT WILL WORK MORE AS A TINK TANK FOR MUSICIAN. BUILT BY AND FOR CREATIVE SOUND ISSUES.
What I read between the lines here would call for a space with maximum flexibility in layout and instrumentation. In other words one control room and one large space and using gobos if you need more isolation between the specific instruments that you are using for an evening. I know that you most likely are not looking for somebody to question your basic assumptions but I would have sought out a strip warehouse building rather than a high-rise office building. Cheaper rents per foot and higher ceilings. Also less hassle moving instruments and such in and out.
