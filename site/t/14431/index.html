Hi, This is my first post to the forum though I've been a long time reader. A new addition is going to be built to our Musical Arts Center. It will include 2 large rehearsal rooms with a large recording studio/teaching space adjacent. I have been working with the acousticians on the design, while they've designed numerous educational facilities in music schools, it's pretty clear that they have limited experience designing recording studios. I thought I'd post the design here for comments. Since this is new construction we have the opportunity to design the space correctly from the beginning. Presently we are in the preliminary design phase with construction slated to start within a year. This studio will be used both for recording and teaching classes with as many as 20 students. The space will be isolated from the rehearsal rooms by double masonry walls. Raw dimensions are about 20' high, 24' wide 27' deep so there's plenty of space to work with. The acoustician came up with a design featuring splayed walls with approximate dimensions of 16' high, 20' wide, 24' deep, very close to an ideal ratio, and it puts the room modes at about 20 - 30 hz which is very good. However he has proposed framing the room with lightweight material, basically making the entire space a large Helmholtz resonator. This strikes me as difficult to pull off correctly - I've worked in countless studios and I don't ever recall seeing this kind of design. We are not planning on soffit mounting speakers, so we will have to deal with the low frequency reflections from the front of the room. He has indicated bass trapping in the front of the room, but does not appear to be familiar with traditional studio trapping design. He plans to treat the rest of the room by hanging acoustic panels on the walls. I have explained to him that a studio is not a performance space, or even a listening space, it must be as flat and even as possible but I think that he is used to designing performance spaces where greater reverberation in the low end is desirable because it has a warm sound. Soundman20201 June 2010#2
a design featuring splayed walls with approximate dimensions of 16' high, 20' wide, 24' deep, very close to an ideal ratio,
Which "ideal ratio" would that be? The dimensions you give turn out to give a ratio that is in the general ball park of Louden's 5th best ratio (which is 1:1.2:1.5, while yours is 1:1.25:1.5). But since you have splayed walls, ratios are somewhat of a moot point anyway. It also looks suspiciously like you are going to have some three-leaf walls in there.
it puts the room modes at about 20 - 30 hz which is very good.
Actually it gives you 39 modes under 100 Hz, and 119 modes below 130 Hz. Only the first two are in the range you mention, at 23.5 and 28.3. But the mode spacing isn't all that great, and room modes are dominant all the way up to 73 Hz. But once again, that is theoretical for a rectangular room, and yours is going to have splayed walls, so all bets are off on what the real modes will turn out to be, other than the vertical and lengthwise axials.
However he has proposed framing the room with lightweight material, basically making the entire space a large Helmholz resonator.
I'm not sure quite what he means by "making the whole room into a Helmholtz resonator"! How would that work? In order to work as a Helmholtz Resonator, it would have to have some kind of restricting neck open to something else, with a slug of air resonating in the neck! How can a sealed, isolated, airtight room also be a Helmholtz Resonator? Maybe he could explain that to you, since it's hard to picture. A room will resonate at its natural modes anyway, but that doesn't make it into a Helmholtz resonator.
We are not planning on soffit mounting speakers,
Any particular reason for not doing that, when you basically have a blank sheet of paper to design on, and soffit mounting is so beneficial?
He has indicated bass trapping in the front of the room, but does not appear to be familiar with traditional studio trapping design. He plans to treat the rest of the room by hanging acoustic panels on the walls.
What about the floor and ceiling? What plans does he have for those? If the inner leaf is basically a masonry shell with concrete floor and ceiling, with that "light weight construction" thing inside it, and the only treatment is some bass trapping at the front of the room and a few panels on the walls, it sounds to me like that room will still be rather too live, to say the least! A concrete cave with just a bit of absorption on the front wall, that isn't really an effective bass trap at all... Perhaps you should consider hiring a real studio designer, who has a proven track record in designing spaces like this, such as John. - Stuart -
