I have joined because of my own work in progress project here in Isleworth, London UK. This is a small-scale loft conversion to an existing 1930’s property. Building regulations, party wall and planning restrictions are being fully adhered to. The windows are all double glazed. Currently I have a home studio setup in a ground floor room. I am looking for advice on soundproofing and acoustic treatement, as well as organising the room. I am totally non-technical so apologies in advance for any omissions etc. but as well as the notes below I am attaching the building engineer's scale drawings, some rough and ready measurements I made yesterday and some photos I have just taken.

There are two studio users here – I am a pianist and work in jazz, acoustic, soundtrack and world music styles (often with female vocals and occasionally live percussion), the other musician is a pianist and guitarist who works in orchestral classical, and sometimes heavy rock with male vocal. I already have most of the studio equipment, a mixture of rack units, microphone/s and PC hardware/software, and monitor with 2x Focal CMS50 and Focal CMS Subwoofer. Neither of us “hammer” the output levels while monitoring, but obviously percussive sounds and live singing do carry. The party wall (on the left hand side of the 1st plan) is protected by some insulated space as you can see, and the neighbours do not currently have a loft room on their side, so I would mainly want to avoid noise carrying ‘diagonally’ down into the neighbour’s first floor area. We are semi-detached on the other side (along with the large volume of unoccupied space between the edge of the loft room and the outer wall) , and with a reasonable size garden and no facing properties, so I am less worried about noise escaping through the windows, roof or other side. It is a pretty quiet area and there is no issue about external noise (traffic, trains etc.) coming in. The loft room sits above two other bedrooms and so sound insulation via the floor is very important; audio monitors and live singing are the primary sources. I may well have a TV in there also. Once insulation is addressed, I am then looking for guidance in terms of acoustic treatment and positioning of equipment. This is a relatively modest setup and a small-ish room so I was not envisioning a sealed vocal area. On the bottom left diagram on the attached sheet I have tentatively indicated where I thought the vocal and monitoring area would go. One consideration was that I would ideally like to have my master keyboard where I could see/interact with the vocalist, since some of the recordings will be “live” performances, but that doesn’t have to be the same location as the mixing area. In terms of budget I am of course looking to use what I have wisely but it is essential that I can use the studio and that means ensuring adequate soundproofing and reasonable acoustic treatment. I'm hoping that £1,000-£2,000 would be enough, but I do have a bit of margin if necessary. I have an experienced builder and team undertaking the work who I hope are capable of undertaking the installation side (and they are very reasonable on cost), but am open to advice on this too. Thanks in advance for any thoughts, advice, comments and so on!! I will post a couple of music links shortly also ...Chris

Hi, Are you currently doing the reno? Or is the work from the exsiting owner? What is your budget for? Just the treatment or the building process? Can you stop works and start from scratch? I don't have the knowledge to help you with the flanking path down to your neighbour in such a small room. But I assume it will need to be fixed via bandaid methods..... The slopping plaster work though, I think you can start again using 2 x layers of 16mm plaster board and build with some more care than standard house builds. Stagger the layers and seal all joins as you go. Sorry I am only going off the pixilated pic attached.
