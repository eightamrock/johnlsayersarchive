Garage studio - what to cover the walls?

Started by mattiasc on 22 January 2008. 21 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 10079.

mattiasc22 January 2008#1
Hi all, I have a 20' x 20' garage that I am renting but the owner has given me the go ahead to do what I want with the space. It has a concrete floor, pitched roof, exposed beams and exposed studs framing the shell of the garage. The object for me is to have a practical mixing room that is relatively flat. I don't mix loud, and I don't need to worry about sealing the room as much as I need good acoustics. The only live recording will be vocals, acoustic guitar and voice overs, so I'm just going to make it one big room. I am currently wiring everything according to the star wiring scheme which has been very helpful. I would have not learned that without this site. But my question now pertains to the walls since that's where I will be soon after I finish wiring. My plan is to first stuff every square inch between the wall studs up to the pitched roof with rockwool. I also have 4x8' large gobos built with rockwool and wrapped in fabric. These will be angled around the room so there are no parallel walls. Heavy bass trapping in the corners as well. Question: After the rockwool, should I cover my walls with 5/8" drywall and later play with hanging absorbtion and diffusors, the gobos, etc. or should I simply cover the walls with a porous fabric? Fabric would make it more dead, drywall more live. But, I have a concrete floor. Maybe I need to go for as dead as possible with the walls? I had considered covering the walls with pegboard but read that is not a good idea and that it would create a helmholtz resonator effect. Thank you for any insight you may have. Matt
Ro23 January 2008#2
Hai mattiasc and welcome. So, your current construction is just a frame with one layer on the outside? In that case, I'd hang GB (Gypsum Board) on the inside after filling up the gaps with wool. It'll increase your transmission loss (isolation). Even if it's not your goal to have added isolation. When done you can, indeed, use wool to adjust the acoustics in your new room. When just adding clouth to the inside instead of the GB you will create a very, very dead room. Why don't you give us some more details on your current construction. (photos may help).

bassman25 January 2008#8
Seems like you need something quick and dirty to get going. If you are not going to seal up the garage door permanently, then the options become less IMO. I would use the shelf to hold your speakers. You could also fill up the remaining space on the shelf with wool to create some massive bass traps which you will need since its 20x20, a square box basically. If you want to get into hanging all that sheetrock and then adding more treatment, that will work and give a better TL but it sounds like in your situation that a modular approach using stand alone gobos would be better. Plus you can take them with you when you go-go! Make gobos that have both a dead side with fabric and a live side with slats. then you can simply place them around your mixing space and rotate some of them to create lively-ness as you need it. Portable studio. I think that is a better investment than building a whole bunch of stuff in a rental space. -bassman

xSpace27 January 2008#13
Yes sir, what I see is there is no air for the building first and you guys second;). There are no gable or ridge vents to throw off buildup of heated air in the roof. The yellow lines (in the included cutout of your picture) pointing to the eave/overhang is an airflow area. It is probably not vented either. I'm just looking at some areas that the building needs to throw off heated air buildup and not seeing them. This affects the building but once closed up will start affecting you and people in this room. Heat will find the path of least resistance ,which will be the ceiling, and enter there. It will also warp, over time, roof decking and shorten the shingle life considerable. I see these things coming back to you in the not so distant future so I just offer it as it is there to consider.
