Is it as simple as installing some roughly 18 ft long joists between opposite walls - would that require a double frame or could it be done with a single frame that is also holding my inner walls up?
It MUST be done on he same inner-leaf framing. The inner-leaf needs to be one continuous "shell", with all parts sealed together, and no part touching anything that is not "inner-leaf". It is an isolated "envelope". However, spanning 18 feet with two layers of 5/8" drywall is not so easy: You'd be looking at 2x10 joists for that, probably, and maybe more. So do make sure you are spanning across the shortest distance, and the span that you measure is just the unsupported section of the joist, between the two inner leaf walls. But your structural engineer can tell you about that more accurately, and what you need to meet your local code.
Okay, I'm confused now. I had read John Sayers in another post recommend a heat pump because he said the normal split units just cool the air but don't actually bring air in. My question is, some(many) of these units have a heat pump built in.
"Heat pump" is basically just another name for the mechanism of an air conditioner, except that it can be reversed to also heat the room, if needed, instead of just cooling it. But it is still the same basic principal: a compressor with a radiator and fan in the box that goes outside, connected by copper pipes to the other "box" that goes inside the room, and basically contains another radiator and fan. That's what a "mini-split" system is. Some are cooling-only, some can heat and cool, in which case "heat pump" is the more appropriate technical term for the box-that-goes-outside. But "heat pump" does not imply that the system also moves fresh air in and stale air out. That's a different part of the system. You need both: You need the actual mechanical system as described above, regardless of whether it does both heating and cooling or just cooling, and you also need ducts to move fresh / stale air.
I'm concerned that the cost of purchasing a split unit and a separate heat pump
The "heat pump" is part of the mini-split system, not something separate. It is the same basic principle of operation, as described above. A heat pump is basically just an air conditioner where the flow of the coolant can be reversed, so that it will either heat the room or cool the room, as needed. You do not need both heat-pump and mini-split. A mini-split in your room is all that you need for controlling the temperature and humidity. But IN ADDITION to that, you also need ducts, fans, and silencers to bring in some fresh air and exhaust the stale air.
Is there a mini-split unit that would recirculate air?
There are a few. Not many, and most don't, but there are a couple that do have a built-in "mini duct" that also brings in a bit of fresh air. However, I'm not aware of any min-split that also has an extra duct to remove stale air, and with a studio you do need that. For an ordinary home, that is not a problem since there are multiple paths that air can take back out of the room. But a studio is double-sealed air-tight, so there is no way for the stale air to get out, and therefore those mini-split that do bring in fresh air will not do the job very well! So you need proper ducts anyway. They don't have to be fancy: just a round duct with a fan in it to move the air, and a silencer box on the leaf penetrations to prevent sound getting through, and that's it. Of course, you do need to make sure that the duct is big enough for the room, and that the fan moves enough air, quietly, but it isn't complicated.
Is that something I can figure or would that require "a professional"?
You can calculate it if you want (the math isn't that complex), or you could just take a look at IR-761, which is a document that shows literally hundreds of types of isolation construction that were tested in an acoustical laboratory, and gives the results of how well each one isolates. If you follow what they show, then you'll get good isolation.
If I just "wing it" - how bad could I screw it up?
How lucky do you feel, and how many rabbit's feet do you have? :) It is possible to screw up pretty bad, without trying too hard. But if you follow the general "rules of thumb" then you should be OK: Those are: Two leaves only, on separate frames, minimum 4" air gap, minimum one layer of 5/8" drywall on each leaf, both leaves totally sealed air-tight, and plenty of insulation in the air gap between them. Or even better, follow the exact method from IR-761 that best fits your needs.
I have been toying with SkethUp but I can't seem to make it behave the way I want it to quickly enough.
Take a look at the on-line tutorials for it. There are plenty of good ones that walk you through the basics. After a while it just sort of "clicks", and starts making sense. The key is to make everything into individual "components", instead of leaving them as surfaces... So the six surfaces that make up a stud should be grouped as a component, then you can duplicate that component as many times as you need. And if you then change the length of any one of those copies, they all change to follow (for example, if you need to change the height of your walls). Etc. But work through the tutorials, until you get the hang of it. You'll be glad you did! :)
