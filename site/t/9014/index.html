moiremusic25 June 2007#1
I've finished designs on the isolation booth... the previous discussion can be found here. I had even less room than I had expected, and this will be a very tight fit. Basically I'm constructing a booth for use with vocals and loud amps, within a space about 4'8" by 3'8". I can fudge the 4'8" side a little more if I needed, but the 3'8" side is not negotiable. A tight fit for a vocalist, but that's compromise I'll have to make. Sadly, this means Pavoratti will never be able to record here. John and Glenn informed me in my previous post that I could line the booth with 2" 703, which would absorb most of the frequencies except the low-mids around 150hz-500hz. Those would need to be dealt with by using a slat absorber. My design has floor-to-ceiling slat absorbers on two sides, rotated slightly off 90 degrees. The slat absorber design I came up with using the Excel spreadsheet floating around here is made up of alternating 3 1/2" x 3/4" and 6" x 3/4" slats with 1/4 gaps between them. I've got a JeldWen ProCore Quiet Door for use with this, an adjustable closer for the outside, and am considering the Qwyatt door bottom. I've got a few feet of 1/4" neoprene rubber that I will use to make two 1/2" strips that the entire booth will sit on. There will be absorption panels made of 4" 703 on the door, ceiling, and wall with window. No clouds or overhead chambers are possible in this space. The height constraints are already maxed out. The walls will be filled with 703 as well. The wall will be made of one 5/8" sheet of drywall on the inside and two 1/2" sheets on the outside. See the images and SketchUp models attached to get an idea of where I'm at with this. Please, if anyone has any input, now is the time. I'll begin construction in a week or two.
