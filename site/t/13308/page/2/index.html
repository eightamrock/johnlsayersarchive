gullfo4 May 2011#30
i believe it's a false ceiling i.e. you have it faced with plastic under the cloth - this way you get a lot of absorption overhead and the plastic preserves from HF. you could readily add ducts, lights etc as well as widely spaced slats and so on to it.
lumisuto4 May 2011#31
Oh I see, so I guess that no gypsum boards are attached to this false cieling. If this is the case the walls dividing the CR from the Studio should cover everything from cieling to floor airtight?
gullfo5 May 2011#32
right - build out all the isolation walls and ceiling to separate the rooms, then add the treatments - in this case the false ceiling. that said, depending on your space, say you have an 10'x10'x10' room, you could make the angled ceiling hard using drywall/plywood/etc to change the mode behavior (and one of the side walls etc.) then again, if you have enough space, then you might make hard angled ceilings for the same reasons but have the height to spare.

xSpace6 May 2011#35
If you already have a concrete barrier, walls/floor and ceiling, with walls that maintain a mass/spring mass between the two rooms, the framed walls may be the deciding factor on your transmission loss/isolation between the two rooms. The false acoustic ceiling helps to reduce the impact of the room on the listening area, so it may well be helpful. But the isolation properties of the existing rooms/walls should be taken into consideration...testing is either required or guesses need to be made:)
