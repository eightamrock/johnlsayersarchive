Hi, I have a ~45sqm space in on the 5th floor of a commercial office block that I would like to turn into a recording studio. It is located in Sydney, Australia. My budget is approximately $20,000.00 USD/AUD. I have attached a sketch-up diagram showing the empty space. It has a concrete wall on one side, concrete floor, and the remainder of the sides are gyprock dividing other suites in the building. The roof is the standard floating 'acoustic tile' you see in commercial buildings which has little in the way of acoustic properties! Ventilation is a individually controlled but centralised air conditioning system. I have done a number of floor plans but I'm not sure I'm really happy with them as I have a number of challenges I do not know how to solve in the best manner. Most designs have been around creating two separate rooms one for live and the other for control. With the use of movable panels in the live room to save from building a separate vocal booth. I don't necessarily want to post the designs as I'd rather 'open it up to the floor' for out-of-the-box suggestions. My requirements are as follows: - Live room for recording vocals and instruments such as electric and acoustic guitars (and drums as desirable if the room is big enough) - Attenuate produced sound sufficiently to not disturb neighbours. The design can also work on assumptions such as only certain instruments during non-business hours, etc - Control room for stereo mixing - Some sound attenuation from road noise which comes through the windows which are not double glazed. My challenges are as follows: - Windows: I am not sure how to incorporate the windows into the control room without feeling claustrophobic. It appears to me I either run with a room-in-a-room concept and place either perspex or glass windows in the room or abandon the room-in-a-room concept altogether. - Ventilation: If I pass the building ventilation into the live or control rooms, I am going to loose all of the sound attenuation of the rooms! I was thinking of passing the ventilation into the control room only as it won't be as loud as a Marshall stack on 11 but then I question why having a room-in-a-room at all. Hopefully some suggestions from experienced builders can lead me in the right direction in terms of an overall design and then I can start worrying about how many layers of gyprock to use or if I should use 4kg or 8kg wavebar! Thanks.

SquarePants3 June 2008#7
And... this should probably be in the Design Forum. Firstly, "fifth floor" rings some alarm bells. Check with a structural engineer as to how much that floor can handle. The designs here have an emphasis on added mass. That ceiling height is not ideal. Allowing for an "inner ceiling", your ears and monitors will likely be half way between floor and ceiling, requiring less than ideal work-arounds. Bigger is better for control rooms. That space might fit a control room and iso-booth, with the option of recording in the control room when required. Air-con and ventilation are 2 different animals. Your rooms must be air-tight to work, so you'll need added oxygen, for breathing (ventilation). Secondly, muso's can get a bit "warm" playing through Marshalls at 11 (air-con). All up, is that space really suitable? Take your time. About a year. Read. Discuss. Get planning permission. Look at spending twice that amount, not including gear. Take your time! I'm no expert, but that's my 2c, Geoff

Soundman202020 September 2008#19
That Wavebar stuff looks interesting, if the specs are actually true, but I'm not so sure I'd just glue layers of it to the back of typical "acoustic" tiling. In your case, you have roughly 45 square meters of ceiling, so covering all of that with even a single layer of Wavebar at 8 kg/M^2 is going to add at least 360 kg of weight to your ceiling. My guess is you'd need two or three layers to make it useful. Is it that flimsy framework REALLY designed to support the weight of 15 men hanging from it at once? Would you really want to sit under a ton and a half of that stuff, and hope that the thin aluminium framework and tiny screws can hold it? Like you, I'm in the design phase of my studio, but from devouring this site over recent weeks, I've figured out that I need some REAL mass in order to control sound going in and out, so I'm planning on having two or three layers of thick sheetrock firmly screwed or nailed into hefty 2 x 4's on my ceiling, with several inches of rockwool on top of that, and yet more layers of sheet rock firmly attached to the underside of the roof itself, between the rafters. That's probably a couple of tons of mass that I have to support, and even then I'm not sure that it is enough. I'm really skeptical that the simple frame used for typical acoustic tile installation can handle the type of mass that you need to block sound effectively. I'm not an expert on this, by any means (just learning like you), but after reading what the real experts have said on this site, I think you are being optimistic about your ceiling! Also, on your idea of floating your floor just using a timber frame: I was thinking of the same idea initially, but once again, after reading this site, I discovered that floating your floor is a really bad idea, 99% of the time. A floated floor needs to be REALLY massive to work. "Massive" as in several inches of concrete. Anything less, and you will actually probably make the situation worse than if you had not floated it! A timber frame floated floor will just create a beautiful resonant cavity under your studio, which will happily sing along with all your music, in it's own way, and in it’s own rather monotonous key… I'd suggest you read this: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8173 That's what convinced me to NOT try floating my floor. But I'm intrigued by that Wavebar stuff: The technical spec numbers are interesting, if true. What about the price? Any idea how much that costs per square meter?
