I would like to divide my basement room (24' long x 12' 9" wide x 7' 10" high)into a recording room section and a studio room section. I am not seeking to build anything permanent and my main idea was to use some sort of divider like an office panel setup which would reach all the way to the ceiling. I built these gobos for a similar purpose to yours. They are ugly, but are quite effective. I was pleasantly surprised at how well they worked... my goal was to record a drum kit without bleed from other instruments, so I put these gobos around the drums in a circle, and got almost no bleed whatsoever from the other instruments.... of course there were no amplifiers, but it was still better than I thought it would be. MJP wrote:
These gobos look like an excellent starting point. Do you think they would reduce the level of noise from one section of the room (ie amlpified guitar, or drums, or vocals) to the other IF I modified them to reach the ceiling and so that they were airtight from wall to wall? Well, there are several variables here. First of all, you won't make them airtight without permanent construction, or at the very least a squirting a ton of acoustic caulk everywhere. I certainly wouldn't want to have to clean that up to get a security deposit back or something. :wink: having said that, the closer you can get to those gobos floor-to-ceiling airtight the better from an isolation standpoint. But understand, microphones "hear" differently than ears do, so I don't think it's necessary. In my opinion, you will want to keep them somewhat portable so that you can modify their useage in the room. Carefully placed gobos, along with careful mic placement, will get you a long way in recording.... I use these gobos to put a circle around whatever I'm recording at the time, and then during the mix I put them on the sides of my mix position to create a Reflection-Free Zone. Very, very happy with their performance, though I still think they are ugly. 8)

OK - you could do this with a wall......... IF you attack the problem correctly. If you were to look in the book at Figure 1.1 - you'd see (on the lower left side of the picture) a single chair sitting in front of a wall.......... That wall happens to be hinged at the right hand side where you see the vertical trims...... the top of the wall is 8' high - and covers over the top of the door that closes into it. When both doors are open there is a 7' wide by 8' tall opening to bring gear through. I cannot give you all of the details for that - but will tell you that a key component for making a movable wall work is the bottom roller assembly. What we used is similar to this: http://www.servicecaster.com/Shepherd_C ... _baron.PDF - You want to allow for a very small settling point in the " at rest" location when the wall closes...... In addition - you want to get very VERY creative with your gasketing - remember that where air goes - so there goes noise - so make it work from a seal point of view - Like I said - I can't give you the details - because this is a proprietary system - but I can tell you that it can be made to work. I would also point out that I wouldn't want to have to use this door every day - it';s a bear - that's why the other operating door is a part of this assembly. Good luck, Rod
