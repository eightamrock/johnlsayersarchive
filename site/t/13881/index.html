Ro5 January 2010#3
The basics say: 1) make a sturdy monitor stand (regardless it's mounting system) which shouldn't flank too much. Decouple the monitor on the stand. 2) expand the baffle on the front at least twice the size of the exising baffle. Make it one smooth area so to speak. 3) prevent reflections coming from the back; use absorbers. If you take those basics I'd not make a closed chamber to put the speaker in, unless it's a super tight fit (meaning you'll only beef up the existing monitor). If the monitor is active (internal amp) then you'll need to expose the back to get rid of the heat. So it's a matter of choices I reckon.

Soundman20207 January 2010#14
But at first, the room sizes are wrong.
Oh brother. I gues you should have read the stickies a bit better! So you just made everyone wast their time here, thinking that your dimensions are accurate!
They are 2.93m width, 2.90m hight, 5.0m long.
The room has a square section. You will have major room mode issues there. In fact, the Bonello diagram for your room is really ugly. I've never seen a diagram that looks quite like that before! You have a major resonance issue at 64 Hz, another one at 131 Hz, and according to Bob Gold's calculator, your room rates "24) A worst case scenario calculated by RPG". I'd say that you need to fix the room dimensions before you even THINK about worrying about speaker layout! Also, based on your speaker geometry, it looks like your mix position is in the exact middle of the room. That's the worst possible place to put it.
we ... quite opposite to otheres here - choose the bigger room for the recording room and the smaller area as control room.
Actually, that isn't the opposite of what others do. Most people DO try to get the largest room possible for their LR, and a smaller room for the CR. It makes sense to do it that way, acoustically and practically.
The control room shape is rectangular because I think it can work and takes less space and it would be quite complicated an more expensive to build it nonrectangular.
Well, control rooms SHOULD be rectangular! Round, triangular and square, for example, would be really, really bad ideas. In fact, your is not rectangular: It is square. The height and width are practically the same. To fix that issue, you need to either lower the ceiling or build new side walls to make the room narrower. Either way, this gives you the opportunity to improve on the shape of the room, and also on isolation.
- monitors on the south wall, because the entry is an the north wall. And north and south wall are the smaller ones.
This is the wall that is 2.93 m long right now, correct?
- creating an RFZ on mixer positions by roomshape and absorbtion, roomshape means ceiling, too.
Correct. That is the normal way to do it for an RFZ room.
- apply strong symmetry, but only in front, (exactly spoken to 1.95m from frontwall, because of the window)
You need symmetry at least as far back as the mix position. 1.95m sounds like it is right at the edge of mix position (assuming that your new dimensions are accurate), so you should be OK with that, depending on how the final layout ends up.
- no absorbtion on the frontwall at least superchunks behind it (south-east and south-west-corner)
Why no absorption on the front wall? It might be necessary on the center panel between the speakers. Also, you cannot have both superchunks and soffits in the front. One or the other. The front panels of the soffits must be hard, solid, flat and dense, with nothing over them. Yes, you do need to put a lot of absorption in the cavity behind the soffits, but those are not superchunks.
