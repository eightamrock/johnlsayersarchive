Hi Joseph, and welcome! First, I'd suggest that you need to re-arrange your studio layout, since you will find it really hard to get good sound the way it is now. Two points here: 1) Your speakers are firing across the short axis of the room (2.79 m), but they should be firing down the long axis (3.17m) 2) Your mix position is almost exactly in the center of the room which is the worst possible place for it! Your head is in a whole bunch of nulls. You can fix both of these major issues by rotating your layout 90 degrees clockwise (to the right). That will have consequences (such as one of your bass traps will have to cover the window), but it will improve the acoustics considerably. Another comment about your speakers: You need to get them off the table and onto massive stands, and you need to orient them correctly to get the right geometry. Getting your speakers right is one of the major keys to getting your entire room right. Some other comments on things you mentioned:
I only have a carpet...
Get rid of it! Carpet is selective, it absorbs too much in the highs, and noting at all on the lows. Makes your room sound dull and boomy. The general consensus here is that the floor should be hard and reflective (such as concrete, ceramic, or laminate) and the ceiling should be soft. The easiest way to do that in your case would probably be to build a large "cloud" that you hang over your mix position, covering as much of the room as you can. Regarding your treatment layout plans, both like fine to me. Either one should work. You seem to have the right idea. Personally, I'd go with the superchunks, and also put some horizontally up in the wall/ceiling corners, since that room isn't very big and you are going to need lots of bass trapping. About the door: You might be able to build a "bass trap on wheels", so you can roll it into position over the door when you need to do critical listening. About the materials: If that stuff all you can get, then that's what you'll have to use! :) The "glass wool board" seems to be too thin, and I can't make out the specs for the "rock wool board", since the image is very low resolution, but it looks like it might do the job.

Soundman202014 July 2010#8
1. The distance of the cloud from ceiling how low should it be from the ceiling? I Hanged it 20 cm off the ceiling
That's OK, but you could probably reduce that a bit if you need the head room, or even angle it. Search the forum for ideas on how to design, build and angle clouds.
2. How much air gap should I leave between wall absorbers and the walls?
A few inches is good.
and does it matter if the absorber is vertical or horizontal?
Not as far as I know! Whatever gives you better coverage.
3. Do you think diffusion in my case should be planned initially in the first design or should I wait to test and hear the room and than decide?
It's a small room, so diffusers wont work too well. Diffusers need lots of room to be effective. If your room turns out to be too dead, then you can liven it up a bit by partially covering your bass traps (superchunks) with plastic, or you could build slot walls instead of plain absorption on the side walls. But yes, you are right: do the basic bass trapping and FRP treatment, hand the cloud, put up a couple of absorbers on the sides, then measure to see where you are and which way you still need to go.
