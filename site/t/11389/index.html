If you could post a SketchUp model of the room and the surrounding rooms, that would help. But basically for good isolation you need to build a decoupled and isolated "room within a room" system, with a two-leaf wall all around you, including the ceiling. That means a normal double stud wall (two sets of studs) with 2 or 3 layers of normal gypsum board (="sheetrock", ="plasterboard", ="drywall") only on the outsides of the studs, green glue between the layers of gypsum board, packed full of fiberglass or mineral wool insulation in the gap between the leaves, everything sealed up hermetically. This will give you the "mass-spring-mass" principle that is the best way to achieve good isolation. The gypsum board layers on the outside of your fist stud frame is the first "mass", the air gap plus insulation is the "spring", and the gypsum board layers on the outside of the other stud frame is your second "mass". It has to be sealed hermetically to work properly, but if you do it right, you can achieve excellent isolation. There are many variations, and many ways of accomplishing this. Use the "search" feature of this forum and you will find them all. You can probably do it without tearing down existing walls too much, and you can probably re-use some of the material you tear out to as part of the treatment. But use the "search" button here a lot. It is your new best friend!

BTW. Putting speakers in corners is usually a bad idea! For correct speaker placement, search this forum for something like "5.1 speaker placement" and you'll find out how to do it right. Since you will also be using a sub-woofer in your system, you'll need to consider that in your wall isolation design: low bass frequencies are the hardest of all to deal with. To stop those, you need good decoupling and lots of mass in your "mass-spring-mass" equation.
