Hi All, I'm a long-time lurker, first time poster. What a great and invaluable resource to the audio professional (and hobbyist) community. I've been waiting, dreaming, and planning for a couple of years for the right time to build a 'real' studio for my audio services business and now is the time! Problem #1: I cannot get a grip on Sketchup. I'm a reasonably intelligent person, but I just can't seem to get good enough to do anything on it. I'll keep trying, but please be patient with me and any help in that department is GREATLY appreciated. Here are the details of my room and goals: The studio will be in the basement of a single family home located in Douglassville, PA (just west of Philadelphia, PA USA). The basement is currently unfinished and has cinderblock walls and a concrete floor. There is an outside exit. The dimensions of the room are: 27 feet by 26.5 feet with a height of 8.5 feet from the floor to the bottom of the joists. The joists are 2"x10"s situated 16" OC There is a stairway in one corner that leads to the main floor of the house. The stairway is 13' long and 37.5" wide. There is a doorway on the same wall as the stairs that leads to another part of the basement that will not be used for the studio (it contains the furnace, water heater, etc). Part of the wall around that door is drywall, the rest is cinder block. There is a support pole in the middle of the room that runs under a steel support beam that runs under the joists. The distance from the floor to the bottom of the support beam is 7' 8". Next to the beam running under the joists are a couple of HVAC ducts and the main water waste line (pvc). The beam and the ducts run the entire length of the room and are about 6' wide total. I am currently working in this space as best I can, although it sounds pretty horrible (but I have to keep working to make money right?!!). My thought is that I'd like to divide it into a control room and a tracking room and then maybe add one or two iso booths. I record MANY different kinds of music from rock to rap, celtic to voice over so versitility is a must. I'm not terribly worried about sound getting out of the studio, just sound getting in. As it is right now, I can hear EVERYTHING that goes on in the main floor of the house and because the main water waste line runs right over my head anytime anyone uses water in the house it's terribly loud. I would like as much isolation as possible between the control room and the live room as well. I ordered Rod's book and plan on working directly from that (based on what everyone has said about it). My budget will ideally be under $10,000 but I'm not closed to getting a loan to get up to $15k. I don't have a lot of construction experience, but if I have directions to follow, I can be pretty handy and have readily available help. I also am planning on hiring out the framing process (I'm near Amish country and no frames like the Amish!) and possibly the muck and tape. I am ready to get moving on this ASAP. Finally, here are three pictures of the space as it is now. One is from one corner, then the opposite and then a detail of the ceiling obstacles. I hope this is enough information to get some thoughts, opinions, and help from this group because I so value the remarks of the people on this forum. Thanks! Ben

gullfo14 January 2007#8
you could put the CR in one corner and free up space for larger performance areas. if you put the CR under the hvac ducting you'll need to soffit that in. if you flip it and put the CR near the electrical box, you lose some head room in the live room... but probably not have as much work for soffiting... eitherway access to the live areas would be from the stairs or outside door. since you do a lot of post production, I'd tend to err on the side of a better control room with more head space....
