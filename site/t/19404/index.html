Hi all I have started a conversion of our garage into a project studio. Foolishly i began without really doing too much research into acoustics (apart from my own very basic knowledge) and as a result i now have a low ceiling (230cm or 7.5 feet), which could have been improved with a bit of extra digging while we were doing the concrete floor. Ummmmm... I really hat to say this, but those sure look like inner-leaf walls to me! :shock: The outside view of the studio shows that you most certainly have an outer leaf there, which connects to a brick wall on the left, and the second photo shows that you have drywall (plasterboard) facing towards the room, so you also have an inner-leaf... so you are out of walls already! If you add any more walls, you will have 3-leaf or 4-leaf system, with the accompanying low frequency isolation problems always resulting from such walls. So if you do indeed want to add internal walls, then that implies that you will need to disassemble some of what you have done already. This is how studio isolation is supposed to be done:
Image not preserved: msm-2-leaf-WallChunk-conventional-not-inside-out-2.png
In other words, you have your outer-leaf framing with mass on ONLY the outside of that, facing the real world, then you have an air gap, then you have framing for each of your internal rooms with mass on only one side of each frame. If that is not what you are planning to do, then you are not doing it correctly for good isolation. I would suggest that you first fix that before moving forwards. Fortunately, you are not too far advanced to do it right. - Stuart -
