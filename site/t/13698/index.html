Alex, you are mistaken - oversizing an hvac unit does not make it more efficient (never mind much more efficient) - it makes it MUCH LESS efficient. Oversized HVAC systems have short run cycles - which means you are paying more for electrical costs because you are starting the compressor more often (this is the time of the greatest load on the compressor) you want long run times to make the costs as efficient as possible. BUT - cost aside - there is another piece of this that oversized equipment screws you up on - and that is humidity levels. Because of the fact that you have short run times - you do not have proper dehumidification within the structure - again you need long run cycles to make the systems work properly. Systems should always be sized properly - and you also have to take into account what the systems provides in sensible versus latent cooling to determine which system to install - and for that you need to calculate the proper use of the spaces. Both fresh air and people release humidity into the air - and those humidity sources have to be taken into account. And (as far as the people go) they release much more humidity if they are playing their asses off than if they are sitting at a desk mixing. Sincerely, Rod
