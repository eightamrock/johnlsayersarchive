Eric_Desart26 September 2009#4
andymel wrote:
I wonder whether it is used for calculating studio behavior too.
It doesn't matter what one uses this software for. Such software calculates values either per octave, or per 1/3 octave. The difference between whatever purpose is in using a suitable weighting curve, representing the typical spectral composition of the sound source. The software is mainly a calculator for the physics (per octave of 1/3 octave band), the rest is simple stuff (calculating already known TL values per band in function of single number ratings as per standards or specific custom spectra) However this is more complicated than it seems. There are lots of models and comparative investigations show that different models can be more accurate in one case and others in other cases. Such articles are typically in Pro Journals (as JASA or Acoustica or .....) and are rarely free on the net (Copyright protected, but you can, if recent enough, buy them on-line) This is not typically free software (from a DIY point of view even rather expensive). One is for example: http://www.insul.co.nz/ And the EN 12354 standard gives a standardized calculation method including flanking effects. (EN stands for European Norm). Several organizations have transformed this standard in commercial software as well. Your example BASTIAN is only one of them (also based on this standard). http://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=w ... a8u4M63X3w Such standards aren't on the net either (this one is thick as a book and expensive).
