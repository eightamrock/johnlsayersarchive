dbluefield11 May 2004#3
Excellent Post. I wish everyone was as detailed. :D Not to discourage you, however, $600 for achieving both isolation and room acoustics make building walls, permanent bass trap and a decoupled room etc impracticable, not to mention you don't want to hurt the floor. PLus your considering re-doing the pannelled walls with gypsum....hmmm...Let's trim the dinner plate down a bit. You need some priorities. As for the barking Dog, I don't know what to say other than record when he's not barking. The isolation you would need to make it a non-issue is well beyond the budget of $600 IMO. So what can you do? I would buy about $200-400 of OC 703 and make modular 2'x4"6" deep 703/burlap panels, using 1/4" plywood, 2x2 corner braces, and burlap. Braced in the back with cabinet style hangers. Hang them where they are needed, basically in picture fashion around the room. They will help tame many issues , and you can vary the acoustics in both rooms by adding or removing them. Best of all you can take them with you. Also why not build some portable polycylindrical diffusers? Both solutions and project descriptions can be found here --courtesy of the late Malcom Chisholm: http://pages.ripco.net/~chisholm/rsdp/INDEX.HTM specifically Here: http://pages.ripco.net/~chisholm/rsdp/FUZPOLY.TXT The poly's are easy to make as well; they don't have to be a full 4x8 feet; they can be built into a mobile frame and placed in corners; and in Malcolms' words they do two things - absorb low end and disperse highs which sounds nice; and finally nobody believes they work; I know better. :lol: Best, Dave

dbluefield12 May 2004#9
Picture says a thousand words........ stuff was confusing to me at first as well... As for the 703 boxes -- notice I simply made a frame from plywood and 2x2's -- no hardware cloth as Malcolm reccomended -- this was my innovation, seeing as you really dont need the boxes any stronger than what is needed for hanging them on the wall, plus the burlap looks sharp stretched over it and it's easy! Which I like. Then I put the burlap down(burlap is dirt cheap - there are other alternatives), layed the frame upon it, layed the 703 inside (in this case I was making a couple of boxes 3' wide), stapled the burlap to the frame, and added two slats of 1x4's for support and hanging the box on the wall cabinet style --lthe top 1x4 cut at a 45 to coupled with it's mate mounted on the wall. Maybe you'll want some gobos too. In the first picture you can see I used 2x4's for them, hardware cloth and R19. Hardware cloth for stabilty and frame rigidness -- cause they roll on the floor. :lol: Ceiling cloud? sure why not -- make it even lighter than the wall panels...maybe a light grid system with just 2" of 2x4 703 dropped in, spaced from the ceiling. You have to innovate for that one. See the bowed masonite in the corners? That's the polycylindrical diffuser ala Chisholm -- you could bow them into a plywood frame per Malcolm's directions, and hang the frame in the corners. Look Mom --a portable bass trap that disperse highs -- all for the price of a little wood and a sheet of Masonite! 8) Best, Dave

dbluefield24 May 2004#20
This isn't drawn to scale, of course. It's about 2:30 am, and my faculties have deserted me. How far off am I? What did I miss?
Looks like your spot on. :D By the way I made mine from (3) panels of 2" 703 for a box roughly 6" deep. But there have been plenty of posts suggesting that 2" is quite useful as well, particularily when spaced off the wall. In either case Owens' Cornings' specs show that 4" of 703 absorbs quite uniformly down to 125Hz. 4"703 Board on wall: 125 250 500 1k 2k 4k Sabins 0.84 1.24 1.24 1.08 1.00 0.97 Malcolm apparently talked with Owens and estimated that 6" would yield: Sabins 1.19 1.21 1.13 1.05 1.04 1.04 for the same frquencies. I would perhaps start with a couple 2" panels? - then build deeper boxes later perhaps. the specs for 2" 703 on the wall are: 0.22 0.82 1.21 1.10 1.02 1.05 for the above freqs. You can see that 4" has marked improvement in absorption torwards the low end; and 6" makes a pretty darn good broad band absorber. Tech specs aside, Malcolm said they work - so I went with 6". :lol: The only other diffrence I saw is minor - I used 1x4's for the back hangers - but 2x4's would space the panel a little farther off the wall - dealers choice. :lol: Best, Dave
