Hi Donal, and welcome! A couple of comments:
so i need to soundproof very well only one side of the studio.
Unfortunately, it doesn't work like that. If sound can get out, then it is out. You cannot tell it which way to go once it gets out. While higher frequencies are somewhat directional, they can still bounce off things around your building, such as fences, other buildings, cars, trees, etc. And low frequencies will spread out all ways at once, regardless of what you do So just isolating one wall is not going to help: You need to isolate all of them, INCLUDING the roof, in order to not bother the nice ladies next door :) That's your first piece of bad news! Any isolation treatment you do, has to cover the entire structure. Just treating part of your room is like just building half a dam across half the river... You have to build all of it in order for it to work at all. If not, then you are wasting your time and money.
so i think a triple glazed pvc door here might be the best plan.
That would probably be a mistake. Triple glazing = three leaves = BAD! Contrary to popular belief (and contrary even to intuition!) any time you have three "leaves" in a wall, you actually make it WORSE at stopping low frequency sound than if it only had two leaves. Each pane of glass is one leaf. Three panes = three leaves = poor isolation in the low frequencies, such as kick drum, toms, bass guitar, etc. What you want in a wall (or a window) is just TWO leaves. Never three, never four, never more, never one. Two and only two leaves is the best way to stop sound at low cost (unless you want to spend lots of money on exotic materials like thick sheets of solid lead, for example). 2-leaf construction is the best and cheapest way to get really good isolation, if you build it right.
where i can have tons of help to build the type of resonators/dispersers /desks and fittings ...
Resonators and diffusors wont help at all to isolate your room: they might help to treat it, but not to isolate it. Isolation and treatment are two entirely different things.
The B&!!!**s on the other side are attached to the house,
When you say "attached", do you mean that their house is physically connected to your house? The same physical wall / roof / floor? I hope that's not what you meant.... Can you clarify this point, please? - Stuart -
