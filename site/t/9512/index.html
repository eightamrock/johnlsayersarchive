Jack Russell wrote:
I'm also new to noise ratings in sones rather than dba. This one's rated at 0.7 -- I know they're different animals but is that comparable to (what?) 28 to 30 dba?
OK - seeing as DB ratings are in sound pressure levels- but have really little to do with how loud sounds appear to be to a human being....... Phons were developed to create an equation relating to the volume perceived by the human ear. Thus - A tone is "X" phons if it sounds as loud as a 1000 Hertz sine wave of "X" decibles or dB. 40 phons equals 40dB at 1000 Hz. Phons are logrithmic and thus 50 phons are twice as loud as 40 phons. Sones (on the other hand) are a method of taking this from the log to linear - which is much easier for most people to understand. The calculation is "a tone of x phons is 2(x-40)/10 sones. So one sone is 1000 Hz @ 40dB 2 sones are 1000 Hz @ 50 dB 3 sones are 1000 Hz @ 60 dB 4 sones are 1000 Hz @ 70 dB or the equivilent perceived volumes for different frequencies. To adjust for this - you have to use the Fletcher/Munson Curves. Just so you understand - on the Fletcher/Munson curves - a 60Hz signal @ 69dB sounds slightly less loud than a 1000 Hz signal @ 40dB. So if you were buying a piece of equipment with a frequency range of 60Hz and it measured (with your radio shack meter) 60dB - then its sone rating would be around .97 sones. And, if the equipment was running in the frequency range of 5K @ 40 dB (measured) then it would be almost 50 phons and would be roughly 1.8 sones. So all if all 3 frequencies were measure @ 40dB - the 1K signal would be the base - then the 60 hZ signal is almost at the threshold of a human's ability to hear - and the 5 k signal would seem almost tqice as loud. I hope that helped,.......... Oh - one other thing - that .7 sones is measured @ 5' from the unit - - @10' you should not be able to really hear it witout fighting to - and with masking probably not at all. Rod

They claim a 3dB reduction is a cutting in 1/2 of the perceived violume - and this is a lie. 3dB is a doubling of wattage - 6 db is a doubling of sound pressure - 10 dB is a doubling of perceived amplitude (on a weighted scale). 3. What is the most absorbtive acoutical lining material (and if fiberglass, do I need filters? Is basic duct board good enough?)
Polymer coated duct lining is recommended so as to not allow fibers to become loose over time.
5. I'm worried about transmitting fan vibration noise through metal ducts in short runs. Is it enough to buffer/warp with neoprene or foam wherever ducts meet walls or ceiling brackets?
Nope - if you use metal ducts - then use fully llined ducts. Where the ducts meet walls - nothing special will be required if you use duct board - and I would recommend a canvas boot just before the wall to decouple the transmitting duct from the receiving duct. There is almost no fan noise with this duct - I would not be overly concerned with fan noise itself - once again - ambient noise is my biggest concern here. Rod
