drthump7010 February 2009#13
Hey Jon, Things are starting to take shape there! The area Stuart is refering to is the front of your control room where your speakers are mounted. Those are called soffits. Typically you want the are above and around your speaker to be wood, and the bottom would be cloth. The area behind there should have rockwool and acoustic hangers. Therefore, you need to have another wall in front of those soffits. That wall would make up your inner leaf. Your first design is actually more the way it should be. As far as how to set up the soffits, you want to create an equilateral triangle between yourself and the speakers. Therefore each angle of the triangle should be 60 degrees. each soffit makes up half of that. Therefore you want each soffit to be 30 degrees in from the front wall. Here is a great link that will explain soffits in great detail. viewtopic.php?f=1&t=66 And lastly stuntbutt is correct. In order to achieve isolation from your upstairs, you will need to build a ceiling that is decoupled from the upstairs floor joists. For examples, see pages 63-77 in Rod's book. Hope this helps a bit. Bryon

Soundman202011 February 2009#18
...my plan for the ceiling of the control room....am I on the right track?
Sort of, but not quite! You have your inner-leaf ceiling joists (on the layer called "ceiling) directly in contact with your outer-leaf ceiling joists (those big thick ones, on the layer called "joists"). You need to drop the height of your inner-leaf walls a few inches and rest your inner-leaf joists on top of the inner-leaf walls, in order to decouple the leaves. Also, if you run your inner-leaf joists in the same direction as the outer-leaf joists but in between them, (parallel to them, instead of perpendicular as you have them currently), then you might be able to save a little more height. Alternatively, build your ceiling inside-out (sheetrock on top of the inner-leaf joists, instead of underneath it): That will also gain you several inches of ceiling height, since you can install acoustic treatment between the inner-leaf joists like that. - Stuart -

Soundman202012 February 2009#22
Framing with new 2x6 joists, I lose additional space and it whittles it down to 8' 5 3/8".
Unless you build your ceiling "inside-out".... (I seem to be repeating that a lot over the last few days, on several threads! All of a sudden I sound like a commercial for inside-out construction!) Seriously, if you build it inside out, then the drywall goes on TOP of the joists, but underneath them, so you have the entire space between the joists for acoustic treatment. You can get the sheetrock up real close to the "outer" joists, probably just as close as with RC. I don't think you need to lose any ceiling height at all, if you do it inside-out.... - Stuart -
