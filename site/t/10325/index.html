Ok, here goes after quite some time of taking in information from this site and reading Rod’s book I feel I’m ready to start on the studio project. The overview of the project is: Location: Norfolk, England Budget: £7500-10,000 The construction will take place in an attached garage 24’ x 15’ x 9.5’ (internal) the internal wall is a typical 9” solid wall construction circa 1890’s with 1” rendering the external walls are typical cavity insulated block work circa last year (although the wife would point out that construction started quite some time before that!!!) There is a room above which is where a problem will arise, but I’ll discuss this later. Floor/ceiling joists are 11”. There is a double garage door to the east elevation (short wall dimension) and a single door in the west elevation in the corner of the south wall. Floor is 6” concrete pad and there is a further 4” clearance to build up the flooring. The use would be mainly for recording bands, playing at typical rock volumes and solo work. My nearest neighbour is 100’ away, we live on a busy road which produces quite a bit of traffic noise. I would like to construct a control room and tracking room and will block/brick up the existing garage door and replace with single door and window. But here is where the compromise begins. The construction should be capable of being converted to a flat at a later date. Yes I’m sure this will compromise things but I’ll post a couple of my ideas and maybe you could pull them to pieces etc. This means that there should be provision for living space, bedroom and bathroom. Due to drainage being at the rear corner near to the existing single door I feel this will limit the design somewhat. Control room would be double wall construction but i'm thinking of framing out the restof the walls with iso bars rather than full room within a room construction. Have not decided on HVAC yet. Back to the ceiling, this is 11” joists with a single layer of 12.5mm plasterboard filled with 8” Rockwool. Here’s where things get interesting. I have installed under floor heating in the bedroom above, unfortunately because we Brits are new to this the supplier would only guarantee the equipment would work with a max 20mm covering. I sought advice from the states who told me it would be no problem to lay a 12.5mm sub floor and then the 20mm hardwood on top of this. Unfortunately I erred on the side of caution and layed the hardwood directly onto the joists. I also used metal heat spreader plates rather than a dry sand cement fill (again on advice from supplier). I now have a VERY warm room but with poor sound isolation from the garage below (yes I know but I wasn’t going to build a studio at that point). Sorry USA I should have listened!!! There is a possibility of altering the floor construction at a later date but at the moment the wife would definitely cut a certain part of my body off if I even suggested it. Could I work on the underside of the floor now and then alter things later? Reading Rods book it would indicate that the existing ceiling should be removed and isolation bars fitted directly to the joists. gullfo21 March 2008#6
www.pac-intl.com - RSIC but also www.kineticsnoise.com and www.masonindustries.com have isolation products. you'll want to attach the isolation mount to the ceiling (walls etc) as recommended by the manufacturer, as i don't believe the 2x2 would be the right mounting. since you have limits on space, it might be best to make the walls squared and use treatments to create the angles. generally speaking its easier to remove the treatment walls later and be left with standard room shapes if you need to be able to resell the space. plus you might be able to take the treatment materials with you...
