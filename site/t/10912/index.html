AVare18 July 2008#5
thenewforce wrote:
At what point does it become beneficial to begin incorporating diffusion on the back wall.
10 feet between the rear wall and the listening position.
Does it depend on length or volume of room?
Yes to both.
Also, with small rooms, is it inadvisable to have the side walls treated to be angled and diffusive?
Yes. the small room is to small for splaying to to help redirect initial reflections in the first 15 milliseconds. Diffusion will only help on higher frequencies, not full range.
Angled slat absorber designs seem that they would have some benefit even for narrow rooms. At what width/volume should these also be traded for strictly absorbtive sides?
When the 15 millisecond/ 10 dB down criteria can be achieved with splaying and diffusion.
The dimensions of the room I'm in now are 100"H x 126"W 136"D.
Use absorption only. The room is too small for diffusion and reflection redirecting to be of value.
Do surface materials matter? It seems uncommon now, but my interior walls and ceiling are all lathe and plaster and the floor is the painted subfloor(?) (1x3's running the length of the house).
Yes. Do reverb time calculations and use the EBU/ITU spec for reverb time design criteria. Andre
