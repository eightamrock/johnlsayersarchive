I was told that I could remove ONE of the beams AFTER installing THREE further braces above the proposed new ceiling line. Which is what I did. I then had a local builder come and check things again before I removed the beam. I was at that point advised to add some metal straps from the plate line to the inside wall. These were 1/4" x 1" x 3' long L shaped metal straps, these were added to comply with NEW building code in the UK. That effectively meant that the roof could not move or splay because the new beams were installed and the plate was strapped to the inside walls, remember this is a double skinned construction anyway. So in extreme environmental conditions, heavy snow, the load would be taken by the added beams and the inside as well as the outside skin of the construction. I have included a photo of the new beams, the strapping is covered by the plaster boarding. It's not too clear, but I have not actually attached any of the ceiling construction timbers to the roof timbers, I was advised not to add any further weight to the roofing timbers. However, I was advised there was ample strength in the concrete block construction to support any framing with upto FOUR layers of 0.5" plaster board without further work to the shell of the building. That is why I was happy to continue with the construction. I hope that clears things up on the ceiling and roof points.
