There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post (click here). Actually, several people, who are experts on this forum, will most likely not reply if you don't do what is written in that post. Many others who are very helpful, will most likely not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. I'm also wondering why you have the mix position at almost the worst possible location in the room: It looks to be very close to the 50% position. Also, you seem to have the space and rough layout to soffit-mount your speakers, but they are not shown as being soffit mounted. Is that a mistake, or are you going to soffit mount them? If you do NOT plan to soffit mount them, then it looks like the splay angle on your inner-leaf front walls could be increased to get a much better RFZ around the mix position, especially if you correct the location of the mix position to get it out of the 50% null.
the external walls are 3ft of concrete and brick the roof is sheet metal with that thin insulation attached to foil --- That said I'd like it to future proof. (ie. if a hostile neighbour moved in next door they would have no reason to complain about noise) --- rock and experimental music its often loud ---
So you need good isolation. So your walls are not a problem, but your roof is a major problem. I guess that's where you will be focusing all your efforts?
The music I make/record is rock and experimental music its often loud and sometimes really quiet
So isolation is going to be an issue, for sure, which means that the roof is going to be even more of a problem!
Double Stud with 2 layers of 13mm or 16mm firecheck plasterboard space 200mm apart leaving enough room for 175mm of glasswool
Why double stud? You already have a really good outer leaf. You only need an inner leaf. Also, forget the 13mm drywall: too thin.
I am consiering resilent sound clips for walls
Why? You don't need them. You already said you are going to decouple your inner leaf with a new stud wall, so why do you also want to use RSIC?
I plan to attach insulation and two layers of plasterboard via resilent sound spring system to the existing joists and then have a decoupled inner ceiling with 2 layers of 13mm plasterboard, insulation and green glue. probably no resilent channel on the internal ceiling
That sounds like you are planning to do a three-leaf ceiling! :shock: But you don't seem to have anywhere near enough mass and air space in there to compensate for the triple-leaf effect. And certainly not with just 13mm drywall. You might want to re-think that plan! - Stuart -
