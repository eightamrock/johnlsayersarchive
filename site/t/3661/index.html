After making the ceiling and floor the height may come down to 3,4 m. I wonder whether ther would be any real audible advantage to raise the ceiling one meter higher to 4,5 m or whether it will not be worth the big work and expense. What do you think ? Thank you for your reply. knightfly2 May 2005#4
Although you would want less absorption in a room for acoustic instruments, this drawing shows the basics of isolation - it's true in all directions, for all barriers between you and the rest of the world - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download.php?id=4001 Don't buy insulated glass though; what you want is two SINGLE panes, as thick as you can afford (up to around 1/3 the thickness of your wall panel total) and spaced as far apart as you can get them (near the inner and outer wall surfaces) and SEALED - you want to use acoustic-rated caulk, check the building materials forum for types and sources... Steve

Ivo, one substitude for what the guys across the puddle call "Acoustic caulk" you can get in the area where you live will be Mapei Mapeflex GB1 (GB1 is the name in Austria). This is Butyl rubber which has high internal damping. www.mapei.com.
