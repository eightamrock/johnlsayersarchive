Soundman202015 January 2009#5
There had to be some factor contributing to the lack of sound transmission that has less to do with decoupled walls, acoustic caulk, and mass-loaded vinyl than it does solid construction techniques.
I wish that were true, but it simply isn't: Like rod said, you can't beat the laws of physics.
Again, what I am trying to do here is make some improvement on the standard construction that will begin to provide some sound insulation from my kids sleeping 20' above me with carpeting, sub-floor, insulation,sheetrock, 10' of air, hardwood, subfloor,and more sheetrock in between. Surely, some insulation is better than none.
Insulation does not stop sound. Isolation stops sound. Urban legend has it that sticking egg crates and packing foam on your walls will stop the sound going through, but that isn't true either. All it will achieve is to make your room sound bad. The sound will still go through, just like it always did. It is a common misunderstanding that insulation stops sound. It does not. It can help when it is part of a system, but by itself it does not work. You cannot stop sound with insulation (unless you want to install mineral wool two dozen feet thick on your walls and ceiling!!). You can only stop sound with isolation. Isolation means two things: Mass and decoupling.
but more to potentially solicit some additional ideas that I have not thought of already. So instead of criticizing me or my contractor, how about giving me some constructive and actionable advice while respecting my given circumstances and constraints?
Well, if you REALLY want to spend lots of money for just a little bit of return, then you could beef up the underside of your ceiling with a couple of layers of sheetrock, put resilient channel (not hat channel) across your studs and joists, put lots of rock wool between your studs and joists, and then screw a couple of layers of sheetrock to the resilient channel, with Green Glue between the layers of sheetrock, and staggered joints. Seal everything with lots of caulk. It will cost you several hundred dollars in materials, plus your contractor's labor, and the cost of the structural engineer to check if your floor can take the weight, and it will gain you a few dB of isolation, but probably nowhere near enough that your kids can carry on sleeping while you play the drums.
Do you really believe that no insulation is better than 6-8" of fiberglass?
"6-8" of fiberglass" will attenuate some of the highs a bit, but will have very little effect on the lows. Once again, you can't stop sound with insulation. If you do not believe me, nail a six inch thickness of fiberglass across the doorway to the room where you play drums now, and stand outside while somebody else hammers away, full bore. Can you hear the drums still? Yes you can. Just a bit softer than inside the room, but still very loudly. And the kick and floor tom will be hardly affected at all. Did the insulation stop the sound? No. Insulation does not stop sound. Isolation does. Isolation implies mass and decoupling.
I am aware that the lower frequencies will always be tough to stop due more to a person's ability to feel these frequencies more than hear them as they travel though a solid surface.
Right awareness, wrong reason!!! :) the reason why lower frequencies are harder to stop is because they have very long wavelengths, not because you can feel them. Long wavelengths require greater mass.
I guess my main question is is there any material that can be added to this construction that will improve rock/insulation/rock, that is at all worthwhile?
Rod already gave you the answer to that: simple answer? No, there is nothing you can add, except oodles of mass, such as a 10 inch concrete slab. There is no such thing as a "magic material" that you can apply to your room to isolate it for drums. There IS an entire treatment that you can apply, which rod outlined, but despite the wonderful claims of manufacturers, there simply isn't any magic stuff that you can nail to the roof. You cannot bypass the laws of physics.
will I get materially better sound dampening results by replacing the 2" rigid foam boards with something like the Audimute 2 mm rubber soundproofing insulation or similar vinyl product?
Don't waste your money on things like mass loaded vinyl and rubber matting. You need mass, and these will give you mass, but sheetrock is much cheaper for the same amount of mass! Sound doesn't care much if you spennt hundreds of dollars to put the mass there, or thousands. Mass is mass, and you can get it cheaper from sheetock than any other common building material. You also need decoupling, and none of these will give you that. Resilient channel will give you decoupling, but not enough on its own to make a huge difference. Decoupling, plus mass, plus insulation will make a bigger difference. But in order to isolate a drum kit well, you need much more mass, and much more decoupling than you are willing to do. Like Rod said: you can spend a lot of money here, but unless you do it completely right (which you said you don't want to do) then your results will be mediocre at best. Anyone who tells you otherwise does not understand the issue. And if you DO decide to do it properly, then your very best first step is to buy Rod's book. In it, he shows how to do it properly. Anything less than that just will not get the results you are hoping for. - Stuart -
