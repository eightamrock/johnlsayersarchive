Soundman202010 July 2012#4
1.) Put some type of dampening material/pad (neoprene?) on the edges of the 2x4's where the drywall will be screwed 2.) screwed the first piece of drywall to the dampening material/pads,
Unfortunately, that won't work. The screws short-circuit the neoprene, negating the effect. Each screw has to go through the neoprene into the stud, so it creates a directly coupled flanking path that transmits sound and vibration, bypassing the rubber. But what you can do, is to put Resilient Channel ("RC") across those studs, and attach the drywall to that. That's what RC is designed to do: it decouples the drywall from the studs, acoustically, and doesn't use up much space. Another alternative would be to use RSIC clips along with ordinary hat channel. That will work about the same as RC, or maybe even better. Either of those would be your best solution, if you can't afford the space for a proper second stud wall. But you didn't answer my other question about how your ceiling is going to work: of there is no stud framing on that side of the room, then how are you going to support the new ceiling joists? You could have the running lengthways down the room, I guess, but that means they have to span more than 14 feet, so they'll have to be pretty large: maybe 2x8 or even 2x10 (you'd have to calculate that). Is there enough ceiling height to fit in such large joists? Maybe some photos of that area would help to understand what you are dealing with. - Stuart -
