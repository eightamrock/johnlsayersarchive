Berre10 January 2009#1
Hi All, Like many of you, I am going to start building my own little studio in my garden shed. As a long-time lurker I have gained a tremendous amount of information already from this fantastic forum. So, for starters some background info. As already said I plan to build my studio in a small building in my backyard. Actually, the building is being built as we speak, well almost (depending on the weather)... I live in a fairly quite suburbian area, close to Leuven in Belgium. This means that I really need all the acoustical isolation I can get. But I think I have covered that fairly good already. BTW, I should maybe also mention that I am an electro-acoustician during the day, so I have quite some background already... As I am a keyboard player and mixing engineer by night and in the weekends, I intend to use the studio mainly for mixing, rehearsing and teaching (in that order). However, I also wanted a small recording booth to do overdubs and also to let my kids practice their instruments. Now, based on all the knowledge I gained here and elsewhere, I was going for a design as shown in the first picture (studio_straight.jpg). Reasons for this design where: - a rectangular room with good room ratios (based on a calculation spreadsheet I cannot recall the name of), built from drywall walls (metalstuds with two layers of 12mm drywall (Gyproc)). This would give me the isolation I want. - Inside the control room I would build the acoustical treatment aiming for as much symmetry as possible. Since this would be a separate construction, I can use it for lots of absorption, and add some diffusion where and when necessary... - no baffled speakers, since at the time I didn't want to constrain my design to one certain type of monitors. Placing them freestanding in the room would give me more freedom. However, this leads to quite some 'lost' space. I know that it is used for good acoutic treatment, but still... Since my room is already so small I wanted to find a design that gave me a good room within the boundaries of the building, while still providing me a all the space I can get. That's how yesterday I came up with this alternative design I wanted to discuss with you guys (see studio_angled.jpg). While I certainely can see that the symmetry (outside the 'acoustical' walls) is far from ideal, I think I have some advantages I cannot simply forget. - all speakers are now baffled - the listener-speaker distance is larger (1.6m vs 1.4m) - the booth is considerably bigger, as is the control room itself - and not unimportantly, I now have the opportunity of adding a window for some daylight, opposite to the window between control room and recording booth. This even improves the acoustical symmetry, when looking inside the 'acoustical' boundaries only... I hope this all makes a bit sense... Anyway, the thing I feel most uncertain about is the loss of symmetry in the control room outside the acoustical treatment. Since the build of the shed starts very soon (next Monday, if it stops freezing (currently it is -10degC)), I would like to know your ideas ASAP to know whether I should/can add the extra window in the control room or not. Other things like building dimensions and position of the door cannot be changed anymore, but the window might still be possible... So please help me out on this one... I really am in doubt what way to proceed. Many, many thanks in advance... Kind regards, Bert NOTE: I know the drawing of the new design is far from complete, but I think already sufficient to give some idea of what I'm talking about. Thanks for your patience...

Bert - there is a third option. http://www.grammy.com/Recording_Academy ... Engineers/ These guys had a conference on the layout of 5:1 and decided that the rear speakers should be further behind you so instead of being @ 110 degrees they should be @ 135 degrees. This configuration is narrower than the 110 so you can have your left right speakers wider. This studio has a 135 degree rear speaker system http://www.johnlsayers.com/Pages/Oasis.htm cheers john
