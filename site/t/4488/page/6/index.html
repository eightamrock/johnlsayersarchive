len-morgan26 April 2006#127
And a few more shots of the back wall MW being mounted. I had a lot of trouble getting something that would hold the MW up to the wall. Impalling clips didn't hold it well enough (it REALLY needs to stay put since this wall will be totally covered with cloth and if one of those rear batts comes loose and leans against the bass hangers, I'll be screwed). The basic idea I used was: 1) Drill and mount SHORT anchors. They were only long enough to go through one layer of the 5/8" drywall so my isolation wouldn't be compromised. 2) Screw a tie-wrap (the kind with a screw hole attachment point) into the anchor. These were just long enough to go through the 4" MW and leave me a little tail to connect to. 3) Tape a stiff wire to the end of the tie wrap to help push it through the MW. 4) Push the two wires/tie wraps through the panel from the back. The panels were 2' x 4' and I mounted them vertically. The mounts are one foot over and one foot in from the top and bottom. 5) Find some sort of stiff "washer" and cut a small slit in the middle of it. I first tried cutting the plastic "bubble" from the front of various packaging I had laying around (ok, garbage I hadn't thrown away yet). Next I tried yogurt lids (these worked really well but they were a little large), and finally I settled on some large felt pads that were designed to go on the bottoms of chair and table legs so they won't scratch the floor. 6) I then removed the wire guide from the ends of the tie wraps and pushed the ends through the slots in the "washers" from step 5. 7) An additional tie wrap is slid over the end of the tie wrap that is sticking through the MW and it's slid down tight against the washer and MW to hold it tight against the wall. 8) Trim the ends off the tie wraps and move on the the next panel. len-morgan26 April 2006#128
Because of poor planning in the beginning (and not asking the right questions at the right time), my ceiling vents came out right in the middle of the new wall so I had to make a little soffit along the top to let the air out. I fashioned a couple of elbows out of duct board to make the 90 degree transition. As the pictures show, I first made a little paper version of what I wanted the finished product to look like. I cut and taped and folded until everything looked right. The next step was to measure the actual sizes I'd need and write these on the model I'd built. Then I cut it apart where the tape was and unfolded it. This gave me my cut and fold pattern for the duct board. After that, it was pretty easy to just transfer the measurements to the duct board and make my cuts and folds.

len-morgan1 May 2006#137
It's not really that big a deal (my mid-life career change from electronic engineer and computer programmer to recording studio operator not withstanding). :-) It happened when I was 12. I got an infection (which my mother swore till the day she died was from a small cut I got while riding a minibike) which almost killed me. I was in a coma for a couple of weeks and was running a fever of 108! The doctors told my parents that if I ever did come out of the coma, I'd be a vegetable because the fever was so high. Well, one of the complications of the disease was my body started throwing out blood clots. I had one in my liver, ankle, and heart. The doctor's think one broke loose and went up into my head. It could have caused a stroke, logded in the artery that feeds an eye (thus blinding my in one eye), or in my ear which is what appears to have happened. I was told by my doctors that at first they had no idea what I had and compiled a two page list of things it could be. My mother happened to mention that they had found I had a minor heart valve leak and all of the sudden that narrowed it down to only a couple of things. The figured out what it was and started treatment with a new (at the time) antibiotic. They did tell my parents that no one had survived this treatment (there were only 10 reported cases prior to mine) but they did know that doing nothing would kill me for sure. The problem with the treatment was that the antibiotic turned toxic after a certain amount of time. The first few people they tried it on died because the cure killed them. The next few they stopped early and the disease killed them. They were trying to find out the right dose that would kill the disease and not the patient. I was the experiment at 6 weeks and two days. Apparently, it worked. :-) I only found out I couldn't hear in my right ear when I was trying listen to my little TV with an earphone (before headphones), and when I switched ears, I couldn't hear it any more. The blood clot thing was the best they could come up with. I can still sort of hear stereo but probably not the way you do. But I have to move my head from side to side and listen to the difference as I do. I'll cope, I'm sure, and if push comes to shove, I can always have a "guest engineer" sit in and finish of the stereo image. When you think about it, hearing stereo reproduced in a control room is sort of an auditory illusion since you have some signal coming out of both speakers which is not what would be happening if you had the band sitting in front of you. I've often wondered if the color that pops into my head when I hear something is green is the same color YOU see in your head? Why is green green? Because somebody pointed to something and said this is green. Human perception is a magical thing that allows us to all agree on something like "red" or "green" and yet really never know if we are all "seeing" the same thing. :-) len
