len-morgan29 June 2006#177
AndreasB wrote:
what's the real purpose of the tables (except beeing tables)? Are you hiding your panels/cables in there or what?
The short answer is yes. :-) I wanted some place to set down drinks, sheet music, guitar picks, etc. I wouldn't want a new customer to try and set them down on the black strip between the rooms because they would have to put their hand through the glass to do that. ':shock:' By making the colors very different and having molding around the edges to highlight even more, I shouldn't have a problem. By having a fairly generous amount of table space, I shouldn't have to add "moveable" furniture to the rooms. Since the tables don't move (I hope) once I know the room, I know the room. Having tables that could be moved around might change the way the room sounds. It's possible that I could have built some sort of panel resonators but I won't know that for sure until I get the room finished and start testing it. Just in case, on Knightfly's recomendation, I will be glueing MW to the inside faces of the tables to dampen any ringing. I also needed some place to mount the patch bays/connector panels and the table openings are 19 1/8" wide to accomodate that. There will also be a door under the rack panels so I can store cables and such under them to keep them handy. ... and [b]I[/] think they look cool. :-) len

len-morgan29 June 2006#181
AndreasB wrote:
Isn't there any paintshop around who do that for you in a whizz
You obviously have not looked on a map to see where Big Spring, TX is! :-) I'm sure I could find someone to do the painting for me but at this point, it's really just the door trim and table tops that need the work (which is almost done) and in order to get the finish I want (like glass as you said), they are going to have to work a little, let it dry, come back and sand then put on another coat, etc, etc... I've worked out a pretty good system where I go down there about 6:30 in the morning, sand/paint/poly what needs it, then leave. Then I go back just after lunch (when it's all dried) and repeat the process. After dinner I go back again and do a third layer of something. I wouldn't normally spend so much time on what seems to many to be a minor detail but this is going to be a commercial facility and as such, people are going to see the studio long before they hear it and that first impression is going to be important. Shiny door trim and table tops that look like glass won't make the recording sound any better but clients might percieve it as better until they can hear it for themselves. It's just basic showmanship. :-) If a new client thinks the place LOOKS great and professionally done, then the music has to be at least as good, right? :-) len
