One thing you have to remember (or take note of if your memory's as bad as mine!), is that wall is at an angle. len-morgan19 June 2006#160
Thanks for the compliments - as you can see, just because I haven't been updating here, I have been busy. I'm not sure exactly what you mean by "schematic." Being an EE for a long time, I think electronics when I hear schematic. Do you mean the plans for the studio or just the wall? The studio layout is on the first page of this thread (about the middle of the page). This wall is along the East wall of the "big room" (the one in the middle that goes across the entire width of the building. Knightfly suggested this wall to avoid East/West bounce and at his suggestion, it runs from the right hand edge of the window down to the corner at the bottom of the room. I have no idea what the angle is because I was building it (as an afterthought) to the "landmarks" that were in the room. The boards are made up of plain ol' 1x4s, 1x6s, and 1x8s that were stained and then 2 coats of polyurethane to get the shine. The frame construction can be seen about 2/3rds of the way down on page 9 (the purple wall). It's just a 2x4 frame on 24" OC (more or less :-) ), then stuffed with mineral wool between the studs (but empty behind that). One thing that can't be seen from the pictures there is that I put up two "walls" of plywood from the studs to the back wall and then these were calked all the way around to make three separate (and different sized) cavities. That let me use the same three distances between slats and the same three slat sizes and still get a wide range of frequencies. Of course, dumb luck counts for a lot in my case too. I can't say whether it's better or worse than it was before but there is a noticable difference in the sound if you stand in the middle of the room and talk at the wall and with the wall at your back. And as Knightfly told me, it can't really hurt. The fact that I have some variability in the room just depending on which way you face (and how close/far away you are) makes the room more versatile. That means that I have a lot more recording possibilities in the same space which is always good. Even the room came out sounding exactly the same as before, the looks of the wood add a "wow factor" that can't be discounted when it comes to showing the place to prospective clients. I mean if it LOOKS good, it HAS TO sound good, right? :-) len

len-morgan28 June 2006#171
Ok, a minor update.... I've started covering the "tables" I've built under the windows in the main room. I decided to face the plywood with cedar planking (14 sq ft / $18.00 @ Lowes). A couple of coats of poly urethane shined them right up. This stuff is tongue and groove and by mating two pieces together, they can just make it around the 22.5 degree corners. In case you're wondering, I put the horizontal strip around the top so that the vertical pieces could be 32 inches long. That lets me get 3 piecies out of each plank instead of two and a big piece of scrap. The bottom will have either another horizontal strip of wood or cove base molding. Haven't decided yet. The cove base will be around the rest of the room and is much stronger (if kicked) than the planking would be. Ok, here's a few pictures...
