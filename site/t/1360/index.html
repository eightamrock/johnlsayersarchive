OK, my brother-in-law has agreed to give me the rest of the garage that I'm using, so I have a lot more floor area at my disposal. The existing garage is approximately 8800x5030 (28'10"x16'6") and has two windows (which I want to leave intact and in place), one standard run-of-the-mill door, and a big double garage door (which we are removing and replacing with a regular wooden frame with studs, firbro on the outside wall panneling inside). I need enough space to comfortably live, hang out with mates, etc. and also need to build a bathroom (with shower, toilet, sink). Well, now the problem I've been having is coming up with a basic floor plan for the studio. I've read the design section at the site and taken it all in, but this is an unusual case because of the placement of the windows, and the fact that the bathroom has to be against the top edge of the diagram, so the piping and be run down the hill straight to the houses septic tank. I've come up with several layouts myself and am not entirely sure which one would work. Remember these are really, really rough sketches and only very basic ideas of where the walls might go. Any input you guys could give me would be greatly appreciated.

TerrorFiend20 February 2004#4
Here's the plan that I'm currently the most happy with. The idea was that I'd make several large panels of absorbant material (carpet, rockwool, foam, etc or slot resonator) and attach strong hooks at certain places around the room, including above the windows. Corresponding hooks or rings can be attached to the panels and when it's time to mix/master the music, then the panels will be strategically hung up around the room at the various locations where they're needed (I dont know exactly where yet because I don't yet know enough about accoustics) including panels that cover the windows. Some of the furniture itsself should also serve to absorb the high/mids and the bass traps should take care of the lows (in theory). This way the room can be used as a general living area, and on those infrequent occasions when it comes time to mix and master the music, it can be quickly and easily converted into a sound absorbing king :-) Remember thats a theory and that I still know very little about accoustics.
