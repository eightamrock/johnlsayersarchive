knightfly9 May 2003#30
Thin carpet will just let you get a little higher in frequency before it sucks out the "shimmer" - putting it back with the high EQ will just add noise and phase problems. If by some rare chance anything you record has too many highs, you can turn DOWN the high EQ and do much less damage. Assuming the same substrate, almost any reflective surface is fine for floors. If you find something inexpensive that you like (except carpet :=) whether it's Pergo, vinyl, painted concrete, clear plexiglas, etc, go for it. If you can tell the difference between species of woods (other than balsa, for example) I've got some $5000 speaker cables to sell you... Steve

knightfly20 June 2003#41
Fiery Jack, sorry I missed your questions earlier - No, I'm not saying that ALL hardwood floors are necessarily best - it's just that all CARPET definitely is NOT a good plan. If you have a hardwood floor, it's a little better aesthetically to throw down a carpet or two where you want them, than it is to put sheets of plywood down over an all carpet floor. Generally, unless your ceilings are 12 feet or more, it's better to have a bright floor and absorptive ceiling. That way, you don't completely kill the room but you do control flutter echoes and sibilance. Dead half/live half - as far as walls go, yeah - but there's such a thing as "too dead for anything" - if you were to heavily absorb floor, ceiling and walls in one part of a room, it would most likely sound pretty un-natural for any normal recordings (might be OK for those "shock effect" times, when the singer goes from normal reverb to a single, totally dead word or phrase) ... Steve

Ethan Winer14 September 2003#45
Doc, > packing all the open spaces in the loveseat < There are two main problems with using a love seat as a bass trap: 1) It's probably not large enough to do much. 2) Bass traps based on rigid fiberglass work best in the room corners. If your love seat is against the middle of a wall, or out in the room, it might help a little. But it won't be nearly as effective as having a sufficient number of bass traps placed in the room corners. > Fell off the latter trying to catch the electric screwdriver < Ouch. Electronics technicians know that when a hot soldering iron is about to fall off the work bench you always let it go. A new iron costs less than a trip to the emergency room! --Ethan
