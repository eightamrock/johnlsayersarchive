Soundman202029 February 2012#4
"does anything work for sealing, or should I use green glue?
Green Glue itself is neither glue nor sealant. Green Glue (the product) is a visco-elastic polymer that is designed to act as constrained layer damping when used between surfaces in a wall assembly. There's nothing quote like Green Glue. The same company also makes acoustic sealant, which is good, but isn't the only acoustic sealant out there. Other companies make it to, and in a pinch you can even use plain old bathroom caulk, provided that you get one that stays flexible and rubbery, and never sets hard..
Does glass leak sound even if caulked well around window edges?
Yes. No Maybe. It depends on numerous factors. But EVERYTHING leaks sound, to some extent. That's why we say that there is no such things as true "soundproofing", since any sufficiently loud sound will penetrate any conceivable barrier. So the question is: How much isolation do you need (in terms of decibels)? If the glass you have isn't thick enough, it will "leak" sound, and if it is thick enough then it won't. The question about "how thick is thick enough" depends on you: What level of isolation (decibels) do you need? - Stuart -

Soundman20203 March 2012#7
I know its too much to ask such a question but; what does level of isolation mean to begin with?
Not too much at all! That's what the forum is for! To ask questions when you don't stuff, or need more details. Like the saying goes: The only dumb question is the one you DIDN'T ask! "Isolation" just means how much sound do you need to stop. This is also called "soundproofing" sometimes, but that's not really a good word for it. So "level of isolation" simply means that you need to figure out, firstly, how loud you will be inside your room, and secondly, how quiet do you need things to be outside the room. "Isolation" is what stops the sound getting out. But there is "subjective" isolation, and "objective" isolation. Subjective is things like "loud", "quiet", "some", "a lot" or "a huge amount". Objective is things like "STC-50" or "TL = 60" or "I need to reduce the total sound level by at least 65 decibels". Those are all real numbers that provide a scientific way of measuring the amount of sound being stopped. For example, if you are playing drums really hard inside your living room, and you have a baby sleeping in the next room, then subjective would be "I need lots of isolation". Objective would be to measure how loud the drums are in the room (using a sound level meter) and to also measure what level of sound will wake up the baby (also using a sound level meter). The difference between the two meter readings is the objective measure of how much isolation you need. Another case: you are playing bass guitar in your house, and the cops knock on the door saying that you are playing too loud, and the neighbors complained. That's subjective, and they can't fine you for "too loud". But if they have a sound level meter, and measured your level at 65 dB while the regulations for your area only permit 45 db, then they CAN fine you, since they have an objective measurement. Just like the cops can't fine you for "driving too fast", but can fine you for driving at 70 MPH in a 50 zone. "Loud" and "quiet" are no use for deciding how to build your room. "I need isolation of 50 dB" is a very useful thing to know, since you can then look that up in research to find out what types of construction will give you 50 dB of isolation, then choose one that fits your budget, and build that.
