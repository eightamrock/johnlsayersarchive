Hi Chris, You have most things in hand. Regarding the cold air coming up from the walls, well: 1. you should fill the gap between the inner and outer leaves of the walls with fluffy insulation, or you could use Rockwool which won't gain you any better TL but will be better for thermal. 2. You can get Rockwool fireblock which you can install at the wall/roof junction. This will keep everything decoupled, safer/better fire rating and prevent the cold air. Also, I don't think you need to caulk that junction you pointed out but even if you did it would not really couple the leaves together if you used a flexible non hardening caulk. Regarding the hybrid roof situation and the risk of condensation due to the extra insulation under the roof deck you can call someone Rockwool technical support (even if you're not using Rockwool products you can just pretend) and they will simulate your exact proposed assembly and tell you if you have a significant risk of condensation or not. I actually did this myself today and the lady was extremely helpful and very knowledgable. Regarding the EPDM being a third leaf - well no, not really. The PIR is a weird one because it is sort of a mass and sort of full of air. In any case it will not significantly reduce your isolation. Your proposed assembly is how I design a lot of roofs for my clients, if a warm roof is what they want. But even if you went with a cold vented roof you would need to have a "third" leaf anyway, otherwise there would be holes for the vents compromising the outer leaf... unless the inner leaf is massive enough or the isolation requirements are not high. With timber framed buildings a third leaf is usually the only way to do it. BTW, have you looked in to using SIPS for your roof? It may be a lot cheaper, quicker and easier than building a timber frame. Regarding your overall TL - well it's only as good as your weakest assembly. So you can use that to give you an idea of what you can expect. If you're talking STC value then you look at the TL at 500hz which in your case is 70... but this is not very useful for a recording studio since you will be listening and recordings sources that go much lower than 125hz. Looking quite good so far, but I'm not sure about your acoustic treatment decisions...For example, I know you don't have much height but 50mm of insulation on the ceiling is not much of anything, unless you are only concerned with controlling high mids and highs. Do you have a floor plan or 3d sketchup?

gullfo5 March 2022#16
actually, concrete, steel, hardwood lumber, etc are all great conductors of sound - faster speeds and longer distances... than air and soft things like rubber, insulation, and so on. the trick is to order them correctly to minimize the transfer from a hard (we tend to call mass) boundary to another hard boundary in an effort to reduce energy transmission. earth damped concrete slabs are good. connected earth damped slabs not quite as nice though. decoupled earth damped slabs, very nice. same principles for walls and ceilings. damping and then energy conversions - absorption via sound to heat (like insulation) and speed reduction (air is about 1/10th of concrete in terms of speed). some examples: https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/soun ... %20rows%20

gullfo6 March 2022#18
earth damped and earth connected are essentially the same thing. one the sound transmission on same slab for two rooms - the frequencies are not restricted to the slab resonance. the slab resonance matter when it comes to decoupled structures to which the slab is exposed. for example: if a floated slab as a MAM spring 10hz resonance, then the only effective transmission through the spring mechanism is 10hz or lower. there is still the possibility of higher frequencies being retransmitted although the mass laws and damping play their part in attenuation of those. so, a concrete box on strings can still transmit energy to the outside world, however, it's significantly reduced, and if the surrounding bits are equally decoupled and damped, then it is negligible. an earth damped slab is nice. two earth damped slabs decoupled are even nicer. in a single slab studio, loud / impact sources (drums, bass amps, guitar amps, dancing buffalo, etc) should have an isolation platform to reduce the amount of energy direct sunk into the slab and this will help reduce the levels which span the rooms.
