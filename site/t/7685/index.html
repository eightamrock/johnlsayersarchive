As a newcomer here, let me please open my question by first taking the time to thank all of you guys for the time you take and have taken to help the multitude of strangers that come here for advice. You truly have my respect!! I have a question that is really going to get some heads shaking with disbelief, but I am hoping that I can convey my "realistic" expectations accurately. I have recently emmigrated to the United Kingdom and will be moving into a semi-detached house in January of 2007 (currently in a tiny flat with all of my beloved studio gear tossing to and fro in some container ship powering up the Atlantic Ocean making its way to a very lonely owner). I could go into a very lengthy explanation of where and why, but to get to the point a little quicker, let me lay out a few points to summarise the situation and set the scene. 1. I will be renting this house and do not have the landlords permission to undertake any serious structural changes. 2. The room to be used measures approximately 3m x 3.5m and is located on the ground floor with wooden floorbaords (fully carpeted). 3. Being a mirror image semi-detached house (actually a mid-terraced house --- ie. flanked on both sides by mirror image houses), the single wall dividing my prospective music room from my neighbours adjoining room, is brick (ie. NOT drywall/plasterboard) and as my terribly amateurish sketch below shows, I am primarily concerned about the one single room which will literally be "the room next door" for now. 4. As mentioned in the above point, I refer to this room as my music room and not my studio, because although I have a considerable collection of analogue gear (5 x 14U racks plus 3 x 6ft racks stuffed with synths, recorders, patchbay etc) and a medium/large format console (Soundtracs InLine 3632) --- this room will be solely for my own use (programmed drums, Pod Pro XT guitars, DI bass etc) with vocals to be done offsite at 3rd party studios and brought home for me to knock up rough mixes/demos, so I do not really need to capture any performances in this particular room as such --- ie. this is really simply a WAY over the top songwriting studio. 5. I will be making little or no attempt to isolate this room from the rest of the house (adjoining lounge/dining room with 3 bedrooms upstairs) --- I say this to give an indication as to "domestic" mixing levels (NS-10's) with a wife and 2 kids in the rest of the house. OK, so my probably obvious question is --- is there anything I can do to the single wall between myself and my direct neighbour to reduce the leakage into his lounge? Damn, I can see the heads shaking already --- but I am really not expecting to get true seperation or isolation. What I am envisaging is that by the time I am playing back anything at the sort of level that could be tolerated by the wife and kids in the room next door (taking into account the unsealed door leading straight into the passage that links the rest of our downstairs living areas), it will obviously be leaking through to the neighbour to a similar degree --- and my hope is that I can do something to that wall to lower the transmission enough to keep him happy enough as I am sure that my wife and kids may be ever so slightly more tolerant than a stranger and this slight decrease in tolerance is what I would like to address. Will I achieve anything worthwhile by placing a double/triple skinned drywall on a frame a few inches off the wall in question --- and if so, are there ANY possible ways to support this frame in such a way that when we leave this house (and probably have to remove this wall), that we will not have completely buggered up the existing walls and ceiling in any expensive (deposit risking) way? I know this is all a bit ridiculous, but I am really looking for minimal reduction compared to your typically louder requirement if you know what I mean --- but I also don't want to waste a few hundred pounds and get a zero result. Thanks for any time and suggestions.

I would think you should also be concerned with flanking noise if the whole structure shares the same floor construction. Beefing up that wall would help that wall, but sound will travel through the floors and ceiling as well. I don't have specific design help, but just a general thought.
