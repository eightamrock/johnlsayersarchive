knightfly31 March 2006#2
Those look like 2x2 foot drop ceiling tiles, set into a metal grid; first thing I'd do is lift one up out of the grid - if it's not full of insulation above, I'd fill it with unfaced fiberglass to improve bass trapping. The carpet will suck out mids and some highs, and your space is already tight; so I'd look for some 3 to 5 PCF rockwool or fiberglass batts (Fibrex, Owens Corning, Johns Manville, Roxul, etc) and build some frames to hold them diagonally across most of your wall/ceiling corners, cover them with cloth and treat the cloth for fire resistance One source if you can't find it locally - http://www.natfire.com/ Diffusion isn't the best way to go for a small room, but if it's too dead after the above treatment and you can't remove the carpet, you may want to build at least one of John's corner absorbers (slat resonator) centered on 300 hZ http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm Some updates on construction/insulation positioning - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download.php?id=4421 And an excel calculator for slat resonator - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1363 For CORNER units, use the AVERAGE depth in the calculator; kick drum's primary modal frequency is around 300 hZ, that's why this is a good center target frequency for a drum room. It will take at least two corners done this way to hear much difference. HTH... Steve

knightfly2 April 2006#4
If you're talking about vertical corners, you can just stand them up in the corner - especially the slat ones, which will have a solid back and sides. For wall mounting of absorbent traps, if they're 2x4 feet and framed with 1x4 wood, the total trap with 3 PCF absorbent 4" thick will only weigh about 16-18 pounds, so they can be hung with heavy rated picture hangers, or small metal angle brackets and plastic wall anchors. For wall-to-ceiling corner absorbers, the frames can be fastened with tie wires to the ceiling grid and to the wall with the plastic wall anchors. Normally you'd want something less visible but you said "temporary" - and this will work. Again, these should be light enough that heavy picture hanging kits will hold them... Steve
