Well today was adventurous and productive to say the least. We beefed up the outer walls (1st leaf) by using a router with a small bearing on it, drilling a pilot hole in each stud cavity and simply inserted the bit into the cavity, moving it until the bearing hit a stud and proceeded to cut out perfect blanks with about 1/4" gap or so. sharward14 December 2005#36
Great idea salvaging the drywall! I kind of wish I had done that when I did my beef-up... It sure would have been a lot cleaner than what I did! (Although I'm sure yours was at least somewhat messy!) I think there's some value in applying some insulation against the outer leaf in an effort to keep it from resonating too much. You may have to fill the cavity with insulation though to comply with code in terms of firebreaks. Check with your local building department. Ideally you would use insulation that does not have any kraft paper on it. I've been promoting the use of Thermafiber Sound Attenuation Fire Blankets (SAFB) - Nominal Density (2.5 pcf) and Roxul Acoustical Fire Batt (AFB) - ≥2" (2.5 pcf) for months here for fire safety reasons. Yes, it's more expensive than the cheap-o fluffy pink stuff... But, then again, so are air bags, anti-lock brakes... ;-) Personally (and I've heard Steve suggest this as well), I think you're better off getting all 5/8" rather than mixing and matching two sizes. Reasons: you only have to deal with one pile, and you'll benefit from the additional mass that 5/8" offers over 1/2". --Keith :mrgreen:

I agree with putting insulation in the OUTER wall as well as the inner (new) one. It's really pretty cheap and if nothing else, the insulation value (not ISOlation) will keep your A/C and Heating bills down because you won't loose as much through the outer walls. Remember to leave an air gap between the outside wall and the inside one. You don't have (or want) to fill the air gap with insulation - just the individual stud cavities in each wall. len

knightfly14 December 2005#42
They will span 16'6" with 10 PSF dead load; two layers of 5/8 drywall weigh 4.7 PSF, plus the weight of the joists (part of the total dead load) would run about 2 PSF for 2x8 on 16" centers. This means that you don't need the full 10 PSF, so the extra 6" span won't be a problem... Steve Oh, I believe I mentioned a Rotozip, not a dremel; the Rotozip is heavier duty. If you can get a 1/8" collet for your router, they make rotozip bits just for drywall that will last longer, if you have more to do - although they don't have the cool little ball bearing thingy that's obviously making things easier for you, so it might be a tossup. And yes, the extra mass of using all 5/8 wallboard will result in a more balanced isolation for your walls...
