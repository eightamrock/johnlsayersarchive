Nashtalon31 December 2005#88
Well I got the two silencers for the outer leaf built and in place...man did that suck (no pun intended) :P I placed them at opposite corners and the inlet will end up near the ceiling and the outlet near the floor. I did this so the cold air (winter) coming in the room will be somewhat warmed by the air near the ceiling, since heated air rises. In the summer, I'll reverse the fans, so the air near the ceiling is drawn out, rather than the cooled air near the floor. In building the silencers, I used MDF, two layers and a layer of LMV between the MDF with fiberglass lining the interior. They ended up being 4' long by 16" wide by 8" deep and I'm using 6" duct, although not flex duct, but the aluminum flexy pipe. I'll splice the regular plastic flex duct before I bring it through the inner leaf, so as to not have any hard connections. Steve had said to use 8" duct, but since it's a rehearsal room and not a studio, airflow noise wasn't an issue. Besides, you can't even hear the airflow and the fans will be inside silencers on the interior walls. I hooked up one of the fans on the exhaust side and went over and felt a nice waft of air coming in through the inlet. I also did the sound test and had only a 2db leak at the vents compared to the rest of the building. I think that's tolerable. Framing of the walls and ceiling starts on Monday...Yea! :!: :!:

len-morgan3 January 2006#91
Nashtalon wrote:
However, he's unable to afford tearing out the existing drywall and beefing it up like advised here
Do you mean he can't afford it financially or timewise? There IS (virtually) no cost to removing the current two layers of drywall. Just take a dry wall saw, saw out the old drywall between the studs (leaving strips of the old dry wall screwed to the studs), remove the insulation (and save it), put the cut dry wall between the studs of the existing wall (they'll fit because they were cut from the cavity they're going back in on, and screw, glue, and calk it down. The only expense is for calk and possibly glue. And of course a lot of (free) labor. You will avoid a triple leaf (which will make the situation WORSE than it is now) and also be able to buy a little floor space since the air gap is measured from the back of two masses, not the distance between the studs. len

knightfly5 January 2006#95
Don't think you're gonna be happy with your doors that way - the type you want are FLAT SOLID CORE, not paneled ones. Where the panels are recessed, you're losing MASS. Adding MLV will cause the air spaces between the thin panels and the MLV to act as multi-leaf construction, and low frequency isolation will suffer. A LOT. If you've not already bought the MLV, you'd be better off replacing both doors with solid core, FULL THICKNESS FLAT doors and forgetting the MLV. Once you do that, seals will be the weakest link so slightly more mass won't help... knightfly8 January 2006#99
Sooo, are we to assume this means you're gonna forgive us for all the "bullying" ?? :wink: Your doors may well be what will eliminate even the dull thud you're getting outside, BTW - I like Len's idea as much as any I'd come up with if you still want the doors better. If you "de-hang" a door so you can lay it flat, then (in two passes) first fill the lowest areas (thinnest part) with drywall mud (sand first), then scuff that up after it's dry and trowel in enough to raise the door smooth and flat, then (while the mud's still soft) glue on a surface layer of 1/4" birch ply, making sure to get good glue joints where the stiles and rails of the door are, that should improve each door by several dB (especially at LOW frequencies) - If the plywood gets in the way of your door frames, make it smaller (not clear to the edge) - you could also put a bead of weatherstrip where the ply meets the door frame, for another level of seal. Or, if you're as happy as it seems, just rock on... Steve
