sharward28 January 2006#128
You can say it's not a business... But if you're charging money for it, it's a business. It may not be a profitable business, but it's still a business. Your biggest obstacle may the business license that you would need to obtain from your city or county. In many areas, to obtain a business license for a residential address, you need to (also) obtain a home occupation permit. I cover this in the "Permits, Codes, Licenses -- and WHY YOU SHOULD CARE!" thread. Many home occupation permits don't allow for regular visitation by customers and/or limit the number of customers/guests allowed at any one time. This is often in proportion to the density of the neighborhood -- the higher the density, the more restrictive the regulations. It's best not to make any assumptions.

sharward30 January 2006#130
Is your attorney familiar with the zoning or municipal code restrictions for your neighborhood? Your profile doesn't indicate where you live specifically... But just as an example, the City of Nashville has restrictions for home-based businesses:
    May I operate a small business from my home? Yes, under certain limitations, and with a "Home Occupation Permit". Permits are required for all home occupations and may be obtained through the Department of Codes Administration at a cost of $25.00. Contact our Zoning Division at 862-6500 to obtain complete information regarding current requirements. The basic limitations include:
    • There can be no employee associated with the home occupation on site that does not reside on site.
    • There can be no signs or advertising of a business at the residence (except in the areas of child care, or tutoring).
    • In no case can customers be served on the premises.
    • One vehicle, not exceeding an axle load of 1.5 tons is permitted, but no advertising is permitted on the vehicle.
    A reliable "rule of thumb" is: if you can see it,     hear it, or smell it, the business is not allowed as a home occupation. (Source)
I'm not saying that these restrictions apply specifically in your case (unless you live in the Nashville city limits)... But you should contact your city or county officials to find out what, if any, restrictions do apply to you. You're welcome to think of such things as "intrusive governmental nonsense" -- many would agree with you. However, such restrictions aren't in place solely to tell you what you can't do -- they're in place to protect others from becoming victims of the actions of others. I once heard of a case in a shiny brand new neighborhood of a homeowner running his ice cream truck business from his corner lot property. He had covered his back yard with concrete, constructed an unpermitted and illegal driveway, and serviced and stocked the vehicles in his backyard. The neighbors were rightfully outraged and were ultimately successful in shutting the operation down with the enforcement of existing ordinances. If you utlimately discover that there are restrictions that apply to you, then work within the law to try to get the rules changed or to be granted a variance. Perhaps you live in a rural area and there aren't any such restrictions that apply to you. (Don't assume that to be the case though.) If so, then you should be set -- provided you have the necessary insurance and legal CYA documents as recommended by your attorney. :-) --Keith :mrgreen:
