Soundman20205 November 2010#52
of course... so with that in mind, how does one get power to the ceiling for lighting?
Surface mount cable ducts. Basically long plastic "U" shaped things, in two parts. You stick one part to the wall, put your wiring inside, then the other part clicks into place on top to make it look pretty. There are also accessories, such as corners, curves, end caps, switch boxes, etc. Here in Chile I normally use a system with the brand name Legrand (they call it a "trunking system"). Here's a link to their UK web site: http://www.legrand.co.uk/en/scripts/en/ ... N&SYNC=YES But you can probably find something made by their competitors too. Are you going to build your walls inside-out? If so, that makes it easier to hide the wiring. Of course, you DO have to bring power into the room somewhere, so you'll have at least one hole, but the idea is to keep holes to the absolute minimum, and seal them well. - Stuart -

Soundman20207 November 2010#66
sorry stuart, im not sure what you mean by inside out. ill put up the studs for the new wall, run the wires to where i want the outlets, then put up the drywall. is that what you mean?
Nope. Inside-out construction is a method (invented by John, as far as I know) where you put your drywall on the OTHER side of the inner-leaf studs. In other words, the drywall goes on the side that faces the wall cavity, so that when you are standing in the room, you see the studs. You can then use that stud space for your room treatment. And also for your wiring. Since most of the treatment (and wiring) is going to be covered with cloth or slats, you won't actually see any of it in the final finished room. Makes a lot of sense! - Stuart -
