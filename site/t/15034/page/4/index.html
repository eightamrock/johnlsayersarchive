Soundman20209 November 2010#82
Another advantage is that it saves you a bit of space: Since the studs are facing the room, you can put some of your acoustic treatment in between those studs: If you had drywall facing the room, then you'd have to put the treatment outside the drywall, using up more interior space. It also saves a little on construction materials: Once again, the studs are right there, in the room, and are able to support your treatment directly. For example, if you need absorption, just fill the stud space with fiberglass, stretch cloth over it, and you are done. Whereas if you had drywall facing the room, you'd have to buy more wood to make a new frame inside the room, to hold the fiberglass and cloth. Etc. There are lots of benefits. - Stuart -
