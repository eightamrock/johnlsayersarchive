nsanenthelane29 January 2009#5
I'm not an expert on acoustics. I'm currently in school for Contemporary Musicianship and Audio Production(I wasn't responsible for the length of the name). My professor seems to think that having things nonsymetrical is the way to go. When he built the studio at school one wall was straight, one very slightly curved towards the center of the room. and the other two were at angles opposite of each other. The theory is that uneven surfaces and angles absorb sound and prevent phase issues. peace out.

Soundman202029 January 2009#7
But what about stereo imaging then ?
Good question!!!! If your control room is not symmetrical, then you will not HAVE any stereo imaging! Symmetry is a fundamental requirement for a control room. However, it is not needed for a live room, of course. I think Nsanenthelane must be talking about a live room, not a control room. AFAIK, a live room can be pretty much any shape you want, and non-symmetry can be an asset. Although I think I'd tend to stay away from curved walls, at least concave ones, due the way they can focus sound and re-direct it to places you didn't want it to be. I guess you could do a live room with curved walls, but it would need very careful design. But a non-symmetrical control room would be a disaster. - Stuart -
