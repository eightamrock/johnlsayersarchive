Aaronw18 May 2006#2
If I purchase a few wall plates with some XLR and 1/4" jacks, how are these wired between rooms? Can I simply put one on each side of the wall and solder the connections between the plates together? I have never soldered before, is this a simple enough job that I could learn on?
You can wire them depending on your needs. You can either go from panel to panel, or you can wire them from the panel to a harness/pigtales. Are you looking to make it basically a through the wall connection so you can disconnect, or will it be more hardwired and plugged into gear and left alone? Soldering, it's not really rocket science. The main thing is to stay consistant with your wiring, and beware of cold solder joints, and don't short any wires. XLR connections are: Pin 1 Shield, Pin 2 Hot, Pin 3 Common 1/4" connections are: Tip Hot, Ring Common, Sleeve Shield Typically if I'm using mogami cable, each wire is color coded in multipair cable. I'll use the colored wire as the hot (pin 2 or Tip) and the clear wire as common (pin 3 or ring), and of course the shield (BTW, w/ mogami you have a drain wire and lots of copper wires...cut off the extra copper and use the drain wire) And another tip for soldering, after you've prepped the cables (skinned off the coating), tin each wire and connector. Tin means to put solder on the tips of the wires and solder in the cups of the XLR connectors. It'll make life easier and it goes much faster. Hope this helps. Aaron :D

Aaronw25 May 2006#11
Soldering is actually the easiest part of wiring. The time consuming part is the prep work. Here's a couple tips... -Depending on how you want to dress your wires, I typically cut the outter skin off of a 24 pair cable 18"-24". -Then I prep all 24 lines, stripping them. With Mogami, there's usually extra copper around the whole wire. Cut off the copper, but leave the Shield drain wire. Use that for you shield connection. -After stripping all the cables, tin each wire. -Next, put on shrink wrap on the extra exposed shield, and another piece around the end of the outter skin where you stripped. -Tin your connectors. -With Mogami, you have different colors on each wire. Usually a clear wire, then a colored wire and the shield. I typically use the colored wire for pin 2 (hot). The clear one on pin 3. -Next, heat up the solder you tinned on the connectors, slide in the tinned wire, let it cool, and it's all done. Goes quick.

Aaronw25 May 2006#13
The shrink wrap around the shield keeps it from shorting out on anything else. The other piece of shrink wrap, keeps all the extra copper wires (that you trim off), from bumping up against a connector and shorting it out too. Back to the dressing for a minute, I typically will run the wires from one side or another. And will cut the wires to length for each connector.
