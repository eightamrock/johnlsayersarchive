Soundman202014 January 2012#31
Those graphs are starting to look pretty good! I'm glad it is sounding better in there as well. :)
What do you think about the up-to-date measuring and the coming slot walls? Should I angle it or better tune it to problematic midrange frequencies with 50-80% wool inside to make it broadband
I would angle the lot walls slightly, yes, and also put plenty of wool in them, but make sure it doesn't touch the slats themselves. Also, don't forget to carefully seal the cavity behind the slats! It has to be airtight in order to work. You could try to tune some of those slats around the problematic frequencies: it can't hurt! Those deep dips might be reflections, but if you are angling your slot walls and you do tune to those frequencies, you'll be covering a broad spread anyway, so that's fine. Also, you seem to be testing with the volume levels a bit low: normally rooms should be tested and calibrated at 85 dB (each speaker individually).
Does it make sense putting something absorbent on the backwall and measure again to find out the exact frequencys that cause the backwall and both sidewalls?
Yes! You absolutely do need something absorbent on the rear wall anyway, and thick, so you might as well put it there now and measure again, before deciding if you need additional treatment. But your graphs seem to be showing reasonably even response, withing about +/-8 dB mostly, with very few exceptions. Looking good! :) - Stuart -

Soundman202025 January 2012#38
Sorry about the lack of response, Aaron! Lots going on here... To be honest, your graphs are looking pretty good, at least down to 70 Hz, and that's a major achievement already for a room such as yours! Those waterfall plots are very clean. Sure, the frequency response isn't flat, but you'll never get it perfectly flat, not in that shape/size room. You are within roughly +/- 10 dB from 20 Hz upwards, across most of the spectrum, and those higher frequency dips are most likely reflections and comb filtering from other surfaces in the room. The lower frequency dips are likely to be the room structure itself getting involved (resonance in the walls, floor, ceiling, and structure) and there's not much you can do about that. But you might still be able to tame things a bit more, so here's what I would do: My suggestion would be to leave that center wall as it is, but I would also put something on the OTHER side of it (the side facing the speakers, above the sofa. I reckon that some of your issues are coming from there. Maybe put 10cm or 15cm of absorption on it. You could even angle it, thicker at the top and thinner at the bottom. So then you'd have the center rear wall with similar gaps on each side, and a large space behind it. I'd put some hangers in that large space, sort of like Plan "C", but make that area as deep as you can. 50 cm is OK, but if you could spare more space, that would help. For example, 75cm would give you enough space to line the walls with 10 cm of insulation, and still have plenty of space for lot of hangers. Space the hangers about 10 inches apart, and angle them a bit, so that you can make them bigger. I think the reason why you weren't getting much effect with the hanger on the front wall, is because they were too close together, and they weren't angled: If you would have angled them at maybe 30 to 60 degrees, I think they would have been more effective. But what you did with the insulation there is fine: you don't need to change that. Anyway, that would be my suggestion! - Stuart -

Hi Everyone, here are the actual measures of the room. What i did so far: *Removing the drywall-> rearwall again but i let the 40cm insulation between the room and floor. *Using 0,5mm pond foil instead thick PP foil on the rearwall (it made the bassrange btween 60hz and 300hz 3db better) *Building two slot walls tuned to two frequencys and broadband 60-100hz and 230-300hz *Painting the slats *Making the front insulation 20cm thicker with 5kPa wool made a huge different of 6db on 70hz instead using 6kPa rockwool #Experience: #Slot walls: I rebuild the slot walls three times total. The reason was i used PP Wool for the first time and it was way to dense. When i generated a tune of the slotwall resonance 100hz, the wall worked like a reverbator and the reverb time was very long after stopping the signal. Don´t use any very low dense pp wool in a slotwall! It wound make you happy. So i used 10cm 6kPa and 5cm 16kPa wool and the effect of the wall was much better, it did absorb around 6db better than before. But there was still a low reverb that i did not like so i rebuild it the 3. time and added a second layer of the 16kPa wool to have totaly 10cm 6kPa + 10cm 16kPa. The fabric does not touch the slats but the absorbtion effect got worse 3db but the reverb was no more there anymore. I stayd with to save my nerves ;) The total absorbtion effect of the slotwalls and the tuned frequencys was only 3-6 db depending on how much and what wool i used in the resonator room. But it made the Room much more better in the high frequency and it is also looking nice :) #Porous absorber I did not belief this before i had have not tryed it myself but the difference between 5kPa and 6kPa seems to be massive on absorbing bass depper than 80hz. You might consider better using for >40cm bass absorber 5kPa insulation instead 6kPa. In my case it was an effect of >6db. #Measures Some of my measures are irritating. I needed to shift my speakers several times to work on the ceiling and walls. When i turned them to the first position they were shifted some cm and this some cm made few huge differences in the measures. I could cure some db in the bassrange by fine tuning the position and angle of the speakers too. #Foils Using foils is well known here but there are not many suggestions about different foil thicknesses. You can buy very thin to thick foils in a building store. From my own experience in this small room it is much better using thick foils everywhere at the ceiling sides and rearwall. The only full absorbtion area is the front wall. I tryed different foils and absorbtion areas from making holes and slots in foils to using no foils on the rear wall and the best thing i went for was buying a 0,5mm pond foil i nailed onto the whole rearwall. I think every foil is a kind of membrane that starts to reflect depending on the thickness and starts to vibrate depending the weight and size. Anyhow i can not explain why but the pond foil made the whole bandwith 2-3db better from lows to highs instead of a thick ppfoil i had used before. Now, I gonna experiment using 15cm 50kg rockwool absorbers on the sides for cureing the 200hz dip. One 6db/100hz mode seems to be caused by the reflection of the slats /width 3,5m between the slotwall ends. but all in all it looks way better than before i think :-) The bassrange is only a broad dip that is 6-8db deep at 100hz.
