Why do you need them at 1.8m separation? You could bring them closer together without any harm. It would also allow you to build bigger soffits (wider front panels), which would be good. Or if you really have to keep them at 1.8m separation for some reason, then you could just angle them more. It looks like you have them at a 60 degree intersect angle (acoustic axes). You could increase that up to as much as 90 degrees if needed. And yeah, 45% of room depth is awfully close to 50%: you'd only have to move your head back a few inches, and you'd be right in the null. I reckon I'd try to get a bit closer to 38% by either reducing the space between the monitors, or angling them more, or a little bit of both. - Stuart -

bwilkinson21 April 2010#8
tripiale wrote:
Apart from which angle i choose (i red by the way that 60 is better than 90..is this true?) is not the RULE to have the a perfect triangle with every side of the same lenght? I mean if the distance from the speaker is 1.54.... does not the distance AMONG the speaker need to be 1.54?
I cannot comment on whether 60 is better than 90, but by definition, if the three distances are the same, the angle MUST be 60 degrees. If you used a 90 degree angle, then the angle between the front wall and speaker face would be 45 degrees, and the distance from you to the speaker would be 0.707*{the distance between the speakers}. So if your speakers were 1.8m apart, the distance between you and each speaker would be 1.27m. If you wanted to get to a 1.54m distance to the speaker, your listening angle would need to be 71.5 degrees - in between the two. However, please understand that if you have two people side-by-side, one will have a listening distance of approximately 1.3m from one speaker, and 1.85m from the second. Even if you were at the 1.8m distance and 60 degrees listening angle, the two speaker distances would be 2.06m and 1.6m. (this is assuming approximately 900mm between the heads of the two people) In both cases, you are nearly half a meter closer to one speaker than the other. So, in reality, sitting side by side with someone else, your ears wouldn't be at the 60 degree listening angle anyway.
