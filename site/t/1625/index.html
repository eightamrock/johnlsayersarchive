i currently have two studios both with windows and both done diffently, the way i would reccomend for you though i havent really had a chance to audition its performance but heres what we did. we figured a the frame dimensions then we took our 2 x 4 frame on the 4" side and ripped a gap just wide enough for the glass, this takes a pretty steady hand and some pretty nice equipment to do. once you have done that you can slide the glass into the frame and caulk all around it. and you want to try then to frame your walls to accomodate the glass and wood frame what i liked about this approach was that we were able to rip the 2 x 4 with an angle going from the top left to the bottom right, which gives you a pretty decent angle and does not take up additional space you would have to surrender if actually angle the window framing, or use the walls for your angle (which was the other way i did it and was lots and lots of fun...yea right!) see with this approach you simple set the window and frame right into the other 2 x 4 flush and easily another strong recomendation before you seal up that window make sure you get some silica (like in a pill bottle) and lay a good bit all across the bottom of the window, then maybe cover it with some nice cloth for apperance. this soaks up any moisture that might get trapped inside the window and avoid growing a mold observatory, great science project , but this is recording studio not science lab, so get some silica of course you dont want to go by 1,000 pill bottles for the bit of silica, check around im pretty sure you can find it in bulk somewhere on the net. this is not professional advice just merly what we did here at the shed

knightfly8 June 2004#10
Junior, first off I've not forgotten you, just been swamped with life's fun and games - I hope to get caught up in a couple more days. For glass, tempered is NOT the same as laminated, other than for safety they are somewhat similar. Tempered glass is still a single pane with no laminations, so won't have as good isolation per same thickness as the laminated will have. Second, going with double the thickness in one panel as the other isn't the best way to do it - you want your glass to have at least the same mass as ALL your wall panels on each side - since glass weighs roughly 3 times what sheet rock weighs, you need at least 1/3 as thick glass on each side of the wall as you have TOTAL layers of sheet rock. So if you have two layers of 5/8" sheet rock on one side, that side should have 1/2" glass - the other side of the wall should have a DIFFERENT total thickness, preferably more - that side of the window should have heavier glass, like 5/8" - this way, each side of the wall has at least 1/3 as thick glass as it has sheet rock, so the mass remains comparable between window and wall for best isolation. I have your drawing about 60% done, and should be able to finish it in the next 2-3 nights - these things always take more time than I hope they will, sorry... If your glass dealer can get 3/8 and 1/2, then that's what you should use - thinner than that will lessen your isolation by quite a bit. The two panes of glass should be laminated if you can get them, if not then the tempered will at least be better than plain float plate glass as far as safety is concerned... Steve
