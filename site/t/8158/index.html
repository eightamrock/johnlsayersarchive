sharward13 February 2007#9
If indeed your existing sliding glass door is your weakest link, then yes, adding a wall to section off a sound lock area with another door of some kind with extreme detail to seals will help. However, I'm concerned about the drop ceiling because it's far from air tight and may become your weakest link if it isn't already. if your proposed wall were to only go as high as the drop ceiling, then I have my doubts as to its effectiveness. Drop ceilings like that really have no place in studio designs where isolation is a major concern. You may have no choice but to live with it... But I just need to temper expectations. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward14 February 2007#13
I don't think that will help you much either, I'm afraid... :roll: You need two leaves in all directions (except the floor in your case) in order to have good isolation. You might be able to get away with 3 leaves in some spots if you overcompensate for them in those specific areas where necessary. Even if you have excellent walls, your poor ceiling will become your weakest link and your room will only isolate as effectively as your ceiling. Don't assume that only noise generated inside the kitchen will creep into the studio. If you can hear the airplanes in your kitchen, then you'll most likely hear them in your studio via your kitchen. :( Have you bought Rod's book yet? If not, you should... It'll be the best few bucks you spend. I realize your goal isn't perfection, but rather, doing the best you can with the least amount of investment and disruption to the structure... Pretty common actually. I just want to make sure you don't do "X" and expect the results of "Y," or worse, do the equivalent effort of "Y" and end up with only the results of "X." Dig? --Keith :mrgreen:
