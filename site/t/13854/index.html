Dear all, My name is Ales, I come from Slovenia, a small country in Europe. I am seriously thinking of rebuilding my studio in the basement of the house that I live in and am in need of advice. But first thing's first... BACKGROUND: I work mainly live PA sets, but have now for nearly 10 years worked in recording studios as a sound engineer, producer, musician, vocalist or vocal coach. I'm a vocalist and guitarist and have played in many bands, but for the past 5 years changed the stage for FOH position and studio work. I work mainly ITB with only few hardware gear pieces (which I hope will change ;)). WISHES/ NEEDS: I want to build a recording studio of a smaller format in my basement. I need a good sounding control room as I mostly work as a mixing engineer, but the studio will also be heavily used for songwriting and preproduction, so I am aiming for a comfortable space to work in. However, I would like to have a live room large enough to record drums (mostly for preproduction purposes) and a separate vocal booth, so we can quickly record some solid vocal tracks during preproduction and recording stages. Vocal booth is something I'm willing to give up if this means a larger live room. EXISTING CONSTRUCTION: This is a basement of a 2 storey house. I live on the top floor, my folks live on the ground floor and the basement is part studio, part cellar, part garage. The house is located basically in the middle of nowhere, meaning that my nearest neighbour lives about 500 meters away. The basement is partially dug into the ground (the house is on a slope) - the entrance to the basement is at ground level, but the opposite wall is completely submerged underground. The wall left of the entrance is exterior wall, the wall right of the entrance is cellar. So I am not concerned with ambient noise or loud music material seeping out of the walls. However, I want to minimise the transmision through the ceiling, as my folks live directly above the studio (their living room is above the control room, their bedroom above the live room - as presented in the initial sketch). The floors are concrete slab (15cm), covered with laminate floors. I have no intention of floating the floors (mostly because of problems with height). The ceiling is also a concrete with steel reinforcement (10cm) - the floors of my parents' apartement are parquet (18mm). The supporting three concrete beams (sorry for not knowing the technical terms) are indicated in the sketchup file by rectangles at the top of the walls (so they run parallel with the wall with the entrance doors). Right now, the entire ceiling is covered with wood planks (10mm thick). The walls are brick (30cm exterior walls, 16cm the right - inner- wall). They are covered with MDF panels (12mm). STAGE OF WORK: I am now at design stages and am looking for the best solution of room layout, according to the above mentioned needs. ELECTRICAL: The entire studio place has rebuilt electrical wiring with enough capacitiy, which is properly grounded and separated from all other electricity in the house. I have worked in this place for 4 years now and never had a single problems with hums from ground loops or found myself in need of more power demand. HVAC: Since this is a studio created for me and I have no intention of "placing it on the market", I am considering not installing a HVAC system. The entire room (since it is half burried in the ground) has a tendency of keeping a constant temperature in all seasons. For ventilation purposes we take breaks and open windows and doors... :) THE DESIGN: I based my room layout on John's Small studio (first or second post of the design section). I kept the proportions relatively the same, except for the lenght, which is a bit longer. I added a vocal booth and rearranged the doors and windows. Haven't yet decided if I will wall mount my speakers... INITIAL PROBLEMS: 1) The entrance doors open into the room. This I cannot change, so I have to come up with a solution which enables me to leave enough space for opening entrance doors and bringing in gear. 2) windows are placed at such positions that I had to reduce the area of the vocal booth. 3) not satisfied with the position of doors that lead from the CR to the live room (I would like to have a slot resonator there), but find no other solution. BUDGET: I'm thinking of spending between 3000-4000€ for this project. I am doing most of the work myself with friends (wall construction, caulking, plastering, electrical...) and have access to large amounts of cheap wood (hey, there have to be some advantages of practically living in a forest.. :)), so most of my expenses will go into floors (If I could keep some of the existing laminate floors, that would be great), insulation, cloth and doors. TIME SCHEDULE: I would like to start the renovations in the spring (April probably). I want to get the thing done by the end of June. I would appreciate all comments and suggestions. Thank you in advance and all the best Alee, Slovenia

Soundman202024 August 2010#9
You are missing one inner-leaf wall! At the front of your room, behind the speaker soffits, there is no wall there :shock: The rear of the speaker soffits faces the outer-leaf directly You need to fix that.
but I am not sure if I can exchange Lenght and Width so freely.... I guess the modes stay the same, only their direction changes??
Not a problem: Sound waves don't care which way "up" is. So you can interchange width / height if you need to.
But the Live room wall has no insulation attached.
It should have! There MUST be insulation on the cavity, or you will not have good isolation between the rooms! I'd also suggest that you re-arrange your room, since the geometry is not making any sense: John did that design for a specific case, but yours is different. I would modify things so that the leaves of the dividing wall between CR and LR fall on each side of that beam. In other words, the beam will be in the air gap between the rooms, and the wall will be straight, not angled (or perhaps still angled for the front half, the straight for the rear half). If that takes too up too much space, then leave the beam totally out of the CR and partly in the LR, and build a soffit around it. The beam should not touch the wall at all. Regarding the beam in the middle of the CR: The best idea is probably to soffit around it, if you need good isolation to the rest of the building. Here again, the beam should not touch the walls or the soffit. You also need to straighten out the left rear wall in the CR: It does not need to be angled like that in your case: you are wasting space like that. - Stuart -

gullfo19 September 2010#14
while you're cogitating... there's no reason you cannot build a rectangular room with proper ratios and isolation, and build in the treatments using the space between the angled treatment walls and the isolation walls... in the attached example, the inner slider door (and inner window) backs onto an isolation space which cuts into the otherwise rectangular room, this allows the slider to be angled and proper isolation maintained. this approach means you can start with the untreated isolation room and build out as your budget allows. plus later if you need to sell, your rooms are readily converted back to "normal" rooms or home theaters etc to improve the resale value. just a thought...
