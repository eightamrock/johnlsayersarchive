I have 3 narrow window openings in my control room entrance door. This door is being built in the style of Rod's "superdoor" - a single door with a jam that spans both inner and out wall. The windows will need to be custom made, and Im wondering if I should use a thick (.5" or greater) single pane window, or a double pane window comprised of 1/4" and 3/8" panes with a 1" air gap? The glass shop I went to recommended the double pane window, but I have my doubts. Wouldnt the sound just flank right around the air gap in the single door construction?
MightyM11 July 2008#52
I've installed 2 runs of 2" PVC electrical conduit to get my audio cables from the control room to the iso booth, one run at each end of the room. These runs will drop in from the ceiling into the area where my corner bass absorbers will be built. I'm planning on building my interconnect panels into the face of the bass absorbers. Both runs have a 3/8" break in the conduit mid run to prevent any flanking from room to room.

MightyM11 July 2008#53
I've completed one of two glass block panel windows that will go in the control room in front of the existing basement window. These will open french door style if I want to get some direct fresh air into the room. I plan on using the same GM K seals on the windows as I'm using on the doors. I have a feeling these glass block windows may not give the best isolation, but should be adequate for my needs, and give me a look that I will be happy with. The window will be backlit to provide some ambiance. The jam still needs to be fabricated, so I placed the completed panel in the rough opening just to get an idea of what it might look like.

Following your build Mighty, and congrats on the beautiful new addition to the family. Quite understandable why studio progress is slow. For all the right reasons. MightyM11 December 2008#60
Thanks Wayne! I was planninng on using a fish tape to pull the audio wiring through the conduit. I'm thinking that it will be stiff enough not to get hung up at the break in the conduit. Even if I put a coupling around it, I think there will still be the exposed lip of the pipes that could snag the wires. I think I'll run the fish tape through a few times before I close things up with drywall, just to make sure it wont be a problem. Thanks for the tip!

MightyM13 July 2009#67
Next up - framing out the corner bass absorbers. The absorber in the corner with the sump pump will have a hinged door to allow access. This will also make it handy to service the connector panel that will be mounted in the face of the absorber as well. My plan is to use 4" 703 (or equivalent) in the frames, leaving the rest of the cavity open. If I find I need more absorption, I can add additional insulation in the form of hangers/super chunks later on.

MightyM13 July 2009#69
In addition to/part of the studio build, I have been working on a new DAW computer to go in the studio when its finished. My trusty AMD Athlon 3400 laptop has performed admirably for the last few years, but ProTools has an insatiable appetite for more and more horsepower, so it was time to upgrade. The new rig is an Intel i7 920 mounted in a 4 space rackmount case with a fanless video card, 6 GB DDR3-1600 RAM, 3 500GB SATA hard drives, a hot-swappable 3.5" SATA trayless drive bay, and a media card reader with an eSATA port and external power ports.
