Soundman202024 March 2009#11
Hi "Zig"! I just noticed your "edited" and updated first post. A couple of comments that might or might not be useful:
I don't have a db reading but best guess based on other drum readings is somewhere in the 100db range.
It's probably more than that: I can't recall ever measuring a drummer playing normally at less than about 105 dB(C), and most are more like 110 to 115. Even higher when they get "enthusiastic"! :) So you might want to raise your bar a little, in terms of how much isolation you'll need. 115 inside to "barely audible" outside means you need to kill around 60 dB, at least, and probably more like 70. Do-able, but requires careful planning ant attention to detail. You already linked to Sharward's epic thread, so you know what is in store for you! Do what he has been doing, and you'll get there.
Since the garage is finished with drywall I would like to build a room within a room, using two existing walls and ceiling for the "outer" room (adding two additional walls), with the actual practice room within.
Yup. That's definitely the way to go.
As I understand from this site and Rod's book, the best solution would be to remove the existing layer of drywall, adding 1-2 layers of drywall to the plywood between the joists and then insulation on top of that.
In your case, I would go with at least 2 layers of 5/8 drywall between the studs, and maybe even three layers. Or four. If you really do need maximum isolation, then mass is your friend, and you needs lots of it.
My concern with this method is the electrical and water hazards lurking inside the wall and how to deal with these and pass inspection. Suggestions?
My guess would be that the electrical stuff will be mostly vertical, up and down,. in the immediate vicinity of the distribution panel. You probably won't find very much (if any) running horizontally, so your problem are should be just one small vertical section. However, you'll need to get the existing drywall off to find out for sure, and since there might potentially be wires anywhere in that wall, you will need to work carefully. Don't just attack it with an axe or a huge huge saw! Once you get off a few pieces, it should be obvious if the wiring goes all over, or is just vertical. The water meter should just be a couple of pipes. Personally, I think I'd be inclined to leave the electrics alone as much as possible, and just block out that one section, maybe as some kind of closet. Moving your entire distribution panel would be great, but won't be cheap, and won't be easy. I'd suggest that you try to build around it. But in the case of the water meter, I think I'd try to move that completely out of the studio, if at all possible. That's just my gut feeling on what I'd do if it were my build. Hopefully others will chime in with their own ideas!
2) The ceiling is 5/8 drywall attached directly to the ceiling joists (empty uninsulated space above garage). My thought here is to add insulation between the joists on top of the existing drywall and additional layers of drywall to the underside. Reasonable?
As your inner-leaf, or your outer leaf? I guess you mean outer leaf? either way, you should check "loads and spans": make sure that the joists can handle the extra load of three or four additional layers of drywall. If that is your outer-leaf, then you are talking about making your ceiling three-leaf, since I guess there is also a roof up above it somewhere. I recall a thread recently by Rod, in which he said (I'm paraphrasing) that a 3-leaf roof is a concern, but the roof does have to be ventilated, so there aren't too many options, and you just have to live with what you've got. So if your roof is going to be your weak "3-leaf" point, then you might consider making the ceiling as massive as you possibly can, in which case your existing joists might not be up to the task. Your basic plan of a MAM "room-within-a-room" sounds fine, with decoupled leaves on separate frames, but isolating drums is a tough job, so I'd suggest that you think about putting some serious mass into those leaves. Consider using several layers of drywall, plus abundant Green Glue, 24" O.C. studs (to help keep the resonance frequency down low), large air-gaps inside your wall (several inches), and abundant insulation in the cavity. Sealed and caulked to the limit. Drums are noisy beasts, and hard to tame, so you need to do everything you possibly can if you want to attain the level of isolation that you need. - Stuart -

Soundman202025 March 2009#19
So building a room, within the garage, with 4 staggered stud walls.
Like Ian said, if you did that, you would have a 4-leaf system, which would be terrible for drums. You would have the existing 2 leaves of you present garage walls, plus the 2 new leaves on the staggered stud wall. 4 leaves is bad. You'd have to add a huge amount of mass to compensate for the lost low frequency isolation. I guess it could work if you did something like five or six layers of sheetrock on each side of your staggered studs, and on each side of your new two.leaf ceiling. But you'd probably need massive joists and studs to handle all that weight. - Stuart -
