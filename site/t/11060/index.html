John Hile2 September 2008#14
In your first post you mentioned:
Then I would hang 1 layer of 5/8" drywall followed by 1 layer of 1/2" rubber sound dampening material and another layer of 5/8" drywall.
You may want to consider loosing the 1/2" rubber and use a layer of 1/2" drywall instead, You would then want to take advantage of having 2 damping layers. You would have more mass in your ceiling which is good, and it may save you a bit of money as well. If ceiling height is an issue, you may want to use RSIC-V Clips and Hat Channel for decoupling, which when put together descends 1-1/8" from the joist and would hold 3 layers of drywall. For RSIC Clips you will want to use 7/8" Hat Channel 25 gauge, also known as Drywall Furring Strips. You want to get the 2.5" wide channel. John

John Hile4 November 2008#18
Hi eightamrock, You are referring to staggering the seams in the installation of your drywall. This works great as the only main seams that you will have in the end will be the parameter. Even though you are using 4x4s you still want to stagger. As far as lifting the drywall off of the floor goes. I would say to let it go all the way to the floor. Usually when you do this there is a little crack around the parameter that you could stick a butter knife into but this is fine. You are going to construct a wall with 3 panels and Green Glue so your wall will be heavy and damped. The thought behind the 1/4" gap is that it allows the panel to flex a bit more. Great for a lab situation, but in the real world with that mass loaded damped wall you will not notice a difference. Then you can attach your molding. I don't think you will need the backer rods and you can use an acoustical caulk to seal the parameters. Make sure that the drywall goes up nicely against the door frames as well. If not doing sound isolation, molding will cover imperfections, but since this is sound isolation, you'll want to stop the sound from finding little flanking paths such as these. Your ceiling assembly is great. The only change I would make is to use all 5/8" drywall (as opposed to using the 1/2" panel) Unless you are concerned about the loss of the little more space. I don't think there is much cost difference and you will get more mass (= better performance) I hope this helped John
