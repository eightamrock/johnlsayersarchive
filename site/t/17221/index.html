The first thing you would want to do is get your hands on Alton Everests' "Masterhand book of Acoustics" Page 276, "The popularity of rectangular room is due in part to economy of construction, but it has its acoustical advantages." He goes on "...The relative length, width and height of a sound sensitive room is most important." Further on he states, "Cubical rooms are anathema." Your room is one dimension away from being "anathema" my friend :) I would say that if you are trying to replicate something from "back in the day" then a square room might be a good fit, since the advances in acoustics that we each currently enjoy, were not available to everyone so readily back in the 60's. I want to say that Sun Records had a square room that they recorded in, but it could have been any number of small "shops" that sold records and recorded folks as a means to make ends meet. But to know what you have versus what you "can" do, versus how truly do you want to replicate a room that has so much potential to sound like shit based on old ideas...hell, throw carpet on the walls and call it a day :) You will also want to read thru John Sayers online guide, a few things that might not be recommended by me, but John is the man here and I thing he knows what he is talking about.
