Thanks. My Events are passive. I have a full picture diary but no time to post all of it. Just follow the general advise of the people here and you will be ok. To me the important thing was the super chunks. In small rooms the general problem is the big wave build up since the room is too small to let them move out and away. It was easier for me to just stack up the rock wool rather that build hangers or whatever. My ceiling is basicaly one big super chunk as is the intire corner above the entrance door and the wall corner in the back. Also calculat the listening position and the angles of the sofit carefully as there will be a great bit of difference in sound as you move away from that position. even a foot back from the listening position as a great difference from being right on it. So make sure you calucate your listening position just right.

gareth.h.rees wrote:
. I was going to go with a simple cloud, but have also been thinking of a similar idea to what you've made. Do you find yours to have a major advantage over a cloud? Also, did you tune the slats to a specific frequency?
I was going to build a cloud too. But, having looked at all the studios I work in, I decided to do what they did. Almost all of them slant the ceiling. Besides it was just easier to build. If the idea is to reflect the frequncies to the back of the room then slanting the ceiling kinda made sense. Yes, I think it is an advantage because it probably works better, is more effective at absorbing the lows and was easier to build. I built the frame and stuffed it with rockwool. I then stetched the fabrik and screwed the slats. Persoanly I did not worry about tuning. All I knew is that my room size had major build ups at around 60 cycles. I felt the best way to absorb them was superchunks. Personaly I don't tink the variance in slat distance does anything, If I had made them all the same distance I don't think anything would have changed. After building the room and mixing in it is seems abvious to me that the general concepts are more important than the details. So my ceiling is a super chunk broadband slat! hahaha It works!! Now that I see how effective superchunks are at absorbing ultra lows, my tracking room is going to get a 2' x 2' super chunk around the intire perimiter of the ceiling. I see in your drawing you have half hangers and half chunk in your ceiling corner. Is that the back of the room? If it is then yes that is almost identical to mine. I also filled that intire space with rockwool. So have a super chunk there is well. As well as the left side corner. Where your gobo is I just filled the frame with rockwool 4 inches. BTW, my speakers are in concrete boxes. I heard some one here say they did that so I did it too.very tuff to make but well worth it!

Hey eruss, If you're still around could you just give me one pointer? Did you put the neoprene INSIDE the concrete box for the speaker itself to sit on, or did you put the actual concrete box ON the neoprene? Thanks, Gareth
I put the concrete box ON the neoprine. You want the box to be as tight around the speaker as you can. But, a word of experience> make sure you have a way to adjust the box so you can have it set level. You don't want your speakers sitting at an angle pointed to the ceiling or off kilter in any way. I actualy put shims in the back of my box to make the speakers sit level. If you build all this right, yuo are going to be one happy canper when you get done.
