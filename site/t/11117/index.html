medboyle21 August 2008#6
I have found that the best way to get help is to start by doing some of the planning yourself, When i started i didn't understand the room placements, ratios etc (Proof of this is in my early drawings!) But i did try to layout the space as i saw it then everyone else came onboard with suggestions and i slowly got to grips with the basics. No one likes being told this but you really have to take time and read as much of the forum as you can, if you can draw up a plan of how you see the space working it shows you are thinking about your build, The forum is about learning and shared experience but you have to be seen to be putting in the effort first. It can be daunting faced with the huge amount of information on the forum but if you start with the stickies in each section you will soon start to pick up the information you need. if you can i would also suggest buying Rod's book as it was invaluable to me when i was building. Cheers Alan

MightyM25 August 2008#15
Perhaps the lack of help stems from the fact that that attic looks pretty scary! That brick chimney scares the hell out me! No way you'll catch me sitting under that with the bass pounding! Seriously though - how much $ and time are you willing to spend? Theres a ton of work to be done there if you want decent isolation (sound proofing) from and to the outside world. My studio build is 60% done at the two year and $5k mark, and I started with a blank slate in a basement. You have many more obstacles to deal with in room like that attic. If I were you, I would focus on improving the acoustics by building a variety of absorbers. This will give you the most bang for the buck. Check out Ethan Winer's site at http://www.realtraps.com/videos.htm for some good info on absorbers. You may want to put your speakers at the far end (away from the stairs) of the A frame room. Wall off the L shaped area and make it a vocal booth. Take a stab at a floor plan layout, and I'm sure that will generate some feedback. Patience is your friend.....
