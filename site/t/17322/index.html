Hi Donal. welcome. Please check the forum rules for posting: You seem to be missing something... :) On the Green Glue question: You don't need it. An extra layer of drywall is about as effective as the GG, except perhaps in the very low end. So if an extra layer of drywall all around your room is cheaper than the case of GG, then do that. If the GG is cheaper, then do GG. But why do you say that you'd do drywall will GG or "acoustic slab-board" without it? I don't see the connection: Mass is mass, and sound waves don't care how much you paid for it. They only react to the mass, regardless of the price tag. So go with the cheapest form of high-density mass you can find. In most countries, that usually turns out to be plain old ordinary fire-rated drywall. So unless that "acoustic slab-board" has more mass for the dollar (higher kg/m3) than drywall, then forget it. There's a lot of products out there on the market that make claims about high levels of isolation, but the fact is that the laws of physics are not subject to magical incantations: Sound waves are stopped by mass, period. There's nothing you can do to mass to make it stop sound waves better, except to add more mass or use it in a tuned MSM system. There are no magical products in acoustics that somehow bypass the laws of physics. (Green Glue sounds magical, but isn't: it works on the principle of constrained layer damping, and it does work, but once again there's a cost/benefit thing here: is it cheaper to add enough mass to get the same effect as the crate of GG, or not?.)
would the 2 sheets of BlueBoard costs a lot less.
I don't know what "BlueBoard" is, but it's easy to figure out which product is better: How much does it cost per kilogram? That's the bottom line. The only time you might want to go with a more expensive product is if you are very tight on space and need to put the same amount of mass in a smaller volume, to save an inch or two of room width. In that case, you could go with something exotic like steel plate o lead sheeting, which are extremely high density, but just a tad more expensive... :)
Which is preferable in terms of acoustics and sound isolation, A PVC door OR Solid timber door for entry and exit.
Solid core wood is what you want. Once again, it's a simple matter of mass. If your door has lots of mass, it will attenuate sound well. If it doesn't, then it wont. There are no shortcuts, unfortunately! The key to good isolation is mass, mass and more mass, plus total airtight seals around both leaves of the room.
Inside the 2 concrete layers of 4" block. This is what is planned. 4" gap. 4" stud partition, filled with rockwool. Then.... 2 sheets of standard 5/8" drywall ...
