Ultimate Acoustics...Mountain-Top?

Started by darkecho on 29 July 2008. 14 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 11042.

darkecho29 July 2008#1
Hello everyone.. I have been searching high and low for a great sounding room for drums.. I tried various rooms in various homes, in rehearsal spaces, yesterday I walked around, a one-man-marching band with a snare in hand banging and listening as I walked through a factory.. I have listened to many spaces and none of them offer me what I want as far as a good acoustic space. I can't afford a studio room, and I can't afford a whole lot in the form of acoustic materials. So I was thinking... since most acoustic panels seem to serve the purpose of absorbing sound (so it doesnt reflect), or diffusing sound (so it scatters), why not record in a room with as few reflective surfaces as physically possible... in fact, a room with no walls... or... a mountain top! On the top of a mountain, you only have the ground to worry about- youve got several thousand miles of airspace above you- no reflections there... depending on how high you are, the only reflections will be from neighboring mountains, probably hundreds of feet away.. If you find the right mountain, and you bring a laptop.. wouldn't the acoustics rock? (no pun intended)

I think it could work very well, assuming the weather cooperates. There is often wind on the top of mountains (at least here in Maine, the only place on the east coast of the US where mountains run right up to the Atlantic Ocean). If the wind cooperates, you should get great results. The drums will be much louder than background noise, probably about as great a difference as tracking drums in an average home studio room with AC (unless it is very well implemented AC). The acoustics will be interesting.... should be very clear with no reflections (except from the ground).
andrebrito31 July 2008#13
If you want to know how it will sound it is easy,,, just get some drum samples, the driest possible, go to your sequencer and make a simple beat. Bad acoustics is something we must avoid but a room with good acoustics is way better than free field behavior. It sounds sterile. Also you will have possible wind problems, birds make a lot of noise, possible airplanes... and even echoes problems due to the hard surface of the mountains and peaks/valleys topography Just to give an idea since I also do environmental measurements of outside noise.. A quiet environment in the countryside is usually around 30-35 dB(A). In a professional studio, the background noise is way below that value.

I think andrebrito hit the pertinent part. Don't assume that because reflections can cause problems that a) all reflections are bad, and b) eliminating all reflections will be the ultimate sound. Unless of course that is the sound that pleases you - dry and isolated. But generally, humans seem to like to hear a certain amount of reflection. Otherwise all hit records would be recorded in an anechoic chamber. But I do like someone who thinks outside the box. You're right that we get too used to the conventional ways of doing things. Besides, those late reflections off of the neighboring mountains has got to have a cool sound!
