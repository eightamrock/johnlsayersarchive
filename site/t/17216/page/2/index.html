Soundman202024 May 2012#30
does anyone have 5 min to look my framing.
It looks good in general but a couple of comments: Sealing. There seem to be large gaps under the sole plate in several places. It looks like the tiles are very uneven. It is very, very important that you seal those as well as possible, from both sides. Acoustic caulk is the answer, forced in deeply to those cracks.
Is it better to fill the whole cavity with the insulation OR to leave an air gap between leaves?
Fill it completely if you can! That will gain you the maximum isolation. However, there might be fire code regulations that you have to comply with, so check your local building code first. If you are allowed to fill completely, then do that, but be careful that you don't "overfill". You don't want to force or compress the isolation to get it in: use just enough to fill the cavity, no more, and don't push it in.
Do we have to compress the insulation between the leaves and if yes, how hard can i compress it?
None! No compression. If you compress it, you run the risk of creating a "bridge" or "flanking path". It should just lightly touch the wall surface on each side, so don't force it in.
i've used 2x4 studs even at the back of the boxes. i will also caulk these boxes. Do you think i should also put some layer of drywall at the back / on the side of these boxes.
That should work fine, provided that they are sealed. Are you going to use conduit for your cables?
The goal is to put three layers of drywall. What sould be the best sequence for you? PB,PB,GG,PB PB,GG,PB,PB PB,GG,PB (maybe the third layer is not usefull with GG...)
Contact Green Glue company and ask them. They also do have some guidelines, instructions and suggestions on their web site: look at those carefully. There might be more info there.
Here in belgium (even in europa) it's impossible the find acoustic caulk. Should i use standard silicone or this kind of product could be better?
Any good quality flexible caulk will work, provided that it is very flexible and always remains flexible, even when fully cured. Also, get one that sticks really well to all of the surfaces that you plan to use: in your case, you need one that sticks very well to both ceramic tile and wood. Get a white or colored caulk, rather than a transparent one, as they are more dense, which is good. I'd suggest that you put up the ceiling framing and do all the electrical and HVAC installation, so we can see how all of that is done before you do the dry-walling. It's much easier to see and fix problems with the HVAC ducting or electrical installation BEFORE you put the drywall on! - Stuart -

RJHollins25 June 2012#40
Another vouch for caulk ! Save yourself time & money ... buy it by the case .... several cases :shock: I lost track how many I went through :| Speaking of caulk ... AND 'backer rod' [another item purchased in quantity]. As it worked out for my build, the scheduling had several days [at times] before the next step. Which I was very happy about BECAUSE, even with high quality caulking, you may find a section or two where the caulk settled, and leaves a gap. Especially when I was caulking at the floor to drywall area. The drywall being 5/8" thick, with a gap at least a 1/4"+, it difficult to see underneath :roll: I ended up using a small hand mirror that I could angle to view the seal along the floor as I crawled along. Point being ... after caulking, be sure to triple check your work after several days ... you may find a surprise !! which WILL compromise all the work. [As Stuart points out, we are to build an airtight room ... a small air gap is all it takes 'for all the water to leak out']. Not quite the proper quote, but still very important. Sincerely.

No, you do not need a dead room. That would be bad. Dead is unpleasant. You need a neutral room, with an even response in both the time domain and the frequency domain.
