jwl15 April 2007#1
Greetings all, my wife and I are now under contract to purchase our new home, which has a separate one-car garage on a concrete slab, measuring approximately 12x20. My goals for the studio are to create a cozy yet comfortable space for making music, with good-sounding room(s), and good isolation. Neighbors are not much of a factor (we will have 2 acres), but my wife and daughter go to bed early and I am a night owl. I need to be able to work on music at night without disturbing anyone in my house. I play mostly acoustic guitar, and my electric rig is based on amp modelling, so I won't get crazy loud at night. Any full-band tracking sessions can be scheduled carefully so as to not bother anyone, but ideally I'd like to have enough isolation that it won't matter. I do have Rod's book (and a few others), and I've been reading up on this and similar forums for a few years now. I read regularly, and contribute as often as I can where I feel I can help out. I feel like I have a solid layman's understanding of acoustics and studio construction, enough that I am confident to begin work on my space. Knowing that the experts here are available for any glitches that come up is a great comfort to me. As far as the building itself goes: There is a garage door on the east wall of the building, a normal door on the south wall, and windows on the west and north walls. As soon as I am able I will post photographs and a diagram. My goal is to teach myself to use Google Sketchup by drawing out my space in as much detail as I can. In a room of this size, I'm currently debating whether or not to use a one-room design, or to split it into a control room, a live room, and a vocal booth. There are of course advantages and disadvantages to both approaches. And if I do choose a multiroom studio, I will need to design it very, very carefully because it's hard to get small rooms to sound good. I definitely want it to sound good. Lately I've been digging on the one-room concept, the current studio I run (not much more than a corner of a basement with a bit of acoustic treatment) is a one-room design. At the moment, there is no electricity or HVAC of any kind. I'm debating on whether or not I want AC since I live in Maine and might only want AC for a month or two out of the year, if that. I'm not worried about heat; a portable space heater will probably be adequate given the amount of insulation in the room. A friend of mine is pushing me to investigate geothermal designs, apparently where you dig a hole into the earth and bring cool air up from there. I dig the whole off-the-grid ethos, so I'll check it out for sure. I'd love to have some alternate power sources for the studio as well (solar?). I don't have a specific budget in mind. Short of an unexpected financial windfall, this work is going to occur gradually over a long period of time. As in, "hey, I have $50... that's 5 sheets of drywall!!" LOL I plan to do all of the work myself with friend-helpers (as much as I can, anyway.... I'll get a licensed electrician to wire the breaker box, etc). I really enjoy this sort of work and am eager to build a high-quality studio that works well with my own hands. I want to document everything thoroughly in the design and construction of this studio. This first post will get things underway, even though there aren't a lot of details here yet.

OK, I have more thoughts about the one-room design vs. the control room/live room/vocal booth design. I should warn people in advance. I tend to either a)wax philosophical or b)spew constant streams of bullshit depending on your perspective. So be warned. I'm verbose, but I try to be clear. Certainly I mean well. :twisted: Basically, the real question is this. Can a control room and a live room, each roughly 8x12 with splayed walls, sound good? If so, then I think I want to build 3 rooms. If not, then I'll go for the one room design. By good, I mean a well balanced control room, perhaps on the dead side (my own preference, especially in a smaller control room), with bass nodes under control and strong imaging with soffited speakers (I will be soffit mounting a pair of passive speakers I build years ago back when Dynaudio sold woofers and tweeters). I want my live room to sound AMAZING with acoustic guitar and drums. Not huge, but just... good. Resonant in all the right places. I plan on building this room using only slot resonators as acoustic treatment.... I'm intrigued by Len Morgan's comments about how differently-tuned slot resonators in his room affect the tone of the recording. I want a really nice room like this, so I want to think carefully about room shape, resonances, resonator tuning, etc. Many people argue for a one large room design (ie, see the EQ article with the studio plans by Ethan Winer and Wes Lachot), but I know that many studios on this forum use small rooms. If I do go with a one-room design, I'd like to incorporate slot resonators like Len's. But it seems to me that it'd be difficult to do that well in a control room, you want a control room to be as even and symmetrical as possible.... Opinions? Thoughts?
gullfo18 April 2007#3
i'd go with the single room that sounds good rather than struggle trying to get 2 or more small rooms sounding good. besides, if you need to divide it up later because you need an iso booth, you can do that later. i'd also start out with inside-out walls to give you the isolation and the beginning of the treatments. instead of just making them flat walls though I'd angle them to get the symmetry and reflections under control. the back wall by the main garage door could be an absorption wall with a poly diffuser to keep it from being dead. i'd remove the garage door and frame it inside-out to add more air space between the exterior wall and interior (also inside out) wall. should be plenty of isolation for your needs. put up a small shed for garden tools and the lawn tractor :-) edit: now that i've seen photos... i'd just insulate the interior walls (you'll need that in ME :-)) and go with the inside-out wall approach. i think it will be easier to construct than trying to beef up between each stud on the existing walls. on the ceiling - if you could beef the ceiling up and keep the open space over the trusses that would be great but if not, again insulate the roof space and drywall on the ceiling joists. if you have someone with a drum kit who can swing by and beat on them you can take some measurements (once you close on the property :-)) or some loud PA type equipment... if you're thinking commerical use of the space the extra meter and separate line works, but if you can upgrade your electric to 200 amp service and then just bury a conduit from the house to the garage it may be better in the long run. get a qoute from the electric company and the electrician to determine costs versus someday wanting to upgrade the house mains...

Lately, I've been really digging having a band set up and recording the entire band with room mics as well as close mics anyway. Thus far I've got a design sketched out on graph paper (still trying to learn Sketchup... grrrrr) that is similar to Gullfo's design. The biggest difference are that there are 3 slot resonators on each side, that can easily be constructed in 4x8 modular inside-out wall sections. I can tune each "pair" of slot resonators to different frequencies by slightly varying the internal construction, though from the outside they will all appear to be identical. In addition, the rear wall will probably NOT have a poly diffusor. I have a design for a large rear absorber assembly with shelves behind it; absorbers work better spaced off the wall, so I figure I'd put each absorber on a hinge and have them be "doors" that cover shelves and possibly a pegboard storage area (for cables, etc). In terms of HVAC, I'm leaning more and more toward just good ventilation, with a fan assembly for air exchange. I am in Maine, so I don't think I'll need AC; it will be hot for a few months out of the year, but oh well. I'm not a big fan of air conditioners in general, mostly the amount of power they suck and their negative environmental effects. So I'd prefer to avoid them if at all possible. That said, with all the insulation in the room, I'll definitely want to make sure I can get plenty of fresh air moving in the room. jwl3 July 2007#22
OK, attached is a re-sized JPG scan of my graph paper floorplan. I'm not the neatest drafter out there by any stretch, but the sketch is pretty close to accurate size-wise (each square = 6"). The entire inner shell will be built from pseudo-modular inside-out walls, slot resonators in the front half of the room, and broadband absorbers in the rear half. The rear wall will also have 3 diffusors, 2'x4', that will be on hinges, acting as doors that cover recessed shelves for storage (mics, etc). I'm not convinced on the diffusors.... I may build some, the Realtraps diffusors look interesting, or I may just go with absorption. The window that's on the north wall of the garage (the long wall at the top), will be removed and plugged, and I'll re-use the window between the soffits. For rear corner absorption, I will use a superchunks-like design, but rather than cut triangles I'll just put 6 batts of 24" R19 ultratouch in. There are 2 angled slot resonators on each side (top and bottom), but they are built slightly differently. The first one is built as one large chamber, the second one is divided into 2 smaller chambers. This will give me 3 different volumes (ie, resonance frequencies) to work with. I'll probalby calculate the room modes and tune the slot resonators to the room modes. For the soffits, I will probalby also run HVAC ducts behind them. The speakers will probably rest on cinderblock/concrete assembly so it is coupled to the floor, and I will build a slot resonator around it. OK, there it is for your perusal.... kinda sloppy.
