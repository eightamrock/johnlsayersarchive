Soundman202025 September 2008#3
Hi Jetbase. That Kinetics flooring stuff does not seem like a good idea, as their web site says it will leave an air space under your floor. That means it will "float" your floor, which is probably NOT a good idea. You might want to read this thread about floating floors: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8173 In order to do it right, you need lots of mass on top, and two layers of plywood is not going to be anywhere near close. More like several inches of solid reinforced concrete! You also need to calculate everything carefully, to ensure that you get the right compression, mass, etc. and that it ends up tuned to frequencies well below the range of human hearing. Basically, if you float your floor and don't get it exactly right, it will be WORSE than not floating your floor at all. Regarding the space that you show in your photos: You should at least post the dimensions, and much better would be to post a 3D SketchUp model, for the experts to get a better idea of what you have. Just posting two photos does not help much!

xSpace26 September 2008#9
" I’m just looking for ideas on how to approach the floor" /quote Hi jetbase. So right to the point. you have a reason for looking at the floor? It's loud? It leaks? Have you measured anything to evaluate your present isolation requirements? Floating floors are difficult because mass is the only thing that sound yields to. Walls, floors, ceilings, windows, doors, et al require mass to contain or isolate from noise. If you have Rods book and/or somewhere around this website there is information on beefing up ceilings. That is what you may need to look into. I know you say floor, but in the final analysis, it's the basement ceiling beefing up information that can help you :) You need to get below the floor where you are. Get out of the room and under the structure so you can look up and make assessment of the ceiling that is over your head. In order to add mass to this leaf of your room, the first place to start is underneath it to determine what you are about to get yourself into :) Hope this helps.
