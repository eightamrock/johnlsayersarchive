welcome! you might read up on the floating floor idea before trying it as a wooden floating floor is not your best option in most cases. if you can get the contractor to separate the newly screed concrete floor from the walls and building that can help although not as much as a fully decoupled floor. you can mix and match isolation walls as long as you're adhering to the double leaf (MSM) approach to avoid triple (or more) leafs and realize that some absorption on the drywall walls will be different than the brick or block walls and could skew the room response. since you're already planning on some drywall type walls, why not just do that all around? just make sure any existing walls are proper to make the final result a double leaf. plan on your ventilation/HVAC from the start to avoid surprises (not the good ones) later.

Not necessarily. If done right, it could even help! BTW "soundproofing" is a bit of a misnomer: you cannot "soundproof" anything, strictly speaking. Any sufficiently loud sound will be heard on the other side of even your best "soundproofing" wall. Find the best "soundproof" studio in the world, and detonate a few kg of explosives inside: I guarantee that you will hear it quite clearly outside! OK, so that's extreme, but a better term is "isolation" or "attenuation" of sound.
Also, if I've started with the MAM wall do I have to do the whole room this way, or can I have the 2 interior walls (as drawn) with 75mm studs with insulation (the soundproofing back in to the house is less of a concern than the external leakage). Or will this undo my MAM walls by providing a 'flanking path'?
The basic rule with MAM (more correctly called "MSM", for Mass-Spring-Mass) is that you have to do the entire room to the same level, all around. At the very least, all four walls and the ceiling must be done to the same level. I often use the analogy of an aquarium to explain this: If you build an aquarium with glass in two walls, carpet on another wall, paper on the fourth wall, and cardboard for the floor, how well will that hold water? In order to get your aquarium to hold water, you have to put glass on all sides, properly sealed. There are no shortcuts. The same happens with your room: all your walls and your ceiling must be isolated to the same level. You can't do some sides better or worse than others, just because you noticed that the neighbors are closer on that side, or because you started out with a poorer wall on that side. All of them have to end up at the same level. I know that doesn't really answer your question yet, but we'll get back to the way of doing that later, maybe in another post.
