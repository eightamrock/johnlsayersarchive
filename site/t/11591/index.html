Soundman202017 January 2009#2
WOW! 3" lean? It must have been a Chilean craftsman who built that wall! :) Anyway, this is just the wall up the side of the stairwell, right? The problem side is not actually inside your studio? Where you have those studs is the outside surface of your outer leaf, right? If so, then do you really need those studs? Would it not just be easier to plaster that wall back out to vertical WITHOUT the studs there? With a 3" difference to take care of, I guess you'll need some kind of wire mesh to get the shape and grip for the mortar, but it seems to me that it would be better in the long run to use plaster to build it back out to plumb, and use that plaster surface as your final finish surface, instead of sheetrock on studs. Just one suggestion. But the best guy to help you with that is probably Brien (xSpace). - Stuart -

Soundman202017 January 2009#4
If you are concerned about the structural integrity of the wall, then I'd really, really suggest that you hire a structural engineer to look at it. ( I was kind of thinking that if it's already unsound, then maybe all that mortar would make it STRONGER, anyway, not weaker! ). One other question: Is that a load-bearing wall? Or is it just a divider wall? If it is not doing anything useful structurally, and poses a danger due to the "drunken-lean", then another option might be to just knock it out, and replace it with a proper two-leaf stud wall... Spending a few days knocking it out might actually be easier than trying to patch it up. But for that kind of issue, I think you'd be far better off getting some professional advice from someone who can actually go in there and inspect it properly. I wouldn't rely on a few comments made about photos on a in internet forum, for that kind of thing. - Stuart -
