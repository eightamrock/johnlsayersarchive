Soundman20203 January 2012#3
Hi Hansen, and welcome! I agree with Brien: I wouldn't do the floor like that either. If you have a good concrete floor, then just leave it like that! Acoustically, concrete is a good surface for a floor. Or if you don't like the look of concrete, then lay laminate flooring on top of it.
Then 350mm Polystyrene. ... Same goes for the walls and ceiling.
Polystyrene is closed-cell foam, and therefore is practically useless acoustically. A drum kit is the most difficult instrument to isolate, due to the high levels (115 dB, easily), and low frequencies. To isolate, you need mass, all around the room: walls, floor, ceiling, windows, doors. The best way to achieve high levels of isolation at a reasonable cost, is with a two-leaf system, just as you see in John's diagram that you posted: In that case, the outer leaf is brick, and the inner leaf is two layers of 16mm drywall. As you can see, there is no connection between the inner leaf and outer leaf. The ceiling is done the same way: outer leaf and inner leaf, with no connections. Now, the amount of mass that you will need, and the amount of separation that you will need between the leaves, will be determined by your numbers: How much isolation do you need? (in decibels). What is the lowest frequency that you need to isolate?

There's a huge difference between "loudest part" and "part most difficult to isolate"! Isolating 3 kHz to 55 dB TL is a piece of cake. Isolating 60 Hz to 55 dB TL is practically impossible (and unnecessary, too). Now, since you are talking about drums, I'm guessing that bass guitar also plays a part here? If so, specifying 60 Hz as your lowest frequency of interest is probably optimistic.
Are there any resources about that anywhere? Someone measuring a drumkit being played in a studio?
I've done that myself, many times! None of them scientifically valid, of course, as I did that in various places, with various drums, mics, drummers, etc., and never in an independent acoustic lab. But for what it's worth, levels of 115 dB are common, and 118 isn't out of the question. Adding a bass guitar can get you pushing 120. Much of that is in the low end of the spectrum, and as I mentioned before, isolating sounds above 2 KHz is nowhere near as hard as isolating sounds below 200 Hz.
That's where I'd like someone to give me some good ideas to the construction of a room that will work for me
For possible construction methods that will get you 55 dB of isolation, I'd suggest starting with IR-761 for walls, IR-766 for floors, and IR-802 for a better understanding of impact noise. I'd also suggest "Master Handbook of Acoustics" by F. Alton Everest. (That's sort of considered to be the "bible" of acoustics.) And if you really want to get a good handle on the whole "fully decoupled MSM two-leaf room-in-a-room" thing, a really good place to start is with the Wyle report (WR 73-5). Finally, I'd add "Home Recording Studio: Build it like the Pros", by Rod Gervais. That will give you much more detail about the practical side of construction. Studying those will give you a really good understanding of how to design your room completely, including things that you haven't mentioned yet, but that are extremely important, such as the HVAC system, the electrical system, doors, windows, and the single most important point of all: airtight sealing. - Stuart -
