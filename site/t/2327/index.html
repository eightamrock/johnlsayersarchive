knightfly15 September 2004#5
There doesn't seem to be much difference due to species of wood, more the thickness and support behind it - although, different species have noticeably different mass for the same thickness. The main parameter is density per square measure of area, as expressed in the formulas for panel resonators. If you back a light wood panel with two layers of gypsum wallboard, it will react much differently than the same panel with only insulation behind it; it's also dependent on the depth of the cavity behind the panel, and even somewhat on the wall backing the cavity (concrete, brick, block, more gypsum, etc - all will somewhat affect the result, but most of it is the front panel weight/cavity depth/insulation) Here's a calculator for panel absorbers that may help - http://www.mhsoft.nl/Helmholtzabsorber.asp The panel absorber part is down the page a bit - do NOT, repeat NOT, use the slat or helmholtz calculators on this site because they are WRONG - instead, use the modified ones posted in our acoustics forum. If you want the actual formula, here's a page - http://www.mbr-design-group.com/tech/no ... gmatic.pdf For coefficients of a ton of stuff, check out bob gold's page, don't remember where the link is right now...
