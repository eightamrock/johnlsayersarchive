A quick look at your pics show a lot of untreated parallel surfaces, contributing to the mentioned echo/ringing. Here are some bits of info on vocal booths I've collected along the way. Perhaps they may of some help to you -
My understanding of small vocal booth/voice-over treatments tend to lean toward the "dead" side. During the insulating phase of my front soffits, I noticed how nice and quiet it got with each 4' x 2' x 3" section of rockwool that was installed. Bryan Giles cautioned however, against building a square booth saying; "Be careful, a dead square booth will have low end issues (you'll hear the issues on your mic.)
Steve (Knightfly) mentioned that "4' x 5' x 7' inside dim's is about the best modal compromise for a small booth." Modally, with a 6'4" ceiling you'd do well with wall dimensions (inside) of 5 and 7 feet. That combination shows no modal problems at all. This means that vocals in such a room shouldn't be "colored" by the room. You would want to keep the upper half, including the ceiling, absorbed with such as rigid fiberglas insulation boards, covered with a light plastic film (they shed) and fire proofed cloth (they're ugly) - you'd likely get better vocal sounds with the singer seated rather than standing, since otherwise they will be nearly at the ceiling. Being that close to a boundary (especially if it isn't absorbed) will cause comb filtering due to early reflections.
Mode-wise, a 5 x 6 x 7 (inside) booth looks very good. It will also fit into an 8 foot ceiling area if necessary, and is big enough for 3 (very) friendly vocalists or 1-2 with a guitar, so that's what I'm going to start with. Once I finish (don't stop breathing while you wait, I have 'way too many projects still open) you could change dimensions to suit, just do NOT make any two dimensions the same, or more than one dimension in even # of feet.
Here are some wall DIY units http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm and some more in depth info on room design and treatments - SAE Institute Reference Material Center – John Sayers http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html Be sure to click on the “acoustics”, “absorbers”, and “construction” tabs at left, and then tabs that show up at the top. Given the $100 budget mentioned, you may have be a bit creative in your treatments. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

Aloha,
Do I need anything on the ceiling? I had a bunch of diffusors up there, but read a post saying that smaller rooms don't need diffusors.
Given the ceiling heights mentioned, having a cloud absorber (or two) over the vocal, mix and parts of the live areas would help to reduce early reflections between the floor/ceiling. Whereas a diffuser at that height and proximity to your microphones may introduce some phase anomalies into your recordings. Aloha 8)
