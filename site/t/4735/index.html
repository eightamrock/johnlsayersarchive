I've been adding one type of treatment at a time, and running LOTS of frequency sweeps with Spectrafoo between each step. The treatment: All treatment so far is of the absorption type, rather than "panel" (resonator) type. Corner and sidewall traps made of 6" of rockwool. Back wall traps are made from 3" of OC 705. knightfly29 November 2005#2
1) Should I definitely put a trap up on that chimney? Would something less dense than 705 panel work better? And when the speaker is so close to a surface, how much of an air gap is best? If most of your problems are in the lower, modal area, I wouldn't worry about going to a lighter absorbent; usually the best place for an absorbent behind speakers is IMMEDIATELY behind them; you could even try doing cutouts and "soffiting" your speakers with rockwool. Main thing is SBIR in this case, so absorbing as low as possible will help in most cases, because you'll have several peaks/dips as you move speakers closer or further from boundaries. Minimum air gap I'd use is about 4", with 4" or thicker material. 2) For the overhead panel of 705, should that be placed according to the mirror/reflection point? Or should that just get placed directly over the speakers? It should be centered at the ray trace points of speakers; with a peaked roof, this can be wierd. For your roof, I think I'd do some frames and "cloud" the entire roof peak. Otherwise you will likely see some compression artifacts coming off that ceiling and back to the mix position, out of the acute angled roof peak. 3) For the overhead trap, is 705 the wrong material for a "reflection point" absorber? I've read here about how higher freqs will "graze" off denser panels. With the way it's hung now, with the front end a bit lower than the back, the sound (if you ray trace it from the front of the speaker) is moving almost parallel to the panel. If you have lighter stuff, like 3 PCF, I'd use it; otherwise, another way is to put standard fluffy insulation in FRONT of the heavier stuff for a more broadband result. 4) I had considered another room configuration: putting a speaker right on each side of the chimney (left and right, with the mixing position on the "bottom" part of that drawing, "below" the chimney looking up). Two things made me wary of this: having a solid mass between the two speakers, rather than air, AND having the speakers shoot towards the angled ceiling right behind me. Are these both valid concerns? The solid mass thing will give a hard-to-predict apparent bass boost, and firing into that ceiling would require complete and deep absorption to keep it from totally screwing up phase coherence at the mix position. Alternatively, you could put your speakers against the side wall, absorb the chimney, and let the ceiling's upward slope give you RFZ characteristics for the ceiling, anyway. In your situation, I'd consider seriously building at least four portable absorbers of 4" or thicker absorbent; if you stay where you are, you'll want at least one of those behind you and in front of the window to avoid early reflections from the rear, and you could experiment with placement distance along the sides for the shallowest dips in response... Steve

Today I tried putting a BIG trap in front of the chimney. This had the discouraging effect of causing a wide dip at about 350 Hz, where before there was just a small (smaller and narrower) notch there. The trap I used for this test was a whole package of 8lb rockwool, in total 12" deep. That's a lot of trap. And it didn't have a very positive effect. At least I didn't go and build a whole new trap for it - I was just using the bundle of rockwool, wrapped in plastic, as I bought it.
