Soundman202028 November 2017#6
I take it from that my original question on beefing up an existing door containing glass is a no no unless the glass is being replaced?
The glass may well be just fine, but we'd need more details to be able to say. For example, how thick is the glass, and what type of glass is it? How much isolation do you need, in decibels?
I've been going back and forth if I want to build a window or have a door with glass.
Either option will work. Glass is not a problem in a door, as you can see from he series of photos I gave you in the example above. Since the window was designed along with the door, there were no issues: that door performs just as well as it would have if it had no glass. The glass could have been bigger, if the owner would have wanted that, as long as the design took that into account. The entire door could have been glass, except for a small metal frame around the edge, once again provided that the design took that into account. In other words, it is possible to design a pair of doors either with or without glass, in any size glass pane, and using pre-hung doors or not as the basis. The issue is not so much the original basic door, but rather the overall design of what is done to that door in the design stage. If the math is correct, and the design is correct, then you can the results you want with pretty much any basic door slab. As long as it's not a hollow core door, that is! Hollow core doors are out. But if you start with a pre-hung solid wood door, with or without a window, that's a good start. You might need to replace the glass with something better, but that's not a bog deal.
If I have to build my own I feel more comfortable with building a window since that doesn't have to move on a daily basis and stick with a solid beefed up door
Putting a window in a door does not change any of that: you need to door anyway, and the door has to open and close, so it's irrelevant if the door has glass in it too. The glass is just a "bunch of mass" that is fixed permanently in place in the door. As long as the design is correct, that piece of glass will provide the exact same isolation as if it were wood, not glass. No difference. In other words, if you want glass in your door, you can have glass in your door! :) It doesn't make any overall difference to the isolation, assuming that the design is done correctly. - Stuart -
