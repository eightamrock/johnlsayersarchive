specialkm24 January 2010#1
Kind of a general question so not sure if I'll get a response. I'm considering the "inside out" wall designs John has illustrated. My question concerns the construction of the wall layers. Assuming you don't have the physical room to apply the outside layers to the constructed framing and have to build them in a more modular fashion say in 4'x10' panels and set into place on the plate, this would not allow for applying your wallboard layers to overlap the seams. You would basically have a straight through seam on the edge of each panel. My thinking is that even with proper caulking between this seam between stud and wallboard edges, this would present a weakness in design over having overlaped seams. My questions are: 1. How much of a weakness is this? 2. Is there any suggested way to construct this type of design and be able to overlap the outside seams? The only way I can see is to build the complete wall on the deck and lift it into place. Am I wrong in thinking that a 20'x10' wall with at least two layers of 5/8" drywall is going to be extremely heavy to lift into place. Also seems like that last wall will be a bitch to negotiate. Any suggestions or comments? Thanks.

lilith_envy25 January 2010#4
hi, Build the wall as one piece on the ground, Caulk, mud all joints. You need a few burly mates or I hired a chain block to left into place. The corners work ok if you double stud the ends of every 2nd wall(a and c) And as you can see in my thread I also left 120mm over hang of plasterboard. I just covered it in no-more nails(glue) and slid the next wall into place.
