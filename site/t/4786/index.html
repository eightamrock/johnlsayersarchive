sharward6 January 2006#12
Yeah, the "exit doors must swing out" thing is true here too, for commercial spaces. In a panic, people push doors open to exit, even if they're supposed to be pulled inward. When there's a stampede of people rushing to exit, it's impossible to get the mass of people to step back to allow the door to swing open. That's one of the things that happened in The Station (West Warwick, Rhode Island) tragedy... (Graphic)

sharward6 January 2006#14
Room-in-room designs can coexist with strict fire codes, but it takes creativity and patience. If both doors must swing in the same direction, then there needs to be room for the "inner" door to swing and not hit the "outer" door... And if two doors open into the same area, neither door can strike the other.

sharward6 January 2006#17
When you say "inwards," do you really mean "outwards," in the sense that people exiting the studio would be pushing doors "outwards" rather than pulling them? And are you certain of the 2 square meter requirement, or are you going strictly by what the contractor is telling you? Have you confirmed this requirement with your building department? The reason I ask is that sometimes contractors can be mistaken, or they only know part of the law that applies -- perhaps the most "common" part -- and not the exceptions. I'm also curious about the door on the live room. In all of your drawings, the hinge is closest to the corner, which seems to make the swing of the door interfere with the swing of the door to the control room. I don't suppose you'd be permitted to build this "lobby" in the existing lobby space -- basically a rectangle in front of both existing doorways... :roll: This reminds me of my "landing" requirements when I was initially planning to do a floating floor with a couple of steps up. Building codes here require a similar "lobby" type thing at the bottom of the steps, regardless of which way the door swings. Once I eliminated my steps up (I decided not to float a floor), I was able to have two opposing doors swinging in opposite directions. Keep in mind my project is residential -- I'm pretty sure I'd have the same problem you are if it were commercial space.
