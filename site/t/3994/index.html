knightfly9 July 2005#5
Here's a simple intro that may help you follow the BBC stuff - http://www.aptcommunications.com/consider.htm#ratios This doesn't include any of the other noted acousticians' contributions to optimum room ratios, such as Bolt, Sepmeyer, Beranek - Here is another page with more info - http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?p=5570 The author of that page is a degreed acoustician whose work includes the quietest studio in the world - Galaxy studios in the Netherlands. As Andre said, windows with your mild isolation needs should be no problem; you just need to use non-multi-paned ones, and two of them per window spaced several inches apart, and you can get enough isolation for what you describe. From your comments, it sounds like a single room might work best for your needs; I've worked this way for some time and as long as you're a solo act there's no need to cater to others' requirements for space, etc - I write/record from a "jet cockpit" approach that's worked pretty well for me. Everything I need can be reached from the same chair, just by rotating around. I don't have pix of the "before" version, and I'm in the middle of a re-do - but basically I have a mix desk with two mixers on top, two video monitors between the speakers (a center channel will be added between the two video monitors next) - two low equipment racks to one side, then a set of V-drums, then stands for bass and guitar each with their own processor, then a key stack, and finally a small section to get into/out of the "cockpit" - the drums are opposite the Video monitors, so I use a mirror on a stand so I can see the video monitor while playing the drums - I set the time code window to HUGE while doing drum tracks so I can see it easily (but backward) in the mirror. Works fine. That whole setup only takes a space that's about 10 x 12, but adding a separate area for PA rehearsals would probably double that; One of the better known "golden rooms" is a ratio of 1:1.6:2.33, so an INSIDE dimension (rectangular assumed for the moment) of 10' ceiling, 16' x 23.3', would give you all the room you need without feeling cramped. (Inside dim's are what's important here, not outside ones.) Those dim's also yield a cubic volume of 3728 cubic feet, more than double the BBC's recommended MINIMUM room volume for half-way decent sound (1500 cu ft) If you want splayed walls for flutter control without excess absorption (more control of desired reverb times) you would need to use AVERAGE dimensions, so the outer shell would need to be a bit larger than for a straight rectangular room. Then, considering that a 6 degree splay requires approx. 1 foot of splay per 10 feet of run, your outer side walls would need to be far enough apart inside to allow for a bit more than a foot extra each side so that the AVERAGE INSIDE width of the finished room is 16 feet, and same with length. (Length, to average 23.3 feet, would need to be about 1.6 feet longer than the 23.3 feet. Outer walls, since you're starting from scratch, get to be whatever is necessary to get what you want from the INSIDE. The short walls can be double-splayed (in and back out) so that the room remains symmetrical and still presents no parallel surfaces. The ceiling will need to slope upward to the rear from the mix position - for the last 1/3 or so, it can slope back down but will need some absorption in spots to keep early reflections from getting back to the mix position. Again, a minimum of 6 degrees splay for the ceiling, 12 is better. 6 degrees is almost exactly 1 foot in 10. Again, these "magic" dimensions are INSIDE dimensions between hard surfaces; so if you have absorbers 6" in from the walls, don't subtract that 6" from your dimension when figuring modal ratios, it's the ENTIRE dimension between hard surfaces that counts. Splayed rooms don't get RID of modes, they just make them more confusing, harder to calculate, BUT less pronounced generally - splays also minimise the need for absorption, so you can choose how dry you want the room based on REVERB needs, not OTHER problems that can be fixed with splaying. Gotta go for now, hope this helps... Steve
