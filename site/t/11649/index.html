dfeiszli1 February 2009#9
There's a pretty straightforward calculation you can use to determine the latency your mic cable will add to the signal path. This isn't 100% accurate in extreme cases, but it's close enough for almost all real world uses. Note that you'll need to double the result for a round-trip: AL = (L*G)/C1 * (P/T) * C2 where AL = latency L = length of your cable in meters G = gauge of wire C1 = constant number 1, which is 7.48 assuming you're working in meters, pascals, and degrees celsius P = air pressure T = temperature in the interior of the cable (in a pinch you can use the surrounding air temperature although this won't be strictly accurate) C2 = constant number 2, which is always zero, since there will never be latency in an analog cable run until you're running your cable a distance that's significant relative to the speed of light.... ;)

Soundman202011 February 2009#19
Hey Hotmetal. It sounds like your best shot would be to build your walls and ceiling "inside out", meaning that you put your drywall for your inner leaf on the "other" side of the studs, not the "room" side of the studs. So your studs, and the spaces between them, are now exposed to the room, where you can fill them with treatment to your heart's content! Ethan has an example on his website of a ceiling built like that, where the space between the joists is stuffed full of isolation. There are also many examples of inside-out walls on the forum, if you look around for them: It's kind of John's "trademark", so to speaker! Doing it that way you can accomplish what you want: hidden treatment. You can stretch acoustic cloth over the studs where needed, or build slot resonators on the studs, or whatever you want. It also makes better use of floor space, since the treatment goes in between the studs, instead of on top of the finished drywall. I'm not sure if I would want to use T&G pine instead of drywall, though: Pretty much all of the studios here are done with ordinary gypsum drywall, which is cheap, massive, and easy to work with. It is a known quantity. The resonant frequencies and acoustic properties for various drywall construction methods are well known and available on the Internet, but I don't know how much you'll fine on pine paneling. If you are concerned about aesthetics, then you could use pine slats for your slot resonators, decorative framing around bass traps, etc. But I think I'd stick with drywall as the actual leaf, especially given that it won't ever be seen if you build your wall inside-out. That's just my $0.02. - Stuart -
