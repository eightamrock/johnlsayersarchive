andymel9 September 2009#1
Hi all, I'm happy to introduce a room I will build my first own 'playground' in. What it should became: A live room for recording my own stuff and in between for renting it to musicians/bands as a rehearsal room. Budget: ~10.000eur (15.000 with heating, cooling, ventilation,...) Some information about the room:(pics below) Location It is at ground-level in a block in the city, but there is a cellar (arch) underneath .
    Adjacencies:
  • In the cellar directly below my room I've seen a big wood-working machine...will make a lot of noise when I record silent things!
  • Diagonally downwards there is a conference room of the neighbouring company...will have a meeting when I record loud things!
  • On two sides the company (working until 6pm)
  • One side is to my other room (maybe a small control room in some years)
  • One side is an adjacent house. There have to be two disconnected thick (fire-proofed) walls (by law)
  • The biggest problem I think (lowest STC) will be the wooden beam ceiling (with plaster) to the flat above.
Dimensions (LxWxH) ~ 6m x 5m x 4.8m ~ 20ft x 17ft x 15ft (The height was the crucial factor to buy this room). What I did until now: I did a rough calculation of the weight of a inner room for this space (10-15to) => a structural engineer gave me the ok after a first inspection of my rooms and the cellar. What I'm doing/thinking about now Now I have to develop a detailed plan for sound insulation, heating, cooling, ventilation, wiring and so on and would greatly appreciate any support :) What to do with the ceiling?! My first consideration is, how I can use this wonderful height best. I don't want to save all that volume for the inner room.
    I think it would be great to have
  • as much space as possible to the upper/outer ceilling.
  • most of the absorption on the ceiling. Firstly because of better acoustics (side-reflection vs ceiling reflection), secondly to save the space on the floor.
  • to have a high inner room...good air, good acoustics, good looking...
The problem with this is, I will have a lot of weight on my inner ceiling and a span of ~5m (~17ft) without supporting it from the wooden ceilling above. Maybe, its an idea to build a rafter-roof / couple roof (I hope it's the right translation for the german "Sparrendach") or a collar-beam roof (Kehlbalkendach). Some thoughts about this: The inner ceiling and the outer ceiling wouldn't be parallel. That could be good (axial modes) or bad (sound insulation decreases with the angle of incidence). I did a first sketchup drawing with this idea. Wall height is 3m. Roof 30°. A lot of absoption could happen between the rafters. Without the suspension from above I have to be careful with the load. Its not calculated now, but although I need 3 beams for the triangle the rafters can be less thick (or in greater distance/or both) than horizontal joists. This point in conjunction with the lower walls (3m) could be a cost-saver without loosing all the volume above 3m. Floating Floor?! The conference room and the machine below me and possible flanking to my sides makes me think about a possible floating floor. The structural engineer told me that typical arches have earth on top. Maybe 10cm (some tons) could be removed and a floated concrete slab could be made without getting a high stair. Sounds like an option to me but I would be happy to hear your thoughts. How to measure?! In october I will get the keys and would be happy to make some measurments after removing the existing planking. I thought about aksing the company and the above neighbours to measure while playing drums and bass and some sweeps in the room. I also think about knock, knock, knocking on andys floooor to get an idea of flanking. Though i don't know whether it tells me a lot without the standardized hammer machine. OK. Thank you for reading my beginnings. I hope anybody can help me with my thoughts. Best regards Andy

Hai! Good introduction, thanx. I'm only gonna say "why not keep the, sloped(!), height of the room as it is.. somehow". What I mean is, you can make use of it by NOT adding a second ceiling parallel to the floor. What you might do is; add mass to the roof. Add another, inner, ceiling parallel to the sloped roof and hang some absorbers in the room it self at the top of the roof. Or have hangers in between the 2 ceilings, but keep the inner ceiling angled as much as possible. Will enhance acoustics. Check this viewtopic.php?f=1&t=13595 also.... nice plans, keep them designs comming!
