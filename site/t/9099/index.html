I'm with Glenn here. I even shouldn't make that ceiling so absorptive (there is no real echo in a room that size). I don't see acoustic disadvantages whatsoever in the bigger room, but mainly advantages. A live room is meant to be used as live. Hence this higher (controlled) reverb is a plus, not a minus. And it can easily be adjusted to lower when needed. I think it's entirely a budgetary thing. I think this studio world hasn't it that easy (too much competition on the lower end side) from a commercial point of view. Hence you need to have a solid name already before you can ask higher prices, justifying/compensating these higher investments and overhead. Having a big room (with related overhead and installation cost) but too little work for it has little sense. In as far as I know a bit about it: it's nowadays a difficult world from a commercial point of view. I assume it's a bit like in sports and music. The toppers can earn/ask a lot, there is a relative small mid area, and lots must do with much less. Hence that big room with a 20:1 factor should only secundary be seen from an acoustic point of view. First question should be: can you get work enough on reasonable prices to justify that decision. A lot of DIY studios aren't really established with clear commercial mid or long term budget plans in mind but more as a side activity.
