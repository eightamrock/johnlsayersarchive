knightfly16 July 2006#2
By the comment "no more construction", do you also mean you don't want to build any DIY treatments? You're gonna need something to kill flutter between parallel surfaces. Also, modal distribution for those dim's isn't exactly ideal, you'll probably need a bit more low bass control than some rooms (looks like possible problems around 40-50 hZ, with a slight "hole" just above that range) For some ideas on DIY treatments (if your answer to my first question was NO) check out the acoustic forum stickies. You don't want ANYTHING centered in your room except from side to side for stereo balance; if you're doing surround, more absorption will be desirable than if just doing stereo mixes because rear surrounds can otherwise cause early reflections back to the mix position. In the acoustics forum, check out my sticky "are my speakers in a null?" for more on positioning stuff. Also, if you haven't already, click on the John Sayers productions logo at top right of the page, then click on "wall units" - the saw-tooth ones will probably be necessary near your mix position to control flutter; those may also help some of your low end modal problems if tuned right, but I think that may be too low a frequency for slat absorbers to help much; more likely you'd need some tuned panel absorbers, but I'm off to bed for now so it may be a while before I have time to help you calculate specifics and positioning for something like that; they won't help anything if not in the right pressure zone. Gotta run, HTH... Steve

Ethan Winer16 July 2006#4
Colin, > How do I best treat the room for optimal acoustic performance? < This is a deep subject, and a complete answer requires far more than will fit into a single reply here. The short version is: * Broadband (not tuned) bass traps in as many corners as you can manage, including the wall-ceiling corners. * Mid/high frequency absorption at the first reflection points on the side walls and ceiling. * Some additional amount of mid/high absorption on any large areas of bare parallel surfaces, such as opposing walls or the ceiling if the floor is reflective. That's about it. Really. > The French patio door to the side of the room that limits the forward position of the mix desk < You can hang an absorber on the door, from wires attached to the ceiling, or on a stand you can move when you need to use the door. > I am planning to orient the mix desk roughly in the middle of the room facing the iso booth, with a cloud over the mix desk. < The middle of the room front to back is the very worst place you can be. Also, a cloud should not be directly overhead, but about halfway between you and the loudspeakers. It can be larger, but the main "first reflection" points are halfway between your ears and the tweeters. The drawing below shows the optimal placement for the mix position and for the first reflection treatment. It doesn't show any bass traps, but understand that you need as many as you can possibly manage. Put them all in corners, including where the walls meet the ceiling. (And even where they meet the floor if possible.) --Ethan
