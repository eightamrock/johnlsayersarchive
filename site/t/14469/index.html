Soundman202013 June 2010#5
Hi Brendan. Welcome! Just some thoughts, in no particular order: 1) "If i need to float the floors then I would use Auralex U-Boats. (Again to help isolation and prevent sound i/o through floors)". Hopefully, you won't need to do that, since you say that you won't be making much noise anyway. Apart from that, floating a floor needs lots of mass (weight) and your existing floor might not be able to support it. That might be an issue anyway, with the rest of the structure you are planning: it all adds up to a lot of mass. Once you have your plan done, you should probably get a structural engineer to take a look at your plans and your existing floor, to tell you if you actually can do it without collapsing everything into the room below! Here's an interesting thread on floating floors: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 2) You said that you plan to build "New stud walls, (maybe staggered constuction) insulated with Rockwool, to make control room, vocal booth and "air lock" entrance. This should complete a two leaf construction.". Correct. But then you say: "Floating all Walls and maybe the ceiling using the "Genie Clip" system." You don't need both. Genie clips and hat channel is one method of decoupling, and building a separate stud wall is another method. But doing both does not gain you anything. Once the drywall is decoupled, then it is decoupled. Decoupling it again isn't useful, and just wastes space, time and money. 3)
Im going to make my own bass traps, (maybe some suggestions?)
Superchunks work great. So do hangers. So do large thick panels of 703 across the corners. 4)
What absorbtion and/or difusion should i use in the control room?
Your room is too small for diffusers to work effectively. Stick with absorption and slot walls. 5)
And how much of it?
How much can you afford? :) Small rooms need lots of bass trapping. 6)
but id like to keep the construction costs under 1000 if possible..
Then you can definitely forget the idea of floating your floor! That takes quite a bit more money to do it right. I doubt that your budget is going to be enough to do what you want, even without floating the floor. 7)
Would i need to do anything special with the ceiling, (i.e. Genie clip/resiliant channel)
Ideally, your inner-leaf ceiling should only be supported on your inner-leaf walls, without there being any mechanical connection at all to the outer-leaf. If you want to use either genie clips plus hat channel, or resilient channel, then do be aware that you will still have to remove the existing ceiling in the room, since the clips or RC should be mounted directly to the joists, and you do not want to have a thin airspace between the existing ceiling and the new one: that would create a three-leaf system with a high resonant frequency, which would be pretty bad for your isolation. So regardless of how you decide to do your ceiling, you will need to remove the existing drywall if you want good isolation. The same applies to your existing walls: the drywall will have to come off, if you want good isolation. If you leave it on, you will have a 3-leaf. 8)
Will either of my designs work?
Your second one looks much better, to me! (As modified by John!) 9)
