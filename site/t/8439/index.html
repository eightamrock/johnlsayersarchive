sharward19 March 2007#2
Not completely insane, but a lot of work to be sure. Two real-life home studio examples come to mind. Paul Woodlock did this in his two car garage in Peterborough, England. His build diary is over at StudioTips.com. Also, one of the current projects here on this forum is Lee's (infiniteposse's) Mystery Machine Studios in Portland (Oregon): he is doing (and, as far as the excavation and slab pour are concerned, has already done) exactly what you're describing. A structural engineer advised against cutting concrete too close to existing walls, so there is a lip/shelf around the entire perimeter of the basement. This gives the basement walls stability against the inward forces pressing against the walls: the "toe" pressing down against the pressure on the "calf." Get it? So, no, not entirely crazy... Then again, the person typing this broke up much of the concrete slab in his garage and dug out about a foot of dense clay with a shovel and doesn't think that was too insane! :lol: I can't remember... Is a standalone building completely out of the question in your case? --Keith :mrgreen:

Luftweg, Forgot to mention this story, I posted this on another thread with regards to lowering a basement floor, but I figured you would have read the story on that thread: My grandfather built an apartment building on bedrock (exactly what type of rock I don't know) in Halifax Canada years ago, maybe in the 40's. 3 units, one unit each floor with a base square footage of around 1500 sq-ft. There was a small basement area of cleared rock and crawl space under the entire building. It was built on poured concrete foundation walls. He (with some grunt help work from me) removed most of the bedrock from the crawl space portion and added actual basement square footage. I'm guessing this added 1000 sq-ft removing 6' high worth of rock, via 5 gallon buckets dumped into a utility trailer hauled away with a ford taurus station wagon. I can remember the exact details about everything but this was done at least 40 years after building was built, its been standing since bedrock removal for at least 15 years. I am 90% sure my grandfather removed all the rock with out a permit and with out consulting an engineer BUT like I said I can not remember all the details. All I do remember is how dirty my and grandpas nose was after working the 1st day without a mask on. I write all the above to say, YES IMHO it can be done.
