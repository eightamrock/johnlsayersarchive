Soundman202027 March 2010#4
so it's just room acoustics that I'm hoping to improve not sound proofing.
Are you SURE about that? Last time I was in Scotland, I remember hearing some rather loud storms, with things like wind and rain, that made a lot of noise around the place I was staying in. Are you saying that those types of noises will not be an issue, when they get into your recordings? :) You also show a bathroom on your plan, so I guess someone flushing the toilet in the middle of a once-in-a-lifetime vocal recording, just isn't a problem? No roads outside? No people will ever talk in the rest of the building while you are recording? No planes will fly over? No dogs bark? And you don't even need isolation between your control room and live room? I'd say that you might need to re-think your "no isolation at all is needed" statememnt. I've never seen a successful studio where there was no isolation around and between the rooms.
What to do with doors? As you can see from the picture I have doors on corners of the rooms, where I would need bass trapping. Does anyone have any good ideas about how to get around this problem?
move them? :) Yeah that sounds like a rather facetious answer, I know, but it looks like you have no choice. Question: Do you need a door directly from CR to LR? Maybe you can solve one issue by getting rid of that door entirely. That means you'll have to go out the CR and up the passage to get into the LR. Is that a big deal? If it is a big deal, and you really do need a direct door, then how about making the central window between the rooms into the door? It could be a glass door, if you need the sight lines. The other door (main CR door) also needs to be moved. Is there any reason why it cannot be moved to roughly where the word "Entrance" is, on your sketch? that would put it more or less in the middle of the wall, which looks like it would be a good place. Just a comment here: Your CR seems to be wider than it is long. Not a good idea. Your speakers should fire down the longest axis of the room, generally speaking, not across the short axis. So how about if you set it up facing to the right?
The only thing to consider is that the wall between the 'Entrance Porch' and 'Control Room' is brick. All the other partitions are plasterboard.
OK, so that means that you could even rip out the existing wall between CR and LR, and re-design the whole setup? That might be a good idea, actually.
I have a budget of £1000 (which needs to also pay for materials for a custom desk).
I don't want to sound like a wet blanket, but that won't go very far for what you need to do. The desk alone could cost you that much, depending on how fancy you want to make it. And the entire £1000 won't go very far to doing all the things that you need to do to make that into a good studio. Is there any chance you can increase that budget, and do things over a longer period of time? Maybe live without the custom desk for a couple of months, while you get the basic shape, layout, isolation, and treatment of your room organized first? - Stuart -

Soundman202027 March 2010#7
If so what would i replace the sliding doors with? Slot resonators?
I'd probably start out with plain walls, then measure the room to see what it still needs, and install "what it still needs" on those plain walls... or leave them plain, as the case may be. You can predict the acoustics of a room roughly, and design the basic treatment roughly, but nothing beats actually testing the room once it is mostly finished, doing some real measurements in it, to see where the remaining issues are, then add additional treatment as need to deal with those issues. So if you have some empty wall available, then you have space to put in the "additional treatment", whatever it turns out to be. Anyway, based on what you say the brick wall at the entrance is fixed and can't be moved, and I guess the windows on the bottom and right walls are also fixed? But do you need them? Will you actually use those windows for natural light, or could you plug them if necessary? At the very least you will probably need to replace the glass in them with some thick laminate glass, but since that stuff is expensive ant your budget is limited, it might be easier 8and certainly cheaper!) to just design a plug that you can fit in the window frame and seal up. What else is fixed an cannot be changed? - Stuart -
