dynamike2 December 2007#2
i'll take a crack at it. 1) if you mean parallel to the wall, yes. . .it all depends on the frequency (range) you are trying to absorb. in a sticky on top of this forum is a slot resonator calculator. that will help you understand a lot more. 2)it can be plywood or MDF or anything else that will make it airtight. i'm not sure about stone. my guess would be to put some sort of sealer over it first ie: paint or drylok or something. and you'd really have to work at sealing the sides. you'd have to get pretty crazy with a jigsaw to get the sides of it to match up with the stones. and still caulk the crap out of it. 3)refer to answer 1 about the calculator. you can go as high or wide as you want/need

gullfo5 December 2007#5
the length of the slats would in some ways be related to the amount of open space in the overall absorber. so depth and width of the slots, the percentages of slat-to-slot, cavity depth, insulation etc. all play a role. if you do a search on google for Chris Whealy's Porous Absorber calculator, its a very nice tool. also the Excel Helmholtz calculator on this site has the corrected formula. for some reason the SAE site just isn't getting updated lately :)
