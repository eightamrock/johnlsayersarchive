Marty wrote:
...(they're non-musicians, not quite getting this room yet!)
A lot of musicians do not have the vision to fully comprehend these types of builds either, so it must be something different. What you are doing is constructing a specific structure to do a specific thing. In typical residential construction anytime a room is developed that is either a bathroom or kitchen or has a specific use outside of a "box" the costs are always higher in respect to the bedrooms, living rooms, etc. So building something like these acoustic environments that we engage in is even beyond that in scope and breadth. And usually has no plumbing :) To fully convey how this is different overall then just building a shed or garage you have to find some of the education on it in order to answer questions that may come up from those around us that do not "get it". These rooms are aimed at holding sound in which makes them loud. This requires MASS and lots of it. It will require at least the amount of materials it takes to build one typical room X 2.5! And then that retained loudness has to be evened out so as to be of benefit to the engineer who is recording and/or the musician who is playing. Since this is usually the job of typical home furnishings, curtains, couchs, tables and what have you, go ahead and figure the cost to fully furnish a room, pictures and everything, X 2.5 :) Essentially you will attempt to take the loudness out of the room without that loudness ever really leaving the room! WTF? This is no small feat and is not for the faint of heart and as history has shown time and again it is often more then even the best musicians can muster. HVAC? You have to push air in or pull it out through enough handmade baffles and plenums to choke a mule at such a slow speed that it cannot be heard by a microphone! This is outside of day-to-day business as usual. Consider your budget busted :) I don't care how much ya got, the HVAC systems that get designed and installed are awesum in design but costly to effect. As a general overall reference, Brien

If this is new construction you would benefit by not attaching it directly to the house. I can feel it coming the reasons why it will have to be attached to the house. But it is a known gremlin that anytime this type of construction happens, the flanking paths become an issue. The want of a great design is a bit of the cart before the horse right now. At least if this was my project these are the things we would discuss in the preconstruction meeting. The plumbing and electrical would follow that. One is a potential flanking path and the other often requires a dedicated service to keep the lines clean and free from the house interference. I'm just saying...
