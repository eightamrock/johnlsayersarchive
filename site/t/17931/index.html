Hi, Inspired by the Sayers inside out wall, I was just brainstorming over ways to try to save even more space and splay side walls. Can anyone think of anything wrong with this type of wall construction? Or maybe we could think of a way to make it work? Here is my late night scatter brain thought behind it... I was trying to find a way to splay a wall without taking up interior space. Saw tooth accomplishes the splay effect right? But I wanted to find a way to do it without eating up interior space. As I understand, one of the reasons for the insulation / rigid fiberglass is to dampen the mass, right? so does it matter what side it is on? It could be completely on the outside, or the inside, or half on each side. It may be more expensive to use osb rather than drywall, but this could help with sheer strength as well as the osb could be used to combine / screw the drywall to. On a 2"x6" stud, there should be enough space on each side of a centered 2"x4" stud to have 1" of mass on each side of it. The 1"x1" strip will help stop leaks through the base and header. Can anyone think of why this would or would not work? or what the pros and cons would be? Also, I apologize if this has been brought up before. I couldn't find a thread on this type of wall. Beau

Soundman20202 November 2012#4
If I understand your sketch correctly, the rear edge of each "saw" tooth would be in contact with the outer leaf, thus creating a flanking path. Or if not, then the air gap is greatly reduced on that edge, thus lowering the isolation considerably. Don't forget that the depth of the air gap is a key part of the MSM equation: reduce that, and you raise the resonant frequency well into the audible range, thus reducing your low frequency isolation considerably. I also do not understand the purpose of the 1"x 1" strips: what are they there for? You would not need the thin strips of drywall on either side of the studs either: Studs are roughly the same density as drywall, so the thickness of the stud is already providing more mass than the thickness of the drywall. Also, 1/2" drywall and 1/2" OSB are too thin for good isolation: too flexible, low mass, etc. 5/8" is much better. - Stuart -
