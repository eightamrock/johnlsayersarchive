:? :roll: Not to be trusted. You really should get a decent sound level meter and measure that properly. A phone cannot do the same job: that's a simple little US$ 2 microphone in there, which is highly directional, limited bandwidth, designed for voice mainly. What you need is a proper omnidirectional mic, with flat response across the entire spectrum, and designed to do the job of measuring sound pressure levels, not voices. You can actually get such a mic that plugs in to your iPhone, and that makes it far more usable (I have one for my Android phone), but even then you need to calibrate it against a meter, and the cost of the mic is about the same as the cost of a used good-quality meter anyway, so it makes more sense to just get a meter.

Crossed wires... the "cellar" is the crappy 1.4m high crawl space UNDER the live room. On the right of the drawings below.
Ahhh! :oops: OK, I get it now. I thought there was a proper cellar down there some place, in addition to the hole...
Will a gap between int structure and ext walls really make a difference to the sound travelling up through the ceiling? I agree a gap in the ceiling will. Sound going through the side wall is not a worry.
Well, yes it will make a difference. MSM isolation is a system, and it all works together. Since the cavity between the walls is also open to the cavity between the ceiling, there is interaction going on: the resonance, and the sound in general that gets into the wall cavity can also make its way into the ceiling cavity, and vice versa.
Ok, even if I'm only concerned with sound travelling through the ceiling, not through the external window?
Think of it like this: I want to build an aquarium, so I get a metal frame for making one, then I decide that I only really need to worry about the side that faces the room, so I can see the fishes: So I figure I only need to put glass in one side of the frame, as that's the side I'm worried about, and I can forget about the other sides... How well do you think my aquarium will hold water? :shock: :D OK, so that's extreme: To make it more like a studio isolation analogy, let's say I decide to fill in the other sides too, but I use cardboard, instead of glass, since I'm still only worried about the front... Get the point? :) Isolation is an "all or nothing" proposition. It works exactly like the aquarium analogy: even though you might only be interested in stopping sound leaking out one side, you still have to do all sides of the room to the same level of isolation, just like you still need glass in all sides of the aquarium. An isolation system is only as good as the weakest link, just like the aquarium will only hold water as well as the weakest side.
But I would still love a single space if it's possible to build an isolated room on top of the cellar. Could it be done or would solid concrete floor end to end be the only workable solution?!
It should be possible, since nobody lives down there, so you won't be bothering them too much with noise! Also, it's a sealed space, with no other entry/exit, so there's no issues with sound getting out another way. However, it is could act like a rather large resonant cavity, so you'd have to be careful how you did it, but it should be possible.
Also I'm feeling like the amount of window real estate would be a problem, could I build successful treatment with those big windows at either end
Is it physically possible to do that? Yup, it sure is. Is it cheap? Nope, it sure ain't. :) In other words, as long as you don't mind spending the money, then you can do still do it, probably. - Stuart -
