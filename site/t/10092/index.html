audio man25 January 2008#1
Hi, I am new to this forum but hope to get some good advice on the below topic. I have a recording studio and want to improve the acoustic properties of my live room. Before starting the construction I would like to see if any of you have suggestions or other ideas! The problem with the current situation is that I have some flutter echoes, I think they are between floor and ceiling but they can also be between the suport beams on the ceiling (see photo's). The objective for the room is to be quiet lively, suitable for recording drums etc. If needed I can alway deaden the room locally with gobo's etc. I therefore want the ceiling to be a combination of absorption and diffusion. General info on the studio: The walls are stone. the floor and ceiling are unfinished wood. I have some broadband absorbers on the walls. The general dimensions (all metric) are LxWxH 10 x 7,2 x 3,5. The ceiling has two angled sections (no acousting problems in that area) and a horizontal area (support beams and the same wood as on the floor). The current plan: My plan is to fill the cavity between the support bars with rockwool slabs (9 cm thick) and cover this with fabric. In order not to deadend the room too much I am planning to cover the ceiling with wooden slats of 12 mm thick and random variating width (44, 69, 94, 119 mm). Between these slats I am planning to leave a gap. I also want to angle the slats slightly in order not to create new flutter echoes! (see sketch) Please refer to the photo's and sketches for clarification. My questions are: 1) What do you think of the design in general? 2) Do you have suggestions for the size of the air gap between the wooden slats (and total percentage gap versus wood)? 3) Any ideas for other (lower cost) solutions? PS: I am planning to build more diffusors for the walls. It has become a long post but I hope with a little more information You are more able to give good suggestions! Many thanks in advance!

gullfo26 January 2008#5
if i'm reading your plan right, you're adding a layer of rigid insulation, a frame with cloth, then slats on an angle. if you're isolation is ok, then i think the ceiling slat idea will be a nice one. alternatively, you could also build a number of 100mm porous absorbers (1200mmm x 600mm) and create a checkerboard pattern across the ceiling (leaving 30cm on the corner-to-corner space) just below the joists. between the space from the hard boundary above and the edges exposed you should have a nicely balanced space also. you could even mix and match and have some slats in some sections and some porous absorption in others...

jwl30 January 2008#11
It won't be a resonator unless you seal things up inside. I also agree with Glenn, I wouldn't do slats everywhere in the ceiling. There will be places where you don't want any reflections at all, ie, a ceiling cloud above the mix position, and also above any crucial recording areas (ie, above the drum kit).
