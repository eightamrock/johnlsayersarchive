jwl7 November 2007#3
If you want dead, you want absorption. You can use some kinds of foam, Auralex is good. Be warned though that there are many kinds of foam that don't do much, insist on seeing absorption specs before you buy, though even that isn't a guarantee because some companies are known to make these up. Proven materials are rigid fiberglass, rockwool, and acoustic cotton. If it were me, I would probably put absorption on one (maybe two) walls, and put slot resonators on the other walls that aim reflections toward the absorptive wall.
sandledfoot7 November 2007#4
greetings and welcome to the forum. as a curtesy to other readers and the moderators, please fill in your location and review the reference threads (conviently located at the top of this forum). a quick answer to your question would be yes, HOWEVER, carpet and eggcrates are not designed for acoustical absorption. They will be very limited in how much and of what frequency range they absorb at. (Not to mention pose as a potential fire hazzard). As you begin to read through some of this forum, you will see that many people use fiberglass insulation, rockwool, compressed fiberglass insulation or a combination of all of the above for acoustics control. Thats the quick answer to potentially years of research and reading here. Please spend the time to read through some excellent build and design posts. sandledfoot
