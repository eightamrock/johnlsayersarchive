jhbrandt21 October 2010#2
Jackie, Ideally the CR finished height should be somewhere close to 11ft. and have good ratios - H-W-D. So, yes centered in the building is a good idea... but the space needed for a good CR might also be served in another position. --- I love those vintage Neves... even pretendy ones. :P The room you have drawn is close to a cube - careful there. -- The shape of your CR is the shape often taken by the interior acoustics and not the actual construction. In other words you need room for trapping. What design approach are you taking for the control room? RFZ? Non-Environment? Both of these designs require quite a bit of trapping. Also, do you plan to flush mount your speakers? This will make a difference the design approach as well. I like your idea of the rustic look and natural 'feel' in the studio. It always helps to be in an enjoyable, creative atmosphere. The problem that I see with removing the ceiling and exposing the trusses is rain, thunder, and aircraft noise. Though, you could build a sound proof ceiling higher up, it would be tricky. You will need good isolation between the tracking room and the control room. -at least STC60-65. I would recommend a MSM wall system. Cheers, John

Soundman202022 October 2010#4
Now it's time to display my ignorance. I'm not sure the design style of the control room. I'm not certain I know what RFZ is.
Reflection Free Zone. Basically, everything that you do in designing your room to ensure that only direct sound from your speakers reaches your ears, that no first reflections get there at all, and that on other reflections get there at levels higher than 10 dB below the direct sound, within the Haas time. Roughly.
I do know this: I probably will not be flush mounting the monitors.
Why not, when doing so produces so many major benefits, with so few drawbacks? It probably is the single most important thing you can do to your speakers and your room to improve the overall sound (after acoustic treatment, of course). Why do you not want to do that?
Another goal (and this may seem painfully obvious) but there must be good visibility between the CR, the LR and the ISO (if one exists).
That can be accomplished in two ways: 1) A properly designed and built window. 2) CCTV.
One other detail : the rear side (small side) of the rectangle connects with the house. I would want to build another wall, or add a really serious barrier of some sort between that and the house.
That won't work. You cannot isolate a room by building a barrier on only one side, any more than you can make an aquarium hold water by putting only one piece of glass in the frame. Isolation is an "all or nothing" proposition: Either you do all sides of the room to the same level of isolation, or just don't bother at all. Sound will simply get around your barrier, in exactly the same way as water will get around the one single piece of glass in the aquarium. The only thing that you achieve by putting a barrier on one side is to make your local hardware store happy with their increased sales for all those expensive materials. But you don't achieve anything useful acoustically. In other words, if you need isolation between your studio and the house, then you have to isolate your ENTIRE studio: you cannot isolate just one wall. Also, like John said, the current shape and dimensions of that room are pretty bad, acoustically: you have your listening position roughly in the middle of a cube! About the only worse shape I can think of, would be a sphere... :) You should re-shape your room until it is reasonably close to one of the known good ratios, and re-arrange the geometry to get your speakers and your head in the commonly recommended positions and relationships.
The ceiling is 15' high at the center and slants evenly on either side ... to 9 feet.
Then by far the most logical layout, acoustically, would be for your control room to face one of those 9' walls, and to have the 15' point at the rear. That would remove the possibility of having a front window into the LR, of course, but from an acoustical point of view it is the best possible arrangement, hands down, no questions asked. It makes the best possible use of space, too.
There is a center wall (that will be removed) that will probably reveal some support beams. (which i actually like...!)
Did you check with a structural engineer if you can do that? Central walls quite possibly are central because they are supporting something important above them! That might not be the case for you, but you can't just rip out a wall without getting a structural engineer or other qualified person to make sure that you can do that.
the goals (besides the obvious of it sounding the best it can) are to retain a sense of "rustic-ness". I want my studio to be a place where I WANT to make music. To me that's the most important thing. It's what I do for a living, and i want to feel comfortable and inspired on a daily basis. That being said, to me a lot of that is simply cosmetics.
Mostly cosmetic, yes, but not all. That does have some acoustic implications too. For example, you might want to consider the kinds of construction materials that you consider "rustic" and that you consider would make you feel "at home" and "inspired". For example, if you like stone, consider it rustic and inspiring, then stone can be part of your finish, and stone can give you good acoustics... if you design it right. On the other hand, if you like wood but hate stone, then wood can be part of your finish, and can give you good acoustics... but that would imply a different type of design, acoustically.
*there is HVAC in the space and two vents in the existing rooms.
Have you done the calculations to make sure that it will provide the correct volume and speed of air flow, and at the correct temperature, humidity and duty cycle for your rooms? You might be OK. You might not. You say that you like to record "everything together", implying several people and lots of equipment in the LR, all at once, all of them working hard. Can your HVAC system handle that load? On the other hand, much of the time you will be alone on the CR with just a bit of equipment turned on: can the HVAC handle that minimal load too, with a sufficiently long duty cycle to ensure proper de-humidification and air replacement? Important things to think about....
*there is electric as well
Is it on a totally separate circuit, and is it star-grounded? Two very important issues.
I guess my first big question is regarding the general location of the control room. Should I be concerned with locating it so that the highest point of the ceiling is in the center of the control room? i/e, in the middle of the space.
Ideally, you should be concerned with getting the highest point at the rear of the control room, and the lowest point at the front, preferably with a ceiling slope of 12 degrees or more, and absolute symmetry for the entire studio on each side of the center-line. Major big time important!
this brings up another question: what about removing ceiling panels and letting the truss and roof be exposed? i like the look of that an it might be useful to get a few more feet out of the height.
You can do that, but not if you want good isolation to the house. Or you can still do that with good isolation, but not cheaply and not easily. That ceiling will most likely have to come off anyway, since otherwise it will be a third leaf in your isolation (between the existing roof and the new inner-leaf ceiling that you will have to build), and you NEVER want to have a 3-leaf situation if you can possibly avoid it. 3-leaf construction is a bad idea for studios.
That's good to hear about the 11 ft height. I believe that's easily attained within the current parameters.
Not really, since you say that you only have 9' for what you are currently showing as the rear of your control room. You should never have the ceiling of your room sloping down towards the rear: only sloping up. On the other hand, if your ceiling slopes the other way (side to side) you will end up with a concave "point" up there, which is not a good thing, acoustically, and will need treatment.
Now it's time to display my ignorance. I'm not sure the design style of the control room.
Your basic layout looks like a good starting point: it needs some adjustment, of course, as outlined above, but the basic idea seems fine. And as John already said, you are going to need to add the second leaves on all of those rooms, to get good MSM isolation. - Stuart -

jhbrandt23 October 2010#9
Jackie, Some good suggestions there. Very good point brought up by Stuart. --- posts/beams - structure points. Please have an engineer (or at least a licensed builder) look at any posts/supports and/or walls you intend to remove before proceeding. ;) Electric: You do not need a 'star-grounded' system but you do need clean, dedicated outlets for the audio run in a 'zero-loop-area' configuration. You must have a good ground connection (ground stake) per code. I recommend a dedicated sub-panel in the studio mechanical room with SurgeX filters. Better yet - transformer isolated/filtered feed to the studio sub-panel. The ceiling slope of the shell is irrelevant. (it should be rectangular for good modal distribution) Ideally, there should be enough room for adequate trapping. Your inside surface of the front of the CR (for RFZ design) should be reflective and slope up from the front to the back to direct reflections to the rear of the mix position. Trapping is behind this reflective facade. The rear of the CR should be fully absorptive for the Non-Environment approach or with the addition of diffusive elements for the normal RFZ approach. I'm sorry I do not have the time to do any drawings for you. I look forward to see what you might come up with. ;) Cheers, John
