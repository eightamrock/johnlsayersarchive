Eric_Desart21 June 2007#5
Nightmusic Can you PLEASE mention the source of the pictures you enter? That's a copyright issue. Since you ask your questions, split as Sharward says, but also in multiple groups simultaneously (parallel discussions, where people of one group are unaware of what's discussed or advised elsewhere. Not difficult you get confused), maybe you can read about copyright here: http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=1159 Points 4 and further down. I do know that picture of these steel springs very well, it's not yours (and probably the other neither). And I do know because I personally designed them from scratch, starting from the steel wire (this aren't commercial springs, but custom designed and produced) And as a rule of thumb: Steel springs are often used more adapted to allow lower resonance frequencies than synthetic stuff. Such steel-springs can be much more expensive and need additional measures for lateral stability. And the lowest resonances are easiest obtained with air springs (outside any practical need for studio use). But no matter what material, it's still the deflection defining the resonance frequency. Hence the choice is more mechanical than acoustical from nature in function of the application. But the main question, as rightfully posed by John remains: Do you need decoupling at all? That's the first question to be answered. Decoupling in itself is not a quality label.
