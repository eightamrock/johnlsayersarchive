xSpace12 February 2011#6
A metal shed is going to be difficult to isolate from sound. I assume there is no venting in this building, gable or ridge vents...any path that air can move around in is that correct? I wouldn't say for certain, not knowing this metal shed personally (pictures would be a great benefit) but I do not know if attaching a hard boundary to the interior side of the structure is a good thing or not based on so many unknowns at this point. Still, what you are trying to develop is a mass/air/mass type assembly. This is a hard mass boundary attached to a frame then an enclosed air space/spring/ and another frame with a hard mass boundary attached. Anything else is just more cost, more work and less isolation than most might think. A typical setup would be to install mass to the backside of the exterior framing boundary, this would be your exterior mass. Then you would give as much air space as possible. Then you frame up a new set of walls that will have a hard boundary attached to the interior side. That would be the final "mass" in your mass/spring/mass. If you did frame the interior with metal studs...how do you suppose you will get them to remain vertical with no attachment to the exterior structure? Is your plan to construct a ceiling in this new room from metal? Are you a talented diy kind of guy or will this be built for you? Lot of things to consider...electrical, air con...and keeping this structure air-tight, these are a few of the challenges you face.

John Sayers18 February 2011#19
Bob - don't treat the outer steel as a leaf as it's not airtight and really offers no sound isolation. Your plaster layer should be treated as your outer skin, seal it totally all round including the ceiling - then build the inner walls inside out with pink batts type insulation or dacron rolls on the back. No green glue is required. Have a look at these finished studios to get an idea of how to use timber and cloth. http://johnlsayers.com and check this thread for inside out construction viewtopic.php?f=11&t=6968 cheers john
