Ouch! So at least you plan to get rid of the carpet by covering it with more plywood, but what is UNDER the original plywood? If it is bare concrete, then maybe ripping out all the plywood, insulation and carpet might not be a bad idea. But you are not a sound wave! You can't even fit half of a 20Hz wave in your room, end to end... :shock: :lol: OK that's not really relevant to diffusers, but the point is that sound waves don't necessarily behave the way things look. Most diffusers produce some kind of lobing up close, where levels vary greatly over a small area. It takes several meters for the lobes to blend and smooth out. So they say. Some people like they way it sounds in a small room, others are more purist and trust the math. Personal choice, I guess.
Would stacking up bales of rolled up fiberglass in the corners work well enough?
Yup!
Besides that if there is already soft walls with insulation behind them, and if I put 703 in the ceiling, is that not trapping bass? I don't understand how I'd go about adding more bass trapping.
basically, the issue with bass trapping is depth. An inch or two thickness of absorption will do wonders for highs, less for mids, and practically nothing at all for lows. In order to absorb bass frequencies effectively, it has to be thick. Several inches thick. John can tell you stories of rooms with bass traps that are several FEET deep, hidden behind cloth. The trouble with very thick absorption is that it does good things to bass, but totally kills all the highs, which isn't so good. So one idea is to wrap the insulation in plastic, to reflect the highs more. Another is to put something reflective in front of it, such as maybe some strips of wood, with gaps between them, tuned to certain frequencies.... also known as a slot wall... :) There are other ways of trapping bass: hangers is John's favorite, I think. Panel traps. Etc. Bass traps are more effective in corners, since you get a free 12 dB boost from the walls (theoretically), so corners are the best places for them. But many people forget that there are twelve corners in a room, not just four of them... plenty of space to put effective bass traps in. - Stuart -
