An important part of the mass spring mass procedure is not being used in your model. Insulation, which you need anyway to damp the door, should be considered first, before you install or place any kind of curtain over this boundary. Not only will it stop the vibration/ringing of the garage door, it helps reduce sound coming from either side plus it's a good idea for the thermal aspect. This might be one of the few areas that MLV would be a good fit. But cost versus reduction in sound should be considered. There may be other ways to build modular and movable "gobo" type mass loaded partitions that would be just as well suited to this application

Soundman20202 July 2009#12
Would building a 3 wall room within a room (leaving the garage door accessible) do anything? Would my neighbors to the rear and 2 sides benefit?
I think you mean 4, not 3. Those would be the 3 side walls plus the ceiling. If you don't do the inner-leaf ceiling, then it would not be worthwhile at all. So, re-talking your question as a "3 walls plus ceiling, built as if it was a true MSM system, lots of mass, fully decoupled, large air gap, and well sealed", then I reckon the answer is: Yes and no. It would achieve something, but probably not too much, especially at the low end. Low frequency sound "wraps" around small object, such as walls and houses. The lower the frequency, the more easily it wraps. So your bass, kick and toms will "leak" out through the door, and wrap around to all sides of the house: Higher frequencies are more directional, so they would most likely be less of a problem. But if you combine that idea with Brien's concept of a nice thick, heavy wooden garage door, that you seal into place really well, with is idea of a few movable "gobos" that can then be dragged into place and maybe "temporarily" bolted to each other and also to the side walls and the ceiling, then what you have is a pretty good facsimile of a room within a room. It might just work! Then each time you suspect a nosy neighbor or an inspector might be on the war path, you just unbolt your gobos, and slide them into the corner... Presto! You only have a garage again.... OK, it still wouldn't be legal, most likely, so don't blame it on me if they give you a fine! And it wouldn't be perfect acoustically, either. But I don't see why it wouldn't work pretty well, if you build it carefully...
Would the Green Glue effectively decouple the 2 layers of drywall?
No. That isn't what GG does. It's a visco-elastic polymer that damps certain kinds of acoustic issues between two layers of drywall. It does not decouple. That is not its purpose. The two sheets still act as one, but with a damping layer between. Similar to laminate glass. That is not the same as decoupling.
Is that a good option for the walls?
Good option for what? You don't say what your numbers are at present, nor what numbers you are trying to achieve. Maybe all that you need is a sheet of cardboard. Maybe what you need is six inches of solid lead plate. Without numbers, it is really hard to say! I thin kyou need to put some real-world numbers to your project, otherwise it is purely a guessing game. If you figure that you need something like STC-40, then it is totally doable. If you figure you need more like STC-70, then all bets are totally off. My US$ 0.02 - S, tuart -
