knightfly16 June 2004#7
Jarkko, first - GOBO is one of those "evolved" words from working studio's, originally derived from "go between", it's just quicker to say gobo. :? The max wall system will work OK, but if you're into building your own stuff you can build similar panels for less. There's no real magic there, just a bit of Reflection Free Zone construction combined with some absorption. Plywood panels faced with rockwool or rigid fiberglass and cloth covered would do the same thing in the same place. If you're NOT into the DIY thing, then the Max wall will definitely work to improve your mix... Steve

knightfly17 June 2004#10
Those units by themselves won't do the whole thing, but will help flutter between side walls as well as take the side walls out of the equation for early reflections - a complete RFZ, however, would require no early reflections from ceiling, front wall, side walls, etc - to do that, you need to put some geometry to work and see where reflection points will be for each speaker. Things like sloped ceilings and front walls, splayed side walls and rear absorption (in smaller rooms at least) are normally what's done. If you can't do that, the max-wall approach tries to do as much of it as possible without attached construction, as do John's complete set of portable treatments. John's are much more thorough, at the cost of time and effort. The "sawtooth" type side absorbers will be a good start, but you would still need corner traps, etc, as shown in the last pic on the absorber page... Steve
