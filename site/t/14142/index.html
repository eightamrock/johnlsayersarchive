More photos here: http://flickr.com/gp/radiobiz/0L41C9 Construction info: The house is on a concrete slab - one story so the booth sits on the slab. The booth is a closet I've converted - no exterior walls. Sheetrock on wood stud frames. The interior side of the booth walls and ceiling are two layers of 5/8" sheetrock (one run horizontally and one run vertically so the seams intersect at right angles) with approximately 2 tubes of green glue per 4'x8' area as recommended by the manufacturer. The "exterior" side of the walls are whatever the original builder used, probably 1/2" I filled the cavities with insulation. All sheetrock on the first layer is caulked with GE Silicone II to the studs, floor and every seam. On the top layer of sheetrock most seams are silicone but some are Green Glue since I had a little left over. The floor to wall and wall to ceiling joints are caulked with Green Glue on the top layer. On top of that I've got tape plus 3 skim coats of joint compound on the seams as I would do for standard construction. There are two electrical outlets and two wall panels for TRS/XLR jacks and KVM. The panels use conduit inside the walls. One has three 90 degree turns, the other has four. Each end of the conduit is stuffed with scrap foam to help seal it and I've used the silicone caulk to seal up anything I could around all that. Other than the door I think the isolation is pretty well taken care of for my needs. I'm still working on a door design but it's on the backburner since I'm out of money. I have a design in mind for ventilation but again I'm out of money so it will have to wait - without the door it isn't a problem yet anyway, ha ha. The big deal right now is treatment. As the photos show I've just got some Auralex studio foam stuck up willy nilly. I built it - I wanted to put the mic in and see how it sounds - terrible at the moment :D . I'm building some panels to hang in there right now - taking a break and posting this stuff. That's about it for now. Feel free to comment good or bad. I know it's not an ideal space but it's what I had to work with.

xSpace5 March 2010#19
Typically a door closer will mount at the top side of a door. Without one, in the event that this door is ever moved by these little hands and is allowed to move all the way to the jamb, due to the weight and mass, it can hurt those fingers (in the worst case) badly. Some people install a pull type handle on the interior of the door and use a barrel throw bolt. Your concern with smaller children is certainly valid so what ever you do, make it out of their reach :) A door knob hole can reduce the isolation ability of doors when you look at all the little things together that can diminish isolation. Still, a typical closet type (none locking) door knob, installed like you mentioned would ease your concern. edit: What are you doing about getting air into this room...anything?
