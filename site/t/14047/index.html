jhbrandt10 February 2010#2
EB, A room in a room design is for isolation only. Since isolation is not a requirement for you... To begin with, trap as many corners as possible. You will probably need to make the sloping ceiling dead to eliminate any early reflections from it. Also being so close to the wall/ceiling you will have SBIR issues. I don't think that, in your case, you have many other options other than dealing with the SBIR - just be aware of it. OR - you could build a soffit mount for them and apply a -6db/octave filter to the low end to compensate. :mrgreen: For vocals: Are you going to use the same room?? If so, you will probably want it pretty dead. - IMO, not ideal for mixing - but effective for your space. 8) Cheers, John

jhbrandt10 February 2010#4
EB2010 wrote:
I assume that you take the orientation of the desk as it is on the picture as the best solution?
From a symmetry stand point, yes. Also you wouldn't want that sloping wall to be your back wall, as it will focus the sound back at you. About the wall behind the mix position: I would recommend that you put a large poly on that wall to spread reflections from it and add trapping. (fill it with rock wool). Or at least put a couple of slanted wall panels to direct reflected sound away from the operator position.
EB2010 wrote:
...would it be a good idea to construct a fake wall on that side to built them in?
Sure, if you think you have the room.
EB2010 wrote:
EDIT: what about subwoofer placement when using suffit system btw?
That will need to be determined by testing / trial & error, as in all cases.
EB2010 wrote:
Voice recording: on the left side of the desk in the picture you can see a small little window from a very small room I'd like to make. This one I'd like to have dry and since it's only for vocals, no bass absorption is needed in there I think.
Oh, I see. No problem then. But you will need to trap that room. VERY important if you don't want your vocals to sound 'boxy'. Small rooms (booths) need more trapping than large rooms. Cheers, John

Hi EB, You also may wish to think about putting another wall across from the vocal booth to the back wall to create symmetry in your room. I know you are not worried about isolation, but this would have at least a little benefit in iso as well as creating a room that is easier to mix in. Steve
