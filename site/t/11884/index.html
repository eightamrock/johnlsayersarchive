At what point...

Started by D3lta on 5 April 2009. 3 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 11884.

D3lta5 April 2009#1
I'm currently in the process of looking for a home to purchase, and the main requirement is enough space to put in a studio. Generally speaking, at what point is the square footage simply too small to invest in the time and money it takes to make a reasonably good space? I built a 6' x 8' isolation room at my mothers house, not knowing I'd be doing lots of drum tracking - suffice to say I simply didn't build the room with that in mind. I'd like to build a room that's larger for a live room; maybe 10' 15' or even 10' x 12', and double use it for an iso room with gobos. Anyway, if you were looking for a home, what amount of space would you be gunning for if you were looking to build a control room, and live room, and maybe a single iso? I should also mention, the search function didn't yield a "your wasting your money and time with a room smaller than blah x blah because physics say it will sound like [whatever means it's awfully bad]"
lilith_envy6 April 2009#2
Ceiling height is a big factor. I'm not sure about whats built in your area? Go with 9ft ceilings and apply the 1:1.14:1.38 ratio + what size drumkit do you have? The 2nd room will have to be big enough for the kit. easy as pie
xaMdaM6 April 2009#3
hehehe... oddly, I searched for the EXACT same thing about 7 years ago... same results. I ended up looking for at least 1200 sq ft of detached space with at least 12 ft ceilings. My primary objective was a control room of at least 16x20. 3000 cu ft or larger would be just about ideal. I got lucky and found this property and ended up cutting my property taxes by a factor of x3.5! Farm houses with barns are great starting points, IMHO. 1200-1600 sq ft is a pretty decent barn... not that big, but in some parts of the country, it's a common size. The other bonus of getting into a rural setting is that you usually don't have to deal with too much air or highway traffic noise.
