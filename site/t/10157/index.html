BradJacob4 February 2008#1
Construction is finished. My vertical walls are screwed right to my joists (didn't know about iso-clips back when I did it). I do however have double layer rock, green glue and isomax clips in the ceiling. My isolation between live room and upstairs is WAY better than it "was". I can NOT hear talking upstairs or even my young kids fighting with each other! But what I CAN hear, is someone pounding on a floor tom - while I'm upstairs (right above the pounding drum). But I'm not sure just HOW much isolation one can get from double rock, green glue, isomax clips and R19 insulation - between 8" joists and thin burbur upstairs. My isolation between control/live rooms is even better. Really better! While someone pounds on that same floor tom, I can have a normal conversation (at NORMAL speaking levels). I can hear the drum - no question, but it's quiet enough that the signal coming from the monitors will be louder than the bleed-through noise. I'm blaming any bleed-through on the sliding glass doors. They're from a salvage yard, pretty old and the weather stripping seems a little worn. Am I getting correct or close to correct results? Anyone else have any test-info they'd like to share? I haven't seen too many posts on this... (although I haven't really searched :roll: )

AndreasB6 February 2008#3
"your isolation is only as strong as your weakest point." I'd go check those sliding doors and make sure they're shutting tight and are isolating as much as the rest of the wall. I found it a lot easier to install traditional doors and adding mass or just buying the insanly heavy ones that I currently got and just re-install the rubber seal. Airtight and big mass... My walls are 2 layer 13mm gypsum with noisekiller (GG), 90mm stud with 90mm insulation. All is buildt on a floated wooden deck, works like a charm. The second wall is 120mm concrete with an additional airtight door, can't hear much of anything going through that, maybe some really low thump from the kickdrum, it's barely audible and definitely not heard during sessions. Couldn't have done it without help from the guys at this forum.. a :arrow:
