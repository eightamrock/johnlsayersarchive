TomVan7 June 2007#206
As summer approaches the temperatures are rising. Here in Portland we had an unusualy hot week, last week. With the AC running full blast in the studio it stayed at a comfortable level. I have two portable 11000 BTU heat pumps 875 square feet of space. Ceiling height varies from 8'-14'. 12" gable fan running through a silencer, Silencer intake at opposite end of building. 3- 4", fresh air, running through silencers that pipe into interior rooms All double wall construction. Now this is what I noticed. First I needed to do some electrical work and opened up the hatch to get to the elctrical panel. which is positioned on the exerior wall. When I opened it up the hot air that was behind the walls and ceiling came blasting out extremely warm. So I went to check my gable fan just to make sure it was running. It was At that point I started thinking about the section between the doors. This area is basically wide open to heat coming in.... and cold for that matter. Stuffed with insulation and then covered with fabric. So then I started thinking everytime doors beween rooms are open the AC is being sucked up into the space between the wall and ceiling, then blasted outside through the gable exhaust. Or the heat radiats into the space if AC is off via area between doors. As far as the fresh air ducts they are pulling in outside temperatures. We had a couple days of 90 derees last week. The building is in direct sunlight all day. So the building exterior is getting very warm. Haven't got the electric bill yet but was wondering if anyone had ideas to lessen the heat factor behind the walls or stop it from entering via section between doors. Tom

TomVan22 September 2009#212
Hey Funky, Still hang around watching others build and help when I can I thought I had it all figured out when I started but understood it better as I got into the build. Yes I used T-111 plywood on the exterior and placed 16' wide stips of gyp/sheet rock between the studs on the interior. Caulked interior with over 90 tubes of caulk. That original concept was for existing structures. Like I said I was learning as I was building. If and when I do this again I will put one layer of OSB and one layer of T-111 plywood and or siding on the exterior and save tons of time by having a 1 & 1/2" outermass. And not have to cut and caulk so many pieces of gyp board. Peace Tom
