knightfly13 February 2007#115
While that's a good guide for ENERGY and MINIMUM requirements, I guarantee that if you use that guide you'll be back wanting us to help "lick your wounds" long before you're through wanting to use your studio - besides the major headache of first GETTING, and later MAINTAINING, a good seal on your doors, keep in mind that glass weighs just under 4 TIMES what gypsum weighs - unless my eye is off, your big window looks like a 3070; which also means that you're using 16" centers for your framing - this will cost you maybe 2-3 dB of TL at the low end, where it's hardest to come by. 24" centers keeps a wall a bit more "floppy" in the panels, which works kinda like the "soft" martial arts... Also, since glass weighs nearly 4 times what gypsum weighs, if you use 3/8 and 1/2" glass the total weight of glass in the light side would run around 9 PSF, or about 200 pounds - the thicker side would run maybe 225. Given that, does your framing still look strong to you? Now, add to the fact that you're using double frames - this means that having extra wood inside the wall will NOT increase coupling to any degree, and where it does, the MASS will be increased to compensate for any decrease in average air gap depth - so the only DOWN side to beefing things up here is more cost up front, a bit more labor, in exchange for knowing you won't ever have to do it AGAIN. Your choice, I think you already know the way I would do it... Steve
