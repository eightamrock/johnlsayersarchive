just need to find the perfect listening position and speaker placement.
Well that's easy! :) There's math for that... combined with a bit of experience... :) Your room is almost long enough to give you two options: Either have your speakers away from the walls, or have them tight up against the front wall. For both of those options, there's math that will get you to the right point, but then you need to change the treatment. Let's do the most likely one first (especially considering that you will be making the room shorter in the future, when you do the vocal booth). Set up your speakers on stands, vertically, such that the acoustic axis of each speaker is 123cm above the floor (51"). NOTE!!! ACOUSTIC AXIS!!!! Not the top or bottom of the cabinet, and not the tweeter: you need to find out where the acoustic center of your speaker is, and set that at the right height. All of the measurements I'm going to give you are for that point. The stands must be very massive (heavy). For speakers, get rid of those huge monsters. (Are those Geithains? they sort of look like it. Or Tannoys, maybe?). Those are way too big for that room, and you'd never be able to position them well. Stick with the Genelec's. So set them up on stands 123cm above the floor, and 10cm from the front wall. That's the distance between the rear corner of the speaker, and surface of the wall. Each speaker needs to be 129 cm from the side wall (acoustic axis). That means that they will be 202 cm apart. Now set up your chair on the room center-line such that your head (ears) will be 245cm from the front wall, and set up a vertical pole of some type (mic stand for example) at a point on the room center-line 280 cm from the front wall (in other words, 35 cm behind your head). Use a laser pointer temporarily taped on top of each speaker, directly above the acoustic axis and lined up perfectly with the acoustic axis, the rotate the speaker until the laser is aimed directly at that vertical pole. If you have two laser pointers, put one on each speaker and get both laser dots lined up perfectly at the exact same spot on the pole. After you do that, check that both speakers are still 10cm from the front wall and 129 cm from the side walls. Adjust as necessary, until everything is perfect. That's the theoretical best position. Do not put the desk in yet! Move the desk right to the back of the room, and also all the other objects in the room (organ, other speakers, equipment, etc). Everything must go to to the back. Right! :) It's the hard, solid, massive surfaces of the room that matter. If you have insulation in front of that, then ignore it for this arrangement. So just because I have 10 cm of insulation and fabric on both side walls. This doesn't mean that the room dimension has change right?
Right! Weeeellllll... sort of... There's two concepts here: For low frequencies, modal response, decay times, SBIR, and suchlike, what you are interested in is the actual solid walls of the room itself. But for some acoustic calculations, it's the face of the treatment device that matters, and often that is also in relation to the wall as well! So it can get complex... :)
In that case I cannot place the speakers 10 cm from the actual front wall. because the whole front wall has 20 cm insulation and then fabric
Then place the speaker with the rear corner almost touching the fabric. The extra thickness of insulation is good, but the extra depth from the wall, not so much. At 20cm you are getting close to the limit of how far the speakers should be away from the actual wall. You are still OK, but don't move them further away than that. The greater the distance between the speaker and the wall, the lower is the first cancellation frequency. If it is too low, then the absorption won't do much too it, because the wavelength is too long, so it's a "double whammy". The greater distance forces the frequency lower, and lower frequencies are more problematic and more noticeable, but lower frequencies are harder to treat because of the longer wavelength, and less effectiveness of porous absorption.... At 20cm you are still OK: the first dip is at about 430 Hz, but at 40 cm it would be 215 Hz, which is getting to be problematic, and beyond 80cm it gets down under 100 Hz, which is a big problem... 10cm of insulation can have a good effect on 430 Hz, but it won't have a good effect on 100 Hz...
But then your dimensions are spot on. ( of course)
