bassman4 December 2006#37
Just my 2 bits here but I think you need to take the whole cavity behind your false wall and stuff it FULL, as in solid, no gaps, of 3-5 lbs/ft3 of rockwool, floor to ceiling. You need a great deal of broadband absorption. If the hangers didn't do it, maybe the solid rockwool will. Helluva lot let expensive than adding masonry to bring symmetry to the back wall. Also, the roof might be your problem. With that ceiling, its like you have a corner directly above your head, more reflections at any angle. Next, try filling that space with wool and see if something changes. The room is too small to do anything but massive broadband absorption, IMHO. -bassman

gullfo13 December 2006#44
one feature of moving closer to the front is that your mix position is not in the middle of the room so some of the modes your were experiencing are now behind you... another consideration - do your soffits extend all the way to the floor or are the lower parts absorbing? do you have a carpet on the floor under the console? you may need to get the ceiling symmetry corrected. you could (potentially) add a sloped low-front, high back false ceiling and put in some hanging absorbers above it. that would help with symmetry and add some bass absorption (plus give you that cool control room look...:) )
