Soundman20202 September 2011#11
a) gypsum plasterboard - 2 sheets b) gypsum fiber board - 1 sheet c) hydrobit (right word?) 3 kg/m2 between 2 sheets for demping - which layers? should I use it (highly recommended by most constructors here in Russia based on Ph.Newell's book)
I'm not sure what "hydrobit" is: Maybe you could post a link to some information about it? Maybe it is some type of roofing paper? In any event, building a leaf from multiple layers of different things is not generally advisable, unless there is slid laboratory research to show what it actually does. It is better to build in a way that is known to work and has been tested amply. The best way is therefore to simply put two layers of 5/8" (16mm) fire-rated drywall (gypsum board) on one side of the studs. If you need extra low-frequency isolation, then use Green Glue between the layers. Green Glue es specifically designed to be used as constrained layer damping in this application, has been tested both in acoustic laboratories and in numerous installations, and works. While sandwiching other things in between MIGHT work, you'll never know for sure. It might also make things WORSE. Unless there is published laboratory data to show exactly how "hydrobit" reacts in different types of walls and under different circumstances, then you have no way of knowing if it will do good things or bad things to your wall. So just go with 2 layers of ordinary 5/8" drywall on the studs. If you need more isolation, then use three layers of drywall, instead of two, or increase the gap behind the drywall. These are know to work, and there are equations that show exactly how they work. Regarding your speakers: I don't think you would be able to soffit mount those, due to the strange angels and multiple depths of the front faces. I expect that would cause diffraction issues if you tried to soffit-mount them. Maybe Eric can comment on that: he knows speakers pretty well. Also contact Genelec and ask them. They made the speakers, so they can tell you if it is possible to soffit-mount them (flush mount), and if so, how to do it. - Stuart -
