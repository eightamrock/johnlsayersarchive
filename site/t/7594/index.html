sharward30 November 2006#3
I think Rod's book has some cross-section illustrations of how to handle this -- he drew up a great technique for getting your mass around ducting in a decoupled fashion. One possible snag with the plan of building one wall at a time is that building permits usually expire after a certain number of months. In my case, mine expires six months after date of issuance, but each inspection I receive automatically extends the expiration another six months. So, you'll want to pace yourself in such a way that you won't put your permit in jeopardy of expiring. In my city, an expired permit can be reactivated by paying a fee equal to half the original plan check fee, but if any building code or planning regulation changes went into effect since the original plan check, you'd be obligated to meet those newer, usually stricter, requirements. :roll: My city allows one "freebie" six month extension. I used mine already. :P Another potential problem is that it's usually considered ideal to finish all framing before working on drywall, and then when doing drywall, doing a layer on the ceiling, then a layer on the walls, then the ceiling, then the walls, etc. There are a number of threads about this technique of interleaving your wall and ceiling layers. Finally, make sure that door is big enough to move a water heater in and out. If that's a 30" door, your clearance is more like 28", especially if it can only open 90 degrees (unless you pop the door off its hinges). --Keith :mrgreen:
