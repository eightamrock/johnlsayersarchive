Ted White23 February 2011#2
I would not recommend resilient channel for several reasons. Decouple with clips and channel, but not with existing drywall. You should remove the drywall or face very limited improvement due to the triple leaf. Using channel directly on the ceiling is a commonly used example of what not to do. I would also suggest stepping the drywall up to double 5/8"

Most likely dichotomy. A coupled assembly is that...coupled, and I haven't seen it mentioned as a two leaf...a two-sided assembly certainly. A free mass, uncoupled, and an air space, another free mass, uncoupled and an air space and then yet another uncoupled free mass, that is a 3 leaf as I understand the terminology. While I do not dispute the reference completely, it is to me, a misconception since the air in a coupled assembly does not have the same impact being bound as it is by the framing bays which produce a different result then free air applying it's medium, as it were, on the opposing boundaries. Still....your answer is the most correct since it is essentially entirely too small an air space with entirely too much field of vibration from the existing boundary.

Soundman202024 February 2011#12
"Breaking the seal" is why I am confused about the ridge vent (and soffit vents), the attic is clearly not sealed.
It doesn't need to be sealed in order to create a third leaf. All that you need is a large fairly massive surface on the other side of an air gap. Sealing improves overall isolation for other reasons, but the leaf is a "leaf" simply because it is a large surface, with appreciable mass, on the other side of an air gap. Somewhere there's a link to a report by a studio builder that illustrates this principle perfectly: he happened to hold up a large sheet of some material (plywood? drywall?) close to an existing MSM structure where loud music was playing on the other side. The simple fact of holding that sheet a few inches away from the wall was enough to re-tune the wall so that the resonant dip moved way up into the audible spectrum, and all of a sudden he could clearly hear the music playing on the other side of the wall. I'll see if I can find that link. I know I have it somewhere.... Regarding your question on cutting holes in the drywall: as John pointed out, that will work fine as long as the holes are large enough and there are enough of them to reduce the effect of that 3rd leaf. But if you are going to knock a bunch of large holes in your wall, then why not just take off that drywall completely? Not much extra work, and it would have the additional benefit of giving you access to the wall cavity, so you can add the (additional) insulation easily, if needed. Do you know if the existing wall is well insulated? Not putting insulation in the cavity correctly can cost you a lot of isolation. - Stuart -
