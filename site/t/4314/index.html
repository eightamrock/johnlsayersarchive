Hi My name is Frank, I have to say what a great resource this site has been so far. I'm a relatively inexperienced at construction .I'm building a studio in my basement. My goal is to be able to record myself and others singing and playing without disturbing the rest of the house. I don't play terribly loud and I don't mix terribly loud either. However I would like to get the isolation good enough to record drums if I want to and also to be able to sing and play at night without disturbing anyone. My idea is to have a control room with maybe an iso booth for doing vocals. If I record drums they would be in the control room. Most of the time I will probably use electronic drums. If the studio is isolated enough I would prefer one big room to two smaller rooms. I've read the posts suggested by Steve for the most part and I've gone through a lot of other threads looking at the projects to answer the questions I've had as I've been planning my own project. I've also gutted the existing space I'm going to use so that all that's left are the 2x10 joists and plywood floor in the ceiling, cement foundation and 2x4 framing and outer plywood on the walls plus the double paned windows on three walls and the cement floor. The fourth wall was ripped out totally which leaves direct access to the laundry room and the outside unfinished wall of the downstairs bathroom. The floor above is two bedrooms and it is 5/8 plywood with underlay and carpet on it. The ceiling height is 7'11" from the concrete floor to the bottom of the ceiling joists. The inside leaf of the bathroom is finished but the side facing the studio area is not. I also shortened the heat vent that ran the width of the room so it is no longer in the room at all and I re-routed the heat vents to the bedrooms upstairs so they come out in a different place. My next planned step is to put 2 layers of 5/8" drywall between the studs on 3 walls and the ceiling and to build a new fourth wall where there isn't one between the studio and the rest of the house. This will also be 2 layers of 5/8" drywall. I understand I need to caulk with accoustic caulk after each layer. After that I plan to build another room inside that. I also plan to float the floor using neoprene and insulation etc.. and build the ceiling on RC. I plan to put a min-split air-conditioner in and also another electric panel to service the studio seperate from the rest of the house. My overall budget is probably around $10,000 to $15,000 Canadian. Tomorrow I will go and try to buy a sound level meter. I vacillated on "to build or not to build a floating floor" for months. I decided not to. (Although I am going to pour a new isolated slab.) That being said, you may want to consider not floating your floor, and then having a separate ceiling frame resting atop your new inner leaf wall frames, much like I'm planning to do on my project. If you're concerned about headroom, you can interleave your ceiling joists so that they go up between your existing upstairs floor joists. What is the height measurement from floor to bottom of joists right now (not counting the beam)?
I plan to put a mini-split air-conditioner in and also another electric panel to service the studio seperate from the rest of the house.
What about ventilation? Mini splits do not provision any fresh air -- they only condition the (stale) air that is already in the room. In my project I've spec'd a heat exchange ventilator to supply me with fresh air and to save some energy too. This, too, was something I struggled with for a long time before finding that solution. That's all I have for now... I'm sure others here will have other comments or questions -- and they may even disagree with me... It wouldn't be the first time for that! ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:

drfrankencopter7 September 2005#15
Hi Bondsong, The LePage stuff isn't that bad....but you need to know its limitations. It cannot be used to span gaps, it needs good contact with the adjacent material, and wants to be applied into a corner. Apply it thin, and it will work....apply it thick, and gravity will pull it right down. I'm using it for my build, because the price is right compared to the alternatives. But, I will not be using it for my ceiling as I don't think it will be able to hold up against gravity when applied to a ceiling surface. You need varsol to clean it up too....and do take care to clean it up right away because it has a tendancy of finding its way onto your shoes and then through the rest of the house. IIRC, Safe 'n Sound and AFB are pretty close in density. AFB is a little more dense, and likely marginally better, but I can vouch for the Safe 'n Sound product, its good stuff. The 24" sheets are kindof tough to work with since they are not as stiff, and tear more easily, but the 16" sheets are great. Cheers, Kris
