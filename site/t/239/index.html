knightfly5 May 2003#4
Here's one way to do it - the dimensions need to be adapted to your window - the total depth to the flange (before attaching the foam weatherstripping) needs to be exactly the same as the depth of your window frame, from the inside moulding measured to the window channel. In other words, the outer face of the plug should touch the window just as the inner flange touches the inner moulding of the window frame. Then, when you glue/stick on the foam seals at inner and outer perimeters (see drawing) you will get two full seals separated by a few inches of air space. This plug will be heavy, as it's made from particle board - sheet rock would be almost as heavy, and not as durable. Get two of the Stanley heavy duty barn door handles, at most hardware departments of either hardware or variety stores. Mount them so the long screws penetrate into the 2x lumber frame - that frame is where you adjust the total depth of the frame so that both seals contact their respective sealing surface at the same time. Mount the handles at a height that lets you grip the handles with your elbows against the front of the plug at a comfortable height when the plug is in the window - that way, you will have good leverage to insert/remove the plug. If the seals don't stay tight all around, you could install some molly screw anchors in the wall and use some brackets that rotate to hold the plug up against the window moulding. Edit, 4-2-05 - since I drew this, I've done some calculations that indicate you may get better isolation if you ELIMINATE the two layers next to the glass, and instead add one more layer to the inner mass (where the handles go) - this eliminates a 3-leaf situation, which would hurt the LF performance of the plug. Best way of doing the new plan is to use slightly thicker insulation than the depth of the space between the inner mass and the glass; that way, the insulation presses lightly against the glass and damps that panel. Even a thin glass, when acting as one leaf in a 2-leaf barrier, will work pretty well if a 4" or deeper air/insulation gap is used, along with a THICKER inner leaf (such as 3 layers of MDF) - All other details should remain the same... Steve

knightfly4 October 2005#6
Sorry, somehow I missed this - yes, the inner insulation should be exposed to the glass - if you must have something more pleasing against the glass for outside appearances, it should be perforated or firmly against the glass to avoid 3-leaf problems. For sealing, any closed cell soft foam will work - its only purpose is to provide an airtight seal, so softer stuff will conform to irregular surfaces better. For inside walls (and inside window plugs) plain old spun fiberglass insulation works as well as anything, and is cheaper and easier to find in most locations. If you can't get this, then maximum 3PCF (48 kG/m^3) mineral wool or rockwool is about the same performance... Steve
