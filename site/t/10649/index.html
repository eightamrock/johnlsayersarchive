adam26 May 2008#1
Hi All! I'm 5 weeks away from settling on a new house on the Gold Coast with plenty of space to build a new standalone studio from scratch. Any/all critique on my design is very welcome! The Sketchup plan, with tabs detailing the construction sequence, is here. It's complete except for cloud/lighting fixtures and hanger trap details. Some design considerations: All room ratios were determined by Eric Desart's graph of Louden ratios Maximum exterior wall height of 3 metres so it doesn't dominate the yard too much. The entire block is almost perfectly flat. I've costed the current design and it's just within the available budget of around AUD$90k. Construction will be mostly DIY where allowed. Bathroom & kitchenette need to be located at the south-west corner for proximity to water & waste services. Isolation: There is a suburban railway line nearby but trains were inaudible from inside the house (standard double brick construction with 3mm glass doors) so I'm confident that 200mm core-filled concrete block, 200mm insulated gap, and 2x16mm plasterboard layers will provide adequate isolation. I'll test that properly when we move in next month. The only thing I'm not happy with are the live room silencers, I'd really appreciate any ideas or suggestions with those.

adam14 January 2009#10
Hi Neil, Thanks! I stuggled with that same the first-reflection-on-side-windows question myself. As I understand it, the problem is mitigated by the dispersion pattern of the speakers. If you draw the first reflection path, you'll see that it's more than 45 degrees off-axis from the speaker. The higher (directional) frequencies which you're trying to avoid for first reflections should be significantly attenuated beyond 45 degrees - depending on your monitors. The large angled wall in the live room is something that I'll need specific advice about, but I imagine it will end up being a wall frame filled with insulation, covered with cloth and timber slats, with hangers on the inside. I'll leave these treatments until last, so they can be based on actual measurements in the room.
