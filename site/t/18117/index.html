Soundman20207 February 2013#2
Hi "Bass", and welcome! :)
The east end of the room (where the control area would be) is flat like a normal basement- but just west of the wall I want to add (in red) the whole floor starts sloping down.
There's a reason for that slope: the architect must have expected water there, and that's his way of getting rid of it. I'm guessing there is some kind of sump or drain at the bottom of the slope?
1. Balance on monitoring position: will having the east wall underground and west wall (to be added- in red) with a room on the other side throw off the stereo balance?
That should be fine. As long as the new wall is fairly massive and doesn't act as a panel trap, there shouldn't be any effect on the symmetry.
2. Is it going to compromise the design of the booth to shorten its external height about 7 inches?
Sound waves like space. Things sound better if you have bigger, taller rooms, so reducing the height by 7 inches means it won't be as good as it could have been, but that doesn't mean that it will be bad. And there's one overriding concept for most studio builders: If the choice is to have a not optimal studio or no studio at all, then "not optimal" wins out, any day! :) Suitable treatment can help a bit.
Assuming that's feasible, the next issue would be what to make the floor of the booth out of, since it would need to 'float' in order to be level.
To lose the least amount of height, I would just pour in some self-leveling cement. Trying to float a floor is no easy task, especially such a small one: it will be very hard to get enough mass on it to actually make it float. Also, have you considered trying to interleave your new ceiling joists for the booth between the existing joists above you, on the subfloor? You can save several inches like that. Another alternative is to build the ceiling "inside-out", with the joists underneath and the drywall on top. That way, you can leave just a very small gap between the drywall and the subfloor joists. - Stuart -

Soundman20207 February 2013#4
Does "fairly massive" mean more than two sheets of dry wall, 703 and 2X4's?
If you mean two sheets of fire-rated 5/8" drywall, then yes, that would be fine. But if you want isolation from the rest of the basement, then that would only be your inner-leaf, and you'd need something similar for the outer leaf: another set of studs, also with 2 x 5/8" drywall.
Should I look at using brick or something?
You could, but it isn't really necessary. Drywall does a pretty good job of isolating for the vast majority of studios.
My main concern was if that would effect the dimensions in a way that should compensated for somewhere else in the design? Like it would create nodes or something?
I think you mean "modes" not "nodes". :) But modes are a fact of life, regardless of the room size. Modes exist simply due to the dimensions of the room, and making the ceiling lower does not "create" or "delete" modes: It just means that the modes will be at a different frequency, which might or might not be good...
certainly wouldn't be opposed to that, but that would probably require a go ahead from a structural engineer? Not quite sure that would be in the budget...
Well, it is always good to get a structural engineer involved in a studio build, due toe large amounts of mass that will be hanging over your head, but it makes no difference from the point of view of where the new joists go: They have to be dimensioned correctly to carry the load of the ceiling, regardless of whether they go above the drywall, or below it, or whether or not they are interleaved between existing joists if they go above the drywall. The load is still the same.
The "inside-out" option sounds like it might be more feasible in this space. Would that mean just using twice the drywall on the top and leaving the frame "open" on the inside with 703 between the booth joists?
You don't need double the drywall: still the same amount. The only difference is that it goes on top of the joists, not below them. The amount of mass does not change. OK, caveat on that: It MIGHT change in the specific case that putting the drywall on top would then bring your inner leaf too close to the outer leaf, such that the MSM resonant frequency rose too high to be useful for your isolation goals. But that is rather unlikely with typical basement ceiling joists, and especially considering this is just a vocal booth. If it were a drum booth, or if it were under smaller 2x4 ceiling joists, then that might be different, but you are probably OK. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 April 2014#10
I wouldn't fill them with sand. Just push some fiberglass insulation down each end, and cap it liberally with acoustic caulk. That way you can pry off the caulk and pick out the insulation if you ever need to pull more cables. You'd never be able to get the sand out, if you filled them with sand. Use ordinary electrical conduit. - Stuart -
