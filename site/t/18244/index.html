Hey there Morgan, and welcome! :)
I just moved in to this weirdly shaped space
You sure know hot to pick 'em, don't you? :) That's a complicate shape...
wrapping my head around this three-dimensional space is driving me crazy.
It's actually four-dimensional: sound happens in the time domain, too... :) (Just when you thought it couldn't get any more complicated....
3. I am planning on mixing with a combination of headphones and monitors. True symmetry seems basically impossible, but what's the guess for where I should place the monitors?
Actually, symmetry is possible! :) Not perfect, but still usable. Set up your "control room" section in "B", facing the two windows. That's your best shot.
Am I totally nutters to think that this might not end up being that horrible?
Being totally nutters is actually the most basic requirement for a studio builder: nobody in their right min would attempt to do what we try to do! So you are fully qualified now. But to answer your question: Yes, I do think it can work out.
2. Where and in what orientation should voices/amplifiers be in this room? (B or C)
I would use "C" as the "live room" part of the studio. Gobos, treatment, and it will be good. I would also use "A" for the "vocal booth" section, as that gets you about as far away from "C" as you can get, but still with good sight lines.
5. For a given answer to questions 2 and 3, do you think of any particular deviations from recommended treatment practices? As I get recommendations, I can respond with my initial treatment plans.
Set up your "control room" in "B" firstly, using the standard layout, and treat that accordingly, then test with REW to see how it is doing. Checkboard absorption panels on the walls of the live room section, and kill the walls of the "vocal booth" section, with thick insulation. That would be one way of doing it.
