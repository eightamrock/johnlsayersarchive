How to apply the normal rules of wall construction, specifically with regards to backing rod and caulking, when you are just making what amounts to a 'patch, and not a full wall build?
You'll need to cut the drywall back a bit further probably, so you can get enough space to put some cross-bracing in there, horizontally between the studs, to use as the nailing surface for the repairs. It doesn't look like you have enough space there to swing a hammer to get that in place. Once that is in place, then apply caulk liberally around the edge, nail the first layer of drywall in place, put in backer rod and caulk as needed, then do the second layer on top of that, also with backer rod and caulk. If this is the final finish surface of the room, then don't bring the caulk flush to the surface: leave it sunken, so you can mud and tape on top, then sand and paint as needed. If the joints won't be visible, then you can just leave the caulk overflowing liberally, all over. Yes, cut the drywall a little smaller than the actual hole, to allow for the backer rod and caulk. 1/4" might be too much, depending on the size of your backer rod: you want a sung fit, so the backer rod does a good job of sealing. The gap has to be small enough that you need to push the backer rod in, and here are no gaps or cracks around it once it is in place. But the important point is to get solid wood behind the joint, so you have something to nail/screw the drywall into. You can't just leave the joint floating over nothing. I also noticed that there's no insinuation in the wall cavity, so don't forget to put that in again, before you seal it up! Missing insulation can cost you a LOT of isolation. - Stuart -
