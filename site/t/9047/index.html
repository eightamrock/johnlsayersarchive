sharward30 June 2007#2
As a rule, glass needs to be (ideally) laminated (helps dampen resonance) and be roughly 1/3 the thickness of the drywall* it's trying to match. (*Drywall = gypsum wallboard.) In your case (concrete), it might be possible to get away with 1/4 the thickness because concrete is more dense than drywall. Since your concrete is 150mm, you're looking at 40mm to 50mm. It seems like you're describing a single leaf system. You'll make major improvements by building another leaf parallel to that one. Finally, STC is complete MEANINGLESS CRAP when it comes to isolating drums. --Keith :mrgreen:
Eric_Desart30 June 2007#3
Hi keith. Here a mistake (slight reasoning error). Glass weighs ca 2500 kg/m3. Poored concrete depending on type/mix between 2000 and 2200 kg/m3 With drywall the mass of the drywall is only ca 1/3 of glass (also depending on type). That's where this rule of thumb originates from. The idea is to use as much mass of glass as drywall on each side, with a bit more for the glass to compensate for the lack of cavity absorption (which can partly be compensated by edge absorption). And using the max space gives some additional compensating gain also since the glass thickness difference with the drywall leafs can be used to add to the cavity. This means that to get to the concrete your window pane should become thicker then the drywall ratio, not thinner. But Govinda is indeed mixing a single leaf with a double leaf system here with different TL behavior. In theory one should aim to 2 panes with a cavity were the resonance of the double pane should about equal the TL of the wall at the same point. But no time to calculate this now. The window the OP described is a good one, but I should increase cavity some more and take care that the frame supporting at least one of these panes outside the wall has ca equal TL than that pane itself. And use edge absorption. But that banging away of these drums, and pretty good now for the wall is subjective. these neighbors will still hear this.

Ro18 December 2007#9
Depends on the matching wall (what material it's made of, what density) Matching a concrete wall of 150mm is difficult, since glass is about as heavy as concrete (density both are somewhere between 2000 and 2500 kg/m3) you'll have to place a 150mm thick glass pane.. ghehe, thaz some heavy shit. Prolly difficult to get too. To match a drywall wall (about 700-800 kg/m3) you'll need a pane about 1/3 of the thickness of the drywall. So a 3sheets 15mm drywall needs 1x15mm glass.. do the math and let the suit keep wearing you. (huh?)
