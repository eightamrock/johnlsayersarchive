building with 2 floors

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 13315.

drumphil27 July 2009#1
Hello I'm looking into purchasing a building that has 2 floors. The building structure has steel posts and beams, concrete on the ground floor and wood floors on the second. Ceiling height on the ground floor is 13 feet, 16 feet on the second. As is, sound travels easily between the 2 floors. I'm thinking of using the 2nd floor for myself, rehearsals and recording, and renting out the ground floor for extra revenue. I want to be realistic about my expectations, is it possible to control the sound going to the second floor from the ground floor? Thanks in advance for your help.
lilith_envy27 July 2009#2
would it be possible to rent the 2nd floor. Is there access via a foyer or outer stair case? Building on the 2nd floor is not the best option for music especially timber. It will save you alot of money and head ache if you build your studio on the slab.

I don't have any experience with studio contruction but I have read Gervais' book. John Sayers27 July 2009#4
Philip - it's really no big deal. You can operate a recording studio on any floor you like. The only difference between a studio above and one below is the flooring barrier, as you pointed out. Below you can build onto the mass of the concrete as Deanne (lilith) suggested, and above you float above it on a floating floor.

jbassino5 August 2009#6
Yes. It can be done for sure. But if you're on a budget, is cheaper to use the first floor and dont float it. If you achieve a good isolation, it wont pass much sound through the rest of the building, and you won't have to spend extra cash on floating a floor
