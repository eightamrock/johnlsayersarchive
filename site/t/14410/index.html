Soundman202026 May 2010#2
Hi Leon. Welcome!
Uses: i would love to be able to use a analoge drum kit in here with minimum distruption to my neighbours.
Looking at your design, that's not going to happen! An acoustic drum kit can easily put out 115 dB. There is no way that a single leaf wall is going to stop that! OK, so it is brick in some places, and that helps a bit, but it is still single leaf, and you show that you are replacing the garage door with a single sheet of drywall?
ceiling: this is still being considered - i have a typical light weight frame and roof tiles. obviously this is going to be a problem but i am also having to consider the fact that it cannot support any real weight
If you cannot support any real weight, then you cannot get any real isolation. Unfortunately, the only way to stop sound is to put mass in the way. Lots of it. There just isn't any other way (unless you can figure how to surround your house with a vacuum...). Mass is number one, decoupling is number two. To isolate, you need to have two leaves in your wall, they each need lots of mass in them, and they need to be decoupled from each other. "Decoupled" meaning "not touching, not connected mechanically, not even by a single nail, and with at least a few inches of air gap between them". What you show on your diagram does not have any of that.
currently i am considering shoving as much insulation up there that i can and stretching open weave material accross the beams to hold it up
That would help the room to sound better, from an acoustical treatment point of view, but it wont do anything at all for your isolation, unfortunately.
any ideas would be appreciated.
A 2-leaf decoupled MSM wall is the only way to isolate acoustic drums at reasonable cost. Since you can't put any extra mass on the existing roof, then that will have to be your outer leaf, and you can compensate somewhat by putting more mass on your inner leaf ceiling, and using a larger air gap than you would have used otherwise. - Stuart -

Soundman20201 July 2010#11
The new design has more mass in it, between the leaves but does not have an air gap.
Ummm.... Yes it DOES have an air gap! You show the air gap on your diagram as being 75 mm and filled with insulation. That is the air gap, and that is fine. In fact, your air gap in this design is much bigger than it was in the previous design. In physics terms, you are building an MSM wall, meaning "Mass-Spring-Mass". The spring is actually damped, to reduce resonance. Your "damped spring" consists of the air and the insulation. Together they form a damped spring. They act together. Take out the insulation (which is what you had in your original design: no insulation) and you lose your damping, which greatly reduces isolation.
In Johns Recording html file it states that this is ok as long as the insulation in the gap between the frame and the brick wall is open cell type.
Yep. Mineral wool and fluffy pink fiberglass are great for this.
The new design has more mass in it, between the leaves but does not have an air gap.
No it does not have more mass in it. The mass of the insulation is practically negligible, for the purposes of calculating your MSM resonance and your isolation. Drywall is many, many times more dense than insulation. The purpose of the insulation is NOT to add mass, but to damp resonance in the air gap.
question: 1) is the new design a better option as it has more mass?
It is better because it has a much large air gap! You have not changed the mass at all, since you show the inner leaf in both cases as being exactly the same: a single sheet of 12.5mm drywall. the new design is better for one reason only: It has a much bigger air gap. However, it still won't give you good isolation, since you still don't have enough mass on the inner leaf. 12.5 mm drywall is too thin to be useful. It suffers from many drawbacks: lack of mass, too flexible, high resonant frequency, etc. I'd suggest that you replace it with 16mm drywall, and if you need really good isolation, then make it two layers of 16mm drywall. And if you need incredibly good isolation for low frequencies, then put Green Glue in between those two layers. So, to summarize, your original design was really bad for three reasons: very small air gap, no insulation, and low mass on the inner leaf. With your new design you have fixed two of those problems by increasing your air gap and adding insulation. Now all you need to do is to fix the mass issue. - Stuart -
