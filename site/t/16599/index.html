Soundman20204 August 2011#2
I read somewhere that "you cannot decouple twice".
Well you CAN if you want to... it just won't do anything! That would be like taking a shower while in the swimming pool: it wont make you any wetter! If you are decoupled, then you are decoupled. Doing it again wont accomplish anything extra.
Is Resilient Channel necessary if you already have a decoupled double walls everywhere?
Not necessary: A waste of time and money.
green glue?
Does not decouple: It is constrained layer damping, a very different principle. Yes, it is very useful and worthwhile, if you can afford it and if you need it. - Stuart -
xSpace4 August 2011#3
"- Are 2x6's (16" O.C.) ok for a 16 foot span if they hold a: 5/8" drywall + green glue + 5/8" drywall sandwich (w/ NO weight from above)? - Are 2x4's (16" O.C.) ok for a 14 foot span if they hold a: 5/8" drywall + green glue + 5/8" drywall sandwich (w/ NO weight from above)?" No and heck No.:) When you say span, is that from outside of the framed wall to outside of the opposite framed wall? If you said yes, then the above answers do not change. If you said no, meaning that it was from inside of framed wall to inside of the opposite side framed wall, then the above answers do not change, and you need to get someone local to help you in this determination. You assume there is no load above or on these joists. I would beg to differ...why do people say that, beg to differ :) Reason why I would bring this up is that you have insulation...you have the weight of the wood, the weight of the sheathing, the weight of the CLD product, you have HVAC, you have silencers, you have wiring...etc., etc. 2X6 with a 5 pound dead load, that is all the things I mentioned above, is expected to span 17' 8"...but is rated for a 5 psf dead load under these conditions. Your conditions match or exceed this limit...thus...no :) On the best of days 2X4 #2, will not make this span even if it is rated @ 5 psf dead load. So you need to talk to a real fella on your side of the screen, and ask him or her if you should bump both materials up to 2X8 and 2X6 respectively.

xSpace13 August 2011#5
Here's the thing...the information I gave you came directly from the Industries suggested spans with the lumber in question. Nothing has changed...the material you are wanting to make this span with is NOT rated to make that span... And doubling the material up does not make it stronger. It pushes the cost up and adds more weight to the already over-spanned material. It COULD be that reducing the layout to 12 inch centers would help you gain more length, but in the real world, the lumber needs to be bumped up to the next larger available size...all things being equal with the current unknown variables that surely exist in this build proposal.
