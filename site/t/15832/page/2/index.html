Soundman2020 wrote:
...marked and cut out the openings from the bottom...
Whoa!!!! You cut great big gaping huge enormous gigantic HOLES in your ceiling???? You won't get much isolation like that! :shock: Especially as the ceiling is coupled to the other room, and to the walls. I think you just made created a major sound leak between your rooms... You might want to re-think your lighting plan, and your ceiling too. Like Brien already pointed out, with your walls coupled to the ceiling and the ceiling coupling the rooms, you already have a pretty big flanking path there. You might want to consider taking out the ceiling on one or the other room, cutting the flanking paths, and doing another kind of ceiling. It's not too late to do that, and doesn't involve too much demo, hopefully. It seems a bit overkill to have great 2-leaf walls with two layers of sheetrock on each side over a 10" air gap, but then coupled ceilings with massive holes in them... That's like my aquarium analogy: you are using glass on three sides and tissue paper on the fourth... Just saying... - Stuart -
To back track to this point for a second. Turns out that the beams holding the ceiling up are also supporting the walls. So there was no way to safely decouple the ceiling without building a more shallow booth interior. My garage is two sided, one side being finished/livable, which is the studio half, and the other half is just your standard garage where years of junk accumulate. Directly over the booth, the ceiling has 3 huge beams that go from the side outside wall of the studio side, all the way through the middle of the garage to the other half's side outside wall. These beams are placed at each 1/4th section of the structures length. One of these happens to go right over the booth. Here's the garage:

DJ Hellfire4 June 2011#42
Day 31-32 was spent building, wrapping, and mounting acoustic panels inside the booth. These came out looking great considering I stuffed 2" Roxul in a 1.5" frame to keep them as low profile as possible. For the backs of the panels I used some cheap muslin. For the fronts I used some good stuff made of cotton. It was slightly expensive. It's no acoustic suede by any means, but it's strong enough that I was able to wrap the fabric REALLY tight, even around the sharp edges and corners of the frame without the fabric ripping. Depending on how much room is left over once the mic is in, I may space the panels an inch or two off the wall. All in all, not bad at all for my first time building these things. I still have one small ceiling panel to make, but I ran out of 1x2.
