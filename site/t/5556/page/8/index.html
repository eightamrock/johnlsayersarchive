len-morgan2 April 2006#182
You can stick with the "traditional" system and as far as where the boxes mount to the studs, just calculate how thick your drywall is going to be and make sure that that much of the box is sticking out from the stud. In other words, if you use 1 layer of 1/2" drywall, the front of the box would need to be sticking out 1/2" (or possibly a scoosh less for looks) from the stud it's mounted to. If you use 2 layers of 5/8", it will have to be sticking out 1 1/4" less the aforementioned scoosh from the front of the stud. len

andy_eade4 April 2006#190
Kathy, I will get down in the basement with the Camera for some pictures later on my beefing up phase. But in essence here was the 13 step process I followed: 1. Mudded all joints, cracks and knotholes in the sub floor. 2. Measured joist spacing (at both ends), and cut drywall leaving a ¼ inch gap around the perimeter. 3. Attached drywall to sub floor temporarily with short drywall screws (check length against depth of drywall + sub floor). I used one every 12 – 14 inches, and only around the perimeter – none in the middle. 4. Mudded the joints and screw heads (screw heads may be overkill). 5. Installed Backer rod into the gap around the perimeter using Keiths hand dandy tool. It looks kinda like a pizza cutter, and is found in the hardware store where the window and insect screen bits are. 6. Applied caulk around the perimeter. 7. Measured and cut the second layer of drywall. 8. Applied Green Glue to the next layer of drywall 9. Attached next layer of drywall using slightly longer drywall screws. (Check length against depth of 2 layers of drywall + sub floor). 10. Mudded the joints and screw heads (screw heads may be overkill). 11. Installed Backer rod into the gap around the perimeter using Keiths hand dandy tool. 12. Applied caulk around the perimeter. 13. Finally, I attached 1x3x8’s to the joists up against the bottom of the drywall to hold it permanently in place. This is what I think Rod is calling “ledger” in his book. And that was it in a nutshell. As you know from my thread there have been some hindsight issues along the way, but I have tried to reflect that learning experience into the above process. It’s great that were both all over each other’s thread. It seems as though we each tackling the same questions but at different times which works out great – only half as much asking! :D Hope this helps and good luck. Andy
len-morgan4 April 2006#191
Kathy, To clarify Andy's response in YOUR situation - YOUR floor is fine. You can use the same technique though on your outer walls to beef them up. Just do it vertically instead of horizontally. Green Glue might not be necessary in your case. The most important part of his post is completely sealing (with backer rod and calk) at each step of the process. You probably wouldn't need the cleats to hold the drywall in (assuming you've got enough "meat" in the outer skin of your barn for the screws to bite into). What you want to end up with is a really solid outer wall that you've "laminated" together. len

It is the eve of May 1st and I have 2 months worth of updated photos to post. For the last few weeks, and for reasons of necessity, you've all noticed I have gone off on a side road and begun some of the interior wall framing even though parts of the floor had not been finished yet. We had successfully worked thru half of the framing when I was able to finally finish up the floor construction. It is now done and here are the photos to prove it! Phase 1: The Floor... IS COMPLETE! Procedure used: • Pulled up two original layers of barn floorboards circa 1900 • Laid down plastic vapor barrier over remaining dirt bays • Built new subfloor joists with 2x6 planks • Laid out hundreds of feet of PVC tubing in sizes 2” to 4” • Ran snakes and cables thru tubing • Ripped cleats for joists to hold the insulation above the dirt floor • Installed 2 layers of R19 insulation in joist bays creating a slight overfill and damping crush • Laid out the first layer of 3/4 OSB plywood flooring with custom cuts around the tubing • Rolled out one layer of 30lb roofing felt paper • Finished off with second layer of 1/2 OSB plywood Below is the first batch of pictures documenting each step mentioned above. The posts to follow will end with photos of the finished floor. Thanks so much for your help this far!
