Thank you. I have to thank those here that recommended pulling up those boards. We were just building the new raised subfloor right over them and I was in the middle of laying down tubing and cabling when someone saw the pictures and said I should salvage it for either money or to use later in the finished room. I started pulling them up only to find a SECOND older layer underneath. By the time I got fully into it I was cursing those who made the suggestion! LOL! I was way off schedule and going down some road I hadn't even mapped out in my plans. Ha!! But I'm very glad I did it. I've been pretty good about keeping a pictoral diary of everything but I somehow neglected to take photos of the planing process. THAT was even harder and longer than pulling up the boards. ALL the nails and bits of rusted metal had to come out of the boards first. Then, Scott had a big planing machine that looked like a giant table saw (that man has everything). He brought it over here in his truck and left it for 2 weeks. It took FOUR full days (two Saturdays and two Sundays from sunup to sundown) to plane those boards down to a nicer finish. Oh, do I wish I had pics of that. Once again, good 'ol Nana was on one end of the planing machine holding the board as it would come out. Some of the boards took 45 passes thru the planer to get them cleaned up! It's because they were all so uneven and different thicknesses. But as a result, what used to be lying on the ground in the early 1900s is now a part of the studio.
Hats off to you Kathy and congrats on your accomplishments, your drive, desire and passion to take this on. Pat yourself on the back for me. I only hope my 2 girls grow up to be as ambitious as you. Tom

THE BIG FINISH: The Entire Studio - Part TWO Second - The Live Area & Drums Ok, so before we get to the control area here's the rest of the live section of the room. The deep blue carpet on the drum riser (and is also in the vocal booth) came from a discount roll I got at Building 19. I bought the padding and laid it down myself. Next to my drums is a computer screen and cables that allow me to bring my Pro Tools 002 rig over there and record drums when by myself. I have a wireless mouse over there. Above the computer cabinet is the main live area patchbay that allows all the connections in the back to be routed underground to my control room patchbays. Basically, that means the outs of the Roland drum brain as well as signals from mini patchbays inside the vocal booth and elsewhere. The last shot is of the drums at night but it didn't come out too well. I ran some of that rope style lighting (blue) around the carpeted riser and the wood walls to "stage in" the drumkit. It looks beautiful at night but the camera doesn't do it justice.

THE BIG FINISH: The Entire Studio - Part THREE Last But Not Least - The Control Room Here we go. A couple of notes: • The carpet I chose for the control room is a silver/gray and had to be put down professionally because of the size and the seams it would need. The snakes that had to be pulled thru were numerous and the carpet guys said they had never in their career seen or done anything like it. LOL! I actually don't have any shots of those areas but will take some later when I do my "I Wish I Had Done This Slightly Different" Epilogue at the end of this almost completed thread. • The old wooden chest you see that is being used as a coffee table in front of the loveseat was actually a lucky find. It is from the late 1800s or early 1900s and is the same color and style as the wooden beams in the barn. We found it up in Nana's attic in her apartment building where it had been abandoned. Not only does it compliment the studio barn perfectly, inside of it is where I have stored all of my digital and analog media as well as software. * The loveseat actually pulls out to a twin bed for sleeping so that's a plus!
