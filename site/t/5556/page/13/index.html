Why is it that such a well insulated building is heating up inside rather than staying cool and keeping the heat out? Can anyone explain that? I thought my biggest problem would be the winters and keeping it warm. Turns out a cheap heater keeps it nice and toasty - I'm sure the insulation and the tightness of the room is the reason. A few things are coming into play here. Technically, insulation retards the migration of warmth. A well insulated house in the winter retains the heat generated inside. A well insulated house in the summer minimizes the amount of heat that transfers from the outside to the inside. Insulation "keeps the cool inside" only indirectly -- it keeps the cool from getting warm, so to speak. If your room were to be a perfect temperature with all of your gear turned off and you not in it, it's only a matter of time before your CPUs, lights, and transformers start to get hot. Where does the heat go? Into the room, of course. It could be cool and pleasant outside, but once the room heats up, the heat is trapped by all the insulation and air-tightness of the construction. In a poorly insulated space, the heat would migrate through the wall to the outside. In your space, this happens as well, but it takes much longer because of all the insulation. Plus, there's effectively a substantial heater in the room when you are there -- YOU! You know how crowded, enclosed spaces get "stuffy"? Well, that's due to the humidity of all the vapor you are exhaling. Get close and personal with your favorite mirror and breathe on it. See all that fog? That's microscopic water droplets condensing on the cool glass. The cool glass attracts the water molecules so you can really see the effect. If you had windows in your room, I'm sure they'd be covered in moisture after just a short visit. Put a humidity sensor in that room and you'll see that the humidity goes up once you've been in there for a while. You should also consider yourself lucky that you are getting uncomfortable, because at this point, I don't think you have yet executed any plans to provision your space with any fresh air! :shock: Go back and reread page 6 of your thread. There you'll see lots of discussion about fresh air ventilation requirements in spaces without operable windows, the fact that mini splits offering fresh air provisioning are rare, etc. Your post this morning is the first we have seen mentioning a specific brand name. I'm unable to find any specs on that brand. What model number are you buying? I have a hunch that it doesn't offer fresh air. More than a year has passed since we discussed this on your thread. Since then, there have been many fruitful discussions about fresh air requirements in air-tight windowless spaces. This is one of my favorites. It gets pretty heated, which is why I had to shut the thread down... But it's full of excellent information and important warnings that I believe should be heeded, including the very real "sick building syndrome (SBS)." Anytime someone dismisses the importance of mechanical fresh air ventilation in a windowless studio, I send him (or her! ;-)) there. Finally, I'm sorry -- I have to say it -- it's the proverbial "elephant in the room." In my infamous "Permits, Codes, Licenses -- and WHY YOU SHOULD CARE!" thread, you wrote:

I'm deeply concerned that if you proceed with the installation of a mini split system that has no fresh air provisioning feature, you'll make your room so comfortable temperature- and humidity-wise that you'll not want to leave, and that then you'll suffer the effects of SBS by inhaling too much carbon dioxide. Kathy, you've created such a beautiful and inspiring space, but it's not healthy. I'll be very interested in seeing how you address this critically important issue. --Keith :mrgreen:
