The layout doesn't make much sense to me. The control room shape has not been optimized, for starters. You're going to want to use a proven control room design. The tracking room has way too many parallel walls. The iso booth and the sound lock/vestibule make sense. Have a look at some of John's designs, and rethink what you have going. If you're gonna go through all the trouble of spending the money and doing the work to build this, you want to do it right.

jwl13 April 2007#4
OK. There are many people on this forum more expert that I, but I can get you started. First thing: go get Rod Gervais' book. Seriously. It will pay for itself many times over, and more importantly you will have a much better studio when you're done. Let's start with the control room. This is arguably the most important room, and the one I can speak the most about. Your room is roughly 11.5x18 based on what I can see. First off, you'll want to think about ideal dimension ratios for your room. p 28-29 of Rod's book goes over those, good ones are: 1:1.14:1.39 1:1.28:1.54 1:1.6:2.33 1:1.40:1.90 1:1.3:1.9 1:1.5:2.1 See if you can get your control room to be one of those ratios. Next, I'd rearrange the room, basically turn it 90degrees from where you have it. have the monitors firing into the long part of the room. It's a good idea to have the side walls, at least from the listening position forward, splayed by at least 6 degrees. Make as much of the room as possible symmetrical from left to right, certainly everything from the listening position forward MUST be symmetrical. One good rule of thumb is to place your listening position 38% of the way back from the front wall. Then place your speakers (whether on stands or in soffits) such that they form an equilateral triangle with the listening position. You can worry about acoustic treatment later, but you'll want to start with an optimized room size/shape. That should get you started....
