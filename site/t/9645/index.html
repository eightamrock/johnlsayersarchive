tmix23 October 2007#4
KnuckleHead wrote:
Should I just resign to the fact that I’m stuck where I’m at? I can’t have both a recording studio and rehearsal room?
Not really. I guess it depends on what you want to accomplish by having a separate control room. For over 10 years I have had a 12 by 22 room for rehearsing a 7 piece band and recording. I wanted some iso for critical tracking so I built a 5 by 7 vocal/ guitar booth and left the rest for a main tracking / rehearsal room. If you need additional iso for noisy computer etc, you can build a 2nd smallish iso room for the noisy equipment. I recently built the new studio (about 900 sq/ft) and I had gotten so used to working alone in a large tracking / mixing room that I made a similar but larger set up in the new place. I think you'll be surprised at how many people prefer to have one larger room that 2 small rooms, particularly if you operate alone most of the time with just a few people. My thoughts anyway. Tom

OK, let's talk treatments then. I'm gathering that I definatly need some bass traps in the corners and some sort of acoustic panels on the walls and perhaps a cloud overhead of my console. What else? How do you acoustically treat a long, oddly shaped room? thanks
gullfo26 October 2007#9
thats the essence of it. corner bass trapping - consider wall-ceiling and wall-floor corners if needed. on the walls and ceiling - staggering the absorbers so they have some gaps and don't completely cover the surface. this way you keep some life in the space and things stay better balanced. use standoffs on the wall and ceiling absorbers to improve their effectiveness. if you have a small budget, start with the corner traps and if necessary, hang some small blankets on the walls (leaving gaps, and folds) to cut the flutter echoes until you can measure the room response and determine what other treatments you need when you can afford it. use only fire retardent treatments to avoid serious injury or death in the event of a fire.
