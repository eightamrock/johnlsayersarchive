Soundman20206 October 2010#27
That sounds like good news! But you do have a noisy environment to deal with. Those levels are a little high to be able to record and mix in! :shock: Have you set a goal, in terms of NC curves, for what you'd like to have in your control room and live room, after everything is finished? You might want to look into that. It seems like you are currently in the ball-park of NC-60, and you probably want something like NC-25, I guess. Does that sound about right? One thing to note: If you are getting a high reading right under the HVAC duct, then that is either due to fan/compressor noise entering though the duct, or to noise from the air itself moving. Either way, that is going to need some treating. Once you get the rooms properly isolated, that HVAC noise is going to be quite noticeable, probably annoying, and definitely problematic for recording. For studios, the idea is to move large volumes of air at very low speeds, rather than small volumes at high speeds, so you'll need to get that modified, and you'll also need to get the ducts lined on the inside, plus in-line silencers and multiple 90° bends in the duct work. You will most likely also need to get the ducts decoupled from the air handler, and also from the support brackets. So HVAC is probably the first thing that you'll need to look at, since that work has to be done before you can start on the isolation. And if you are going to do all that, you might even want to make the most of the opportunity to have the ducts re-routed or use ducts that don't take up so much headroom, so you can get more height out of your rooms. - Stuart -
