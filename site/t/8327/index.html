PhilR6 March 2007#9
geoff_fry01 wrote:
1. Tapered plaster/gyproc is tapered just on 1 long side?? Correct?
It's tapered on 2 edges and square at the narrow ends where it was cut to length. The purpose if the taper is to provide a very shallow channel to allow for a better mud & tape finish.
2. is straight edge actually going to be BETTER or easier but JUST more costly because of backer rod and sealant? My local supplier "Travis Perkins" only stocks straight edge so I can easily get it??
Straight edge has an advantage in multi-layer walls because you don't need to mud & tape it. Just make sure your seams are filled. But tapered is generally used in exposed walls as it can be finished a lot nicer.

My opinion: use the same (tapered or not, preferably tapered) throughout. Yes, you'd need to tape and mud rach layer... geoff_fry01 wrote:
BUT I have found one supplier of backer rod in the uk but it seems pretty expensive in comparison to acoustic sealant..
Yes and paper is much cheaper to cover your walls with than plaster board. They both cover the walls but would you say they are equivalent? Of course not. As Sharward said, it's not a matter of saving calk, it's whether the calk will even work! If you have a large gap to fill, without the backer rod your calk joints would pull away from the surfaces they are supposed to seal and then ALL of your calking is wasted. As I understand it (and did it) you only need the backer rod at the ceiling <-> wall joints (each layer) and wall <-> floor joints. It wouldn't hurt to put it along the vertical corners too. Are 500M and 1000M the only amounts you can get? I did double layers of drywall in a 4 room studio with only 500 FEET of backer rod and still have a lot left over. len
