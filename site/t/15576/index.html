xSpace4 February 2011#6
What winds up happening is the microphones, in a tracking room, are so close to the ceiling that if you do not have an absorptive cloud overhead the early reflections from the ceiling distort what you think you are hearing...but to be fair, a shallow ceiling is prone to compress the sound so it is always a challenge to get good noise in/good noise out. In a control room it is the same thing but with differences only in that usually a microphone isn't being used, but the early reflections still play a big part in what you hear, so you have to take those measures to reduce hard surfaces that are close to the ears first and in difficult places second...but in any event, it's not undoable just has some built in issues but you can overcome these, many, many have here already once reasonable expectations was established.

Only one way to find out: Ask him! :) He' doesn't bight. And he won't get angry if he quotes but you figure you can't afford him. That's what this forum is for: folks who can't afford to pay for a professional design, and are willing to learn spend the time to learn how to do it right themselves. That's why John made this forum in the first place. So I'd definitely go with the wood-frame garage, due to the height. But there are other factors that you might want to consider. Isolating any room (also incorrectly called "soundproofing") implies that you are going to add lots of mass to it. I mean LOTS of mass. You should check that the structure of that garage can handle the extra mass (floor, foundations). You say that it has a concrete floor, it looks decently built, and it IS a garage (designed to have a heavy car sitting on the floor) but all the same it would be a good idea to get a structural engineer to check that, once you have your design done. There will be tons (literally) of mass on there. Which leads to another question: How much isolation do you need? How loud will you be in there, and how quiet do you need to be outside? Conversely, how loud are things around you, and how quiet do you need that to be inside, so that you don't record the neighbor's dogs barking, your phone ringing, street traffic, wind and rain, along with your masterpiece once-in-a-lifetime best-over vocal track! You need to answer that with real figures (in decibels) to determine what type of construction you will to put up, in order to isolate adequately. - Stuart -
