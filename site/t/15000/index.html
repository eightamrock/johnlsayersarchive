gullfo25 October 2010#4
you need to consider that if the isolation walls are asymmetrically, that simply adding some treatment walls may not be effective since the modal activity may not be changed enough. plus adding isolation walls internally could introduce third leaf effects. you might just keep the approx position in front of the window but move the position (along with the monitors) to find a spot where you listening position is good. then add a corner bass trap on the vertical corner plus bass trapping along the top of the room. on the two flat walls add rigid insulation absorbers. consider some widely spaced slats to preserve some high frequencies. otherwise, i think you'd need to consider tearing down some walls and re-doing them and the feasibility (costs) of that may be dependent on the overall configuration of rooms - i.e. opening a can of worms...

i looked at potentially adding a wall across the corner and then turning the desk to face that so effectively the window wall and opposite wall angled away but the room asymmetry is still problematic. so having just the treatments as described is really a first step to leaving as much room volume as possible and trying out the absorption approach to balancing things. worst case, you need to pull the corner bass trap and some off the ceiling and add the wall. this approach just tries things in a least disruptive set of steps...
