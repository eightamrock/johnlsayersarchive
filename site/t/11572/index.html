You are right: carpet probably won't help. The idea of a cloud only up to the tube will help a bit, but extending it more over your mix position would help even more.
- I can not make big bass-traps at the rear wall in the controlroom, because I have the door and the studiowindow there. But the sofa in the back is quite a good basstrap (as I can tell from my old studio-room). Any suggestions what I could do furthermore with my rear wall?
A room has 12 corners, not just two! Bass traps can go in any corner. You do have space across the top of the entire rear wall for a good one, plus the top of the two side walls, plus the corners behind your "bookkshelves", plus the corner across the top of your front wall... You have lots of space for bass trapping. Use it all. You will need plenty in that room. You could also build a "bass trap on wheels" that you can push in front of the door, and another one fixed in the opposite rear corner.
- The cupboard left and right to the center-speaker: What would you suggest: cloth-front (absorbing) or wooden-front (reflecting)
I would suggest not making it into a cupboard! That would make it into a large resonant cavity right next to your speakers, which is not a good idea. Other comments: 1) Why is the glass in the window to the live room angled like that? it reduces isolation a lot. 2) You have the speakers firing the wrong way into the room. Yo have them firing across the short axis of the room (3.4m), instead of along the long axis (4.07m). 3) Your head is 56% back from the front wall, practically in the center of the room. - Stuart -

Soundman202012 January 2009#5
Sorry, I don´t see what you mean. Why "wrong place". What would be a better place.
All your speakers are supposed to be at the exact same distance from your head, but your rear surround speakers appear to bit further away than the front speakers, by a few inches. The rear surround speakers are supposed to be at an angle of 110 degrees. John recommends 115 to 120. Others have gotten good results experimenting at 125 degrees. More recently, some experts even say that 135 is better still. But your rear speakers are at an angle of 104 degrees. That is within the ITU recommendation, but only just (minimum 100 degree), but it is going the opposite way from what current consensus says works best. Fortunately, since your rear speakers are going to be mounted on stands it will be easy to position them correctly. You can probably mark the floor in some way, so that you can always get them back to the exact right place if you move them. Also, for 5.1 you need more absorption on the front wall: DEDE design seems to be the current best recommendation for 5.1 rooms, so you don't get reflections from your rear surrounds bouncing back at you from the front wall. But I think your biggest issue is the "bookcase" thing. I don't see how you can make that work. Soffit panels need to be large and heavy, and the speaker needs to be mounted in an airtight box on a solid decoupled base. I just don't see how you can do that effectively with a bookcase and hinged soffits. - Stuart -
