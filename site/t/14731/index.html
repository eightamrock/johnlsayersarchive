Soundman202027 August 2010#2
There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post (click here). Actually, several people, who are experts on this forum, will most likely not reply if you don't do what is written in that post. Many others who are very helpful, will most likely not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. Several comments: You say you have a concrete floor, but you also say you plan to put a sand-filled "false floor" on top of it: Why do you want to do that? It will do nothing at all for you acoustically, you already have a great floor, it will be hard to do, and will cost money. I don't see why you need it. Next, you say that the dimensions of the room are 20' x 20'. That is bad. It is square. The only shape that is acoustically worse than square, is round. So I'd suggest that you will have to change that by moving walls, or building new walls.
and adding a second sheet of 1/2" drywall.
Bad idea. 1/2" drywall is too thin to be useful. Go with 5/8" drywall.
I figure i can put in a flat ceiling and treat that with some hanging traps.
You want to put hangers on the ceiling? I guess you must have a lot of space up there! I mean, you can if you want, but in order to have useful size hangers in the ceiling, you'd need a lot of room height. How much height do you have available?
What I am most concerned about though are the walls. I need to build a small, but comfortable booth,
You seem to have enough space for that. It should not be a problem. So you basically want to build two rooms, being a control room and a vocal booth? Is that the idea?
and also kill the reflections off the parallels.
I think you are confusing reflections with flutter echo. Parallel surfaces can give you flutter echo problems, which can be treated either by splaying the walls, or by treating the walls with absorption. Both will work, if done right. But splaying the walls will not kill reflections: you will still have reflections, except that they will be at different angles. That might or night not be a good thing, depending on what else you are trying to achieve!
Whenever I try to sketch something out, I still end up with parallels on the left and right,
Maybe you are too concerned about parallels? Many successful studios have parallel walls. Many successful studios do NOT have parallel walls. Success or failure does not depend on whether or not the side walls are parallel. It depends on many other factors. The decision on whether or not to have parallel walls will affect other things, of course, but you don't have to reject an otherwise good design just because your side walls are parallel.
I guess the other option is to position my desk in a place where the monitors won't reflect on the parallels.
Exactly! Search this forum for the terms "RFZ" and "Reflection Free Zone", and read through a few of those threads to get an idea of how to lay out your room. That should set you on the right track so you can post a rough design here. - Stuart -

jhbrandt22 October 2010#7
The control room you have drawn is A-Symmetrical. I would not recommend this approach. Note: Corners focus sound and IMHO, a corner CR is not ideal, unless it's the only place to put it. Why not build a good ratio CR at the bottom of the room and have the entry door open into the Tracking room?? How about a CR 13' 10" by 11' 2 1/8"? leave the rest for tracking. I just re-read your building materials list... That's a very expensive way to build. :cop: You would be better off using type X gypsum board and regular thermal building insulation in the cavities. Also, plexi-glass is a very poor isolator and not recommended for many reasons. Use either laminated safety glass or tempered glass - minimum 13mm thick. Green Glue is not glue, it is a Constrained Layer Damping Material. It is only 'called' glue in it's name. Do not caulk seams with this stuff! Use OSI SC-175 or similar. - or any good acoustic rated caulk.. even Butyl roofing caulk does well for acoustic sealing. Buy Rod Gervais' book http://www.amazon.com/Home-Recording-Studio-Build-Like/dp/1598630342/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&s=books&qid=1262507469&sr=8-1.. This will help you a lot. cheers, John
