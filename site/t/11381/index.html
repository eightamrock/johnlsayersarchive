Before you pour that slab, I'd suggest that you take a break and get your design totally in place first! that new slab can be a big part of the solution, if you do it right. Or it can be a big part of the problem, if you do it wrong. So my first suggestion would be to hold off on doing anything drastic, like pouring slabs, until you have your plans absolutely defined. For example, pouring that slab in two isolated sections, one for the outer leaf wall to stand on, and the other for the inner leaf and the floor, could make a big difference to your overall isolation. Soundman202011 November 2008#11
OK, so you are stuck with the slab. Pity. But what's done is done. Regarding your "shell":
My contractor is going to frame the building with 2x4's 16"oc then OSB covered in tyvek home wrap then hang smart panel siding ... Does it make a difference if I add mass on the indie of the first leaf? "
Sure, you can beef up that outer leaf by adding a few layers of drywall to the inside of your OSB, in between the studs, carefully caulking each layer as you go, and holding it in place with cleats. There are several examples here on the forum of people who have done that. Maybe it's not optimum, but it works, as long as you do it well. Clicking on your link to the "smart panel siding" web site still does not work, so I can't really comment on that. In fact, even if the link did work I still wouldn't want to comment on it, because the acoustic properties of siding is not something I understand. I guess that you'd need to make sure that it doesn't create a third leaf, and that it doesn't vibrate or rattle, but I hope that someone who really DOES understand siding can comment. I guess 16¨O.C. is OK too, but I've seen numerous recommendations here to go with 24¨OC, for the purpose of getting lower resonant frequencies, but I'm not sure if that applies to outer leaf walls with siding, or just to inner leaf walls. By the way, when you say "wall mounted electirc", do you mean that all your electrical work is going to surface mounted, outside the walls? Or that it is going to be inside the walls with switches / sockets poking through? One other comment: Did your contractor make provisions for some place to put the heat pump end of your mini-split system? If possible it would be good to have that decoupled from your main slab. Not a huge issue, I reckon, but worth considering. You don't want those things hanging on your stud framing, that's for sure! I have a couple of those at home already, for cooling bedrooms and my home-office, and even though the heat pump end is not too noisy, it still vibrates plenty when the compressor is running. In my case, the unit that cools the bedroom is on a bracket bolted to the outside of the bedroom wall, which a very thick reinforced concrete-and-brick wall, yet we can still faintly hear the wall vibration. If you can attach those units to a separate structure, totally decoupled from your slab and studs, that would be useful. How about positing a plan of your room layout? It's really hard to guess what it might be like!. Also, your roof design looks rather strange: Any reason for that? And how high are your ceilings going to be? The higher the better. - Stuart --

eightamrock15 November 2008#16
If you are planning a room in a room, then you wouldnt put any sheetrock on the inside of your first layer.. Instead you would frame your second room about 1 inch away from the existing wall and only sheetrocking the inside of that leaf.. You can put OC 703 on the exterior wall to add mass instead of layers of sheet rock, that is what someone suggested I do in my ceilings to add mass. IM NOT and expert on this, and it all depends on your budget. Hopefully someone else will chime in here. What I do know that optimally you want to have the same mass on the outside wall as the inside wall to get the best results from a 2 leaf system.. So you will need to calculate how much mass you plan on putting on the inside leaf and mimic it on the outside leaf.. There is a lot of material on this if you read the stickies..

joshF wrote:
The shell: My contractor is going to frame the building with 2x4's 16"oc then OSB covered in tyvek home wrap then hang smart panel siding http://www.lpcorp.com/sidingtrim/lpsmar ... panel.aspx. I know this is not enough mass for first "leaf"... so my first question: Does it make a difference if I add mass on the indie of the first leaf?
If your interested in keeping the work to a minimum, you have options. The exterior wall needs mass as much as the interior, to be effective. But it doesn't "have" to be cut up into small pieces and installed in between framing. You can upgrade the OSB sheathing to 3/4 inch. And then apply an exterior grade gypsum type underlayment directly to the OSB. Then you would install house wrap and siding. EightAM: "You can put OC 703 on the exterior wall to add mass instead of layers of sheet rock, " :), not instead of. The sheetrock adds the required mass and the insulation damps the cavity/panels.
