Hi Talyor, and welcome to the forum! :)
A builder recommended adding supports to the actual ceiling,
You know, it always gets me how builders, who should know better, just don't think things through logically. Ask that builder what he attaches the roof of a house to, up above, when he builds one.... Does he screw some hooks into the sky? So if a house doesn't need support from above, then why would a room inside the house need support, if it is built the same way? :) I mean, there's no reason why YOU should know this, since you aren't a builder, but HE should have been able to figure it out! He builds houses for a living... If a house can stand up all by itself without needing support from above, then obviously if you build a smaller version of that house inside a bigger house, then it too can stand up all by itself with no support from above... So you just frame the walls, and the ceiling, exactly as you would for a flat-roofed house, with OSB on the studs and joists as your first layer, and that's it. It will stand up. Just like a house. :) (If you need more isolation, then add a layer of drywall directly over the OSB.)
I am planning on framing the floor with 2"x4" ... The floor will be filled with sand

Soundman202016 May 2013#5
Yes, we are building on top of a concrete slab, however I figured OSB on the floor joists wold create a drum-head effect. What do you recommend?
Just plain concrete! It's a great surface, acoustically, and anything you put on top means less height in the room. If you don't like the look of concrete, then lay laminate flooring (with the correct underlay), or ceramic tiles, or linoleum, or something like that. The idea is to keep the floor hard and reflective, then treat the ceiling accordingly. There are some very nice finishes available these days in laminate or ceramics. Excellent acoustically, easy to install, and take up very little height. Another option is to polish the concrete, and/or stain it. If you look around the forum, you'll find several builds where the floor has just been stained and looks spectacular.
Sorry for the confusion, I meant one layer of 1/2" and one layer of 5/8". Would you recommend two layers of 5/8?
Yes. For good isolation, two layers of 5/8" is ideal. 1/2" drywall, OSB or ply is not very useful: it's resonant characteristics are not as good as 5/8", so the general recommendation is to do everything with 5/8".
My isolation goal is around 20dB.
:?: :shock: That's about one tenth of what a normal house wall gets you. Maybe you meant an additional 20, above what the walls are giving you already? An ordinary house wall will give you about 30 dB, give or take a few, and 50 dB is good for a studio, so I imagine you meant to say "20 dB more than what you have currently"?
I just need to attenuate traffic noise.
Can you be more specific as to what type of traffic noise, and how loud? It's one thing to isolate typical tire noise from cars driving past at a fixed speed, and another thing entirely to isolate engine noise from heavy trucks or buses climbing a hill. So it would help to understand your problems and goals better if you could provide more info on that, and also on any other types of noises that you need to keep out (eg, wind, rain, hail, thunder, aircraft flying over, radios, TVs, phones, people talking, etc.) - Stuart -
