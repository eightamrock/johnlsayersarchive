Gobo design for maximum bleed reduction vs. flexibility

Started by timmcallister on 31 May 2006. 9 replies, 2006–2008. In the Library under Acoustic treatment.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6339.

timmcallister31 May 2006#1
I've spent the last hour looking through this site for information of actual performance data for gobos and haven't found any. I want to make some gobos for the purpose of attenuation (bleed reduction) between instruments in a tracking room. I am interested in attenuation over flexibility, meaning I don't need a "reflective side". Also - it seems to me the reflective side would probably introduce comb filtering, so I just want to avoid it. The gobos would be 4'x2' and could be stacked. I am thinking a 3/4" or 1" MDF core with 4" of rockwool on each side. No air space between the Rockwool and MDF. Is this the best way to maximize isolation? Any potential problems? Other ideas? THANKS!

riffster18 September 2007#8
First post here. Excellent info here BTW. I gotta say though that I was inspired by timmcallister & jwl to build these gobos with some minor changes. http://riffster.com/gobos/gobo.htm I used 1x10's instead of 1x8's and since I had unlimited access to ceiling tile I used this brand - Capaul Commercial brand 2" medium density fiberglass, NRC 1.0. Anyone here use this stuff? Not sure if these are up to par but they would be simple enough to remove. My 2x4 gobo weighed in at around 48lbs which isn't too bad and am kinda glad that I didn't use jwl's suggestion by adding sheetrock just for the excessive weight (good idea, but too heavy for me) Thanks again for the excellent info here and maybe one day I'll build myself a real studio.
