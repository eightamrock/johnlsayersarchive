jhbrandt4 July 2010#2
The CR is a bit small, below the 1500 cubic feet that I recommend. You will need more trapping than usual. It could work, no obvious issues that jump out at me. I would recommend that for the sake of anyone upstairs that you insulate between the joists and then cover the ceiling with a layer or two of gypsum board to improve the sound isolation. You have enough room to cover the ceiling with absorbent or hang a cloud over the mix position for RFZ. For the Vocal booth, the insulation between the joists and covered with fabric is a great idea as long as there will not be any noise coming from upstairs... You will need a lot of trapping in that booth. The Tracking room is great with all the different areas and angles and can work for a variety of groups/instruments. Cheers, John

Soundman20207 July 2010#6
Like John said, you have a small room, and there is no way you can get it usefully larger. It is going to need heavy treatment anyway, so maybe the counter.intuitive approach is to make it better by making it fractionally smaller. :shock: Also like john said: inches count for good ratios. Like you said: you need isolation. So maybe the solution is to drop the ceiling a bit, fit in some isolation, and get a better ratio to boot! For example, right now you are in the ball-park of Louden's fifth best ratio. By making your dimensions H=8'10", W=10", L=12'4" you hit Sepmeyer's second best ratio right on the head, and have plenty of room for great isolation in your ceiling. Good mode spacing, reasonable Bonello diagram, reasonable overall numbers, just a bit tight on volume. I came up with that after about 90 seconds of playing around with Bob Gold's calculator, and it does eat into your room volume, but you can probably do even better by spending some time with it. You also know better than we do which dimensions you can change more easily, so you can fiddle with them more knowledgeably, until you come up with a good ratio that maximizes room volume and also leaves you space for good isolation. Regarding your bass traps, you said "I have a feeling I'll have to play a little bit of that by ear when I get into the space!". Not really true. Small rooms need lots of bass trapping, period. The trick is to get that done while NOT also killing the highs! So plug in all the bass trapping you can, make it thick and large, but then also take into account that you do NOT want to kill the other end of the spectrum, so think about things like covering the bass traps with thin reflective plastic, and using slot walls liberally to keep the room lively while also getting good broad-band treatment. etc.
As for sound coming in there is very little of it. I'm in the countryside and I got almost no traffic, the occasional dog bark is about as bad as it gets. So that shouldn't be an issue.
No wind? No rain? No planes flying over? No noise elsewhere in the house (TV, vacuum cleaner, people walking around, phones ringing, people talking, pots and pans rattling in the kitchen, dishes in the sink, doors opening and closing, toilet flushing, radio, water running, etc....)? :) Small things, easy to overlook, but disastrous if they ruin your once-in-a-lifetime perfect take of the best performance ever! - Stuart -

jhbrandt25 July 2010#8
Luke, The "Actual" bar in any given 1/3-octave band, should be equal to or exceed the "Ideal" bar. If all "Actual" bars exceed by 50%, this is very good indeed. The room is really too small for diffusors. To maintain a balanced frequency decay, I suggest using some of John's slanted resonators in the RFZ and covering the corner traps with 1/8" plywood polys. You need as much trapping as you can get in that room. Cheers, John
