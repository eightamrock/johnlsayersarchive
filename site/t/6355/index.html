Hello, Say I have a wall made of cinder blocks and concrete and I want to improve the TL in building a second wall in the concept of a mass-air-mass separation. I would have to use the actual wall as one leaf of my two leaf separation and build another one on ONE Side of it. Now on the other side, this concrete wall is not decorated so I would like to add some board of gypsum just to be able to paint. To do this I will have to have some kind of frame again the wall to fix my boards onto. This will create a little space of air and thus will transform my mass-air-mass separation in a mass-air-mass-air-mass one. I know that creating another mass-air layer is almost useless but can it worsen the TL ? Do I need to avoid any air on that leaf or won't it just matter ? Thanks Alex
cyeazel2 June 2006#2
Yes, a second air gap (3 leafs of mass with two airspaces) will make low frequency (bass) TL worse. If you can avoid a second air gap, then by all means do what it takes to avoid it.
alexidoia2 June 2006#3
ah! thanks, I was about to make a mistake ! what are my possibilities then ? Mortar and paint ? Thanks Alex
cyeazel2 June 2006#4
After re-reading your first post, I realize that I'm a little confused with your description of your existing wall and the wall you propose to build. When you say cinderblock and concrete do you mean hollow blocks filled in with concrete or...?. A photo or a drawing would help a lot if possible. If your cinderblocks are hollow, then you already have a mass air mass system. Any new wall built inside of that will create a 3rd leaf or more depending on how it's constructed.

cyeazel2 June 2006#6
Given that your blocks are hollow, your best bet (if possible) is to fill in the cavities first. Then build your second leaf out from that. If you can't fill the cavities, then you are better off just adding layers directly to the block wall. It also depends on how much isolation you need too.
