Soundman20209 July 2013#2
I think you mean "gender", not "sex"! :) But yes, I would have some lines going the other way, both balanced and unbalanced. Have some of the balanced lines on XLR connectors, and some on 1/4" TRS connectors. The unbalanced should only be on 1/4" jacks. You'll need the TRS for headphones, but you also might need the XLRs to feed a mini mixer, or active monitors, or other equipment, and you might need the unbalanced 1/4" lines to connect guitars, pedals, passive speakers, or whatever. When I'm installing post facilities, I always add in a few extra lines of all types, going in all directions, since I never know what the customer might need in the future, but I'm damn sure he'll need something that he didn't think about when we designed the place! So I also put in CAT-6 lines, VGA lines, USB lines (if you are close enough), phone lines, intercom lines, serial control lines, and anything else I can think of. For example, one day you might want to run your DAW from the booth, so you need a way to get a video screen, mouse and keyboard in there... Hence, you need VGA plus USB or CAT-6 plus extenders. Etc. All those cables are cheap, compared to the cost of having to chop holes in your walls a few months down the line! :) - Stuart -

Papus12 July 2013#4
VGA boards and chipsets will no longer be produced by Intel as of December 2013. In my line of work I already encounter desktop PC's and laptops equipped with ONLY HDMI or Display Port, this will become the norm. To future-proof your studio I recommend you run HDMI and Cat6a between rooms. Cat6a can be used to transmit just about anything. Regarding 1/4" TRS tie lines, be sure to run some with shielded coax for carrying signals and some with heavy-gauge speaker wire for carrying amplifier outputs between the control room and live room. You don't want to run a cranked 100w Marshall amplifier through coax cable! - those things can pump out 70v running flat-out. One of the quickest ways to blow up a guitar amp is to connect the speaker via ordinary signal cable.
