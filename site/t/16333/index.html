" There is no whole permit, as each trade is required to get a permit for their craft as it pertains to work in your home. I was pretty clear on what you were saying, a permit only lasts 12 months, so you may well have to purchase another depending on the time frame. My thinking is that maybe I'm not being either too clear or understood. Replacing a load bearing wall is no simple task, and I strongly recommend people that are thinking they can do it, to consider what it is they are attempting. With a truss floor,,,it gets even uglier...by far. The simple process with joists is to shore up the existing overhead joists with a temporary framed wall that falls on either side of the "pocket" area that will be cut out of the joists to allow for the LVL to be installed, giving enough clearance for workers to access this area with limited movement restriction. Wiring, plumbing and all the existing trades either have to be rerouted or taken into consideration as part of this move. But we are talking about a truss floor...and the procedure is completely different and requires a skill level that you cannot achieve from simply reading about it. The floor trusses have to actually be modified >before< they are cut for the pocket. They have to perform like a standard floor joist in that if they do not have vertical bracing/blocking installed at the potential cut mark, the trusses can actually break and or begin to be caught under the pull of gravity and the existing weight of the structure including overhead walls that may be placed on the truss at a point overhead that you have not considered yet. This requires a very high degree of accuracy and timing in truss blocking/temporary wall placement, where does the LVL enter the room and does it need to be placed on the floor below BEFORE the temp walls go up to assure that you DO have access to the beam parts and not essentially lock your material out of the ability to complete the job. If you do construction, and you have done this type of thing before, then you may have a better chance of success. When I said get the permits, it was in reference to the construction folks that will be handling the LVL installation, since I find it difficult to believe that you and your buddies should attempt this at the risk of life and a place to live. Is that more clear, my concerns that is?
