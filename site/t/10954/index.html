Pre, I seriously doubt that it's a MAM system - the odds are the 2 wythes are tied together with mason ties. I say this because single wythe brick structures do not stand up very long. There is also going to be a whole lot of mortar in between the 2 wythes - which couple the walls even more. In addition - the 1" air space is almost a non-existent spring when it comes to a leaf as rigid as brick......... I would opt for the room within a room approach. All of that being said - loose the "softboard" it's a waste. If you want something between sheets buy green glue - if you can't afford greenglue on your budget then add another layer of drywall....... and make sure to use 5/8" drywall so as to maximize mass. Use 2x6 framing at 24" oc - with fluffy insulation in the bays - and a 1" space between the framing and the brick - that will give you a 6 1/2" air space - which is not fantastic - but is adequate. Sincerely, Rod Please let me know what you think of my idea and also what would be the consequences of doing the 3 leaf design, would it honoustly not be worth it and would my idea be better as in the pic.[/quote]

marz2 August 2008#23
PreludetoJune Personally I have built Timber Frame houses in the past, so prefer this method instead of steel framing. Keep in mind the thicker you can make the walls the better as well. Some suggest 2 x 6's instead of standard 2 x 4s (IE 50 x 150mm or 50 x 100mm respectively) - so Timber frame is better to achieve these thicknesses. Typical steel framing is 3" or 75 mm I think. Standard in SA for Timber framing is 1.5 x 4.5's which machined are actually 32mm x 110mm - Better to get Planned all round - PAR - for uniformity of wall thickness. For added security, your wall sills on the concrete should be treated - Tanalith treated, to ensure no hassles later. I just used Tanalith treated throughout my walls. Be careful with the bottom sill and top wallplates - as you easily find a situation after 2 layers of ceiling where there is no place to fasten the wall plasterboards to. I use double sill and wall plates to ensure no problems - same applies to fastening the skirting boards, you may not have any sill left and then obliged to find the vertical studs. The recommended spacing of verticals is 24" IE 60 cm. Aim to have the ceiling beams sit on top of the vertical studs. Needs good planning! It is highly recommended to nail the Timber frames together and not be tempted to screw them together. I use galvanized Ring Shank nails for added grip. These come in 90mm and 120mm lengths. The 90mm are good for most joins, but sometimes you need the 120mm. I cant recall your room width, but you probably need around 2 x 6's for the ceiling beams - IE 32 x 144mm in South Africa. For longer spans, you may need to increase that to 220mm depth beams. For the Green Glue - I decided not to use it and did not research too hard, but have not seen any. I did order some stuff from the USA though - the Metacaulk Putty Pads from ebuildingproducts <[removed]> and that worked fine. So probably you could find a supplier to ship some if you cant find any. Dont stop now regards Marz
