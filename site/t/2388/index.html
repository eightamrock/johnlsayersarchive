knightfly26 September 2004#4
The english word for Moulure is molding - As to treatment, the first places you should place absorbent in your room are those places where, if they were a mirror, you could see either speaker reflected from your seat at the mix position - in your case, you would need to place some of your rockwool on the walls at each side, halfway between your speakers and your head; have a friend hold a large mirror against the wall and slide it along the wall as you sit in front of the speakers where you will mix - place a piece of tape on the wall where you just start to see either speaker, then place a second piece of tape where the OTHER speaker just disappears in the mirror - add at least 150mm to each side, and this is the area you need to absorb. Vertically, you should use at least a meter width material, centered where the reflection of the speaker shows between floor and ceiling. Next, measure between your head and the center between the speakers - half this distance should be the center of your "cloud", which should be at least a meter front to back and a meter and a half or more from side to side, centered between the side walls (your speakers also need to be equidistant from their respective wall) Beyond that, you may already have enough absorption in the room, only listening will tell for sure. If you want to build some of the wall units, don't worry about a small gap - if it bothers you, build a pedestal that is recessed in the rear (like the toe space under kitchen cabinets) so the unit can fit flush against the wall and still clear the Moulure... Steve

knightfly1 October 2004#6
Yeah, looking at your layout you will probably help the sound by using some corner absorption - even something as simple as a bale of fiberglass insulation (not opened) and stood up in the corners will make a difference. Be careful that you don't get too much high absorption though - if the room gets too dead, it won't sound natural and won't be pleasant to play in... Steve
