Soundman202027 July 2013#3
Do you guys think I will be able to make a vocal booth out of this?
You could, yes. It is possible to get high levels of isolation ("soundproofing"), if it is done correctly.
would i have to put a new wall over the tiles or could i just apply bass traps or acoustic foam on them?
That would not block sound coming in: that would be treatment inside the room. Isolation and treatment are two entirely different things. The materials used for treatment are not much use for isolation, and the materials used in isolating a room are not much use for treatment. very different things.
would i have to create new walls for the booth? including new walls for the wooden side?
The correct way to isolate a room is to have walls around it that are built as two separate "leaves" that are not connected to each other. Not one leaf, and not three leaves. Two only.
what would be the best way to soundproof the entire room?
As above: Built it as a decoupled 2-leaf system, where you have an outer leaf on one stud frame, and an inner leaf on another stud frame, with no mechanical connections between them. That includes all four walls, and the ceiling, as well as doors, and windows. They must all be built as two leaves.
Does it look possible to create a successful booth before i go through with it?
The space is not very big, especially in height, but you could build a decent vocal booth in there, if it is done right.
i give up, it's just too noisy, even at night. I'm just gonna do rough takes at home and find a professional studio to record, gonna be expensive though
You want to give up after only 7 hours? :shock: :) Most people take several months to learn how to build their rooms, then several more months to actually build them. So giving up after seven hours seems a bit premature! If you have the space, and the money, then you could do it. - Stuart -
