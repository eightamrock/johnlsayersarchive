Soundman20205 February 2009#2
The experts here, such as Rod, Ro, Andre, etc. recommend that you should NOT splay your windows at all, as doing so reduces isolation through the window, and all that you succeed in doing with regard to reflections, is to put them in a different place. You'll still get reflections off the glass, but they will just come from a different point on the glass. The diagrams that you sometimes see on the internet justifying angled glass treat sound as if it only ever travels in single straight lines parallel to the floor, from the sound source to the window and mic. Of course, in reality sound does not behave that way. It travels in expanding spherical wave fronts. So there will always be some part of that wave that hits the glass at an angle that bounces back into the mic, unless your room is so huge that you can have both the mic and the sound source be many, many meters away from the glass. There is also the practical issue of angled glass making your wall unrealistically thick: Basically, IIRC, you need at least 8 inches of air gap between the panes of glass at the closest point in order to keep reasonable isolation. If you angle the glass at the minimum angle necessary for keeping reflections out of microphones in a small room, while still keeping the glass at least 8 inches apart at the closest point, then it means that there is so much gap at the other end of the glass that you either have to make your wall extremely thick, or you have to build a "box" that protrudes from the wall on both sides, in which to mount your window. The ONLY reason you might want to angle your glass, is for aesthetic reasons: to reduce glare and reflection from lighting. So that you don't have reflections from the lights obscuring your view through the glass. But careful placement of fittings can help with that. But acoustically, there really are no real benefits to angling glass. If you search the forum for terms like "glass angle" then I'm sure you'll find several references to this subject. - Stuart -

Stuart, I can't speak for anyone else - but i do not recommend against the splaying of glass. This is what i say in the book:
In my mind, the loss in isolation from the air spring is made up for in mass. We will always have greater mass than the wall surfaces if we do this right, so what little difference there may be we just live with. In Chapter 1, we looked at the fact that in the real world sometimes there are things that we just live with. This is one of those things. However, if you want to gain the maximum isolation benefit (at this point the walls will be the weak point and control your total isolation), then feel free to install the glass parallel. This is the sort of thing that is really a matter of personal choice, and it won’t affect the outcome to any great degree, so I won’t push you in any one direction. It’s only important to me that you understand enough about the subject to be able to make an informed decision.
Just want to clarify my position on the comment made. Rod
