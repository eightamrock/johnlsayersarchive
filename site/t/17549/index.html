These clips decouple, and decoupling is a great thing. The walls you describe would be formidable, however not decoupling the ceiling would perhaps leave that surface vulnerable to low frequency flanking. Might you be able to decouple the ceiling and use two layers of mass?

Ted White2 May 2012#4
#1 that resilient channel is likely very similar to one you can buy at a local warehouse for 1/3 the cost. #2 on its best day resilient channel is a poor performer. #3 you really plan to hang all that weight on that channel? Here in the States, resilient channel isn't even a building product with a formal load test rating. There is no authoritative body that has rated its load carrying capacity, or is there a standard for its fabrication. In the States you could manufacture your own resilient channel any way you want as there is no standard. In my opinion, this is a potentially catastrophic move. Better to use a solid building material such as code-rated Drywall Furring Channel placed at 24" spacing to increase the flex of the panels.This isn't decoupling but it also isn't dangerous.
