Search the forum for the term "drum riser". Once of the other moderators here (Glenn) has posted a design several times for a simple, effective, low-cost method of doing that.
1. Do you believe that the walls should possibly be floated?
Nope! Not necessary, and dangerous unless done properly, which is expensive. Your wall must be attached to the floor with bolt, nails or screws (earthquakes.... :!: :shock: ). It is not an option to eave them unattached. There are systems that allow them to be attached and still decoupled, but that adds complexity and expense, and you don't need to do that, unless you need very high levels of isolation.
however I am wondering if the tiles make any difference here?
No difference. I'm assuming those are ceramic tiles, properly attached to the floor with tile adhesive, or cement?
the ceiling. Can I combine the wood as outer columns with standard aluminum profiles?
I suppose you could, but I'm not sure it will be easy, or even legal.
The reason I want to do that is simply due to less cost. An aluminum profile of 3 meters is around 3 euro
Is that for the heavy-gauge load-bearing type??? :) Or is that for the lighter-gauge type that is just mean for building partition walls?
3. Do you believe that I even need the wooden columns for increased stability (mainly for carrying the ceiling) or do you think I can use alu profiles for all walls ?
There are two types of metal framing, as I mentioned above. One type is meant for building simple partitions that are not load-bearing, and is made from thin gauge metal. The other type is specifically designed to carry load-bearing structures, and is made from thicker metal that is more expensive and harder to work with. You cannot use the thin, cheap type here, since this is a load-bearing application. You must use the thicker ones. I think you'll find that there isn't much difference between those and ordinary 2x4 wood framing.
4. What is your opinion about the layers of drywall? Should I consider 2, 3 or more
It's hard to say without having real decibel measurements.... but probably 2 layers will be enough (they must be 16mm, not 12mm, not 10mm....). You would only need 3 layers of you have a need for very high levels of isolation.
what is your opinion on the neccessarity of adding green glue?
It works! It does what the manufacturer claims it does. You might need it, or might not. Once again, that depends on how much isolation you need.
5. Any other suggestions or input on my plans so far? I have done my research but of course I am no expert and might still be missing something.
Your outer leaf must be sealed air-tight, and you need insulation between the inner leaf and outer leaf. You could make your studio bigger inside by building your inner-leaf walls closer to the outer leaf walls, and making a "kink" around the support columns. So most of the studio would be wider, except at those points. More room volume = better acoustics. - Stuart -
