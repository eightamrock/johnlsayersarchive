Hi Mark, and welcome! Some suggestions to get yo on track, no particular order of importance: It looks like you want to do just one big room, rather than separate CR and LR? If so, you should probably at least separate the gear out into two areas, one being your CR area, the other your LR area. Symmetry: For the "control room" (CR) area of your room, you need to have symmetrical construction around you, and a reflection free zone (RFZ) is a good idea to have around your mix position. An RFZ needs to have the ceiling sloping up and away from the speakers, and the side walls splayed by at least 6 degrees each side (12 degrees total. Also 12 degrees for the ceiling). So I'd suggest that you move your console and speakers up close to the wall at the top of your diagram, and center them on that wall. Now figure out your speaker soffits: You want each speaker mounted in the middle of its soffit, about 1.2m above the floor (standard listening height). You want the soffit panels as wide as possible, 48" if you can spare the space, or as close to that as you can get, and they should be angled such that the imaginary acoustic axes of the speakers intersect just behind your head at an angle of 60°. The speakers should be the same distance apart as they are from your head. In other words, the speakers and your head are at the apexes of an equilateral triangle. It looks like that door towards the front of the left wall will have to go: it is in the way of everything. Basic treatment: Deep absorption at first reflection points. Heavy bass trapping in all corners. Hard floor (concrete, laminate flooring, ceramic tiles, etc,), soft absorbent ceiling (or large clouds). I'm not certain about using diffusion n that room: It is almost big enough that diffusion would be workable, and it will be both a CR and LR in one, so maybe diffusion would be worthwhile... Hmmmm HVAC: Long duct runs, multiple 90 degree bends, ducts line with acoustic duct liner (or plain old 703). You'll need silencer boxes in-line with the ducts. Calculate your air flow and cooling requirements carefully (high volume, low speed flow), and dimension your system, and ducts accordingly. Take care to neither over-size nor under-size your air conditioner unit: it must be like the three little bears: "just right", never too big, never too small. :) That should at least help to get you started! - Stuart - (Edited to fix spelling errors! :oops: )

Anyway, my point was that since you didn't show any CR / LR dividing wall, I figured you just wanted one large space that doubles as both a CR and LR, all together. Personally, I'm not a fan of having an acoustic kit drum kit in the CR (to me, drums are basically large tuned resonators that tend to hum along with certain frequencies, and "color" the sound in your CR), but other folks don't seem to to mind, and if you have limited space or just happen to like having your drums close by, then whatever works for you! In your case, you now clarified that you use electronic drums, so it isn't even an issue at all! (But on your original diagram they sure didn't look like they were electronic...) Anyway, if you are going to divide the room into a CR and LR (or CR and booth), then that doesn't really change things much in the CR design: You still need the RFZ up front, speakers in soffits, bass trapping in corners, first reflection point treatment, etc., so it isn't a big deal.
