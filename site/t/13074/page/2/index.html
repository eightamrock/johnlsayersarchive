It might not be possible to keep the splayed walls, but I reckon you can keep the soffits, since others have done that successfully in similar sized spaces: But I would still go with inner-leaf side walls, built inside-out (even though you say you don't need much isolation to the sides) for three reasons: 1) Isolation: Even though you say you don't need isolation to the sides, you DO need it to the LR. You'll have a 2-leaf MSM wall to that room, which is great, but if you don't also have a 2-leaf ceiling and 2-leaf sidewalls around the CR, then your 2-leaf divider wall won't be able to do it's job, as sound will "flank" around it. And you also won't have any way of holding up that wall! So you also need the side walls structurally. But build "inside-out"... 2) Space: Built inside-out, they don't take up much space in the room at all. If you use a 4" air gap, then I can show you how to build them so that you only loose about 12 cm of depth (acoustically) on each wall, or 24 cm width. I think you can afford that! Especially considering the benefits. And if you take it down to a 3" air gap, then you only need to lose 9 cm on each side, total of 18 cm. 3) Acoustic treatment: Building inside-out leaves the stud space facing the room, and you can use ALL of that for acoustic treatment! Free space! If you build the walls "normally", then not only do you lose extra room width and depth, but you then have to build your acoustic treatment ON TOP of those walls, which takes up even more room width... So that would be my recommendation: Build those sidewalls that you were no considering before because they were taking up too much space, but build them inside out. And don't get depressed: it can be done. It has been done before. So you can do it too. - Stuart -

Yup. Really. Like I said: there's only a couple of inches of surface around them, which kind of defeats the whole purpose of simulating an infinite baffle. Really. Not to mention that curious absorber half way down the baffle, which greatly reduces the effective area of the baffle. Hard to see how such a soffit panel can achieve the acoustic purpose of simulating an infinite baffle. Hard to see how it meets the "2x" criteria that you mentioned, as well. :) An infinite baffle, just like the name implies, has to be infinitely large with respect to the wavelengths in question. 2" is hardly "infinitely large", compared to most sound waves! 2 feet would be a lot better, but still fall short of ideal. 2" is large compared to frequencies of 15,000 Hz and higher, which is the top 3 notes of only the highest octave on the scale, and isn't even audible to most people. 2 feet is large compared to frequencies of 1000 Hz, which is half of the entire musical spectrum, and is clearly audible to everyone. 2 feet on each side of the speaker, plus the speaker itself, probably gives you more like 5 feet, which is everything above 500 Hz... So yes, I stand by what I said, despite your ignorant, petty and rather childish comments: those tiny little "baffles" just a couple of inches wide are NOT useful as soffit panels, because they are way too small to have any effect on practically the entire musical spectrum.
