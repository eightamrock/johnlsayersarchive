Soundman202029 May 2009#6
I've measured 60 degree angles from 15cm behind the 38% monitoring position and, if I'm reading this correctly, this means the room is too narrow and the monitoring position needs to move closer to the monitors.
If that's the case, then increase the angle of your speaker axis intersection from 60 degrees up to as much as 90 degrees if you need. That sometimes is necessary in a small room where the geometry just doesn't work out the normal way. It will squash your sweet spot a bit, and give you a slightly wider sound stage, but other than that it will work fine. That will allow you to place your speakers far enough apart to still get your head at a decent location in the room. If you have splayed walls, then you will also need to increase the splay angle from 6° up to 9° or so. One other point: the "38% rule" is not carved in stone! It's just a starting point. If you have to move a bit forward or backwards to get the best sound, then nobody is going to arrest you! Many engineers prefer sitting a little closer than that anyway, according to one paper I read on the subject a while back. So don't feel guilty if you end up at 35%, or 41% or whatever. As long as you are close to 38%, and far away from 50% and 25% then you should be OK. - Stuart -

Soundman202029 May 2009#8
wouldnt that mean that the monitoring position would be brought even further forward
Yes. that is, after all, what you said you need to do! Originally you said:
.... the room is too narrow and the monitoring position needs to move closer to the monitors.
. In other words, you originally said that the monitor position needs to go further forward. That's what you get by increasing the intersection angle: the mix position moves forward, and the speakers can be set further apart. In order to move the listening position closer to the monitors, you need to increase the intersection angle. That's what you seemed to be saying! In other words, you need to turn the speakers so that the face more towards each other (making the the axes less parallel). If you increase the angle to the absurd extreme of 180 degrees, then the speakers would be pointing directly at each other, and your head would be directly between them. If you turn them the other way until they both face the back wall and are perpendicular to it, then the angle would be zero and your had would be infinitely far away. (Actually there would be no angle at all, since the axes would be parallel). So right now, you have an angle of 30 degrees between your room center-line and the speaker axes. 30+30 = 60. If you increase that angle to 45 degrees between the center-line and the speaker axes then you have 45+45 = 90.
I thought the 60 degrees related to the angle where a straight line drawn out from the centre of each speaker intersect
That's correct. But first you said that you wanted to get the mix position closer to the front, and now it seems like you don't want that, so I think I'm the one that is confused! Where exactly is your intersection point now (in terms of percentage of room depth), and which way does it need to move in order to get it 38% away from the front wall? Do you need to get your mix position closer to the front wall, ot further away from it? Increasing the speaker angle will bring it closer to the front wall, thus allowing you to move your speakers further apart, where they belong. Decreasing your angle will move the intersection point towards the rear wall. - Stuart -

Soundman202029 May 2009#11
I think my point is that I hope the monitoring position doesnt have to come forward too far (towards 25%) in order to maintain the angle, given the narrow dimension of the room....
OK, I see what you are saying, from that SketchUp model. Once you draw the speakers at their correct locations and correct separation, then you'll need to move the listening point BACKWARDS, not forwards, to put your mix position at the right location too, or it will be too close to the front wall. Your control room is very long and thin. What ratio is that based on? Since your limiting factor is width, maybe one idea would be to use a different ratio and make the room shorter. Either move your dividing wall towards the rear (chimney) end of the room, or maybe build another wall across the back, to create a small storage area, or even a vocal booth. That would make the room shorter, which automatically moves the 38% mark closer to the front wall. Another comment: Your speakers should not be in the corners. They need to be in the middle of the soffit panels, and those need to be as large as you can possibly make them, given the limitations of room geometry. That will probably place them roughly one quarter of the room with away from the walls, meaning roughly half of the room width between them, meaning they will be about 1.3 m apart, and about 50 cm from the walls. Somewhere around there. That isn't much to work with, but it is what it is! --------- EDITED TO ADD: I have since learned that the speakers should NOT be in the middle of the soffits, but rather offset to one side or the other, in order to avoid creating peaks and nulls in the frequency response. It seems that a position about 2/5 of the width is good. They should also not be centered vertically, for the same reason. - Stuart - ---------- So if your speakers are 1.3 m apart, then your head needs to be about 1.3 m from each speaker. That would place your listening position around 1.3 m from the front wall, meaning that your room needs to be about 3.4 m long, instead of the 5.8 that you have right now. Those are very rough figures, of course. Rule of thumb and "guesstimates". But it should give you an idea of the geometry and dimensions that you need to play around with, to make things fit. - Stuart -

Soundman202031 May 2009#17
Am I right to accurately draw the splayed walls first before placing the soffits? Do you think splaying the walls like that is the right thing to do in this room?
I'd suggest that you figure out your soffits first, then add the splayed walls from there. They should be 6° to help control first reflections and flutter echo, but if you can only get 5°, that you have to live with what you have. And if you absolutely cannot get both splayed and soffits to work, I think I'd dump the splayed walls, keep the soffits, and go with inside-out side walls that are parallel to the existing walls with as big an air gap as you can afford. Splayed walls would help for isolation too, but you have concrete in your first leaf, so you probably already have reasonably good isolation there, and you might be able to skimp a bit on your air gap. That will increase the resonant frequency of the MSM system, but once again, if those are thick concrete walls, then it might not be that important. What is on the other side of those side walls? How thick are they? And how loud are you? Key points. - Stuart -
