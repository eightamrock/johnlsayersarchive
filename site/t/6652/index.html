Hello all, I've been lurking around here for a couple of years as a guest, on and off. It's amazing how much information and help is available here. This is my first post on the forum; I'm trying to follow knightfly's rules to the letter. Who and where am I: I'm a professor of music at Central Missouri State University, in Warrensburg, Missouri, USA. Warrensburg is about 45 miles from Kansas City, in rural Missouri. At CMSU, I'm in charge of the music technology degree program, which is focused on audio engineering and music production. The program has been operating since 2000. Overview of goals: My goal is to build a new recording studio complex and teaching facility. This facility should include 2 recording studios, at least one of which will be able to handle 5.1 projects. In at least one of the studios, there must be a tracking room large enough to hold a jazz big band (15 horn players, piano, drums, guitar, bass). Each of the studios should include multiple recording spaces (e.g. drum booth, vocal booth) as well as a larger tracking room. Each of the control rooms should offer enough room for a teacher and 15 students to interact for class. It would be great if one of the studios allowed for 20 students. There should be one office in the facility, as well as men's/women's restrooms and at least one oversize door to facilitate load-in/load-out. I have not begun the sketching process as of yet, but as of now, I'm thinking of a facility around 3,000 square feet total. Height is not an issue (see below), so it might be 2 stories in height to increase cubic footage (open space, not 2 floors). Where we are in the design process: Just barely begun. Basically, we currently have two recording studios, with around $250,000 of equipment. I'm pretty happy with our equipment at the moment, but the space it's in is terrible. These studios are located in temporary spaces in a converted dormitory, with a minimum of treatment or special construction. As you can imagine, the sound is less than I would desire in a variety of ways (transmission to/from other spaces and vice versa -- there's a clarinet teacher across the hall -- acoustics of the tracking and control rooms, etc. ad nauseam). We want to get out of those spaces entirely and build a new building. That seems much more practical than trying to alter the spaces where we are -- they're not large enough in any dimension and if we move out, other music people who really need the space could move in. We are currently trying to put together the information we need to pursue funding for this project. Our proposals will be much more effective if I can flesh out the design with more detail. Ideally, I'll eventually take the design all the way to the point where it's ready to give to the university architect. HOW LOUD are you, and how picky/loud are your neighbors? We will generate typical rock studio levels in the tracking rooms. Our nearest neighbors to the space where we plan to build are in two college dormitories. One of those is across the street, the other is next door; there will be green space between us (how much depends on how large the facility gets). We typically have students in our studios 24/7, so transmission to the outside should be minimized. Include as much detail as possible about the existing construction. This is an easy one. There is no existing construction. We plan to build on an open plot of land between our current building and the dormitory up the street. This plot opened up when a previously existing building was demolished. The available space, based on the plans for that building, is at least 200' x 50'. The buildings to either side go up 3-4 stories, so we could easily go up 2 stories if justified. My budget: Well, that's the question. For the moment, we have none. That's why we're busy writing proposals. I'm hoping that we will be able to build something along these lines for $300,000 or less. My actual questions (at the moment -- more will come, I'm sure): 1. Would it be better in terms of cost, but also effectiveness, to think in terms of masonry construction or frame construction? From what I can glean from the Reference area and multiple searches on this site and elsewhere, it seems like either can work well. I think masonry may have the edge in cost-effectiveness for new construction in this part of the country, but I'm curious about whether or not that's right. 2. Does it seem like 3,000 square feet is enough to meet the design goals above? Could it be done in less square footage? 3. What is a ballpark figure for cost-per-square foot for new construction (masonry vs. frame if you know) in this part of the USA, given that we already own the land? I found a couple of threads mentioning cost-per-square foot in my search of this forum, but none that I saw were located where I am. Any thoughts there? Thanks in advance for any help any of you can provide. Best regards, Eric Honour

John Sayers19 July 2006#6
Hi Eric - welcome to the forum - pricing can be quite variable - the lower reckoning is for an internal construction whereas the higher usually include the outer building, as in your case. In residential construction a great deal of the cost is in two major areas - the kitchen and bathrooms because of the cabinet work, plumbing, waterproofing and tiling etc. Studios tend to balance this out because of the additional layers, increased insulation etc. Having taught music production and been responsible for the design of the teaching facilities myself in a classroom/lecture situation I've recently pondered a control room layout that includes a rear tiered seating arrangement where a group of students can all be seated close to the speaker center line so when you are discussing stereo spread/panning etc they can all hear it correctly. I'm not sure about your University's student access but we ended up giving 24hr access to the facilities so isolation from residential areas became additionally important.
