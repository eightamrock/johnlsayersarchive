Soundman202021 September 2012#2
Greg, I wouldn't worry too much about that. It's not a big deal. The position of the insulation has only a very slight effect on overall isolation, nothing to be worried about. The purpose of the insulation is to damp resonance in the cavity, and it will still do that in the middle of the cavity, just as well as it would if it were in the other location. - Stuart -

Soundman202021 September 2012#4
Actually, looking closer at your diagram, it does say "Insulation compressed together". Does that mean that they forced the insulation into the tiny gap between the studs on the outer leaf and inner leaf? If so, then you have a problem. Compressing 6 inches of insulation into a two inch gap between studs is way more than enough to create a flanking path. In other words, if he just laid the batts into the stud bays but did not force them in sideways between the two sets of studs, then you are probably OK. But if he did do any "compressing", and the compressed insulation is touching both sets of framing, then you do have a problem. If you have any doubts at all, then get your builder to take off the drywall on one side, and correct the problem, AT HIS OWN EXPENSE, not yours. He screwed up by not following your very clear instructions, so to my way of thinking, it is his responsibility to fix it and absorb the cost of doing so. Of course, he cannot re-use the same drywall: it will have to be junked and replaced with new stuff,and new Green Glue. That's going to hurt his pocket... - Stuart -

You have to compress insulation to where the parts become hard (or near hard) due to the compression. To allow them to "touch" which seems to be this case is not going to harm you at all. If in the future, you have more flanges of your batt insulation to install, install the flanges on the top side of the stud rather than to the inside, it makes the placement better, the air barrier is tighter and you have a better overall wall assembly.
