Soundman202029 July 2010#4
The problem isn't just thickness, but rather the total acoustic characteristics of the glass: a 1/2" pane of plate glass does not behave the same as a 1/2" pane of laminate glass, and also not the same as two panes of 1/4" plate glass stacked together. The best type of glass is laminate glass: the others don't really give you the same isolation. Ain't it typical of studio building that the best way to do it is also the most expensive???? :twisted: :!: So unfortunately, if you want the best possible isolation, then laminate is the only way to go. That isn't saying that you can't do windows with plate glass: you can still do them like that, but you won't get the same effect. And I definitely wouldn't just stack together two panes of ordinary glass without a laminate interlayer! I reckon you'd have some major problems with rattling, ringing, and other unpleasant issues. So if all you can afford is plate glass that isn't quite thick enough, then that's all you can afford, and it will work to some extent: it just won't be as good as doing it with real plate glass of the correct thickness. - Stuart -

rod gervais30 July 2010#6
On the issue of glass.......... if you use annealed glass on one side of the wall equal in mass to the wall covering - and then install a piece of annealed on the other side that is 1/4" thicker - this will be as good as it gets (form an isolation point of view) the TL value of the window will be slightly greater than the TL values of the wall it is installed in - anything more than that is a waste........ The advantage to laminated glass is that you can use glazing with less mass than the wall while still achieving the same TL values......... One is equally as good as the other - it all boils down (in the end) to cost. On the question - unless the glass was exceptionally thick - there would be vibration of the panes of glass that would cause noise - which would be undesirable in any setting - the same way that a heavy vibration can make the dishes (etc) in your cabinets rattle under the right conditions........ Very bad idea - there is nothing I can think of that you could use to create the equivalent of laminated glass....... lami glass is created under high heat and pressure - sort of difficult to re-create outside of a glass factory........ Rod
