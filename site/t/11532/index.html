Soundman20208 January 2009#5
Hi Árni, and welcome! A couple of non-expert comments from me (the non-expert): To me, it looks like you have the right idea, basically, with your layout. You have the speakers firing down the long axis, you have symmetry, etc. However, you might want to re-position your speakers, since they seem to be at a distance from the front wall where you are going to be getting things like cancellation, comb filtering, and that kind of fun stuff. Search the forum for "speaker position", and you should find plenty of information on how to find the ritgh palce for your speakers. You say that your speakers are sitting on top of concrete pipes? Are those pipes hollow, or did you fill them with something, such as dry sand? The reason I ask is because I'm wondering if those pipes won't be resonating a bit, and maybe doing strange things to the sound in the room? As far as I know, concrete is good for speaker stands, but they should not be hollow. Maybe one of the speaker or acoustic experts can comment on that. Regarding the acoustic tile ceiling: My guess is that it is not actually doing very much for you. that kind of tile is not meant to deal with high levels of music in a studio. It is really meant to help a bit with normal office and classroom type noise. So you might want to consider putting a "cloud" roughly above your mix position. But beware: you probably cannot hang it from the same frame as the acoustic tile, since those frames are not very strong. You might need to figure out another way of keeping your cloud up there, so it doesn't fall on your head!
Someone might say "that table is going to kill you sound-wise" and I know that,
Yep! You are going to get reflections off that, by the looks of it.
It's just such a nice table that I'd rather not lose it (I've been thinking about putting dense carpet on the top of the table)
I'm not sure that carpet will do the job. I agree that its a great table, cool design, etc., but I don't think it is helping your acoustics at all! And I don't think that carpet will fix it (it might also look strange with carpet on it!)
I was told by a friend that bass traps on in the corners of the back wall
Yep. Those back corners, on each side of the door, are just begging to have big bass traps built in there! Search the forum for "corner bass trap", and you'll find plenty of ideas on how you can build them yourself, at low cost. But don't stop there, eith just the back corners. There are 12 corners in a room ,not just two, so you should also think about treating some of the others. You need lots of bass trapping. You might want to at least do the front corners, behind the speakers. And maybe the wall/ceiling corner above that door would be another good place. Make them big.
and some absorbers at the.. what's it called.. the first point where the sound coming from the monitors hits the walls (early reflection point ?)
Again: "Yep". You do need absorption on your first reflection points, and that includes the ceiling (which is why yo need the cloud up there). Once again, using the "search" button for "reflection point", and you'll find more than enough stuff to keep you reading for hours. - Stuart -
