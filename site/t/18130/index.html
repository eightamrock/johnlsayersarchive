It's hard to see from your drawings, but it looks like you have the isolation mostly correct, except there seem to be some areas where there are issues with continuity of the leaves. But the basic plan is correct: two leaves, two layers of 16mm drywall for each leaf, double studs, GG, and sealing.
I have a big doubts... this type of construction is enough to completely isolate the sound between the live rooms?
that depends on what you mean by "completely isolate"! :) There is no such thing as totally soundproof: any sufficiently loud noise can penetrate any conceivable man-made barrier. Even the best isolated studio in the world, with an amazing 100 dB of isolation, cannot isolate the sound of a shotgun blast. That's why we tend to speak in terms of "decibels of Transmission Loss", when it comes to isolation. There are many, many research papers on many different types of construction, and how much isolation each of them gives in true laboratory settings: What you are planning, if you build everything perfectly, should give you in the region of 60 dB of isolation, which is considered pretty good for a studio, and about as much as you can expect on a reasonable budget in a typical building. But it's a long way from "completely isolated", if you mean "all instruments totally inaudible". So is it "enough"? Only you can answer that question. Vocals will most likely be inaudible between rooms, even very loud vocals. Must instruments will be inaudible or greatly muffled, except for ones with a lot of low frequency energy, or ones that are very loud to start with. Drums, bass guitars, loud keyboards, for example, will probably be audible, albeit very muffled. Drums, for example, usually come in at around 110 to 115 dB, when played reasonably hard, so in the control room you would hear those at around 50 dB, which is a bit quieter than normal speech, and it would be mostly the low frequency "thump" that you would here, not the cymbals or hi-hat. Is that acceptable? For the vast majority of studios, it is, but only you can judge if that is acceptable for YOUR studio. It would be good for you to measure how loud it will be in their, by measuring (with a sound level meter) the level of a typical recording session that you do today, in whatever place you are doing that. That number is part of the equation that you need to calculate. Now, while the band is still playing inside, walk away, out of the room, down the hall, outside if necessary, until you think the level you are hearing is "acceptable", and note down the level you are seeing on the meter. That's the other number that you need. Subtract that one from the first one, and you have answered the question: "How much isolation do you need?". Let us know what that number is, and we can help you choose an isolation plan that will give you that number. But most of all, first concentrate on getting a decent, usable shape for your control room, following the normal guidelines for that. - Stuart -
