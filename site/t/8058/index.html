Aloha and welcome to the forum! :D Congrats on your studio build! Any chance you could please:
Edit your profile to include your location. This is very important, because this is a worldwide resource, and as such, material costs and availability vary widely. For example, masonry is cheaper than gypsum in some parts of the globe, whereas it's the exact opposite in other regions.
You'll probably get more of a response by doing so and this will really come in handy two or three posts down the road. It's also part of the forum guidelines. Thanks! :wink: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=7572 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3231 Eliminating reflections at the mix position will help clear up a lot of haze and will help to make your sessions less fatiguing. You've got cloud coverage to handle some of the floor/ceiling reflections. And as you've pointed out - side wall and rear treatments, either a pair of side absorbers with angled slats or at the very least, absorption on either side of the mix position, corner traps in both rear corners and an absorber on the rear wall to finish off your treatments. Here's a link to some DIY wall unit plans - http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

kendale28 January 2007#7
Aloha, The side and rear wall units can be done as free standing units. Keep in mind that a 6' x 8' x 8" unit filled with mineral wool and faced with slats will be moderately heavy. It may not be a bad idea to use a 'L' bracket to keep them from tipping over, which shouldn't leave any more than a couple of screw holes that are easily patched if your lease allows. If you'd care to see how these look, try here: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... &start=165 You may also want to angle your speakers in about 30 degreees each. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
