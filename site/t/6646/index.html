Mark Blasco wrote:
I understand that to meet residential building codes, you have to have fresh air circulation if the windows are removed. Does that fresh air have to come from outside the house?
Yes, it is. I consdered supplying my room with "semi-fresh air from the house," but that idea was knocked down by my building department (and by code). Fresh air mechanical ventilation has to come from the outside, and the exhaust air must go directly outside as well (i.e., you can't take a shortcut and push the exhaust air into the attic or a garage). --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward19 July 2006#4
The ventilation system does not have to be on all the time, just as windows need not be open all the time. It's just that the system needs to be able to be activated on demand by the room's occupant(s). A Heat Recovery Ventilator, such as the Fantech HRV-1404, can be used, which will (supposedly) recycle heat in cold weather and transfer heat in hot weather. See the "Is this overkill? Studio AC wiring layout" layout thread, especially the very end, where koberlin1 posts about his success using the Fantech HRV-1404. See the point in my thread where I discover the Fantech HRV system and how I incorporated into my plan, in conjunction with a mini split air conditioner for cooling comfort. To answer your question specifically about whether or not there are ways to incorporate windows that won't affect isolation much -- yes, there are, but it's expensive, especially if they're operable. If they're not operable, then you're still at square one. Typically, though, operable windows are not found in studio designs due to sound isolation concerns. Here's an example of fresh air requirements for residential construction (City of Boise, Idaho, using IRC 2003). Don't assume that these requirements are exactly the same as those in your local jurisdiction -- you need to check with them for the details. However, that handout should help clarify some of the concepts. Hope that helps... --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward19 July 2006#6
The Fantech VHR installation instructions do show that you can split the lines. After all, these units are designed to ventilate an entire house, not just one room. (Although, I think they would work extremely well for a single or double room studio, where the ventilation replaces operable windows.) However, for maximum isolation, you should not structure the lines like they show in their diagrams -- rather, do a "home run" configuration (i.e., from unit, to a "Y" split, and then each end of the "Y" going to each room). For specific heating and cooling code requirements, talk to your local building department. Really. There's no substitute for getting face-to-face with them with your cocktail napkin sketches, with a winning attitude about striving to do everything to code. They love that crap. ;-) Honestly, though -- I would imagine that you'd need air conditioning. Have you ever been in an air-tight studio environment that was too chilly? ;-) (That's easy for me to say right now -- our outside temperature right now is 99°F (37°C)! :lol:) --Keith :mrgreen:
