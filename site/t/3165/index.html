John Sayers11 February 2005#2
Hi Jeffery - that's a pretty cool setup you have but I can see how you need to move into a better facility. Your question is hard one because it really depends on what is available in your area. Finding 6m ceilings in a rental space (or purchase) is probably a hard one, but if you do you can save yourself the expense of the outer shell. There again the internal walls mightn't be suitable for a studio. If you can build from scratch it's so much easier as you can create your own layout, isolate your rooms correctly and wire and aircon it properly. Having new electrics with correct earthing, stabilised etc is a major advantage. Proper layout with good visuals makes such a difference whereas in existing buildings you are usually building around existing walls or avoiding posts etc. Parking and loading is also solved on your own block :) hope this helps cheers john

John Sayers12 February 2005#4
Jeffrey - my take on this is that for every studio that closes another opens - that's been the case here. The new studios are different because they are digital -no 56 input 300K consoles - just 50k worth of puters and preamps. I believe the recording market has increased. Sure there were these big studios catering to wealthy artists backed by wealthy record companies - now the market is the local band that wants to sell CDs at gigs but wants it to be a cut above the previous efforts they recorded in a garage or bedroom somewhere. They might do all the sequencing, song structure etc at home but they want your studio because you have all the gear setup correctly in rooms that have good acoustics and a control room that' s comfortable and gets a decent mix. They also get your years of experience - what's the price on that. the other markt IMO is the touring band that doesn't get time off road to set up a recording environment - they just want to take a couple of weeks off and put down some of their new songs because their crowd wants to buy them. I believe that for too long inexperienced engineer/producers have put their hand up claiming recording expertise because they've bought the lastest piece of homerecording gear only to produce a mishmash of bad recording and inexperienced mixing. It might be cheap but it sounds cheap also. Hope this helps cheers john
