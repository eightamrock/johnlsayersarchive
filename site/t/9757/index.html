xSpace16 November 2007#2
Hey brother. It looks like your neighbors in Slovenia are far far away from where you are at. I guess noise isn't much of an issue with neighbors? Looking at your diagram on the floor view, are you guys thinking of floating the floor, and if so, how important is it really? How much sound leakage into that hill behind you really matters? Based on the little bit I see here, option C is not an option, it could be one of those, how did you say, capitol mistakes? Option C is a fine drawing of what some folks call a three leaf system. I have also seen it referred to as the demon three leaf or the dreaded three leaf. In any event, it is not much cared for, the 3 leaf, like in option C. So if you lose the floated floor and beef up the studio build floor on the top side, this would be better. Then go underneath the floor, from the bottom, and add dense material pressed up against the wood floor by going in between the joist beams. Then you would insulate the joist cavities and apply your ceiling on to the very bottom of the ceiling/floor joists. Have a look at it here: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html Once at the above link, select "construction" from the left hand menu. Click the "walls and ceilings" tab at the top of the page. Study that, especially the part all the way at the bottom of the page, it's your situation. I realize these types of buildings can literally hold a truck up, but how long can good luck run? Get someone trusted, not Uncle Ed from the feed store either, that knows thier way around buildings and design. They call this person here stateside an engineer, you guys may have a different name. Actually I have a different name for them but it depends on what day of the week it is and how many are grouped up in the same place:)
