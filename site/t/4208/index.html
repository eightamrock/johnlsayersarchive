There's not exactly a 7.1 system standard set in stone yet. However the commonly (non SDDS) used setup is composed of Front Left, Center, Front Right, Left Surround, Right Surround, Left Rear Surround, and Right Rear Surround. Basically, if you were to look at a the room from above as a clock face, with the listener being the anchor points from the hands, the speakers would be laid out like this:(approximately for demonstration and or explanation purposes) FL=11 o'clock C=12 o'clock FR=1 o'clock LS=9 o'clock RS=3 o'clock LRS=7 o'clock RRS=5 o'clock any help? This is completely different from 6.1 setups where the standard 5.1 setup sees the addition of extra rear center speaker. However, with the current 6.1 encoding formats, the rear center is not discreet and is matrixed from a combo of the rears. Now SDDS, or Sony Dynamic Digital Sound, is a whole different bird with 8 discreet channels. However it has 5 in the front, 2 in the back, and 1 bass unit (sub). They're trying to get rid of the gap as the sound pans across the front of the room. In a lot of ways, especially dealing with phase, this is much better, but it's not used by nearly as many theatres nor home theatres as DTS or Dolby Digital.
