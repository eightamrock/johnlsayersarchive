knightfly14 June 2006#2
Before I can answer your questions about isolation, I need a bit more info (although you've been more thorough than most :) ) - On your side walls, are the frames inside your concrete walls TOUCHING the concrete or are they spaced away a bit? If possible, a detail sketch with dimensions would help. Other problem areas I see for isolation between neighbors - IF your ceiling drywall is directly attached to the joists, and if those joists run continuously into your neighbor's place (or touch something that DOES intrude into that space), you will HAVE to remove the drywall and hang it instead from resilient mounts; either Resilient Channel, or (better) from RSIC clips and hat channel. IF your wall's inner frame is NOT separated from the concrete walls, this will ALSO be a flanking path to the neighbors and will also require resilient mounts, which will entail removal of drywall, install mounting system, and replace drywall (minimum two layers of 5/8") Drum isolation (especially flanking thru the slab into neighbor's) would be helped somewhat by a floated riser (if done correctly) but would also require heavier wall (at least one more layer of drywall on the resilient mounts, better if Green Glue is used between layers. Biggest problem is not being able to permanently close off the garage door; an operable garage door is typically a REALLY poor sound isolator - almost no mass in panels, poor seals... Even if you were to be able to seal those panels better, the lack of mass would mean that to get any serious degree of isolation you'd need to compensate by using REALLY heavy mass for the INNER leaf (and placing it at least 12-16" away from the door) - so even if you were to come up with a design that allowed multiple 4x8 wall sections to be moved into place and tightly sealed, each 4x8 section would weigh about 350 pounds. Also - acoustically, a non-symmetrical control room will make it nearly impossible to get balanced, translatable mixes - the slight angle at the rear isn't a large problem, but that rectangular "bite" out of the front corner is. Those are the main challenges as I see them... Steve

knightfly15 June 2006#6
Without trying that, I can't say it will work; but here are some of the factors - A constant contact with insulation across it's breadth will damp a panel more than intermittent strips, so there's more potential of resonance with the strips. A 2" thick batt with the same exact panel laid on top will resonate (be weakest) at about 1 octave higher than a 4" batt - in most cases, the lower you can get this resonance (preferably over an octave BELOW the lowest frequency you want to isolate) the better. Realistically though, this would take a 4" (AFTER compression) panel with about 6" of CONCRETE on top of it to get that low in frequency, so it's possible that the higher frequency "weak spot" might fall in a frequency range that's less obnoxious by using a thinner batt. 705 should be stiff enough (without being TOO stiff) to support your panels, but if possible I'd use FOUR of your 4x8 panels - lay two side by side, then two more oriented at 90 degrees to those on top, then your (preferably thick and heavy) panels. A second pair of wood panels (again, at 90 degrees to the first pair) would help tie this together, and just a few screws between layers should make a pretty stable platform with good damping. The lighter the wood panels and the less contact with insulation, the more you'd be building a giant drum head (not good) Steve
