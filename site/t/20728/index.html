You seem to be missing a couple of things! :) There just isn't enough information in your post to be able to validly offer any advice on choosing between those two. Both seem viable, but we'd need to know all the usual set of information to be able to help you. I can only offer some very general comments on a couple of things you said:
I guess I would have the control room be part of the "tracking room" or maybe not?
Personally, I much prefer to have a separate control room,for many reasons. One of those is that control rooms absolutely must be symmetrical, and absolutely must have totally neutral acoustics, not changing the sound in any way. But lie rooms, tracking rooms, vocal booths, drum rooms, etc. should NOT be neutral: they are supposed to have individual "character", as needed. So trying to use a single room for both means that either you have lousy acoustics when you are mixing, or you have lousy acoustics when you are recording. Or more likely: both! Another reason is that when I'm tracking, I like to hear exactly how each instrument sounds in each mic, on the CR speakers by themselves, cleanly, to make sure I'm getting the best possible sound. But you can't do that if the instrument and mic are in the same room as the speakers, for obvious reasons. And other reasons too... But some people do manage to track in the CR, at least for vocals and things like acoustic guitar, that sound just fine in a neutral room.
but the basement needs a subfloor system
No, it does not need a sub-floor system! That would be a terrible idea. What it needs is a level floor, that is also massive, solid, hard, rigid, and well damped. ie, it needs a slab on grade. It already has that, except for the lousy lino on top and an uneven surface. So pull up the lino and use self-leveling cement to get a smooth, flat, level floor: There's nothing better, for a studio. But do not, under any circumstances, build a sub-floor on top of that. You cannot, have air-filled cavities under your floor, and you cannot have a thin, lightweight surface over that, such as plywood or OSB. Anyway, please provide the rest of the info, and we'll see if we can offer anything that would help you choose. - Stuart -

Soundman202028 November 2016#4
Its interesting how the basement is long and narrow, so not sure how to divide it up for control room considering the dimensions.
Long and narrow is not good for a control room. There's a bunch of good "room ratios" (relationships between dimensions of the room), but very few of them are in the range "long and narrow". So I would go with whichever location gives you a "NOT long and narrow" option! :) You can use the calculator at this link to help you decide on which space is better: http://amroc.andymel.eu You could still use the basement if you wanted, by building a wall across the "long narrow" bit, to make it "not so long", such that you get a reasonable ratio inside the Bolt area (or close to it). - Stuart -

Soundman202011 January 2017#7
the control room would be pretty small but i mix at quiter volumes anyways and dont seem to have many issues.
The size of the control room is not related in any way to the volume that you play at. The size of the control governs the acoustic response. Specs say that the minimum size for decent acoustics is 20m2 floor area. That's for plain vanilla stereo. If you want the room for 5.1 or 7.1, then it needs to be 30m2. Take a look at BS.1116-2
I mainly want as big as possible tracking room
The volume of the tracking room needs to be about 5 times larger than the volume of the control room. Normally the ceiling height in the tracking room is much higher than in the control room, so that's ot too hard to do, as long as the floor area is about 3 times larger than the control room.
the halways is there to possible use as a reverb mic for some cool effects or just to make it easier to sell the house later on, but I could do without the hallway as well.
It's wasted space. I very much doubt that you'd be able to convert that into a successful echo chamber. I would use that to expand the CR. Other things I noticed: 1) You have no direct access between CR and LR (LR= "live room" = tracking room). It will get very old, very soon, having to go through multiple doors simply to adjust a mic, or move an instrument. Especially drums: You might have upwards of a dozen mics on the drum kit, and each one might need a dozen adjustments to get it just right... running back and forward through several doors, up and down an echoey corridor, while you make hundreds of adjustments to the drum mic, will get VERY old, very fast.... Do yourself a big favor, and put a direct door between the two. Make it a pair of sliding glass doors, back to back, so you don't even have to open and close them as you go through... 2) You have double-wide doors on the CR. Good luck trying to seal those to get decent isolation! :) Stick to single-wide doors only. 3) CR shape: What design concept are you following there? It looks like it might be an attempt at RFZ or maybe NER or perhaps CID, but it doesn't match any of them! Your mix position is way too far forwards, and the speaker soffits are angled way too extremely, 4) You have the bathroom INSIDE the LR???? :shock: :cop: ??? Ummm.... Nope. Just no. Many reasons that's a really bad idea. Put it out near the waiting room, where it belongs. - Stuart -
