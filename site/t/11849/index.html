Ro29 March 2009#2
We have to discuss the term "soffit" I think. WHAT do we mean when we say "soffit" mounted speakers. I prefer "flush" mounted, but it's just semantics... The whole idea of soffit or flush mounted speakers is, indeed, the infinite bevel. You get that by extending the speaker's front case with a non-absorping material. So, no, your idea is not "soffit" mounted. But, it's a good idea to have it mounted in a basstrap (suppachunk!) IF you're gonna place the speakers in the corner (which I can understand if the room is small). There's lot's of low-end frq hanging around in them corners, putting a speaker in that hot area is generally not a good idea. So, yeah, covering it up with as much wool as possible would be a very good idea. You might even wanna place a denser material in front to keep it from absorbing too much mid/high frq AND have some sort of bevel anyway. Why not make it a glass-soffit :)

The monitors I use are EVENT TR8 (which are powerful active midfield monitors
Then your current design has a problem: Active monitors need cooling, which implies free space and air flow. But your design calls for a very thick blanket, basically! You would need to modify it somehow so that there is a free passage for air up past the back of the monitors, right from down near the floor somewhere, to up near the ceiling somewhere. Take a look at John's design for soffits to get the idea. Think of a kind of duct a couple of inches deep and as wide as the back panel of the monitor, with a clear, open, free passage for air to move through it and cool the rear of the monitor. - Stuart -

Soundman202031 March 2009#10
Oh yes, I forgot to draw it, but the ceiling would be just 10 cm higher than the top of the superchunk... but that's a good idea too to make a hole in the front and to make the superchunk as high as the ceiling !
Yeah, you do want to take the insulation right up to the ceiling. That's where you get the best acoustic effect, so certainly do it that way. I think you'll probably need to build some kind of "chimney" around that airflow space, perhaps with plywood, drywall, or something like that. You want to make sure that nothing blocks the airflow, for example if some of the insulation shifts during installation, and you didn't notice.
However, i'm still wondering about the problem of absorption of low frequencies inside the superchunk. Do I need to put the monitors inside a tight and strong wood box (as usually on soffit mouting)?
Well, yours isn't a soffit! Like Ro implied, if it's not going to be a soffit at all (which yours isn't), then I don't see any need for boxing in your speakers. Your situation is far more like free-standing speakers on stands, and you don't box those in! About the only reason I can think of for a box is to keep the wool from touching the speakers, but that doesn't seem to be an issue with your design: you have the sides and back clear of the wool. I'd say just put your speakers on really dense, solid, heavy, massive stands (like the brickwork columns that you show: that's good!), and decouple the speakers form the stands as well as you possibly can. I guess you are going to build some type of frame around the entire trap, and cover it with acoustic cloth, to hide the "ugly stuff"? If so, then you'll need to figure out how to make a frame around the front of the speaker for the "hole" in the middle of the cloth where the speaker will be. You can probably attach that from to the stand somehow, taking care that it does not actually touch the speaker. You might need some sort of shelf just above the speaker, though to support the weight of the insulation above. You probably don't want all of that resting on your speaker. Especially if you need to take the speaker out for some reason... - Stuart -

Soundman202031 March 2009#13
to have a controlled and precise low end, so as to be able to work it, not to have a huge and big bottom to make the shake the walls
Well, coming full circle here: What you mention there is EXACTLY what real soffits do!!! Not the type you were trying to design here, but real flush mount soffits with large, massive, dense, flat front panels)! They focus and tighten up the bottom end amazingly, giving you much better control. Of course, if you flush mount (soffit mount) then you do need to have something that can roll of the bass response of your speakers by 6 dB at the bottom end, since the soffit WILL boost the bass by 6 dB: it forces the speaker to radiate into half-space, so according to the laws of physics, that will give a 6dB boost. Many active monitors already have controls on the back that allow you to dial in 6dB roll-off, for exactly this reason. If yours don't, then a good parametric equalizer will do that for you, accurately. Get one that introduces the least amount of phase change.
what about a real soffit/ flush mounting in a such small room
My control room will be very small too (2.57m high, 2.26m wide, 2.81m long), and I will be building flush-mount soffits for my speakers. My soffit panels will be made of concrete, since that is a very dense material that can be molded to the exact shape I need, and it doesn't need to be very thick due to the high density, so I can gain a couple of precious millimeters of room space like that! I'll just build a frame the size and shape that I need, and pour concrete in it. Bingo! Instant soffit panel! So yeah, you can do flush soffit mounts in a small room. I'll be using some of the space below the soffits for a bit of bass trapping, and the space between them for absorption. My bass traps will be in the rear wall-wall corners, the rear wall-ceiling corner, the side wall-ceiling corners, and part of the front wall-ceiling corner (the part between the soffits). I expect that will be enough! Check my design thread later this weak, and there should be an updated model there, with all the details of what I'm planning to do. - Stuart -
