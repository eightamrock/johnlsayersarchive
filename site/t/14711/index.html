So I've just moved house and I've got the upstairs set up as a mix room. I'm just looking for some advice on what people would do if they had my room. Soundman202021 August 2010#3
Hey Jamie, welcome! Looks to me like you have a really difficult space to work with! Right now, you don't have room symmetry so your mixes will not translate well. You need to get the front half of the room (from your head forwards, at least) symmetrical, if you hope to produce good mixes. In other words, you need to arrange your head on a central axes of the room, equidistant from both sidewalls, and set the speakers plus your head at the apexes of an equilateral triangle. That's the basic geometry that just has to be in place. Based on that, you can start figuring out where to put your initial room treatment (bass traps and first reflection absorption) then measure the room response, to see what still needs doing. (By the way, please abide by the forum rules and upload your photos here, instead of hosting them externally, and size them correctly). The only real place where you can get some kind symmetry in that room without building new walls, is by placing your desk in front of the fireplace. One big factor in choosing that spot is the peaked ceiling. That isn't ideal by any means, but I just can't see any better way of arranging the geometry. - Stuart -
