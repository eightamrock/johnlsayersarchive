gullfo25 September 2007#11
sorry - my fault - i shouldn't discuss math and use dashes to break up sentences.... :oops: assume a hung trap constructed of free-hanging fiberglass-covered panels, each measuring 24" long (l), on 6" hangers (s) (total length = 30" or 2.5 feet) would be effective above 2.5' x 7 = 17.5' Effective cutoff wavelength 2.5 x 7 = 17.5 ft. Frequency = V/l = 1130/17.5 = 64.5 Hz so you have f = 1130/(7 * (l + s)) but you need some minimums for hanging distance and inter-panel gaps. you can adjust the heights of the hanging panels as well as the panel size. you want the panels to be mostly perpendicular to the sound entry which for a corner trap is forward and back, not at a 45 degree. the cavity of the hanging traps is lined with insulation (peak pressure) and usually a layer in front (peak velocity) as a function of the 1/4 wavelength depth of the cavity. also, the width of the panel must play some role in efficiency but i'm not sure its easily calculated. ultimately what you want to achieve is sound going in and not coming out :-)

gullfo26 September 2007#13
Joka wrote:
the frequency will be ... 18.2Hz. Should I put the insulation in front of the hangers just behind the cloth face?
the estimate of 18hz is right based on theory but in practice i'm not certain you will get that solely on the hanger size as the cavity in which it sits plays a role. you should probably target something around 45-50hz as reasonable for the corner trap (unless you can build it about 1,5m deep or so). a panel trap may be more likely to help you with the lower problem frequencies (which you can determine approximately from room mode calculations). yes to the insulation behind the cloth.
Joka wrote:
It is very difficult to find an appropriate cloth here in Macedonia so I read somewhere on this forum that any cloth you can blow through is probably good enough. Is this true?
yes and remember to treat the cloth with a fire retardent agent for safety.
Joka wrote:
slat helmholtz absorbers. My constructor engineer suggested that maybe I should use 12.5mm gypsum board instead of wood because we have a lot of left in stock, and it is easier to cut the gaps between slats.I have attached just a preview as I imagine how to do it.
you could use gypsum but it can get messy - flaking, etc. wood is generally a nicer finish. there is a helmholtz calculator on this site you can use to compute the proper sizes.
