doublehelix15 October 2007#2
I think you should be OK with the R-19, but of course it depends on what your wall design is going to be. Are you going to build another wall inside of that existing wall (room-within-a-room) or possibly use resilient channel? If the existing R-19 is going to be pointing towards an airgap, you might want to point the paper facing towards a wall or drywall rather than towards the airgap. I did this originally, and had to go back and tear out a bunch of insulation and reverse it! Ouch!
Dan Scott15 October 2007#3

xSpace16 October 2007#4
Dan Scott wrote:
I suppose the blue foam board is ok to leave as well? Thanks, Dan
The blue rigid foam insulation does supply you a purpose, a dual purpose. It has an R value which, coupled with the R value of the existing wall, allows the wall a better overall ability to take on the day-to-day fight with keeping the outside elements out and the inside elements in. The blue panels also serve as a first defense against all manner of whatever goes on behind the brick veneer. Like wind bursts from the weep holes or just plan cold/hot air from outside when it is cold/hot outside. I'd leave it, but that's just me,

Dan Scott20 October 2007#8
Well, I made a little more progress since the last post. I got all the tear out debris cleaned up and finished insulating all of the existing walls with R19. I also removed all of the drywall nails and screws from the joists and studs. I also framed in the garage door rough opening but didn't sheet it yet as I decided to acid stain the concrete instead of using bamboo flooring. I'll have to use a presure cleaner with acid to prep and clean it and it would have been a hassle draining the fluid out if the room was enclosed. The reason I decided to not use wood flooring was that the garage floor had leveling issues which I realized while framing and I just knew that there would be issues with buckling and seams coming apart, etc with tongue and grrove type flooring. The acid stain and acrylic sealer will be the bomb and look killer. There's a big mall in our area with the same treatment and it's tough as nails. I'm going with a terra cotta color. On the bottom pressure treated sill plate of the enclosure, I put a bead of backer rod where the sill plate butted up to the concrete lip. The lip was the exact same depth of the 2x4 so it actually sat flush with the inside of the garage floor which was nice. I will seal this gap as well as the front bottom at the exterior of the sill with acoustical sealant before putting on the fiber board then sheathing and siding. I will also instal backer rod in between the sheating boards then seal. I'm also including some before photos of the future bathroom. I met with a plumber yesterday and he will have to bust through the concrete and elbow into my crawl space to tie into the drain but he said it could be done. In the area where the water heater sits will be replaced with a pedestal sink. High on the wall above the sink will be a tankless waterheater. See ya'll next time! Dan

Dan Scott2 November 2007#14
Greetings Everyone, Well it was a couple busy days and as planned, I got all my framing finished except for the CR monitor soffits/traps. I did install the first layer of sound board over the garage door opening and sealed with backer rod and acoustic caulk. I will cover with OSB and apply backer rod/caulk again before adding the "fake" windows and siding. I also got the HVAC system installed today! I was able to install a whole separate 2 ton heat pump unit. The air handler was able to be located in the attic space in my house far away from the studio itself. The insulated wall behind the unit is the wall to the staircase that goes up to the bonus room above the studio. So, there's that wall then the width of a staircase, then the next wall which is the back wall to the lounge, vox booth and entrance area. The unit is very quite in the first place and far from the studio so it won't be heard at all. I must say that it worked out very nicely. I'm getting all of the wiring installed on Saturday and decided to run snake boxes to each room vs. wall plates. I had to special order 2/4 mic input boxes for the control room and lounge area as I don't need alot of inputs for those areas, so this will be the only thing holding me up on the sheetrock. I'm actually amazed at how smoothly things are moving along so I can't complain :P I owe alot of credit to the folks who participate on this web site and dedicate there time putting up information for us to learn from. Dan Scott16 November 2007#24
Hi Everyone, I've made a little more progress since the last post and thought I would post some more pics. My electrician is almost finished wiring my sub panel and I recieved the 2 cases of puddy pads for the outlet boxes. These are fire rated AND have an STC rating of 55. I wonder if the inspector will know what they are? In my county, there is only 1 inspector for everything. They are real picky about fire blocking but with the double wall construction, it is impossible to isolate every wall cavity without connecting the walls which defeats the whole purpose of rooms within rooms. With the fire stop puddy pads and the double R39 fire bat, it shouldn't be an issue..... I hope. The insulation I purchased can burn at 2000 degrees for 4 hours!! I had to special order 2 smaller snake boxes for the vox booth and lounge/auxilary sound room. They finally came in and I ran them to the control room using 2" pvc elbows with a short 12" extension to brace to the studs. The elbows stick out and with be flush with the double sheetrock once finished.
