The control room was starting to look great, but it was still lacking the finishing touches. Sarah said you need some cushions for the sofa. What follows is an account of how to make a couple of unique items that really do tie in the visual appearance of the whole room. Now like most blokes, I wasn’t known for being particularly good at needlework at school. So, with Sarah’s supervision, first take some off cuts from the fabric you have left over, and carefully cut long parallel strips of random width.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/166-6675_IMG.jpg
Then arrange in a random combination and sew them together – Yes, I did do this!
External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/166-6676_IMG.jpg
External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/166-6677_IMG.jpg

External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/166-6684_IMG.jpg
Then comes the ingenious bit. Accurately cut them at 90° to give you 8 multi coloured strips (4 for each cushion).
External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/166-6689_IMG.jpg
Finally, arrange them as follows: Put the strips side by side in the order in which they were cut, take strips 2 and 4 and swap them for 6 and 8. Lastly turn strips 1 3 5 and 7 through 180° and sew 1 to 4 and 5 to 8 together! Hey presto a whacky but unique random patch work cushion. :wink:

I was absolutely gutted, but Sarah came to the rescue as you’ll see in a moment. :D Now then for anyone who hasn’t wired up the back of a desk before this is how it’s done. Please bear in mind that in my situation (and I’m sure I’m not alone) I have virtually no access to the rear as the desks are so tight against the front wall, and there was no way they were coming out again. Firstly, pull through enough cable from the live room to enable you to work on it in the middle of the control room. Strip the 16 pair (in my case) back enough to cover the entire width of the desk you’re wiring to, hold all cores accross all chanels and accurately cut each one in numerical order, so that it lines up with its corresponding channel strip.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i195.photobucket.com/albums/z194/m2uso/album%20name/170-7015_IMG.jpg
In this case the cable was fed from right to left, and so, cable 1 was the longest and cable 24 (part of the other 16 pair) the shortest. Then thread all plastic sleeves onto the cables and secure in place with tape. There’s nothing worse than soldering the perfect connector to later realise that you’ve forgotten to thread the sleeves! :oops: :cry: :oops: PS – If you look to the right of the cable by the iron, you can see how Sarah sorted out my burn mark. Now it’s barely visible – Nail file!!! :roll:

This is possible because of the accurate cuts made earlier, i.e., all cables are shorter than each other by the same amount.
