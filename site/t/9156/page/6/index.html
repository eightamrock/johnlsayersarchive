Lou, it's not per definition a bad thing you installed HC to hang yer boards on. It's just that many times HC is confused with RC. HC ain't resilient as Rod just mentioned. RC might be a bit more expensive than hat, but it'll boost yer trans.loss. (as Rod mentioned). So why not use RC instead of HC. Or in your case, why did you NOT use RC instead of HC?

Hi guys – well what a couple of days, just about every emotion possible flying around my head – excitement, frustration, dismay, confusion, gratitude, self-anger, you name it chaps, ‘been there, done that’. :evil: :evil: So then – last night I did, indeed, go down the pub for a few bevvy’s, with Rod’s book in hand. I sat in a quiet corner and read a bit, and then, some more and,…….you get the idea. Firstly I need to know that I now have a much better understanding of what’s going on in my build plans, so if any of you want to chime in after reading this, then as always, please feel free. :wink: As I see it at the moment, whilst being correctly advised (by someone very important) to employ resilient (bloody well) channel over the entire outer ceiling, driod-features here, ignorantly purchased the wrong product. Whilst I hope you’d all agree that the ceiling I’ve installed is impressive for its structural integrity and level-ness etc, I’m sure you’d also all agree that I’ve built a rigid structure, and directly coupled it to the existing floor joists of bedroom 4 via the stuff that I cannot speak of, thus making the isolation properties of my ceiling marginally better than timber battens. DUH! :roll: Given that it’s the three month anniversary of the project start tomorrow, I was, as you can imagine, totally hacked off and frustrated at such a mess-up on my part. Being the kind of bloke that rarely accepts ‘grey’ and who prefers ‘black or white’ every time, I decided to do a serious reality check. Up until 12.30 this morning, the whole lot was coming down and being re-done properly! :shock: I woke at 6.00am and got straight on the computer and found this product in Warrington (north-west UK) http://www.cmsacoustics.co.uk/c-acoustics/isomax.htm Now please feel free to check out the site, UK builders, since they seem to know their stuff. Once they opened, I called them for prices. 216 clips at £4.53 each – 38 lengths of their channel to fit - £273.60, all plus V.A.T. – Grand total: £1,471.19. Adding to this the cost of what would be wasted materials in the rip-out, plus the extra time to do it all, and, the wait for delivery etc, made the whole concept seem like a bit of a non-starter to say the least. There had to be a more cost/time affective alternative. Back to the reality check for a moment – “Constructing an isolated system is only as good as the weakest link in the assembly-with the greatest challenges being spaces directly above or below you” Dude – you’re so right on that one, I’ve heard it for myself. :wink: :wink: Bedroom 4 is occupied by an eleven year old who is at school all day, and who does spend a lot of time watching TV, but even more time reading. The amount of ambient air-born noise from above is therefore difficult to calculate but for the fact that, when she’s not in her room, it’s zero. :D Ambient noise in the loft hatch/bass-trap hanger area? – Zero. :D Ambient noise over new flat roof and pitched roof? Other than rainfall shielded by trees, – Zero. :) So here then is the proposal, I’m sorry if Rod – John – Eric etc disagree, but it seems to make sense (to me at least – unless I’ve missed something else!). :oops: Live with what I’ve got for the minute, keep going blah blah etc, and at the end of the job (as and when necessary), go up to bedroom 4, clear it out, take up the carpet to reveal the ¾” tongue and groove chipboard flooring. Then, carefully fix 10mm solid (circular section) neoprene extrusion all around the perimeter. Lay neoprene extrusion in a criss-cross mesh pattern at 300mm centers over the entire floor. Lay a new ¾” tongue and groove chipboard floor to the top of this ‘neoprene mesh’ with a ¼” gap around the entire perimeter, fill that gap with neoprene extrusion, and lastly seal all gaps and joints with acoustic caulk. Then lay a new carpet to the new chipboard without attaching it to the original floor, thus de-coupling bedroom 4 floor from studio 4 ceiling. A sort of 'arse-backwards resilient channel!' Total cost £32.60 (I’ve already got 200m of neoprene as you know), plus whatever carpet her ladyship would like in her bedroom. It will give a gentle softness to her floor without a ‘springy’ bounce…………………trust me I’ve already tried it, photo to prove if required. :D :D Thanks for putting up with the long winded read, but I’ve been real down, yet am also pleased that I might be able to salvage the cock-up. From now on - don’t start any new part of the job without re-reading, re-asking, and re-researching first! :roll: :roll: :roll: Kind regards Gentlemen as always, Lou. 8)
