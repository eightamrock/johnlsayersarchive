Hi Guys – Hope you’re all OK! John – many thanks, mate, and I’m sure I can speak for many up here when I say that any comment from the likes of you and Rod is always welcome. :wink: DH – thanks as always, my friend. Don’t ever be ashamed of your marvellous efforts – and, yes – she’s a fine woman indeed. :D :D Kalet – nice to hear from you, buddy, and thank you. Dan – many thanks to you as well my friend, and Sarah adores cooking. Actually we both love entertaining friends, parties etc, so to be without our kitchen is a real headache. :shock: Not quite sure where to start this one as, to be honest, this could well be my first ever update without pictures. Not much I can do about that I’m afraid, although. There’s certainly a bit to tell you about my intense frustrations over the last week! No DH, nothing to do with tightly packed jeans! :wink: :wink: So, we were, as you know, doing pretty much ok, but I had to turn my attentions to chill-out and kitchen area. Here it comes my friends. Rule 1: When site managing and planning a job – Do not take anything anyone says for granted (including yourself). :? Rule 2: When on a ridiculous schedule, always remember that someone around you will inevitably screw up and it will, of course, be your fault. :evil: Rule 3: When plant and machinery aren’t being used flat out – ‘off hire’ said devices. Rule 4: Don’t allow the almighty power of ‘Sod’s Law’ to totally throw an unbelievably frustrating spanner in the works. :roll: :roll: Why the banter? Well folks it’s simple. I’ve done some serious evaluation over the last few days and reckon, quite sensibly, that I’m around two and a half weeks behind schedule. This can, of course, be made up but, it will take some doing. Bryan and Trev have been nothing short of guardian angels this week, and it was all going fine until………………….9.30pm last Mon night. Andy, as you know, was due to start the plastering in the chill-out/kitchen area last Thurs week – 1st Nov. I heard nothing from him until a text on the following Fri night to say that he would call me Sat to arrange 8.00am start Mon. I heard nothing more all weekend. Mon night I learned via text that he has serious family stuff to deal with, and work is not on his agenda at the moment. :( Now I’ve been brought up to be a compassionate person, and naturally, Andy’s family come before my plastering job. However, that piece of news did really drop me in the preverbal ‘shit’. I have, if you recall, a kitchen arriving this Tuesday to be fitted this week. If I’d postponed that delivery, we would have had to wait until mid December! :x :x So, there I was last Mon panicking like hell and also remembering that we still had to get the under-floor heating down before the tilers could get in. I tell you guys, although it doesn’t sound much, logistics can be a real headache, especially when you’re relying on other people. :x :x Add to this, the fact that it’s three/four days to plaster the entire kitchen/chill-out area, plus a day for under-floor heating and then two days to tile and grout, and you can’t walk on it for the rest of the day once completed. Time was definitely running out, my friends. It gets worse – I spoke to four tilers about my ‘heating on a roll’ cable system, and each of them said they never touch that stuff as “it’s a total pig to lay”. Time then to get back to the guy who supplied it, and a long series of back and forth comments about who was right or wrong etc, until I basically instructed him to call at my house on his way home from work to deliver what the tilers would lay, (a sort of pre made mesh on a roll). And, remove the stuff they wouldn’t. :evil: It was now Tues afternoon and we still didn’t have a plasterer! Very luckily, the guy who’s doing the tiling knew another guy who could be pulled off a job to help out – great news. Finally at around 8.30am last Wed, a chap turned up and started plastering our kitchen well aware of the tight deadline to finish so that we could get the tiles down before the kitchen arrival. This chap shall be known from now on as ‘Numbnuts’ :roll: I say this because, whilst his finish is fine, (even to my fussy eye), he’s unbelievably slow, ridiculously messy, and to cap it all disappears for up to two hours during the day and ‘knocks off’ at 3.30pm!!!! Like I said, ‘Numbnuts’. I won’t bore you with how I’ve had to practically spoon feed him through the job, but suffice it to say, it’s been a crap week to say the least. :evil: :evil: :evil: We do, however, finally have the under-floor heating down, all floor tiles laid ready for grouting tomorrow morning, and the kitchen plastered – chill-out area still to be plastered whilst the kitchen is being installed – great, and Graham – my star spark, goes on holiday for ten days tomorrow, so although we will have the kitchen/chill-out area finished this time next week, and be able to get back to normality, we wont be able to use it, as none of it will be wired up! Stress or what? :roll: This week sees a mad effort to finish the outer beef-up in the studio. This includes a layer of plasterboard on all the walls followed by a layer of MDF, then the final ceiling layer, finishing with the final wall layer. Then it’s onto front and rear outer-leaf window ‘beefs’, 6” of insulation all over and then, at last, we can start on all the inner wall framing – not before time! The whole project has been thrown off course now so we’ve really got our work cut out! Thanks for reading guys, Regards as always, Lou. 8) Oh, ok then, here’s just a few pics of where we are! :wink: Spur and 30amp feed for new cooker.

Now, I, like you guys, am no expert in this area but this is what he’s told me. Both internal units will be hung on isolated strap hangers. The two internal units in question are the Daikin FDXS25CVMB, and FDXS35CVMB. The ducting will be 200mm x 200mm, and the design of the layout will ensure that none of the ducting paths cross.
