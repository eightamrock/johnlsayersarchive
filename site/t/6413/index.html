knightfly11 June 2006#5
Actually, since the "mystery distance" is somewhere between 10 and 30 FEET, your best bet (short of finding a different space) would be to build HALF of that wall but put ALL FOUR layers of 5/8 wallboard on the same side of the frame, such that your air gap between existing and wallboard is at least 12" - (not feet this time) I made some assumptions here - one, that the furring strips on neighbor side are "2x2's", 1.5" thick, and two, that your block are 1" thick web 8" thick overall. IF those are close, then the added furring/1/2" drywall will contribute a resonance (weak spot) at around 115 hZ, right where serious bass starts to kick in more or less :cry: 1" light concrete block webs have a critical frequency between 400 and 500 hZ, another dip in wall performance. By adding a second, free-standing frame with 12" air gap, you make the side of the blocks toward YOU, combined with the 12" gap and 4 layers of drywall, into approximately STC 75 wall with a m-a-m resonance around 23 hZ, and only a slight dip around 500 hZ. Don't cheer yet; this is BEFORE we deduct the weak spots of the other half of your block wall, centered at 115 and 400-500 hZ - these will weaken the combination of your side of the blocks and your added 4 layers of drywall, mainly at those two frequencies or near them - I've no way of calculating exactly how MUCH, but if you have that much space moving the drywall away from the blocks by another foot will help. The end result should make it so you'd need to be at around 100 dB SPL playing drums in order to just be audible next door - this is of course assuming no OTHER flanking paths for the noise, such as common floor slab :cry: If the space either side of yours is either vacant or has an understanding tenant, I'd set up some drums in your space and have someone kick the crap out of them while you're next door listening, so you can get a reference point - that should give you an idea whether the space has gaping holes or not - as it stands now, the kick drum and probably one of the toms (maybe the snare, depending on tuning) should be the loudest thing you hear, and they should be pretty noticeable. HTH... Steve

knightfly14 June 2006#7
Deeper furring (2x4) would lower this frequency to around 68 hZ (not good) - there is no "magic bullet" for specific frequencies, only more mass and wider air gap for the parts you DO have control over - so if that wall is furred with 2x4's, you'd just need to widen the air gap and maybe add another layer of drywall to the inner wall you're contemplating. References for this - sorry, proprietary software that costs $1000. OTher than that, you may find something free/useful here http://www.studiotips.com/ join, and you can download useful calculation stuff for free. I'm not sure why you think floating an entire room isn't an option; what did I say (link please) that led you to that? IF this is commercial space, the floor slab may be plenty strong enough for this. A fully floated inner room would eliminate 98% of any isolation problems, especially if you keep that extra leaf in mind when doing the side walls... Steve
