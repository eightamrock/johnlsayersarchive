knightfly5 July 2004#8
Charlie, you're saying that your walls are already constructed as in fig.B, and that you intend to remove the lower portion to build your floated floor? If so, you may be stuck with less than ideal isolation options. Even though your brick may not be airtight, it will still act quite a lot as a mass layer, lessening the improvements of a second air space and more mass - Eric Desart has made this pretty clear to me in past comments about wall construction, even though I didn't want to hear it any more than you probably do. Still, sometimes you just gotta go with what's available, and try to compensate where you can... Is there any way to gain access to either side of your brick layer, enough to seal it? And, before you answer, are there already "weep holes" along the bottom of your bricks? (They'd just be small holes, possibly in the mortar between bricks) - You're messing with mother nature here, so it's very important we know EXACTLY what is done, what CAN be done, and HOW it was done with WHAT - otherwise, you can run into problems with rot, mold (possibly life-threatening kind), and falling walls (hydraulic pressure buildup from blocking weep holes) - The more info we have, the closer we can get to suggesting workable solutions... Steve
