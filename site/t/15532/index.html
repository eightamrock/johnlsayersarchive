hi guys, first time poster here but i've been a lurker for a while. i've been reading this forum with great interest and im starting my own little room next week. i will be dividing my kitchen space so i can have a dedicated space for practicing/tracking drums and mixing. id really like to have separate tracking and control rooms but its not possible due to my space limitations. the new room will be an irregular trapezoid and im planning to build a room within room using the inside out method. the internal wall lengths are 4m, 4m, 5m and 1.5m. the room will be 2.5 meters high. the outer walls are 6" CHB with grout and the 4th outer wall will be constructed with light steel framing with 2 layers of 5/8" gypsum. the outer ceiling is 3 meters high and is made from 4" thick concrete. the floor is also a concrete slab but not isolated nor floating. for the new inside walls, im going to use 2x3 lumber which is actually called 2x4 here (local wood is always undersized). i might probably use 2x3 also on the ceiling but i dont know if this lumber can carry 2 layers of 5/8" gypsum with a span of 4m. the concrete floors will be covered with hard wood over a vapor barrier. i will be using single super door and there will be 2 windows: one is fixed looking over the kitchen and the other one is a regular sliding window that opens to the garage. gullfo22 January 2011#2
Welcome! It's definitely an interesting space. while tight it looks like you've angle the soffits appropriately for the space. Lookup some topics on soffits to see how to construct them and use that space behind for your bass trapping. Plan on some additional bass trapping in the back corners of the room as well. Are the soffit and speakers as shown to scale? If so they should be ok from a size perspective. The ceiling is tricky. You could possible split the ceiling span using 2x8 (10?) beams and run your 2x3 off that. Using a layer of OSB to strengthen the ceiling before putting on the drywall layers. Since you have a lot of space left overhead, you could put the ventilation ducts up there. If in doubt get a structural engineer to review and comment.

gullfo26 January 2011#8
you won't increase the density (32Kg/m3) by increasing the depth - but if you double layer it and compress it to 50mm, then you are close. use the 2x layers - 100mm wherever you can. on the hangers, you can use lighter insulation (15-18Kg/m3). same for pockets of air in the corner absorbers and in the wall (between walls).
