len-morgan23 January 2006#3
One thing that jumps out at me is I believe you use RC OR Green Glue but not both. RC is generally used on ceilings when you have to attach to your existing joists. As far as flanking noise, considering what you are planning to record, I don't think this is going to be big problem (I'm sure others who know more will chime in if I'm wrong). Your primary problem with flanking is low frequency and since you didn't mention recording live drums, you might be able to do what I did and just use anchor bolts to screw the bottom plates to the slab. Is there any reason there is so much of a gap between walls D and E? You are going to lose a lot of floor space and anything beyond about a 12" air space (between D & E) is not buying you much. On your point 2) - You WILL create a triple leaf if you finish the "back" of wall D. What you'll need to do is leave it open and then beef up between the studs on wall E with at least a couple of layers of 5/8" drywall. This IS something you can do yourself (no need to pay somebody). Just remove the insulation and then cut strips of dry wall and put them between the studs (I mean right up against the wall too - no air gaps!) and calk the stud/drywall edges at each layer. Two layers would be the minimum - 3 or more would be better. When you get to the last layer of drywall strips, before you calk the last stud/drywall layer, put a wooden cleat (maybe 3/4" to 1") on each edge to hold it all tight. Screw this into the studs (NOT INTO THE DRYWALL) and then calk the edges. That should go a long way to sound proof from the rest of your house. You havent' mentioned anything about the ceiling. Do you already have a finished ceiling? 7.6 feet seems awful low for an unfinished ceiling. How is the roofing system between the house and the garage built? This could be a bigger source of flanking noise than the floor. We'll need a few more details before we can help here. I would council taking your time with the design. Don't rush into this or you might end up spending your $8K and not have a room that meets your needs. As far as your building abilities, you can do a lot more than you think you can. I know because I thought the same thing and I've managed to do a pretty good job (I think) on my studio and it was a much bigger project (3 recording rooms + control room and office/lounge/hallway). I had someone helping me that knew what he was doing but in many cases, he would show me how something should be done and then I'd do the bulk of the work myself after he'd left. Have a little confidence in yourself. We'll provide all the support you need. We're generally a pretty friendly bunch. len

knightfly26 January 2006#13
Common mis-conception here; if you have 3" between frames, and your frames are 3.5" wide each, then the effective air gap (for m-a-m calculations) would be 3.5 + 3.5 + 3, or 10" - which is plenty when combined with a couple layers of drywall on the outside of each frame. IF you do a complete cavity fill, then the standard "fluffy stuff" will give within a couple of dB the same isolation as using the high priced 3 PCF rock wool or rigid fiberglass. If you're in a humid location and want some air circulation between the wall layers, you can use the rigid stuff and fasten fiberglass pallet strapping across the studs to hold it in position AGAINST the wallboard for damping (important) - this would leave an actual air space of about 3" between wall frames, and wouldn't hurt isolation much (maybe a couple dB) - You'll get better isolation staying with 24" centers for framing; if it were possible, the best isolation would be had by letting the panels float in space but practicality says 24" centers :? Check out the REFERENCE section for info on caulking, drywall, etc, there's a LOT there... Steve

knightfly30 January 2006#16
Your door idea should work fine, seals are as important as mass in some cases so that's a good idea. Noticed a comment about cinder block; have you seen the comments that hollow block is ALREADY a 2-leaf wall? Any chance of filling them with sand, or do you not need a lot of isolation? Isomax - haven't used it, need more study time to compare, sorry. HVAC - when deciding on parts/pieces, keep in mind that the noise caused by moving air is approximately equal to the 4th or 5th POWER of velocity; so it's a GREAT idea to keep ducts as large in cross-section as you possibly can. Let your HVAC guy do whatever he needs to do to balance the system, but do NOT let him talk you into small diameter/cross section ducts. Also, don't route ducts (especially cold or warm air supplies to the room) between you and your speakers - dropping cold/hot air between you and a sound source on a sporadic basis is a good way to go insane trying to figure out what happened to your mix and where those weird flanging/doppler thingies came from... Insulation - if your construction is perfectly dry, a complete fill will improve iso by a couple dB over partial fill, and about 10 dB over no insulation at all. With the fluffy stuff, 10-15% compression is a good idea; it helps damping of the drywall panels (a bit like Green Glue, but not as much effect) If you need a fire stop without hard contact between inner and outer frames, using rockwool is usually acceptable to most inspectors - about 6-8% compression of 3 PCF mineral wool should hold it in place and provide an acceptable fire block. No cracks about your Sketchup skills, just saying that theoretically a free-hanging (but sealed) panel would stop more sound than a rigidly fixed (and resonant) one... Steve
