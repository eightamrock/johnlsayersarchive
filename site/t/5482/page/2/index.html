http://www.pac-intl.com/pdf/RSIC-1_EXT0 ... _Guide.pdf These instructions include specs on the RSIC clips - I would stick with this after looking at your piping nightmare. Each clip will support up to 36 pounds; if your joists are on 24" centers, two layers of 5/8 gypsum will work out to 24 pounds per clip. In your case, what with the ducting, etc, I would put THREE layers on the ceiling and two on walls, alternating ceiling, wall, ceiling, wall, ceiling. This would mean that the standard spacing of clips would MAX out the design spec of the clips - NOT GOOD. From looking at your pix, it seems as if your joists are on 16" centers - if so, and if the joists wouldn't be overloaded, I would use the spacing shown for 24" centers (every other joist, NOT every THIRD one) so three layers can be supported. IMPORTANT - Before you go off thinking I'm telling you you can DO this, I'm NOT (just yet, anyway) - first, I need to know exact dimensions of your joists - 2x8, 2x10, etc, spacing between joists, and maximum UN-supported lengths of joists, any place where a joist has been NOTCHED for pipe instead of DRILLED, what your upstairs flooring is - how thick, what material, how many layers, EVERYTHING you can think of that would affect strength. We don't need the death of musicians to achieve silence, it's much less painful to get it with design... :? Exhaust will be fine where it is, but if you MUST use metal make sure it's well-damped with insulation; the three layers I talked about (for the ceiling) are mostly because of your ducting... Steve

knightfly11 February 2006#29
Lookin' good so far; the notches are close to the ends of the joists so won't cause problems. Your worst case loading (plywood and flooring) figuring 2x10's on 16" centers, with just framing and upper floor layers runs around 7 PSF - adding a total of 5 layers of 5/8 gypsum (2 up, 3 down) would add about 12 PSF, about .5 more for insulation - total comes to just under 20 PSF dead load, and 2x10's on 16" centers will span just over 14 feet with 40/20 loading. If you want to stay more conservative, two layers under with GG would probably just about equal the performance; you'd need to ask Brian (studiotips) about that though, GG is HIS baby and I've not yet used it. If you decide to use three layers on the RSIC's, you will want closer spacing than they call for in the PDF; it's aimed at TWO layers, and three would put the clips too close to design max. Springs are only springs when they're not at the end of their travel, and the closer to max you load rubber the shorter the life. OTOH, if you load it too LIGHT, isolation will suffer so stay in the middle ground. Adding 2 layers of gyp under the floor and 3 suspended with standard fiberglass insulation, when properly sealed and overlapped, etc, should get you about STC 63 and around 35 dB TL @ 50 hZ for that ceiling - meaning, you would be at around 90 dB playing drums down there before anyone in the upstairs would know you're there. This is NOT a guarantee, since I'm not you and can't watch every thing that's done during the build; it's only an approximation. Don't let your "sparky" cut ANY notches, you're close enough to max that only HOLES drilled AWAY from the EDGES of joists should be allowed... Steve

knightfly12 February 2006#33
Single layer 3/4 ply, same everything else, 3 layers 5/8 gyp on RSIC - STC 60, TL@ 50 hZ of 32 dB. This is assuming hermetic seal of course. Realistically, the rest of your construction may limit things anyway. And yes, weakest link applies - if you can't beef everywhere, and can't beef at least everywhere the floor is NOT double thickness, don't bother. One thing you could do that would likely get back AT LEAST the 3 dB from no-beef, would be using Green Glue between at least one layer of your lower three. And you're welcome... Steve
