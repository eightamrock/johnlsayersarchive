I've also read the Rod Gervais book and some questions are pertinent to that source. I've located a one story building in brooklyn and am about to lease it starting Sept. Its unique to my neighborhood as it does not connect to any other building and is ground level. It was built probably in the 50's as an iron works shop. Its roughly 900 square ft. It has no plumbing but does have 2 phase electricity coming in with several outlets and lighting. The dimensions are roughly 35'X25'X11' (the current tenant has shit everywhere and I couldn't get the measure straight across anywhere in the room). The walls are cement blocks covered in plaster resting on a single concrete slab. 4 steel beams run the width of the ceiling with wood planks resting on them. The space is completely open except for a 5'X8'X7' stud/drywall tossed up as a storage for the landlord. It is rarely used and has a separate entrance from the south (35') wall about 10' from the west (25') wall. I'm intending to build one "room" at 25'X20' (ignoring the storage) and the control room facing it at 10'X20' (unless this is not a good idea). We record on a 24x18 soundcraft 6000 onto an a800 2" 24 track. Primary live based music focusing on jazz, rock, and pop. The need for a "live" and large tracking room is paramount. We have a steinway upright, B3 w/ leslie, wurly, and rhodes that all have to fit with a kit and potentially up to 10 players. Some questions I still can't seem to find answers for are as follows: 1.) Since I want to create a 2 leaf system and one leaf is the foundation cement block wall already in place, do I want to use the pins specified to hang insulation from the foundation wall? Or can I more simply create my new stud wall [insulation, dywall, greenglue (hopefully!) drywall) 1" off the foundation? 2.) I've worked in, been inside, and seen many studios (RCA B, Goldstar, United Western B) that use perforated tile (gypsum?) on walls and ceilings. If I use perforated gypsum adhered to the layer of drywall with greenglue is this better? I realize two different materials creating one leaf is superior to two of the same materials but do the small holes of perforation create air between the tile and drywall that I'd want to avoid? We used perforated board directly in front of oc 703 for gobos, but I realize this is something much different in function. 3.) There is the existing conduit, lights and outlets which will have to come out before the new walls/ceiling go up. Should I keep the two leg power and safety ground and throw money at a pwr balancing unit or Star ground with the already new outlets. lilith_envy17 August 2009#2
hi, 1) If you have the money to lay insulation against the external wall, then it will help. But if you build far enough from the external wall then, maybe no. 2) Gluing perforated platerbord to regular will do nothing! the purpose of perforated plasterboard is to allow some sound into the glasswool behind it. then behind that, on the other side of the frame, would be another layer of regular thick plasterboard. It's using similar science to a slat res. 3)I doubt you will get clean power out of the existing power runs....??? 4) sand filled will work. Don't think of it as a floating floor, it's just a raised floor for the purpose of cable runs. So the answer is MASS MASS MASS. the last thing you want is to mix on a giant drum head. the other guys will tell you more about 703.
