knightfly14 June 2005#28
All good comments; real, serious studios don't even break ground (or consider a lease, depending) until a site survey has been done; this entails logging the local noise levels for at least a week, preferably a month or more, noting any likely problem areas/times - it also includes a thorough check of any and all "neighbors" - their proximity, any already "in place" isolation, etc - Also, an assessment of the types of music to be generated and their maximum sound pressure levels in all circumstances (recording, monitoring, "dog and pony shows", etc) is done. ("dog and pony show" is my euphemism for "turning it up to 11" to impress some clients...) Once this is accomplished, you have a QUANTIFIED set of conditions - then, and ONLY then, can you even BEGIN to know what type/amount of construction it will require to satisfy ALL the requirements for that studio to be successfully isolated from its neighbors (and vice versa) - and whether the studio can operate "24/7/365", or just til people want to go to bed (or are not home) - I apologise for not getting into this more often (and sooner) - doesn't mean it's any less important. If anyone knows of reasonably priced solutions for doing "site surveys", such as Jade software, etc, http://www.ptolserv.com/jade/jadedata.html this would be as good a place as any to post them - or for that matter, your list of things to check/do if you've done this before... Steve
