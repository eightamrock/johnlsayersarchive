Man am I glad I found this forum. I have been looking at every article I could find regarding soundproofing a garage for drums for about two months now. Didn't find this site until two days ago. And I was just about to order materials from that soundprofing s(h)ite. I first saw this article about a month ago and thought "my prayers have been answered" It desctibes my setup and main problems perfectly and seems to have the answer to everything. After weeks of trying stuff that didn't work and listening to my ill-informed Dad ("carpets and eggcrates, that's all we need!") I emailed those guys for a quote, but I didn't get an email back. My Dad said, "just get as much as you think we'll need" but I decided to ring them first. The phone number on their is not in service. I was still getting "just order the stuff" in the ear when I stumbled on this site, totally by accident, and one of the first threads I read I see someone mention that soundproofing site and get told that they are scammers! So I read on. And on. And on. So far I have read about 40 whole threads and glanced at more (how do you do it Knightfly!!!) and I realise now that those soundproofing guys are not to be trusted. The problem is, I'm still getting the "carpets and eggcrates" in the ear and now I'm getting "acoustical curtains" as well so I need to absolutely know, I need to see, in print, confirmation that these things will provide next to no benefit for what I'm trying to achieve. Which is this. I'm trying to soundproof my garage so I can play drums in there. I live in a semi detached house. My garage is seperated from my neighbours garage by a brick wall with a 1/2" layer of concrete on each side. All the walls are concrete-bricks-concrete. There is a normal width wood door at the back of the garage and an up and over car door at the front. Funnily enough it is not my closest neighbours that are complaining but the ones on the other side of the house. We think the main problem is the up and over door, which is corrugated metal and leaks sound like a sieve. I fully intend, after reading a lot of reference material here and elsewhere, to build a proper room within a room, and float the floors, and use the two leaf system, and only put RC on one side, and leave an air gap, and not to touch the studs with the outer drywall nails and everything. I promise. And so does my Dad. But. For the moment, he's saying we should get some carpets and stick them to the walls two or three thick "and sure it'll only cost €50". I'm killed trying to tell him he'd be better off investing in a roll of neoprene (wherever we can get that in Ireland) and get craicin on the floating floor at least. In the short term, is there anything we could do, just to reduce sound coming out of the garage door? Are acoustical blankets as useless as they sound? Is that MLV stuff useless too? Is the method of "sound isolation?" for the up and over door described on "the website that shall not be named" useless? Would hanging carpets over the door be useless? Would stacking a wall of sandbags in front of the door be useless? Would we be better off starting to buy the materials we need to start building our room within a room, or wait till we have all the money and just put something small up now? Sorry to burden you with these n00bish questions, but if I can't literally show my Dad concrete evidence from someone who knows something about soundproofing, that there is only one way to really make a difference in the noise outside the garage, and that is to construct a room within a room, I might just commit patricide. And who knows, maybe some other nearly-duped amateur like myself will be saved from that evil site if they read this.
gullfo10 July 2007#2
the simpliest thing is to put a drum set in there and give it a work out while using a sound level meter to measure inside and out. also using a rubber and metal hammer to test for "flanking" (sound transmission through the building construction itself) - both in your home and the neighbors. this will give you some idea about how mouch (or little) additional isolation you'll need. a sound level meter (Radio Shack) is probably 50 euros new and will do way more for you than carpets. for another 35 euros or so, you could order Rod's book on home studio construction which has numerous details on sound isolation techniques which are cost effective. meanwhile read more... Sharward's thread discusses the level of effort needed for total isolation of a drum room.
