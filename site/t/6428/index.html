Soundproofing of Ducts / Pipes in Basement

Started by bdurst on 12 June 2006. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6428.

bdurst12 June 2006#1
I've been having trouble finding information about isolating ductwork and water pipes in basements so I was wondering if anyone had any advice. I have a trunkline duct in my basement running from my furnace and hanging below the floor joists directly above the area I am building a studio. This trunkline feeds two children's bedrooms also above the studio and I am trying to find suggestions for reducing the amount of sound that makes it through the heating registers in the rooms above. I also have some water pipes that hang down a couple of inches below the joists. My plan is to hang a double layer gypsum board ceiling suspended by furring strips on isolation clips attached to the floor joists above. I have some MLV I'll put between the joists. I am thinking about building a 2x4 frame around the duct and attaching two layers of drywall to furring strips suspended from isolation clips (same as the rest of the ceiling). Does anyone have a better suggestion? Should I be doing more than this or be doing things differently? Thanks in advance for any help you can give.
