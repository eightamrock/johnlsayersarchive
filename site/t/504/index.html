knightfly30 July 2003#2
Luke, long time no type :) Basic ideas are looking good - Floors: In my situation can I just stick the (2) layers of particle wood (aspanite) on the neoprene pads and forget about the 2x4s? *Sure, as long as the pads are close enough together, but acoustically it would sound better if you had more air, filled with mineral wool batts. Ceilings: Would this be similar to wall construction? Additionally, how do I (and should I) angle the ceilings? They will be 10Ft ceilings. *Yes, same basic idea. One air space, two mass leaves (each leaf can be multiple layers, just no air) You could angle the ceilings, but I'd recommend leaving the REAL ceiling straight for easier sound proof construction, then building a FALSE angled ceiling that's lower at the front for redirecting early reflections to the rear of the room. I'm also wondering if I should leave the inside of the outside walls bare? Insulation and vapor barrier only? Same thing for the ceiling....but then how do you hold the insulation? *It's not really clear from your drawings, but remember the 2-leaf rule - extra leaves or air spaces can make isolation worse, not better. So if the outside of your outer frame is clad, then only the inside (or outside, not both) of you inner frame should be clad. If you use John's inside-out walls for part of the control room, then the panels for those walls would go on the outside of the inner frame. It looks like you have the space to do that and not compromise isolation if you wanted to. On the ceiling, you could do double wallboard on the upper frame (where a normal house would have it) lay heavy insulation above that, then double panel (different thicknesses than the outer ceiling though) the inside of the inner ceiling frame, and lay mineral wool batts in the cavities (the batts would have to go up before the panels) For a false angled ceiling, you could use 2x4's for framing and run them up and to the rear over the mix desk and cover the center area with 1/2" wallboard - put bass hangers of various lengths hanging from the flat ceiling down into this tapered cavity, leave the outermost left and right stud bays open and cover them in rigid fiberglas (703, knauf) - that way, you'd have Reflection Free mix area, a large bass trap entered at the two points of highest sound pressure, and hangers inside to tame them even further. Just remember not to "short out" any resilient mounts for the inner wallboard with the false ceiling frame - avoiding that might get as tricky as just angling the whole inner ceiling. John's done lots more of that than I, you might want to ask him... Steve

knightfly5 August 2003#14
Luke - yeah, your live ceiling WAS probably better the other way if that's where the drums will likely go. I just posted an answer to your wall questions, in which are a lot of reasons/rules of soundproofing - as to your floor/ceilings, we're not there yet. I've been trying to find time to finish the drawings I started on that, and still haven't got there yet. In the meantime, you're getting further afield without my realizing it. Here's what I mean - keep in mind that the OUTER shell is for soundproofing, and that the ONLY function John's vaulted ceiling has is for ACOUSTICS. Not the same. Your drawing shows double wallboard over RC, with bass hangers above, then only one leaf above that in the level part of the ceiling. This will sort of work for sound proofing, but there is no point in putting those hangers in that cavity with that much mass over the frame. It still won't act like a bass trap, because the panels are too rigid to flex enough. What you need, is a double leaf ceiling where it's LEVEL on top, then a light weight plywood cover on light studs for the false ceiling, with absorbent along the wall intersection so the high pressures from bass waves can enter the triangular chamber above the mix area. Once these LF waves get in there, the hangers impede the wave motion, and absorb even more bass. The hard plywood cover over most of this frame will redirect early reflections to the rear of the room, while keeping the sound at the mix position from being sucked dry of highs. Also, the lightweight plywood panels over most of the frame will act somewhat as panel absorbers, but at a different frequency than the chamber, so you get kind of a "two-fer" out of the deal. Or a "three-fer" considering the early reflection bonus. What's taking me so long on real drawings is this: coming up with a way to fasten the frame of the false ceiling without negating the result of resilient mounting of the wall panels in the control room. Acoustically, they should be isolated from each other and yet one has to support the other. I think I'm going to talk to John and see how he's done it in the studios he's done, since he's done more odd-angled construction than I have. Right now, it's after 3 am and I'm gonna crash. Check out my answers on your wall post... Steve
