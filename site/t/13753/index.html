Hi Darren, That looks like a fun project. I agree with your thinking about creating as much open space as possible, makes for better sounding rooms. Is this going to be a commercial facility? How high a priority is having great sounding, isolated rooms? Do a search about other people who have done this style of build. If it was me, and I absolutely had to build on an upper floor, I would be thinking about a commercial style floor system. By this I mean owsj, qdeck, cement. But, I think this is overkill for what you want. You will still need a heavy, overbuilt, floor system just to support all the drywall going on top of it. What I'm getting at here is you're going to have to involve a structural engineer. The first question an engineer will want answered is how much load is actually going on this floor? So you're going to have to figure out your studio design to have an answer. Again, if it was me, I would plan for complete isolation. Room inside room, 2 layers of drywall on walls and ceiling plus remember all your equipment etc. This does not mean you have to build it this heavy, but at least the option is there. The heavier you build your floor, the less it will act as a drum, at least until you reach the resonant freq. Have a read on flanking while your researching. So, overbuild for the heaviest construction you would ever consider, make the floor as heavy and rigid as possible, then test and see what you actually need. If you want good separation between your live room and your control room you will need room within room constr., and multiple layers of drywall, you will also need a heavy, rigid floor. Some things to think about. Steve

I understand and am a compatriot in this regard. Built for love not money. Now on to your questions. It would be a whole lot easier if you don't have to build a free span. Here is a thought, though it may be impractical- Separate your slab from the footings and foundation walls. Build separate footings inside your slab and use columns to suport your second floor, keeping it free standing from your outside walls. Make the floor as heavy as possible to limit flanking between control room and live room (this is going to be a limiting factor in your build). The more columns you use, the heavier you can build your second floor and it's associated load. Yes this does couple your first floor slab to the second floor..... you could also isolate your column footings from the slab to limit this as well. This may be more work than you want to do but it would certainly limit the flanking, which is going to be your biggest isolation factor in this build. Food for thought anyway Steve p.s. do a search for second floor garage builds, I've seen a bunch on here

Darren, If your going to construct a wooden framed two story barn and install the recording studio part upstairs, I do not see how pouring the footer, then the interior of the slab would make any difference. It would just be more work with little return. The biggest issue will be the structure flanking. The path of least resistance. I would especially wonder about that almost circular roof, with round aspects of ceiling known to focus sound. It, if it were me, would have to be broken up with knee walls minimum. Which adds more flanking :) I think you have to ask yourself one clear question. Do I want a barn or do I want a recording studio? And if the recording studio takes precedence, then maybe sliming down of the garage, broaden of the building footprint, there are a lot of ways this can still go, if your not dead set on this barn type structure. If you are...well, there's your answer as well. You'll have to live with what this type of structure gives you in the way of flanking, less isolation due to the shear mass required in an elevated structure, and more work because the build is off the ground which means transportation of materials just got harder. And HVAC... Good luck
