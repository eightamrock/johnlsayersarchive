gullfo16 February 2007#8
if you have the time, it is best to do a thorough investigation. talk to neighbors about the sounds they hear in their places and consider involving them in some testing. Steve (kightfly) had a great idea in using a metal and rubber hammer to tap around places (gently) to identify where you have the flanking noises and it will help you map out how the sounds gets out of your place and into your neighbors, and vice versa. also it may make sense to get a sound level meter and do some checks on ambient noise levels. and to use it with a "drum kit test". Since a drum kit is generally the hardest thing to isolate for most small studios, having a drummer (just need a kick, snare and cymbal) bang it out while you take measurements in and around your place. once you have the map of how sound is going in and getting out of your space, you can then work out what it will take to isolate it.
