Hello all! First time post.....here we go!! My wife and I purchased a new home with a five year old detached garage that the current owner built. He really built it right and its the perfect starting point for building my semi-private recording and production facility, though its a little less space than I was hoping for. We get possession in a week so I'll post what I know now and get pictures and fully accurate info once we're in. I have been doing a lot of reading on this site - Wow, there's a lot to go through! I've read Rod's book once through and am working through it again. I'll be hiring a contractor to do a significant portion of the construction (depending on how the budget stretches). He is a guy I know and trust and he actually has some experience with acoustically-minded construction. Build Budget: $20 000 Max!! (Canadian Currency) So what's a semi-private studio, right? The purpose of the facility will probably be at least threefold. PURPOSE OF STUDIO First, I work a lot with a company that develops music for therapy products. I am the audio engineer on their product development team and provide them with a LOT of post-production work (just me and the DAW cozying up for hours on end). Second, I'm growing into the roll of producer (hopefully) and will use this studio as my home base for pre-production, recording, and all the rest. This could include classical instrumentation, African & Latin percussion, and most certainly the usual suspects for pop/rock/jazz instrumentation. And third, I have a business relationship with a professional voice artist - we will record all of her jobs in this studio and there's the possibility of this growing into a significant portion of the studio's use. Additionally, my business also does video transfers which I'd like to keep doing in this space although they may happen out of an office in the house. CURRENT STRUCTURE The interior dimensions are 22' 11" long x 17' 8" wide x 8' 4" tall. It's got 2x4 stud walls (no drywall) with vinyl paneling on the exterior. The walls for the most part are not filled with insulation yet (he has a work bench at the back of the garage with a plastic sheet sectioning off the back 1/3 of the garage. He insulated just this area and uses an electric heater). I'll be taking out that insulation before starting as I'll be beefing up the outer leaf. The ceiling is open rafters, which start at the 8'4" mark are rise up approx. 3 - 4 feet to a peak that slops to the sides (the width of the building). I'd love to vault the ceiling but am worried about the cost - we'll see. Its all on a concrete slab. I don't know anything about what kind of slab it is but I'll be talking to the previous owner in the next few days so hopefully he'll know something about it. It has its own electrical sub-panel that runs from the house main - 100 amp service, I believe. There are two windows at the back of the garage and a standard sized door. There is a double garage door on the front. I want to leave the exterior appearance unchanged, which means taking out the mechanics of the door and sealing it up. I'll also attempt to beef up this outer layer while still keeping it a single leaf. DESIGN REQUIREMENTS AND IDEAS I've always assumed that I would do a dual-purpose room in a space of this size. Mainly because I will want to record drum kits in this room with as full a sound as is possible. I am a drummer :shot: and will likely have my kit set up in the space when I'm not tracking (I've been practicing in a tiny box of a room - its not inspiring!!) I also really want to have an isolated equipment room for all the noisy stuff, while keeping a line of sight to the rack of gear. I was hoping for two iso booths but in this space I may just settle for one (with the possibility of the equipment room occasionally serving as a guitar amp iso booth. And it does need to be reasonably comfortable for 3-5 people to be in at any given time. As far as isolation: The neighbour nearest the garage is only about 10 or 12 feet away, although their garage is the first portion of their house (an attached garage), so it's not like its their bedroom. That said, my goal is to be able to play the drums at 1 am. Our house is approximately 8 feet from the garage, and it IS our bedroom on that side. Soundman20201 December 2008#15
Well, it's good use of space, and your CR is symmetrical, but it gets narrower behind the mix position, whereas the recommendations that I've seen are for it to get wider. For the same reason, it looks like you will be getting serious "crossed over" reflections from your speakers back towards the mix position, off those rear angled walls (from the glass, particularly). I think your stereo imaging would be pretty bad like that. You also have no space for bass trapping in your rear corners. And your first reflection points are way off to the side, in that kind of "cavity". I'm not sure what effect that would have, but my guess is that it would not be helpful! What about if you flip the two "booths" around so that they are at the front of the room, and extend them all the way to the back, at an angle? That would solve all three problems, although it does mean that you would probably have to walk through one of the booths to get to the LR (unless you make your front window into a sliding glass door!).

I think there was still some room above the couch in the corners for some bass treatment - limited space, for sure, but some. And for a bit of diffusion probably as well.
I hate to tell you this, but here comes another "burst bubble": You do NOT want diffusion in such a small space. I can say this with authority, because I just learned it from some of the "authorities" here last week, following another thread. Basically, diffusion on your back wall is only useful in much larger studios (I forget the exact dimension, but 6 meters from mix position to rear wall rings a bell). If you put it in a small studio (like yours and mine), then it will tend to smear your image at certain frequency ranges. Not good. So you can dump the idea of having a cool quadratic residue diffuser on the back wall. . . . You can search for that thread and find all the details, if you are interested. - Stuart -
