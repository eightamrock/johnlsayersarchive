That size iso booth may have enough physical space for your talent, but with the significant amount of acoustic treatment required for such a small space, I recommend at least 100 ft², with 150 ft², assuming a 10 ft ceiling, a comfortable minimum. You have the space to do it right, which you are already with the other rooms' sizes. Good luck!

Soundman20202 November 2010#7
Please, give me the realistic numbers then.
Good rule of thumb: What does it cost per square foot, in your area, to build a normal up-scale home? That's roughly what you'll be paying per square foot for your studio, as the absolute minimum, and probably quite a bit more. The construction methods for a studio are a bit more complex, and the finishing is very different: acoustic treatment is nothing at all like the normal finishing you'd use in an average home. Isolation is different. HVAC is different. Etc. It all adds up. Your wealthy constructor customer doubtless already knows what those figures are for your area. Since he is footing the bill, you can subtract his profit margin and figure in his discounts for materials in arriving at your "dollars per square foot" figure, so that should make it a bit cheaper per square foot. You say you want a studio of around 2000 sq.ft., so with a budget of 30k that means you are planning to spend just US$ 15 per square foot. That doesn't sound realistic to me. Can you really build a house for US$ 15 per square foot in your area? - Stuart -
