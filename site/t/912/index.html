Paul-Doug wrote:
Anyway, they're the same size and shape as the standard puck but they're made of dense foam rubber. Since a puck is 1 inch think I suppose they could be cut in half. You could check for them at your sporting goods store - and I'll post if I find them up here. Paul
The perceived elasticity can vary based on the load. A "real" hockey puck can be compressed if you put on 2000lbs of pressure. Of course, having them a foot apart under a floating floor eliminates the chance of that weight being on any given puck. However, there are other ways around such things - instead of having a crossover point of 2x4's resting on the puck, you drive into the underside of the 2x4's a hammer-in threaded collar, then screw in a carriage bolt, facing down, at the puck. What we have scientifically done here is convert a wide, even load into a point load (the center of the rounded head of the carriage bolt). This will increase the perceived weight on the puck, right in the very center of the puck, and probably would be good for a floating floor. Just an idea, no warranty expressed or implied. I'd love for someone to try it, and think its wonderful however, because it looks good on paper to me.
frederic13 November 2003#7
BTW, cutting hockey pucks is easy on a bandsaw. You wedge the puck on its edge between two 2x4's, clamp, then feed the puck into the bandsaw. This way your fingers are safe. Just don't nick the clamps though, sparks bad.

Before you waste a lot of time building something that is unlikely to do what you want, please check the links in Building materials here, and look harder for 1/2" 60 durometer neoprene - otherwise, you'll very likely be unhappy with the results. Please re-read this thread http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=839 and look here - http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=984 If you insist on trying hockey pucks, try and set up a test as I outlined in the first link - you could make ONE of the brackets I drew in the second link, and find out what kind of weight it takes to compress your puck by at least 20-25 %, then calculate the entire weight of your room (the part that will be floated, including walls if they are on top of the floated floor) - read the threads, I think most of it is explained there - If you're not willing to spend that much time/effort, you should consider just forgetting floated floors - done right, they are very effective. Done wrong, they can actually HURT isolation and INCREASE PROBLEMS... Steve
