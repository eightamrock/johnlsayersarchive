I would NOT recommend using "inside out" walls between the CR and the live room, as you won't get as good isolation by quite a bit - this method cuts down available air gap between your two masses, which drastically reduces low frequency isolation. As to other "guesses" about acoustic needs, you can get a REALLY rough idea of your room's approximate acoustics by using the reverb calculator here - http://www.saecollege.de/reference_mate ... m#verbcalc The basic goal, for a live room, is to get the RT pretty close to the same value (probably around .3 to .4 seconds in your live room) at ALL frequencies - note the little "button boxes" at the bottom of the calculator, for each frequency - you need to check your wall/ceiling/floor settings at ALL these frequencies, and compare to each other. AFTER the rooms are built, you can actually listen and test to see what more needs to be done - The ETF program (about $150 USD), along with a Behringer ECM-8000 mic for about $50 USD is one of the more complete and reasonably priced ways to do this - your ears, along with some WELL-recorded CD's, is another. I'm glad you've read some good books on the subject, makes it easier to discuss that way - Need to know a rough budget and some material costs (unless this is not a problem) or at least the type materials that are available to you (gypsum wallboard, MDF, concrete block, brick, wood or steel framing, thick glass, etc - If this is to be a pro facility, I will assume you want EXCELLENT isolation between the CR and live room at least - how loud is the surrounding area (minus gunshots/artillery fire, of course :=)) and would you like to keep the natural light from at least some of the windows? Anything else you may want to know - The simplest decent wall construction is a double frame, with two layers of 15mm gypsum on the outside surface of each frame, leaving a distance from inner surface of wallboard on each side of 200-300mm - this gap filled with mineral wool or rigid fiberglass insulation. Glass for windows should be minimum 9 and 12mm, 12 and 15mm would be better. On the 15mm side, an extra layer of 12mm gypsum between the two layers of 15mm will balance out the isolation so that it's at least as good for the glass as it is for the gypsum. have a look at the last wall on the right, bottom of the page here - http://www.domesticsoundproofing.co.uk/tloss.htm For infinite variations including actual TL at all frequencies measured, download this pdf - http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/irc/fulltext/ir761/ir761.pdf Again, for a pro installation I would float ALL floors - both for isolation between rooms and to the outside, and so that there are no differences in floor height between rooms - you want two leaves of mass (with no air gaps between layers of that leaf) between any two locations you need isolation - so your outer shell should NOT have an air gap and two mass leaves, but instead one outer mass with each room providing the OTHER mass of a mass-air-mass system (air usually means insulation, NOT the closed cell foam kind) Ceiling construction - same thing. if your building has a pitched roof, it will probably have an attic that is vented to the outside - keep these vents open, if anything vent it MORE - then, your ceiling should be thickened (make sure it can be supported) and will become your outer leaf - each room's individual inner ceiling can be supported on its walls, which should be resting on their individual floated floor. This gives complete floated room-in-room construction for all rooms. Air lock doors between vox booths, etc, may violate the "2-leaf" rule in order to keep isolation while doors are opened during recording - this can't be helped, but WILL worsen isolation somewhat. Because of this, any walls affected should be increased in mass from the 2-layer, 2-leaf design. Let me know what else you need to know, waiting for the DXF's... Steve

Sorry for the delay, been really busy - "because the wool is 10 cm and the joists 16 cm.. my question is.. should i glue the wool to the gypsum boards of should it be gypsum, then 6 cm of air and then the wool.. ?" - I would put the wool even with the bottom edge of the joists, this will let it work to lower frequencies by giving it an effective depth of 16 cm instead of 10 - but don't expect any isolation from the wool, that only happens if there is wallboard on BOTH sides of the wool (like INSIDE a wall or ceiling) Outside walls - "should i just stuff it up with wool and cover it with a cloth or a slat resonator.?? or should i put one layer of gypsum boards as well??.. i want the wall to be acoustic treatment as well as some small isolation.. " If that's what you want, you need to find a way to do one of John's "inside out" walls - since you're not looking for MAXIMUM isolation, this shouldn't be a problem. What MIGHT be a problem is building the wall on the floor, putting the wallboard on one side, and THEN tipping the wall up into place - these can get heavy, and you might need more help to do it that way. windows - natural light and a view are usually good things, and since you don't need a LOT of isolation, again I would try to keep a window or two. This would mean good seals on the existing ones, probably an air gap between old window and new (part of the new wall) - 250 to 300mm is a good air gap for sound windows, and the glass should be about 1/3 as thick as your wallboard total. This will keep the glass from being much of a weak spot. I would NOT put rubber under your wall frames - it's too easy to end up with a bad resonance in the wall, ESPECIALLY since we don't have ANY specifications on the rubber you're using. You would be much better off just framing those walls normally, and making some sway braces for the tops of the frames - if you will have an inner, false ceiling resting on the tops of the walls, then you may not even need sway braces at all. A better way would be to put your rubber closer together around the perimeter of the floated floor, and build your inner walls on top of the floated floor. I hope that helps... Steve

On the Armaflex - until I have proof of life and specs, I won't recommend it. If you're happy with what you've heard from someone else, and YOU feel comfortable using it, that's up to you. The best I can say is that I would NOT use it without knowing more about it - you're gambling on setting up a resonance that can make your walls WORSE for isolation instead of better. If you do use it, I would stay with the thinner 1/2" stuff and make sure, if you use the fastening method you posted pix on, that you do NOT tighten the lag screws too tight - if you completely compress those rubber top washers, then you've just shorted out your resilient mount and wasted your time. The sleeves in the pic are necessary to keep the bolts from contacting the sides of the holes in the frame plates. Another thing to watch - after the frames are in place, and BEFORE the wallboard goes on, try this; give the frame a firm thump, either with the heel of your hand or (better) a rubber hammer - if it rings at a particular frequency (it will) try alternately tightening and loosening every other one of the lag bolts through the rubber, so each has a different tension on its rubber washer - this may broaden the resonance a bit which would improve the wall performance slightly, at least in terms of ringing. Looks like you have a nice area above your false ceiling for some heavy duty absorption/bass trapping. \ Mainly I would suggest re-reading through some of the REFERENCE section just to make sure you're following the general rules... For good isolation between these rooms, ALL of the inner wall/ceiling/floor surfaces need to be physically de-coupled from the outer shell - I'm not seeing that, and I'm wondering how you plan to get good isolation if this is true. You are showing your false ceilings for BOTH rooms resting on the same beams, and this will cause FLANKING between rooms. I am posting a very basic concept drawing or two, hopefully these will help you understand the principles of room-to-room isolation better - In the unmarked drawing, gray is mass, yellow is air/insulation, and brown is other - note that for a sound to escape either room, whether traveling to the OTHER room or to the outside, it must pass through TWO leaves of mass - The roof of almost all construction doesn't count when figuring out your mass-air-mass layout; it WILL affect isolation, and not in a good way; however, you can NOT do away with the ventilation requirements for outer roof because you will get rot and mold; so you need to compensate for this third leaf by making the other two real leaves heavier, and possibly adding MORE vents to the roof to lessen the 3rd leaf effect. Inside your two leaf contaiment area, you can add non-sealed false walls or ceilings for acoustic purposes (better sound) - these will also have some negative effect on isolation which can't be helped - best you can do is heavier outer walls to compensate. These inner, false walls/ceilings can attach to the inner containment leaf (the inner gray part in the drawing) - but should NOT compromise the separation between each room's INNER envelopes - each room should have NO contact with its neighboring room that isn't SOFT, like a sway brace. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... &start=165 Scroll down a ways for a diagram - and a pic of one installed - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... &start=210 It's the 15th pic down from the top.
