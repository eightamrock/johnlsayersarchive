knightfly8 March 2005#78
Sorry for the delay Petar, I'm off to work in about 5 minutes; just quickly, good caulk should NEVER completely harden, it needs to stay flexible so it won't crack. Butyl, silicone, not sure about polyurethane. It should feel kind of like a thick rubber band when it's "dry", but should NOT be brittle, EVER. Floor - 5/8 particle under 3/4 chipboard will be OK, just make sure you alternate the layout for minimal gaps, and seal everything as you go. Rockwool - if it's transmitting, it's too compressed. Either make your framing deeper or use less rockwool; then maybe the strips of softer insulation as I mentioned before; all you want is SLIGHT pressure on the panels, so they don't RING. Outa time for now, please organize your questions in ONE new post, one paragraph per question, and I'll try to catch you up in the morning when I get home... Steve

Lookin' good. :D You'll be much happier you put the extra supports (stringers) between the joists. Aaronw10 March 2005#86
This is a quote from Steve from my thread:
Allow for a few extra pucks under areas where heavy things will be - if you're using a serious console, put a few extras under where the legs will sit - same in areas where a piano will be. Allow for twice as many pucks around the perimeter and under any other walls. Don't add extra pucks, steal them from less rigorous areas and re-adjust the spacing.

knightfly17 November 2005#95
In a double wall construction.. if my rockwool touches.. (now only thing connecting the walls) will it decrease the isolation? It shouldn't, unless the rockwool is compressed quite a bit. IS this by chance the wall you had your inner rafters bearing on top of? This cavity between floated floor should be filled with woll or left empty? It should be filled. am i still ok if i use 2 layers of 16 mm screwed to the metal studs? Yes; recent wall tests show that two heavier layers work better for lower frequencies, which are the hardest to stop. Cloth; you can use light weight plastic (like the thin stuff used for painter's drop cloths) to cover the wool first, then whatever cloth you like the look of; test it with fire, and if it sustains flame you'll need to find something like this http://www.natfire.com/ or take a chance on dying by fire. The thin plastic will absorb a bit less high frequency so keeping the room a little brighter sounding, but not much; the main use is to contain the fibers from the insulation... Steve
