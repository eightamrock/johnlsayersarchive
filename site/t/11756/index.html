Soundman20208 March 2009#6
Hi Barry!
with a starting point of placing the monitors about 2.7 m apart.
That might be too far apart, depending on what speakers you are using: The normal recommendation is to have them about 1 to 2 m apart, otherwise it implies that you are sitting too far away from them to be in the near field, especially in a small room.
38% from the front wall, this time it's nearer to the back wall...I'm not sure if that's a good or bad thing...Does the 38% rule apply to designs like these, or is it more for rectangular rooms?
The 38% rule is due that being the "least bad" position between any two parallel surfaces. Your front and back wall are parallel, as they should be, so yes the "rule" applies. By "least bad" I mean that it is the position where there are the least detrimental effects from standing waves However, it is not really a carve-in-stone rule: It's just a starting point. After you actually build the room and start fine-tuning, you'll probably find that you will end up at a slightly different position, probably a bit further forward than 38% (closer to the speakers), so don't worry too much if you can't make the geometry work for you. BUT! It looks to me like your current position is very close to 50% of the room, and that is, indeed, the worst possible position you can have. At 50% of the room there are serious standing wave dips and valleys for all frequencies. You do not want to be there. In any event, the intersection of the speaker lines should be a couple of inches behind your head at the mix position, not right in the center of your head. You want the lines form the speakers to intersect your ears, not your nose: It's hard to listen with your nose! So the 38% position is where your ears are, but the lines intersect a bit behind that.
So we have a low ceiling, 2.1 m. with 16 cm of absorbtion and a concrete floor.
Concrete floor is good! Leave it like that, or put laminate floor on it. Do not put carpet on it. 16cm of absorption on the ceiling is also good! You mean something like fiberglass or rockwool, I guess? But 2.1 m height????? WOW! That's a low ceiling, for sure. That's only just a bit higher than a standard door frame. That is not so good. but I guess you already know that.
The dotted line across the sofa is where the ceiling comes down to the top line at a 45 degr angle
. The ceiling should nto come DOWN at the rear of the room! It should go UP. IT should slope up away from the speakers at an angle of 12 degrees or so, if possible. If not, then just build it flat. What do you mean by "comes down to the top line"? I downloaded your sketchup file, but it is just a flat floorplan! It would help if you actually make it into a full 3D model. - Stuart -

Soundman202010 March 2009#8
I intend to soffit mount main monitors and have my nearfield ns-10's on the console
You often see studios with speakers sitting on the console, but it is actually not a good place to put them! You'll get comb filtering from the console surface, and it's hard to decouple the speakers from the console. Far better to put them on solid, massive stands just behind the console. In any event, your listening position is still practically right at the 50% position, which is the worst possible spot in the room. If you move your mains closer together, then you can bring it further forward, away form teh 50% spot and closer to the 38% spot. The other thing you could do is to increase the intersection angle of the speakers from 60 to as much as 90 degrees. That will also bring your listening position further forward.
I know, but I've read that people made it work, and I hope I can too!
Well, as the folks here often say "You have to live with what you've got". Just make the best you can of it, while recognizing the limitations that it will impose.
