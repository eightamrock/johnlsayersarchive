I had a couple of questions regarding the use of the Green Glue product which I posed to them directly. Their responses have been impressively prompt and informative, and I thought I would post them here for the benefit of others. Below is a somewhat abbreviated email exchange with them: I'm not clear on one point. When used on a sheetrock wall, in between two sheets, are you saying that you screw the two sheets together after application? If so, why doesn't that negate the effects of the Green Glue, as you have now made the sheets "as one"? Thanks in advance for your response. That's a really good question. Green Glue doesn't function by mechanically seperating the layers of drywall, it functions by converting mechanical vibration into heat (its a viscoelastic material). When sound passes "through" a wall, the mechanism is sound vibrating one side of the wall, this vibration transmitting as sound through the air cavity in the wall or mechanically through the studs, and then vibrating the other side, which radiates new sound into the next room. With Green Glue in the assembly, mechanical vibration is constantly being converted into very small amounts of heat, resulting in less sound transmission. At resonance points, where walls tend to bleed the most sound, GG is effective in the same manner. The stored resonant energy is dissipated as small amounts of heat. OK, and I'm with you on the effect of viscoelastics dissipating the energy as heat. But where I connect the two layers of drywall via a screw, it would seem that gives a direct path from one board to the other for the vibration (through the screw). Is it that this represents a negligible amount of energy coupling, and a necessary compromise in order to physically mount the board? Or maybe I am missing the bigger picture. Is it that the combination of board, Green Glue, and another board become a complete damped system acting as one but with much less vibrational characteristics than the same assemby without GG? Side question: One thing I don't remember seeing was how many screws are typically required to fasten two 4 by 8 sheets together. Thanks for your assistance in this. I just want to make sure I understand before investing what appears to be a considerable amount in my studio. I can't argue with the accolades, both on your site and elsewhere. But I just wanted to get straight in my head exactly how to do it, and why and how it works. With a material like GG, or a rigid adhesive, or even no adhesive at all, the two drywall layers aren't decoupled, but rather coupled (by physical contact with no glue, or adhesion with the adhesives). You said:Is it that the combination of board, Green Glue, and another board become a complete damped system acting as one but with much less vibrational characteristics than the same assemby without GG? And that is exactly it. Its not decoupling at all, but rather just a less resonant / less conductive (for vibration) assembly. Generally normal building code calls for screws to be 12" on-center for 24" stud spacing and 16" on-center for 16" stud spacing, and thats what we test at. The first layer needs jsut minimal screws to hold up the board (perhaps 24" on-center). Its never a bad idea to ask the local building inspector what local code is.

Eric_Desart28 November 2007#3
studio_drums I applied it on a floor, without any mechanical connection (wasn't possible there). And I discussed it personally on the phone with Brian (the designer of GG, also responsible for these numerous tests and measurements). He honestly told he couldn't guarantee anything, and was interested in the outcome of that experiment (which it was). I also made sample test pieces (which I still have on my desk) to see how GG behaves over time. I could disconnect layers manually (on small pieces) even after many months if I wanted, but not easy. I can still manually offset these small pieces of boards somewhat. If I should wrickle with a kitchen knife they come apart again. :finger: And it's still as sticky as it was after a couple of days, a week and a month even the parts where GG is exposed to the air in standard ambient in-house conditions. These screws/nails wont matter that much since we're not speaking about decoupling but suppressing the vibration. The contact between the panels and the wooden studs themselves is much more defining than these point contacts of screws. The main limiting factor of such a wall remains the location of the MSM (:) GG can suppress resonance/vibration, but not alter physics). Now the necessity of screwing the panels (beyond safety reasons) is very simple: You can't press these boards together during curing time (which is very long) and spreading out this GG stripes as coming out of the tube on the board (they become devided over the surface by this pressure resulting from screwing the boards together). Hence this screwing helps the application. It should be a nightmare without. In order to do my floor. I had to make something to apply pressure between ceiling and floor, just to make up for this lacking mechanical fixing. Made the whole thing much more complicated. Lucky that was a relative small floor.
