scottmichal17 July 2012#3
Hey Ryan, Congrats on the new house!! (and esp. the new studio space!)
a 12'x12' (yep, dead square but it’s all I’m going to get!) room
The square room will create problems :( I am assuming that actually moving one of the walls is probably not practical. I think the best solution might be to build a stud wall out of 2x2s covered with fabric along the right side (looking at your floor plan) of your room about 2' in. Then fill the empty space with hangers made out of homasote. (you can find lots of information on building hangers by searching in this forum) Some of the more experienced readers might have a better suggestion :yahoo: Basically, anything you can do to shorten the length that sound waves get to travel for one of the dimensions of the room is going to help. You might even try moving large items of furniture along one wall. not ideal, but it won't hurt to experiment.
1) Which wall should I place my desk/monitors on? I understand that I'll want my listening position about 33% into the room, correct?
If the room is square it won't matter which wall you are facing. If you can make the room a rectangle, you will want to have the speakers aimed down the longer dimension. 32-38% is a good place to start with the location of the listening position, but you will want to experiment with making small adjustments till you find the best sounding spot! A cheap measurement mic like the Behringer ECM8000 and measurement software (I like Room EQ Wizard, and it's free) can help you find the perfect spot!
2) I'm deciding between carpet and vinyl w/ an area rug for the flooring. Which of these would yield the best results, acoustically speaking, for a room that is primarily used for mixing (and by 'primarily', I mean that I may track vocals in there from time to time)?
With the limited budget you have, I would leave the floor alone for now, and put your money into to acoustic treatments. Concrete is a decent floor surface for a studio.
3) How many broadband absorbers do I need and where should they be placed? I'm thinking I can place 3 of them in the 3 open corners, 1 floating above my listening position, and several along the back and side walls.
Sounds pretty good. One cheap trick I found for building clouds and absorbers is to call some of the HVAC contractors in your area and see if they have scraps or miscuts of duct board that they will give you (or sell cheap) I have built some great clouds and bass traps for almost nothing this way! Have fun, and I look forward to seeing some pictures of your new studio!

Hi Ryan. Welcome! :) Agreeing with and adding some random comments to what the others have said: YOUR FIRST POST:
The room size is 12'x12'x7' (well, the dropped ceiling is 7' - in the closet, which does not have a dropped ceiling, it's 8'

Great! So your floor is done already! :)
My budget for the flooring and treatments is about $1000.
Skip the flooring: concrete is fine, and any other type of acoustically decent flooring would eat such a large chunk out of your budget as to leave you with basically nothing for treatment.
1) Which wall should I place my desk/monitors on? I understand that I'll want my listening position about 33% into the room, correct?
38%, not 33%, but that's just a guideline. In a square room, it doesn't really matter too much which way you orient it. But I think I would use the "outside wall concrete block" as the front wall, as it gives you the best options for treating the room. However, if that would cause symmetry issues with the strange ceiling arrangement that you mentioned, then I'd switch to another orientation, to get good symmetry.
2) I'm deciding between carpet and vinyl w/ an area rug for the flooring. Which of these would yield the best results, acoustically speaking, for a room that is primarily used for mixing (and by 'primarily', I mean that I may track vocals in there from time to time)?
If you really are set on doing flooring, then vinyl would be the choice. Carpet is bad for so many reasons. But there's no need to do anything to that floor: if it is in decent condition, the plain old bare concrete is a great floor, acoustically, provided that you treat the ceiling suitably.
3) How many broadband absorbers do I need and where should they be placed? I'm thinking I can place 3 of them in the 3 open corners, 1 floating above my listening position, and several along the back and side walls.
I would start with bass traps before worrying about broadband absorbers. Large superchunks in as many corners as you can. With a square room that also has a mathematically related height, you are gong to need stacks of bass trapping. So make them LARGE! :) Then if you still have money left over, do your first reflection points next, then the back wall, then the front wall (between the speakers and the wall). SECOND POST:
I could go with just painted cement, but I'm not sure I'll like the looks of it.
Or if you can increase your budget, then think about laminate floor: that can look really nice, and is also excellent acoustically. THIRD POST:
