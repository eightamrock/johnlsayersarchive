Skip-trowel texturing as a diffusion surface

Started by mmcfarlane on 18 August 2006. 5 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6848.

mmcfarlane18 August 2006#1
I was wondering if anyone has tried using a skip-trowel texture on wallboard (gypsum/blueboard) inside their control room. The skip trowel technique leaves the wall's surface irregular. Basically you put mud (joint compound) on a trowel and lightly drag the trowel accross the wall at about a 40 degree angle leaving small (or large) globs of joint compound on the wall. You finish this off by leveling off the high spots with a clean trowel. The result is an irregular texture that can be typically from 1/8" to 1/4" in relief. I'm just finishing up a new combined control/tracking room (which has a LOT of glass in it: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=48849#48849) and I'm thinking about texturing all the remaining wall and ceiling surfaces. Some absorbers will obviously be placed over the textured walls,... What do you think?
len-morgan18 August 2006#2
If I'm not mistaken, you need to have the irregular surface related to size of the waves you are trying to diffuse. In other words, what you're talking about might do something for very high frequencies, it wouldn't do much for the lows and mids and the whole idea of diffusion is to even up the sound across the spectrum of frequencies. len
