clunk23 May 2008#4
Hi Acle, you have a fair few problems here, the first of these is that gap between your garage and your neighbours. Effectively it means you have no isolation between you and the other garages, this may not be a major problems if they are rarely used but if your neighbours use their garages for diy for example you will have to deal with lots of noise from here. Insulation alone will not be enough to maximise your isolation from these garages, you need to find a way to seal your garage off from the rest using some form of solid barrier which can be sealed all round. Next you need to build rooms inside your garage, it may be worth looking at erecting inside out walls given the restricted space you have, these are stud walls with gypboard on the outer face of each wall, allowing the inner frame to house acoustic treatments and are a space saving method. having built these walls you could also use these as the support for an independant ceiling effectively creating 2 sealed boxes within the garage space. Read the post about floating floors before you go this route, it seems that these can cause problems if not constructed right and in many cases you may be better not building such a floor. 2 options seem to be worth considering, one is a concrete floating floor and the second a sand filled wooden deck. If you do decide to build a floated floor then you should build the walls of that room on this floor Have you checked the reference section out yet, it has lots of information that will be useful for you in your planning, you should spend sometime and read through this before you jump into your build. Get as much information as you can about the current structure of the garage , for example are the exterior walls single block or cavity walls, this makes a diffence to what you can do, what is the roof finish and is it possible to add mass to this. Good luck, Clunk

clunk23 May 2008#6
Acle your drawing seems to be suggesting that you intend to build a double frame wall which is not necessary in your case( except between booth and mix room), you should be looking at building a single frame wall for each room with gypboard either on the inner or outer face of this only, the existing walls of the garage act as a single leaf and you only want to create a second leaf as this helps gain the best isolation. As I said above you need to create boxes within you garage with an airspace between the boxes and existing walls, building a double wall assembly loses you isolation as well as space. Read the section about walls for further information and you will see what I mean. All the best Clunk

clunk4 June 2008#16
Hi Acle, sheetrock is Gypsium board and when Avare indicated that you don't need to add anything to the existing walls he meant no further sheetrock( I think). Adding insulation will help but it's probably best if it touches the gyp-board on the studwork. What you need to achieve best isolation is a Mass/Spring/Mass solution. The garage wall acts as one leaf and is the first mass, the airspace with insulation acts as the spring and the gyp-board as the second leaf of mass. According to the advice I have found here this achieves the best isolation for your money. BTW I am not an expert but just trying to share some of the information I have found here while designing my own build, I know how it can get a little confusing but I think you will do fine, just be careful to pay attention to the detail. When your basic room is finished you then need to treat the room for reflections etc and advice on room treatments can be found mainly in the acoustics section of the forum. Hope this helps, all the best Clunk
