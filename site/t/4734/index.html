sharward23 December 2005#5
Yikes -- 2nd floor (or, for our European friends, first floor), trains nearby, metal bands with huge drum kits, and commercial tenant below you? Ben, I'm sorry to be a downer... But I agree wholeheartedly with Chris. You are facing a huge isolation challenge. Budgetwise and safetywise, you can't afford to guess and you can't afford to miss. Please review my "Mason Industries' FSN Floating Floor Concrete Slab Test Data" thread. Learn as much as you can about floating a concrete slab with a jack-up system like those described in the thread. I'm sorry to say that most of the Mason Industries links are no good because they must not take their Web site very seriously... :x ...but you can still learn quite a bit from the thread. Mind you, my "aha" out of the whole thing was that I personally chose not to float a floor -- but in your case, given the constraints you described, particularly the fact that you have people below you who need to not be disturbed (right?), you need to seriously look at this option -- and it's not easy and not cheap! :roll: Of course the first thing you'll need to do is research and post back with the details of your floor construction, and then certainly get a structural engineer to tell you how much weight your floor will safely hold. The only thing worse than disturbed downstairs tenants is flattened and deceased downstairs tenants! :!: cyeazel just got his engineering report for his 2nd floor commercial space project -- go to page 9 of his project thread -- and then read the whole enchilada to appreciate the overall context. Is this commercial space of yours already in your possession? Or do you still have an out? :roll:
