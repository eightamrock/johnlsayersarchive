Eric_Desart11 April 2007#3
Jeroen, If you want something light and moveable, and isn't to sensitive to damage, in this case you could go for flat foam. Search the yellow pages or the net for Merford (Netherlands company). They also have a shop for this kind of smaller customers. But only go for the flat thick foam. They can inform you about this stuff (in base that's a huge industrial acoustic company with high knowledge, but having made something to make stuff also accessible for smaller not industrial customers) It's one of the only companies I know that publically states that if linear or good absorption is a priority you should go for flat foam, and if eastetics or high frequencies are defining you should go for sculpted foam. You can go for the boards you referred as well and cover them with whatever breathable cloth. The choise between these series I should let it define by stability and weight of the board (not the lightest type). They all suit your purpose. The 223 should be a good choise (others too). You'll notice the higher the number the more expensive (increasing density). And if you want nicer tiles, I don't know where you can find them in small quantities, but there's Rockfon and Ecophon. If you call them maybe they can point you to distributors able to deliver small quantities. Possible Merford has even left-overs from own projects.

Eric_Desart12 April 2007#9
Jeroen, An important note for people not able to read Dutch and/or not familiar with the name "Kundt" and that Standard number underneath that Graph in that text. And also for you: This are impedance tube measurements. Hence the y-axis is really absorption in % or real energetic absorption coefficient unlike Sabine absorption coefficient values. Also this is measured exclusively at straight incidence unlike the normal Sabine method in reverberation rooms where random incidence is measured. Hence this graph can not directly be compared with the graphs most people are used to see. Normally these numbers should become better when measured in a reverberation room resulting in the traditional Sabine absorption coefficient numbers. There is no simple formula to calculate from one to the other, but in the low frequencies differences are possible upto 40% (as per some authors). This Kundt name: Kundt is the inventor of this tube.
