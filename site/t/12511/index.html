I'm not sure I understand the purpose of doing that at all: If you build it with 2x6's and fill the gaps with sand, then it won't be floating at all. It will just be a bit more mas on top of a lot of mass, directly coupled to it, so it won't float and won't help you with isolation at all. If you just want to run some cable ducts, then why go to all the trouble of floating a floor that doesn't float? Why not just run the cable ducts in the air gap between your two-leaf (three-leaf? dour-leaf?) walls? Much easier, much cheaper.

Elevating the floor is a bad idea. It'll introduce resonances which are higher in frequency than your already concrete floor has. If it's "just" for cable management I'd look into a better solution. Elevating the floor will also take up, precious, space. To name a few...

You could install conduit in the walls / ceiling. Use oversize conduit, and install more than you need: If you figure you need 4 parallel pieces of 20 mm conduit, then install 6 pieces of 32 mm.... That way it's easy to install new cables in the future. - Stuart -
