Soundman20205 March 2014#8
1-the study is located in an area where I have no noise issues with neighbours
so your neighbors don't make any noises that might be a problem if they get into your recordings? And you also have no problems with thunder, rain, hail, wind, aircraft flying over, cars leaving/arriving, traffic on nearby roads, people talking elsewhere in the building, phones ringing, radios, TV, water running, people walking, opening and closing doors, vacuum cleaner, etc, etc. ? All of those, and hundreds of others, are typical sounds that can cause problems in a recording studio. That's why studios are normally isolated well: so the mics don't pic up all those thousands of sounds that can destroy a good recording, or upset the musicians, or break the concentration of the mix engineer.
2-the study will be used to professional recordings, mainly for rock, soul music, jazz
Soul and jazz, particularly, are types of music where you are recording instruments are fairly quiet levels, and wanting to capture a lot of ambient room sound, so isolation is very important for those.
3-the size of the control room H 2.67 m 3.97 m L W 6.75 m 27 m 2 area
That's a good ratio, and a reasonable size. The ratio is 1 : 1.48 : 2.53, which is close to Volkmann's and Bolt's ratio of 1 : 1.5 : 2.5.
4-the existing exterior walls are concrete block's 25 cm
That's very good. It should be easy to get good isolation. What is the floor made of? Also concrete?
making a calculation of resonant modes of control room came out different resonant frequencies, especially those very strong low
Use Bob Gold's calculator for that: It's a lot easier to understand: http://www.bobgolds.com/Mode/RoomModes.htm
You can with hanger bass trap can be solved ??
Yes, you can damp those modes with good bass trapping. It is impossible to make the modes go away, but you can damp them well with large bass traps (including hangers), and the results will be good. - Stuart -
