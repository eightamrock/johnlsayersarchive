Soundman202028 December 2008#13
my chair has me my ears sitting about 45% of the way from floor to ceiling. It seems like a "good" height for a chair, but should it be higher or lower?
In addition to what Mika said (which is totally correct: you need more bass trapping in the other in the other 10 corners) it looks to me like you have a problem with your speaker location and your mix position location. As a test, try placing your speakers absolutely up tight against the front wall, with just a tiny little gap of a quarter inch or so, such that they are not actually touching the wall, and at the exact height of YOUR ears when you are normally seated in the normal chair that you use. By "height" I mean the acoustic center of the speakers. If the manual for your speakers does not tell you where that is, IIRC you can guess it as being roughly in between the woofer and the tweeter. You want that part of the speaker at the height of your ears when you are seated. You want the speakers and your head roughly at the corners of an equilateral triangle. ie, the distance between the speakers should be the same as the distance from each speaker to your head, and the angle made by imaginary lines that poke out of the speakers perpendicular to the front faces should be 60 degrees where they intersect at your head position. In this scenario, your head should be at about 38% of the depth of the room, from front to back. Try the speakers in that position, and see how it sounds. If they are up against the wall then they will not be in any nulls that affect the bass range, but where you have them now (according to the photo) looks like they might well be in an area of bass nulls. Also, regarding your "45% of room height" issue: That may nor may not be a problem. After trying the above setup, keep the speakers in the same place against the wall, but now raise them up above your ear height until they are, for example, 76% of the height of the room. (Note: 76% is not a magic number. It's just a different position that might be good and should be better than 45%.) Now, the speakers will obviously be above your ear height, so you will have to tilt them down a bit to aim at your ears. You can tilt as much as 20 degrees, according to some speaker experts, but hopefully, you won't need to tilt that much. If you can keep it to about 10 degrees of tilt, that would be better. More tilt is bad, for several reasons. Try it in that position, and see how it sounds now. I'm, not saying that this should be the final position for your speakers: I'm merely giving you some ideas to try that might improve your speaker positioning, and thus also improve your bass. But you do also need to do what Ethan and Mika said: Install lots more bass trapping in at least some of the other ten corners of your room. Install it symmetrically, of course, and deep. What are the walls, floor and ceiling made of? - Stuart -

A cloud is simply absorber panels mounted on the ceiling. The general goal is to cover a fairly large area extending from above your head to above the loudspeakers, and at least as wide as the speakers are apart from each other. --Ethan
