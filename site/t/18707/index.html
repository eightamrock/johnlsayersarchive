Hi Carl. Welcome! And Merry Christmas, too! Please read the forum rules for posting (click here). You seem to be missing a couple of things! :) In answer to your question, it's impossible to say. Seals are critical to door isolation, yes, and without good seals you won't get isolation: that's guaranteed. However, even the best seals in the world won't help if you don't already have the rest of the isolation story in place. Insufficient mass, for example, will leave you just as poorly isolated as having no seals. The typical doors found in most homes, for example, often just consist of a layer of very thin plywood (pr other similar wood) on each side of an empty air space, with a light frame around the edge. That's practically worthless as a studio door, especially where drums are involved, and even a pair of those would be useless. On the other hand, a pair of massive, thick, heavy solid-core doors, mounted on suitable heavy-duty hinges, and with good quality automatic closers and good seals, will go a very long way to keeping drum sounds out of the rest of the house. However, even then that's only part of the solution. Even with a pair of fantastically massive doors, if you don't also have the proper two-leaf isolation structure around the rest of the room, then you'd be wasting your time and money. For example, if you only have typical 2x4 framed walls with 1/2" drywall on each side, then there's no point at all in going for massive doors and expensive seals. Ditto if your HVAC silencers are not up to much, or your electrical system was not installed and sealed correctly for high isolation, or any number of other possibilities. Since you didn't tell us anything at all about that, we can't tell you what you need to complete the doors correctly. But to summarize: Doors are just an extension of the walls they are part of, so your inner door has to be consistent with your inner-leaf, and your outer-leaf door has to be consistent with your outer leaf. As a typical example: If your inner-leaf is 2 layers of 5/8" fire-rated drywall with Green Glue in between, then your inner-leaf door needs to have a surface density of at least 30kg/m2 if you want it to be effective. If the leaf has 3 layers of drywall (instead of just 2), then the door needs to be more like 45kg/m2 surface density. Likewise, If the MSM cavity in your wall is 8" deep, then the air gap between the two doors needs to be at least 12" deep. Etc. The goal is to maintain the same consistency as for the rest of the wall, and even to improve on it, since doors are always the weak point in any studio. So if you can provide more information about how your studio was built, details of the walls, floor, and ceiling construction, how you did your HVAC system, how you did your electrical system, your isolation goals (dBC, preferably), and also photos of everything, then we can give you a better answer. - Stuart -
