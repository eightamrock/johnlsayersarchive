barefoot27 April 2004#2
Hi sui-city, Glad to see you made it over here!:) Browse around the site for some control room layouts and have a look here. The same basic principles for shape apply to a mastering studio. Having 802s, your biggest area of concern will be front and side wall reflections, since these speakers have such wide dispersion. Have a look at my Wall Bounce Calculator. You're going to want a fairly large room so you can pull the speakers away from the walls. High ceilings are also very helpful. You'll also want a little more absorption and a lot more diffusion in the front than a typical recording control room. A nice sized room would be something like 24'x16' with 10' ceilings. Don't know how much a space like this goes for in your area, but I think it's a good reference point. Thomas

lowdbrent4 March 2005#6
I disagree. The mastering studio and the control room of a production studio are completely different. The concept is different. Yes, one still has to have proper ratios, etc. But the desired result is completely different. I would recommend reading some interviews with Fran Manzella, at www.fmdesign.com. He addresses this. Also, I would take a look at the best mastering houses yourself to see how they are different. If you cannot, atleast go to their sites. If it were the same, projects would be handled in-house at the studio. Have you decided on your monitors? The monitors may dictate room volume, ratios, etc. If this is going to be a real room, with real monitors and amplification, I would seriously consider the Legacy Audio Focus at minimum. They are about $3,300 EA, plus amplifiers (which I would recommend PASS or comparable. This type of system is proven in mastering rooms around the world. Then there are B&Ws, PMC's, etc, etc. For all of these systems, be ready for a room length of about 34ft or so.
