Soundman202019 July 2013#2
It would help to have a diagram or model of your room, or photos, to better see what you are dealing with. For 5.1, there's an alternative that you might not have considered: use two video monitors, instead of just one, spread them apart a bit so that they are not blocking your center channel. That's what I try to do for 5.1, to avoid the very issues you are talking about.
The speakers I have should not be placed horizontally.
Good! Because speaker placed horizontally can create other problems, unless they are specifically designed to ONLY be used horizontally. Vertical is generally better. What speakers are they?
Is it okay to raise the speakers and tilt them down?
Yes, but with caveats! You can go to only about 10° downwards tilt. After that, you start creating psycho-acoustic issues with the way your ear and brain deduce direction of sound sources, so you upset the sound stage and stereo imaging. Some people say that 7° is the limit. Then there's also the issue of reflections: If you raise speakers and tilt them down, you are creating the perfect conditions for reflections coming off your console or desk to be aimed right back at your face. Bad. Then there's the issue of comb filtering and other phasing artifacts: Tilting the speaker down will create more prominent comb filtering form the desk(console surface. Not good. And there are other practical issues, such as if you have someone sitting on a sofa behind you: the speakers will be pointing at their feet, not their ears, so they will be way off axis, and therefore not getting clear, clean sound. The will tend to hear the mix more muffled and bassy than it really is.
Is it okay to have the center speaker be higher than the others?
Once again, you can, but it is not recommended. Take a loot at the ITU and EBU specs for 5.1 control rooms, to get a better idea of the geometry you need for your speaker layout, and how the room should be done. - Stuart -
