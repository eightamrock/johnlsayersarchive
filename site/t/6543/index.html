x-t I think you misunderstood some things in your research. I assume you talk about fibrous spray insulation (exists in mineral wool and synthetic fibre). This can never in itself increase insulation that much (it's the same as putting rockwool or fibreglass boards beneath your ceiling). There is really no way this can substitute drywalling. Check the conditions in which this 50 to 65 dB TL is obtained you found via your research. Absorption and insulation are entirely different things. Absorption can be used inside a cavity to enhance insulation, but is no insulation in itself.

rod gervais1 July 2006#16
The terms are synonymous - however - in the US - we always refer to insulation as the act of creating a thermal barrier - and isolation as the act of separating one space from another space acoustically. In this manner it's always clear exactly what we are referring to. Just as a point of intrest- The ANSI manual of Acoustic Terminology (ANSI S1.1-1994) defines isolation - but not insulation - as follows:
5.25 Isolation. Reduction in the capacity of a system to respond to excitation, attained by the use of a resilient support. In steady-state forced vibration, isolation is expressed quantitatively as the inverse of transmissibility.
As far as the claims of the spray insulation company regarding their product - this is indeed for their product used as a part of a 2 leaf system, and a critical part of that assembly is that the spray material is never the full depth of the stud or joist bay - otherwise (due to it's rigid nature upon curing) it creates a "short circuit" between the 2 leaves - creating (in effect) a one leaf system. I considered using this in the high rise hotel I just completed - (for party walls separating guest units) but found that it was not cost effective when compared with standard wall assemblies using fire safing. I also found (upon a deeper examination of the manufacturer's claims) that their tests indicate better results than reported in IR 761 - which I believe is attributable to specimen size - the manufacturers test a 4'x8' specimen - and thus have no interior joints which may affect leakage - whereas the IRC tests are performed with 8'x10' assemblies - which more accurately resemble real life conditions. Sincerely, Rod
