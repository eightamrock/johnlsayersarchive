gullfo15 December 2010#2
welcome! what is the ceiling? is that concrete or is it a drop ceiling with more space above it? some concerns - the concrete structure will likely conduct LF sound pretty effectively so you might want to do some tap (metal and rubber) tests to see how much transfer is going on - perhaps on the floor above and below as well. you might need to build some floating isolation walls and floating floor to handle things. for the new separation wall, could that be block or framed? if you could build a block wall and then build your isolation shell inside that it might be pretty decent isolation. if framed, since you don't control the other side of the wall, someone else could end up easily punching holes in it... perhaps the same for block...

gullfo15 December 2010#5
the smaller room would probably be less costly since you can simply build up your inner isolation walls and ceiling there and it's already relatively secured. at which point then you should be OK (and your neighbors too). otoh, having the small adjunct room could be handy for storage and/or a machine closet and having a larger space is nice although the overall costs will be higher because you have more material and need to build the additional isolation wall to divide the space. i'd do some noise testing and see what's going on in terms of LF and vibrations.
