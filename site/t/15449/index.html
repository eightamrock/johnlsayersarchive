With a view to implementing a variation of the exchange chamber design as outlined in both editions in Rod’s book, here is where I’m at. gullfo3 January 2011#5
i'm name agnostic... sorry, i hadn't seen the returns before - venting them directly outside is fine. the idea in Rod's design is that the exchange chamber is like a normal room with AC and air is exchanged as normal, then that room air is exchanged throughout the rest of the studio. you have to have enough capacity in the exchange chamber to handle the air being cycled frequently enough not to be stale. (which is not an issue in your design). one thought is to pay attention to the possible pressure variations due to differences in the overall volume of the supply duct/exchange chamber versus the return ducts.

i cannot pretend to be an HVAC engineer... that said though, you will have some loss of efficiency so if you need 150CFM then maybe you need 175CFM fan? i believe there are some online calculators for figuring out the pressure for given duct sizes and so on (http://www.builditsolar.com/References/hvacrs.htm) as a general rule - trying to keep your overall noise level down to NC25 or less is good. using low speed air movement but more volume is desirable. you could do a single supply which branches off to the other rooms and skip the chamber approach.

Hi, I have come up with a more detailed plan for the fresh air supply and extraction from the 3 rooms in the studio, here goes... Room Details CR = 1401.37 cubic ft, Drum Room = 918 cubic ft, Main Live Room = 1993 cubic ft. I will need to provide approx 150 CFM of fresh air to the studio rooms CR will need 45 CFM (I have a window to open if more is needed) Drum Room will need 30 CFM Main Live Room will need 75 CFM The Plan Direct Supply of fresh air from an exterior vent ducted to each of the rooms. The returns will extract to an exterior vent also. An inline fan will be placed on either the intake or the extract duck line – fan needs to be able to push or pull approx 150 cfm, due to resistance, duct turns etc, it’s probably wise to rate the fan a bit higher – say 180cfm. I propose that the ducting be 8”, this will mean if the volume of air is approx 150cfm, then the velocity of the air moving thru the ducting will be around 428fpm. Before entering each room the fresh air supply will pass through a silencer (see design below) designed to double the volume of air and therefore cutting its velocity by ½. This means the air will enter the silencer @ 428fpm and exit hopefully @ around 214fpm. The supply register will be 8” x 16” and hopefully of sufficient quality not to generate any noise or a speed up in velocity. 214fpm seems to be an acceptable figure for studio air velocity from what I have read. I am restricted to 8” ducting due to joist sizes and other variables. Now for a whole lotta of questions Silencers I have tried to follow Rods guidelines with regard to building these silencers properly; I would really appreciate if someone with a bit more knowledge on their construction could take a look at my design.
