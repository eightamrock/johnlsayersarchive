Eric_Desart27 August 2007#2
gmore I don't have experience with them all, but used both brands (mostly Bostik) http://solutions.3m.com/wps/portal/3M/e ... 8HT14PGTgl http://www.bostik.com.au/distribution_p ... t_page.htm I have best experience with contact spray glue (looks as the type you suggested isn't a contact glue type). I'm not saying the others shouldn't work. In practice there are 2 types (from a practical type of view). Some spray glues spray a bit fluffy, other spray really fine. I like the fine spraying ones. If you respect the open time (contact glue principle) you can make nice work, without making the fabric stiff or without the glue penetrating the fabric. Sorry can't remember exact types and have no cans here with me. I'm sure other brands will/can work as well. For the absorption they are all OK. You never will use enough glue to really harm that absorption or you're working wrong with this stuff. I should go to your hardware or whatever store, ask which brands they have, ask for contact spray glue, ask if he knows if it sprays fine or fluffy, and take the fine spraying one with you at home.

Eric_Desart27 August 2007#4
Dean, I know your type is a spray. The key point I referred to here is the contact glue spray. Since you spray both surfaces, you have direct good contact when still moisty. You need VERY little (a vague shade of glue). Once you bring both surfaces together (after respecting open time) you have a perfect connection. The only thing is that you must do it immediately right, because once connected it stays that way. But any spray you feel happy with will do. On the net people become keen on known types and brands. That's often just because they are repeated often enough. And 3M of course is a safe name (and expensive brand). It's a bit like OC 703 which became some kind of magic absorption standard, while being nothing more than a fiberglass board for which there are lot of valid regional alternatives.

Eric_Desart27 August 2007#8
gmore, If it works, it works, whichever glue. I've made at least 30 absorbers several months back gluing with contact spray at the edges and where needed. I had no problem whatsoever. I standard glued boards together 2*4 feet, and some of them I glued with 1 layer turned 90° to make stable 4*4 feet panels. If you have trouble now, I don't think it can harm buying another type (1 can) just to compare how that goes. I can not imagine that you should influence the absorption of that board in any significant manner, if you use not more glue than needed. Using your 77 or whichever typical contact glue spray (spraying 2 sides) will stick very well when applying it correctly. Maybe you have trouble by applying it only one-sided.

The 77 can actually be used either way - just as a one sided application (less permanent) or both sides as a contact adhesive. As Eric said, if you do the contact method and just spray the perimeter (and the back to wrap and stick), the vast majority of the absorbtion is untouched and functions just as though it just had the cloth in front of it.
