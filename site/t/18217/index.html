Hi Paul, and welcome! :) So this is basically just a rehearsal room, for now, with the top priority being isolation? In order to answer your questions, you really need to put numbers to the situation. Numbers as in "How loud are you" and "how quiet do you need to be", but in terms of decibels, not words. You can't plug the phrase "not disturb my neighbors while they are inside their house" into the mass law equations and MSM resonance equations, to come up with answers to the amount of air gap and surface density you need. :) You have to have real-world decibels to plug into those equations. Without those numbers, it's all just guesswork. All i can give you are the absolute minimum recommendations for ANY type of isolation wall, but that might or might not be sufficient for your project:
Any suggestions on the air gap size?
Minimum is 4", but more is better. It depends...
thickness of drywall?
Always at least 578", never thinner. However, you might need more than one layer. It depends...
number of layers of drywall?
It depends...
Should I bother with green glue?
Depends on your requirements. If you need high levels of isolation at low frequencies, then yes.
Decoupling?
Always necessary, no question about it. Without decoupling, you are stuck with mass law, and mass law is not your friend.
What to do with the ceiling?
The same as the walls, always. That's about all that can be said, until you figure out your numbers. It would also help if you could post a basic plan of what you have in mind, preferably in SketchUp. - Stuart -
