Greetings - I've been lurking around these forums for some time now. Figured I ought finally register and post with my own project. The room I'm seeking to work with is for monitoring full mixes and tracking. For the tracking, it'd be mostly acoustic guitar and vocals, but other non-drumkit instruments are game too. Realistically, I only seek to record one instrument at a time. From the floor to ceiling measures 93 inches. Floors are some kind of wood (see pic). Ceiling and walls are standard plaster with lord knows what behind em. Its an old New England house. Not much I can do about sound isolation, but I live on a quiet street and can get other people in the house to not make noise for recording sessions. I'm not too worried about it. Here comes a frustrating phrase: I'm renting. I might only be here 7 more months, I might be here 19 more months.. but one way or another Im out after that. I can't really mount stuff on the walls, so anything thats portable is game. If whatever mount procedures could easily be undone, I'd consider it... but I'm all about portability. I've seen some bedroom designs similar to mine, but most seem to have the flexibility of being about to mount stuff, and was really curious about tube traps, and maybe in the style of that ASC Attack Wall. If thats the case, I'd mount my monitors in those. I figure I'm going to be adding most of my reflections and room with reverb and short delay, but it would be nice if I could work with a non-dead, clean "natural" reflection of course. If the tubes are effective and portable, I'm really really interested in that well before any mounting. Also, I figure I have to worry about the heating vents being close to bass traps... and since they're already 4 inchs off the walls.. am I going to be running into problems? If I did mount traps, would I have to mount them above the heating vents? Will that be a hinderance to the trapping? Maybe I could custom build traps that have cutouts for the metal heating vent dealie? You can see why that Attack Wall is so appealing... Similarly, for the corners currently where the futon bed is, would I simply move the futon away from the wall and put the traps in the corners ? Or would it be okay to have them come flush with the futon bed? I know my bedroom's decorative style might have just become uglified with that thought... but I kind of like the idea of sleeping in a studio for whatever reason. Dunno if my girlfriend will be as keen... heh. any thoughts on all this? If I intend to put one in the corner that the doors open to, it'll obviously need to be removable. Is there anything not-that-invasive that can be done to a ceiling? I don't know what's above it... so foam is about the only thing I can think of.. but I don't know that it would accomplish all that much though. I don't have any level measuring equipment currently, but I don't plan to crank the crap out of my monitors. I value my hearing and plan to monitor at reasonable levels. Think - someone appreciating the details in a Boards of Canada or Radiohead mix, not someone getting physically blasted by the timbres of 12 multi-tracked metal guitars. My budget - If I could get a really quality solution for monitoring mixes and recording primarily acoustic guitars and vocals that fits in that room (and could be reasonably moved to another room), I could be tempted to spend a lot. I'd love to keep this under $1000 (hell, I'd love to keep it under $500!) but dunno if that's unrealistically low. How much would my theoretical scenario run me? Truthfully, I don't even know what that Attack Wall costs, as I can't find a link to anyplace that sells ASC's tubes, though some sell their own version and it doesn't seem like it'd cost me both arms and legs.. I need to mix with something after all. Final note: Making and listening to music each bring unmeasurable joy into my life, so thank you for your valuable time in reading/responding; thank you for helping to bring unmeasurable joy into my life! ! -Chris

kendale3 November 2005#5
Aloha Chris, While I have a lot to learn and am certainly no expert, here's the basic layout of the treatments I'm planning. Each of the units is being built in a modular approach: •the front treatment was built in three separate sections and then screwed together to create one unit •the side absorbers are planned as eight sections which are stackable (36” long x 36” tall x 8” deep) •the rear corner absorbers are planned as two separate units (one per corner) •the rear absorber is planned as a single unit (6’ long x 6’ tall x 8” deep) •cloud treatment (not pictured) 8’ wide x 8’ long x 4” deep) Some of the reasons for this approach are: •I am in a “temporary” location (family room) •Units are more manageable to position and store as needed •I can use the units in the live area as needed for tracking •Easier to afford treatment costs in installments These may or may not work in your situation; hopefully they will help to get the creative juices flowing as you consider how you might go about treating your room. While the designs may not be 100% optimum, the improvement in the mix position is considerable. Disclaimer: Product design subject to change, actual mileage may vary :wink: kendale
