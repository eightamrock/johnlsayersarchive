RESTRICTIONS: *Choosing this room means it also has to function as a living/entertaining room. *I want to make the least possible damage to walls, ceiling and floor. Any damages will be charged when I move within 3 years. * I need to set this up with a minimum of delay and work, as I need to finish some ongoing projects. I have 2 weeks before commencing a new and demanding position at work. I could try to do a more accurate measurement, but I haven't got the proper equipment.. QUESTIONS: 1. Is this the best placement in this room, or should I go against one of the walls instead? Would a floor-to-ceiling bass/broadband trap build by stacking rigid mineral wool (covered with a drop-down cloth) behind the monitors help enough to counter the nodes in the room? 3. What if I add a broadband absorber (http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm) in the opposite corner beside the sofa? 4. I'd rather not do *any* ceiling treatment considering the damage I would do to the walls to mount it, but if necessary I could perhaps put something in the first reflection point monitors/mixpos.. 5. Any other thoughts and suggestions you might have. BUDGET: Around $1000-$2000 but I'd love it if it was lower, having just moved countries with many things to replace, two people to feed and one salary.. Warm regards and thanks in advance. I will monitor this thread closely.. - David

This is to give somthing to read until you get response from someone who knows what they talk about. Some of MY THOUGHTS (could be wrong). I started my own room analyelysis with cardioid and data collected was definitelly useful. Proximity effect is smooth curve, and modes have quite big variations in relativelly small frequency range. I hope hypercardoid should "see" problems as well, but there is risk of phase reversal from back of the microphoe to cause problems in LF. Anyway I bought digital SPL meter that has output from it's omni mic, for ~100$. In your budget it's not major expense, and it should prove useful in the future. Treating room with measurements is whole lot more fun. Because some changes in trap placement are to subtle to judge by ear (mine at least), but obvious on ETF.
The Radio Shack SPL meter combines a microphone and a preamp (line level) that can be used to drive the input of a sound card at the line input (not the mic input). This is a perfectly good choice for a microphone to tune low frequency acoustics and see room reflections at higher frequencies. If this is the intended purpose of the software, then the low cost RS meter is all that should be used. A calibration file link for this unit resides on our site on the support page. This file makes the microphone roughly accurate to a dB up to about 5 KHz.
Another thing, I've read somewhere in these or other forums that you need at least 30% of are treated to have some effect, preferably more that 50%. And indeed I can confirm that single or two corner treatment has some positive effect, after which you get addicted to treating your room. (check my thread http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4038 to see how crazy are my plans). I have similar requirements as you - don't want to ruin the place completely, and take treatment with me when I move. The chunks I make for ceiling/wall corner will be held by 2 screws at ceiling/wall junction each. The rest is free-standing stuff. Moving away, hopefully, I will unscrew these screws and caulk the holes unnoticeable. Ahh.. almost forgot, there will be two "flat" traps on the ceiling - some more holes. But unless you have really exotic ceiling fixing holes should be no big issue compared to building all the treatment initially. Hope that helps, Tomas

Well, I'd not put it on 2 screws in gypsum. It's meant for concrete walls. The design is in my topic. Basically it has long bolts running through all length and extending ouside the chunk. I've been consulted for specific hooks for concrete walls, and they said they can hold much more.

what kind of corner absorbers? anything that's light, looks good and does not destroy walls when taken down?
to my information, you don't want "light", because it won't absorb lows. You have to see how heavy can you make in your current situation. 3pcf density is recommended for acoustic treatment. And the thicker it is the wider the Q of absorbtion. Actually even a single sheet of 4" rock would make the difference. And I've seen in some forums how it can be attached using just 2 screws in the corner. Something like attached in my picture. They use springs instead of regular cable. Also built like florian suggested can be hung this way. But you should really calculate the costs of repairing the holes in walls, to see if it's really worth compromising sound quality for 3 years instead of maybe some 100$ to fix later. I believe it's more mental than real problem.
