There is a whole history of competitive behaviour between these two companies, with both claiming their product is superior, even if by only a small margin this way or that. There is no doubt that Green Glue came on to the market first and that they weren't happy when a competitor finally arrived some years later. QuietGlue were the new kids on the block (10 years ago) so they had to openly compare themselves to Green Glue if they were to be taken seriously. Thus, they acknowledge the comparison openly on their website and still do. It's no surprise that you won't see any mention of QuietGlue on the GG website. They would rather not draw your attention to the fact that there is another product on the market that can do the same thing, usually for less money! By all accounts, GG is an effective product with loyal customers and high customer satisfaction. But they were never going to maintain a monopoly forever. Slowly but surely, through word of mouth on forums just like this, QuietGlue has been eating away at that monopoly. I've done quite a bit of research on this and all I can say is that all the recent test reports I've seen rate the two products more or less equal, and almost all the online discussions I've read reveal customer satisfaction with QG to be just as positive as it is with GG. I went looking for a definitive winner, and it can't be found as far as I'm concerned. Heck, I want the best for my studio like everyone else. If QuietGlue is a new discovery for you then I completely understand your wary eye, but the way I see it...Coke or Pepsi? :wink:

stevev wrote:
Stuart has said '30kg/m for fibre glass' and '50kg/m for mineral wool' (rockwool). You might have missed that bit. The two products are different and have different gas flow resistivity properties. Generaly in oz we use fibreglass or polyester insulation. Rockwool is available but I generally don't see it around as much, and I don't believe any one has the gas flow resistivity numbers for polyester. (but I could be wrong) You don't want low density (10-15kg/m) fibreglass for your cavity insulation (the pink or yellow stuff) as it doesn't have high enough gas flow resistivity, and conversly, you don't want 50kg/m fibre glass as it has too much (and acts like more of a solid wall). The best fibreglass product I found for cavity insulation in Oz is bradford soundscreen 110mm batts at 24 kg/m. You could also use Fletcher FI32 which is 32kg/m but if I remember right, the price basically doubles :shock: I did use FI32 for internl CR treatment though. .....and yeah, get ready to have a mild heart attack at how much money you'll spend on getting the right kind of insulation. You won't need supertel for the cavity, but soundscreen isn't cheap either :lol: all the best, Steve
