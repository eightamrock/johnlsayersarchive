AVare12 May 2006#6
how do you apply these ratios to non-rectangular rooms?
For angled walls, the usual approach is to use the average of the dimension. Something usually overlooked by people working on room dimansions and ratios is that all the theory is just that. In real life, things get, well, strange.BBC RD 1992-09 is a great resource as it shows theoretical vs actual results for several rooms. Table 1 is a great start with theoretical vs. actual room mode frequencies.s. All the room mode ratios are based on these frequencies. :D It also has examples of rooms with asymmetrical features, and discussions on the actual modes. A great exercise is reality :D If after absorbing the above report you get the feeiling that room med prediction and room mode ratios are educated stabs in the dark, congratulations! You have groked one of the major points of small room acoustic design! May all your room modes be even in frequency spacing and liner in peak amplitude: Andre
