Welcome to the forum! Personally, I wouldn't use galvanized metal duct. One reason is that I would think sounds would travel longer distances and possibly be a little louder than with other type ducts. This is including if the outside is lined with fiberglass. Now if the outside AND inside is lined with fiberglass that may work although I've never ecountered any galvanized metal duct with fiberglass on the inside and outside. If they do make that type of duct make sure the fiberglass used in the duct has a "history" of being safe for particals not breaking off and travelling through your ducts. I believe the duct of choice (recommended) on this site is Johns Manville Superduct. Below are some posts concerning HVAC, including a few that mention about the Johns Manville Superduct. http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic ... highlight= http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4290 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... highlight= http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=6374 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4106 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4878 If you can afford the Johns Manville Superduct, I would go with it first. There may be another duct that's just as good or better than this, but you'll have to do some research to find that out. After this duct I would suggest lined ductboard. It's a pretty rigid duct that's lined with some type of polymer material (I think) on the inside. I believe it has fiberglass insulation around the polymer lining with an aluminum foil type outside backing. The last choice I would use is flexduct. I used this along with the ductboard mainly because of price, convienience and construction reasons. If I could have, I went have went with the Superduct though.
One input and return for the studio room, and one input and return for the CR. I don't think I need the extra isolation of having another set of input and return for the machine room. I think I can share the ducts with the CR, but I haven't made up my mind.
I wouldn't "share" those duct lines if at all possible. Any noises from your machine room could be transmitted back through your duct. Also, because your machinery will be transmitting heat, I would run an air supply AND return there "IF" possible. If nothing else, I would have an air return line there. The one thing I would really REALLY suggest you do is research everything you want to do concerning HVAC. "After" everything is done isn't the time to say "I wish I would have"... I'm saying this because after I got finished I later found info that could have changed some of my plans. I know it may not be the popular thing to say but if you have any doubts about whether or not what you want to do will be the correct thing for your studio, S-T-O-P and research what you're in question about! Things like "how do I get fresh air in", "How will I dehumidify", "What location would be best to locate the vents?", "What are the best/quitest type vents to use and should they be oversized?", "Should I put a duct in the front where my speakers will be?" etc etc are things to consider. If you haven't already, I'd suggest you purchase Rod's book (a post is in the sticky section). It gives a LOT of info on how to figure EXACTLY what type HVAC system you need based on people, equipment etc etc. *NOTE* I'm by no means an expert, but just sharing my opinions based on info I've come across along with personal evaluation. Hope some of this helps!
len-morgan12 June 2006#4
Having just finished the HVAC phase of my studio, I can attest to the EndraGold duct board from OC. It ran me about $38 for a 4x10' sheet. I used two ducts off of my A/C system: 1 24" x 18" for the supply and a 24"x24" for the return. They are each about 25' line. Connected to each of these I used flex ducting (I've been told since that this may have been a mistake but at the time this was the suggested way to go). I have a separate flex line going from the register all the way back to the supply or return duct that run along the back of the building (and which is separated by a wall from any of the recording areas. I have 2 supplies and 2 returns in my CR, same for my "big" room, and a single supply and return in the vocal booth and another pair in my drum room. I ran a separate supply duct and return duct for my reception/office area. 14 duct runs in all. None of them connects through "Ts" or "Ys" A little complicated and a lot of material but well worth it. I can turn up the speakers in the CR and not hear ANYTHING in the duct work in the other rooms. In order for sound to get from the control room to any other room (through the A/C system), it has to travel a minimum of 50' through insulated duct work. The reason I used such large main ducts (and 8" flex duct) was to slow down the air but still get a lot of air volume. The slower it moves, the less noise it will make. My ducts are ONLY made out of the EnduraGold board, folded up and taped. No galvanized duct work at all. The board is stiff enough (I used 1" thick) to support itself although I added a little support at the joints between 10' sections. It is very easy to work with but you should get the right tools (V cutting knife, etc). As to finishing up the work yourself, unless you know about HVAC systems and balancing static pressure and such, you should work with the HVAC contractor to give you the design (which you can then do yourself). While it seems like it would be easy to just run some tubes to pass air, it's a lot more complicated than that and if you aren't careful, you might end up with a very quiet system that just doesn't work right (i.e., it's too hot to work). Hope that helps a little. len

len-morgan13 June 2006#9
Sorry, I kind of missed that little detail about part of the ductwork being outside. One thing you might consider though is getting the thickest duct liner (for inside the galvanized part) AND seeing if you can have your HVAC contractor build some sort of "tent" over that. The reason for the tent is because when is rains, the rain will hit the duct and it will act like a big drum being hit with little rocks and that sound will be really hard to get rid of once it gets into the duct work. You might be able to do away with the tent if you put a big silencer just inside the studio (where the galvanized enters the building. Just a thought. len
