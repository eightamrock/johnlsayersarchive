TomM wrote:
The floor joists for the first floor are around 10". There is a good section where there is nothing inbetween them. I was wondering if to save height, I should attach 3 layers of drywall in-between all the joists against the bottom of the flooring on the first floor. And then attach another two layers of drywall to the 10" joists (or maybe do a ceiling on the inner walls...but i think that would take away too much height). That would leave about 7 1/2 inches of airspace.... except of course the 10" deep joists. Is that a good idea? or a bad idea??
That is a massive amount of weight you're adding to the existing ceiling / floor above you. I would be shocked if the structure is capable of accepting that much additional weight. While it may make sense from an isolation perspective, it would not make sense from a "probably totally flattened basement with anyone inside of it at the time of collapse killed" perspective. :roll: I would recommend that you hire a structural engineer before adding even one layer to that ceiling. Some may think that's overkill, but I'd rather "overkill" than "be killed by what's over."
What would you recommend for the flooring considering again that I don't want to lose too much height?? Considering that I'm on concrete under ground.... would it be better to float a floor or use the space so that the inner wall ceiling isn't attached to the floor joists of the first floor. Or would I really be better off losing the height?
You don't have much room to spare at all. Not knowing where you are located (since your profile isn't updated with your location... tsk, tsk...), there are probably code restrictions regarding vertical clearance (ceiling height). Often the minimum is 7' 6" in habitable spaces. Note that is finished clearance, so if you've got 8' to the bottom of your joists, you've got only six inchs to spare to account for the thickness of your ceiling and your floor. I would think floating for you would not be an option.
I'm a bit confused on the concrete slabs too... does that go on top of the existing concrete of the basement? how effective would it be to just use a drum riser? using those rubber blocks that auralex sells and some 2X4s/plywood/laminate.
You might be confused by discussions of isolated slabs, which are different from floating slabs. Make sure you understand the differences, and then if you have more questions, please post them. I have gone on record here numerous times recommending against the Auralex U-boats. Here's an example. --Keith :mrgreen:
