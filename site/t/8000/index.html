A moveable wall and my studio? Help/comments please? I have seen a variety of studios each having splayed walls. I downloaded sketchup (thanks to John Sayer) and have made the plan up to scale. The first file shows the dimensions. The second file shows modifications to splay one wall and to make an attempt to have the monitor speakers be flush with walls. I was considering making the movable wall from a standard timber frame with plywood and inner insulation. I was considering making one side reflective and one dead (see file two) as I have done with the other panel (see file one). My space is severely limited and that is why I have opted to use the studio for the control room also. I have rigged up two boxes “A” so I can use a junction box in order to record drums live have good isolation and separation when mixing on the far east side if I have to. I have used the garage door side, (far east side) as a waiting room when I have taught students in the past and may teach from home again in the future. I thought of a nifty idea by using the large sound-lock to house the computers thereby keep all the fan noise away. The mouse and screen wires to go through a hole which I intend to cover the hole with lead. I am aiming for a dead room as possible and I will be using PC software such as reaper to record with. The walls are 2400 mm tall. We are in a quiet street so I am more concerned keep the band noise in. The mass spring mass rule has got be worried with the wall that faces the house because it is typical plaster (drywall) stud plaster (drywall) on the south side. The walls will be filled with timber, plaster, bricks and whatever I can get my hands on. The roof of the existing building (the north, east and west walls is two layers of 5ply with a true garage ceiling above it. The ceiling and all the walls to not touch the existing garage except for the floor which is solid concrete. There you go absolutely any comments will be welcome be they positive, or critical. Thanks guys. By the way, the yellow stuff is the current insulation. And the unsymmetrical walls where the table is on the south side are the extension to the existing studio that was already made.

gullfo21 January 2007#5
Moving walls around seems like a hard thing - for a large wall, you'd have to move a bunch of stuff around to make space and then how would you move it? hinges? wheels? if you change the walls, how do you know you're improving the room modes versus making them worse? given that a few inches can make the difference, you'd probably want the wall to move in pre-determined increments. more complexity... and if high isolation is important then moving the walls and retaining the isolation would seem difficult at best. one approach would seem to be to create a room with good acoustics with moderate reverbation and mode balancing treatments, and then have some other treatments/panels/gobos/etc that can be used to vary the liveness-deadness of the space, and/or provide some moderate isolation or additional absorption as needed. using the variety of wall and free standing panels you can create the spaces you need within your overall good space.
