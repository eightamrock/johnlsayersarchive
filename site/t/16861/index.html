Soundman202025 August 2011#2
Hi Brandon. Welcome! Three things: 1)
I'm planning to build a small room in my garage that will measure 8'x8'
Bad idea! Not the drum booth, but the dimensions! You should NEVER build a room where any two of the dimensions are the same. That will cause your modal response to be very poor. And seeing that most rooms are also 8 feet high, you would be building the absolute worst possible shape for a room, acoustically speaking: A perfect cube! You will have MAJOR modal problems at 70 Hz, 100 Hz, 140 Hz, 210Hz, 280 Hz. etc. depending on how you tune your drum kit, you are going to have a very "interesting" room like that! In other words, change your dimensions a bit. Make the room 9 x 7, or some such. But not 8 x 8, and certainly not if the ceiling is also 8'! 2)
Since I will only be in there to use the drums (no recording or anything) is it practical to just run an extension cord in somehow for lighting instead of having to hardwire in electrical?
That depends.... How much isolation do you need? What does your local building code say about powering a habitable space from an extension cord running through a partly open door? My guess is that it is not permitted, and apart from that you would end up with lousy isolation. In order to isolate well, the room MUST be sealed air-tight. 3)
For ventilation is there a way to get air in/out cheaply without compromising the soundproofing?
Like they say around here: You can have "cheap" you can have "simple" and you can have "soundproofing": pick any one of the above. They are mutually exclusive... :) Having said that, there are ways to ventilate your room, and yes you most certainly do need to do that: In order to isolate, your room basically needs to be wrapped in a very thick blanket and sealed inside a plastic bag (figuratively speaking). So yes, ventilation is a necessity, if you like breathing. And yes, there are ways of doing it without compromising isolation. There are dozens of examples on this forum of how to do it right: search for terms like "HVAC" and "silencer" and "ventilation" and you should find plenty to keep you busy. The basic idea is to build two ducts, one to bring in fresh air, the other to take out stale air. Each duct is isolated and decoupled correctly, and silencers are used to retain the room isolation.
Also, if I were to build the room now without ventilation would it be possible to add it later when I can afford a better system or is it a terrible idea to build it without any ventilation?
That depends on how long you can hold your breath without dying! That's about how long you can wait before adding a ventilation system... :) In other words, it really is a terrible idea to build an airtight room with no ventilation. Some folks think "I'll just open the door every few minutes".... Nope! That doesn't work either, in practice. You do need ventilation, especially in a small room. - Stuart -

Soundman202025 August 2011#4
It IS the only option for keeping the noise down! No doubt about that. But don't get discouraged: it certainly is doable. The basic concept is simple: seal up the outer shell of your existing garage, to make is as air-tight as possible, build a simple 2x4 stud frame inside the garage, so that it only touches the floor (does not touch any of the existing walls or the existing ceiling), and put some drywall on that frame. That's it! If you can pick up a hammer and saw and use them reasonably you, you can do it. Sure, there are more details that you need to be aware of in the design and the build, but that's the basic concept. It isn't hard. Your ventilation can be as easy as a hole in the wall of your new room that attaches directly to a silencer box (which is also easy to build), with a very quiet fan on one end. Not hard to do. Your electrical work can be as simple as a single socket attached to the wall somehow, with "putty packs" spread around it to maintain the seal. This doesn't have to be hard: It just has to be designed carefully, and built carefully. But pretty much anyone can do it. Post some photos and a rough diagram of what you have in mind, preferably in SketchUp format. That way, folks here on the forum can see what you have, and help you along the path of getting where you need to go. - Stuart -
