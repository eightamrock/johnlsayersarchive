Cram15 January 2008#2
my brother and drummer uses a carpet from the living room carpet we had replaced. If I were concerned about your floor losing its sheen, I would put padding between the two - it's softer than the fabric on the bottom of most carpet. There have been issues with stability with stands though - the highhat stand in particular wobbles so I cut a small peice of plywood out to rest that peice on. I even put small nails through that plywood so they stuck out the bottom end and kept the plywood from sliding. But yes, a carpet you don't care about works great. A carpet you care about will wind up with holes near the kick pedal and other contact points. hope this helps.
frostgfx15 January 2008#3
I would lay some carpet down with padding and use some carpet tape (double stick type) to hold all in place. The tape probably won't take the finish off the wood. I plan to do this in my studio build as well. Check with you drummers to make certain they have rubber baby buggy bumpers on their stands and especially the Kick and Hi Hat stands. The sharp points on a Kick will mess up your floor in a heartbeat. Good luck, Jon

tmix16 January 2008#6
I will give you my thoughts. I have an epoxy coated cement floor. I used the rubber backed office floor mats with the carpeting on top to "protect " my floor and to keep the drums from sliding. The first drummer I had in did not have all his spurs concealed so they went through the mats and damaged my new flooring. What I have planned to do is glue rubber mats to the underside of some exterior grade plywood and make a place for the drums that not only reflect the snap of the snare drum but also allows a wear surface that is easily replace or painted. You can always use a rubber backed door mat for the kick to keep it from sliding, but at least the floor is protected.
