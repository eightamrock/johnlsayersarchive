Soundman202024 March 2014#3
Hi there Kenete, and I'll add my "Welcome!" to FriFlo's I'll also agree with the comments he made. The forum is a "self-help" place, where you can find a huge amount of information on how to design and build your studio, then post your design when it is done, and people here will comment on it, offer suggestions on how to improve it, tell you what's wrong, what's right, etc. As FriFlo said, you basically have those two options: 1) Learn to do it yourself, in which case I'd suggest two books: "Master Handbook of Acoustics" by F. Alton Everest (that's sort of the Bible for acoustics), and "Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros", by Rod Gervais. Realistically, it takes about 2 or 3 months to learn the basics of acoustics, and probably another 2 or 3 to learn how to build it. Then figure another month or two for the actual design, then several more months for the build. It takes most people at least a year, maybe 2 (or more!) to complete a studio, from the time they first start out with the idea. 2) If you don't want to do that, or don't have the time, then you'll need to pay a studio designer to do the complete design job for you, then you can just do the building based on his design. That can be a lot quicker, and should only take a couple of months, max. It might even be quicker: perhaps just two or three weeks, if your needs are very simple. FriFlo is also right about your budget. The best way to figure how much it will cost to build your studio, is to call around local contractors and see if they will tell you their basic "flat rate" per square foot for turning an unfinished garage or basement into a home theater or guest room. Then add about 25% to that. That's a realistic cost for your actual build. But as FriFlo said, 2 grand is not going to go very far at all: 2 grand for 235 square feet implies that you want to spend about 8 dollars per square foot of floor area. That wouldn't even cover the cost of the floor, without considering the walls, ceiling, doors, windows, HVAC system, electrical system, and acoustic treatment. Even if you do all of the labor yourself, and can buy materials with a huge discount, 2 grand is still not nearly enough. For example, to do your inner-leaf wall and ceiling, just for the insulation alone you'd need about 400 square feet of 4" insulation, (or 800 square feet of 2" insulation). If you use OC-703 insulation (the best), you'd need a pallet of that (48 sheets of 2"). That pallet goes for about US$ 1,200, according to the Home Depot web site. There goes more than half of your entire budget, just on insulation for your inner-leaf walls! To do those walls, you also need a whole bunch 5/8" fire-rated drywall, 2x4 studs, nails, tape, mud, and stacks of caulk. As you can see, your budget is way short of where it should be. About ten times more would be a reasonable estimate.
