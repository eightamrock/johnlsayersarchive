Hi Carl, and welcome! :) It's good to see someone who did their homework, and took the time to follow all the forum rules! Photos and diagrams would help to better understand what it is you are dealing with, bit if I understand correctly, it seems to me that this might be one of those occasions where you have no real choice but to build a 3-leaf ceiling. If you can't seal the area above the joists, then sound will, indeed, escape, and you won't have a proper MSM system up there. So it seems like your best bet might just be to put mass on those existing rafters (drywall, OBS, plywood, etc), and seal off the space underneath for building your actual inner-leaf. So that "ceiling" mass will become your middle leaf. Then, to get a high level of isolation (50 dB is pretty high) you'll probably need to build a separate stud and joist frame inside that space, and put a couple of layers of drywall on one side of that, for your inner leaf. To maximize your final ceiling height, you could use Rod's concept of "interleaving" the new joists with the existing joists, assuming that you put the "middle-leaf" mass above the existing joists, or between them. - Stuart -

Air borne sound is the issue in this respect, the path of least resistance, where air goes so does sound, etc. There is nothing uncommon about this issue and it is typical of any structure available. The air path required to extract heat from a roof has a joist/rafter system that gives access to convection to work cooler air from the ground to move up and in and hotter air to move up and out. Contrary to popular belief adding mass to this (the rafter) will only add cost to the job but will not help you in any way. Far from it. Encapsulating the air path with a hard boundary reduces/removes the ability of air to move freely in this cavity and adds an area of uncertainty as to how much heat is being removed and how much is retained and this is never a good thing to the health of the structure subject to the most extreme of natures natural heating cycles.

Cold1 November 2012#6
Hi Carl, Here is the solution I would use. On the inside of your brick wall, seal the cavity between the the loft floor joists, the loft floor and the brick wall. Make sure to use the same amount or more mass as your existing brick shell. Insulate these areas as they become part of your wall system. The standard is board foam, sealed around the edges, or spray foam. Now add a second layer of board to to the top of your loft floor for extra mass, I believe there are examples of this in rod's book. Make sure the structure can support any extra mass being added. As a note, if you have an stairs up to the loft, close this off with a wall, door etc. as part of your first leaf. This should give you a sealed first leaf. Build your second leaf inside of it. Steve Ps I looked at the other thread, wanted to double check that you have a vent at the top of your roof and that you left a clear airspace under the insulation so that the roof can breathe properly.
