lovecow8 November 2007#12
There have been a couple of AES papers on this. One I'm familiar with is (edited) "The Effect of Diffusers on Frequency Dependent Room Mode Decay" by Angus, et al (AES Preprint 5060). The merits of this paper were discussed in another thread somewhere (don't even ask me what forum - maybe here, maybe studiotips, maybe RO, maybe :?: :?: :?: ). There are a couple of other papers I found, which - if I get a chance - I'll peruse this evening and comment. In general, I think low frequency diffusion is not at all practical for small rooms (to say the least). The theory is solid, though. If a mode can be "diffused," then it would, in theory, more evenly distribute low frequency energy in the room. I don't ever see this catching on in studios, though. And, like y'all, I have trouble seeing where it would ever be truly practical, even for larger spaces. {André: The authors used scaled electromagnetic simulation to confirm LF diffusion in the paper cited above. It can be done! :)}

lovecow9 November 2007#14
Note: I changed the AES citation above. The paper I had specifically in mind was #5060, not #3851, though both are good papers on this subject by Angus. I also found another that might be of interest: "Low Frequency Control end Acoustical Optomlsing of Small Rooms Using the Schroeder Diffuser" by Morton, AES Preprint No. 4034. Morton's main point is that room design can be used to "build in" LF diffusion. I often have to disagree with this approach, however, for the same reason built-in "bass traps" are often a flawed notion. As Doug Jones (and I) pointed out in Chapter 4 of the Handbook for Sound Engineers (pp. 81, 3rd ed.): "If there is enough space to build a 'bass trap' that is large enough to have an impact on wavelengths that large (lovecow note: authors are referring to 20 Hz "traps"), most likely one could move a wall, improving if not optimizing the modal distribution, obviating the need for 'trapping.'" IMO, one could easily replace "bass trap" with "LF diffuser" and "trapping" with "diffusing LF" in the above statement. Does that make sense?

gullfo11 November 2007#25
a large QRD in the right-hand room should work because its a series of wells anyways (stacking several sizes together - nesting them - is called a "diffractal" and can increase the bandwidth of the diffuser). a skyline in the left-hand mix room closet would not be good since the shorter pieces will have virtually no effect as it would be constrained by the side walls and any absorption in there. the shallower diffuser in front of the bass trap will give you the bass trapping and most of diffusion you're looking for. or consider the use of a poly diffuser as both diffusion and bass trapping (which it look sort of like your drawing). i did read a some of the thread but there's a lot going on there... while no doubt the history of how you got to this design decision is interesting, its not entirely relevant to correctly setting up your space... :wink:
