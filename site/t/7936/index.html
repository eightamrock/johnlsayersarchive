groff15 January 2007#2
Don't now much about Stopgap, but in general, except air temperature, the temperature of substratum/undercoat is often also very important for applying/drying. Method of drying (loosing or taking the moisture) and the time of drying in ideal conditions counts too. If the recommended temperature is i.e. 25 deg. now on 15 deg. it's possible it needs double time to dry. This winter is extremely warm, but no one could tell you what's going to be in February. IMHO that's gambling. I suggest you to contact manufacturer directly, or wait for springtime if heating is not the option. regards
rod gervais15 January 2007#3
Alex, If you are installing more than a single layer of drywall on each wall it is critical that you caulk as you go......... Otherwise you are never going to get it right (things like 2 point caulk joints are critical to assure you don't have connection failures over the years.......) Here in the states you can rent (purchase cheap even) small pot heaters that use propane gas for fuel. Look here: http://www.northerntool.com/webapp/wcs/ ... 598_503598 They burn very clean and are what we in the construction industry generally use for house construction when it comes to drywall finishes in the off seasons...... On the plus side of things - they burn very VERY clean - no smoke to mess up your interior surfaces.......... On the minus side - they do put out a lot of moisture as a part of their combustion process - so you don't want to let them just run wild..... You should only need the heat as long as it takes to get those joints all caulked and cured - once cured the colder temps shouldn't be a big issue........ Sincerely, Rod
