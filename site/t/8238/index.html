What do you do in this situation: I have recessed lights (4" housings) and 2 layers of 5/8" drywall in the ceiling. My 4" housings are NOT height adjustable. Because of the 2 layers of drywall, the trim for the recessed lights does not reach high enough into the housing (it won't stay in the housing). Aside from cutting out my drywall and replacing my housings with height-adjustable ones, what are my options? Are there such things as "housing extensions"? The only other thing I can think of would be to contact a friend of mine who is handy with metal - and could possibly manufacture some new "fingers" for my trim that would be long enough to reach into the housings.

sharward22 February 2007#5
Doing that may be a fire hazard. I think those fixtures are designed to vent a certain amount of heat upwards, so modifying their design in such a drastic way may increase the risk of fire. Personally, I don't think recessed lighting is very practical in a studio, unles the recessed lights are a part of a soffit of some kind that is not technically a part of the mass-air-mass envelope. --Keith :mrgreen:
