jhbrandt26 September 2010#2
You need to treat your room. Trapping is first. Trap the vertical corners where you can and alos the wall/ceiling corners. Move your mix position to the center of the wall. the corner is a bad idea and you will have severe imaging problems. Place a 'cloud' trap over the mix position between the operator and speakers. Absorption panels on the side walls for early reflections and the rear wall heavily. Use a thick rug. You can get some help with placement from my First Reflection Calculator herehttp://www.jhbrandt.net/pubs.html. good luck. Cheers, John

gullfo27 September 2010#10
make sure you layout the treatments so you get proper bass control and don't make it dead. so trap the corners (including the ceiling-wall corners) and i like to put loosely spaced slats over them to keep some high frequency in the room. if there is any way to get those cabinets out of the room and put somewhere else, it will help avoid things like buzzing as bits rattle about, and of course add more room... here's a couple of corner and super chunk absorbers faced with a set of slats sized per a primitive root sequence of p=11 (or you could just use random sizes etc). stack these in the corners all around. note that each slat in the group object has the dimensions of it. simply cut some lumber (MDF, plywood, planks etc) into strips. since these aren't actually Helmholtz resonators, you don't need to worry about sealing the edges etc. you can build the strips vertical or horizontal or diagonal as your aesthetics desire. the flat ones can be used on the ceiling as well.

gullfo28 September 2010#12
the idea would be to create a good balance between have the room frequency response and reverberation tight enough for mixing and enough life in the room to record. that said, in cases where you need more reflective surfaces, you could make some movable panels to (say) surround a guitar or flip them (assuming you made one side reflective and the other absorptive :) ) for a vocalist. etc.
