jwl29 October 2007#3
If at all possible you'll want to be facing the slanted ceiling, even if it puts your desk out toward the middle of the room. Think about how sound will reflect off that ceiling, you'll want to have a good strategy to deal with those reflections. If you are facing the slant, the sound will reflect up and over your head so they won't taint your image. If the slant is behind you, it will reflect the sound right to your head as you listen. Ideally the mix position should be 38% of the way back from either the front or the rear wall, though I'm not certain about how the slanted ceiling will affect the "38% rule". If you *must* go with the setup you have then you will want to get as much absorption on that slanted ceiling as you can to control those first reflections. Apart from that is the standard, get some form of bass treatment in there (like superchunks in the corners or whatever), and do your best to create a RFZ.
