geoff_fry01 wrote:
The brick will will have studs drilled on then mdf cut and placed in between. these will be held in via cleates on the studs. the plaster board will then be screwed to the mdf and acoustic sealant applied to the edges. then a second layer added.
You may not have to go to this effort. A brick wall makes a pretty darned good outer leaf in and of itself. In fact, there may be reasons not to do what you have described. If you press MDF against the brick, it will not handle moisture well at all (MDF pretty much disintegrates when it gets wet :(). OSB is more moisture tolerant, but still, I wouldn't apply it to a surface that is expected to wick moisture. I am by no means an expert on this, so don't take my word for it. Hopefully this will be enough of a nudge to get you to research that on your own, and if we're lucky, to have some experts or more experienced folks here respond with their points of view.
geoff_fry01 wrote:
Stage 2 to follow will involve building an inner room, I intend to have a air cirulation system but not aircon. that will use a current air outlet in the garage, but I need to research my alternative fans etc yet.
My only word of advice on that would be to be sure you won't need air conditioning. Often we make these judgments based on outside temperatures. Remember that lighting and gear generate heat, and so do human bodies. Ventilation alone may not do the job. Consider also the fact that true A/C, if properly sized, will act to dehumidify the space, which will cut down on the amount of moisture in the air inside the room, which would then want to migrate to the outside through your double walls. If that moisture gets trapped between your walls, you could end up with a mold problem. Again, not the expert -- just something you'll want to research before finalizing/building even phase one.

geoff_fry01 wrote:
Q3. are the principles used by you studio builders standard building methods or will I need to check that the builders are doing as you recomend?
Excellent question. No, they are not standard. They are anything but standard. If you have this work done for you, you will need to very carefully supervise every step. Steve has written quite a bit about the phenomenon of contractors thinking they know how to build studios or structures where sound isolation is important. Most often they don't know what they think they know and what they think they know is often wrong. Steve is quick to say, and I am too, that it's an understandable situation, and that nobody is calling them stupid... But as a rule, they really don't have a clue how to build this stuff, why certain things are important, etc. So much of this stuff is darned unintuitive, especially when you're accustomed to building via conventional methods. For that reason, I think that sometimes less experienced contractors make better studio builders because they haven't necessarily started going on autopilot by then. :roll: Hopefully that's enough to get things rolling in terms of research for you to continue doing and for others to share feedback. --Keith :mrgreen:

len-morgan21 September 2006#4
If it were my project, I'd just cement over the brick outer wall (I think they call it "rendering" on your side of the pond. I think it's Bob Gold's site (check the References section of the forum) that shows simply putting a coat of cement over the brick really increases the TL of the wall. Sharward's advice about mounting ANYTHING directly against the brick wall is good and should be heeded. Rendering your wall is quick an cheap (and could really be done by just about anyone leaving a lot more of your budget for the inside walls/AC ect. If you are NOT going to put in an A/C system, at least consider a dehumidifier to avoid future mold problems. len
