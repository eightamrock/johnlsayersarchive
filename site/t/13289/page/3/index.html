I noticed you never did complete your vapor barrier. As a rule, an unbroken barrier is >required< in order for it to display the attributes of a barrier. Not only is yours "broken" it is missing entirely in some of the areas. This is only mentioned in that when you have moisture issues somewhere down the short road, you may have an avenue to trace the path to what is or is not creating this problem. A solid unbroken moisture barrier is a requirement, anything less is unacceptable. Vapor will creep around the broken areas much in the same way anything else water related will take the path of least resistance.

The function of a vapor barrier is to retard the migration of water vapor. Where it is located in an assembly and its permeability is a function of climate, the characteristics of the materials that comprise the assembly and the interior conditions. ... Incorrect use of vapor barriers is leading to an increase in moisture related problems. Vapor barriers were originally intended to prevent assemblies from getting wet. However, they often prevent assemblies from drying. The fundamental principle of control of water in the liquid form is to drain it out if it gets in – and let us make it perfectly clear – it will get in if you build where it rains or if you put your building in the ground where there is water in the ground.
