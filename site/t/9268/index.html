liax,
liax1975 wrote:
I mixed already some songs and these are nearly exactly the frequency it is very, very hard to realize any soundchanges when tuning frequencies with an equalizer during the mix ..
I am having trouble understanding this statement. Are you saying it's hard to hear the difference with and without an EQ? Are you hearing problems in your mixes - i.e., are they not translating?
Has anyboda tips for me how to get rid of these mega-dips in the frequency spectrum ?
If your graph is a true representation of the sound at your mix position, you're in pretty good shape. From 20 Hz and up, the response is more or less ±5-6 dB, which is very good. The two dips you see are likely caused by loudspeaker / ear / boundary distances and not modes (they're too high in frequency). Give us your room dimensions and we can get a better idea. Otherwise, I'm willing to bet that if you move the microphone a matter of inches, the dips will move / disappear. In which case, you might try to figure out which surfaces are the offending ones and treat them with some 2"-4" panels, which should be plenty to address reflections in the 300-800 Hz range. Besides that, I would venture to guess that actually hearing these dips would only be possible if someone hit the notes (roughly a quarter-tone flat E4 and almost exactly G5 for 320 and 784 Hz, respectively) perfectly. Otherwise, the bandwidth of the ear should compensate well at these frequencies. Again, give us an idea of the dimensions of the room, loudspeaker and mix position locations, and a little bit better description of the problem(s) you're having and we'll be able to help you more. :)

John Sayers18 August 2007#7
I see a couple of problems. First is the soffit mount construction. There is a part missing below the speaker. The idea is to continue the baffle to 1ft above floor level. I do it by adding a plywood/mdf to the back of the front studs below the speaker and add insulation between the studs. This keeps the baffle going, adds a port at the floor/wall join back into the cavity under the speaker which is a bass trap. The insulation stops reflections off the back of the console coming back to bite you. Second - the gap between the slots is too great, they are tuned too high. they are ineffective as slots and because the angle isn't great they don't really create a RFZ. It's a pity you didn't fil the rear wall hanger sections with bigger hangers - you had the space. Here's Warren's front showing what I mean. cheers john
