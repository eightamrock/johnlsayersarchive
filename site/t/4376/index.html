camistan wrote:
One of the HVAC guys had a good point when he said "By incorporating outside air into the system, it will make the unit run longer and harder to process the recirculating air due to more heat drawn into the system in the summer and more cold air drawn into the system in the winter. This will definately increase your electric bill".
He's right. That's one of the reasons I like the idea of having the ventilation and the air conditioning (and/or heating) be considered separately and, ideally, controlled separately, regardless of whether they are all part of a single system or (as in my case) or separate and distinct systems. There will be times that you'll want "maximum coolness" and there will be times tnat you'll want "maximum freshness." I would imagine a fresh air system tied to the air conditioning which did not allow the occupant(s) to turn off the fresh air component would be rather uncomfortable and it would be much more expensive to operate. Take a good hard look at the heat recovery ventilator I'm going to use. Here's the post in my thread where I introduce it. --Keith

2.) Do I need an air return in a small door air lock? (Approx 1-3ft between doors)
I don't see the need to do any ventilation or conditioning in the area between the doors. This is not habitable space -- it's just an ultrashort hallway. In fact, by conditioning the space, you're attempting to make it habitable by code definition, and then a whole can of worms is opened that you won't be able to handle. :roll: Since you're not supplying that area, you wouldn't want a return there, since it is a sealed area/room -- you'd create negative pressure situation and thus would have difficulty opening the doors (which I presume would swing away from the air lock). Hope that helps!
