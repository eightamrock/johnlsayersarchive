Ground-up studio concerns, please help!

Started by Jesse on 20 November 2007. 7 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 9775.

Jesse20 November 2007#1
I am having a room within a room studio built in my backyard, and we are creating the structure from the ground up. I would love some input on my current plans, and have a few questions that I really need to address. Both the exterior and interior walls will be framed with wood on a newly poured concrete slab. I have been unable to find much information about how the exterior walls should be created.The plans for the exterior are to hang paper and wire mesh on the wood frame, apply lathe and plaster, and then 1 inch stucco. I am worried that the stucco will not be an adequate sound barrier without an additional layer of plywood or some other material. Does anyone know if this is the case? There will be a one inch air gap between frames, with a mass-spring-mass configuration including insulation in both frames. The interior walls and ceiling will consist of two layers of 5/8 drywall with a layer of soundboard sandwiched in between. I had planned to hang the interior walls on resilient clips with hat channel, but I have read that it would not help since I will have completely independent framing assemblies. If anyone could clarify that for me I would greatly appreciate it. I have also considered forgoing the soundboard and using a damping compound such as green glue instead. Would this give me a better STC rating? At this point, any mistakes in the design would mean that I waste all my money. I want to be able to record and have my band practice without disturbing the neighbors, so the higher the STC rating I can achieve, the better. Any help would be greatly appreciated!
