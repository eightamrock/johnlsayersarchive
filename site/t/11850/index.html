Well, modes are modes, and it doesn't really matter which way you look at them: they are all problematic, not just the vertical axial ones. Tangentials and obliques play their part too. With those dimensions, you have modal issues starting at 21 Hz and all the way up to 86 Hz, roughly. In fact, you have at least twenty nine modal issues, just between 20 Hz and 100 Hz. 9 are axials, 16 are tangential, and the final 4 are oblique. And that's just for the part of the ceiling that measures 7'6! With that steep slope on the ceiling and parallel walls with no treatment anywhere, you probably have all kinds of strange stuff going on, right across the low end of the spectrum! For the part for the ceiling that measures 11'6, you have 10 axials 18 tangentials and 8 obliques. And who-knows-what going on in between... :) When you say "insulate the ceiling", what do you actually mean by that? do you have exposed joists right now, inside your room, and you plan to put several inches of fiberglass or mineral wool in there, which will be remain visible in the room? If so, that will help a lot, especially if you make it thick. But if you have a drywall ceiling, and you plan to put insulation ABOVE that, then it really wont make much difference at all to the acoustics.

I'd say that you definitely also need bass trapping in there! If you have such noticeable modal issues, then just doing the ceiling wont be enough. I'd go for large bass traps in at least four corners, and more corners too, if you can. That would be my plan, if that were my room. - Stuart -

Soundman202024 May 2009#4
if, IF, i do everything right. i should get an stc of say 60+. which would be heaven for me.
You won't get that until you finish the inner leaf. Your existing single leaf will get you maybe STC-30-something, if you are lucky, and beef it up really well.
anyway this situation is only temp until the inner room is built. then 2 sheets of 5/8" drywall with green glue.
... and on a separate decoupled stud frame!!! Or else you won't get STC-60 even then! If you have two separate frames, each with two layers of 5/8 drywall on one side, and a 10-inch air gap between the leaves, and isolation filling the entire cavity, and everything perfectly sealed, then theoretically you could get around STC-65 or better. Unless you put a door or window in, of course, in which case your overall isolation is defined by the door or window. Getting STC-65 in a door or window takes some doing!!!! - Stuart -
