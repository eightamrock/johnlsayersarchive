Hi Vc! WELCOME! Wow! It's great to have a new comer who read the rules, did their homework, and posted carefully. That doesn't happen too often. Great first post! :yahoo: Regarding your space: I'm assuming that the live room is the part with the brick wall and arched windows, while the booth is the enclosed room on the left, and that all of it already exists. Correct? If so, then your room orientation should be facing that bench thing and the recess thing, next to the "sticking-out" thing. Is that a closet? fireplace? What is it? Whatever it is, it's going to be a problem... The bad news is that your room dimensions are are very nearly multiples of each other (203 cm wide, 414cm long), but you say that you can't change that. The other bad news is that "sticking out" thing that destroys your room symmetry. If you cannot remove it, then you'll have to fill in the recess next to it somehow, so that your front wall extends all the way across the room evenly. Without that you cannot get symmetry, and of course without symmetry you cannot get an accurate stereo image with good balance, so your mixes will never translate well. That would be my first priority: Even out the front wall. Also, is your ceiling really 3 meters high? That's unusual, but good.
I don't have a sound level meter,
Not life-and-death, but useful. You can get them used on e-bay for maybe 30 euros and up. You don't HAVE to have one, but they are useful.
The control room has laminate flooring (just the regular everyday bog standard fit yourself type thing) and the live room/booth has very thin carpet on the floor.
Those are probably fine to start with, and you don't have much budget, so you can most likely just leave them as-is.
I would firstly like to know where these panels should be placed.
Standard initial treatment is as follows: - Bass traps in as many corners as you can do. A normal room has 12 corners. In your case it looks like the only corners available to you are the wall/ceiling corners, but you have high ceilings so there should be lots of good space up there for you to do that. - Absorption behind the speakers, on the front wall, to help deal with early reflections and comb filtering. - Thick absorption on all first reflection points, usually meaning both side walls and a cloud on the ceiling. - Thick absorption on the rear wall.
I am unsure of how to mount my speakers. I currently have the workstation fitted (as for instance a kitchen worktop would be). Should I elevate them on any particular type of stand, or mount them to the wall?
Standard recommended speaker geometry is to have them mounted such that the acoustic axes are at your ear height when seated at the desk, which means they should be 1.2m above the floor (note! that's the ACOUTIC AXIS that is 1.2 m above the floor, and NOT the top or bottom of the speaker. Big difference, in some cases). The speakers should be set apart about 1.5m to 2.5m from each other, and angled such that the acoustic axes meet about 20 cm behind the middle of your head at an angle of 60°: In other words, the speakers are perfectly aligned with and aimed at your ears. Your head and the speakers should be roughly at the corners of an equilateral triangle. Meaning that your head is about the same distance away from each speaker as the speakers are away from each other. Your head should also be at roughly 38% of the distance form the front wall to the back wall. Ideally the speakers should be soffit mounted (flush mounted). Second choices: The speakers should be at least 2m away from all walls. Third choice: the speakers should be right up against the front wall, with as little gap as possible. You don't have the budget for #1, and the room is too small for #2, so you will have to go with #3. Do not attach the speakers to the walls! Bad idea. That will couple them to the wall. Instead, put them on really heavy, massive stands (many people get large hollow metal stands and fill them with sand or concrete). The speakers should be decoupled from the stands.
As you will see, the left corner behind the workstation has a recess, which makes wall mounting slightly difficult.
Yup. That will have to be fixed, as I mentioned. You cannot work like that.
In the control room, we have a laminate floor, should we keep this, or switch to carpet, or use a mixture of both?
Laminate is excellent! Do not use carpet. Gneral rule is "hard floor, soft ceiling".
The live room/ booth is currently carpeted, do you think this needs to be changed and introduce laminate/hardwood floor? (considering there are foam acoustic panels on the ceiling)
If your budget will extend that for, then yes, go for it! But if not, then you can live with it for now, and do something else later. One cheap but ugly option might be to just lay a thick sheet of MDF, plywood or OSB on top of the carpet.
but would like the scope for recording multiple vocals as well as instruments such as drums, percussion and guitar amps too,
Drums? :shock: That room isn't very big, and drums are VERY loud. You probably can do it (and you do have high ceilings, which is great for micing drums well), but you might need to extend your budget a bit. And warn your neighbors....
Considering the instruments I would like to record, would you suggest bass traps are necessary for the live room? What other treatments, if any, would you suggest for the live room/ booth?
Checkerboard absorption. Thick. Meaning that wherever you have a hard surface on one side, but a large thick soft absorber (4" or even 6" of 703) right across on the opposite wall: So put a panel of 703 in the middle of the wall across from the window, then put 703 panels on the wall around the window itself (above, below, both sides). Put a panel of 703 right opposite the door, and more panels on the wall around the door itself. I would probably try to do bass trapping in at least two corners, if you are going to be doing drums in there. Ideal would be angled slot walls, but you don't have the budget. That would be my approach. Others might have different ideas. - Stuart -

"the speakers should be right up against the front wall, with as little gap as possible." Say what? I like your three options, but they are only options that apply to specific footprints, and there are always other options. Wes Lachots' 38% guide is something we all agree is a good starting place (thanks for that Wes), the typical 12/14 foot deep bedroom, which is where many are attempting to establish a mix environment, will establish 7 1/4" and 8 5/8" respectively(using an equilateral triangle) as the distance from the wall the monitors will be. As the room gets deeper the distance does the same. And then there are backside ported monitors to consider.
