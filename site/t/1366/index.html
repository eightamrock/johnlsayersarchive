Jai21 February 2004#2
Are you to scale? If so, what is the size of that vocal booth? Sure looks small. The hangers in the back are fine. But it looks as if you are making them about 4 foot deep since they are the same depth as your Iso Booth. Thats not a bad thing, just using a lot of space. With such low ceilings this will be your only sub traps in the room. You could angle and share with the Iso booth, giving you various depth in the trap for dif. Freq. Values and traping. I'd angle the wall in the Iso booth that is next to your hanging traps so you dont have two like angle (avoid Flutter echo) walls. you could angle the wall into the back half of your hanging traps by about 18 - 24", fill it with hanging traps that vari in width then frame out a new fake straight wall where you have the one now and 703/fabric cover it. This would give you some great bass traping in the Iso booth without sacraficing space in your room. I guess I am a bass trap freak. I have worked in a lot of rooms that where built great but Hanging traps left out. It doesn't make for a very flat sounding and feeling room. That's about my 2 cents. John over in the design side is the best bet for real world angles and design ideas for the rest of the room. He seems to find a whole nother Iso rooms worth of space when he redesigns for ya :D Hope this has helped. jai www.themixstudio.com

dymaxian22 February 2004#4
That's actually a pretty good design. Using the notch in the back is a pretty good idea, I think- since you've gotta shape the back that way for the stairs, what better to put back there than bass trapping? And in a space of that shape, yeah hangers are the best thing to put there. You could close that area off with 703 covered with fabric, but if you get a lot of people coming thru there, you may want to protect it from someone putting their hands thru it (I plan on using expanded metal sheet for this, like the front of a guitar cabinet- Jai gave some good advice regarding this). Anyway, same thing for the area in front. If you have a fan pulling air out of the computer box, you may want to put a supply and return air duct into there; even with just one fan moving air around, this'll make your computer much more comfortable. And yeah, use the remainder of that space for hangers/bass trapping. Having odd notches and alcoves in your room isn't a bad thing. Just try to keep them symmetrical around the long axis of the room, so your stereo image remains equal. Since both the oddities of your room design are right on the centerline, this should be easy for you. Looks pretty good so far! Kase www.minemusic.net
