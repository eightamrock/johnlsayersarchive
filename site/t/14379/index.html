Forrest13 May 2010#1
Hi fellas, I have an old service station that I'm converting to a studio .lovely place. semi-rural setting. 4.2 metre high ceilings. fantastic for drums etc. Of course, being a service station its close to a highway (about 7m away) - and this one has 4 lanes of quite a bit of heavy truck traffic measuring peaks at about 80db. I started with the live room - which suffered from 2 old roller doors 3m x 3m wide and leaking so much air that it was indistinguishable from being outside. hard even to talk... I removed the roller doors and bricked them up with acoustic maxi bricks to be flush with the external 20cm thick ferrocrete wall ( an old school construction style - a sandwich of thick render, concrete, timber and steel frame). I then rendered the external brick wall with about 30mm of render. I then built an internal brick wall to the ceiling that joins to the side walls (not the wall facing the highway). I left a 100mm cavity and filled with rockwool. This is quite a big wall. 7m long and 4.2 high with a brick pier 1m wide filled with concrete in the middle to keep it up. the wall depth now is about 50cm out to 70 cm at the pier. It has about 8 tons of bricks in it, plus another couple of tons of mortar and render.. Thankfully its done a great job on the road. I figured I had a big problem so I went with the most solid construction style I could use and went with the highest STC ratings I could find. I haven't pulled out my mics to test ( too much dust around ) but I'd estimate that even without having done my ceiling yet that I've knocked out between 50-60db. Maybe more. Normal speech now drowns out the loudest trucks. Ceiling is next. I'm hoping it will get to be near silent in there. I'm still a bit concerned that it won't be enough. Thing is , when I've finished my ceiling I'd like to do a little more to the big front wall (as well as build a cavity/drywall inside the other external ferrocrete walls. My plan is to use the cavity between the internal pier and the brick returns ( about 200mm) to stuff with insulation and put up a stud frame with 2 layers of drywall. Then it would all be flush. (see attachment) This is most definitely a triple leaf, I fear. I've read every post I can on it but it seems to be something that depends on the amount of mass and the width of the cavity. I'm wondering if the sheer mass of my wall and the doubling of the size of the internal cavity would do the trick and make the resonance/bass loss less severe. What do you think? If the addition of a new cavity is a problem what would be the best solution to give my wall just a bit more stopping power? Just add more mass to the wall? Is there an advantage in rendering the internal wall as well ? Perhaps doing it in reverse- with drywall attached straight to the internal brick wall and then hang absorbers on it. Any benefit in using resilient channel here ? I fear with such a massive wall that I'm in a position of diminishing returns but I'd like to achieve something that will a) kill anything that may still be getting through. b) look better than the internal bricks c) tame a bit of internal inflections. any suggestions?

Soundman202013 May 2010#4
This is most definitely a triple leaf, I fear. I've read every post I can on it but it seems to be something that depends on the amount of mass and the width of the cavity.
Yep, it would be a triple leaf for sure, but in your case triple-leaf isn't something I'd be worried about: You have so much mass in that wall that it is probably a moot point, and adding a few layers of drywall to it is not going to do anything much to change that. The resonant frequency of that wall is already way down low enough (about 6 Hz., theoretically) that a few sheets of drywall isn't going to trash it. (You'd have to add something like 40 (yes, forty!) layers of drywall just to equal the mass of one leaf of that existing wall.) So I agree totally with John: Deal with your roof, which is doubtless your weak point right now, followed by doors and windows. Then if you still need more isolation afterwards, just build an entire separate de-coupled inner leaf using a couple of layers of drywall, and that should take care of pretty much all noises, short of an atomic bomb right outside your front door! - Stuart -

John Sayers17 May 2010#11
Forest - I'd replace the iron on the roof and I'd make sure it sits on foil backed insulation to reduce the rain noise. I would not consider the iron as a leaf as it will have airflow under the eaves. Then I'd remove the fibro joining pieces and plaster straight over the fibro with a double layer of 16mm Gyprock Fyrchek. That with the brick becomes your outer leaf. I'd paint the brick internally to seal it. Then build a separate structure within that outer shell for your studio.
