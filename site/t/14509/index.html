Hi Kayle, Just a couple of quick comments. If this was me, the first thing I would do is take some actual acoustical measurements of what is going on in the room. Get a feel for what's happening at your listening position. Secondly I would absolutely prioritize what I want to use the room for. If primarily this is a room for creation, and your most creative with the piano at your fingertips and looking out the window as you work, then leave the room set up as is, treat what you can, live with the remaining issues. If it is most important to you to have mixes that translate well and a fairly accurate room, some major compromises will have to be made. $200 will not a great room make, but it will make it sound better. For the most part, the regulars here want to build the best rooms they can. They want what they mix to translate well, they want to take the room out of the equation as much as possible. Decide how high a priority this is to you. Now some actual advice. Treat your first reflection points with absorption (703) including your ceiling. Turn the closet into a bass trap and change out your flooring to something more reflective. The elephant in your room (piano) is going to be an issue for mixing no matter what you do (unless you want to pull it out of the room every time you want to mix :D ). Regards, Steve
