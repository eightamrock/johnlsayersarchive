dymaxian24 February 2004#2
Greetings! Looks like that'll be a nice space, but you'll need a bunch of absorption- the exterior walls look like concrete or CMU, which will be great for keeping noise out. The best spot to put them is on the narrow end. The idea being that the sound bouncing off the walls will tend to head for the back of the room, behind you. There you put as much absorption as you can. If you angle the ceiling, the same theory comes into play- low end should be over the speakers and listening position. Good luck! Kase www.minemusic.net

When side walls are angled only a little, as Jay intends, the angle is not enough to deflect early reflections away from the mix position.
well it is enough in fact. you must remember that it's the top end we are talking about and in most speakers systems this is directional - i.e. a 30 degree splay. If you follow the line from a 30 degree splay - i.e. 15 degrees either side of axis you will note that it does in fact reflect the main signal behind you. By reversing the room this factor is defeated IMO. cheers JOhn

dymaxian28 February 2004#9
Carl, that's a heavily debated subject. Most of the folks around here agree on a 12 degree difference between the sidewalls to eliminate flutter echoes, so angling each wall 6 degrees off will be sufficient for that. That applies mostly to tracking rooms, tho. For control rooms, the splayed sidewalls are meant to push sound to the back of the room to be absorbed by something behind you. There, 15 degrees is the target. Kase www.minemusic.net

dymaxian28 February 2004#11
So, it's 7.5 degrees, or greater, for each wall. Correct?
Nope. 15 degrees per wall, for a 30 degree total splay, according to John's post earlier in the thread. That is for RFZ design as much as anything else. If you have the space/layout available for that, great, but if not, do what you can. Even just eliminating flutter echoes will make a difference. The other thing you can do is build side-wall panels as shown on the Wall Units page- those will push reflections to the back wall where they can be absorbed. Having the rear wall reflective is tricky. Are you going to be tracking drums near that wall? That's one of the only reasons I'd think you'd want it to not be absorbtive... Steve (knightfly) posted a particular distance for first reflections, but I forget what it is... anyway, if sound can reflect off the back wall and make it to your ears within a certain distance, it'll cause comb filtering. I think it's close to 30ft, but even then I'd be cautious with it. You can either build the switchable wall panels on the SAE site (which will swing open or shut to change reflective to absorptive) or just put up bare plywood gobos when you're tracking in there, and take 'em down when you mix. Kase www.minemusic.net
