New member from East Tennessee, USA. Hello everyone. I have no pictures or floor plans to submit...yet. Getting ready to start building a studio in a recently finished basement addition to my house. The space is 28x30 and is a blank canvas at this time. Does anyone have experience using metal C studs in lieu of wood studs for framing purposes for isolation rooms? In my occupation, I can get that material quite easily, and it allows any batt insulation placed between the studs to be nearly continuous (rather than being separated by the thickness of the wood stud). However, they may be worse in terms of allowing sound transmission through the stud itself. I don't want to create lots of problems because I wanted to save some money on materials, but if they would work, and they are free to me...then why not? Thank you, Dan Odom

Dan - Welcome to the forum. I'm smack dab in the middle of my build and I'm using metal track/studs. They work great. But metal studs are working out great for me. Also, being a carpenter myself, I have plenty of experience, but building a studio has a lot of specialized techniques and materials. Lots of "conventional" building practices are opposite with studios. I recommend picking up the book written by Rod Gervais form Amazon. Rod's a senior member on this forum and has picked out and save me from making some pretty hefty mistakes. So best of luck with the build my friend! Read as much as you can in the reference sections, and if you have any further questions, create "build-thread" and contain them all that thread. Then we can all have the pleasure of watching your build along with you.

Rod Gervais 24 January 2008#5
The bottom line is that light guage metal studs work well in single frame construction (without using RC-1) acheiving the same TL values as 2x4 frames using the resilient channel. Thin guage (25 ga or less) metal studs in a double wall configuration will not acheive the same low frequency TL values as double frame 2x4 walls (and double framed 2x6 @ 24" o.c. are better than 2x4 @ 16" o.c.). Heavy guage metal studs (20 ga or heavier) give the same basic TL values as wood studs based on the comments above as regards spacing. Rod
