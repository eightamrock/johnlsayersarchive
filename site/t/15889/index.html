gullfo26 April 2011#2
you probably would want a bunch more of those because i think i drew them about 2'6" wide so your listening space is only around 5' wide... but you could so that. i would (if it's in a much bigger room) space them apart about 6" or so like this. vary up the slat/slot spacing/sizes and plan on adding some absorption around the larger room to keep resonances down. overhead - a cloud or two can help as well. sound operates in 3D so the space about above the clouds can have hanging baffles to temper the resonances there.

Yes, the ceiling is the roof sheathing, it has a flat part, farthest from the camera in the pic to the beam farthest from the cam. From there is slopes up gradually (2:12 slope maybe, have not measured, you can see the angle at the ceiling joist ends). The retail space is kind of behind the camera, it is heated with radiators and water. The forced air unit can be seen for the "big" room. Yes, it gets cold in there in the winter, and warm in summer. The place is old and leaks enough air to get fresh stuff. Sadly, it is not up to me to do as I want, the boss comes before me, and the landlord before that. Behind the wall on the left (the stage wall) is about 8-10 more feet of space, the boss put the wall up and has a small shop, vocal booth, behind there (the drum riser goes into it too). The room sounds good for live performances, which is it's main function. We (I) am slowly going to try and get it modified and "spruced up" so it will look a little more slick and function better for both live shows AND recording. Short term is to just get a good space for listening as I have just started to learn mix downs and my balances sound so different from mixing to listening in the car, on the stereo, etc. Then on to mastering lessons. We have our first paying customers recorded and next is mixing and mastering. We want a good product at a low price. Once money starts trickling in, more things can be done. Personally, I'd like to gut it, get rid of the posts, and start from there, but that is for another day (and being able to get a structural engineer to look at it). I would put up interior walls about 5 1/2" off the cold exterior brick walls (that would be, brick wall, 5 1/2" space, then 3 1/2" stud, for a total of 9" from inside of drywall to brick wall) and have them sprayed with 2" of "Walltite" closed cell foam, then fill the rest with Roxul or fiberglass (behind and between the studs). The ceiling would get the same Walltite/Roxul treatment. But do I need to drywall the ceiling? Can't I just vapour barrier it and cover it with fabric for a soft ceiling?
