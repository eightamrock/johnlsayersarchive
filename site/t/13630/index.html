Hello, I am considering building an addition on my house to be used as a recording studio/practice space. The area available is about 23 feet by 25 feet. The ceiling will be 10 feet. The dimensions for the studio room are close to the 3rd Sepmeyer ratio. The room will also need to be used as a master bedroom if we sell the house, so there will be a full bathroom included. I dont want a separate control room, so it will be one big room with a couple of iso booths on the side. I will probably use double wall or staggered stud construction with 2 layers of 5/8" drywall and green glue. Im also considering some sort of resilient channel or hat channel on clips to increase the isolation. It will be built on a slab. It will have its own HVAC unit. If I could get enough isolation to be able to have a rock band practice/record in the evening without driving the house occupants or neighbors crazy, that would be ideal, but its not an absolute requirement. I could restrict the really loud stuff to the daytime if necessary. The budget for the whole thing is about $60,000. My questions are: 1. Are there any obvious problems with the layout? 2. Are there any suggestions to improve the layout? Its a blank slate now, except for the outside 23x25 dimensions. 3. Do you think the soundproofing/isolation will be sufficient for rock band practice at night? Thanks! --Byra

xSpace2 October 2009#3
"The budget for the whole thing is about $60,000." Awesum! What does it mean when you say "I cant add drywall layers to the inside of the existing wall, so its going to have to stay 1 layer." ? You should remove the interior layer of sheetrock, at least. You either lose the interior leaf or make it an inside-out type wall. A three leaf wall results in LF reduction and in this case an inside-out wall would too but the latter of the two being more effective from a material/man hour/acoustically useful perspective. Biggest issue is that isolation numbers aren't going to get too high with a coupled two leaf wall, that is your existing wall, yes?

TomVan2 October 2009#5
Hey Byra, You're getting it. Yes remove the T-111 from the exterior. Now think about this!! First off you "need" to beef up the one layer of sheet rock inside the living room. Two ways to do this One. cut strips of sheet rock wide enough to fit between the studs and caulk or Are you going to employ Sheet rock installers to hang, tape and mud? If so it would be much easier and less time consuming to hang a new layer on the interior side of your living room. Have it professionally finished and that way your wife can choose a new color for that wall since it will need to be painted :lol: and its only six sheets to hang. If you had to cut and fit strips from the (existing) outside you would have to cut and hang over 19 pieces. Just a thought Peace Tom

If the roof line of the existing structure will have the addition roof line tied into it (in any way) then the rebar/epoxy/solid connection is standard and there is no deviation. And you being under the watchful eye of Dade County code enforcement will most likely encounter other things that seem less studio like and more residential like. You would have to make the space a separate area, completely to get outside of the hard connection you have to submit to in the slab.
