Hi I am new to this forum but have been reading it for a while now and find it unbelieveably useful! My question is :- Is polystyrene sheets approx 2" thick any good for acoustic insulation within a stud wall, I'm building a small studio just for my own use and wondered what material would be best for both sound and heat insulation. BTW I am meaning sound isolation, I am not going to be using the room for drums mainly for monitoring and recording some live guitar. Thanks.
knightfly8 June 2006#2
No. Polystyrene is closed cell insulation, fine for heat but SUCKS for sound, either inside a wall or outside. You want nearly anything that has the word "wool" in it - rockwool, mineral wool, sheep wool, fiberglass wool, rigid fiberglass, slagwool, etc - inside a wall you want UNDER 3 pounds per cubic foot density, or (metric) 48 kG/m^3. If you can't BLOW through it, throw it away... Steve

Would Polystyrene or (EPS expanded polystyrene) be a spring in construction, it's a closed cell foam so would this be a soft layer in a leaf, of a spring? I ask this because the american ingenuity domes, i was considering have pre-built panels with EPS in between concrete and gypsum wallboard.
sharward12 June 2006#7
It would be considered more of a "wimpy mass" -- i.e., a solid in the sense that air doesn't pass through it, and since it's firmly connected to the other materials and most likely not compressed the "ideal" amount so as to form a spring, it pretty much increases the thickness of the outer leaf without increasing attenuation any noticable amount. That's just a guess, of course, since there probably aren't any published specifications or tests on it in this regard. --Keith :mrgreen:
knightfly14 June 2006#8
pre-built panels with EPS in between concrete and gypsum wallboard. This would be considered a single leaf of mass for isolation purposes - and Keith's comments are good; the EPS shouldn't HURT anything, but won't add any noticeable mass to the leaf. You could assume the same effectiveness as using just the concrete and gypsum as far as sound isolation is concerned, and should be really close... Steve
