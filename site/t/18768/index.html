stevev6 February 2014#3
hey Garry, if it's purely a rehearsal space and not intended for recording then at least you don't have to go over the top with the acoustic treatment on the internal shell. that's the good news. at 110db (and thats probably being conservative with amps, bass and drums) any kind of wooden external shell won't get you anywhere near the kind of isolation you need for people to not hear you outside the room. Unless it's possibly 24 inch thick solid oak.
splinterbungee wrote:
Build a stud wall (can this be attached directly to the wooden shed wall, or will I need some kind of neoprene pads)
If you couple the two walls together you will not have a two leaf wall. you will need a two leaf wall....and ceiling. If you aren't sure about what a two leaf wall is then have a look in Johns manual and you'll get the idea. Alsio, think of the ceiling as another wall, it has to be built with the same concepts in mind.
stevev6 February 2014#4
splinterbungee wrote:
Am I best of leaving the walls at the original 45mm and spending the extra on better quality soundproofing?
I think you might be confusing soundproofing (better known as 'isolation') with acoustic treatment.
splinterbungee wrote:
The cabin I linked to can also come with a "dualtherm" option of 2 x 28mm walls with a air cavity of 70mm.
It sounds like this is already a two-leaf construction in itself, and also that these two leafs are already connected. this is not good for isolation (which is what you need). you need two leafs, completely decoupled in a mechanical sense. no nails, screws, bracing, anything.
splinterbungee wrote:
The genie clip system is the same as above (with similar results) but more expensive?
whilst this will help in the kind of coupled two leaf construction that this cabin appears to be, it will still not get you anywhere near the isolation you require.
splinterbungee wrote:
Is this whole project is even possible
yes......is it possible to do it the way you are planning on going about it at the moment.....no. At the moment you'll spend your $7k and end up with the same isolation you would in the spare bedroom of your house. You really need to read through a lot more of the threads on this forum to grasp the details of what you're trying to accomplish. Start thinking more along the lines of a concrete slab, with brick walls, no windows, one door, several layers of marine ply sealed roofing under asphalt shingles and you'll have the kind of external shell you need to START with to rehearse a full rock band in. after that comes the internal build of your two leaf construction.

splinterbungee wrote:
The cabin hasn't been bought yet but would have thought building a brick building would swallow up the budget before I even started any soundproofing?
you're probably right in terms of budget there. It will cost a LOT to build a brick building. What you're missing is that the brick building IS your soundproofing (or part thereof in a two-leaf system).
splinterbungee wrote:
I think i'll have a proper look into another kind of shell which would fit the budget, if its possible.
Whatever kind of shell you build it will need to have a lot of mass. Mass (in a wall or ceiling or floor) is one way of impeding a soundwave from transmitting it's energy to the other side of that (wall,ceiling,floor). Have a search around for material densitys and you'll find brick/concrete to be amongst the highest density and most easily constructed for an external shell.
splinterbungee wrote:
However as its been noted, it isn't good value if it doesn't do what I want it to do.

splinterbungee wrote:

And it's a really great move to start here :D You'll get advice form people who know what they're talking about because they've done it before (and made the mistakes) so you won't have to re-invent the wheel to get the result you want. It won't always be the advice you want to hear but it will be from people who don't want you to throw a load of money at something and not end up with what you want. Be prepared though...you will throw a crap-load of money at it to do it right, which is where I think you might be underestimating your budget. And just to give you a real world example...my studio is an external brick wall shell 110mm thick, then there's an air gap of another 120mm filled with heavy fibreglass insulation, and then there's two layers of 16mm plasterboard (all properly constructed and airtight sealed). If you're standing close to the building you can still hear a kick drum outside....only just though 8)
