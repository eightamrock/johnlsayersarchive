camistan4 March 2007#2
I don't know about anyone else, but in all the years I've worked in or been in different studios, I have yet to hear a drummer playing full steam and not hear a "lick" from a drummer's booth to a control room. The degree of exactly how much you hear a drummer playing will vary from studio to studio I would think. One place may have 1 sheet of 1/2" drywall all around a drum booth with no acoustic caulking and another studio's drum booth may have concrete walls with all sealing done with acoustical caulk and a thick double pane glass to see him with. Guess which one you'll probably hear more??? Simply put, to me, how much you hear depends on the construction of the booth and even the soundroom. They both need to have adequate mass, air space and to be sealed properly. So, in Stan's humble opinion...In the "AVERAGE" studio (not one of those multi-million dollar jobs) it's normal to hear "some" sounds from a drummer between a drum booth and a control room.

sharward4 March 2007#4
Four windows = bad... That's four leaves. You want two. Two windows made of LAMINATED glass that are at least 1/3 as thick as the sum total thickness of each wall not counting studs = good. For example, if the walls have three layers of 5/8" drywall each, then the laminated glass should be at least 5/8" thick. --Keith :mrgreen:
tmix4 March 2007#5
Yes it is more usual than not to hear a bit of drum leakage outside the drum booth unless there is a bunch of mass, AND some airgap. Some places have the control booth on the other side of a main room from the drum booth. In that case you may not hear the drums in the control booth, but wherever the drums are sharing a common wall with the drum booth, it is tough to get enough mass between them, particularly with the glass. Most studios, you would be extremely lucky to get over 65 decibels isolation through the glass, when the normal drummer is playing at or over 100 decibels.... you can hear it. Tom
len-morgan4 March 2007#6
If you think about it, it doesn't matter one bit whether you can hear the drums or not. The question is if other MICROPHONES that are recording can "hear" them. Even a cheap cardiod or figure 8 mic should have about a 20dB loss in the null areas so even if you are recording more than just the drums, mic positioning should be able to cut any bleed. Another thing to remember about your monitor speakers getting back into the drum mics is that if you have any isolation at all, if the mic drums can hear your monitors in the control room, your monitors are turned up too loud! Also, the walls and glass that the drum sound had to travel through to get to the control room have to be passed through AGAIN to get back to the drum mics. It's been my experience that most drum mics are not that sensitive because they are subject to such high SPLs that in order to get the transients when the drums are hit, they sacrifice the sensitivity on the quiet end. On the other hand, if got a couple of cheap MXL mics that can pick up a fart through the walls of 3 rooms! And I've got pretty good walls. len
