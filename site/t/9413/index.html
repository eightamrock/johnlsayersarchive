Have I done this all wrong

Started by jtcoops on 14 September 2007. 9 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 9413.

jtcoops14 September 2007#1
Hello. Great forum - wish I'd found it sooner! 3 months ago I started work on converting a single story terraced industrial unit into a small studio here in West London, UK. The live room measures 5m x 4m, and the control room 7m x 4m. The building is brick walls and concrete floor. The walls are brick inner, 4" cavity and brick outer. The roof is a weak point, basically hardboard and bitumen on timber rafters (I had been told it was concrete, but its not!) I had some mis-information when I started the live room work, and as a result it is not a "room within a room". It has 75mm Rockwool (RW3), resilient bars and 2 x layers of 12mm thick gypsum green glued and caulked. Therefore the walls all round are 2xlayers green glued drywall on bars with rockwool before the 9" brick cavity walls. The ceiling at the moment is 2 layers of 12mm drywall, green glued and caulked, with 75mm rockwool and then the top hardboard and bitumen. The dividing wall between the live and control room is 3 layers of GG'd drywall on resilient bars one side and 2 layers of drywall on the other. The window is 2 x double glazed units (4 layers in total). Problem is drums are still way too loud for the neighbours. Kick drum and cymbals are the main problem, toms and snare are pretty well dampened, although the room has no internal acoustic treatment just yet - didnt want to do anything until I was sure enough plasterboard was up. What more can I do without running up huge costs to combat the noise? I think most of the sound leakage is from the control room side - as too much noise is passing through the dividing wall, but the kick drum really thumps and can be heard 3 neighbours down. Budget wise I guess up to £1,000.

cyeazel14 September 2007#2
Hey man. I hate to say this but it sounds as if there really isn't going to be a cheap way to fix your problem. It's no mystery that the kick drum sound is your biggest problem. That's because lots of mass and properly decoupled room within room construction is needed to stop low end and bass. It sounds like you may have actually created some resonance issues because of the way your room has been built which actually amplify low end sounds. If you're willing, there are many on this forum that can help you fix the problem as long as you provide enough info and are patient and are willing to spend the extra cash.

jtcoops1 October 2007#10
These pics hopefully show how I'm trying to reduce flanking. The open ceiling space between live and control room has thick T50 barrier matting suspended from the joists. There is then 2xlayers of 12mm drywall. There is then a box made of 12mm drywall, filled with rockwool and with sunken lights built in. Therefore sound hits the box, is absorbed a bit in the rockwool, hits the double layer plaster and is absorbed a bit more in the T50. I'm going to add mass loaded vinyl to the tops of the walls and ceiling in an effort to prevent sound escaping via the roof. Any other ideas for keeping drum noise under control?
