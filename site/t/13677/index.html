Hi everybody! I am a couple of days away from finishing this long work in the basement to create an acceptable space for tracking. My main concern now is "soundproofing" the room as much as possible in a low budget way, but load of free work. Here is the previous post I did: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=13151 By now, by breaking down the concrete of the floor (it was all cracked and terribly uneven anyway) I now have a 7 feet floor (from compacted earth to gypsum in between the joists). I had a load of 36” wide ciment compactor track made out of 1/2” rubber that am going to install as the room’s floor. I think it’s going to be a good sound absorber, won’t it? Now I am about to start mounting the walls on existing frames. Here are my main questions for this exciting operation: Concrete walls In this room, as you can see on the pictures below, there is two fondation walls made of concrete. Here are the different possibilities I figured out: - Leaving the concrete walls “naked”. No drywalls, no resilient channels, nothing. I don’t think this would be the best option since the concrete on one of those walls is very falky so I might have to take off concrete bits off the floor all the time. Also, I’d like to put on isolation (pink fiberglass) for confort, on the walls (I’d like to save the more expensive roxul for the empty walls since I believe concrete walls don’t need further sound isolation). - Installing drywall (1 or 2 layers of 1/2”?) directly on the frame (no resilient channels). My concern is that the wall frame is attached both to the concrete and to the ceiling joists... - Installing drywall (1 or 2 layers of 1/2”?) with RC. Empty walls: I am more sure about what I should for the 4 other walls (my room has a slighly “L” shape) : exterior of these single frame walls would be ½” drywall directly on the walls frames. Interior would be 2 layers of ½” on RC channel with Roxul inbetween wood studs. Does it make sense?
