xSpace3 September 2010#6
Well, the concrete creates the coupling. Imagine if you will that the noise level you rated at 90 dB create a lot of vibration, airborne and structure borne, using the road as the structure in this instance. To mitigate the hard connections the engineer would have to build this in such a way as to resemble a typical sky scraper, e.g. hard footers/spring/column, which may or may not be code in your neck of the world. You have to decouple the structure from the offending vibration. Then the designer has to calculate and explain how much weight your build will require for the interior mass PLUS the actual structure to assign the dead/live loading required, tons will be required. And the placement on the interior footprint will be key to a successful and efficient distribution of the load in your build. The design of this will not be something that anyone can tell you here. I said that already but you refuse to understand what it is that you are asking and how impossible it is for people on a forum to explain to you how to do this. You need blueprints designed by someone in your area, engineered and with specifications applicable to your region. That plane and that simple. You tell your architect that you want a structure that can offset 90 decibel of air-borne ambient noise and all the vibrations that it brings and see what they say. They will tell you that you do not have the budget for it.
