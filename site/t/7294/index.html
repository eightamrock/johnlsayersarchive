rod gervais18 October 2006#12
OK, In the case of your walls - you are already using isolated construction - so (besides being installed wrong) you don't want RC because it will actually degrade the wall systems. In the ceiling (however) it's a different situation. The intent of RC is to try to duplicate isolated constuction - so there is a physical decoupling of the structures that takes place. Although the green glue is a magnificent product - and I use it and reccomend it to my clients all of the time - it is not doing the same thing. So on the ceiling - GG without RC will not decouple the above and below - and flanking directly through the structure will still take place. My best advice would be to utilize both in your case - and don't be misled by the reported STC - because first of all - it has to do with an assembly as a whole - and 2nd - it is not indicative of the amount of LF isolation you gain - because it never measures those frequencies as a part of the equation. Sincerely, Rod

Bass drum hits sound nicer (most of the time, depending on a lot of variables and arguably) if we pump up the 80 Hz EQ on channel or main EQ. 80 hz typcially makes a lot of difference in EQ'n a bass drum. BUT that does not been the bass drum hits at 80 hz, the peak frequency of the bass drum could be anywhere in the low end (depending on size and tuning) and then harmonic frequencies are added above and below that peak mostly in octaves. IF bass drum peak is t 80hz then 160 hz (x2), 320 hz (x4) and 40 hz (divide by 2) are also strong frequencies which will effect sound. You could hear this on a well tuned tom with a long ring, the attack is a lower pitch and the ring has the upper harmonics in decay of hit. On a guitar harmonics... 1st one is at half way from neck to bridge, place finger on string at 12 fret not pressing on fret board, hit string and you get a pitch 1 octave above the open string... other harmonics are all over the string and ring with an open or fretted hit, giving the guitar part of it's color of sound. OK I am babbling... point is your bass drum and other drums hits peaks at certian frequencies (concert tuned snare is F#) and other frequencies related to harmonics and other variables are produced. If you were to add a sub harmonic (noise maker) proccesor to your bass drum or mix (BBE, DBX and others make hardware versions, many plugins are available) you could with recorded or micked drums accent let's say 40hz, although almost useless 20 hz and make other rumbly noises. Hope that helps, BTW we are talking about the frequencies your drums play, unless you live next to an airport and need to keep that noise out. Marc

kindohm wrote:
Also, what you say about being misled by the reported STC is a good point. However what about transmission loss in dB? Are dB's measured at the low freqs? GreenGlueCompany.com presents a number of charts in their comparison of GG and TC and the dB difference at the LF levels appears significant: Are the Green Glue folks really measuring dB's at the LF's? Or are they making that data up? Am I thinking too hard about something that in the end won't make a huge difference?
An STC rating is essentially the weighted average of noise loss through a barrier across a range of frequencies ( 125Hz to 4Khz) - they can (and did) measure TL loss above and below that range - BUT - when they report the STC rating for the wall - THOSE VALUES OUTSIDE OF THE 125Hz TO 4kHz ARE NOT TAKEN INTO THE EQUATION. Thus you could have a vry significant increase in TL value at 100Hz that would not affect the STC rating at all. That was my only point. As to the question of RC on the walls or not - I wouldn't bother in your case - I would (as I suggested above) just stick with the ceiling - and make absolutely sure that you completely seal all of the wall / ceiling layers EVERYWHERE to stop sound leakage through any potentiasl air passages. Rod
