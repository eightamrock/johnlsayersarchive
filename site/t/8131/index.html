Greetings from Chicago - Go Bears! Slight repost here but with much more detail... While having a structural analysis completed on my attic project I'm throwing around partitioning ideas. Just finished Rod G.'s book and have met with my contractor. It's a long 40' space with a ~17' wide floor under eaves that angle up to a 7'3" false ceiling. My intention is to open the space to the peak of the roof as much as possible and possibly out to the edge of the floor area. One of my major concerns is shrinking ceiling level once the floor joists are increased by 2" and the drywall closes in from the top. There currently are lath and plaster walls - probabaly 1". We need at least half the floor to be piano-worthy and, of course, be able to support the control room/iso booth walls. Hopefully lifting the false ceiling, even if it means soffiting some suport ties, will keep the space open enough to have some useful color... There are a couple of large dormer windows in the live area I want to preserve for light/character and treat with double walls & windows. In addition to a useful control/mix space I'd like to have a iso booth large enough for a jazz drumset for dual use as vocal booth. (Note: The chimney depicted in the .skp will be removed and relocated to a sidewalk in the back yard.) We'll be mainly doing classical or jazz projects and, fortunately I live on a relatively quiet cul-de-sac. Most noise is birds, jets, dog barking, occasional delivery truck. I assume, from my research to this point (with structural approval) I'll have my guy do the two layers of 5/8ths DW/Green Glue sandwich on RC2 hanging from the rafters. And decouple the walls of the various areas. Budget is $25-30K. By the way - is there an advantage to RC clips with hat channel vs. RC2? Is one less deep? Isolation from the second floor below is not too critical as it's just my wife and I and, very luckily, she's a musician, too. Hopefully we''ll save a little weight there. One of the reasons we chose this house was for the attic space studio possibilty. Now, of course, I'm getting nervous about the details. I realize we will have to compromise but I'd like to make the best use of the space possible. I've scanned the control room vs one big room debate but prefer the formal presentation of the split approach, particularly for client impression. I have to say I'm very pumped about this whole thing. It's kind of a dream-come-true for me to have a space to both to experiment freely and continue the production-oriented work I've been doing for years in other facilities. Although much better with staff paper, the piano and my DAW I've tried to work this in sketchup with limited success. Here are a couple of reasonably accurate representations (take the wacky increments >1" with a grain of salt.) I'm deeply grateful for suggestions or advice. The talent and experience on this board is impressive! Thanks, Brad W, Chicago http://RevengeoftheKissingBug.com
