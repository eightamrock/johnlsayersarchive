Hat Channel verses RC (Resilient Channnel) Please see pic on 1/2 way down this page: http://www.ta-inc.com/newshtml/rc.htm AND a quote from that page:
It is important to distinguish acoustically effective resilient channels from hat channels, z-channels, and other lightweight metal furring systems. These other systems may resemble resilient channels, but they afford no movement and are simply too rigid to be effective. Only resilient channels have any acoustical benefit.
Hat Channel has no holes in it, RC does which creates decoupling of wall board (drywall) from structure. There are more ways to decouple RC further (when done correctly). CEILING: I assumed that hat channel would be beneficial. I'm searching for answers, not questions.
Hat channel has no benefit - as already pointed out to you - and you really need to (as also pointed out to you) begin searching for the questions you don't know about yet - because other wise you will continue to throw money away. My best guess right now is that (unfortunately) you can't get from A to B without removing work already in place. You start with a task that's daunting to say the least - and begin with taking the worst possible step you could - with the creation of the 3 leaf wall system - and that with an outside leaf that has little mass and little isolation value. SO if you build a wall within - and add a layer of 5/8" drywall to it - you'll have (with the plaster) maybe an STC rating of 51......... remove the drywall and plaster and it will grow to about 57 - take the drywall in between the walls and move it to the inner-most face and it will become 60 - add a layer to the outside face and it will become around 60....... add another layer to the outside face and it will grow to around 63 - so a weighted average isolation of 63dB with the exterior sheathing - + 2 layers of exterior drywall + 2 layers of interior drywall. 5 layers total. Beyond that - you would add double the mass to the interior to gain another 5dB of isolation. 2nd scenerio - add the innner wall (leave the outside exactly like it is now) - add 2 layers of drywall to the inside face and you will have around an STC 54 - to gain 5 more dB in reduction you have to double the mass - so that means another 5 layers to the inside face buys you up to STC 59 - and then it would take another doubling (add 10 more layers) to reach 64. All this because of the center layer (the third leaf) so STC 63 = 5 layers - or STC 64 = 20 layers. You do the math. Now - This all assumes that flanking doesn't take over - and your deck - although decent from a construction point of view - is not isolatted - so it's a direct planking path to the outside world. Isolating the ceiling framing from the existing structure will help of course - but when everything is said and done - your entire assembly is only asd good as it's weakest point. I hope this helps you to understand what you're up against. Sincerely, Rod
