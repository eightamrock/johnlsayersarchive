Soundman20208 June 2010#2
1. Am I on the right track with these plans?
Not really. Your speakers should fire down the long axis of the room, not across the short axis, so rotate your design 90 degrees. Also, your mix position should be roughly 38% of the distance from the front wall to the rear wall, but your current design shows that your head would be almost up against the rear wall! :shock: Bad idea. Major bass issues if you do that. Once again, rotating your layout 90 degrees would fix that.
2. What DIY bass traps do you recommend me?
Superchunks are quick and easy to build, and very effective. Hangers are another good possibility (John loves them!).
3. Any other tips?
You seem to have a poly-cylindrical diffuser on the back wall. Your room isn't big enough for diffusers to work well, so I'd replace that with absorption. - Stuart -

Soundman20208 June 2010#6
That looks a bit better. But you also need absorption on your front wall, behind the speakers, and you really do need some kind of bass trapping in at least a few corners. It's a very small room. Superchunks can be as simple as a few bales of mineral wool or figer-glass insulation stacked in the corners, and hidden behind a frame covered with cloth. Not too expensive, very easy to do, and very easy to move if you have to go elsewhere. - Stuart -

Soundman20209 June 2010#8
I can put absorbers behind the right speaker but not the left one, because there's a window behind it which I rather not obstruct. But is a bass trap needed there?
No, but it IS a first reflection point, and therefore does need treating, unfortunately. If not, you will get reflections and comb filtering from behind the speaker coming back at your head...
When I'm mixing I could just close the curtains so mid/high freqs are absorbed, right?
Well, if your curtains are made of fabric that is about 10 cm, then that would work! :) Otherwise, you need absorption there. Curtains won't do much.
On the other hand, I could make a big absorber that I put in front of the window when I'm mixing.
That sounds like the right idea!
Can I just add corner traps to the corners, and by that I mean triangle shaped bass traps in the top corners, or will that not be effective?
It will help, but that is a small room: it needs LOTS of bass trapping. As much as you can get. Don't forget that there are actually twelve corners in a room... Most people think there are only four... :) Try to bass-trap as many corners as you can.
It's going to be hard to install corner traps that go from floor to ceiling because of the window at the front of the room, and the door in the back of the room.
For the door, you could make a "bass trap on wheels" that you roll into place once the door is closed, and roll out the way when you need to leave the room... - Stuart -
