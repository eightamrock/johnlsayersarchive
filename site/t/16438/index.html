Ro15 July 2011#5
Sorry but it HAS been discussed. But I'll give you the straight answer: Covering the back of an absorber might come in handy if you want that same absorber to reflect/bend sound. In case of a ceiling absorber (aka cloud) above a mix-station, for example, you want to break the first reflections by angeling the cloud and put a hard surface on the back (more mass is lower frq reflection). That's the reason why you wanna hard-back an absorber. Now, that info should be enough to cover all questions; you now know when or when not to. So, for regular wall absorbers which only intent is to absorb broadband frq you should NOT add mass to the back. Space it the same distance as your wool thickness if you can. good luck, don't forget to post pix when you build! ro
