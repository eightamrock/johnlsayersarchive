dennis cham13 September 2006#1
Hi everyone, I have been lurking on this forum for some time and our company has just rented a space in our building for a new studio. A brief backgound first. We are the top audio post production house for advertising sound in the Philippines with 6 Audio Post suites running on ProTools HD and 7 midi writing rooms running on Logic or Digital Performer. We have a small music overdub booth which we use to record jingles and live insturment overdubs. When we build the new room next year we will convert our current overdub suite to another audio post room and move the music studio over to that room. If anyone wants to check out what we do and how some of our current studios are built, you can go to www.hitproductions.net. I have un-officially asked John if he'd like to come on board as one of our consultants (hope he accepts), but would like to get others involved as well. We plan to build mid or late 2007 and are saving up the funds (capped around $100K) to build and equip the room. So between now and then we'll have lots of fun working out a design that would go into that space. Little background on our market and studios . . . being sort of a third world country, most studios here look nothing like the big budget SSL rooms you see in the magazines. Almost all of them, ours included resemble more project studios. Our rates are around US$70 an hour including editor (many studios charge as low as $20 an hour for fully equipped ProTools HD room), so that should give you an idea of the kind of studio we can build around here and still stay in business. This is our big studio project, most rooms we built have costs us from $30K (for Midi rooms) to $60K (post rooms) to build and equip. So the $100K (though tight) buget is really pushing it for us. Still those limitations have not stopped us from doing over 1500 TV and radio commercials a year and winning several international awards. OK enough of an intro here is the space . . . The unit is on the top floor of an office condo where the rest of our studios are located. It overhangs a driveway so there are no floors below which is great for isolation and only one tenant on the left wall. So far structural low freqency vibrations seem easily controllable and I do not see any major problems with isolation. Not being on the ground floor would limit the kind of load the sturcture can hold, still the building is cast concrete and pretty solidly built.

dennis cham14 September 2006#5
Here is the detail of the control room design. I plan to splay side walls with slatted bass absorbers and put bass trapping in all the four corners. The ceiling not shown would have hanging clouds that are a combination of absorbers and diffusors. In the back I have two QRD 17 diffusors which are around 18" deep and have 17 wells each. According to my diffusor calculator that should diffuse from 350Hz to 6KHz. Smaller slat diffusors on the side wall would diffuse the higher frequencies.
Image not preserved: back_wall.jpg
dennis cham14 September 2006#6
Here a close up of the recording space. Basic rule of thumb was to put a more reflective wall (slatted bass absorbers) opposite a more absorptive but diffuse wall. The ceiling would be a little more dead towards the control room to do vocals and very diffuse and bright in the back for horns drums and stings. Looking more for a tight but live sound in this area. The carpeted area under the drums is acutally an isolated drum riser built into the floating floor (yup both the control room and studio will be floated ). This portion would be decoupled from the main studio floor and carpeted to keep the spiked drums legs from ruining a wood floor and keep the drums from sliding. I have found that isolating the drum mics by placing their stands outside the riser helps tighten the sound a bit.

Ok here are the recording spaces. The vocal booth in the middle is very dead with 1 live wall just to add some liveness back to the space. dennis cham24 September 2006#18
Did some quick revisions in the live room . . . I remember that in most of the rooms at Avatar, the lower slats had wider spacing and the slat spacing gets narrower at the upper parts of the room. This allows this to have a dryer acoustic at the lower part and brighter at the top. My spacing is 2" between 4" , 6" and 8" slats on the lower portion and 1/2 inch spacing in the upper half. I could increase this to 1" to deaden the space a bit more. Hoping for your suggestions regarding this concept. I was hoping to get a warm diffuse sound to contrast with the deader acoustic of the iso room. Chreerio! :)
