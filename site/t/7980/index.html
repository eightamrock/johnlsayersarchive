HI, I am in the process of designing a small studio in my garage out the back of my house, used for recording and rehearsal for my band. I live in typical urbanity (Keilor, Victoria, Australia) with houses each side and to the back, but RIGHT next door is the neighbours garage (see front image below) We are a loud band (think AC/DC) :twisted: so we're going to have to have some decent sound proofing. I am a complete noob when it comes to construction, so please bear with me as I learn the lingo The existing construction is as follows: Floor - Concrete Slab Walls - Double brick (Bricks are 23 x 7.5 x 10 cm) with some brick pylons spaced evenly on two of the inside sides of the garage. Ceiling - Tin top (the tin with ripples, but not corrigated) which is nailed on some wooden beams (19 x 4.5 cm x length of garage). Stapled to the bottom of the beams is some blue plastic stuff (looks like tarpolan) and on top of that there is some pink bats insulation. Nailed to the wooden beams (from underneath through the blue plastic stuff) is some wood sheeting about 5 mill thick.

Don’t know if it helps but, I made a large box (coffin size) and made the air flow though a variety of wells above the studio’s ceiling. Inside this coffin, I packed it with fibreglass and covered it with ground cover plastic (got small holes in it). The air then has to go around all of this fibreglass panels to escape thereby the sound gets absorbed. One end of the muffle I attached some duct to the studio and the other end I attached duct up to the roof where it was attached to a whirly fan thingy. At the studio end, I put a plain bathroom exhaust fan to suck up the stale air. My regret is that I didn’t put it at the far end opposite the door to maximize the amount of air moved. It works very well. I will state however that studios get very hot when full of bodies and equipment. I bought a reverse cycle standard air conditioner to heat/cool the room. It is now going to be placed in the sound lock near the exit door. The air con’ is not quiet but I can switch if off when recording by remote thereby saving $100's of dollars. If you are worried about the sound leakage, you can make a heavy duty cover that clamps over the face of the air con’. :D I am new here so someone else might have some better ideas. I would also think of the break down on equipment of the studio eg leads 15-10% air con X% furnishings X% and recording equipment etc. Hope this helps. :D
