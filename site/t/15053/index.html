Soundman20203 November 2010#7
1- Ho much space will I loose, doing that kind of wood framing that I can see on most of the studios ? (including air gap, layers, ...)
Unfortunately, there is no simple easy answer to that question. It could be as little as 5 inches (13cm) on each side, or it could be several feet. It all depends on what you want to do...
2- what is the typical layering for a wall ? starting from the outside, I can guess there is an airgap (hom much ?), a layer of plaster, the wood framing with wool on it (80mm ?), and again some plaster, but for some studio I can see there is only fabric on it ?
Typical "layering" for an MSM wall is (from outside to inside) : - 2 x layers of 16mm drywall - 2x4 wood studs - separation (at least 1") - 2x4 wood studs - 2 x layers of 16mm drywall That gives you an air gap of about 8 inches, which keeps your MSM resonant frequency down low, and gives you good isolation. For maximum isolation, the entire air gap should be filled with insulation such as mineral wool or fiberglass.
but for some studio I can see there is only fabric on it ?
Then it isn't a wall! :) If you see a frame with fabric on it, the that is NOT a wall: it is "acoustic treatment disguise". The fabric is just there to hide the treatment behind it. The fabric itself has itself has absolutely no acoustic uses: it is transparent to sound.
3- Will I need some additionnal acoustic treatment after that (except some bass traping in the corner), or is the insulation doing the treatment job too ?
Isolation, not insulation: two different things. But yes, you will definitely need additional treatment in your room. Isolation alone will make your room sound WORSE, not better. The entire point of isolation is that it stops the sound getting out by keeping it in the room. Doing that makes the room sound bad, so yes you will always need to treat the room after isolating it. That said, there are some things you can do in designing the isolation that will help to minimize treatment.
4- Are the windows a problem ? I have 5 of them in the whole studio, plus one door. And I you look carefully, the wall are not straight, and I've beam in the ceiling, maybe it's just impossible to do a good job there ?
The windows may or may not be a problem, depending on what you mean by "problem"! :)
5- Is it worth doing it ? I mean it will always remain a personnal studio, not a commercial one. I can spend maybe 10000 thousand € on it, but not more.
What are your priorities? Do you need a lot of isolation, or is that not important? Do you need excellent acoustics? If you need both very good isolation and also very good acoustics, then US$ 10k is probably not enough, but if you don't need much isolation and only want great sounding rooms, then 10k should be plenty for doing that. Tell us more about what your needs are. - Stuart -

Soundman20204 November 2010#11
Can I end with a layer like this, starting from the outside : - Existing walls - separation (at least 1") - wood studs (around 2'') - 2 x layers of 16mm drywall
That would work fine, yes. It should give you excellent isolation. Considering your existing massive outer leaf, maybe drums are NOT out of the question! With so much mass in your outer leaf, you should have really good isolation if you do the inner leaf as above.
For the windows, maybe the coimplication/cost increase too much ?
Not really. If you want to keep the natural light in your room, then just put thick laminate glass in the inner leaf, opposite the existing windows in the outer leaf. If you want excellent isolation, then replace the existing windows with thick laminate glass, too.
The same for the concrete beam that you can see on the pictures ?
Two possibilities: 1) Angle your new inner-leaf ceiling such that it makes the best use of height while bypassing those beams, 2) Just do a flat inner-leaf ceiling that sits about an inch below the beams.
Can isolation be achieved with simple design change ? (I thinking about adding drywall layers)
Exactly. Just build a "room within a room" using drywall on studs, and you have good isolation. If your new inner-leaf is well designed (good shape, good ratios) that will help a lot with the acoustics, but you will still need treatment. - Stuart -

gullfo10 November 2010#21
what's under that wood floor? unless it's something really special which can handle the weight of the isolation walls, then i'd cut it away to mount it on the structural base. if it is a treatment wall, then you could just build it on the wood floor. if you're supporting the isolation ceiling on the walls, i'd go to 50x100 at least and perhaps 50x150 if the room is large. use resilient hangers to stabilize the walls. one option is to float the wall on neoprene to decouple it. this can be pucks, Mason ND isolators, 5mm thick strips, all depends on the total isolation needed and mass-spring computations. on joining the angled walls - you can square them on the finished side so the join is neat and rip a stud to fit on the inside and join them.
