Hello All, I’ve been quiet observer of this site for over a year and I must say I am very impressed with the level of expertise that is present on this site. Currently I am building a modest project studio in Queens, New York (USA). The studio is in a rented space on the second floor of a commercial building (there are only two floors). Since I work mostly at night there really isn’t problem with the level of noise that I’m making, as this is a primarily commercial area. I do however need to isolate my ISO booth from incoming noise (i.e. there’s a bus stop on the side walk right under me. In accordance to forum rules please note the following: Room Dimensions – 10 x 9.5 feet w/ available ceiling height of 8.5 feet Noise Levels outside – I don’t have SPL values, however the bus line and the occasional Police/Fire Truck/Ambulance siren noise is the most noticeable (and the targets of my isolation effort) Budget – I am willing to spend as much as $1,000 US to build the ISO Room (I have access to more $$, however I’d like to cap my budget there) Issues – The existing walls do not rise up to the ceiling joists, there is a drop ceiling that is about 7 feet 10 inches high. There are remnants of previous ceilings (tin tiles etc higher up, but I want to leave that stuff alone…the demo itself would be a nightmare). There is also a circuit box in this room, as well as a window. My plan is to remove the drop ceiling, frame 4 walls inside of the room (8 feet high), and run new joists across and create a new ceiling. I would use 1 sheet of 5/8 drywall on the exterior side, with 3” insulation in the cavity, followed by 2 sheets of drywall on the interior side, using green glue in-between the 2 sheets. I would also use the 3” insulation and the 2 sheets of 5/8” drywall in the ceiling as well (using the Green Glue in-between as well). Obviously this plan dictates that I cover up the circuit box (as well as the windows) with walls. I am planning on installing a solid core door in front the circuit box so I don’t lose too much of the isolation benefits but at the same time maintaining access to the circuit box. My real question is, before I spend the money to build, is my plan solid enough to provide decent isolation? Or am I way off base? Any suggestions will also be appreciated. I have included a (very bad) Sketchup drawing and a wall cross section pic below. Thanks in advance for the help! Lew Savage
