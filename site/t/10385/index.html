xSpace27 March 2008#2
So I wanted to get confirmation on my thinking..
The room in a room works for several reasons. It has two dense layers and two seperate sets of framing. Start from the exterior of your garage and count the existing hard layers on the walls. The outside is 1. Then on the inside you have another, that is 2. Installing another hard layer will produce 3, and so on. Because 2 is the number of layers in a room in a room wall design, people will tear out existing sheetrock on the exterior walls of thier buildings. Often they will "beef up" the exterior leaf. Then another framed wall will be placed on the inside with as much air space as can be or is required between it and the existing outside framing. Another dense layer will be installed on the inside of the newly framed wall. Your obsession has not been remedied. This is the >most< important aspect of your isolation and you have not been obsessive enough in learning this one thing :)

The effect is you have a three leaf wall. The effect is that you begin to lose the isolation in the lower frequencies, the real annoying ones? This is counter productive if isolation is the goal of building.
If I layer the inside of the studio with 3 layers of 5/8" followed by insulation/air/insulation/2 layers 5/8 on the outside.. What kind of frequencies are actually going to escape?
You can do this. It would require you to remove the exterior siding, install the exterior dense material and then reinstall the siding...with a few bumps along the way now that the depth of the wall has changed.
