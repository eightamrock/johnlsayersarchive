Soundman202014 March 2009#4
Pink fiber glass insulation is not easy to work with for acoustic treatment on walls. That's why Lilith suggested 703. Your best bet is probably to build wooden frames a couple of inches deep, fill them with the fiberglass, wrap them in plastic (so as not to lose the highs) and warp that in acoustic cloth, for aesthetics. Build several of those, and you can experiment with them, to find the best placement. Make them 16 inches by 24 (outside), and it will be easy to fit them on the walls: 6 per wall (2 x 16 inch "modules" across, by three up). Or if yo make them 16 x 16, then you could fit up to 2 across and 4 up the wall, and up to 4 on the ceiling. My guess is that you'll need to cover the ceiling completely, maybe 3 inches thick (I hope you are not taller than 5'8!), but nothing on the floor (leave it as bare wood, no carpet), then experiment with a few modules on the walls. Perhaps do each wall one module wide, and from around knee-height up to above your head. Or maybe floor to ceiling on two adjacent walls, and nothing on the other two. Or partial coverage on the other two. Or "checkerboard" patterns of modules, such that where there is a module on one wall, there is nothing but bare wall on the opposite wall. The advantage of building modular frames is that it makes it easy to experiment like this, before deciding on the final position and fitting them permanently. You might even find that you need to stack them two-deep in some places, to get enough absorption. But like Lilith said, that's a rather small space, and it won't be easy to make it sound good. This will be a lot of work to make the modules and experiment with them, but it's the only thing that makes sense to me. - Stuart -
