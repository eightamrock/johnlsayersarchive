Howdy all! I've got a studio build coming up and have a quick question in regards to the existing walls. Firstly, this is in Dallas, TX at what was formerly an auto-body shop. The old shop had 2 large garages whereby 1 in this project is being converted into a control room. There is a medium sized gated parking lot in the front of the building (North), another auto shop about 30 yards to the West of the building, a tattoo parlor about 30 yards East of the building, and an empty lot/convenience store to the south of the building. The auto-body shop to the West primarily focuses on paint jobs and do not generate much noise. It is a busy area but the street noise is pretty low in the building apart from commercial airlines. My concern is that the East and South walls are brick, the North wall is cinder block, and the West wall is a finished wall about 7.5 feet high with 1/4 inch boards nailed into the tops - the adjacent room to the West is the kitchen and about 15 West of the doorway heading into the kitchen, above, is the A/C unit. When constructing the room, will these outer walls act as leaves whereby to achieve a 2 leaf system I am to finish with gypsum board only the interior framing? Or should I also attach the drywall to the outer framing and ignore the "leaf role" of the existing brick, concrete, and half wood walls. gullfo10 December 2010#2
welcome! please update your profile with your location. the iphone app looks interesting. if it's just a control room and it's basically an isolation shell within the space and it seems well short of the existing ceiling, you should be OK to build some massive walls (3x 5/8" on 2x6) and ceiling (3x 5/8" on 2x8 or 2x10 as dictated by the final span calculations). limit the penetrations to the entry door and HVAC baffle boxes. if there is any significant LF noise in the structure you might consider floating the isolation walls and a decoupled/dampened interior floor.

If he's building walls next to the block wall, the resultant wall is a decoupled double leaf. Block wall + air space + new framed wall with drywall. I'd suggest 3 sheets of Drywall with less GG in between the layers. Split the fixed amount of GG into two thinner layers.

Ted White15 December 2010#14
Also maybe an interesting point (maybe not), as we drop in frequency and approach the MAM frequency of the system, elements like decoupling, absorption and damping become less and less germane. You are approaching a point at which the decoupled assembly is now air-coupled and the system is now stiffness controlled. The stiffness gained with the third sheet of drywall now governs the system.

Ted White23 December 2010#16
Well, that's a good thought. Such a material would only bring practical benefit if it could damp the original wall or add significant mass. The original block is too massive and stiff to damp, and you'd have to add a great deal of mass to make a difference. So from a practical perspective, I don't think there's a product that would inprove things by being added to the block itself. The remaining viable option is still to decouple from the block, and add a massive damped leaf to the assembly. Lightweight absorption in the form of fluffy insulation will help lower the resonance frequency you'd be introducing.
