Ethan Winer4 November 2007#5
Yes, too much bracing can shift the frequency, so the obvious answer is don't do that. :) The frequency of a wood panel bass trap depends on the mass of the front panel and the depth of the air gap (springiness). Not so much the panel's surface area. Yes, if the panel is small the edge bracing can dominate, and if it's too large it will flop around and flex in modes other than the main one you want. Others here can better explain the theory's fine points, but I'm certain it will work well if you simply build them as shown in my plans. --Ethan

Ethan Winer5 November 2007#11
jwl wrote:
Hmmm, I wonder what 2 panel absorbers in the corner mounted flush to the wall, as ethan describes, and then with a standard broadband absorber straddling the corner in front of those would work? Perhaps the best of both worlds (pressure and velocity absorber) there....
Yes, but if I were going to do that I'd tune the panel absorbers to a center frequency around 50 or 60 Hz. The "low bass" trap in my plans is centered around 90 or 100 Hz, which is where porous traps straddling corners also peak. So it makes more sense to use the panel traps to target the really low stuff where porous absorbers are less effective. This is the big feature of wood panel traps - they absorb low frequencies with a relatively thin profile. A 4-inch deep panel trap hits 100 percent absorption around 100 Hz. Even if you aim for 50 Hz you'll get 100 percent absorption with a much thinner profile than any type of fiberglass. --Ethan
