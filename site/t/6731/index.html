sharward30 July 2006#4
I'm stealing this bit of text verbatim from another thread I posted on recently... Here is a very compelling post from Paul Woodlock on the pointlessness of floating lightweight floors. If by chance you don't recognize Paul's name, then that's more evidence that you have more studying to do on the subject. ;-) Check out his monster thread at StudioTips. Plan on spending a few days reading what he has gone through to do a proper heavy floated floor system. Also, don't miss this post from Rod Gervais on the "trendiness" of floating floors (actually, slabs in this case). If you don't recognize Rod's name (or even if you do), then definitely buy his new book. Bottom line: you can't just assume that floating a floor is going to be better than not floating it, because you have to take into account so many factors, including resonance frequencies, the risks of overloading and underloading your elastomers, and especially the combination of both -- bad resonance issues combined with a floor that doesn't actually float due to a lack of "springiness" due to underloading or bottoming out -- the latter being the equivalent of a nasty "drum head" as a floor that amplifies certain frequencies, with zero benefits! :shock: --Keith :mrgreen:
