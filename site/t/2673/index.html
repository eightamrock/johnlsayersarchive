benbrackett8 December 2004#2
Two key things for your control room........... 1) No right angles 2) No parrallel walls If you position the room right, the rest is mostly down to acoustic treatment. Just make sure, if you're putting a window out into the live room, that you slant the glass at an angle, to keep sound from directly reverberating. If you do that, the rest is mostly left to acoustic treatment (bass traps, diffuersers, absorbers, etc.) I'm just in the plannig stages of building mine. But I've been in a few studios, and those are the two important things I was told (by numerous people). Good Luck.

benbrackett wrote:

greetings hmmm? :) point 2] might diminish flutter echo a bit, which after you've done the acoustic treatment wont' be there even in a parralleled walled room. Non parrallel walls WILL NOT get rid of standing waves, which are the main reason for a non flat freqeuncy response. Some control rooms are built with extremely splayed side walls and front ceiling to direct early reflections away from the listening position, but it's an awful lot of work and hassle, when the same can be achieved by decent absoprtion panels in the right places. Slightly splaying the walls IMO is pointless. Unless it's done for aesthetic reasons :) The space gained by keeping the room rectangular, can be utilised for lots of broadband standing wave absorption. Plus the room itself is a LOT easier and cheaper to build. Slanting glass: If your employing a double leaf glass window ( which you would be wth a double leaf wall between rooms ), then you want the biggest airgap you can get. slanting the glass compromises this. Sound hits a surface in a room at many different angles not just perpendicular btw Paul
