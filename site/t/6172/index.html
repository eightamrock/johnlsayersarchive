1. Would it be better to lower my ceiling to 8ft?
NO!!! More cubic feet is always better
2. Are the floor trusses going to give me trouble spaced at 24" O.C. ?
All depends on the dead/live load rating and how much weight you are going to put on them but...
3. Is the 2nd floor even a good idea?
Not really, sound isolation is always better from the 1st floor, 2nd floor studios are not the general recommendation. Weight of studio gear, double walls, and acoustics is another issue but if you do go 2nd floor hopefully these trusses have the correct amount of load rating.
4. Will I need both the 703 and a vinyl barrier on walls? Floor?
Hmmm... If you meaning 703 in the walls then you may want to think about more cost effectively and only losing a couple DB's using fluffy fiber glass with no paper like R11 or R13. Vinyl is effective but more sheets of drywall are always the recommendation. 703 or rigid isolation is definitively suggested for acoustics in room. sharward8 May 2006#3
14K wrote:
3. Is the 2nd floor even a good idea?
No, building a studio where isolation is critical on anything other than a ground floor can be bad because you run the risk of overloading your floor with the heavy construction required. Overloading = collapse, and collapse = injuries and death.
4. Will I need both the 703 and a vinyl barrier on walls? Floor?
MLV is a joke. See this thread for more information. Use search term "MLV" for more threads that debunk that product. --Keith :mrgreen:

knightfly10 May 2006#13
Depending on just how much isolation you need/want between floors, you may want to do a floating floor upstairs with your inner walls resting on that - if so, your floor joists will need to be spec'd at around 100 PSF dead load to even consider that type construction. Any partition walls downstairs, if supported well UNDER the lower floor, can help improve dead load by reducing the span. Also, if you're gonna put 6 tons of vinyl on a shelf system UPstairs, I'd start figuring out what's gonna KEEP it Upstairs NOW, not later - that stuff is HEAVY, and so far nobody's come up with a legit way of defying gravity :? Your upstairs, INNER walls should be 2x4 on 24" centers, BTW - this lets the frame flex a bit more, which improves the losses occuring through that barrier by making it more limp. Another thing - if you're building a room-in-room upstairs, then the Green Glue approach makes a LOT of sense; you get roughly the same LF isolation with TWO layers of drywall and green glue as you do with FOUR layers and NO GG - half the weight, about the same materials cost, less labor - hmmmm.... HTH... Steve
