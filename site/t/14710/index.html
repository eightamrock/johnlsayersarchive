jhbrandt22 August 2010#2
Start by trapping the front corners of your room. You should move your monitoring position back from the wall so that your ears are about 89 inches from the wall in front of you. You'll need to cover all reflection points with absorption panels. The window to your right may be in a reflection path. If so, you can put a panel on a stand in front of the window when recording/mixing. Your rear wall is far enough away to treat with diffusion if you are up to building any. Trapping for this wall and the alcoves would be a good idea. You will need a 'cloud' trap overhead to kill reflections from the ceiling too. There are some great articles on the Realtraps site, http://www.realtraps.com/articles.htm the GIK site, http://www.gikacoustics.com/education.html and my pubs too http://www.jhbrandt.net/pubs.html. Cheers, John

jhbrandt31 August 2010#5
This is only a 'starting point'. The 38% position in the room helps to minimize the disturbance effects of nulls and peaks in the wave patterns in the room. At 25%, 50%, 75% of the length of the room would be axial mode peak and null points and are to be avoided. Can you make your set-up flexible? fix it so that you can push it up to the wall when you are teaching and pull it back out into position for mixing. Cheers, John
