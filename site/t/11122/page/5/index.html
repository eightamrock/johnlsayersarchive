Soundman202026 January 2009#109
Hi Ken, To be honest, I'm still trying to figure out the construction sequence, but the basic idea is to build the walls lying down, then lift them into place. The issue with that, of course, is the weight: Those things are going to be heavy! The plan right now is to first do the two walls that face the existing brick walls, and once they are upright, brace them in place with temporary braces. Then I'll do the side walls, to which I have free access, as normal framing, then add the drywall. That way I think I'll be able to get into the joints to caulk them well. The toughest one is going to be the LR corner next to the garage door frame. I haven't figured that one out yet. But the part that I'm most concerned about, is the ceilings: They will also be "inside out", with the sheetrock on top of the joists, instead of underneath. That, of course, brings up the minor problem of how do I get into that tiny space between the ceiling and the roof, to hammer/screw the drywall in place! I have a horrible feeling that I'm going to have to take of the roof to get access to that, then put the roof back on again.... Not fun, but it seems to be the only way. One other idea I'm playing with is to drill up through the joists and use toggle bolts to attach the drywall, but I'm not too keen on that either, as I think it will compromise the isolation. A third (silly?) idea is to build the ceiling lying down on the floor BEFORE I build the walls, then lift it into place and support it there temporarily while I build the walls underneath it. But that would make it hard to build the north and south walls lying down and lift them into place... There would not be enough space to do that, I think. So basically the sequence is still not defined 100%. Work in progress! If you have any suggestions, I'd love to hear them! - Stuart -

Soundman202028 January 2009#112
HIGHLY SUGGESTED IDEA!!!!! ====================== I just did something that I should have done ages ago, and STRONGLY recommend to anyone who is is still in the design phase for the studio: I built a "mock up" of the space. OK "mock up" might be a bit of an exaggeration!! What I actually did is set up stacks of chairs in the church hall, and position the stacks to the same size as the dimensions of my room, so I got a rough outline of the room with the chair backs. I also set up a couple of pedestals, put the speakers on them, set up a table with my notebook, a chair, etc. everything to the exact dimensions that I was planning, or as close as possible. Then I sat down for two days and worked on the mix for a CD that I'm busy with, in that space. It might be a real simple mock-up, but I sure am glad that I did it. I learned a LOT! I need to re-think my speaker positions and angles slightly, to start with, as the mix position is not optimal for my head (and I figure it's easier to fix the speakers, than it is to fix my head!!!). My door positions will probably change, too. I figured out that I actually do have room for a small couch in the rear (I was hoping that I would, but dreading that I would not). I figured out that my rear bass trap positions will work just fine. But most of all, I realized that I need to re-arrange my desk work surface: I'm getting reflections off it from the speakers (comb filtering), and it is uncomfortable for long hours. The mixer will have to go elsewhere, as I need much more space for the DAW, space to keep session notes clipboard, space to put things down that I'm not using but will need shortly, storage space (drawers?). I now see where I can put my racks for easy access. I need to figure out what to do about the sub-woofer, as it is really close to my feet, and I'm concerned that if I stretch out in a hurry I might kick the poor thing. etc. etc. etc. WOW! I am SO glad I did that! Once again, I strongly, STRONGLY recommend that you should take the time to do this, if you are still in the design stage. It opened my eyes. - Stuart -

Soundman20207 March 2009#119
Got a little free time, and I've been spending it on improving my studio design. A question for the experts: The local Home Depot equivalent here in Santiago is now stocking a new product called "Duraboard", which is basically an 8mm thick (roughly 1/3 of an inch) fibercement board that weighs around 11 kg per square meter. That works out to roughly 1,400 kg/m3, or about twice the density of drywall, if my calculations are correct. It costs a bit more for the same amount of mass, as compared with drywall, but I can live with that, because the main benefit is the density and thickness: I only need half the thickness to get the same isolation! Would it make any sense to use this stuff instead of drywall? They way I figure it, 2 sheets of this stuff gives the same mass as 4 sheets of drywall, but is only 16 mm thick, instead of 40. So I could gain two inches of space in each dimension of my room! 2 inches might not seem like much, but to me it is a LOT! You all know how desperate I am for every extra millimeter in this tiny studio, in order to get a decent room ratio, and a bigger air gap (not to mention leg room!). Going with this stuff instead of drywall would allow me to increase my air gap to 4 inches, and also gain a bit of extra floor space. Does this make any sense? Is it worth considering? The down side is that it would be harder to work with, and is damn heavy to maneuver around (each 4' x 8' sheet weights 32 kg!) But I can live with that if it makes sense otherwise, since it gains me both space and isolation. Comments? Opinions? Suggestions? Dumb idea? - Stuart -
