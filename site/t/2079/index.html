Innovations5 August 2004#4
With wood no doubt cheap where you are then going with the staggered 2x4s on a 2x6 plate is likely to be cheaper than a single 2x4 wall with RC. The nice thing that you have with all-new construction is the ability to make your outer sheathing nice and heavy and crackless. Most folks who are having to build into an existing space are having to either live with a three leaf system, have a weak outer leaf, or resheath both sides of the exterior wall! As to 2 layers of gypsum, sheetrock is the cheapest mass in the construction industry. As for HVAC depending on the relative cost of electricity vs other heating sources in Auburn I would consider a split system heat pump. They are simple to install and have minimal puncture of the wall. You will need to think of some other means of ventillation, though. As for your CPU closet, if it has enough ventillation it does not need its own AC, ditto for the bathroom. With a site as scenic as yours it is going to be tempting to put in big windows, but that will send up your costs when you need good STC windows. Maybe just some solatube skylights. http://www.solatube.com/res_products.php On the other hand, even if you don't put in windows now maybe frame in some headers so it would be easier to add windows later in order to sell your place later as having a 'guest house' rather than a studio.

If you really intend to have this built, keep in mind that labor is going to be 'way more of your budget than materials, especially for precision construction - also, maybe one carpenter in 100,000 has any clue what works for sound isolation or acoustics. If you stay with either staggered studs or complete double framed walls, you might stand a chance of getting a "regular" carpenter to NOT screw it up. Your best bet would be if you can find a carpenter/contractor who is willing to join the forum and "get up to speed" on sound construction - the downside is that this will add to the cost also, unless the guy is interested enough to do the learning part on his own dime - not a guarantee, but remind him that more and more people are putting in home theaters and that a knowledge of the construction techniques could raise his income potential... AS to backing up against a hill, you would need to treat it just as a basement http://www.buildingscience.com/resource ... ystems.pdf including proper drainage, etc - Your least expensive way would probably be to use poured concrete footings and pressure treated poles supporting a PT wood frame, then build similar to this only larger - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1589 Just some thoughts... knightfly11 August 2004#21
Not sure if you're aware of this, it's about $200 but allows any PC program to run on mac - even music software. Saw one review of a heavy duty PC multitracking software run on a mac with this installed, no glitches - http://www.microsoft.com/mac/products/v ... =virtualpc Then you could also run any PC based drawing software as well. Still, not cheap... I'm not sure if you mentioned what your main beef is with your present spare room - acoustics, or sound isolation? If there's any way to ease the problems there, it will take some of the pressure off time-wise and allow you to get this right the first time. If you do a "cut and fill", sometimes known as a "push-out", be aware that it takes about 4 years for a fill to settle enough by natural means before you can build on it - two ways around this; do a big enough one so that your entire construction sits on the "cut" part, so you're still on naturally compacted earth - or, pay big bux to have it machine compacted - not too practical for a smaller building and budget. This is another reason why poured piers and "Pro-Tools" framing :wink: make sense - you can use a large tractor-mounted auger if necessary, and drill down to solid ground and pour your main piers if the ground is too steep for a "cut only" building pad to be practical. Since your biggest isolation concern seems to be room to room instead of neighbors (be careful here though) you might be able to just float the tracking room (better outside isolation) and not the CR - you'd still need to frame each room different though, or you'll end up with a step up between rooms. I would be REALLY careful when engaging ANY professional who has NOT done serious sound studio design - very few seem to have a clue as to what works/doesn't work for isolation, and that can cost you a TON of money for nothing. If you can alleviate the time crunch with any changes to your existing situation, we can eventually help you work out your best plan of attack - if that's not possible, you might be hard-pressed to accomplish what you want in a short time. I always try to keep in mind what I call the "want" triangle - Good, Fast, Cheap pick any two, you can NEVER have all THREE... Steve
