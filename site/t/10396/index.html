gullfo30 March 2008#2
i definitely wouldn't put the mix position facing into a corner. rather, i'd face the desk into the room and put some nice heavy absorption behind it like the corner traps you see in the control rooms, but in this case without the control room walls... :-) why not add a new roof layer over the existing roof? that is create a new and isolation roof over top (obviously clear off shingles etc) to get the isolation and keep the nice interior... then add absorption hanging in spots as needed. check with a structural engineer to determine if you can do this but in theory if the walls can handle the additional dead weight plus still meet live load, then you should be able to do it.

xSpace30 March 2008#4
If you look at the last outside truss: http://www.ssdarchitecture.com/works/co ... l/verdant/ You can see that it has been over sized, maybe 2X12? From what I can see, trusses are installed, plumbed, etc. and then decked, dryed in. Then, inside the house, framing nailers are installed on the sides of the truss rafters. Measuring down from the top of the existing 12" rafter you mark at 5 inches on both ends, both sides of the rafter. Then you pop a chalk line to that. Then a 2X2 nailer is installed on this line. Insulation can be installed above and should allow enough room to have a vented airspace to allow heat to escape. Your finished ceiling product is installed to the bottom of the newly installed nailer. All dimensions given by me are for examples sake only. /end Now what about those metal studs on the exterior? They are inbetween posts supporting a beam?
