kendale23 October 2007#12
Aloha,
It's good to see that the 32'x32' design could work fairly well. I think I finally understand about ratios and the highest priority being placed upon control room size/ratios and of course the inevitable room treatments.
Glad to hear that the design works for you. IMO, it would be very difficult to make accurate judgment calls on mic selection and placement, dynamic & eq processing, etc, without having an accurate point of reference and the least amount of anomalies to contend with. It's something to reach for at the very least. This is not to say, however, that the acoustics of the other rooms aren't important as well, especially when using multiple micing techniques. As the saying goes, "Garbage in, garbage out."
About ceiling height... My best case scenario will probably be 16' in the recording spaces and 12' in the control room. My worst case scenario would be 10' in the control room and 14' in the other spaces. It is my sense that the higher the overall ceiling I can get the better (then ceiling treatment to follow). Is this accurate thinking??
You sir, a very fortunate man. Generally speaking, a higher ceiling is better than one, say at 7' (or less, as is the case, sometimes) and providing that the ratios and treatments are as "best case scenario" as is feasibly possible. This would be a good place to start. :wink: There are, however, other factors that can and will affect the overall "sound" or performance of the room - proper acoustic treatment, isolation, and construction designs, not to mention accurate monitoring, just to name a few. And then you have all the gear selection (and operation of such gear) talent/instruments/program material being recorded, etc. to manage as well. All said, think of it as a system. It's only as good as the weakest link in the chain. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
