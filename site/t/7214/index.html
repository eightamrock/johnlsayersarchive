Yes - that's a serious problem. Musos playing hard consume oxygen and if it's low they become sluggish. That's the problem with the split units in that they don't give fresh air. What you need is a fan mounted that will bring fresh air in and another duct to force air out. A cheap, yet efficient way is to build your own ducts out of MDF, Plywood or chip board, and line them with rigid insulation to make them sound attenuating. A simple fan mounted at the end of one of them will create the airflow. I've attached a drawing of a simple aircon unit install, modify it by putting a fan in the duct where the grill is and leave out the aircon unit. cheers john

sharward5 October 2006#4
The unit you found is a great concept. It's known as a Heat Recovery Ventilator (HRV), or an Energy Ventilator (ERV), depending on your locale. (Your profile doesn't say where in the world you are located... :roll:) Here's another manufacturer of that, along with good information on how they work.. In short, they cycle fresh air while saving energy. In the heating season, the air being expelled warms the air coming in without actually mixing with it. In the cooling season, heat is transferred from incoming air to outgoing air, which has an effect of cooling the incoming air by some extent. There is also humidity and condensation to factor in, which is the distinction between HRV and ERV. If you do a search for HRV on the forum, you'll turn up lots of threads where it has been discussed. (I think most are from me. :twisted:) Here's an example of an HRV actually installed and in use in a studio context. Although HRVs and ERVs are not cheap, their saving grace is the fact that you're supposed to recoup their upfront cost in energy savings over time. Plus, I think they offer greater comfort. Hope that helps! 8) --Keith :mrgreen:
