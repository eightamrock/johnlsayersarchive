guitardad725 December 2006#3
I could knock down some of the worst parts of the stucco and deal with it that way.
I've been dealing with stucco lately and I'll attempt some direction for you... I think you can knock down some of the stucco on the inside between the stud bays. You should go about this very carefully and not with a hand held chisel. Use an angle grinder with a diamond tip blade. I got my Dewalt grinder for $70 and blade for $15. [url=http://www.homedepot.com/prel80/HDUS/EN_US/diy_main/pg_diy.jsp?prod_id=100401582]DeWalt 4-1/2 In. Heavy-Duty Small Angle Grinder [/url] There may be another tool for this job BUT from my limited experience, since my grinder has become my fav tool and it's a cheap tool I recommend it. Diamond tip blade is the important part. Cutting the stucco will be like cutting butter and very, very messy. Dust will go every where. Be sure to where mask, goggles and gloves. Of course be careful to not cut through the paper but plan on vapor barrier band aids if you do. This is not easy from the inside because you want the paper to "shingle" overlap down the wall. I believe you or someone else asked this in another post about vapor barrier from inside... just be careful and Tyvek tape could be used to help place the band aids. Also be prepared to patch cracks on the outside of wall BUT if you're careful you should not get any. From there on follow what Dan F did on his outer leaf walls OR Rod recommends compressed (to 25%) Rock-wool in a lot of gap/void areas in his book. The 3rd leaf is not the end of the world. I think you'll be better of taking your time to beef up this outer leaf between the studs then adding more mass to your inner leaf. Just do you best to fill and slop materials into the voids. Use PT OSB if you're going to put it next to mortar or other cement product. Hope that helps. Marc

OK, I'll repeat I got limited experience with this AND... Yup you could knock some of it out BUT I presumed your Stucco was going to look more like mine where the "clumps" on inside are mainly are the bottom and top edges of wall. You'll still have a lot of uneveness to take care of none the less. Boy am I glad the small stucco wall in my house with out any sheathing behind it is not one of my studio walls. You may already have thought of or may be aware of the following, and again I'll try to help. Have you thought about the (most not likely cost effective) option of tearing down stucco all together, installing new OSB sheathing, vapar barrier and stucco? If you make the stucco 1 1/4" thick (like I'm about to do) you'll then end up with a wall slightly better then 2 sheets of 5/8" drywall and if you want more beef you can then easily add drywall between studs. Be careful about installing new vapor barriers with in a wall. You do not want two vapor barriers, vapor or water to get stuck inbetween barriers, it'll rot out what ever material is inbetween. The wall has to drain and let vapor out. IF you're unfamiliar with stucco walls system and vapor barrier stuff then: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lath and read the "see also" links, including stucco. The paper with holes in it behind your stucco is paper lath, the holes were used to hold the stucco up when installing the base layer of stucco and the reason for the uneveness. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2125 See Steves plentiful links on moisture barrier stuff. The Building Science Links are good, especially this one: http://www.buildingscience.com/resources/walls/Vapor_Barriers_Wall_Design.pdf Got to go, baby crying. guitardad726 December 2006#10
Say i just lay in some mineral wool and 2 layers (OSB and SHeetrock) on the external wall...
Yes I think that would be the route, except... try to get the stucco as even as you can before hand (which may be to PITA to be worth it) make the mineral wool or Rock wool as thin as you can, compress about 25%, then OSB and sheetrock. The rockwool will be the air space which will create the 3rd leaf. To my understanding compressing rockwool in there and making the air gap as small as possible is the best route (that is if you figure out a more solid material can be used). The rockwool will minimize the 3rd leaf. I've seen Steve guess a 10db loss in TL from 3rd leafs. Your scenario could not be that much. Here are some numbers:
1 single layer of 16 mm type X gypsum board (5/8") 2 single layer of 16 mm type X gypsum board 3 90 mm wood studs at 610 mm on centre (2x4's, 24"OC) 4 90 mm of glass fibre insulation in cavity (regular fluffy stuff RC13, no vapor barrier) 5 25 mm gap filled with air (1") 6 90 mm wood studs at 610 mm on centre 7 90 mm of glass fibre insulation in cavity 8 single layer of 16 mm type X gypsum board 9 single layer of 16 mm type X gypsum board STC 69 LF - TL 50 Hz - 26.7 63 Hz - 31.8 80 Hz - 36.6 100 Hz - 39.5 etc...
above from http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/fulltext/ir761/ SO... if you beef good between the studs, add an inch or two of air space and compensate on your inner wall with 3 layers of 5/8" drywall (or equivalent layers of 1" MDF, you got cheap?) then you should surpass your goal. I do not know though if your goal is high enough for acoustic drums. Have you been through Sharwards mighty thread? AND one more thing about stucco from the inside... Only PT lumber can be next to concrete in any form, like stucco. You would have to wrap your 2x4 frame in building paper or tyvek before putting stucco or mortar in there. Marc

n8nod9it wrote:
I never drum after 8pm so my demands for a totally soundproof environment is not as strict, but i would like to be able to jam at any time during the day without disrupting the neighbourhood.
Bear in mind that many municipal codes don't allow that much leeway for "daytime" vs "nighttime" noise levels. Mine only affords a 5 extra decibels during the day. Granted, they may enforce the law more rigorously at night... But complaints usually drive enforcement. Here are the City of Sacramento's objective noise standards. Obviously your town's may differ, but I don't think Sacramento's are that unusual. My city also has subjective noise standards. This means, technically, I could be in compliance with the objective standard, but still get nailed on the subjective standard. In my city, each 15 minute period of violation carries a fine of one thousand dollars. Even if your neighbors are "cool" and appreciate your consideration by keeping it down after 8 p.m., you can't predict how "uncool" a future neighbor might become. The "cool" guy next door today could be replaced by an "uncool" night shift worker who sleeps during the day tomorrow. There's a retired gentleman who lives two doors away from me. He wears hearing aids and is extremely hard of hearing. He has a wordworking hobby and sometimes runs very loud woodworking tools in his garage shop on weekends during the day. I snuck a sound level reading once on the sidewalk outside his home and was shocked at how loud it was: over 80db(A), pushing 90db(A) as I recall -- clearly in violation of our city codes. Any one of us neighbors could have him cited, and someday, someone might. --Keith :mrgreen:

Well after re-reading Dans thread about the stucco uneveness and the PIA it is to fill it (with uncertain results as to the small airgaps), i've come up with how i am going to treat mine. I found that i could use compressed fiberglass ceiling tiles pressed up against the stucco/vapor barrier works pretty well at conforming to the irregularities. I set it against the stucco and work the tile with a gloved hand and rubber block so that it presses into the low spots. I then follow up with another layer (they are about 3/8 inch thick each) on top of the first. I am then putting exterior grade OSB on top of those followed by 5/8 gypsum. So its: Stucco Vapor 2 panels of fiberglass tile OSB 5/8 layer Gypsum R-15 OC pink insulation 12" Airgap Interior 2x4 framing R-15 OC pink 7/16 OSB 2 layers 5/8 Gyp w/Green Glue Interior treatments of 703 wall panels, Bass traps and rubber flooring. I decided to go with not floating the floor and using 1/2" thick vulcanized rubber mat (odorless gym style) over the concrete floor. Ill make this thread my ongoing one...and post some pics of progress of the walls i'm working on guys.
