Soundman20204 March 2009#5
I'm not much of an expert on vocal booths, but seeing no-one else answered I'kll give you my thoughts, for what it's worth:
- Is 4x4 ft. large enough for a vocal booth?
Bigger is always better, but if that's all you have available, then it will have to do.
- Can I use wood instead of drywall?
You can, but drywall is usually cheaper, more massive, and easier to work with.
- Can I build in a plexiglass window?
You can, but plexiglass is not a very good isolator: It is not very dense, so you need to make it at least twice as thick as normal glass to get the same amount of isolation.
- Should I make a double wall with rockwool in between? For insulation/absorption purposes.
If you need isolation, then yes! If you don't need isolation and all you need is treatment, then maybe you could save money and just hang the treatment on the walls of your room! The big question is: do you need isolation? Only you can answer that. You mentioned a noisy handyman next door, so my guess is that yes, you do need isolation. HOWEVER! Do NOT build something that will give you a three-leaf wall. A three leaf wall is BAD for isolation, not good (contrary to what you might think). Since your existing room wall probably already has two leaves (the roof on the outside, and drywall on the inside, you have a problem. Anything that you do is going to add another leaf. So if you do need isolation, your best option is probably to remove the existing drywall from the walls of the room. This will leave you with two options: 1) Leave it like that (no drywall on the studs) and build your vocal booth as a single leaf inside the room. 2) Don't build an actual vocal booth, and just use the entire room! In this case, you will use something called "resilient channel" (or alternatively you could use RSIC clips and hat channel) across the existing studs, then attach two or three layers of drywall on top of that. The resilient channel "decouples" your inner leaf from the rest of the wall, which greatly improves isolation. (You might also need to beef up the roof side of the studs in some way, depending on how much isolation you need.) And you will need to put lots of insulation in that cavity between the new drywall and the roof. either of these will give you much better isolation than you have at present. The second option will give you a much larger space, of course, since the entire room becomes your vocal booth. But it is much more work, too
- What kind of material should I put up on the walls so I don't have that nasty hollow-room-effect that I have now.
Take a look at Ethan Winer's web site to get an idea about that. www.realtraps.com
- Should I put a carpet on the floor?
Probably not a good idea! Make the ceiling dead if you want, and treat the walls with absorption, but leave the floor bare. That's my non-expert opinion. Others may have different ideas for you. - Stuart -
