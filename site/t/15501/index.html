Pictures work wonders Ed. Basic stuff is that insulation goes in between any wall assembly, between the hard boundaries and if you are looking directly at insulation, you have something that needs to be corrected, assuming it isn't an acoustic panel for a specific tuning reason. I'm thinking that you may be on the road to wasted material, if you have framed up a wall assembly that has staggered studs, inside of a basement since this is not a requirement because the basement wall is the first leaf in your room-in-a-room configuration. Also, the 2X4 for ceiling joist running the short dimension may be OK, but 2X6" would be a better option from a safety stand point.

gullfo18 January 2011#4
will the rooms be used together like a studio? or just separate isolated rooms? if the former, you might consider adding a window into the separating walls - something like 3'h x 5'w in the middle (horz and vert). you also want the drywall supporting studs to be 24" o.c. not staggered (on the separation walls) which is making it 48" o.c. for the remaining walls, you want to have the drywall on both sides to ensure a mass-air-mass configuration and no drywall between the two rooms (as the inner wall on each is part of your MAM). 2x 5/8" is good all around. on the remaining walls, the staggered studs is OK but still need to be on 24" o.c. for each side. R19 overhead. R13 in the walls.
