xSpace13 July 2008#14
Ok, I see said the blind man. If I was doing this, I would push(the owners) to have the rafter tails land on the straight long side of the building. This would ensure an even wall height for your bird mouth and all measuring. I do not know UK requirements for roof systems so this is just me thinking out loud on how I would frame this monkey! Anyway, to get any degree of pitch will require that a knee wall is built of sufficient height across the top of the angled wall where the rafter tops will rest. I'm going to use 4 ft. as my reference knee wall height. First I'd install a pressure treated 2X6 laying flat on the top of the block wall, for my example, all around the perimeter. This will allow me to build and attach the knee wall onto for our height requirement. Nail the bottom of a temporary 4 ft. stud(vertical) secured to this pressure treated board. The rafter tail would have already been established and birdsmouth cut out on one of my long rafters. I'd leave the length of the rafter uncut until we know more about the length. One man on the high side holding the rafter on to the >side< of the vertical temporary stud and one man on the bird mouth side holding the rafter tight to the wall. Have your man at the rafter tail end drive at least two nails into the birdsmouth securely into the plate. Plumb up the temporary stud as if it is a wall and then you may nail the rafter to the side of the stud. After it is nailed, mark the rafter around this temporary stud as you will also have a notch/birdsmouth at the top of each rafter. This rafter and stud need to be able to stand unattended for some time while you get some more figures. While still on the side with the temporary stud and rafter, measure from hooking the birdsmouth wall area back towards the temporary vertical stud 15 feet and make a mark on the top of wall plate at the 15 feet intersection. This is the distance of your short side wall correct? It is better to actually measure the existing shorter side(hooking outside wall to outside wall) to make certain that your measurement is as accurate as possible. We will use 15 feet since that is the goal. Once the mark has been made, using a level, plumb up from this 15 ft mark along side the rafter. Mark along side the level onto the rafter. Measure the distance from the >top< of the rafter down to butting the plate laid on the wall. This is the height that the other end of your kneewall has to be in order for the rafter tops to remain level. So one end of the kneewall will be 48 inches and the other end is whatever the measurement is that you just located. Since it is always best to use a double plate for loads like a rafter end, subtract 3 inches from the 48 inch measurement and 3 inches from the number you found from the above process. (3 inches is the thickness of two nominal 2X4's, a 2X4 being actually 1-1/2 inch X 3-1/2 inch , your measurements may vary) Cut a stud to these shorter measurements and install them on their respective ends of the building. Then attach a string line across the top of these vertical members. If you laid out your top of block wall plate already you can measure from this plate to the string and have those boards cut and get them installed. Once complete with the vertical studs you can cut and install the top plates for the kneewall. You should already have joists installed so a great idea would be to nail a diagonal board about every 48 inches from one of these joists to the kneewall stud to hold the wall plumb and keep it stationary. Then lay the top of the kneewall out from the longest rafter side to the shortest rafter side for your rafters. (((((((((((((((Please be careful. Do not depend on this wall to keep you in the air or out of harms way. It may not.)))))))))))))))))) Now you may install the original rafter on too the first layout place. It should be a simple process of "squaring" over from the next layout mark back to the newly installed rafter. This will let you determine what the next (shorter) rafter length will be, bird mouth to top plate. Continue this process until all rafters are up and you have reached the 15 ft side. The rafter will get shorter on the top side but the bird mouth and tail will remain the same. This should keep the top of the roof line straight and the decking area flat. Make sense? As a point of interest, if you use the nonparallel side wall for the birds mouth, your tails may twist out of a straight line configuration if the math isn't worked out :) Conversely, the tails can do this same twist if they are laid on the parallel wall side and the top math isn't worked out .
