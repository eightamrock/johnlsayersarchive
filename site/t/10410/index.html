How would YOU repair a hole in your wall?

Started by Cram on 1 April 2008. 9 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 10410.

Cram1 April 2008#1
If you had to repair a problem behind an Isolation wall, how would you do it? I am in the later stages of my build project and organizing my records for dimensions and measurements for things located behind the walls. There are utility pipes in a few places that could develop problems and I'm trying to formulate a plan of attack in the event of a pipe burst or an other type of need for getting behind the layers of wall board. At first glance - I believe I would try to cut a small hole at the spot where I want to have access. I would route a square (being careful not to puncture anything of course...), establish how big of a hole I would need for access to the area and then cut out the inner most (room facing) layer of wall board. This is my key point for how mutli layer drywall would be different -- My point about cutting the innermost layer to a larger size is to provide a flanged (for lack of a better term) seal against the seam I am creating by making this repair. This area would have Green Glue re-applied when it is put together. The seam would also have backerod and caulking to fill any gap or air hole. This would be a slight pain because of the "devil's snot" (GG) which has been previously sandwiched between the layers, but I think it'd be worth the effort. Standard Repairs - In the past, I have repaired holes created by errant punches, feet and legs falling through the floor and puncturing the 1st floor ceiling and pipe bursts. When I do this I usually back the hole with a thin peice of plywood that is slightly larger than the hole (I work it through at an angle and at times have used two peices. This acts as a place to screw the new peice against and the borders of the existing wallboard. After taping and finishing it off I'm left with a section of wall that's actually stronger than what was previously there. Pretty much the same way. Keyhole out a hole big enough to get my hand into if required to locate the element of my concern. Once found then the sheetrock out in the room would be cut out, large. I wouldn't try to skimp on it, cut it out 3' X 3'. 'Course I wouldn't concern myself with squaring it either. Reason being the cutout will only fit back in one specific hole! Then step in the successive sheetrock cuts 2 inches or so. To offset the joints when re-installing and allow mudding room. Keep on until your in the wall:) Something inside the wall to attach the first piece of rock to would help, two pieces, one top and one bottom where I come from. I'd pay attention to the existing screw holes and try to use them for re-installing the following layers. IF I had to do that:)

xSpace1 April 2008#5
True enough. It comes down to do you remember the wall stud layout. If you could do that then you measure to a point that represents the center of a stud. Then locate the next stud 16 inches or 32 inches over, whatever the layout is. If you have two layers of rock then it would be a simple matter of measuring again 2 inches outside of the stud marks just made. This would be the plumb line for your first cut into the wall. I didn't say yet, but this is also a good place to use a cordless circular saw or a powered one if that is all you have. So you can cut the first layer without going into the next layer in order to keep those stepped in flanges available. If you were right and remembered the stud layout correctly, then next cuts would be directly on top of the wall studs. Pretty good idea, your a lot smarter then you look :)
