It's an interesting idea if you want a moveable structure (as opposed to one that is bolted to the floor. For your requirements though, I don't think you'd get enough isolation because: 1) No top 2) The corners are not air tight (air tight = sound tight). The biggest part of this design is that the foam he's talking about is FLAMABLE and the smoke it gives off is toxic. Audio foam is expensive for a reason. This design would be much better if you replaced the foam with slabs of mineral wool. The only drawback to this is you would have to cover the MW on the inside with some sort of (flame-resistant) fabric. Also, a single layer of MDF isn't going to give you the isolation of a couple of layers of 5/8" drywall and it's much more expensive than drywall. I suggest you look through John Peel's studio build on this site because he built just this type of wall and is pretty happy with the results. It's a much bigger project than yours but the technique is the same. Bear in mind that you are going to need some sort of top/ceiling on this structure to noise out and it needs to be every bit as good as the walls. You haven't mentioned what building skills/experience you have or what tools you have available. I haven't seen any information as to how you would get the materials down into the basement. 5/8" drywall (or MDF for that matter) are EXTREMELY heavy and you'd need two people to move it from the truck to the basement. If you have no skill at all in building, I would advise you to hire a builder to at least draw up some plans for you to make sure it meets building codes and such. You will also need to get a permit from the city BEFORE construction begins. Failure to do so could have bad consequences from having to tear it all down, to having your insurance company refuse to pay on a claim, to making your house difficult to sell in the future. len len
