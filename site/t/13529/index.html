subfloor mass - cleats vs screws

Started by biggator on 21 August 2009. 2 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 13529.

biggator21 August 2009#1
In Rod's book, it shows cleats as the method for securing drywall up under a subfloor (between joists). Is there any reason why this is preferred to just screwing the drywall up into place assuming that you have enough material to screw into? I have 3/4" sheathing with solid wood floors above - so plenty to screw into.. and it would be WAY easier for me to do this (joists are wood i-beams, so there's a funny lip to work around if I try to cleat them into place). Thoughts? Grats.
Ted White21 August 2009#2
The screws are fine. That's what we recommend. Screws also provide the requisite pressure to compress the Glue. The general concern may be regarding selecting a screw that is too long and penetrating the finished floor above. Another concern may be that in some multi-family scenarios the underside of the subfloor above simply isn't your property and you therefore cannot touch it.
biggator22 August 2009#3
Thanks, Ted. I'm not using GG on this layer - only between the two layers on the new wall .. this is strictly adding mass to the existing floor, though I guess that's irrelevant here (except for the glue-squishing part). Anyone else have a good reason not to use screws?
