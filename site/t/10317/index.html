I would recommend going back and reading it again. Your terminology is upside down and you design is in need of repair. I'm sure it is all covered ;) A sole plate, also called a bottom plate, is located on the bottom of the stud wall. Attaching any part of your isolated structure to the existing structure terminates the isolation. Ideally you do not want your walls to butt against the building. This can introduce flanking into your space. If the interior wall height is lowered just enough to remove contact with the exterior structure then ceiling framing can be introduced into the above ceiling cavities between the existing joists. These joists sit on top of the interior studio walls. This ceiling can be decoupled from the walls in various manner if it is really needed to meet a specif number. Do you have pictures of this structure? That helps a lot...a whole lot.

If you google this is one item that pops up
The sill plate is the framing member which sits directly on the foundation, slab or footing. Other names may be base-plate, mudsill, sole-plate, sill plate, etc. In newer construction this plate is bolted to the foundation.
And a wall plate is typically something that covers a penetration.ie light switch, AC receptacle, phone jack or in our case XLR or TRS connectors RSIC clips with hat channel for your ceiling http://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/rsic_ ... _clips.htm And here is a way to attach your walls with decoupling http://www.silentsource.com/rsic_prods.html#RSICDCxx Peace Tom
