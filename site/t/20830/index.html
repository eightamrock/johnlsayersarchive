Soundman20207 January 2017#2
stapled fabric to the two door frames to cover the space.
That is, indeed, one way to do it. Here's a series of photos from one of the studios I designed, that shows how it was done: First, the pair of frames are prepared inside the room, tied together with temporary bracing plates to keep them firmly together with the correct spacing, then the gaps are covered with cloth, which is stapled and taped in place, carefully stretched taught:
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-10-going-in--sml.jpg
Then the entire assembly is presented to the rough opening, and carefully maneuvered into place.
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-11-going-in--sml.jpg
Then it is shimmed and adjusted to get it plumb and square, and sealed in place with backer rod and caulk:
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-12-going-in--sml.jpg
The result prior to final sanding and staining:
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-13-going-in--sml.jpg
Details of how the gap looks after sanding, before staining:
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-14-going-in--sml.jpg
And the final end result, with the complete jambs, seals, and doors in place:
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-02--sml.jpg
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-03--sml.jpg
Image not preserved: Door-gap-detail-04--sml.jpg
For these sliding doors, the procedure was a bit different. First one of the metal-framed doors was set in place, then a small piece of semi-rigid insulation was carefully cut to be a firm fit in the gap between the frames. It was wrapped in fabric, and press fitted into the gap, then covered with with wood trim, then the other frame went in. There's still some masking tape on in these photos, protecting the edges of the wood:
Image not preserved: Sliding-doors-gap-detail-01-SML.jpg
Image not preserved: Sliding-doors-gap-detail-02-SML.jpg
Image not preserved: Sliding-doors-gap-detail-03-SML.jpg
There are other methods too, but that's the way I normally do it. - Stuart -
