chrisrnps10 July 2009#6
I agree - definitely use the symmetrical rectangular area as the 'control room' and the quirky-shaped area as the 'tracking room'. The odd-shaped walls can be advantageous for a tracking room (asymmetrical walls are good, plus you would have different shaped 'zones' to experiment with placing instruments and mics to get a variety of sounds) and you want the 'control room' 100% symmetrical. What are the actual dimensions of the space (including elevations)? I'd decide where to add the 'fourth wall' of your control room based on possible room ratios dictated by the existing walls and ceiling height, then creatively make some surfaces in the tracking room side even less symmetrical than they are now, with things like angled slat-resonator panels, or making one or more of the longer walls at an angle (as it's still a complex 'rectilinear polygon' aside from the small 45-degree angle wall section as-is) if you're adding another leaf.
