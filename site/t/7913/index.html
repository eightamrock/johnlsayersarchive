I'm turning the part of old European masonry into my temporary recording location (home/project studio), starting with recording (live) room. The building (a house with arched basement, ground floor and one floor above) has massive brick walls, most of those are over 19’’ (50 cm') covered with stucco (plaster). The bricks are solid, without „air chambers“. Recording and control room are surrounded with other rooms (empty and out of use) and only one side of the room (with windows on north side) is exposed to street with moderate traffic. Nobody lives in the house. Recording room is 16.4' (L) x 15' (W) x 10.5'(H) (5 m x 4.6 m x 3.2 m)

Because of room dimensions (it's already too small) and budget limit I can’t play with the room in the room design so the only way for stopping noises from outside is to make some frames and fill/cover it with gypsum boards and OSB. Of course there would be some flanking noises I have to live with, but the less the better. Lumbers are 4.3''x 3.5'' (11 x 9 cm) and the frame is 5.9' x 9.8' (180 x 300 cm). Similar construction, but with the door, would be on the right wall with existing two wings door between the live and control room. Here’s what was done: wooden floor is replaced with reinforced 2’’ concrete, windows are fixed and repainted, many old layers of wall paint are removed, lumber frames are attached to the wall. Now we go to - act two. First, I’m planning to fill the lumber bays with one OSB layer then with 2 – 3 layers of drywall. The OSB will be attached with screws on the inner wooden frame and then drywall layers on the OSB. After that, I will add one OSB and 3 - 4 drywall layers on the face of the lumber frame (room side). After reading Dan’s thread (6th pg.), I’m not so sure about adding more than 2 layers of drywall (2+1 OSB) inside the lumber bays. The mass and density of wooden frame is the weakest link so adding more layers into the bays seems to be throwing the material and money. I suppose adding more layers on the face of the frames will be better for higher value of STC and TL. http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=2274&highlight=cloud Question one: With how many layers of drywall inside the bays and how many on the face of frame to go? Hmmmm … OK, I will rephrase the question. Have you any data about comparison of the mass and density between gypsum and wood?

groff12 January 2007#14
Now, what bothers me a bit more, is caulking the bottom edges of every gypsum layer inside the lumber bays of my frames. It's common practice to leave and caulk usually 1/4'' gap around the perimeter because of „swing and breathing“ caused by temperature, moisture, etc during time. But why the gap under the bottom edges? The whole mass of inner gypsum boards will be supported by the horizontal lumbers, the inner frames and screws just keep and prevent the boards from falling off. If the lumber moves slightly down (or banding up) mass should follow. That was initial idea. Now if I leave the gap under the bottom edges of drywall boards, the whole mass would be hang on inner frames. Hmmmm .... that's not good. Question 3: I’m wondering what’s gonna be if I put the caulk bead on horizontal lumbers and immerse the bottom edges of boards in, without the bottom gap?

Hi, I'm done with first layer on outer/lateral sides of frames. It wasn't hard work, just goes slow and takes lot of time for drying between steps. The moisture resistant drywall attached on the wall with flexi tile adhesive. Nothing much different than doing regular tiles. 24h drying time. The big problem was to find the backer rod because it is not in common use here. After long and frustrating searching I found it only in one store, but they won’t sell less than 2 miles !!! No words can tell you how angry I was. So the only option left is to cut the foam and make it by myself. And dammit I did it. The gap filled and caulked. 3 days of drying. Next drywall comes on lateral frame side. I'm using primer on the contact edges for better surface adhesion of caulk. I did the test before (with/without the primer) and adhesion was enormously better. This drywall is attached with caulk on the back and screws. Then the backer rod and caulking again. The second layer goes after placing OSB and drywall in the frame bays, but before that I have to do revision of the window plug. I realize that, according to my previous plan, this plug goes over 80 kg. It's just too much. Best regards
