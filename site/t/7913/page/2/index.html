New progress at my place. Purchasing custom window from professional carpenter is cheaper than investment in good tools for making one. Anyway, making a good window is not easy and there is a high potential where things can go wrong. I got fair price (50 $ for workmanship, material not included) so I decided to purchase it rather than risk. Here's jamb and wing. Wing is sandwich of two 12.5 mm drywalls between two 12.5 mm MDFs. The jamb is with double rubber seal. Dimensions: 128 x 84 cm, weight: 40 kg. The next step was customizing the hardware for the window. I have no clue about welding but my friend Sime has tools, knowledge, experience ... and most important a good will to help me here with this. Thanks a million my friend. Now, the first impression about functionality before adding more drywall layers on the faces. In the middle and upper range it’s quiet enough. I can’t hear sound in that part of spectrum coming into the room. Fair enough. But the lower range is awful. I can here every car engine rumble from outside. Moreover, empty room “amplify” that (like a resonator). I expected some flanking noises, but not that loud. Adding 2-3 more drywall layers would probably help with 6-9 dB in noise reduction but I’m not sure would this be enough. I have to say that I didn’t fill space between the OSB and the glass with insulation in the left (windowed) frame yet. Suppose, this wont be a crucial factor in TL. Measuring with the ears …. blah … I know … I just can’t find any affordable SPL meter here, only 200+ € and this is out of my already burned budget. Any trick in calibrating and measuring TL/NR with standard recording equipment? Even roughly, just to see where am I with all this. :roll: best regards

groff26 March 2007#35
It's perfectly clear to me that I can't get pro-studio high level of soundproofing without room in the room design and floating floor. Unfortunaly, except financial I have building/structural limits, permits and allowances I have to respect for multiple reasons. short resume: - can't build room in the room (weight, small room) - can't build the floating floor (arched basement under) - can't build the brick/concrete wall at windows place (not allowed) - all new structures must be non permanent or easily removable - reasonably affordable - it's live room not the control room (otherwise I wouldn’t be such a pain in the ass) Pretty tough. So, in my desperate attempt in this - single leaf impossible mission, the bags of sand comes to my mind. I expected flanking noises, yes, but not so loud like this. Actually, it's very hard to say that’s flanking only. How to sort that mess anyway? - The street is roughly 30' from the house - I have noticed that, using the palm, I can't feel vibrations on the surface of over 50 cm thick solid brick walls but it's just opposite when I'm touching the frames with drywall. - Direct sound is also clearly hearable when I'm standing in front of the frames. - In past 3 months I've noticed floor vibration just once but that was extreme situation (steam roller). - I didn't touch approx. two feet dropped ceiling much to find out how's going there but I will. - The hallway and one smaller room behind with the back yard windows are far more quieter in the low range Mr. Knightfly mentioned once that 24 cm concrete walls should make about 60 dB of STC, with 45 dB @ 50 Hz. I'm wondering how close can I come using sand barriers with similar thickness in addition to framed drywall? Thanks a lot.

groff22 December 2007#47
Hi, Long time no post here. I made a few steps forward, so I suppose it's time for update. My biggest problem was to find dried fir wood for framing absorbers and I loose damn too much time. I was completely stacked. After many troubles, finally I've found rough B/C-class boards, witch were treated with jointer and thicknesser machines at local carpenter shop later. Next problem was resin pockets (more than hundred in 0.5 m3) some were small, others wide and deep. I boiled the resin out with the heater gun, ducts were cleaned with the sand paper and filled with the mixture of glue and saw dust. Frustrating and time consuming work.
