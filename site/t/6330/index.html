sharward30 May 2006#2
Those were probably from a pneumatic nailer... I had to grind off a few nails that "missed" studs during my outer leaf beef-up. Slow-going is right... And, yes, sparks do fly. :? I don't know if this would work or not, but perhaps a very good quality long-handled wire cutter would cut the nails close to the surface, then you can use the grinder to smooth them and make them more flush... :roll: How far do they stick down? If they protrude less than 1/2", and if you can afford losing the height, then maybe you can use closed cell foam insulation as your first layer of beef. The nails will poke right through the surface of the foam. Then you can apply your first layer of drywall firmly against the foam. The foam doesn't breathe, so it won't act as air and you won't have a third leaf effect. It might also help a little with footfall impact noise. --Keith :mrgreen:

Bryant1 June 2006#6
Hey Luftweg, When I said "metal" blade, I meant a cut-off wheel that is installed on the grinder that is intended for cutting metal. The "stone" type blade that you describe sounds like this type of blade. Try using a "continuous rim diamond blade" on your grinder. The blade itself is metal, the cutting surface is comprised of industrial diamonds. Typically this type of grinder blade comes in either a segmented rim or a continious rim. This type of blade is further categorized by its use as either, "Wet" or "Wet/Dry". You want the "Wet/ Dry" or just plain Dry. It'll make short work of those nails, probably ruin the blade but save alot of time. Sorry for the confusion earlier. Bryant
knightfly1 June 2006#7
If you can't find (or don't want to PAY for) a diamond blade, ask for 1/32" thick cutoff disk (assuming this is one of the smaller, 4 or 5" grinders) - the extra thin blade will go thru steel like butter (because it's cutting so much LESS of the metal in order to reach the other side) - use a relatively light touch (so you don't nick the thin blade), wear leather gloves, a full face shield, button up your shirt (or put on a jacket, BACKWARD) so you don't end up with hot nail ends down your neck) and HAVE SOMEONE BE A FIREWATCHER all the time you're doing it, and afterward for a half-hour or more... Steve (oh, and take those open buckets of paint thinner and gasoline outside before you start :? )
