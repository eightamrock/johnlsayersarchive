It would be good if you measure the levels with a sound level meter: both the maximum level when the drums are being played, and also the level that you think is acceptable for others in the building. The difference between those two numbers is how much isolation you need.

Soundman20202 July 2013#6
I´m going to have a laminatefloor that I´m going to lay over the linolium with just a foam in between hope thats fine?
If the lino is flat, smooth and level, then no problem. But if there are bumps or dips in it then you need to fix those first. Check with the manufacturer of the flooring that you want to use to find out what the maximum permissible bump or dip is. They normally express that as "x mm per 1m", meaning that that the level of the floor must not change by more than x mm over a distance of 1 m in any direction. so if you lay a 1m steel ruler on the floor, then there should be no dips deeper than x mm, and no bumps higher than x mm.
Do you think the detailpicture in my second post about isolating the windows is ok?
IT looks OK, yes.
Do you think that the blueline is to far to go from liveroom ro CR?
That depends on you, and how you work! If you only need to go from the LR to CR one or two times each day, then it's no big deal. But if you need to run back and forwards a dozen times an hour, to set up mics or tune instruments or adjust whatever, then you'll probably get really tired of doing that, really fast!
Can I use any fabrics for my traps, superchunks etc or a special quality?
Any breathable fabric will work fine. "Breathable" means that if you hold a piece of the fabric over your mouth and nose, that you can breath through it normally, without noticing any resistance.
What kind of insulation should I use in the superchunk. Ridgid or regular wallinsulation? I use a brand called ISOVER.
Use semi-rigid insulation, with a density of about 50 kg/m3 (or a bit less) if it is mineral wool insulation, or about 30 kg/m3 (or a bit less) if it is fiberglass insulation.
I have an SPL meter and even though I think it´s quiet it shows around 35-40db on C and slow. What db can we call quiet enough inside a liveroom?
Once again, that depends on what you want to use it for. If you will use it mostly for heavy metal drums, then 40 dbC is great, but it you want to use it for soft romantic vocals, that's not so fine, and if you were planning to use it for ADR or Foley work, then that's totally unacceptable. The normal way of deciding on how quote a room needs to be is by using the NC curves (or NR curves, in Europe). A good live room will have something less than NC 20, and a Foley stage will likely need to be under NC 5.
I have some sketchup pictures below where I drawn som treatment in. is it a good start? (se pics below)
The general idea is fine, except that I would not put slat resonators on the rear wall. I would make that totally absorptive, with thick insulation, instead. If not then you will be getting reflections of the back wall coming back to your ears too soon, and too loud. Apart from that the basic idea seems OK.
And now my last question for today: I´m thinking about to build my shellwalls with 1 layer of 19mm mdf and 1 layer of 13mm gypsum on 2"x4"studs with 4" insulation.
Great! But I would make it OSB or plywood, rather than MDF, and I would also use 16mm gypsum instead of 13mm. - Stuart -
