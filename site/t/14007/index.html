The outside diminisions do not specifically matter, what does matter might be you said it has an interior of OSB. This is fine as long as you are not considering a double leaf (two wall) assembly. If you have a Cathedral ceiling then you have horizontal framing members holding/tieing the walls together. What are the layout measurements, how many and what type of building material are these ties made from? Oh, it's a truss roof? "inside 2X6 or 2X4 studs" Figure it out, the more you guess the more we guess and the less anyone can be of any genuine service to you. Do a search for "room ratios" then get acquainted with "wall splaying" and RFZ (reflection free zone). A one room environment is used by many, I have one and so do a lot of others. Especially for a singer/songwriter using MIDI, it's the way to go. My 2 cents, Brien

OK lets see First ,No it is not trusses and if I understand your question correctly " horizontal members" as in bottom cords on trusses or horizontal cross ties like beams etc., then no , no horizontal members running wall to wall. The ceiling is what we call vaulted- upside V shape the common roof and ceiling framing members are rafters, which run on a "Rake" - diagonally up from the top of wall to a ridge beam with no bottom (tie) cord. The Raked framing members tie into the walls, the pressure blocking and the ridge beam, this and the diaphragm, created by the exterior wall and roof sheeting are what tie the walls. And last there is sheet rock applied directly to the bottom of the rafters . I hope I have been clear ?

Great, but what about sound coming the OTHER way? Isolation isn't just to stop the noise you make from getting out. it is also to stop the noise others make from getting in! "Others" here includes things like vehicles on roads, trains, aircraft flying overhead, wind, rain, hail, thunderstorms, people talking, radios playing, animals, etc. Even if you live in the middle of the Atacama desert, you'd still have issues with some of those. Are you SURE that you don't need to think about isolation?
What is the conventional wisdom for studio design, In terms of optimal acoustic environment ? A one room solution (combo tracking/mixing room) ? OR would a two room solution be considered more desirable ?
Personally, I reckon that two rooms is better than one. The only time I'd say that a single room makes more sense is when you are the one and only guy that will ever use it, all by yourself, as a one-man-band, that you will never, ever consider you recording someone else, and where you can get absolutely all your equipment isolated outside of the room. The other possibility would be when you simply don't have enough physical space to build two usable rooms. Apart form that, for me at least, I think it makes more sense to go with two.
The criteria for optimal for the two different environments (Tracking and Mixing) are somewhat different ?? is this correct ?
Yup! Your "mixing room" (generally referred to as the "control room" or "CR" on this forum) should probably be as acoustically neutral as you can possibly make it, neither adding nor subtracting anything from the sound that comes out of the speakers, and carefully designed so that you, at the mix position, only get direct sound in your ears, no reflections, perfect symmetry, etc.. Etc. on the other hand, the "tracking room" (generally referred to here on the forum as the "live room" or "LR") should probably have some kind of character to it, should probably not be symmetrical, and might even have movable parts in it to change the "character", by providing more absorption / reflection / diffusion (or less, as the case may be), at certain points in the room. So the ideas behind the two rooms are kind of different. That's not to say that you can't use a single room for both: just that it makes more sense to have two different environments. - Stuart -
