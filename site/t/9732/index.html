gullfo9 November 2007#2
since you have a limited budget, and you've already had a run in with the local law enforcement regarding noise, you probably want to get things right rather than spend $3K and find yourself slapped with a $5K fine. my 2¢ - 1) get a sound level meter from Radio Shack and a program to measure the room (ETF or Room EQ Wizard) 2) go out and buy Rod Gervais's Home Studio Construction - Build It Like The Pros book and read it, 3) build the isolation completely and correctly. if this means taking things apart and reorganizing how its put together then thats what you should do. one thing - redo you sketch as a really accurate 2D floor plan to scale (one of the nice things about sketchup) and mark doors, windows, odd features, plumbing, beams, etc... that would impact using the entire space. So I've "Scoped" the room, more or less with not so surprising results. And the budget is limited right now, but the way i see it, i have years to complete this since it is basically a project studio, and a place for me to learn the art of micing, mixing, and production. Scoping The Room I used a Behringer DEQ2496 (RTA, SPL, + other uses) and a Radioshack Digital SPL meter. I Used the Noise Oscillator from my AW2816 ( White Noise ) and MegaDeth's Symphony of Destruction CD and a Bass Guitar compressed to within an Millimeter of its life run through a Loud P.A. I took SPL measurements, A, C, and No weight, with program ~ or = 116DB The current control room was 88 DB Highest Frequencies Outside, Megadeth CD, 116DB Inside were: HZ - DB - Behringer Readings @ 3' from front wall 40 - 74 45 - 69 80 - 77.6 125- 75 Basically a hump from 40 - 130ish The RS meter confirmed an average of 78DB. At 15' the bass coming through was indistinguishable from the highway 6 blocks away. At the driveway you couldn't hear anything above the 55Db ambient noise from my neighborhood @ 4PM. Cars driving by drove the meters on the RS to 60-68DB, while talking went as high as 75DB ( meter in hand). ON the neighbor side the same result; The Decibel readings on the property lines are, on average, 18DB below the Penal Code requirements for ambient sound level AFTER 11:00PM, when there is 116DB of full bandwidth noise. So please let me know if you think I missed anything, or I did the test inaccurately. On to Helmholz....
