Cold19 March 2011#2
Hi, Couple of comments. Overall it looks ok, but a few things to think about to make your life easier. 1 Consider adding a door between your break room and live room. 2 consider moving the bathroom door to the side for ease of adding a sink to the room. 3 consider moving the stove sink etc to the opposite wall to ease traffic patterns and limit pipes etc entering the isolation wall 4 review the sight lines from the control room to the live room. For me this would be the deal breaker with this design. If I was going to the trouble of putting in a window, I would want the most complete sight line I could get. 5 Have you figured out a plan as to how to treat the wall first reflection points and maintain room symmetry with regards to the acoustical treatment and still have the window and door located in those positions on the same wall in the control room? 6 If you use the break room as an air lock, put the previously mentioned door into the live room from the break room, you could then remove the door between the control room and live room giving better isolation. 7 No plans for an iso booth? This isn't necessary but very useful. Steve

not sure if it will work but if you look at attached photo I will try to explain my plan. If I build frame on the floor then attach and seal gyprock and then erect wall with gyprock facing the concrete wall that would mean the insulation and the frame would be facing into the control room/live room.
That's called "inside-out" construction, and this forum is the home of it! As far as I know, John himself invented that method, many years ago. And yes, it does work. The sound waves don't care which side of the mass you put your studs on. As long as you have enough mass on each leaf and a big enough air space, you can build it with studs on either side, to your heart's content! - Stuart -
