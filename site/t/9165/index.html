Eric_Desart26 July 2007#2
Joe, You should start with broadband absorption, since there is a broad range of frequencies to be handled. Panel absorbers or OK when after your basic treatment you're still stuck with specific frequencies which you couldn't get under control with your base absorption/treatment. Also panel traps can vary from bandwidth somewhat (higher or lower Q depending on design). But basically they are indeed mainly aimed to handle descrete frequencies, which is what Rod likely refers to. Sometimes ones resorts to paneltraps to safe space. But it's exactly (combined with damping by absorption effects) the more space you save the higher Q you get (becomes more narrowbanded). Helmholtz resonators is a broad concept. The slat/slot wall treatment standard here at John Sayers forum is also Helmholtz and is relative broadband from nature. Also here Rod refers to a comparable principle (I don't know if that's a measured corner type or not in that page you refer). If there is a difference or seemingly difference between Rod's book and Ethan's site or FAQ you better follow Rod's book.

A simple setup is to be found on several sites: Minitraps from Realtraps: http://www.realtraps.com/placing_mt.gif Ready Acoustics solution: http://www.readyacoustics.com/images/st ... l-view.jpg I think there are more sites with suchs setups. Hang panels on the first reflection points (walls and ceiling). And hang some more panels to get a equal distribution of absorption over the room and prevent flutter. This approach gives a broadband absorption. Further you can use the Helmholtz approach (slat/slot wall/corner absorption) of which you can find almost countless designs here in the forum. And after that I built the specific trap if there'are freqs to treat. But, what method is the right one to determine what freq should I treat?
Most rooms benefit from broadband absorption rather than trying to target specific frequencies. Let's say you have peaks or nulls (or both) at 50 Hz, 100 Hz, 150 Hz, and 200 Hz. All rooms have more problem frequencies than that, but let's say for now it's just those four. In order to improve each frequency a little, but not a lot, let's say you need four bass traps. So that's 16 bass traps, and you still don't have enough for those frequencies, and you have none for other problem frequencies. Many people don't realize this, but you can have peaks and nulls at all low frequencies. Not just those that resonate in the room. This is why broadband absorption is generally the better choice.
