I've been in the HVAC business for a little over 8 years. Adding the 90 degree turns like you propose will increase your TEL (total equivalent length) of the ductwork like proposal 1 said. However, by implementing turning vanes in those turns, it will greatly reduce the effect of the 90s. Instead of 50 feet TEL for each turn, the turning vanes can bring that down to, say, 15 feet each turn. Is your home a single story or two story? With single story, you may not notice a huge difference with airflow. However, if that trunk feeds 2nd story rooms, you may see more airflow loss. Hope I've helped you out some. Let me know if you have more questions and I'll try my best to answer.

Ross, the first thing begins with the manufacturer of the equipment...... Your air handler is designed with particular maximum static pressures in mind - and if your system is designed on the edge of those pressures as it is - then just adding more into the system would be problematic....... I have worked with a lot of equipment manufacturers who will not allow anything less than 6 times the duct width away from the duct before introducing a bend - and doing so would actually void the warranty on the gear........ If you have the paperwork on your air handler - you should be able to see what their requirements are...... Also - one way of overcoming problems like this is to add an inline booster fan to make up for the losses you incur upstream from your source.......... this would (of course) need to interlock with your air handler controls). you can use (and I always recommend) turning vanes as suggested above- you can also increase the duct size to decrease the static pressure - The bottom line is - first and formost - the guy who told you this was nonsense is full of bull - I certainly wouldn't deal with him on any of my projects.........
