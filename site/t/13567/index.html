Hello! Long-time lurker here. I finally registered. This place is clearly a HUGE wealth of knowledge, and I'm hoping those more experienced might be able to offer me some guidance. Soundman20207 September 2009#4
A number of things pop out: 1) Trying to figure out the purpose of the "doorway" in the center soffit panel, and the "arched thing" on the wall behind it? 2) The speaker axes intersect way back in the room, looks like around 70% or maybe 80% of room depth. That should probably be fixed! 3) The "doorway" thingies on the side walls appear to be at the first reflection points. But that should change anyway when #2 above is fixed, so it shouldn't be an issue. 4) Why does the room widen out suddenly at the rear, at such a large angle? Not sure, but that doesn't look like a good idea, acoustically. 5) It doesn't look like you have anywhere near enough space between the mix position and the rear wall for a diffuser to be of any use. The room is too small for good diffusion anyway, according to they way some folks see diffusion. 6) "2-foot-deep hanging bass traps for the walls"? Do you REALLY need that much trapping in the low end? What kind of problems do you anticipate that you think you'll need that much tuned trapping at that frequency? 7) The speaker soffits don't seem to have much solid area at all below the speakers: Is that just an optical illusion, or do the soffits really end just a couple of inches below the speaker? That's the things I noticed, anyway. FWIW. - Stuart -
Podgorny8 September 2009#5
First off, xSpace- Thank you. I had wondered about employing a system like this, but never knew the name for it. Soundman2020, I hugely appreciate your input. Thank you. I work every day in studios and I can see how some things are implemented, but it's difficult to get any perspective on why things were done a certain way. I really just have no experience designing rooms. Anyway, to address some of the issues you mentioned; I think the images I uploaded did not give a clear picture of the space, so I've made some that should show it better. The doorway at the front of the room goes to the machine room. I have amps and computers and power supplies that I would definitely like to keep out of the way, and cut down on their noise. The reason for the placement is that one, it seemed like unused space, and two, I like the idea of keeping cable runs as short as possible (especially considering that my console's connections are mostly unbalanced). The arched thing is a window at the front of the house, and the glass door is to let in whatever kind of natural light I can get. Regarding the placement of the soffit-mounted speakers; I do most of my listening on my nearfields, and reference on a pair of MixCubes. The Big monitors exist for checking low end, blowing-back client's hair, and just general fun listening. So I thought they should be aimed with consideration for both the mix position AND the couch - hence the intersection behind the console almost exactly between the two. Is this a terrible idea? Oh, and yes, I know they are d'appolitos and should be mounted vertically, but this was just a mock up - They will be JBL 4412s. The "doorways" at the sides of the rooms are cutouts in the bass traps for outboard racks to slide back into when I need more space for say, multiple clients or synths. I *ahem* "borrowed" this idea from East Iris Studios here in Nashville (a Tom Hidley room). The room widens at the rear because the room opens up to a staircase, and people need to be able to get down the stairs. I think the new pictures give a better perspective of this. There is about 12' between the mix position and the back wall (and about 8' to the half-wall). The back of Omnisound's ICON room is a little closer than that, and they use RPG Omniffusors on the back wall (and that room sounds pretty good). The side bass traps ARE pretty deep, but if I have the room, will it hurt? The rooms that I love seem to have a good deal of bass trapping all around, and considering that I only have the traps on the walls, and the walls are not very high due to the sloped ceiling, I should utilize whatever space I can. Besides, the racks are already taking up wall space, I may as well take advantage of that. BTW, what affect will the extra space provided by the staircase have? The soffits were based on the design John posted in another thread (http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download/file.php?id=69). Since it's an upstairs room, I can't build the soffits out of cinderblock, so I'll need to approach it with a lighter-weight ported design. And in John's design, it has wood above, and then fabric below for porting. -Kyle
