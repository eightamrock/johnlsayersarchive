knightfly28 December 2006#2
Not sure what you're going for - if you do secondary joists you shouldn't need RSIC system - but in order to span 12' with 2x6 (#2 and better, douglas fir) you'd need to use 16" centers. typically you'd build inner frames for your walls, rest those on the slab and rest the new joists on the inner walls, for a (nearly) room-in-room construction. If it's to be a single room, then a continuous ceiling would be fine; if 2 rooms and you want isolation between them, then separate ceilings/frames would be necessary. Also, interleaved joists need to be placed so cross-ties can be added or they will twist; so the new joists need to sit a bit more than half their width below the existing joists (assuming new and old are on same centers but offset) or there won't be room for cross-bracing. This means that a 2x6 sub-ceiling, using 2 layers of 5/8 gyp, would be approx. 4-1/2 to 5" lower than the bottom of the existing joists minimum. It also means that, for practicality, your floor space would be reduced by at least 5" on each wall in order to leave room for a gap between existing walls and new frames, and 2 layers of 5/8 gyp. If you can draw a sketch of what you intend, (please keep it under 750 pixels wide) we can cheerfully rip it apart and make you feel bad :wink: - seriously, without a sketch neither of us knows for SURE what the other is talking about (words have a way of meaning 6 different things to 5 different people :? ) HTH... packrat29 December 2006#6
imho, you don't have the height to be giving up inches. also in Rods book it shows how you can add layers of sheetrock between the joist up against the subfloor pinning them in with finishing nails and caulking down the side gaps (don't waste the GG on this but maybe between the sheets). Add several layers if you want as you still have plenty of air gap left. After the last layer nail a 1x2 or 2x2 down the edge if the joist to hold it all up secure. Stuff the space with normal fluffy insulation and finish with sheetrock. I'd pull down the strips and nail the rc directly on the joist.

knightfly3 January 2007#20
Assuming your spans can support the weight, in order to salvage as much ceiling height as possible in this case I'd bring your new wall frames within 1/2 inch of the existing ceiling joists (the between-joist beef-up is a good idea if your total weight doesn't exceed span capability, I've been recommending this for several years) - Then, I would mount your RSIC's up into the joist cavities such that the hat channel they support ONLY clears your actual joists by 1/8" to 1/4" - then put one layer of 5/8 rock on the hat channel, then one on the wall frame, second one on hat channel, second one on wall frame, following all the stickies about caulking, etc - this will make it so your new finished ceiling height is only the thickness of 2 layers of wallboard plus about 1/4 to 3/8" for the resilient suspension. This works because the weight of wallboard will load the rubber inserts in the RSIC's, increasing clearance between the hat channel and the joists enough to NOT cause hard contact. As I mentioned, read up on all the stickies in the REFERENCE section pertaining to methods, fastener spacing, caulking, insulation, etc... Steve
