I am curious with such a small space, wouldn't it be better to use the entire space and fill a large amount of it with effective bass trapping instead of optimizing the ratio and leaving that large empty space? My understanding is that with such small dimensions, the modes will be so high in frequency that they will be more noticeable and destructive to mixing. In any small room you need a great deal of low freq absorption which we all know takes space. gullfo7 March 2008#22
generally speaking, good ratios can help more in smaller spaces (but not too small) because of imbalance in the low frequency spacing that causes the major modal response to become apparent (and disruptive). of course the closeness of the space adds a proximity effect (like comb filtering) at mid-high frequencies so even if you get the modes right, there is still absorption needed to control the nearest reflections, SBIR, and rear wall reflections. it also all depends on how you're using the space... and once you get too small in size, then low frequencies below the room cutoff is just a big press-and-release feeling than an actual discernible sound (which still impacts your judgment and equipment performance)... so you will still need bass trapping and other controls in the small room but good ratios cannot hurt...
