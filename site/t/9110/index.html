Eric_Desart13 July 2007#7
Normally flutter is a relative easy matter to solve. Sorry, I saw wrong (thought for a moment Kendales pictures where your's also). You can use whatever absorptive panels. Mostly this flutter is relative high in freq. So relative thin absorption will do. But you also could hang bend panels (whatever) free from the ceiling, acting as diffusers. This keeps RT higher. If you use transparent panels (acrylic, polycarbonate or pvc) it even gives a cool look, and visibly won't alter the volume and height from the room.

Eric_Desart13 July 2007#11
Sometimes people refer to polys (bend panels). The idea is is to scatter the sound. Here is a low quality picture of such diffusers I used this year for a tracking room.
External image, not preserved — original: http://img131.imageshack.us/img131/6173/eddesigndiffusersie7.jpg
On the ceiling I used them twice (surface) as large. You do NOT need to use the absorption behind them on the ceiling. The use of that absorption depends on how they are mounted. It's there to prevent resonances between wall and diffuser. Note that these are hollow to the wall side (focusses sound). This are transparent panels, but that can as well be plywood or whatever other type of stable panel. I've used this principle a lot already.

Eric_Desart13 July 2007#14
:twisted: You may guess 2 times. :) Wasn't my name and © not explicit enough on the picture??? You can copy them for strict personal use. 1) Take a panel with the right bending stiffness and mass. This can be whatever (wood, special stuff, plastic). Testing a bit. It should weigh > 5 kg/m2. These ones are > 8 kg/m2. 2) Soften the edges and corners (or round corners somewhat). 3) Buy chain, eye bolts and washers. 4) Drill 4 holes. Shampher these holes on both sides (if using breakable material). 5) Use from these bands they use to fix loads on trucks (handle Ratchet Straps) http://www.americanmovingsupplies.com/i ... 25x105.jpg 6) Span them in any shape desired (and possible of course). DO NOT OVER-SPAN THEM, because that's dangerous, not for the straps (they are more than strong enough), but not to break that panel which can have ugly effects). 7) fix chains on eye bolts. The safest are closed welded eye bolts and from this closable hooks. But the choice all depends on wire thickness and tension you put on it, which in it's turn depends on the thickness and material/bending stiffness of the panels. In this version the chains are spanned diagonal to deform the panels in all directions and asymmetric. You can also use the chains parallel. You can also use thin steel cable with special spanners. There are several options possible. Check your material and test it in how far you can safely bend it. This year, for the first time I did a stupid thing and over-bend one of them. It broke, during absence of people, but it was as an explosion (dangerous). This was my fault. Just making one I still needed with a left over from a non-adjusted thickness versus radius.
