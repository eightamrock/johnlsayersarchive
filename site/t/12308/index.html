Cold19 April 2009#1
Hi, My name is Steve. I am in the design phase of a new construction studio build. This is going to be built on my suburban property to replace a poorly built (wood on dirt), 30 year old garage. OVERVIEW I wish to build a small project studio for personal use and some paying clients. Main uses: full rock, jazz band recording and mixing. My preference is to track as much at once as possible-often in the same room-depending on the quality of the musicians. I need a good sounding main room, an accurate and reasonably-sized control room, iso booth, washroom and a small break room.

more photos in the next post. HOW LOUD? WELL... Full rock bands with enthusiastic drummers around 110db spl? 1 neighbour about 50 ft. away My house (with wife sleeping) about 40ft. away Busy highway 100ft. away (average 70db noise floor outside at proposed studio location) Decent sound isolation in and out will be a priority. Outgoing isolation won't have to be "perfect" due to the traffic noise floor, but I like our neighbours (and my wife) and want to remain on good terms. I'm thinking around 60db isolation would be acceptable. BUDGET I'm expecting in the 40,000+ range. I will stop construction and wait for funds rather than make serious compromises. PLANS I'm planning to build a 25' x 25' x 16.5' (to peak) garage type structure with a small bathroom addition (outside dimensions). Here are some pictures of what I'm thinking about design wise.

I intend to use a nonvented roof system for my 1st leaf. shingles-> felt -> sheathing -> sprayed foam -> sprayed fireproofing. 1) I would like to beef this up with more mass. suggestions or thoughts on where to add mass in this system? Maybe an extra layer or two of heavy sheathing? 2)While I know I'm going to want the extra cubic feet from a vaulted ceiling in the live room, I'm debating how to do my ceiling in the control room. The slope of the roof is opposite the direction needed for an angled ceiling, but I'm not willing to reverse the control room and lose all sightlines. Any thoughts on the best use of the ceiling space. 3)General thoughts on layout, usability, accoustics? I need to keep the live room entrance in it's current general location for load in access. The break room entrance can be anywhere along the current wall or possibly moved to the wall opposite the bathroom. The bathroom location cannot be moved due to location of septic tank. 4)The nook next to the iso booth will more than likely become a storage closet, mic locker, and where the power panel is located. Any thoughts on how I frame this out? 5)Any general stupidities within the design. caveats: Have not applied for a building permit yet. Will get one before construction commences. Have not decided on wood or steel stud for the interior construction leaning towards steel stud. Exterior will be wood frame. All opinions, suggestions, comments, criticisms, snide remarks welcome! xSpace3 June 2009#4
I would spend every waking moment getting my head around this build, the external part of it at the very least. When you start adding mass to the exterior of your walls and roof you will be in a better position overall. You wouldn't want to build this new and then have to come back in and add mass in an attempt to get your exterior mass up to an acceptable level. I like your sketched drawing. I would also be interested in what type of exterior material you plan to install on this building. This is your first line of defense, the exterior, don't hack your way through it ;)

xSpace4 June 2009#6
Steve, Your vapor barrier (plastic) will go where it almost always goes in your part of the country, it should be placed on the interior most wall of the build before sheetrock is installed. If the foam you are referring to is sheathing designed for exterior wall applications, then yes you might want to consider this as well.

xSpace5 June 2009#8
Cold wrote:
Hi Xspace, Just clarifying, are you saying that in a studio build that has double wall construction, the vapour barrier goes on the inside wall just before drywall? I had thought of the dew point being closer to the exterior wall....
Correct, but the barrier always goes to the inside of an assembly in a colder climate, it isn't because of the double wall configuration...and it is also based on typical type building materials. Wood sidings, batt type insulation, etc.
Cold wrote:
I was thinking of sprayed foam on the inside of the interior wall, say 2 inches thick, enough that the dew point would fall inside the foam and negating the need for a plastic vapour barrier. I was going to add pink insulation over this to help with the spring in msm. But, if the area between the two walls is considered cold zone... Have to give this one a good think and a little research. Thank you for the feedback. Steve
I can be corrected, but I don't know if sprayed foam is going to change anything about how the cold interacts with the wall assembly. Some polyurethane foams are water-vapor permeable, if this is the type you are considering using.
Cold5 June 2009#9
Thanks again for the reply, I was thinking of using a closed cell, non water permeable sprayed foam. I will talk to some of the pros and pick there brains. This is not a pressing issue yet, but I do want to make sure that if I seal up the exterior cladding air tight, I don't trap the moisture at the dew point between two sealed surfaces. I believe your idea of putting the vapour barrier just before the drywall should work as long as there is enough air movement between the walls to allow moisture to escape. What I don't want to have to do is compromise my exterior wall so it doesn't mold or rot. In the climate I live in (rain forest), water in all its forms has to always be a consideration. Thanks, Steve p.s. any other thoughts about prefered methods of adding mass to the exterior walls? What else am I forgetting?
