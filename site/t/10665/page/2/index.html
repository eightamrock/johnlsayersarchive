i think you should ask your town what their definition of public means. Eran, I ran into the same thing here... EXCEPT... In my case, I made sure that the permit clerk and the inspectors knew that this was a business venture... why? Better to go back and deal with the commercial structure issue head on, than getting half way through and something goes wrong... that is if indeed you wish to operate as a business where you will have a work for hire situation. rod gervais8 June 2008#31
I don't know about everywhere - but here - in CT - a commercial public facility means just that - open and accessible to the public. If they can walk in and cut a deal to do business with you - then you are a commercial facility. A private studio that sometimes earns a dollar is not always operating a commercial business - for example........ you happen to be an artist - and your friend Joe comes over and says he wants a portrait of his wife to surprise her on her birthday - and he'll pay you a thousand dollars to paint it......... taxable income - yes - commercial business open to the public - not by defination. Or - your neighbor pays you 50 bucks to tune up his lawnmower - and you choose to do it in your garage - that doesn't make your garage a mechanics shop. This because your business dealings are limited to family - friends - and not open to John Q Public. ON THE OTHER HAND - hang a sign out on your garage that you do tune-ups for lawn mowers - or "Please stop in and have your portrait painted" - or run an ad that your studio is now open for sessions and tell them to call to set up their recording time - All of those are commercial business ventures open to the public becuase anyone in the world can partake of your services. Rod
