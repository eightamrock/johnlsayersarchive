In the control room, I will not be doing a room-inside-a-room. In fact, hardly anything will be done at all insulation wise for that room. I'm far enough away from my neighbors to where the level I'm mixing at won't be an issue.
Aren't you concerned about the possibility of outside noises disturbing your mixing sessions? Such as wind, rain, road noise, neighbor noise, noises from inside the house, etc. 2)
I'll put in non-permanent sides later, making it 6 sided. I don't know if those should be giant bass traps or just wood panels... what do u think?
Bass traps don't really have "sides". They are just bundles of absorption, so I doubt that they will do much to change the geometry of your room. Are you concerned about flutter echo, or room modes? 3)
Recording Room Outer Leaf: 11' 9" x 10' 9" x 9' tall 2x4 Wooden Frame 1/2" drywall
1/2 inch drywall isn't much! :shock: Especially if you are hoping to isolate a drum kit! 1/2" is too thin to be useful, and has other undesirable characteristics. You need at least 5/8 drywall, and probably a couple of layers of it. 4)
Inner Leaf: 2x4 Wooden Frame spaced 2" away from outer wall
Are you going to build that inside-out or normal? 5)
5/8" drywall (two layers, glued together with GG)
Green Glue is not glue: You cannot use it to "glue together" two sheets of drywall. You still need to nail or screw the second layer of drywall into the studs. 6)
1 wall will be wood paneling instead of drywall... no details to provide on that yet
In order to get good isolation, that "wood paneling" needs to have the same mass as the two layers of 5/8 drywall. If you go with less mass, then your room will not isolate well. 7)
Floor: Wood built directly on concrete slab
When you say "wood built directly on concrete slab", are you talking about laminate flooring, parquet flooring, sold wood flooring, or something like that, that is laid directly on the concrete with on air space below it? If so, great! If not, then beware... 8) What about the ceiling? You didn't mention anything about how you plan to do the ceiling. It should be the same construction as the walls (two-leaf, lots of mass) if you want to isolate your room. If you just do the walls and not the ceiling, then you wasted an awful lot of money on the walls... The entire room must have the same level of isolation, on all sides, including windows and doors. If you don't treat any one part, then that part defines the isolation for the entire room. 9) You also didn't mention HVAC and electrical. Those are two very important points for a studio. The HVAC alone could eat up your entire budget. 10) What are this big square things in the rear of the control room, on each side of the door? Are those closets? How do you plan to deal with them? - Stuart -

Soundman202027 June 2010#6
Great to see progress, Neal! Now that you have the walls open, you have the opportunity to "beef them up"! Meaning that you can install strips of drywall INSIDE the walls, between the studs, to add extra mass to the leaf. Carefully cut strips of drywall so they fit in neatly, hold them in place with cleats, and seal around the edges with caulk. Repeat as necessary (adding more layers of "beef"). Presto! Instant mass. Regarding the 1/2" stuff: If possible, remove it and replace it with 5/8, or if you reay cannot do that then at least beef it up with an extra layer of 5/8, either on top of the exiting 1/2, or beefing up from inside the wall, same as above.
Any idea how much sound isolation will be lost by not installing two 5/8" layers on the outside leaf?
Lots! Using just a single layer of 1/2" for one of your leaves is not very useful, for several reasons. One of those is the low mass, another is the lack of stiffness (which is what governs your very low end isolation), and yet another is the high resonant frequency of the drywall itself. Combined, they rob you of isolation. So I'd really suggest that you beef it up with extra layers of 5/8, or just bite the bullet and replace it with 2 layers of 5/8. - Stuart -

Soundman202029 June 2010#9
I believe I'll have to glue the drywall strips to the outer leaf's drywall.
Nope! Bad idea! They need to act as two separate layers, not one monolithic layer. Just lay the second layer on top of the first layer, between the studs, and tack small pieces of wood sideways into the studs to hold the drywall in place. Or even just plain finish nails, driven diagonally into the studs right behind the drywall. Those are "cleats".
but "Green Glue" was mentioned about 2500 times
Green Glue actually isn't glue at all: It is a special acoustic damping material, that you spread between layers of drywall to greatly improve low frequency isolation. Great stuff, if you have the budget. - Stuart -
