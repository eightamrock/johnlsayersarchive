Soundman202022 September 2009#2
I think what he is planning is sand in the walls and ceiling, instead of rockwool...from what I can gather! ... So is sand better than rockwool?
That's kind of like asking "Is a fish better than a bicycle?". :) They are entirely different things! But sand cannot be used to replace rock wool, as they serve two entirely different purposes in acoustics. Sand is mass, and assists with isolation. Rock wool is absorption, and assists with treatment. Mass resists the passage of sound by sheer brute force, so it is good at isolation, bad at treatment. Absorption does not stop sound: it merely converts some of it to low ground heat. So it is good at treatment, bad at isolation. Two entirely different, almost mutually exclusive, concepts. For example, if you have specified a proper 2-leaf MSM wall, built from two leaves of drywall with an air gap filled with rockwool, then you CANNOT substitute sand for rockwool! Doing so would destroy the MSM properties of the wall (and probably destroy the wall, too!), making into a single-leaf wall. That does not mean that the wall won't work to isolate sound. It does mean that you have changed the principle of physics on which the wall works from "resonant filter" to "mass law". Two entirely different principles. In this case, your wall would be governed by mass-law equations, not MSM equations. (Apart from that, the weight of the sand inside the walls will probably rip it apart, but that's another matter....). You'd be limited to 6 dB increase per mass doubling, so you'd need a very massive wall to get good isolation. That's not to say it wont work: just that you cannot simply substitute without taking into account that you are using entirely different principles of acoustics and physics. And construction, too. I'd think three times about using a contractor who tells you he can substitute sand for rockwool in an MSM wall..... Then I'd turn and run, real fast. I'd also be rather wary of a contractor who thinks that a single-leaf mass-law governed wall can do the same job as a 2-leaf MSM wall for the same price and with the same mass and thickness... - Stuart -

Hi, Sand works in our walls. But it has to be dry and compacted. And if you're going to want anything heavy hung afterwards think about it before (drilling). We have 20cm hollow concrete blocks filled with sand. In some places we have 2 such walls, in others we have an outer 40cm stone wall. In all cases there's a 30cm air gap and we have rockwool stuck on the inner face of each cavity wall (not stuck with glue...shear...). And concrete floors are isolated as are walls to ceilings. Works a treat. http://montesaaudio.com
