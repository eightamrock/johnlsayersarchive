I agree with John completely here (as if that is surprising - can't think of a time where I ever disagreed with him - John is a pretty sharp cookie) - but would another reason for those who may be reading this - and that is because the steel frames most of you would end up working with are what is referred to as "Light Gauge Framing" typically 25 ga steel. These materials are great for high and mid frequency isolation - in fact a 4" LG stud wall with drywall attached to each of it's faces - is equivalent to a 2x4 wood stud wall, with resilient channel with the same number of the sheets of drywall in STC rating. BUT - that is where it ends - in order to control the lower frequency regions you need not only mass - but also stiffness, and for that you need structural steel framing - and even with structural framing - you will never achieve the stiffness you need with less than 4" heavy gauge members in normal height walls..... Stick with wood - and - if you have the room and want some extra bang for the buck - opt for 2x6 frame at 24" centers over 2x4 at 16" centers - the same number of board feet of lumber in the body of the wall (a slight increase in the plates) BUT - again - an increase in the isolation of low frequencies..........
