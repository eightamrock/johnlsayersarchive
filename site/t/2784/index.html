Sorry.. :oops: I've been lurking around the forums here for quite some time now (enjoying it thoroughly BTW) and after much agonizing, thought I'd finally break down and ask some questions. I've been torturing myself with a studio design for my basement and am utterly unhappy with everything I can come up with (including a dual trapazoidal room design that I just couldn't get enough symmetrical listening space for). So...... HELP!!!! (please?). As a precursor, I've looked at everything here including all the basement designs and caveats. At SAE (though a bit basic for me). Scoured the web for other studios to use as an example, but to no avail (including John's "Blue Bear", BTW as you're sure to see some similarities; then there's the damn stairs...). I've built a pair of great sounding commerically used rooms in the past (80's - yeah it's been a while) so I do have some experience with that, but this one is really knawing away at me... (by now, some of you may be questioning my judgement in wanting to build another room). So (he takes a deep breath and, apologetically, spews forth, a long winded post) ... It's pretty much a traditional 8" block construcion basement in a center hall colonial. However, there are several problems to contend with. The first, is a really low ceiling (at 7' to stringers). The next is a lovely 6x10 prop girder running the center (just off by a foot) length of the basement bringing the ceiling down to about 6' there), supported by 4" steel posts at 7' 8" intervals. (see pic with rough layout). That girder effectively divides the basment into 12' and 14' halves. The length of the drawing is 70' 8" x 28". Of that, 49' is the basement (the right hand side). Out of that, about 30' x 28' is mine to use; plus or minus a bit (to the wall that's right of the space labeled "Shop")... I have a few requirements: 1. A large and visually (and preferably spacially) contiguous tracking space capabale of housing about 8-10 musicians (horns and rhythm sec). It's a squeeze but.. I like live spaces and don't have much use for dead ones. After all, you can deaden a live space, but you just can't go the other way without involving the local demolition experts :wink: and my trusty home-made Plates ... A "removable" drum iso space at one end could be nice. Losing tracking space in favor of a larger control room is not cool for me, no matter what the "current" thinking on that on is so, please.. I also want to keep the concrete block walls as much as possible in there (I'll add hanging attenuation). 2. An Iso booth that can be used for high-volume sound sources (hence it's current location surrounded by 8" block with an 8" concrete porch on top of it all of which (save the top foot of the block, surrounded by earth; it is a basment after all). Visibility is not necessary. BTW, that porch/iso space is 6' wide to center and about 8' long (with the same damn 7' ceiling - ungh) incase you were wondering. 3. I'd need some natural light in the control room (cause without it, I'll die; kind of an anti-vampire sort of thing). Since I do my mixing at lower levels, noise is not an issue. Besides, my neighbors are pretty far away. In the pic, I've put the CR on the side with a hopper window that gets the best light. There is an addtional hopper that get's some light on the far, long wall (about center mass of the space). 4. The control room has to have enough room for my MR-2 console (at about 11 feet wide (by 39" deep) with a side car- 9 without - I could rig a sliding patchbay underneath (though I always thought that was asking for trouble...) and a recorder sofit (for my A80 widebody 2" and 1/4" VU) plus iso space for any computers/faned outboards (cause them damn fans make me crazier than this process has made me...). There also should be a way to get that damn anti-christ sized (but GREAT sounding) console in/out of there without entirely dismantling it (and the room to boot). 5. I can't deal with a mixing position that has a dramatically tangental line of sight to the tracking space. I just don't like it and would never get used to it, though I know that it does open up some space use possibilities.. (Been in a few and it just feels too wierd for me). 6. All the usual (though not in the floating extreme ) CR to tracking room iso concerns (eg double walls..). However, I am a bit curious as to how I can possibly work in a "strung" floating ceiling without losing more than about 3'4" of head room (cause 7' is damn claustrophobic attack inducing for most non-splunkers to begin with). I do have some interestingly stupid ideas on that, btw. I'll save it for a possible future discussion though as, well... 7. I've made no provision for HVAC yet as the routing thereof will obviously be influenced by the layout. The drawing does have a pic of the current 5 ton heat exchanger for the A/C that should be adequate to run a split off of ( though I might be replacing it soon as it is kinda gettin' old). All my heat is electric and staying that way (radiant ceiling panels - ask me if you need more about this - I'm sold on em'). I've tried tracking under the the ones in the rest of the house and can't even get single coil guitar pickups to get a buzz off of 'em though I can't fathom why given the field those panels can supposedly put out... 8. I'd really like a shop space for myself as well (it's the one that's in the pic, btw) cause I've gotta have someplace to keep that bunch of crap I call "gear" (also known as vintage...) in the top shape it's in now. :twisted: There are also a few minor, but immovable (lest I file for divorce. :oops: ) limitations: 1. There is an area that is to the left of the stairs (and shop) that looks like it has some gym equipment in it and some walled in space. All that space is OFF LIMITS as it's my wife's workshop. 2. I'm not doing any structural changes (like getting rid of the header or moving its support posts). That includes digging out the space to get an extra foot or 2 of head room (not possible anyway due to the miserably townies and the underground stream to drive the last nail into the coffin on that). 3. I'm comittied to using this space as building out is not an option (Or was that I should be comitted; I can't remember anymore). The garage is also not usable (cause the wife needs her indoor parking - I don't even have that...). Don't ask me to argue it. I've come to accept it as gospel. And if I have to, well.... 4. My budget is kinda meager but "reasonable" if I stage thiis correctly (again, I've done this before so I know what I can get myself into :wink: and out of for that matter). It's going to be DIY (also done that previously). As far as the pic is concerned, please don't lecture me about symmetrical listening spaces and parallel surfaces yet... I know.. It's just a rough that's more use as a scaling refenence (to give y'all a bit more breathing room/creative space) and I've done "those drawings" but again, was not even remotely happy with any of them. I know that because of the constraints I have, it's going to, at best, be a real compromise but.. Whew... /longwindedpost. (At least it was punctuated...) So, any help that you kind, generous folk can offer in the layout department would be graciously appreciated. Regards and thanks in advance! Eric Z.
