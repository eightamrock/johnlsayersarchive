Innovations2 August 2004#2
Well, here is how I understand it. In a typical wall, starting from the studio side, you have absorbtive materias which control sound inside the studio. Then you have the sheathing is on the surface of the wall, then there are the studs filled with insulation, then the air gap, then the second wall's studs with insulation, then the opposite side sheathing. in the inside out wall you have some sort of fabric on the studio side to hide the insulation, then the studs filled with insulation, THEN the sheathing, then the air gap, then the second walls's studs filled with insulation, then the opposite side sheathing. The advantage of the system is that the insulation on the studio side does double duty in controling sound inside the space. The disadvantage is that the total air gap between the two leaves is narrower and tha the wall must be sheathed on the ground and then tilted up, which can be heavy.
dymaxian4 August 2004#3
For long walls, you can build inside-out walls in sections, then fasten them together and seal the joints. There are two other advantages that I don't see mentioned very often: FIRST- for fire safety, if you're building double walls, you've gotta put draft-stopping of some kind on the top of the wall system. This means putting something solid between the walls, which will be tough to do if you're trying to structurally separate them. Having the drywall on the INSIDE of the wall cavity will make your building inspector feel a lot better about your project. SECOND- all your electrical stuff is INSIDE the drywall, so you don't need to carve a whole bunch of 2"x4" holes in your sound-proofing. Obviously you can surface-mount, too, but with inside-out walls your outlets can be flush with the acoustic treatment, and look just like a normal wall system, and you'll only need to drill a couple holes to get the wiring into the room. These two are the points that sell me on this idea the most.

Yes, worth pointing out that you will not be allowed to use inside-out on a wall that requires a fire rating and may have trouble getting away with it on a structural wall. The reason is that the gypsum normally protects the studs from a fire inside the room.

hey all, to me, the benefit was space. I didn't have the 4" to loose in the room for the client. I was already working with low low ceiling height and small space. However..... I learned a few added benefits, 1. Electrical - You only have to punch one hole in your system and mount a large j-box to then distribute to all plugs lights etc. Didn't have to punch 20 holes in system. 2. Much faster to drywall and caulk on floor rather than in up and needing ladders, etc. - I built in 8' sections so weight tilting up would not be an issue. 3. I hate to mud. I suck. this way you are anly caulking cus the wall is inside out. 4. Cost. yes it is small in the hundreds of dollars, but time = labor cost and nailing a bunch of boards up to hang fabric from is a pain in the ass and it cost to pay someone plus the matirial. This way you are ready to fabric as soon as your walls are up. 5. time. I built an entire studio in 5 1/2 weeks with this system and it usually takes me 3 months. Building the walls on the floor and the ceiling on the floor and hoisting it all up takes much less time than building in place up and down ladders, holding rock over your head waiting for someone to screw it in, caulking over head, trying to get nail boards in and hitting studs over your head. Built on the floor I had my entire ceiling system built and in place in less than two days. 6. Hvac - You can duct through the open joist in the ceiling system and go anywhere you want without punching a new big ass hole in your ceiling. Is it hard yes. Sealing all the joining units takes paying attention to detail and trusting the people you work with to stop everything if they see an opening. hoisting the units to the ceiling and resting them on your inner leaf is a bitch on the first one, but then you see how it can be sooooo much easier and the rest go in very fast and effecient. Not a sales pitch, hell there is nothing to buy, just my 2 cents and how it worked for me. Check local codes and talk with the plans reviewer in person to explain the whole thing and it will go very smooth. Jai
