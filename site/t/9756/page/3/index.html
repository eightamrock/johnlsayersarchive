BradJacob24 January 2008#65
Thanks man! Yeah those "walls" I had to frame out were a real bitch... If I did a regular sofit, the horizantal studs (because of their thickness) would have pushed my finished ceiling height below my sliding glass door opening. With the Isomax clips, hat channel and the two layers of rock. So building these "decending walls" from the joists saved precious inches, and even gave me some back. Metal studs are great for this because you can get into tight spots with a screwgun and attach them to the wood joists. And if you need to move something, it's a snap. BradJacob4 March 2008#75
OK fellas, so it's been a little over a month since my last "real" update. And as promised, the pix of the COMPLETED studio will follow!! Lessons Learned 1. If you plan to take on a project like this, plan to spend the money to do it right. 2. Plan, design, and do it all over again. Seek out the expertise of the people on this site - they're pros in what they do, and even the non-pros can offer experience. Experience that saved my ass a number of times. Learn and use (Google's) Sketchup. This is the one thing that WILL let you see how your project will look when complete. Love that Sketchup!! 3. Buy Rod's book, and the read it. Then read it again. 4. If you do the work yourself, plan on the project taking A LOT of time. This has the potential to make some serious waves (the bad kind) within the household. Needless to say, I blew off my family for 5 months. Really, I did... 5. Make a project plan, create a timeline. It helps to keep you on track. 6. After each day of work, CLEAN UP after yourself. Nothing makes you feel better than a clean area. Nothing is safer either. I cleaned every night and never go hurt - thankfully. 7. And remember this - there is no more important safety rule than to wear (these) - safety glasses! ($5 for the first person to guess that quote...) Photos Ok so, I have a TON of pictures (I tried to be very detailed) so I'll let them do the talking. I'm also going to create a photo-essay of my acoustic panel build - so check it out in the "Acoustics" forum. Enough with my babble, check out the pix!
