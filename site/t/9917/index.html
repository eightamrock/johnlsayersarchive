orangenumerik21 December 2007#4
Any opinion on my r-30's kraft paper backing facing up AND down in the joists? That has me nervous. I had a hard time deciphering which direction would be the best solution anyway... vapor barrier to the warm side (which side will that be?), hi-freq. reflective propeties of the paper vs. the insulation side (but it will have drywall or ceiling tiles over it), will it form another "leaf"? (even if it is up against another surface either way and I don't have any substantial sound isolation with all the flanking anyway)...
Hi CidyZoo, What you need to understand here is that the dreaded so called "third leaf effect " will only occur if your paper backing is located in between an air space appart form the two outer leaves.The paper backing must be placed against one or the other outer leaves (touching) to avoid the third leaf Fx. Now as for the vapour barrier fuction of that paper backing i'd say to place it against your inner leaf. Vapour barrier are used to protect the building structure from moisture. Placing it on the inner leaf will keep moisture from building up between your outter leaf (floor above) and inner leave (studio ceiling). Hope this is clear enough. You may ask Rod about that, he's the man of the situation for that kind of question.

I wish I could take credit for all the work! I still can't really do any substantial work with my left arm, so I am more a supervisor... The contractor I have is doing a great job. His son is a touring musician, so he was familiar (vicariously anyway) with a lot of the terminology I was using and the concepts of sound absorption, reflection, diffusion, isolation, etc. I think he is excited to do something different than standard house construction and then he gets bragging rights about "doing a studio". I have been very careful to always make sure he understands what I am doing, as well as explaining the things that I am making compromises on and "why". So he realizes that I'm not doing it as "right" as I could be. We started by discussed beefing up layers on the ceiling, but joist loading, ceiling height, and expense were going to be an issue. So given that limitation (the weakness in the isolation potential of the ceiling), most sound "proofing" options were off the table, (except in putting together the iso booth). We focused mainly on acoustical treatments for recording and mixing in the rooms. He has made some great construction suggestions that I wouldn't have seen since he has building trades experience. I just got another shipment of putty packs and acoustical sealant today, so I can do some work too (no heavy lifting)! :wink: ~ Bill
