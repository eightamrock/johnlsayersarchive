jhbrandt4 October 2010#2
Hi James, welcome. Well, you don't need strange angles to have a good CR. The most important foundation for a good room is dimensions. There are several 'good' ratios published.. in a nutshell; you should make the Control room at least 2500 cubic feet or 70 cubic meters in volume. The dimensions should support modes down to the bottom of the music spectrum - 20Hz and have a dense and even distribution. Only rectangles will give you this. The walls that are usually seen inside a control room are very thin plywood and/or fabric which is covering treatment calculated to give the enclosure a balanced acoustical environment. I have a few calculators that you can download and use here http://www.jhbrandt.net/pubs.html and also some of my papers. Much excellent info at the GIK site http://www.gikacoustics.com/education.html and Realtraps site http://www.realtraps.com/articles.htm. Cheers, John

bwilkinson5 October 2010#11
One other thing that you may want to consider. Two and a half acres isn't nearly as large as you might think - especially for a large studio build. You will definitely have more than enough room to build a good size studio, but from a distance to neighbors, you may be surprised how close you are. As an example, I live on two acres of land, and am designing my studio (ever so slowly). Even with that land, my closest neighbor is 130-150 feet away. A drum set doesn't lose a whole lot of impact at that distance. Two houses down (approximately 400 feet away), there is a band who practices in their pole building - admittedly, it is not acoustically insulated at all - and I can hear them clearly at my house (estimating, I never measured it, but at my house, their volume is still in the 50dB range). Just take that into consideration as to the isolation you will need.
