Soundman202021 November 2011#6
I thought about having the CR facing the 7'1.75" wall ... I just was having troubles visualizing how to layout the room so the speakers do not focus the sound too close to the 7' wall
I'm not sure I understand: If that wall is the front of your room, the speakers will be pointing the OTHER way, AWAY from that wall, towards the left of your drawing (the wall labeled "10'11 1/16"). So there's no reason why the speakers would be "focusing sound" on the front wall.
I guess I just am having troubles with the speaker angles.
If you set them up correctly, they'll be angled as normal, with the acoustic axes intersecting at an angle of 60° a few inches behind your mix position (the place where your head will be), and your mix position will be set up so that your ears are roughly 38% of the room depth (front to back). Looking at your rough sketch, I don't see that there will be a problem with doing that.
or force the CR to become a narrow 7' wide room
Well, it will have to start that narrow at the front (a lot narrower, actually, to allow for the isolation), but it will widen out the further back you go, getting to maybe 10 feet or so (depending on how long you want the room to be, what ratio you choose, how high the ceiling is, etc.) You didn't mark the angle of that front wall, but assuming it is the same as the other tiny wall, at 105°, that's fine. That gives you 7.5° splay on each side wall, which is fine for a normal control room. Probably not enough to get a really good RFZ, but a good start! I'd suggest that your next step should be to put some numbers on The Big Question: how loud you are, and how quite you need to be. Measure those levels with a sound level meter, and check your local noise ordinance with your municipality, then based on those figure out the amount of isolation that you need, in terms of decibels of transmission loss. Based on that number, you can then choose a construction method that will give you that number while also matching your budget and construction skills. That brings up another question: Your budget. What do you have in mind for this? It would help us if we had a number, since different isolation methods come in at different price points. It's no use us suggesting exotic materials if you are on a show-string budget, and equally pointless suggesting cheap but bulky materials if you have the money to save space by using thinner, more dense but more expensive materials. So a real-world dollar estimate would be useful. (If you have no idea and need a ball-park figure, call a couple of local contractors and ask them for their flat rate per square foot to convert an unfinished basement into a luxury guest bedroom with on-suite bathroom. (You won't have a bathroom in your studio, of course, but add that in to get a more realistic quote).
since the contruction is just underway,
:shock: How much of that area is built already? Hopefully, none of it! ?!?! You can save a lot, and end up with a much better studio, if you start our right with the outer leaf, instead of having to adapt to a bad situation later! - Stuart -

Soundman202023 November 2011#13
That's the basic idea, yes, but you need to move the speakers much closer to the front wall: the soffits should be as close as you can get them (allowing reasonable space for the speakers, ventilation, etc.) to maximize room volume. You can adjust the speaker spacing and angle as necessary to accommodate. That will also help to get your mix position where it should be: roughly 38% of room length (front wall to back wall). Right now, it appears to be in the exact center of the room: not good! You also need to turn that entire setup slightly, to get symmetry. Right now it is parallel to the left wall, and it should not be: draw an axis centered between the two side walls, and use that to align your room. Symmetry is critical! I would also put the door in the center of the rear wall, not over to one side: the corners are good for bass trapping, so you don't want to lose that by putting the door where a bass trap could be. - Stuart -

Soundman202023 November 2011#15
Looking better! You could bring your speakers a bit closer together, and you can change the angle a bit if you have to. 60° intercept is ideal, but you can go as high as 90° if there is no other choice. Try to make your soffits as wide as possible for maximum effect: The purpose of soffits is to act as an infinite baffle, which improves bass response, and eliminates all of the nasty stuff associated with the front wall (reflections, phasing, comb filtering, etc.). To accomplish that, it needs to be as close to "infinite" in size as you can make it ("infinite" is obviously a very relative term here!!!) You also need to angle your rear wall, so it is parallel to the plane between the speakers (perpendicular to the center line of the room). - Stuart -
