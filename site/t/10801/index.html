TexTone wrote:
Sandersd....what are your thoughts about the LATEST layout and description of the build? 2x6 base/top plates and staggered studs with double sheetrock with greenglue and resilient channel. What is your take on my control room ratio? Thanks!!
I think you're asking a lot from a 20'x20' space. Step back and look at your overall goals: what exactly do you want to do in the room? You have to be specific and realistic. First, define the concept: It's obviously too small for a commercial studio; You might be able to make it work as a "project studio" or songwriter's demo studio, but it's still too small for tracking full bands, especially with acoustic drums. By the time you add the necessary isolation you'd be left with little room. I guess it could be done if the guitarist and bassist went direct in the control room, but it'd be very tight, uncomfortably so. But it could be done. The problem with that senario is you have no flexibility with recording techniques. So first, clarify exactly what you want to do with the space and how you will go about utilizing it. What will be your work process? As for room ratios, there are endless combinations that will work. Pick one that fits your layout. If you go with a non-rectangular shape, forget room ratios. Did you look through the links at professional designs in small spaces as suggested? Many current designs use a wide, shallow space for mixing. You might consider that. Once you've settled on a layout you really should construct it virtually in sketchup. You'll work through the design and construction problems you'll encounter during the build and prepare solutions. As for the construction details and isolation requirements: if you plan on acoustic drums you'll need maximum isolation - it doesn't get any louder than that and double walls are the way to provide required isolation. To summarize: 1) Refine your concept 2) Finalize the room layout 3) Develop the construction details. Here's my take on your original layout.
