Soundman202014 March 2009#2
Hi Paul, and welcome! First, regarding your room layout, it looks like you have your mix position almost exactly at 50% of the room length. Worst possible spot! You also have your speakers tight up against the walls. Not a good idea. I'd suggest that you move everything forward, and also get your speakers away from the walls, closer to the center of the room. You want to get your ears at roughly 38% of the front-back distance of the room. (That 38% figure is not an "engraved in stone" rule: its just a starting position You might need to move back or forwards a bit. but it does represent the theoretical "best" position for your ears). Your speakers should be angled such that the imaginary axes projected out of the front of each speaker (from the acoustic center of the speaker) intersect at an angle of 60 degrees, about 20 cm behind your head, more or less. That will place the axes aimed right at your ears, right in the middle of the sweet spot. (Theoretically. Your mileage may vary...) You might want to consider soffit-mounting (flush-mounting) your speakers. That helps to tighten up the bass and the stereo image. Regarding the measurement of the room acoustics: don't wast your time with an RTA. It will only give you 1/3 octave resolution, which is way inadequate. There can be half a dozen dips and peaks hidden inside a range of 1/3 octave, and they can average out to flat response, so your real issues will be disguised and hidden by an RTA. Instead, go to Ethan Winer's web site ( http://www.realtraps.com ) and look for the instructions he has on how to test your room with 1 Hz. precision(!). He provides the instructions, and also the files you will need, for free! Just download them and run them. Or download ETF software ( http://www.etfacoustic.com/ ), or EQ Wizard software (their web site seems seems to be offline right now: Google it.) These will give you a very accurate detailed representation of what is going on in your room, which will suggest the best way of treating it.
As you already know bass traps and other treatments are not cheap,
They can be, if you build them yourself! Or at least, they will be cheaper than purchased ones, and just as effective.
... and we just installed new carpet on the floors,
The general recommendation here is to not put carpet on the floor. Keep it live (maybe laminate wood flooring, stained concrete, etc.), and treat the ceiling instead. There are many arguments in favor of this, but one of the most logical is that your ears are accustomed to always being the exact same distance from the floor, wherever you go, your entire life. So your brain is vary accustomed to hearing reflections off the floor. But the distance to the ceiling varies all the time, so your brain is not very accustomed to dealing with reflections from ceilings. So the idea is to keep your ceiling dead, and floor live. And you'll need a cloud at all of the first reflection points on the ceiling anyway, so you might just as well do the entire ceiling. If the floor turns out to be TOO live after you have treated the rest of the room, you can always spread around some throw rugs. It's hard to spread throw-rugs on the ceiling! :)
We have looked into different pricing for foam treatments, to find out that we just cannot afford to treat every inch of the room.
DIY treatment using common building materials like mineral wool and fiberglass are very effective, and much cheaper than buying manufactured products. And you almost certainly do NOT want to treat every inch of the room! That would make it very unpleasant to work in.
Any insight on where to get a good price would also be appreciated.
Talk to Ethan, at Real Traps (see link above). Check out his web site. He posts here regularly too, so he might notice your thread and offer some advice. There are other manufacturers out there too, but research the products carefully before you buy. Not all of them are as honest and upright as Ethan! Watch out for "cheap" treatments fro some places. The usual "Caveat Emptor" rules apply: if it sounds too good to be true, it probably is. If the recognized manufacturers sell a certain type of treatment for around US$ 200, but someone claims to have a bigger and better model for only US$ 29.95, then red flags should be popping up all over! I'd also suggest that you download SketchUp from Google, and use that to model your room in 3D. We all use it here, and it makes it easy to interchange files. - Stuart -

Soundman20203 April 2009#7
Around the ceiling (pictured above) there is a slanted wood trim about 12" wide. Should I tear down all the trim around the room, or just in the soffit cavity, or neither?
Hey Paul. I think if that were my room, I'd tear out all that wood trim. 12" is a lot of flat surface to have in corners where bass traps belong.
Is it going to offer an acoustic benefit by keeping it there?
On the contrary, in my opinion it will interfere with your acoustics. I suspect that wood would so all kinds of nasty things to sound, reflecting it around at strange angles. And it's also occupying the best position for bass traps.
Does the speaker need to be centered in the soffit wall?
As close as you can get it within reason, yes. It doesn't really matter if there is just a small difference, but if your speaker is 2 inches away from one edge while being 24 inches away from the other, then something is wrong!
I noticed it will be tough to maintain the 60º angle between them and put them in the center of their soffit walls.
Then increase the angle! You can also go to 90º intersection angle if you really need to. In a small, tight room the geometry sometimes just doesn't add up to allow you to use a 60º angle and also have your speakers and mix position located sensibly. So try 90º, or anything else in between. It will work fine too, the only caveat being that your sweet spot will be "squashed" into an oval shape, but that shouldn't be a problem. - Stuart -
