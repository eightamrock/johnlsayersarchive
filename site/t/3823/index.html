drfrankencopter2 June 2005#2
Yes, your posts will affect your monitr's early reflections. It's really a function of the dispersion pattern of your monitors, but if you can see the monitors in a mirror placed on the post from the listening position then there's a reflected path direct to your ears. Basically you will want to treat the hell out of those posts. What size are they? If they're less than 10" or so, then all you need to worry about is freuencies above 250 Hz, which is easy to treat with some fairly thick 703 (2-4"). Frequencies below 250Hz will just pass around the posts as if they were'nt really there. Also, don't forget about the horizontal post, which can cause some flutter problems...just treat the front of it with 703. Another idea would be to bring your monitors closer in to the listening position. Ideally, look at a 120 degree arc coming oit of each monitor (i.e. 60 deg on either side of the 'direct line of fire'...you don't want early reflections from these angles to hit the listening position within less than 20 ms (<20 feet additional distance versus the direct path), or if they are less than 20 ms, they should be -20dB relative to the direct sound (i.e. absorbed) . Yet another option is to angle the fronts of the posts so that reflections are re-directed away from the listening positon.... Cheers, Kris

drfrankencopter3 June 2005#10
You'll definitely need to move that LCD monitor back. As drawn in your last sketch, it's worse than the posts in terms of early reflections. Also, consider your desk carefully...monitor splash off of the desk surface can cause bad comb filtering. Basically, the desk needs to be as small as you can get away with, particularly for mastering. Consider angling it downwards to direct any reflections away from you. Kris

drfrankencopter4 June 2005#15
You still need to consider the 'console splash' . Look at your setup from the side, and see if sound soming at 60 degrees or so from the monitor will hit the desk surface and reflect back to the listening position. This will influence the angle of the desk, and how far it can protrude. You can play with both the desk's height, position, and angle, also monitor height position, and angle can be adjusted. There's alot to consider.....but better to do this first, before building, and then considering.... Cheers, Kris
