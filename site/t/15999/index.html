Like the title says, I'm researching the idea of using 'inside out' wall construction for my studio build. There are A LOT of factors for starting any business, and I'm in the process of writing a business plan, as well as scouting locations and drawing up designs, etc... In other words, I don't have any specific designs just yet; I'm just trying to decide which construction techniques make best use of my budget. But, for sake of discussion, here's some basic dimensions/rooms I'm thinking about (all dimensions in feet): Control Room: 10H x 12.6W x 15.9L Live Room: 10H x 16W x 23.3L Iso Booth: 8H x 9.12W x 11.12L Proposed Budget: $25,000USD (the more I read this forum, the more I realize how little this is, sadly :? ) Ok, so the "inside out" wall makes sense in theory. Flip the wall around and the insulation in between the stud cavities now acts as an acoustic absorber. Cover with fabric and slap on some nice wood trim, and I got myself a nice, albeit 'dead', space. The same can be said for the ceilings as well; I suppose it depends on the ceiling height of the existing structure. I.e., securing a few layers of drywall to the TOP of the ceiling seems impossible if the building is only 11', and my rooms are 10'. But I digress... In any event, lets say I wanted to utilize this technique for all the rooms of my studio. How much air space is needed in between each leaf for the walls to approach the same TL as a traditional double-stud wall? 6"? 12"? Next question (for now), let's assume I constructed the Live Room in the "inside out" fashion, but found acoustically it's just too dead. If I added sheets of say, plywood (fabric covered or painted/finished)) would that act as another leaf? If I go this route I thought about just doing slats over the stud/insulation cavities, but with my limited budget, I may not have that option. TIA! -Dan
gullfo10 May 2011#2
welcome! adding the slats will provide by HF support as well as broadband (assuming the right slat/slot combinations). yes, by its nature there must be some third leaf effect but there is also significant absorption and diffusion. plus adding plastic under the cloth will help. ultimately your design and build will ultimately be site specific but as a general rule - consider the cost per unit area for a high end home in the location you intend to build, add 25% or so. deduct 30% if you're doing all the work. so if you have 250ft2 space and it's $100/ft2 (after deducting DIY %) and you are looking at $25K for construction. then factor in equipments etc etc. what can you do with 250ft2? (or maybe half that if the cost is $200/ft2) then determine how that matches your business plan: live recordings of 20 piece jazz bands etc versus an acoustic guitar or single vox, etc to ensure there is strong alignment with your budget planning and your business goals.
