Flush mounted speakers are the way to go. Broadband trapping should be more than just the back corners. You have 12 corners in a rectangular room.. use all of them if you can. -- Corners and angles.. hmmm I am not a fan of angled walls unless they are the thin facade or covering in a room to control reflected energy. If you are trying to eliminate modal issues by slanting the heavy shell walls, you will most likely make things worse and at the very least incalculable and possibly incurable. The only successful slant or angling of a wall to control modal issues or to spread the modes into the oblique is the Geddes approach. Again, I wouldn't use this approach in a control room. But it is wonderful in a Tracking room. I am currently designing a studio with such a tracking room. So to make a long story short; Rectangles rule! If you are not building a rectangular room - don't bother with modal calculations or room ratios. With slanted walls, those calculations and ratios go out the window... and pfft! You're lost!

jhbrandt10 July 2010#4
2.5m is pretty low. It looks like you are building double walls everywhere. If so, you will need to have ceilings on those double walls. You could go with resilient clips and channel to hang a couple of layers of gypsum board on the ceiling to improve TL. I don't know if you mixed up the room shape and treatment. -- There seems to be a consensus here that slanted walls are cool. Yes, they really do look cool. And a wall slant can function to create a reflection free zone, eliminate flutter echo, to direct 'ray region' sound to diffusors and/or absorbers. There are many great uses for a slanted wall. There are also many assumed and incorrect uses for a slanted wall. One of them is that the slanted wall is used to 'break up' modal behavior. This is not true. The only wall slant that I know to actually work, is the double slant of a massive wall (Geddes). Splaying walls will not remedy modal issues and sometimes can make things worse. At the very least it makes modal calculations impossible. That's why I didn't offer any opinions on the best mode response of your rooms. I hope that this helps. Cheers, John

jonaskellander wrote:
Hi. I discussed the construction with an arcitect. If I was to add new rafters to the existing ones to reach 2.9m inside hight it´s a god idea to have atleast one wall conecting the outer walls for stability. Take care! Jonas
Trusses, you are saying rafters but you surely must mean trusses. To add more rafters, which suggests you have existing rafters and you are going to add more to these, adds more weight to an already weighted structure. It also suggests that ceiling joists will not be installed, and this will not happen with a stick framed roof without support at the ridge line. The potential for the exterior walls to spread apart is significant with not only the existing weight of rafters but the added weight. For a structure that you say will be 40 feet long (12200mm) this would require posts at both ends and 2 and maybe 3 posts placed at intervals from the ridge to the slab. Your building seems to be about 720 square feet, with a 25K budget, that comes in @ $34.75(USD) a square foot. That is a really low number. Brien
