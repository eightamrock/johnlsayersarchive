knightfly27 October 2004#2
Information that's missing - ceiling height, whether one or both speakers driven, exact distances from center of woofers to front, back, sides, as well as vertical placement relative to floor and ceiling; same with mic position during the tests. Also, any mic that isn't omnidirectional will get different results depending on direction it's aimed; all mics will exhibit boundary effects relative to all boundaries in the room. Directional mics have a larger response variation with on-axis vs. off-axis placement (whether they are pointed at the source or not) In addition to this, the specific speaker used will change the results; basically, any and all dimensions from anything to anything can/will skew the results of the test. A basic start on tracking down where your problem(s) come from would be to download this spreadsheet from Harmon http://www.harman.com/wp/index.jsp?articleId=131 Enter your room dimensions into the sheet, it will graph the peaks and nulls for each axial mode of your room in all three dimensions for the first 4 harmonics - for a basic starting point which doesn't take tangential or oblique modes into account, you can make sure neither the speakers or your head/mic are at either peak or (especially) null positions in any of the three axes; in smaller rooms (most of us) as little as 2-3 inches can change results, especially for nulls. One method I use that works fairly well; I copy the graph area of this sheet to the clipboard, dump it into Paint (open 3 or 4 instances of paint) - then select each axis separately, copy into another instance of paint, re-size to match your room plan, rotate as necessary, and paste beside that wall; draw RED lines straight across the plan starting at all peak and null points on the graph, and GREEN lines in between; do the same with an elevation view of the room for vertical placement; then, make sure everything (woofers, head, mic, instrument) are in a "green" area. I'm out of here for now, but will try to remember to post some examples when I can; if I don't get back, "bump" this to remind me... Steve
