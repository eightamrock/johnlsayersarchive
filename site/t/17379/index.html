"I read that concrete blocks act as a 2 leaf system so given its double brick with an air gap I may have a 5 leaf system to start with. I don't fully understand that so wanted to confirm that was true." No sir it does not. Looking down on a block you can see that it is connected at all corners...there is no break what so ever in this path, so it is only , in a wall assembly, one leaf. Now , why you have a double wythe hollow block wall is beyond me, but you can be assured that the "air space" between the two layers is not an air space since the mortar it took to assembly these walls has coupled the two together...so again, not a four five of even a three, but a coupled assembly.

xSpace14 February 2012#6
"Based on the theory of adding more mass would it be sensible to fix two layers of fyreheck directly on the internal concrete block wall via screws & plugs in the wall? or would that only be of benefit using green glue?" Mass law, for every doubling of mass you get 4-5 dB of isolation. Now, if you have a concrete block and add sheetrock of any nature, the mass doesn't add up, so it is not a good idea to add the mass directly to the sheetrock. I think Roguejackal was suggesting to paint or mortar the interior side of the block, and this is a good idea, since the blocks are porous and tend to loose sound in the high frequencies.
Soundman202014 February 2012#7
Just to confirm what Brien said, with more detail that might help you understand the issue: drywall weighs around 800 kg/m3. Concrete weighs around 3,600 kg/m3. So however much concrete is in those blocks, you'd need to add drywall about five times thicker to double the mass, and that would increase your isolation by a grand total of 6 dB max, and more like 4 to 5 in reality. So let's say that the total concrete in those bricks works out to be the equivalent of 2 inches thick, if you added SIXTEEN layers of 5/8 drywall to the surface of those, bricks, then you'd roughly double the mass, and gain about 4 dB of extra isolation... As you can see, mass law is not your friend. It's not very encouraging. On the other hand, if you put just two sheets of that same drywall on a stud frame an inch or so away from those bricks, you could increase the isolation by 10 or 20 dB, or maybe even more... MSM RULES! :!: :D - Stuart -
