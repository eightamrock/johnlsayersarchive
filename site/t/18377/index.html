I try to figured out which settings is relevent, dBA, C? Slow, fast??
"C" weighting, and "Slow" response. "A" weighting is more used for measuring low level sounds, and usually what legal regulations mention, which is good because it is less sensitive to low frequencies! "C" is closer to the way the human ear perceives loud sounds. "Fast" shows more peaky measurements, "Slow" averages over a bit longer time periods.
Is the cement roughcast on the outside is good with it?
I would still paint the outside with a good quality masonry sealant, just to be sure, and also the inside. A porous wall means that air can move in and out, which changes the acoustic properties, and can potentially reduce isolation.
is it ok to have a widder than longer CR?
Yes... provided that the room is deep enough behind you. So the rear wall should be at least 3.5m behind your head, and that means the room needs to be about 5m (or more) from front wall to back wall. I think you have enough space to do that? That's why the normal recommendation for very small rooms is to have the speakers firing down the long axis of the room, since most home studios are not 5m wide. Many aren't even 5m long! But you have the space, so you can do your room either way.
I don't follow here, what is my "outer" leaf, the roof? You mean I need to cover all the roof with on leaf and do another ceiling in top of the inner wall?
The roof IS your outer leaf: How is it made? It might be possible to "beef it up" with more mass and a better seal. If not then this might be one of those cases where you have to have a three-leaf ceiling. Fortunately, you have plenty of height to do that properly.

As you can see from the equations for MSM resonance and mass law, the ONLY thing that really stops sound is mass. Lots of mass. The sound waves do not care how much you pay for the mass! They cannot read the price tag, and they are not "snobbish" anyway, so the really don't care. :) They just react to the mass, plain and simple. There's nothing in the equations that says one type of mass is better than another. So obviously, the most sensible thing to do is to use the cheapest mass that will accomplish the task. In most places in the world, that turns out to be plain old fire-rated drywall. OK, to be fair, there are some other factors that affect different parts of the spectrum, such as stiffness and damping, but the biggest factor is mass. MSM is mostly about low frequency isolation, but low frequencies is the most important part. So go with the cheapest mass that will work. Check your local pricing on the following: 16mm drywall, 16mm plywood, 16mm OSB, 16 mm MDF, 8mm fibercement board. Whichever is cheaper for the same size panel, is the best for you.

OK, to be fair, there are some other factors that affect different parts of the spectrum, such as stiffness and damping, but the biggest factor is mass. MSM is mostly about low frequency isolation, but low frequencies is the most important part. So go with the cheapest mass that will work. Check your local pricing on the following: 16mm drywall, 16mm plywood, 16mm OSB, 16 mm MDF, 8mm fibercement board. Whichever is cheaper for the same size panel, is the best for you.
