doublehelix17 May 2007#2
As gets mentioned frequently around these here parts: "It's all about mass." It sounds like you probably need to get a structural engineer in to make sure that the roof will support the extra weight, but I am guessing that you need to add a few layers of 5/8" sheetrock in between the ceiling joists, sealing each layer as you go. Then below that, add a thick layer of insulation, and then below that, add a couple of more layers of 5/8" sheetrock. This is of course assuming that the sound is *only* leaking out through the roof, and not the side walls, or via flanking sound through the building structure itself. Once again, you have to make sure that the structure can support the added weight, so check before you start, and DO NOT GUESS on this part! It is important!

poorman wrote:
ultimately, i just want to knock my decibel level down by approximately 20-30 db.
MP, you said you've read the book (and thanks for the purchase) you know that for each doubling of mass you reduce sound transmissions by roughly 6dB.......... and that is a total doubling of mass - on both faces - not simply on one side. So when you begin talking about wanting a reduction of 20 to 30 dB you should realize that there isn't any easy way out of this. You want to reduce your transmissions by somewhere around 300 to 500 percent.......... That's a lot of mass......... and right now - although they hear it coming through the windows - you might find that you have as much of it coming through flanking as you do airborn......... My best reccomendation to you would be to first invest a couple of hundred duckies to hire an acoustician to determine what your problems and real needs are - and then if that turns into adding mass to the existing roof system - verify with a structural engineer that it's capable of the load.......... Sincerely, Rod
