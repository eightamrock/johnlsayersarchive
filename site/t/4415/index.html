sharward24 September 2005#13
OK, I think I know what's going on with those 2x4s. The 4x12s are 8 feet on center, right? Well, then, it looks like those plywood sheets are attached to the tops of the 4x12s, and the job of the 2x4s is to keep them from sagging too much. :roll: That being the probable case, definitely don't add any weight to them. (I realize you're not planning to. It just beared repeating -- again! ;-)) What you might be able to do is use a joist hanger to hang new 2x6 joists perpendicular to and attached to those 4x12s, flush with the bottom... Something like this would probably work.
    Simpson Strong-Tie LU/U/HU Standard Joist Hangers
Disclaimer: This is just an idea -- it's of course up to you to do all the homework necessary to confirm that those 4x12s can safely support the additional weight! You would then be able to fill these 8-foot wide areas with insulation and attach 2 or maybe 3 layers of gypsum, perhaps on hat channel supported by RSIC clips. You'd still have a potentially weak upper leaf though. Your "beef-up" options could get tricky. What exactly are all of the construction materials on the roof? Since it's a flat roof, you may be able to beef up the roof from the outside. However, that's going to get expensive, and heavy, and again, you run the risk of overloading those 4x12s. There's also Deluks' idea of "single layer of sheetrock attached to the roof between the 2x4's (heavily caulked)," which might work too. In either case, you may have to set some posts to split the long 4x12 span into two or perhaps even three smaller spans. You need to weigh (no pun intended) the pros and cons of building two new lower leaves that don't rely on the roof at all and living with (and compensating for) a third leaf effect, or trying to stay true to "two leaf" and working under the physical constraints you have.

knightfly25 September 2005#16
From the pix, it's at least an 18 foot span those 4x12's are across; probably more. I don't have time to calculate the beams ATM, but the 2x4's on 24" centers at that length will support approx. 8 PSF dead load; if this is a flat roof, it's likely the plywood is at least 3/4", more likely 1-1/8. which weighs about 3.3 PSF by itself; so your roof, not including the 4x12's, would weigh about 4 PSF; I'm guessing (you can help here) that over the plywood is some kind of flatroof product, depending on age it could be tarred roll roofing, or?? Another couple of pounds per square foot, bringing you up to 6 PSF. Adding even ONE layer of gypsum between the 2x4's would raise this another 2.35 PSF, putting you at 8.35 PSF roughly; this would be the ABSOLUTE MAXIMUM this roof could support, and that's ONLY if those 4x12's aren't already over spanned. We need EVERY measurement you can give on each part of your building before going any further with this - As it stands, I wouldn't recommend adding ANYTHING to the ceiling except a thorough caulking with acoustic caulk; then I'd build inner walls (what's the floor, in INTIMATE detail?) and support an inner ceiling on those walls, strong enough to support full insulation fill and 3 layers of gypsum. Details, every one you can think of - length of each and every piece, sizes, spacing, what's under them, etc - Next (or first) do you have a Sound Level Meter? You'll need one, and not just to figure out how loud/quiet you need things... Steve
