len-morgan2 October 2006#4
To answer a question from your earlier post, I think you would end up with a 3 leaf wall even if you put 703 in between the studs. You've got two options: 1) leave up the wood studs but fill in the space between the brick wall and the top of the studs with drywall to "beef up" the wall (see Sharward's thread for more about this). 2) Remove the wood and put a layer of cement or motar over the brick wall. This increases isolation of a simple brick wall. If you like the look of the wall, you might want to fix up the window issue first and see if that ends up being enough isolation. It could be that the window will STILL be the weakest link for isolation and doing anything to the walls isn't going to buy you anything (unless you remove the window completely. I don't think you mentioned whether the window is double panes in separate frames or not. That could make a big difference in what is possible for you. len

len-morgan3 October 2006#6
Hello again, Upsidedown. 1) The brick wall - The reason I didn't suggest cementing it over first was because in your first post, you mentioned that you like the rustic look of the wall. If you can live with the cement surface, I think Bob Gold's website mentions a several dB gain in TL by cementing. You can a a couple more (2 or 3 I think) by painting the cement after it dries. 2) The window - A) Are the double panes in two different frames (one on each side of the wall)? B) Before you start calking the gaps around the window, you want to make sure they aren't too big. Calking will only go so far (like 3/8" or so). If the gaps are too big, you might consider filling in the gaps with cement first. What you'd probably end up having to do is remove the glass and the frame, then make a form out of 1/2" plywood or so on either side of the brick (actually, either side of BOTH walls), then fill that will cement. It would give you a nice tight fit to the window frames and the seal would be much better. In fact, if you can manage it and can get some 1/2" or so backer rod, you might consider laying the backer rod (maybe three rows) in the drying concrete (carefully). This would give you an additional seal(s) against the window frame when you put it back. Couple that with calking around the frame when your done and you'll probably have the best you're going to get out of a window. How thick (and what type) is the current glass? len
