xSpace15 October 2010#15
"Vapor barrier will be placed along with the three (3) layers of drywall and the green glue." A vapor retarder or more correctly called, a vapor diffusion retarder, has a specific purpose and placement. Placement is to the outside walls of whatever structure/building it may be installed into and depending on yearly temperatures can be placed in one of two different sides of the wall. In your case you have to ask yourself "does this apartment I am in already have a VDR, because if it does I am about to screw up by making a double "vapor barrier" which is never a good thing". And secondly, most of the walls in this room are part of the interior climate so the inclusion of a VDR is not required, and should not be used at all.

xSpace15 October 2010#18
No matter what the drywall manufacturer says, a dual vapor retarder will have the ability to trap moisture in between the two barriers. This is never good as this moisture will be right about where the insulation is on the one wall and directly on the face of the other existing wall creating mold, as enough time is allowed to pass unchecked. And then there is the concern that it is mostly an interior room, correct? It isn't specifically not done, but it is by and large, just not done on the interior of a building since the reasons why a barrier is placed is to mitigate vapor migration as the temperatures permit. And since it is part of the interior it is part of a controlled environment. Hope that makes sense to ya,
