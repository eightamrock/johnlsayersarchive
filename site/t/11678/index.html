Francesca21 wrote:
well the space i am considering is on the third floor of my building
First thing you want to do is get familiar with the term flanking, if it is not already part of your lexicon. Anything that gets off the ground and tries to contain sound will be held hostage by flanking, air borne and structure borne vibration. Think of sound as air filling a balloon and this may be more clear as an example. Then you'll want to look into wall / floor assemblies, weight per square foot and how it will interact with the building. Mass is a barebones requirement to contain sound [sound proof, isolation,etc.] The more you have the better your isolation is. The question is, can your floor and wall assembly on the third floor stay standing with this additional load. And if it can, does the building have enough rigidity to stop this solid mass from making the building itself become the noise maker. A typical question asked here is "how much in dB do you need in isolation"? I haven't seen a typical answer :) But they are all different with different expectations based on different modes of thinking. A solid brick building and I am betting it has a solid wood framed floor assembly. This is your Achilles heel. You need mass and mass is heavy. You require mass because you are on the third floor. It could be the second floor, you would still need it. If you were lucky enough to be on the ground floor, the weight issue is reduced. Flanking is always an issue, but you will have far better results on the ground then in the air. But you may not be referring to isolation. Maybe you just want a "good" sounding room. Then this is a different process all together. Too little information to be accurate, but these are the things you will face in this space.
