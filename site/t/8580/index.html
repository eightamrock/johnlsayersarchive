I'm not quite clear on how the water that's running through the stone under your basement slab is getting into the sewer line. That's a sealed pipe and there should be no way for the water to enter it to run away - unless there's a drain tile system that is connected to the sewer. Regardless, there are a number of ways you could control the water - which is best depends on your exact situation. Obviously, one approach would be to excavate around the perimeter of the house, install drainage tile and run that either to grade (if you have sufficient slope) or to a sump that is emptied by a sump pump. This is a pretty intrusive and expensive approach, mind you. There are also systems where the contractor will break out a narrow section of slab adjacent to the exterior walls from the inside of the basement. This area is hand excavated and drainage tile installed. Once again, it has to run somewhere - either to a sewer line (if your local code / bylaws allow) or to a sump where it is pumped away. I've found this approach to cost about half of an exterior excavation / waterproofing. It's difficult to come up with an actual solution without knowing for sure what's happening under your slab. Sounds like a very high ground water table around your house.
