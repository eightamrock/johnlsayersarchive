knightfly17 October 2004#6
OK, here goes - Window ideas - what you have won't stop a lot of sound compared to my suggestions here - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=272 Also, I need to know more about your "3 foot fire wall", as to how exactly it's constructed, from the outside surface all the way through to what you see inside the room. FYI, any time you add any kind of "trap" to a wall, whatever frequencies the trap "kills" will also be the frequencies that get through the wall easier - so, before you add a slot-wall (usually called a slat or slot absorber) to your wall, you want to make sure the wall is BETTER than you need by several dB, or else the sound you "trap" INSIDE will be the sound you HEAR OUTSIDE. That said, at least part of your slot wall should be designed around trapping 300 hZ - this is the modal first harmonic of 22-24" kick drum shells, and nearly always needs to be reduced in a room to get a more even drum sound. Using slats to do this rather than broadband absorption helps keep the drum room from getting too absorptive at high frequencies, so you don't lose that "sizzle" from the cymbals while you're controlling the low end modes. Can you describe the other walls in the room, starting with the visible surface on one side and continuing through the wall to the visible surface on the other side? Once I've got an accurate idea of what is actually there, I can try to help with ideas on containment/acoustics... Steve
