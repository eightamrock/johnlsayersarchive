Ted White1 May 2012#8
Soundman2020 has the exact correct dialog with respect to the use and function of the Green Glue. It is effective to damp vibration when placed between sheets of drywall, plywood, cement board, or some combo thereof. A thin film is desired. Thick films don't damp particularly well. Too little damping compound and you can't generate enough resistance from the shear forces. Similarly, too thick a layer will also offer insufficient resitance to shear and no damping occurs. It appears i the images that the product was used in the wrong way. This is the danger of selling technically difficult projects / products from Menards. A sealant seals, and will not damp, regardless of an offhand claim on a label.

Ted White1 May 2012#12
Ah. Thanks for the clarification, Stuart. Very kind of you. This is a common ceiling noise remediation. The general recommendation is for two layers of 5/8" drywall and damping compound to be secured to the underside of the subfloor with drywall screws. The screws provide the compressive force to squeeze the damping compound to a thin film. If we want to damp the subfloor itself (we do) then it's important that the damping compound be in direct contact wih the subfloor. Having said all that, I would not recommend doing this unless footfall noise (impact) were an issue into the room below. For isolating sound within the room below, I would focus on decoupling the ceiling (damped) mass from the joist with isolation clips.
