Soundman202015 September 2011#32
HVAC isn't my strong point, but I'd suggest that you put your silencers on the noisier side of the run, and don't try to bridge directly across a wall cavity. Rather, run your ducts in the ceiling space or other are where you have lots of clearance between leaves. Worst case: make the silencers slim enough to fit between studs in the cavity and mount them to the studs on some type of isolation mount. That would make them tall and skinny, but that isn't a problem as long as the air flow cross section is large enough along the entire length of the silencer, and you have a few baffles in there with duct liner all over the interior. That way you could have the intake through the drywall into the top of the silencer, for example, and the outlet at the bottom of your silencer, into the ducting. - Stuart -

gullfo12 October 2011#41
the treatments - like slat resonators - can act as 3rd leafs but they have a variety of absorption and diffusion properties which also negates a lot of the effect relative to an actual sealed cavity in a wall. you can use smaller slats set into a wall which is mostly absorptive so you have some life in the walls as well as avoiding a complete 3rd leaf. on the soffits vs not, conceptually you could cover up part of the window in the CR to add the soffits. in any event, attempt to keep the speakers on a common angle if possible (30, 45) etc but symmetry is critical so focus there. on hvac - once you have the air exchange isolated then consider the use of a plenum to expand the supply side to reduce air flow and a low noise diffuser. similarly as plenum on the return can also smoothly compress the air flow back into the ducts.

That's pretty much it, yup! I'd also suggest that you might want to re-angle those a bit, and juggle the geometry until the edge of the soffit ends at the door line. Otherwise you won't be able to attached the treatment modules to the door. They need space to move into when the door is opened
What problems will I have to consider when attaching treatments to the doors?
Firstly, to make sure that it won't bang into things as you open / close the door! Plus: that it is firmly attached, that the hinges can handle the weight, that it doesn't affect the seals over time as the door sags, etc. Mostly mechanical issues, rather than acoustic ones. - Stuart -
