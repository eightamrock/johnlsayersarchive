Pasz, - I don't mix at very loud levels, but it would be nice if my neighbor next to me (behind the west wall) didn't hear too much coming from my mix room. Do those traps soundproof the room too? Should I soundproof the back wall (west) before adding traps? No, acoustic treatments can actually LESSEN the isolation you get between rooms; the absorption caused by panel type traps causes that portion of the wall to act as if it has an extra leaf of mass, which will worsen LF isolation and slightly improve mid-range isolation. If you're concerned about that wall, you should add more mass to it before your acoustic treatment. - Because I have the top appartement I don't have neighbors living upstairs. The ceiling is made gypsum attached to a wooden frame. The attic is very low (maybe 1.5 meter at the center of the appartement, and maybe 50 cm to the sides of the appartement (so it's a pointy roof). Now i was thinking: Do the lows pass through my ceiling, into the attic? So are some lows already maybe trapped there? Yes, the lows will pass through into the attic; if you have a neighbor, and your walls stop at the ceiling level, you will probably have noise transfered across the attic and back into your neighbor's space. Depending on the construction/framing of your space, you may not be able to add any more weight to the existing construction; if so, you're stuck with what you have for isolation. - I really have NO idea what kinds of traps I should place WHERE. Don't know if I should use slat resonators or just 703/704/70whatever covered with acoustically transparent fabric or maybe something different. In that small a room, when used for mixing, it would partly depend on if your room modes are well distributed - I ran yours and they are pretty well balanced especially for that small a room. Because of this, I would mainly use broadband absorption (no slats) although for the sides (as John drew for you) slats would keep the room somewhat brighter sounding - he's kept enough angle to those sides to maintain Reflection Free Zone at the mix position, then any other absorption would be to catch first reflection points and to bring down the room's decay time uniformly so it is short enough not to confuse your ears into thinking you already have enough reverb in the mix. - I read that A mix room shouln't have diffusion because it's a working environment. And that the overal reverb should be very short. How to do that? Will there be room for mid-high aborbers when all the bass traps are installed (I probably will need to install a lot of those?). Actually, a SMALL mix room shouldn't try for diffusion; you generally want a diffusor spaced at least 3 times the wavelength of the lowest frequency of the diffusor away from your ears; for most diffusors, this translates to about 10-12 feet. Kind of hard to do with a room that's not 12 feet TOTAL. Reverb time should be short in a mix room; see my previous paragraph's comments. You accomplish this by using absorption, which needs to be as balanced at all frequencies as you can get it. Different materials have different amounts of absorption at different frequencies, so finding a combination of materials for the different parts of your room entails calculating all the surfaces at all their frequencies and making a chart of the total absorption at each frequency, then seeing which frequencies have more or less absorption total, and changing amounts of each material until you can get absorption amounts balanced. This might help with the above - http://www.saecollege.de/reference_mate ... m#verbcalc Your room won't take as much bass trapping as some, because its modes are well-distributed. You will still need to watch placement of the mix position for peaks and nulls, including vertical placement (NOT centered between floor and ceiling by at least 6" (150mm) - However, you DO need to be centered left-to-right or your stereo imaging won't work - even though this puts you in a couple of nulls, it can't be helped if you are to have a good stereo image at the mix position. HTH... Steve

knightfly25 February 2005#11
1. Should I soundproof the wall at the front? Depends on whether someone will be sleeping in your bedroom while you're making noise; Also, if noise will be a problem ANYWHERE in the house you need to reconsider; sound travels through nearly any building material, so keeping everything quiet can be difficult. 2. Should I put a trap between the speakers (on the wall) aswell? Shouldn't matter, unless you plan to have surround speakers behind you. 3. I keep reading about LEDE (Live end dead end) CR's. So I'd have wood panels in the front and have just Knauf fibreglass panels covered with fabric in the back of the room (right?). A LEDE room! Can mine be one and should it be one? LEDE refers to an older, not much used method of room building - the real LEDE rooms were live at the REAR, dead at the FRONT so that all the engineer got at the mix position was direct sound, until the sound had traveled enough to become diffuse and was late enough NOT to cause imaging problems. What sometimes LOOKS like LEDE is really "DELE" when viewed from the same perspective - an RFZ room is backward from an LEDE room, and its walls and ceiling are splayed to avoid early reflections to the mix position. The dead rear half keeps reflections from returning as strong as they would if the rear were "live"... Steve
