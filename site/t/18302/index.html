Hi "crunch", and welcome to the forum! :) Before you do anything, it would help to find out objectively what is wrong with the room. Download and run the REW acoustic analysis software (it's free!), and post the results here. That will show exactly how the room is reacting. To me it looks like the issue is a major lack of bass trapping. For a room that size you will need lots of bass trapping, and you don't seem to have much.
The dimensions of the room are: width - 10' height - 9' length - 14' 5"
It's not terrible, but not fantastic either. Theory says you need 356 sabins of absorption in there to get within ITU / EBU specs. That's the same as 356 square feet of open window, or 356 square feet of perfect acoustic absorber. It does not look like you have anywhere near that.
I used to have the console in front of the studio glass, I wouldn't mind going back there again, it would make sense,
It wouldn't. :) In a small room, the speakers should always fire down the longest axis of the room, not across the short one, in order to ensure that the reflections off the rear wall are delayed as long as possible, and attenuated as much as possible, before they get back to your ears. The ideal is more than 20ms delay, and more than 20 dB of attenuation. That isn't possible in most small rooms, but you should still shoot to get as close as you can to that. And setting up the room sideways will take do the exact opposite...
Also, you'll note, we have some traps hanging, and while they seem to tame flutter echo (that was never really bad in there anyway), I'm not entirely sold that it's doing anything for the bottom end.
Those do work, but they have to be set up in the correct place, and you have to have enough of them.
Anyway, I look forward to your suggestions,
Do the REW test, post the MDAT file here, and also add some photos of the room so we can get a better idea of how it is set up at present. Based on that, we can probably figure out something. - Stuart -
