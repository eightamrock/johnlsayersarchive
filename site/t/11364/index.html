Well, it takes a while, doesn't it? I started sometime in August with demo and have just made that wonderful turn around from demolition to construction. This studio is probably similar to a lot of others. I have basement space available in a 3000 sq ft house and plan to not only use if for my own creations, but offer it commercially as a studio/B&B with a wonderful environment - parks, fishing, 'family' feel - really inspiring stuff for my target customer base. One of my efforts during construction will be to keep costs reasonable through recycling of materials and using community support salvage depots like Habitat for Humanity's ReStore for materials. These are wonderful places where you can save a buck and help those in need. I have a couple of tips for those new to the game like me: - you will need a lot of space for demolition stuff going out and for materials coming in. Plan carefully your construction phases - be careful not to put stuff where you're about to be working. I try to handle materials as few times as possible. - keep your work area clean. You've heard this a million times, but if you twist an ankle, you'll delay your studio build by weeks. Also keeping similar items grouped together (eg hvac stuff) will often prevent you from re-buying items you already have. - shop around and barter. In this economy there are a ton of deals to be had by _just asking_ ! - be prepared to go slow. This is a labour of love and detail. If you rush or cut corners, you will become frustrated. Enjoy the journey. Cheers, Shaun

shamca3 November 2008#15
Meet my helper. When I started putting the drywall strips from the walls into the spaces between the joists, I needed a T-bar to help support the far end. The drywall from the walls filled about 2/3 of the joists, so I had to buy about 10 additional sheets to finish things off ($8.99/sheet - 1/2" - 8' long). I ended up cutting these new sheets width-wise in the garage with my sawzall 4 sheets at a time. I'll never do that again as the precision wasn't very good the last few sheets I did with my drywall T-square and the fit was much better. Overall, 3 sheets were added; backer rod filled some gaps, sealer was added only to the last layer and then 1x2 strapping was added for add'l support. Instead of suppporting with #4d nails as suggested in Rod's book, I used 3 sizes of drywall screws to attach the sheets to the subfloor above.
