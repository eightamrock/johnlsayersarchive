rod gervais26 December 2006#2
Kevin, You're talking about needing some serious isolation for the train - and my best guess would be that flanking is going to be an even bigger problem than air-borne noise is once inside the space. The best thing you can do is to hire an acoustician to take some test readings for you - he'll test for airborne and can also use an accelerometer to test the existing slab and structural components. Understand - it isn't as simple as you just adding a floating floor inside of an isolated room to get the benifit of the floor. The floor becomes an integral part of the entire room - and must be a part of the design if it is going to happen in the end........... Sincerely, Rod

sharward28 December 2006#4
Staggered studs are useless in that context. Before attempting to utilize "soundproofing" construction techniques, it's necessary to understand their purpose so that you can apply them appropriately. Staggered stud construction is a technique used to partially decouple two sides of a single frame wall assembly. It's an inexpensive method that eats onlt 2 inches of floor space and is OK for, perhaps, residential construction where one might want to decrease the noise levels between, for example, a bedroom and a family room. It's a compromise. In your case, your outer leaf appears to be a concrete block wall. By building a frame separate from that wall, you've already decoupled it from the outer leaf (except the floor they share). Staggered studs on the inner frame does you no good. Buy Rod's book... It's in the Sticky threads. --Keith :mrgreen:
