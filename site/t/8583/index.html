rod gervais10 April 2007#2
Matt, this isn't as bad as it looks at first. For one thing - the block walls do not act as 2 leafs - but rather as one.... so you still have a 2 leaf system What I would do here is this - first i would take advantage of the fact that those block walls are open at the top - which means you can fill them with sand without a problem - and sand is a great mass and a excellent damper.... will help cut LF transmissions real well. BTW - I would remove the wires I see running down int the block walls and slip some conduit down there to protect those wires... Then I would fill the very top course of the wall with concrete - and set a 3/8" anchor bolt about every 4 feet - let them stick up 2". Then I would add a 2x6 plate on top of the wall - and frame in a wall up to the underside of the ceiling above...... Let the new wall sit flush to the inside face of the block. Now - go inside of the wall (over the top of each room) and install 2 layers of 5/8" drywall - caulkk and tape nicely - let the bottom lap over the block a couple of inches and set into a bed of caulk. Once you're done all of those - do the same with the outside face..... make sure to fill the cavity with insulation. This way - the sound traveling up and over will be stopped by the new wal - and will then have to climb over the ceiling above and back down- which wil increase your isolation quite a lot. Then I would test again to see the gain - If these rooms sound great the way that they are - it would only be as a last resort that I would consider adding any more mass to the inside of the rooms - because the more mass you add - the more it will change the room - especially at low frequencies........... you'll wind up adding a lot of room treatments if you take that route....... Sincerely, Rod

Ok... Im the original poster, under my original username... seems I didn't report back soon at all! I took some of Rod's advise and we ended up ripping out the whole ceiling and raising the height so it now graduates from 2.25m to 2.53 which has given me some extra height. We ended up NOT filling in the walls with sand as it was too costly and the landlords were skeptical, but we DID end up putting in a new celling taking it right to the edge of the frame where it meets the cinder block, putting in a double 20mm dry wall and caulked it in. It did WONDERS! I now have drummers banging away at all hours of the day/night and had only once compliant in a year of business from my neighbor below me who could hear the low muffle of a double kick when I recorded a heavy metal band... but other than that, nothing! And leakage within the space has been drastically minimized. I retuned and tweaked the control room and live room and am pretty happy. Getting a bit of build up around 300Hz in the live room and have posted a thread here about that: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=10597 Thanks again to Rod instilling confidence and the advice!
