Sound absorption panels are no use for what you want, which is isolation. Absorbers are for treating a room, acoustically. Isolation and treatment are two different things, and what you need is isolation (then you'll need treatment afterwards). Isolation (also often incorrectly called "soundproofing") is accomplished with mass: Heavy, solid, rigid, thick barriers, such as brick, concrete, thick drywall, thick wood, etc. Light-weight things, such as absorption panels do nothing at all, or very little, to stop sound. But mass alone is also not enough. In reality, you need mass plus "decoupling". to do the job. Decoupling just means that you physically separate the mass on one side of the barrier form the mass on the other side, usually with just air. But mass and decoupling alone also are not the entire solution: you also need air-tight seals around the room, so that there is not even the slightest gap where air can get in or out. The combination of those three is how you stop sound getting in or out of a room. In your case, it's hard to suggest a solution without seeing what you have, so posting photos and a detailed diagram (in SketchUp) of what you have would help. But most likely what you'll have to do is to take off the existing ceiling, put resilient channel across the joists up there, then hang new drywall from that: probably something like two layers of 5/8" drywall, perhaps with Green Glue in between. That might not be all that you need to do though, but that would be the best starting point. - Stuart -

If you have either hat channel on clips, or resilient channel without clips, then you do not also need furring. The channel by itself is sufficient.
my issue now has become this....the contractors don't want to do it.
Maybe it's a good time to find another contractor? :) But I'm not sure I understand what you are saying: if there is ALREADY a ceiling up there (popcorn or otherwise), then it MUST come off before you can put up your resilient channel (or clips plus hat channel). You CANNOT simply put the clips (or RC) directly over an existing ceiling: doing so would create a 3-leaf system, which very likely make your isolation WORSE for low frequencies. It is always a bad idea to have a leaf over a very thin air gap, since the resonant frequency is very high, and thus the final result will be poor low frequency isolation. So regardless of what else you do, that existing ceiling has to come out, completely, leaving only the joists.
who cares about the celing being popcorn. its ugly, sheetrock it over and call it a day....
:shock: The sound will care, very much. Thin air gaps with leaves on each side will not isolate well in low frequencies, as I mentioned above. This is not about what looks good or ugly, but rather what works acoustically. The existing ceiling has to come out first. And if it is a popcorn ceiling form the 60's or early 70's, then you have an additional problem: asbestos. Before you do anything to that ceiling, you better get it tested for asbestos.
