Hello, greetings to all readers! This is my first post on this forum. My name is Peter Tielemann and i come from Bocholt, a relatively small town in the western part of Germany. Me and my wife are looking for a place where to freely play our drums and guitar. Recording the stuff we do, should be an option in the future. For now, we would be satisfied with some kind of soundproof rehearsal studio. Fortunately we own a house with an unused rectangular room in the basement. It measures 9,01m x 4,62m. The downside of this relatively large room is the ceiling height: it’s only 2,25m. The room lies in the south-east corner of the building. So the south- and the eastwall are build of 37,5 cm thick concrete while the other two walls are made of 17,5 cm thick bricks. The 7cm concrete floating floor (it contains a floor heating) lies on a roughly 20 cm thick concrete foundation. The ceiling is also made of concrete. The south wall contains 2 windows sized 100cm x 50cm. The panes are not sound isolated at all, but the entrance door in the north wall actually is (i’ll post pictures of the room later)! The line that separates the floor is an expansion joint (i hope, that’s the right expression!?). In the western part of the room are two small areas where ducts enter and leave the room. I’ll photograph them too later. The 44cm x 43cm rectangular structure beside the entrance is a chimney. My first question is quite simple: Would the remaining ceiling height (after sound isolation and possible other treatment) still be sufficient to build a reasonable studio room? I’ve read quite some literature about the importance of adequate room ratios to achieve a good sound. But in all studio examples i discovered by now the ceiling height was always greater than mine. Cheers, Peter PS: Please excuse my bad english. School is long lost and forgotten... :wink:

gullfo15 June 2007#5
nice space. somethings to check would be - how much sound is getting in or out of the room? is it through the floors? windows? door? chimney? what is the sound level outside (play something on the drums while measuring)? this will help determine how much isolation you need. also, there's only one way in or out of that room. so you should probably check on codes regarding egress windows. I also don't see any ventilation.
