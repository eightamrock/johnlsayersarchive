britune29 August 2010#4
Do you want one big room? Two or three rooms? You can look at my basement build and do a search on this site for "basement" that will show many others. I wanted to record drums and amps and not bother my family above me or the neighbors. I built independant wall structures that did not touch my basement walls. If you read this site and books like Rod Gervais book, you will see that most folks wanting sound isolation use a Mass Air Mass design. So you have an interior wall, air, and an exterior wall. Each wall must be sealed. I used green glue isolation clips with hat channel for my low ceiling areas. I used two layers of 5/8" drywall with green glue in between for my walls. I boxed in and sealed my electrical outlets. I built HVAC silencers to my heating sends and returns. I used laminated glass for my windows. I used solid core exterior doors with weather seals on them. It sounds like you're in a hurry. But be careful and think every detail through before you build. A lot of folks have had to tear down their construction and build it twice because they didn't build it right the first time. For sound isolation, every component of the build must be done correctly, or your work and money will be wasted. It only takes one weak link in your build to let all the noise out. Many contractors think they know how to do this type of work and almost none of them do.

about 6.5' high to the bottom of the floor joists.
:shock: Ouch! Given that datum, I'd say your floor will absolutely have to stay as plain concrete, sine you do not have enough head room to put anything at all on it (except maybe a coat of paint, and even that much extra height is cutting it fine! :) ), and your ceiling will practically have to be done using Rod's technique of inserting the joists for the new inner-leaf inside the gaps between the floor joists above you. Even so, you are going to be left with less than 6 feet by the time you are done with treatment... I sure hope you are not very tall, and don't need to track drums in there!
- Is it important to fill any air gaps / plumbing penetrations with a particular product (expanding spray foam? something else?) before putting up the ceiling?
Simple answer: Yes it is very important to seal up everything. Use caulk, not spray foam. depending on your isolation needs, you light also need to beef up the floor above you.
- How to deal with the vents - cap them from outside and fiberglass on the inside above the ceiling?
Those vents are there for a purpose: before you do anything to seal them up, you should get advice from your contractor / inspector on what to do about them. Are you permitted to seal them, by your local building code?
- RC channel __is?__ __is not?__ a good idea in this instance (I'd like to be sure as I need to spare as much vertical height as possible with such low ceilings.
It is an option, and would work, but you didn't say how much isolation you need, so it may or may not be right for you. And like Britune said: Slow down, spend LOTS of time researching and designing it right, and only then think about building. - Stuart -
