doublehelix24 October 2007#10
Jeremy: You are right in assuming that you need to have some fresh air in your room. 1) you need to breathe, and 2) it gets really hot in there with a bunch of gear and sweaty bodies! I think xSpace is just trying to make sure that your requirements and goals match your plans. Anytime you put a hole in your wall, you are leaving a place for sound to come out. If then, you are looking for a sound proof room, you will need to consider that in your plans. At the bottom of your drawing you show a "vent with baffles", and at the top you show the "window sealed with a duct for a vent fan". Sound is going to leak (pour?) out of those holes like nobody's business. If that is OK, then cool, but then I would alter the rest of your plans and not worry much about sound proofing since it will be lost anyway. You might as well save the money you are planning to spend on sound proofing materials since it will be wasted with your current design. (That is assuming I understand your plans correctly.) The XLR wall jack is not bad thing if you need it. I think xSpace was confused since it was located on the opposite side of a noisy area, and he (as well as I) could not understand what you might be recording on that side of the wall. I see now that you are planning on putting a sort of machine room outside of the live room. That makes better sense. xSpace was trying to say that the least number of holes in your wall, the better. (I am not sure I understand having the mixer in the noisy environment, but this is your place, not mine.) Most studio builds that I have seen that are trying to design a semi-soundproof area, use some form of HVAC (heating/cooling) system and vent into the room with vents and cold air returns. You are still going to get sound leakage through these vents, but there are ways to control it and reduce it. If you use your "vent with baffles" design, you might as well just cut a huge hole in the wall to let sound out, and forget about any other soundproofing techniques. Your room is only going to be as quiet as its weakest link.

(http://www.pulsartech.net/product/P1650-XXS_F9) I'm sure there have been plenty of other people who have done similar things.
I think you are ok putting in an XLR panel, and as you mention, most people do that. I think xSpace was wondering where it was leading. There are several ways to do this, but the objective is to make as small of holes in your walls as you can, and then plug them as tightly as you can! Wherever air goes, sound goes. Some things to consider when you are running wire through walls: 1) Do not place the ends directly across from each other on both sides of the wall. 2) Try not to put more than one hole in the drywall between any two wall studs. Stagger the holes as best you can... one hole on the inside of the room between 2 studs, and the hole on the outside of the room between two different studs. You will need to run the wire or snake between the walls or drill holes in the studs to pass them through to another "chamber". 3) Conduit helps! And as many bends in the conduit that you can make, the better. By buying a ready-made snake, it is going to be difficult to route the wire through the walls, and certainly you will not be able to run it through any of the studs unless you cut off the ends on the fan out, and solder them back on later, which sort of defeats the purpose! These same things apply to your electrical wiring. A lot of folks use surface-mount wall boxes and switch boxes to avoid making big holes in the drywall. Others (myself included) opted for flush-mounted boxes, but tried to make sure that they were sealed from behind as best as I could. Best of luck! You will come up with a working plan, just keep at it! Keep reading, and keep asking questions. It is better to do that now rather than later. Take care.
