If I understand you correctly, what you are referring to is the insulation used in the absorbing panels, usually of the fiberglas/mineral wool variety. Out here, Owens Corning 703 and Roxul AFB are among the most often used. kendale2 February 2007#13
Aloha, Here are a few bits of information I’ve collected along the way… • Soffits angled 30 deg. in from a flat wall result in speaker baffles being perpendicular to the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head • The baffle extension (the face of the soffit) should be as large as you can make it, up to about 45" in each direction from the woofer in a system with a LF cutoff of around 45 Hz - There's a formula based on LF cutoff that ensures full 2 pi response for the woofer if the baffle is at least the size obtained in the formula's solution - • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position • Monitor angles 60 degrees • Monitor height – woofer at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls 6-12 degrees • Ceiling 12 degree slope Out of curiousity, were you planning to soffit mount your monitors? If so, you will want to use a hard, dense material for the bezels - as in 2 layers of mdf or 3/4" plywood, which ever is available. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

Lou2 February 2007#20
Hi Djo – hope you guys are cool. Ok, as I’ve already said I’m new here too and not really qualified to comment but….. I’ll do my best as the real talent for this game will asleep right now! 8) From your Maputo STUDIO B.jpg it appears that you’ve rotated the whole room 90 degrees to the right. I’ll stick my neck out with what I’ve learned and say ‘spot on – well done’. This will ensure that your sound is travelling down the longest dimension of the room rather than the shortest. (Easier to deal with re room treatments etc). :D You seem to have ended up with 90 degree geometry in your monitoring and ‘sweet spot’, again I’ll stick my neck out and say that whilst this is by no means incorrect, this configuration is usually used when there is much more width available. :) If you look at your jpg (which even with good eyesight is difficult to see!) try to bring the bass traps/soffits closer together, so that you can achieve the 60 degree geometry that Kendale suggested this afternoon. In other words, when you sit in the mix position and look straight at the wall in front, both speakers will be 30 degrees off your line of vision left and right, NOT 45 degrees. Or if it’s easier, the front of Kendale’s speakers (193) jpeg, are 30 degrees off the horizontal dotted line that joins them, and your head completes the perfect equilateral triangle. :D :D Next bit…….Soffit mounting. You mention your English, trust me its fine! English is my first language and I had trouble really understanding this one - honestly. :roll: What concerns me here is your question about speakers being inserted in the bass trap panel. Well yes and no. :shock: Ok don’t panic – now that you’ve seen a picture of a beautifully hand crafted bezel, the speaker you can see in that picture is NOT touching the bezel anywhere! It’s actually sat on a wooden frame, supported by the outer frame. The Dude’s here call it the outer leaf. This is so that the speaker has no means of physical contact with the wall that you see, (ie the ‘bezel’) or if you prefer, the ‘inner leaf’. In short Djo the bezel is a piece of dense sheet wood material with a perfect whole cut into it, through which your speaker can be seen and of course heard. If my English makes sense to you you’ll now know that this procedure requires much skill and attention to detail. This is possibly why so many people chose not to soffit mount, (everything must line up perfectly). I highly recommend that you look at the ‘Kendale’s Studio’ thread on the construction forum, and you’ll see in great detail exactly how that soffit was constructed. Djo – just tried to find that thread for you, but either I’m tired or it’s been moved. You’ll soon know where it is I’m sure, but don’t build those bits until you’ve read it all!! Hope this helps, Kind regards. Lou. 8)
