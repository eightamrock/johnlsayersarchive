studio_drums4 December 2008#55
Yes, my build did end up as originally planned. It's a true 'room within a room' on it's own isolated concrete slab. Remember, I started with an attached garage. I basically added 2 more layers of drywall to the existing garage walls and then built a room inside of it with 3 layers. I haven't done any measurements, but the isolation is remarkable. Standing right next to a rock drummer I'm guessing produces about 125db. The ambient noise in the neighborhood(birds, bugs, heaters, A/C, cars, roads, highway, etc.) is probably close to 55 db, and it easily masks the little bit of bass that still makes it through the walls. That's a difference of about 70db! My neighbor is about 17' away. Standing at the property line, you have to really, really listen to hear the little bit of bass. I imagine that inside their house they hear nothing at all. I showed them the completed studio and played drums while they went outside and they were quite impressed. Everyone is when they hear the difference. For the best results, maximize the airspace between your two walls/leaves. Every inch counts. I might have to break down and get a radio shack db meter and post some results...
studio_drums9 December 2008#56
for anyone interested... http://www.radioshack.com/product/index ... Id=2103667 I got this sound-level meter yesterday and did some testing. guitar amp played loudly: 105 db (3 feet away). drums played loudly: 105-107 db (3 feet away). outside ambient noise(wind, cars, fan units, etc.) 55-60 db guitar amp outside: no measurement (10 ft. from studio wall, closest to neighbors): drums outside: no measurement (10 ft. from studio wall, closest to neighbors): Neither was louder than the ambient noise a suburban neighborhood produces. All of the sound treatment inside the room- bass traps, panels, etc. really cuts down on the sound level inside the room, which then directly translates to lower levels outside the room- I would say about 5-15db depending on the frequency. For example, what would normally be a reverberating 115 db rimshot only registered 105 5 feet away with all the treatment. I should also mention that the treatment wouldn't work as well in this capacity if the walls weren't so well-constructed. They help the treatment work by bouncing all that sound back into the room. I would definitely call this one a success!
