xSpace6 September 2007#6
It's just my opinion here...24" layout, cutting overhead joists, one top plate...you are asking a lot from your structure to support it's own weight plus the weight of more heavier items. Get the few extra studs to get your layout on 16" centers and even if the joists are not going to be up sized to a 2X8 at least place them on a typical overhead 16" center as well. Don't cut yer joists. Drop your wall height before you do that, you need that support overhead more then you may want to consider. Your about to put something in the neighborhood of half a ton +, on the ceiling framing and over your headache not to mention what the walls have + this ceiling. Brien

Well, I am certainly not an expert, in fact, I am about as low as you can go on the studio construction food chain, so take my comments with a grain of salt... Your wall detail drawing looks to me like it might be a 3 leaf system rather than a 2 leaf system. I would think that you if you are putting up another wall inside of the garage, you should remove the existing drywall layer rather than beef it up! Your 3 leaves: Outside wall (siding) - - Inside wall (3 layers gypsum) - - inside wall (3 layers gypsum) Like I said, I am not qualified to offer any expert advise, but thought I would at least bring up the issue to see if someone else can clarify. Best of luck with your build out!

Dan Scott12 October 2007#17
Hey Everyone, I am also seeing a 3 leaf system with the garage door enclosure. As Rod writes in his book "Build It Like the Pro's" "Two leaf walls systems are good- two leaf wall systems are your friend. Three-and four - leaf systems evil devious fiends that suck up the money in your wallet and give you less than nothing in return" I am going to have to enclose a 16' garage door and will be faced with the same issue. I will be using concrete based "hardy plank" siding over layers of thick sheathing which I will leave a gap all the way around for backer rod and acoutic caulk. I will seal under the sill plate with neoprene. Of course this wall will be insulated but the inside will contain no sheetrock. I will build the interior wall next to this "enclosure" open on the back as well with sheetrock layers on the inside (control room). This will be a 2 leaf system.... "mass - air - mass". The extra leaf in the middle can decrease your STC rated by over 20!! That's why Rod's quote needs to be understood loud and clear. I also understand that for the optimal ST isolation between rooms, outside, etc., it is neccessary to tear out existing drywall becuase this creates that dreaded 3rd leaf if you don't. I have a bonus room above my garage (teenage dauthers bedroom), which will also require me to tear out the ceiling drywall as to increase isolation when I build a secondary ceiling creating a 2 leaf ceiling system. I also have to do the same with the wall with windows. I plan on dealing with the windows by adding a secondary window on the inside constructed on laminated glass and secured/sealed to the interior double wall. If anyone sees any holes in the construction plan for my garage opening, please let me know. I will be framing in opening very soon. Dan
