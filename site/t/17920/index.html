I own the apartment below the attic, so I'm not concerned about disturbing myself. However, I am concerned about how much sound reaches the apartment BELOW mine from the attic above.
The problem might not actually be the isolation of the floor itself. It is very likely that you have "flanking" paths through the structure of the building itself. It works like this: the sound waves inside the room eventually hit the wall surfaces, causing those to vibrate slightly. Since the wall surfaces are nailed to the studs / joists behind, they transmit the vibration directly to those. And since the very same studs/joists are also solidly connected to the wall surfaces in the apartment two floors down, those wall surface will vibrate too, thus transmitting your music sound directly into the apartment of your downstairs neighbor. Even if you isolate the floor to extreme levels, it still might not reduce the sound levels in your neighbor's apartment by much, since the sound is "flanking" around your floor, through the building structure. It might also be leaking into the wall cavities through air gaps in your walls and out again through air gaps in his walls, or through any HVAC system, or even out your window and back in through his window. They only real way to isolate a room successfully is to isolate all parts of the room equally: walls, floor, ceiling, doors, windows, HVAC and electrical. They all need to be isolated to the same level. So, while adding mass and damping to your floor might help, it make not make enough difference to be worthwhile, unless you do the rest as well.
I was planning to put the V-Drums on thick rubber matting such as the type used under weight-lifting apparatus in gyms.
That might or might not work. Depends on the rubber. That would help eliminate impact noise. Another way of accomplishing this is by building a drum riser. Basically just a couple of thick sheets of plywood screwed together, resting on a 4" thick panel of OC-703 semi-rigid insulation. That is known to work well.
I currently am unable to measure volume since we're not rehearsing right now, but it's definitely a lower than average volume.
Next time you do play, take a sound level meter with you and check to see what your acutely level is. Set it to "C" weighting and slow response.
The floor is currently very old tongue-in-groove hard wood. It's far from air tight.
Bingo! There's a big part of your problem. That needs to be sealed air tight, as do all other possible cracks and gaps around the room.

Did you check with a structural engineer? You can't go adding loads of mass to your floor until you do that. Get him to come in and take a look at what you have, and tell you how much extra mass you can add safely.
