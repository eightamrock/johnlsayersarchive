gullfo22 May 2011#2
you're going to need a lot more than some foam as the width and height are nearly square and your second axial modes are reinforcing each other. you are also in a corner which is increasing the effect of that corner. so... you want to position your desk centered on that wall or a wall. you want to add a cloud overhead made from 150mm of rigid insulation with a 19mm MDF backing hung down around 300mm from the ceiling on an angle. this will disrupt the floor ceiling mode and not take of space you need for the rest of your room. build a couple of 100mm thick rigid insulation covered with cloth absorbers for your left and right reflections. maybe on stands. make sure you speakers are not mid way on the wall but have the spot between their LF and HF driver set at 1.2m off the floor (or aimed at your ear). while the foam isn't good, take advantage of the foam to make it thicker in spots on the walls, doors, and ceiling behind you.

gullfo24 May 2011#4
square rooms reinforce room frequency response which means that peaks and nulls will increased more than uneven ratios. to overcome this, bass traps in the corners etc plus you might build a hard backed cloud over the mix position can help. while it might be hard to change positions (on the wall currently or elsewhere) you have to decide how important your mixing is... being close to a corner it going to make it much harder so finding a wall in your room to put your desk so you have some symmetry left to right and then treat the wall behind you as well as first reflection points.
