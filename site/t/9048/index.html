gullfo30 June 2007#6
one thing - small volume - small dimensions mean room modes will be spaced apart or grouped rather than evenly (or better) distributed. and because the width and length are close, the modes will be close. in your chart, 55, 112, and 168 should be targets - keeping in mind you want to use broadband initially and then after you construct the room and do the basic treatment, measure and then do final treatments on any remaining problem frequencies.

jwl1 July 2007#9
One problem with a 45-degree angle in a nearly-cube room is that the ideal listening position will then be almost exactly in the center of the cube/room. This is not the best place, it will almost certainly exhibit a null or a peak. Remember the 38% rule.... I'd put the listening position there, and angle the speakers accordingly. In that small of a room, I'd probably go 38% out from the rear wall.

gullfo2 July 2007#11
just to follow up with previous questions you've asked in other threads - the slat resonators will be (slightly) diffusive as well as absorptive. the angled versions John drew for you will be broadband in nature and if you look at the wall unit designs, you'll get a sense of the range for them - which should mostly address the modes in your room. generally speaking, the angle of the soffits will depend on your monitors, space, desk, etc - but ultiimately you want a balanced equilateral triangle. the SOS absorber was geared toward a vocal booth more than a larger room - it might work but i'm not certain its effectiveness in louder volume or lower frequency situations - as compared to hanger traps or slat resonators, or superchunks.
