knightfly16 April 2005#2
Archie ??!? First, welcome to the board; second, there is no such thing as a cheap soundproof enclosure, especially when you want enough isolation for a clean recording. Third, can you give more info on What you call cheap What your space size will be What floor it's on, and how that floor is built What your DIY capabilities are - tools, abilities What your time frame is (or you would LIKE it to be) And, if you've not already found it, the REFERENCE section listed at the top of the forum, along with http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=25206 As a general rule, from bad to good performance so called "normal" wall construction would be, single frame with wallboard both sides, single frame with wallboard both sides + Resilient channel one side on the frame, lightweight steel studs single frame, wallboard both sides, NO RC (about the same as wood WITH RC), Staggered studs with wider base plates, separate double frames with wallboard layers ONLY on ONE side of EACH frame. In all cases, more mass in each leaf of the wall is better; wider air gap is better; 2.5 to 3 PCF insulation is best, but not a lot better (inside a wall) than plain old fluffy fiberglass, optimum in either case is light contact with wallboard for some damping of the panels. Total hermetic seals using acoustic rated caulk is necessary or don't bother building. Anything you can't BLOW through is MASS, and you only want TWO centers of mass between any two spaces for isolation. Not more, not less; exactly TWO. Most of this, plus lots more, to be found in the above links... Steve
