Welcome to the forum, BootsyGraham. You mentioned that you've spent about a week here and have learned a lot so far. That's good. 8) But there are some things you missed in the "Before You Post" announcement, such as including your location in your profile. This is a worldwide resource, and it is imperative that we know what continent you're on. The first thing you should do is order Rod's most excellent book so that you can get a thorough single-voice authoritative overview of soundproofing. Not to pick on you, but the fact that you mentioned having carpets in your room in an effort to reduce the amount of sound exiting the room is an indication to me that you need a lesson in acoustic treatments vs. sound isolation. (And carpets are horrible as acoustic treatments for the most part, by the way... :roll:) While you're waiting on your shipment of Rod's book, study more threads here, especially those linked from the "Reference Area" announcement. I'm also quite partial to my own monster project thread, for obvious reasons. 8) I really think you'll be well served by studying it from the beginning. Your isolation goals and neighborhood issues are similar to my own. You'll see the great lengths I have been going through for over two years to achieve my goals of a room that will allow me to play my acoustic drum kit in a very aggressive manner with fellow rock/metal musicians without bothering neighbors or my bride in the house. I must warn you before you venture over to my thread that it is very long, is still very much a work in progress, and my plans changed pretty significantly over the course of time. You can benefit from my lessons learned -- just be careful not to consider my initial drawings to be "the way to go" because they are not. Bottom line, your garage conversion was done extremely poorly if soundproofing was your goal. Nothing short of a complete gutting and major retrofit construction project will give you the results you are looking for, or even results you'd find even slightly satisfactory. Beware of soundproofing snake oil CRAP that you may have encountered in your research before now. Contrary to the overly optimistic and/or fraudulent claims on the Web sites of such purveyors, there are no quick or easy or inexpensive solutions to your problem. A notable exception is Green Glue, although it's hardly quick or easy or inexpensive, and it's important to note that it alone is not the panacea you seek. Please post back with more information after you've studied up a bit more, and feel free to ask questions. :) We're here to help and learn from each other. --Keith :mrgreen:

n8nod9it7 February 2007#14
GOod pics. You know, i don't know how you are really going to achieve any useful soundproofing that will further satisfy your neighbors for two reasons. One is, you've already set a basis by playing loud so that the neighbors get angry, and so they'll be pre programmed to get irate even if you managed to reduce the noise to lower levels. The history has kinda been put forth that there is a rock band in a garage in what i gather is a quiet neighborhood. Second thing, to achieve any soundproofing that will keep your DB levels to make the neighbors happy (less the history) will require you to really think about dealing with every facet of the space. This means removing the garage door or sealing it with a wall, sealing every crack and hole in the structure, creating mass where mass doesn't exist (stud bays, windows etc), removing existing inner drywall, building a room in room (neccassary for your playing), and on and on. If you are really serious about it, and really want to keep playing without the cops showing up or having bad relations with those neighbors who tolerate the noise (its not noise to us musicians right..lol), then you have to invest the time and money and hard labor and planning to get a plan together to retrofit it into a proper studio or jam place. Plan to spend at least 3k minimally, but more like 5-10k for a relatively nice, "soundproof" place to play. It will take a few months to build and deal with everthing. Plan to think about things like safety, room acoustics, whether you want to render the garage door useless, how long you want to be there...etc. Read through EVERY thread here, i have and sometimes read through them several times, taking notes and bookmarking specific conversations. You can never learn too much and you learn alot by others mistakes. You may want to forgo this whole thing and rent a place to play? But unfortunately there is no quick fix, or acoustic foam or thing you can just put on the walls to help with isolation. You need mass and air and mass to stop TL (bass waves) mids, from just passing through common construction walls and ceilings.
