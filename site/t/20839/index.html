Or is there an optimum number of turns and length we should go for?
I seldom do more than about 5 or 6 baffles, or the boxes get ummanageably large.
What should we aim for in a design that’s not restricted by size?
Get the interior cross section as large as you can, at all points. That makes the box even longer...
(is this all correct so far?)
Yes
then provided the air exchange requirements are met, there would also be an optimum size to make these ducts.
Duct size is normally set by a combination of: Maximum allowable air flow velocity - available space - need to change cross section as much as possible. If you have very large ducts, then yest you get slow air flow which is great, but it also means you need huge silencer boxes in order to double the area... etc.
Q3) If we are only concerned with reducing the musicians sounds from escaping (not external noise getting in to the room),
Why would there be any difference between the two?
Q4) Reducing the cross sectional area of a duct increases the air velocity. Does this also create a point of acoustic impedance?
Yes, changing the cross sectional area does in deed create an impedance mismatch.
Q5) This seems to orient the baffle boxes with the large hole of the inner baffle towards the musician tank, and the large hole of the outer baffle towards the outside world. Is this a desirable design?
Yes, in general, that is a desirable design. I often end up with silencer boxes where the cross sectional area changes two, three, or even four times. So it doubles where the duct comes into the box, then doubles again due to a 2:2 split, then it doubles a third time just before the final exist path into the room, and then there's the final massive change in area at the register itself, where it interfaces with the actual room.
Q6) Is the entire inner (1st) baffle box and duct to be considered as an open pipe at this point?
No. It is not an open pipe. It exhibits impedance changes that are similar, and based on the same principle, but it isn't an open pipe.
Q7) with a silencer design of Inner Baffle Box -> duct -> Outer Baffle Box, is there now an optimum length, number of turns, cross sections etc. to achieve optimum isolation vs fan size?
Fan size selection depends on three things, basically: the total volume of air flow that you need to move (CFM), the velocity that you need to move it at (FPM), and the static pressure of the complete duct/room/box system. IT is no related ot the length, number of turns, or cross section, except insofar as those have an impact on the static pressure.
Q9) Is a design where the joining duct is greater cross section than the baffle boxes desirable? E.g. 12 inch baffle boxes joined by 18 inch duct.
Well you COULD do that if you wanted to I suppose, but I sure do hope you have a LOT of space available! an 18" duct is pretty big...
is it desirable to use an 8 inch duct connecting two 6 inch cross section baffles versus a 4 inch duct?
I try to use the smallest duct that will do the job, without increasing the static pressure too much. Smaller ducts are cheaper, easier to handle, easier to install, and easier to interface to the silencer boxes. Why wast money using a duct that is large than the box, when there's no real benefit to that?
Q10) If there should be a register on both the fresh and exhaust air openings, then can the noise heard by the exhaust inlet being reduced in cross section (air speeding up just after the exhaust register) be heard?
Why would the reduction in speed be audible? The only time that airflow becomes audible at these slow speeds is if it is turbulent. As long as your register does not create undue turbulence, then there's no reason why the change in cross section would cause a problem.
Q11) is the supply side currently the best practice for the fan location? (Positive room pressure)
Not for me. I prefer to have the fan on the "downstream" end: the point where the exhaust duct meets the real-world, outside the studio. Some people prefer to do it the other way.
Q12) when choosing a fan, assuming it has the correct capacity, what is a good diameter fan to aim for?
Going for one that matches the size of the duct seems like a good idea! :)
On page 29 there is a section on fixed duct fittings. There is a very nifty graph (see 2nd attachment) showing the effects on octave band power attenuation and the difference between gradual transitions and abrupt transitions when reducing the duct size from 24x24inch to 12x36inch. It shows that gradual transition is greatly improved over the performance of abrupt transitions for frequencies over 125hz.
Take another look at the title of that graph! It says "Velocity-generated sound of duct transitions". It's not what you think it is at all! It's about how much sound will be GENERATED by sudden velocity changes vs. gradual transitions. As you'd expect, a sudden change generates more turbulence, and therefore generates more air-noise. But look at the speeds they are talking about there: around 2000 to 4000 FPM! That's fast-moving air. 4000 FPM is over 45 MPH (70 kM/hr). Stick your head out the car window next time you are driving 45MPH (70 kM/Hr), and see how noisy the air flow is... Those speeds are much, much higher than the speeds that we need for studios. But anyway, that graph does not show what you think it shows. It does not show how sound is attenuated due to changes in duct section. In fact, it shows the opposite: how sound is generated by fast moving air at duct section transitions.
Q) has anyone had real world experience on using gradual transitions say in a silencer?
Not personally, no, as I would never do that if I could avoid it. If you don't have a sudden transition, then you don't get the sudden change in impedance that you need. The only time I use gradual transitions in ducts, is at take-of points, to keep the speed and pressure constant. But not inside a silencer box. Or I MIGHT do that if I needed to reduce turbulence for some reason, and that was a bigger problem then having a good impedance mismatch. But I would not do that for purposes of improving TL in silencer boxes. - Stuart -
