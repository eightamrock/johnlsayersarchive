knightfly9 February 2005#3
If you can, spread the sand out some so it will dry more - also, it will compact more as it dries. If you MUST fill the blocks before the sand is dry, try tamping as you go and do NOT paint or cover the blocks on at least one side until you MUST - then, only paint with latex paint and NOT oil-based paint, so the moisture can escape. When the sand finishes drying, it will settle some more; this won't hurt your isolation too much, as it will only affect the top foot or so of wall... Steve

knightfly9 February 2005#7
Yes, tamping means to pack down - best done every few courses of block - dry sand takes up less space, you are just "helping it along" a bit. I'm wondering if by "plastering" you mean "rendering" - this is where you mix mortar solution and spread about 1/2" thick on the block after the wall is built - this increases mass, seals the pores of the block, and improves STC a bit. If by "plaster", you mean adding gypsum wallboard to the block after it's built, then NO - don't paint it first. If you're going to continue to use wet sand, at least wait to put ANY finish on at least the outside of all the walls - this will help some of the moisture find its way out of your walls. Otherwise you will probably have mold growing in your rooms within a few months... Steve

knightfly1 March 2005#19
Hi, Soul Food, and welcome; your question would depend on which materials are available in your area, and their cost; generally, you want high mass and low cost without giving up durability or ease of building - not always possible. Also, the outer mass of your mass-air-mass envelope will (in the case of a free-standing building) need to be weather resistant, or there's no sense in building it :cry: You can help us help you by checking around your local area for price and availability on any masonry block products, gypsum wallboard, particle board, OSB (Oriented Strand Board, similar to plywood) MDF (Medium Density Fiberboard), chip-board, cement board, and of course framing lumber; 2x4 for framing (35mm x 90mm roughly) and larger for ceiling framing, typically 2x6 or 2x8 (35mm x 140mm, 35mm x 190mm approx.) Also, it will be better if you start your own thread; that way, it's easier to keep track and the threads don't wander around so much. Again, welcome; I think you're our first Slovenian member, or at least the first who's posted 8) Steve
