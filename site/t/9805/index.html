rod gervais27 November 2007#8
it would be a stange world where I say what you have ducted in your drawing........ I have never seen a split take place at a fan coil unit - and they doin't like to have any such things occur for generally 6 times the duct width..... So you will probzably have to relocate your access door - and rething your ducting........ BUT - the good new is that you do not have to use a fan for fresh air if you connect directly (within a foot or so) of the air handler unit with a fresh air supply duct - and then just install a relief duct at least 10' DOWNWIND (thus still on the return air duct) of the inlet - this with a Barometric pressure relief mounted on it to adjust for positive pressure within the space....... I NEVER recommend 2 fans for this operation......... All you have to do is verify the static pressure at the inlet point - and then use this knowledge to size the fresh air supply duct - use a "Duculator" to determine the actuall size that works. Example - if you had 1" of static pressure at the inlet - then you could size the pipe for 6" - wide open using .5" of that static = which would feed you about 260 cfm - adjust the baometric damper to open at .01" static - and the system would balance out at about .1" of positive static pressure within the room - flow would drop to 250 cfm while maintaining the systems original 1" of static pressure on the original fan coil........ Use the fan you already have to your advantage. Save money long term on equipment costs - energy costs, etc. Rod
