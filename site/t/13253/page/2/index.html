Fair enough; The concrete on wool seems like a good solution (I'm gonna assume it's an active spring). That part is, imho, good. The "second" floating part isn't. Floating a floor is more than "just" put a frame on rubber, as we learned in the threat mentioned earlier (it's even a sticky). To "float" means that there must be some sort of "spring" activity in between layers. To engage a spring, it needs load. (again, I don't need to explain the basics). And we start with deadload (the deck itself and other things that's gonna be permanent attached). The deadload should be higher than the expected live load in order to have a stable floor. The finished decks has no (descent) mass to start with. The auralex pucks, besided being questioned by many, don't "spring" in this case. They are just stiff pieces of rubber flanking all the way through. And IF the upperdeck should indeed float, you still have a M-A-M-A-M construction. Again, basics. Eric Desart has written some great words of whisdom about activating a spring, it's here somewhere. Perhaps in the same threat we discussed earlier. (I keep loosing track) Hope this clears things up concerning my 'opion' :)

Achillx wrote:
Is this drawing ok?
Yes, that's looking great. It's 2-leaf wall, ceiling and floor. You might wanna settle with a more "common" type of rockwool which is much less in weight (less density). A common wool is about 50kg/m3 top. You don't need to use a mix of plaster and mdf. Gypsum is one of the cheapest and easiest mass around. 3 sheets of 15mm will be great! The floor seems cool. What's that "deadsheet" you talk about? I'd advise to glue the whole finishing floor (laminate or real wood) to the concrete floor. That'll keep it more "silent". You plan to rest the whole inner room structure on that floating concrete floor? That needs some serious calculation since the pressure on the floor perimeters will be much higher than where you finishing floor goes (the weight of the walls and the ceiling on top will kill the "spring" if it's overloaded)
Is the air gap small-big-ok? I count the air gap with the rockwool inside isn't that correct?
The gap is ok. Bigger means a bit better "isolation", but 30cm is cool. Yes, including the wool (it's part of the air cavity indeed)

Your global plans are looking fine. It's the details that come in play. Make it a perfect sealed room and you'll be in the save zone. The concrete already does a great job. That's your biggest friend ;)

John Sayers13 July 2009#34
Yes Achillx - that's more like it. The air gap needn't be as great 100mm would be fine. The plaster, mdf, plaster is correct - sound doesn't like changing mediums, so changing from plaster to mdf and back to plaster reduces it's strength. That's why FM Design used it in the link I gave you. Most people settle for 2 x 16mm Drywall as it satisfies most conditions. I've stood outside a room built like you have drawn with a band playing inside and I couldn't hear the band. One other point. The floated concrete slabs I've built were based in the legendary Tom Hidely's design where he uses a similar sandwich like the walls. It goes insulation, plywood, insulation, vapour barrier, concrete. cheers john
