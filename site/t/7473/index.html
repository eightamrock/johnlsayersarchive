Cut off tapered part of drywall???

Started by andy_eade on 10 November 2006. 10 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 7473.

andy_eade10 November 2006#1
My Drywall contractor just came up with a time-saving idea that I wanted to share with you guys and see what you think. When doing multiple layers of drywall, the challenge is that you need to make sure you apply enough mud to the tapered edge such that it will fit flush against the next layer once added. This requires a couple of passes. This means waiting for the first pass to dry before doing the second, and then waiting for the second to dry before applying the next layer of drywall. You want to make sure you do this since the mud often shrinks a little when drying and you don't want to leave any airgaps. SO, with that it mind the suggestion in question is whether you could just cut off the 3 inch tapered part so you'd only need mud or caulk one very small joint for each layer, hence making installation a whole lot quicker and easier. What do you guys think?

Untapered drywall is actually pretty hard to find. Home Depot doesn't stock it. In my case I just did one layer of mud. I didn't sand it down and I didn't apply multiple layers in order to make a perfectly flat surface. It saved a lot of time. The only layer I plan on making "perfect" like that will be my inner leaf final layer -- the one that will have knockdown texture applied to it. I'll change that plan only if I'm convinced making every layer perfect and even will make that big of a difference. --Keith :mrgreen:
guitardad7214 November 2006#5
This topic is somewhat related to my recent thread: Drywall moves 7/8" OFF O.C. w each layer of drywall? Result of that rampage thought was to measure industry standard OC and do not bother accounting for overlaps of wall coverings at corners. How it relates... when we install 2 or more layers of drywall we do not want seams to overlap, we want to stagger the seams of each peice of drywall from stud to stud. Also we want to alter long deminsion of drywall from vertical to horizontal for each layer. We want the last layer of drywall to have the long seam going horizontal for a good finish cause a horizontal seam is less likely to show on finished layer. SO we have to install at least one layer going vertically. IF 3" were cut off each side we would not have 4' to work with, only would have 3' 6", and obviously these custom taperless edge peices of drywall would not line up on either 24" or 16" OC studs. IF it was the middle layer of 3 layers, maybe... the 1st and 3rd layers would sandwich 2nd layer in place, but still got to make sure that 2nd layer is fastened for mud. Also I don't like the idea of wasting the material. Marc
len-morgan16 November 2006#6
If the problem you are trying to avoid by cutting off the tapered edges is that you'll have a small air gap behind the next layer of drywall (unless you "build up" the tape/mud), I think you are creating a worse problem if you cut off the tapered edge: You are now going to have a raised up section behind the next layer and the air gap, though small, would be bigger than a "dip" behind it if you used the tapered edge. len
