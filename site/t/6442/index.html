knightfly15 June 2006#10
Panel traps work by sound PRESSURE, not VELOCITY (at least from an exterior point of view, such as your sound) - the sound pressure excites the front panel at it's resonant frequency, then the movement of the panel tries to move the interior air back and forth through the internally mounted absorbent. Losses occur because of the force required to move the front panel itself, which turns the sound pressure into heat; further losses occur when the internal air is restricted in its movement through the absorbent, due to the air friction within the interstices of the absorbent material, also turning the energy into heat. Although a panel trap doesn't need to be hermetically sealed to still WORK, (air being a relatively stiff medium, it can't "get out of the way" fast enough NOT to still allow approximately the same physical response) the formula is based on a sealed trap - so if you tried to build a NON-sealed trap, you'd need to build and test in order to get close to your target frequency. Angling a sealed trap across a corner might improve its ability to target the same range of frequencies in two axes instead of mainly the one it is perpendicular to, but otherwise would NOT function the same as corner-mounted porous absorbers.
