Eric_Desart8 August 2007#6
deed it should be good that you take pictures. Your window panes are more than thick enough compared to your wall leafs. I don't think you have edge absorption do you? Pictures will tell more. What I also know is that it isn't easy to localize sound where it's coming from. That works VERY psycho acoustical. Your panes are respectively 50 and 62.5 kg/m2. Your wall leafs ca 22 to 25 kg/m2. I also have the suspisious feeling that your window frame fails. It's not that easy to make a frame respecting the TL qualities of these window panes. You say it's laminated glass. How is that build up? 2 or 3 layers of glass? how many layers and/or thickness of PVB (foil) in-between. Mostly the glass code on your invoice answers that question. Hearing nothing also depends of the masking level of the reception room. I have experience with ultra high isolation rooms. It's not that easy to obtain the "hearing nothing" level with high emission levels certainly in the lows. It's also relative to expect with that wall build-up obtaining a "hearing nothing" level. But that "hearing nothing" is relative of course. At a certain moment you're blocked by flanking as well. If that's the case, or that window frame is too weak then it's better that a guy with some experience has a look locally. Let's hope it's some simple thing, otherwise you need local help. There are measurement techniques able to find the sound contribution of every individual part of the boundaries in a room, hence allowing to track weak spots, or defining the difference in TL of different parts of the same wall (practically impossible with standard sound pressure measurements). But that is expensive equipment, hence mostly handled by expensive guys. This technique is called intensity measurements.
