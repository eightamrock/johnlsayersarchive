sense of what i might be getting into by trying to transform the garage in my backyard into a studio space. so first, thanks loads to everyone who knows ten thousand times more about all this than me; please know that i'm not just posting blind and expecting freebee advice.. i'm doing my research as best i can; hopefully any response i get here will help me distill that wealth of information into landlady tells me i can build whatever i want in it , play music in it, whatever; this is a huge plus. the building has brick walls, a concrete floor and a bare concrete floor which appears to be about a foot deep (i mean, i measure a foot from the base of the outside walls to floor level). it has three large (30" x 48"ish) normal house type windows with no glass in the panes.. these are covered by huge rusty metal plates on the outside to deter folks from peeking in. the roof is supported by several wood joists (maybe 1 1/2" x 9") that are set into the brick at the top of the walls. the roof itself is wood with a tar covering. there is a normal size door frame at one end of the garage (currently occupied by a shoddy piece of plywood that is hinged to the wall and has a hole brooding, i find myself going in loops in my head about the best way to attack this space. here's what i want: a room with decent isolation - doesn't have to be totally soundproof , as i'm not this scope?) a room that is comfortable enough for a drum kit and three amplifiers (two guitars and bass, for instance) to be recorded simultaneously without completely bleeding all over each other. this is also a mic issue, i know - i guess i just mean itd be nice if i could build some sort of sub divisions inside the live room ... a slightly more sturdy version of propping mattresses between feedback.. take care bryan

John Sayers31 August 2006#2
Bryan - I can see where you are coming from but your scale is out. A drum kit is around 8ft wide - your looks like 5ft. That control room would be a small hell hole - you need room volume to get a decent sound in a control room. I understand your saying you need a larger live area than control area but if you were to put Scott's Studio (see thread on small studio in 3D) into your space the whole area can be a work area just by opening the doors. I've found that people with a decent control room end up using it as the rehearsal room with everyone going through the console and the monitors acting as the PA. A drum kit is going to take up almost 1/3 - 1/2 of your space so if you can use the control area as a practice area everyone will be more comfortable IMO. cheers john
