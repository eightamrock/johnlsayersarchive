Jester23 April 2010#182
I had a client call that wanted to fly into town to record the voice over for a training video. So I had a strong motivator to finish my vocal booth. I worked around the clock for a week and managed to get it finished (or at least close enough to record). I put in 2 angled slat resonators and most of the rest of the walls are 3" rock wool covered with cloth panels. I also put in a nice little desk. It's really great to be recording after all of the work I have put in. The booth sounds good although I still need to put in a cloud. The ventilation seems to be working well with 2 people in the booth for long periods of time.

seamus6 May 2010#186
Hey Lief, Amazing and inspiring work. I have one question about your soffit design. It looks like there is not any decoupling between the studio walls, the shelf, the speaker box (inside or outside) and the baffle. Is that correct or did I miss it? Thanks, Seamus
Jester7 May 2010#187
The soffits are not decoupled. I went back and forth about decoupling but in the end I went with John's design (or at least my understanding of it) His design calls for the soffits being built very solidly with lots of mass. I did experiment with isolating the speakers with various isolation materials and I left room in case I want to isolate the speaker boxes later. So far I really like how they sound but it's too early to tell if the translate well. I'll report back after I have finished a few jobs.

DJ Hellfire wrote:
OK, you say you set the window at a 3 degree angle. How exactly do you get the top and bottom stop bevels at the same 3 degrees as the side stop rips? Do you just set the table saw for a 3 degree bevel or something?
The top and bottom stop are the only ones that require a bevel of whatever the degree might be. The sides are flat 90 degree pieces, the place that these side stops need modifications on are the ends that meet the head and foot stop, and this will require a "bevel" of the chosen degree at the top and bottom of the side stops. If I had more time and less beer I'd draw ya a picture, I think based on the work I have seen that you are doing, you will understand the process...just get the "plane" flat and you can cant the degree of the whole stop asembly as much as required or needed.
