here's one thought on getting better angles in the control room. i'd also block off whatever window you're not using with brick or other heavy mass. multiple window panes (like triple leafs) will likely be a concern. i'd frame off the inset and use that space for conduit runs, hvac duct, etc. the floating floor question: since you're suspending the ceiling your weight on the floating floor will be less than if you supported the ceiling on the new interior walls. this could throw off your calculations if you haven't accounted for that effect.

Like I said before: I don't see nearly enough mass on that floor to work properly, and I don't see how you calculated the spacing for the resilient pads. You don't seem to be taking into account the different loads in different places on your floor: the load under the walls is much greater than the load under the part of the floor that has nothing on top of it, but you don't seem t be accounting for that in your plan. Did you do the calculations? How much deflection do you need in order to float, for the pads you are using? How much load do you need to get that amount of deflection? Did you take into account variable live loads, such as people and equipment, that will change at times?

Nothing at all! At least, acoustically the best idea would be to not have anything on your floor: just leave it as bare concrete. However, you do have isolation needs, so you might need something on the floor to deal with flanking and impact noise. How much isolation do you need? Did you look at IR-802 and IR-811, to see if you can find a system that will meet your design goals for isolation? Based on IR-802, it looks like the plan that you have right now will make your isolation WORSE than bare concrete in the low and low-mid frequency ranges. I'd look for another solution, definitely.
