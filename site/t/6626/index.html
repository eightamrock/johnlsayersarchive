sharward14 July 2006#5
Aj, I was thinking about your iso with the neighbors -- and I think it might be possible (though a bit pricey, but hey, it's only money) to do inoperable windows (no sliders, but framed so that windows can be replaced with sliders later) -- basically, the same type of thing you plan to do for your control room window. If you do it correctly (twin windows, one in each leaf, using laminated single-pane glass about 1/3 the thickness of the overall thickness of each leaf), you'll have great looking windows that will let in a lot of natural light (which is a sticky code requirement, by the way). For the big slider facing the outside, you could a wide but short window that's up pretty high -- say 5 or 6 feet wide and maybe < 2 feet tall, with the bottom of the window about 4 feet off the ground. Then, later, if you need to convert the room into non-studio purposes, you could remove the windows and the wall below them and around them, and drop in slider on the outer leaf. Let me know if you'd like me to sketch that up a bit (it may take me some time though). As far as the sliders between live room and iso booth go, I'm sure you can get away with conventional solid core wooden doors there, swinging on hinges. John likes designing with sliders, probably because of their ability to join rooms visually and the lack of swing that makes the floor space more usable. Although, come to think of it -- the nice thing about having the iso booth in its current configuration is that you could slide the doors open and, in theory, still rehearse as a six-piece band in one large room, kind of. The currently layout does offer that flexibility if you can think of it that way. 8) --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward16 July 2006#10
Hey Aj, The "to float or not to float" question has been discussed here many times. Do some searches and you'll find some great stuff. In short, floating a floor can be effective if done correctly -- but doing it incorrectly (which is very common) can make matters worse. Rod's book discusses isolated slabs vs. floating concrete slabs (that's right -- floating a concrete slab on top of a concrete slab). In fact, it was Rod who pretty much convinced me to do an isolated slab in my project. Regarding the forms -- that is something I probably would have done differently if I had the benefit of Rod's book before I prepped for my pour. Rather than go into a lot of detail, I'll let you wait for the book and read about it yourself -- he's far more qualified to discuss it than I am anyway. ;-) As far as the "blue line" plan goes, you're close. you need to isolate your outer leaf from your inner leaf too... So you're actually looking at probably three pours -- one for the outer foundation (and perhaps the booth, foyer/sound lock, and bathroom at the same time), then the control room, then the live room. --Keith :mrgreen:
