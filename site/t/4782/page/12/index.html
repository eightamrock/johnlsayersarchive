knightfly23 October 2006#277
Finally got a few minutes to read your PDF - just a couple minor things - Page 2, section #8 - Resilient Channel is NOT the same as "hat channel" - if you use this term when talking to a typical construction guy, you'll get solid steel hat channel which is NOT resilient. If you're referring to Dietrich's "RC-2", this is a double-legged RESILIENT channel that, at first glance, LOOKS like hat channel but actually has a bunch of little slots cut into the two legs so it is RESILIENT. If this is what you mean, be SPECIFIC by manufacturer and part number and a PICTURE - otherwise, unless you're dealing with WSDG or RBDG for your studio build, (or Rod :wink: ) you'll be SCREWED, and not in a GOOD way. For an example of the pain you could be in for, check out this http://www.recording.org/ftopict-22469.html I'm really NOT trying to be a raving lunatic on this, but this is a COMMON mistake because almost ZERO local contractors will know NOT to make this mistake unless you lead them by the nose to the EXACT materials you must use, and I felt it was time for a more current reminder of the pitfalls... In that same paragraph you mention using BOTH RC and Green Glue - I hadn't had time to read all of Brian's excellent contributions/tests yet, so went looking. I think you'll be happier with your choice after looking at two similar tests, one with and one without RC - at the lowest tested frequency (80 hZ) they're virtually identical, but for the "mid-bass" region apparently the combination of BOTH RC and GG is notably better. Good plan. Ceiling - your terms of vertical and horizontal need to be changed, perhaps to east-west or north-south, to avoid confusion. That's all I saw that could be improved... Steve

sharward30 October 2006#283
First of all, I'm not in favor of the DIY wall brace concept. Sorry. I just think there's too much opportunity for error. Suppliers specialize in making these things and they've got the test results to prove their worthiness. Far be it for me to think I can come close to duplicating their success with a couple off off-the-shelf Home Depot brackets and some rubber I found on the Internet... :roll: That being said... If you insist on going down this DIY road, you need to understand that the rubber must be slightly compressed all the time -- if not, it's nothing more than fusing your leaves together, which obviously defeats the purpose of trying to isolate them in the first place! :shock: Bona fide, quality-made anti-sway braces are designed that way, out of the box. There's a slight compression to them already. That's what makes them magical. (No actual magic, of course -- it's pretty straight forward.) When you're driving your car, your shock absorbers act as springs because they're under some compression already. Watch a parked car as people get into it, and it bounces a little. (In my case, quite a bit! :lol:) Andy, my vote is that you wait a couple of weeks to get the good stuff and do it right from the start. You've waited and/or been delayed by unfortuante discoveries this long -- why rush on this critical and "unfixable if you get it wrong" component of your plan? :roll: We're not talking about a paint color here -- we're talking about an integral part of the skeleton of your project. Get this wrong, and you're quite probably f*&%ed. :roll: --Keith :mrgreen:
