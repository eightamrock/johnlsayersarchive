Hindsight tip Picked up a nice little tip from the drywall contractors over the last couple days that works like a charm (Maybe this was already obvious to you, but not me). After installing the backer rod and caulking the joints; in order to eliminate any hairline cracks, and especially to make the joint seal flush ready for tape or next layer of sheetrock; take a pass at it with a putty knife. It'll give you a really clean and tidy look. More pics to come later on. We're done with the control room and just hanging the 2nd layer in the performance room.

andy_eade19 April 2007#331
SO in the spirit of "no pictures - didn't happen". Here are some updated pictures from about 2 weeks back. I need to take same as of now (paint is up on walls an ceiling.) But these show the doors and windows in, custom door frames built etc. Enjoy! A couple of hindsight notes that I hope might be useful to you all. 1) If you use contractors - watch them like a hawk - REGULARLY! I have had my biggest screw up to date here in the last week and it has caused much frustration, a major change in esthetic design, and a delay of 2 weeks to correct. The problem also compounded over two separate contractors blunders (and my own for not checking). Here's what happened: The electrician had stapled all of the cables in place (or most it would seem) where they should be brought through in the wall. On the day the drywallers began installation of sheetrock I made it clear to them that these were for outlets etc, were going to be Wiremold boxes, and NEEDED TO BE ALL AT THE SAME HEIGHT. The main failure was with the cable for the track lighting. I don't believe this was measured, nor stapled, so the drywall guys just pulled it through wherever it was. So what I ended up with, having finished, sanded, primed, and painted all walls and ceilings was track lighting that does not line up and a couple of outlets that are up to 3 inches higher than all the others. Just to add to the nightmare, the electrician sent his "assistant" out to install the tracks (bear in mind that we did not notice any of this upon completion of the sheetrock - hence why I painted). The "ass"-istant spent a whole day installing 7 tracks and at the end of the day tells me he's leaving so I'm like "let me come done and check it out". My chin must have hit the floor when I saw nothing lined up. I think the exchange went something like this: Me: Ummm, nothing lines up Ass-istant: yyyyyeah Me: Well don't you think it looks... uh... weird Ass-istant: YYYYYeah I did think that My internal voice: THEN WHY THE EFF DIDN'T YOU STOP EVERYTHING AND COME ASK ME BEFORE YOU CUT THE ROMEX FLUSH WITH THE WALLS AND CEILING YOU JACK@$$. You see, considering the direction that the cable comes in from, once he cut it; we no longer have any slack to move it. MAJOR PROBLEM! It has been a real pain to rectify whilst still maintaining the soundproof integrity of the construction. So, be sure to follow these guys around with a tape measure. My other piece of hindsight you'll be pleased to know, is a positive one: 2) If you're planning on using adjustable door seals and automatic door bottoms like the zero international ones - SHOP AROUND. I had originally been scoping out two different products from two well-known, competing, acoustic supplies websites. One of the products (was way out of my price range) was coming in around $500 for the complete door kit, and the competitor was more like $300. I did a little research online to find who the manufacturers of the seals were (and there are many; Reese; Pemko, Zero etc..) and called them to ask about sales; they referred me to my local shop here in Alexandria, VA who sold me the exact same set up for about 50% of the cost. $50 for the auto door bottom, and $96 for the adjustable jamb seals. They even had real nice seals for the second seal around the door for about $10 per door. I can't wait to get these puppies installed. I am pasting below links to the products I am going with. These savings were so significant that it was the difference between only using pro seals on two of the four doors vs. using them on ALL of the doors – yippee! Hope this is all helpful info to you all. Jamb Seal Auto Door Bottom weatherstrip Cheers! Andy

andy_eade19 April 2007#334
I know Keith - it has been quite a year! So the solution was complex to say the least. For the performance Room: Remove two of the tracks, and move the romex back towards the wall (where the line feeds from) such that it lines up with it's neighbouring track. Bear in mind that I have 3 layers of sheetrock in this room, so to attempt to jeapordize my isolation we cut the first layer a square about 12 inches square, the second about 6 inches and the third very small. This allowed to re-seal each layer and overlap the seams. The layout of the track has also been changed from 4 independent tracks in four corners to a U shaped configuration - this helps eveything line up and look intentional rather than sloppy and un-measured. Everything will be clearer with a few photo's I promise. For the control room: Here there was only one track that was next to another that wasn'y lined up. By turning it about 30 degree's it too looks intentional and actually better! Who'd have thought! For the few outlets that are off; luckily for me the two that are really bad are on their own wall with no neighbouring outlets so we punched out the lowest (or highest) hole in the back of the wiremold box to buy as much adjustment as possible. They will still be off, but hopefully it won't notice too much being as they are on their own wall. I have been told that when you're working on a project of this magnitude to expect problems to occurr and it's all about the solution you implement, which in our case always has to be thought through very carefully to consider it's sound implications. In my case I put everything on pause and slept on it for a couple of days before I came up with the solution. As Winston Churchill once said "Success is the ability to go from one failure to another with no loss of enthusiasm". Funnily enough I heard this on a David Blaine special on TLC the other night when i was desperately trying to come up with solutions to these problems. It definitely made me smile! =)
andy_eade19 April 2007#335
Oooh one other piece of hindsight advice if you use a contractor for the sheetrock. mark on the floor wherever there is cable that should be pulled through. That way if the contractor misses on you will notice before it's too late. Thankfully this didn't happen to me, but hopefully because I was careful with this. I spray painyed an "O" on the floor wherever there was cable to be pulled for an outlet, an "L" for lighting, and"S" for switch. If there were multiple switches I would add a number - 5S for example. I also used "T" for the two room thermostats. hope this too helps you. Andy

Hey Guys. First of all let me get the apologies out of the way. It's been such a hectic time since I last posted. The studio is 90% complete, I'm now engaged, and even though there is still much to do to complete the studio, I am very busy down there with clients, my own band, and a whole lot of solo work. On top of the day job (which has been busier than ever too) it's been tough to find time for anything lately, but I did at least want to update you with some pictures of the studio in operation - taken during my first official session back in May. I do have some other pictures when it's looking a bit tidier ob my other computer that I need to get up here too. What else. Well, I guess I'll give you the status on problems / lessons learned since launch (of which thankfully there are only two): 1) No matter how many thermostats and mechancal dampers you have, if you only have one A/C unit that is shared with the rest of the house - it will only pump cool or hot air if it is cooling or heating the rest of the house. If you consider the temperature difference between a basement and the rest of the house; you can probably imagine that this is sometimes an issue. Not huge, but worth noting, and totally not what the heating guy explained to me. 2) Having followed the spec on electric / grounding to a tee - I still have a lot of issues with hum from guitars and keyboards. The desk itself is silent ass a church mouse, but bring a guitar or keyboard into the desk and enjoy the wonderful sound of 60hz as it decimates your mix! With guitars it does seem to be very sensitibe to where you stand and your orientation, however it doesn't seemed to be linked to proximity to the electrcal panel or anything like that. I have tried trouble-shooting in a number of ways- - Switching off circuits one breaker at a time to see if any have the most impact. There were two or three that reduced the noise a little. - Building a faraday cage over the electric panel to reduce EMI. No dice. - Experimenting with Ferrite Chokes on cables. No dice. - Trying different outlets to see if the noise is les noticable. Interestingly it is. When plugged into the same outlet as the desk the hum is reduced drastically (though not completely); which made me think it was a ground loop. However there is only one ground for the house and each outlet has it's own run. So we're going to try adding a power conditioner to the circuits that seem to be affecting the noise (as found in the first trial above). I'll keep you posted! So how about the good news? Well, I accomplished the main goal I set out to achive. We have two isolated rooms, they sound great, and people can sleep upstairs while I'm tracking drums downstairs. Similarly you can't hear a thing outside of the house when I'm tracking a full band. Sweet! :D I think that's about it for now. Again sorry it's taken so long. Now I need to find some time to catch up on everone else's threads. I did see that Keith is still building instead of rocking - come on man - put down the hammer and pick up the sticks already! ;) I can't even begin to tell you how much I love those two rooms, how happy I feel down there, and how much time I spend down there. Thank you all so much for you help, and for saving my lungs from Aspestos! I have told countless people I would probably be dead were it not for this forum! Sincerely, Andy "The Green Room" Studio.
