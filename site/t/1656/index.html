stevenicel17 May 2004#1
Hi all I have built the first stud wall of a double leaf construction mixing room wall and would like to know if it is that critical to cut the acoustic insulation to fit in the cavity between studs and noggings? or if you could just lay the insulation over the frame in betwwen both walls and affix it to keep it steady? I am using a product called tontine Acoustisorb 2 and it is very difficult to cut, so it would save me a lot of time if i could literally roll the sheets over the whole inside of the frame and use fasteners to keep it in place? Any thoughts anybody?
knightfly17 May 2004#2
You get a few db more isolation if the insulation rests against the inside panel of a wall - this dampens the panels which improves the loss through the panels, so overall performance is better. You also improve music frequency isolation by staying under 48 kG per cubic meter in insulation density, and completely filling the walls. Hope that helps, sorry it isn't what you wanted to hear... :? Steve

knightfly19 May 2004#4
Any insulation (other than closed cell foam) is better than none, so if you can't afford enough to completely fill the wall frames between the two leaves of wallboard, any you can put it will help. In that case, I would probably put the insulation against the inside of the wall toward the mixing room - The wierd numbers refer to the density of the insulation in kilograms per cubic meter - that spec is normally either shown in kg/m^3, or in pounds per cubic foot (PCF) - for in wall insulation, you want between 2-3 PCF, which translates to 32-48 kG/m^3 - this density gives the most even sound reduction inside a wall... Steve
