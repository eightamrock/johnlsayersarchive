laptoppop16 February 2005#5
OK, thanks - that gives a bit more to start. First, I'd really consider if you want only a single recording area. Even in a basement, you can often partition off two spaces - and that can be VERY helpful -- because of drums. Drums are so loud that unless your room is very very large, you get a lot of bleed into the other microphones. This will drastically cut down on your editing and processing options later. There's a ton of difference in cost between basement and seperate building. How is the area? Do you have close neighbors? How much sound do you need to contain within the recording studio, and how much can leak out?(i.e. bass, kick, etc.) The biggest question - what is your budget? What is the "best" is typically only able to be answered in the context of a budget. Building a great sounding studio can be more expensive than most folks want to think about. Ceilings - as high as possible. If its in the basement, its worth the extra money to get them to put in 10' or higher ceilings. The problem is that the ceiling and the floor form two parallel surfaces (unless its a seperate building with a sloped ceiling). You need space to put in the acoustic treatments to keep the sound from bouncing back and forth between these surfaces. In a darn good scenario, I'd want a drum room, a live room, a control room, and a closet or two where I could stuff amps or screaming vocals for more isolation. This can all fit in a basement if you plan carefully. -lee-
