AVare15 December 2008#7
All I have to work with is the Sketchup file. It shows a ~6" gap between the inner and outside walls of the control room. Rebuild the side walls with a 1/2" gap between the inner walls and outside walls. This will make the width of the control room ~14.5'. To get the best possible: stop all construction; spend half an hour and go through the Sketchup tutorials (they get you started very quickly); correct your skp file to be accurate; read and follow the BEFORE YOU POST READ THIS at the top of the forum; start posting, wait for a couple of days for responses; and resume construction with the new plans in January at the earliest. Sorry, but that is the best way to both get the best possible room and avoid problems that may destroy the potential capabilities of the studio. Good luck either way. Andre ps. Soundman202016 December 2008#13
Here's an off-the-wall idea about that pole: It seems to me that the pole is a major problem, and that you need to consider some options for getting rid of it, not just working around it. What is it supporting? Would it be possible to transfer that load to a lintel (steal beam?) that stretches across the width of the room, supported on poles at either side? You probably cannot answer that question yourself, but a qualified structural engineer can answer it for you, and tell you how to go about doing that. Even if that hypothetical lintel beam eats into your ceiling space, it still beats have a pole in front of your speaker, and also beats having a non-symmetrical room. - Stuart -

Now here's the fun part.... Getting closer to the end of this studio build - it's going fine now. I have been in touch with Auralex to try and get some ideas for acoustic treatment. They weren't able to complete a modal analysis as the rooms are not rectangular, but did offer some good suggestions. I wonder if anyone would be able to sort out the best way to go? It seems like the best solution for the control room is to go for Elite C24 panels set a couple of inches off the upper walls and ceilings as I don't have easily accessible corners to put bass traps in. They said this would help with low end absorption. Also to make sure I covered the 1st order reflections, and stagger the other panels so there wouldn't be panels directly opposite each other on parallel walls. I seem to like pretty live control rooms so they suggested 40% coverage rather than 50%. There was a lot of confusing discussion about the back wall and whether I should absorb, diffuse or have a combination of both. I have no clue... The live room, with its low ceilings, would have 30% coverage with the same panels, but I would have to have the ceiling panels right on the ceiling and not a couple of inches off - there's so little height.... Then the vocal booth would be 60% coverage with the pArt Science audio tiles - that sounds like a good bet. I would love to get some input regarding this as I would hate to spend $6,000 and get this totally wrong! Looking forward to some suggestions - thanks! happy New Year - Jonn
