In the inllustration in Rod's book on window design there is shown a peice of "fabric wrapped insulation" that goes between the two walls to hide the gap. For the bottom there is no problem but there is no way to get such a loose thing as fabric wrapped oc703 to stick to the upper inside of the window frame. Glueing or stapling would surely result in sagging at some point. Even getting the fabric to "wrap" around the insulation seems impossible because you can' glue or staple fabric to insulation. It seems there is no way to hide the gap exept with a wood baord of some kind. Is that what people here do? Remember, if what ever is in that gap comes loose over time the wall would have to be ripped out to fix it.

Nope. Tested that already. It does not adhere enough to trust it long term. Besides once you adhere the fabric to the rigid fiberglas there is no way to adhere it to the top. It will sag or come off if you glue it. If you nail it you will see the indentions from the nails. THe only way I see to do it is to stretch fabric and staple it then put wood trim over the staples. However, even this way leaves at least one seam showing.

Russ, 1st off - remember that I told you in the beginning of the book that I was not going to teach you how to construct - thus not how to use a skil saw - nor how to use a lathe or drill press......... well this is one of those things that I figured any experienced tradesman would be able to figure out - so I didn't bother to explain. There is no flaw - you just aren't thinking creatively...... It's not that tough......... and you don't even need adhesive........ if it's done right it won't ever sag. cut your rigid insulation for a tight fit (and the top should go up before the sides) the fit the cloth wrap so it fits the face, wraps the sides and laps about an inch over each other in the back. Make the full wrap and use standard sewing pins to pin everything tightly together....... then take a regular ordinary staple gun - the type you hold in your hand like this: http://www.shoplet.com/office/db/EPI90119.html and carefully work your way from 2 corners and then down the length of the piece - making sure to hold everything even and snug..... once you have the piece made - you can take standard Panel Nails - 1 1/4" or so, pre-paint them to match your cloth color and tack the piece up into place. Then (finally) you can install your 2 sides and then the base...... all 3 of which should receive a few tacks as well to keep them in place over the years. When you're done - if you didn't know the nails were there you'd never even notice them.... they just sort of dissapear in the scheme of things. Sincerely, Rod
