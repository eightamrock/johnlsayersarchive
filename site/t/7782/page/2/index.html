The recording booth will occasionally be recording instruments, maybe even drums – but that will be rare. 90% of the time we will only be recording vocals. Being a smaller room, we decided to have the ceiling frame not touch the existing ceiling for a bit of extra isolation, using the room in room 2-leaf principle (existing walls are over 1 foot thick stone). The room will have a live half and a dead half. The control room is larger. We will be using RC on the existing ceiling to hand the drywall in the control room. (Anyone know where to get RC in Germany? Or the German name for it at least??). We are intending to have 2 layers of drywall throughout both rooms, and we got a great deal on 3.2 inch thick rockwool to line the ceilings and walls. We’re still searching for appropriate acoustic caulk over here. Hard to find. We haven’t decided on a color scheme yet, however seeing as space will be tight, I’m leaning towards a lighter shade to try an give both rooms a larger feel. Framing is almost complete. Electrical starts this weekend. More pics on the way… D.

marz15 June 2007#47
Dingo, Looks like you are spending a lot of money and effort and its coming along nicely. But as Ro and Sharward have several times suggested that you post the contruction plan details to help them to help you, I think you missed a great opportunity. I guess Timber Frame construction is not very common in Germany, but typically the members (Studs and Rails) would be built the other way around. IE the wall would be at least 8cm deep, preferably 10cm (with the classic 2 x 4 's for those used to inches or 100 x 50 mm) and not 6cm deep as yours are. The strength of any beam is not in its thickness, but in its depth, which will affect the strength of the door frame to hold a heavy door when it is open, and the force is not directly downwards. This would make the walls much stiffer as well and allow effectively more space for Fiberglass and Air to increase isolation. Usually there are also Jack (Shorter) Studs under the header of the door frame, and the header is also usually a bit more of a heavily constructed box. There are several threads referencing this in this forum, somewhere. Hopefully you can beef up the door frames before adding doors.
