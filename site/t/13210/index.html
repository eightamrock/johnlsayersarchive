I will post some Measurement charts in the next Post. Room Purpose The room is mainly used for tracking and mixing drums and small acoustic groups that i play with. So i am engineer and drummer at the same time for the recordings that are made in my room. As i have access to a professionally build soundproof recording booth next door in my neighbours studio, i will mainly record live instruments in my room. Mix Position Right now my first concern is to make the Mix position as good as possible, acousticaly speaking. I was thinking a lot about moving the Mixing Desk to another place inside my room but for me i don't see any other practical place to put my desk... Soundproofing The Room is located halfway underground with 0,5m heavy brickwalls. Sound proofing is not an issue at the moment. Treatment for Mix Position For Figures see Treatment Plan further down this Threat! 1.) Cloud (Fig.A) I want to build a really heavy cloud over the mix position for broadband absorption. 12cm of rockwool (40kg/m3 and 12 kPa*s/m2) with an air gap to concrete ceiling of about 10cm. All build into a wooden frame and hung to the ceiling on screw-hooks. 2.) Sidewall Absorbers (Fig.B) I want to cover the Sidewalls Right and Left of the Mixposition with 10cm of insulation (40kg/m3 and 12 kPa*s/m) in a wooden frame and fabric covering hung directly on the walls. Starting about 1m over ground and going up to the cloud. This should give sufficient control of early reflections from the sidewalls (i hope...). 3.) Front behind Speakers (Fig. C) I already have two huge broadbandabsorbers filled with insulation and going from floor to ceiling covering the whole frontside behind the speakers. At the moment they still have a wood cover at the back which will be removed and covered with fabric so the soundwaves that arent absorbed can go through it. 4.) Sidewall opening on Right Side (Fig. D) Option A: One Option would be to have a big absorber (or slat resonator or angled reflector) fit in that opening and the same unit on the opposite wall. I would prefer if the rack could stay where it is so i only close the opening obove 1m from floor. Option B: I could close the whole thing building a drywall-gypsum-wood-mineralwool-construction. I'd rather avoid that if not absolutely necessary...
Image not preserved: Treatment_Plan.jpg
How does the room sound at present? In generall, the room is good sounding. It was perhaps a little bit on the dull side, but that has been (over) compensated by putting in the new wooden floor. Room modes are present but there are no spots where the buildups are ridiculous. I did several recordings / mixes in the past that are considered pretty good sounding. Especially the Low End has been a problem though: I never knew if the low end of the mixes was right or not. (I solved that by running around the city playing back my mixes everywhere and made notes so i could get a better idea how my mixes transformed to other playback systems) And now: The Question :D Can this work? What do you think about the whole project? Can I get my mixing spot decent enough to make good mixes? I know that the mixposition is to some extend unsymetrical, but i hope that i can still manage to get a decent place for mixing. If you want to say: "That is a waste of time and money, STOP now before it's too late!" - say it now please! :roll: What You get in return? I will do a photo documentary of every building / Treatment step i do and i will do a measurement at listening (mix) position (like the ones in the next post) after every step of treatment installation to track my progress. So you can follow the progress with pictures and charts... Waiting curious(ly?) for your answers Simon
