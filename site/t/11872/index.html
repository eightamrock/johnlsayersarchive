Is my GG idea on top and bottom of flat 2 X 4's good or would strips of rubber or similar work better?
well, if you want to float your entire room here is what is ahead of you: You need to calculate, in advance, the final total weight of your entire room, including all the walls, floor, ceiling, equipment, people, HVAC, etc. Everything. Then you need to figure out exactly how that will be spread around on your 2x4 frame. So for every square centimeter of that frame, you need to know exactly how much weight will be pressing on it. Then you need to do some research and find some material that will compress to exactly the right amount so that it acts as a spring, when it has exactly the loading on it that you figured out above. Obviously, the loading under doors and windows will be higher than under the walls, and the loading under the empty floor space will be lower than both. But it will be different under each part, so you will need different materials under each part, that are just right to support the load for that particular section. You MUST do these calculations precisely, with no errors. If you get it wrong at any point, then there are two possibilities: 1) There will be too much mass over that point, it will over-compress the material, which will bottom out, and your floor will flank: So you just wasted an awful lot of time and money. 2) There will not be enough mass over that point, so the material wont be compressed enough to act as a spring, and your floor will flank: So you just wasted an awful lot of time and money. If you think you can just guess, and throw in any old bit of rubber, neoprene or EPDM that you happen to find, then don't bother: you would be wasting your time and money. The chances of you lucking out and getting your entire floor to float are about the same as your chances of winning all the lotteries in all of the states that run them, on the same day. Remember, if your floor flanks at even one single point, then that's it. End of story. Game over. Wasted effort. You have to get all your calculations right for every single point of your floor, or else it WILL flank.

A drummer is even worse than the city!! The drummer downstairs is probably producing 115 dBC. There is no way on this planet that normal city noises get anywhere near that. Perhaps 80 or even 90 in a really, really noisy place, but that's still nearly one hundred times less energy... Drums are the hardest things of all to stop, since most of the energy is in the low frequency end of the spectrum.

So you DON'T want to do a slab because of the weight, and instead you want to do a couple of layers of 5/8 drywall, a couple of inches of sand, another layer or two of drywall, a layer of 3/4 plywood, then underlay and carpet? So what's the difference? That sounds like about the same weight as a slab anyway! At least, if not more! Drywall is already half the density of concrete, sand is even more dense than that, plywood is not light, plus framing, carpet, etc.... you probably have enough mass there already for a three inch slab, or something close to that! Wouldn't it be easier to just do the slab? Either way, did you do the math on the floor? Can it take the kind of extra mass you are talking about? That sand-filled floor with drywall, plywood, drywall and carpet is already very heavy, then you want to build walls with four layers of drywal on top of it, plus a ceiling with another four layers, plus the framing, etc....
