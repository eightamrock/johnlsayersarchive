Hello all, i'm still in the design/material/ cost calculation phase for my mix/control room and booth. the mix room size is 10.6x14.7x8.2. walls front and back of the room bordering on the neighbors are concrete, right wall is glass, left wall is Ytong blocks. floor and ceiling are concrete. i'm thinking to do a room in a room construction. but the space is already small so i rather not do it if not absolutely necessary. other option is to do just the walls that need extra mass and seal that off properly. i cant see how adding a leaf of gypsum/studs construction would add anything to a 1 ft thick concrete wall, and ceiling. if at all effective i would just treat the 2 walls that have less mass. is this an effective approach or is my way of thinking incorrect? thanks! gullfo7 October 2007#2
generally speaking, the concrete walls will conduct sound very efficiently into your neighbors space. you might want to do some tests to determine how much is making it through. using a rubber mallet and a metal hammer, have someone tap the wall and floor in various spots while you make notes on the sound coming through from your neighbors place. also, if you have a stereo or PA capable of playing something with a lot of bass and drums (or pink noise) loudly, you can take some measurements all around using an inexpensive sound level meter. this will give you some idea on how much sound is escaping your room and from there you can determine how much isolation you need and what it will take...
jaye7 October 2007#3
i will do that to determine whats needed. if not much sound travels through( and i suspect it wont, i know the building and not much sound travels to the neighbors) is it possible to only build another leaf along the walls that need it, or if i do anything i have to go with a full room in a room construction? thanks! jaye
gullfo8 October 2007#4
in my opinion, if you don't have much transfer, then you may be able to get away with adding the single leaf or even just treatment walls (like John's inside-out walls). you would then concentrate on windows, doors, etc. to keep sound from getting outside or upstairs (if you need it).
rod gervais8 October 2007#5
uuummmmmmm........... that's actually where it gets pretty hairy......... checking things out with a SLM is one thing - determining where the sound is coming through is another thing altogether......... for example - assuming for a moment that the walls aren't your weak point - then adding 10 layers of mass to them is not going to add anything to your isolation. Determining what levels are making it through is a good start - but if you really want to know how it's making it through - then you should hire an acoustician in your area for a couple of hours to check things out for you...... it's foolish to just make assumptions based on measurements that can never tell you the path the sound is taking. Sincerely, Rod
