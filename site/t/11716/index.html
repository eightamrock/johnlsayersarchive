Soundman202025 February 2009#5
To be honest, I'm not convinced that using layers of different thickness GB helps all that much. I've seen arguments both ways, and I understand that theory behind using different thicknesses to avoid duplicate resonance, and help with refraction paths, etc., but I can't recall ever finding any papers or real laboratory tests that show a noticeable difference in isolation. On the other hand, more mass will always give you better isolation, all other factors being equal, so in your case I think I'd be inclined to go with three layers of 18 mm GB, which gives you more than 30% extra mass, beyond your original plan, and that alone is worth nearly 2 dB of extra isolation (if my calculations are right!) across the spectrum. I very much doubt that varying the thickness of the panels will gain you 2 dB. Now, if you add a layer of Green Glue as well, then you gain much more, especially in the low end. As far as I recall, the independent lab tests on Green Glue were all done using 5/8" GB, with no variation in thickness, so I think I'd go with what they have already tried and tested. Perhaps there would be a difference using GG and different thickness GB, but unless you can find real figures for that, then I think I'd just go with what has been proven to work well. So, to summarize, I think I'd use the thickest GB you can get, plus GG in between. (But that's just my non-expert opinion.) - Stuart -

rod gervais25 February 2009#8
In the case of differing thicknesses of drywall on a wall assembly - don't waste your time. Put on the thickest you can afford - as many times as you can (or 2 layers at least with GG) and understand that the gain in mass more than makes up for the slight dip you get around the critical frequency. When you start talking about a 10 dB dip in iso from TL values of around 80 down to 70 - you realize that these are not your problem areas to begin with. HOWEVER - a doubling of mass will gain you 6dB (that's right - 6) in the area where you need it the most - low frequencies....... Rod
