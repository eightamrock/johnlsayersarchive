Soundman202013 January 2009#2
Hi Bosone, and welcome! You sure are trying to put a lot of stuff in a small place!
i would be happy just to obtain a good listening environment for mixing and listening!
The main issue I see with your room, is that it is not symmetrical. You will never get a good stereo image like that. The bookshelf on the right wall is going to do all kinds of bad things to your acoustics, and if you want a good room to mix in, then it will have to go. You need to make your room as symmetrical as you possibly can, at least from the speakers to the mix position.
the floor is made of wood (palquet)
That's good! Leave it like that. Don't put carpet on it.
the entrance door has glass, but the glass will be replaced by wood.
I would suggest that you replace the door with a "solid-core" door, and put good seals around the edges. It will help to reduce the noise going into the rest of the house. Not much, of course, sine you are not doing any isolation, but it will help some.
my greatest concern is where to place the 2+2 speakers. i thought about placing them along the side walls, at the height of my ears (when i will seat...) because putting it on the table would mean to reduce the working space a lot and, perhaps, to place them very close one to the other.
Good! You do not want them on the table, for several reasons: vibration, reflection, height, etc. Put them on stands. Heavy stands! Very heavy! The center of the speaker should be at the same height as your ears when you are seated, so choose a stand that gets the correct height. You want the speakers and your head to be at the corners of a an equilateral triangle, with an angle of 60 degrees at your head. Actually, the triangle corner at your head should be a few inches BEHIND your head. You want the speakers to be as far apart as possible, between 1 and 2 meters apart. If this geometry does not work for you, then you can also set the speakers to get a 90 degree angle at your head. That usually works better for very small rooms. The speakers should NOT go in corners! Unless you have a very steep bass roll-off control on your speakers, do not put them in corners. That will distort the bass response. I have to do other stuff now, but I'll try to reply to the rest later. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 January 2009#4
i'm thinking also about building a slightly beveled ceiling with some sound damping into it but i have to ask about the price... (sorry but i don't know the correct translation, i hope it is clear!). in this case what would be the a "good" angle for the additional ceiling?
To be honest, I don't think that is worthwhile in your case. You need an angle of about 10 or 12 degrees, at least. The ceiling needs to be lowest over your speakers, sloping up behind you, to be highest at the rear wall. It's a lot of work, and I don't think you would get much benefit in the room you describe.
i have place a library and some bookshelves because i have to place a lot of things, papers and books on them.
Maybe it would be better to get a desk that has a lot of drawers in it! Those shelves are not a good idea.
but i have no idea about the best position of the listening spot into the room (closer to the balcony door? closer to the entrance door?)
Start at about 38% of the length of the room (front wall to back wall). That should be a good place, but it is not a magic number. Start there, and move your head and the speakers around a bit, until you find the best place.
i dont' know if rotating the working position by 90° (either clockwise or anticlockwise) would be a wise solution.... what i know is that it would be a much less confortable and "ergonomic".
Exactly! Turning sideways would not be good. You would have your speakers firing across the room, over a very short distance, and straight at a wall! Regarding treatment, you will need bass trapping, lots of it, in the corners. Very thick fiberglass or mineral wool, floor to ceiling. You also need absorption at your first reflection points. Start with that treatment, then figure out what else need to be done, based on measurements. - Stuart -

hat about trying to get a symmetrical shelves both at the left and at the right? placed at an heigth of about 2 meter?
That would make the room symmetrical, yes, but it would not solve the issues with the weird acoustic things those shelves will be doing to your sound! If you really HAVE to have shelves, I would place them as far back as possible, well behind your ears, and do some kind of thick absorbent treatment on the front surface (facing the speakers) and underneath the shelves, to maybe help mitigate the problems a bit. Soundman20202 June 2009#13
I think I'd go with the biggest possible bass trap, even if it is not at a 45° angle. The angle isn't that important: Even if it is flat against the wall it will still have some effect (as long as it is thick), except that it will lose 6dB of effectiveness. The slight change in angle you show probably won't make much difference at all. Just make it BIG and THICK. You could also do another (additional) bass trap across the top of that door, angled 45° to the ceiling, for extra trapping. - Stuart -
