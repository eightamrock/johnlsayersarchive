Hi Frank, and welcome to the forum! :) Sorry about the looooong delay in responding, but there's a lot of activity on the forum right now, and not many moderators around!
I only rent the room so I prefer construction and dividing the place with objects that I am able to take with me when I need to leave,
It should be possible to build modular furniture, devices, treatment, so you can take it with you. Modular walls to separate the rooms might be a bit harder, especially on a tight budget.
Maybe I overthink too much but the low ceiling really worries me at the moment. it is 2,40m (exactly 8 foot) and puts my nearfields and my ears directly in the middle of the room height.
That is hard to avoid in many rooms. You could consider lowering the effective ceiling a bit, perhaps with an angled, hard-backed cloud. It won't make a huge difference, but should be enough to accomplish something useful. You could also perhaps combine that with raising your chair, desk, console and speakers a few cm higher than normal. The combination of those two should get you away from the worst location. For example, raising the furniture height by 5cm and with an angled hard-backed cloud that is 30 cm down from the hard ceiling over your head, puts your ears at about 60% of the effective room height (or 40% down from the cloud back), which is much, much better.
Is there a big difference if everything else is the same ? Am I able to remove or at least minimize significant the peaks&nulls problem with clouds or similar ?
You can't minimize peaks and nulls with clouds: all you can do is to change their relative location, but that should be enough. The room modes will always be there, as they are a direct consequence of the dimensions of the room. You cannot get rid of them, but you can choose dimensions that spread them around smoothly. A cloud can help to do that. So the issue is not so much the absolute dimensions, but rather the relative ratio of those dimensions to each other. So the best idea would be to look for a good room ratio for your control room that allows your head to not be in the middle on all three axes, and hopefully only one axis: Since the room must be symmetrical (left-right) about your head, you are already on the center line of the room (although your ears are offset a couple of cm each way, of course). That doesn't seem to be too much of a problem for most people, as long as you are not also on the center line in the other two axes. Bass trapping also helps to damp those modes, so with sufficient bass trapping, plus the angled hard-backed cloud, you should be able to get a decent sounding room.

What are your plans for the yellow lines? Are those solid walls, built as stud frames with drywall on them? I would suggest that you turn the room 90° to the left of right, so that your speakers are aimed down the longest axis of the room. They way you show it now, your head does indeed appear to be very close to the 50% depth of the room (front back). I would also suggest that you use SketchUp to do your design, in 3D, to give you a much better idea of how things will fit into the space, and how they will look. - Stuart -
