Brad From my perspective I see no problem using Mineral Wool in walls if that's what anyone chooses. Yes, I've seen a lot of people use Mineral Wool in walls, ceilings, traps etc. I think the thought stated in the linked posts are that because there's very little difference in the performance of Mineral Wool compared to Pink insulation, it's more "cost effective" using the Pink insulation. Mineral Wool will give you slightly better performance at some frequencies over Pink insulation, but it's overall performance isn't too much better. Here's a quote from Steve (Knightfly) in the first link I listed in my last post...
You don't need rockwool for inside ceilings or walls; use standard fiberglass insulation and do a complete fill with slight (about 20%) compression - it'll cost closer to the same amount and do just as well. Save your rockwool for aocustic treatments.
Here's another quote from Steve from the 2nd link in my post above...
Within walls, there doesn't seem to be more than a dB or two difference between rockwool, mineral wool, rigid fiberglass or the "fluffy stuff", with a few cautions - this assumes that the cavities are mostly full, or (in the case of the softer, "fluffy stuff" insulation) slightly overfilled - letting the insulation touch the wallboard surface tends to help damp the panels, which improves wall performance.
It's just a matter of choice... :)

knightfly28 July 2006#12
Brad, in addition to the above you should be careful NOT, repeat NOT, to use HIGH density insulation inside your walls; this will actually LESSEN the sound isolation at lower frequencies. Optimum for music isolation inside a wall is from about 1 PSF to 3 PSF, higher than that and you'll start to pass more low frequencies... Steve

knightfly28 July 2006#14
Before that can be answered we need to specify WHERE the insulation will be used; inside a wall employing mass-air-mass construction, ANY insulation that's LESS than 3 PCF density and can pass AIR thru it will work essentially the same as any other; so the least expensive NON-foam insulation you can find is the best deal. For ACOUSTIC treatments INSIDE THE ROOM, 3 PCF fiberglass, mineral, or slag wool are normally the best; if you want less high frequency absorption (already have enough in the room) then the higher density wools from 5 to maybe 8 PCF may work for you; this is where you should "ballpark" your absorption needs by octave, and PLAN your acoustic treatments; at least a rough idea, which may/will need to be adjusted to taste. Two different uses, two different answers; HTH... Steve

knightfly28 July 2006#16
Absorption within the wall does make some difference, but it's different. I think part of why heavier insulation inside a wall WORSENS the low frequency isolation may be partly due to some "third leaf" effects of the higher density stuff acting more like another leaf of mass, but that's just conjecture on my part. What is NOT just conjecture, is this mammoth project by our Canadian neighbors - http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/irc/fulltext/ir761/ir761.pdf If you check each similar wall construct there (about 350 of them) you'll see that ACTUAL TL at low frequencies is affected by the type and density of insulation, and that very little beats plain old lightweight fiberglass insulation - but, if you just want to spend more money, I'll not stop you :wink: Steve

knightfly28 July 2006#19
If the Roxul you used inside your walls is less than 3 PCF density (not thickness) it's fine. Tests have shown very little difference in any breathable insulations between about 1 to 3 PCF, and any trends tend to show slightly better LF TL with LOWER density - but I would NOT tear out a wall for such a slight change, EVER. Unless you can find a REAL bargain in Roxul or other higher density (more than 1 PCF but less than 3) I would just use the pink stuff inside any future walls... Steve
