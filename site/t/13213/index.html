Mordent123 wrote:
So, how do i attach the cloth the fiberglass strips before glueing them into place?
Other than potential 'sag' on the top piece you can probably do everything with friction. Can you get pieces that are "unfaced" (i.e. no paper or foil backing)? Typically just the friction between the fibers of the fiberglass and the fabric will be enough to hold it in place, but you can also use spray adhesive (tip: put the fabric down on a clean, flat surface, smooth it out, and then put the fiberglass piece down on top of it, and then wrap the fabric up around the sides. Then you should be able to just slot the pieces in place between the window panels with no adhesive (except for maybe the top piece, which could rest on top of the side pieces for support at the ends, but might need a couple 'braces' across the span - maybe a couple short pieces of dowel, small "L" brackets covered with fabric or paint to match, a bead of glue run across the two 'sides' of the top slot before sliding the top piece of fiberglass into place, or similar?

Mordent - I use homosote wrapped in cloth for my window frames. The idea is to not link the two wall frames but cover up the hole between the two. The homosote is light fiberboard so it doesn't transfer vibrations from one wall frame to the other. Al;so - looking at your picture it appears that you have a double pane window in a single wall frame. That poses another problem, humidity and glass windows fogging up. When you have two wall frames the air between the panes is free to mix with the air in the cavities between the inner skin and the outer skin but when you have a single wall frame the air between the window frames is sealed in there and temperature differences between the window panes can cause the air to fog. The solution is to add silica crystals into the cavity that will absorb any moisture that is in the trapped air thus stopping it from fogging up. The method is to cut some holes in the bottom divider before you wrap the cloth around it. Fill the holes with silica crystals and hold them in place with some tape over the hole. When you turn the divider over the crystals won't fall out and they also won't be seen when the divider is put in place. In Australia you can buy the silica crystals from a chemist as they use them when packaging certain drugs into bottles. They are the same crystals use see in pouches when you buy a new camera or piece of electronic gear - its to stop humidity in the packaging from damaging the product.
