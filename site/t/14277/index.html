... with a 5 cm air gap between both panels,
5 cm is not enough. You need at least 10 cm of air gap, and even more if you can afford the space. Especially if you need very good isolation. On the other hand, if you really do build solid wood leaves that are 15cm thick, then 10 cm would probably be more than enough. Maybe you could use the SketchUp program to draw a diagram of what you have in mind for your wall construction. The normal way of building a studio is with a wall that consists of two "leaves". Each leaf is a large flat and massive surface, built with high density materials, such as concrete, brick, drywall (plasterboard, gypsum board), wood (MDF, HDF, plywood) or something like that, separated by an air gap. If a leaf is built with drywall, MDF, or plywood, then that needs to be supported on a stud frame of some kind. The studs can be either wood or metal. The air gap should be entirely filled with mineral wool or fiberglass insulation. The inner-leaf ceiling rests only on the inner leaf walls. the outer leaf roof rests only on the outer leaf walls. There is no contact at all between the inner leaf and outer leaf: they are two separate structures. Building like this is called "MSM" construction (Mass-Spring-Mass). the "spring" is the air in the gap between the leaves, and the "mass" on either side are the two leaves. This is a tuned system, that works something like a notch filter or a parametric equalizer: It is tuned to one specific frequency, and it PASSES sound very, very well at that frequency. It blocks sound increasingly well at frequencies greater than twice the resonant frequency. So it has to be tuned to a frequency well below the lowest frequency that you need to isolate. You tune the wall by changing the mass or the air gap, or both. The resonant frequency goes down as mass increases. The resonant frequency goes down as the air gap increases. In other words, for better isolation you need high mass in both leaves, and a large gap between them. If you do NOT need good isolation, you can reduce the mass, and reduce the air gap. - Stuart -
