dynamike23 April 2007#49
eruss, 1. the space was really just a money saver. that slats cost a TON of money. high grade pine is what i used, it was about $3-5 a piece. it added up quick. i'm hoping the 2 feet of parallel wall doesn't hurt me too much. if it does, i'll just put up some 703 i guess. 2. i believe this is the best way to get broadband (low-mid) absortion but keep the highs alive in an 'orderly' fashion. 3. i did ALL the math to figure out the slat size, depth, gaps, etc. to get the widest range. i alternated 3 different size slats (2", 3", 4") with 4 different size gaps (1/8", 1/4", 3/8", 1/2"). i felt this gave the best assortment of sizing/spacing. it absorbs as low as 122hz and as high as 569hz, assuming my math was right, and my construction was right. 4. i haven't listend yet, i probably should, but most of my gear is hooked up in the studio's old location still, so i'm just waiting till everything is CLEAN enough to bring in the monitors. 5. if i remember correctly, the 6' slot resonators are 4" 703 all the way. from the soffits it's 12" off the wall, and comes down to 4 inches. the 'sawtooth' resonators are 3' lenght and go from 8" depth to 2". they have only 2" of 703. all of them are totally sealed in the back/sides and have air-space behind the 703. cloth is over the insulation, and the slats are touching the cloth/insulation. i researched these a TON before i started building, so i hope i did it the best my room could have.
