Or do you mean that the whole box needs to be air tight except for the air channel?
yes in my case, it needs to be.
Does the top of the sofit (all trimmed out with wood) screw up the bass trapping of your room?
yes, this is one of the sacrifices of a combo room. the angled walls of the soffits and resonators connected help break up flutter echo (when tracking, not mixing) but they don't do any bass trapping. though i haven't tracked yet, i'm not too worried about it being a problem. there is enough thick absorbtion around that my bass should be under control enough.
And most importantly, what is the real *purpose* for sofit mounting your speakers?
the real purpose, in my words. . . is to *focus* the sound coming from the monitors all to one plain (the bezel). and because of the focus, and in correlation with the slot resonators and cloud, when the sound hits my ears it will be completely pure, a reflection free zone. Hey Steve! Thanks for the compliments. First off, how come everyone is so much better than me with Sketchup? Okay, to your question. . . If your monitors are soffit mounted, as it looks from the drawing, you're missing the main point of the angled resonators. you're absolutely going to want them all the way to the soffits. They don't just absorb, they help direct the reflections to the back of the room. If they aren't catching all the 'rays' coming from the monitors, they won't be fully doing their job. If your monitors are going to be on your desk, or on stands, you only the soffits to go as far as your monitors are. And put as much absorbtion on the front wall as possible. Do a search here for "RFZ" short for "Reflection Free Zone" Make sure you get a good handle on what you're really trying to accomplish with this. Hope this helps.
