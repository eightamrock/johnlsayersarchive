lowdbrent wrote:
That,s the question. Just wondering if you have had clientele or people on the board go that route.
Which kind of metal prefab building? This sounds very general. Often prefab elements are designed to have a large spanwidth (high stifness) at a minimum weight with maximum thermal insulation. In fact the design is the reverse of the requirements for good TL The steel sheeting used in Metal buildings varies between 0.63 mm (sometimes even thinner when used in sandwich panels) and 1.2 mm where the 1.2 mm is already very exceptional. If you refer to such prefab that isn't worth much for sound insulation. Other types based on mineral wool elements (depending of brand between 90 and 170 kg/m3 density of the wool with 90° canted mineral wool fibers) aren't that cheap at all anymore, are better than the others but still can't be compared in function of TL with most standard building practices. So I wonder which type of metal prefab is referred to here???? And what you expect from them in function of TL.

Eric_Desart23 July 2004#11
Aaronw, Giles Yep I agree, therefore I said also: depends on what you expect as TL. Giles I don't know the system, just take into account that standard industrial building requirements are different. Lightweight, strenght an thermal insulation as mostly the design goals, not acoustic insulation. For info: I'm more an industrial/technical acoustician than a studio one, so I've been confronted a lot with steel building, factories, power stations etc. The idea of sound insulation is either heavy and stiff (isolating above coincidence), or flexible and light (as drywall = isolating below coincidence). The design goal of efficient industrial buildings is light, large and stiff, certainly with prefab elements. So while it's perfectly possible that it's OK in circumstances as Aaronw describes, take it into account.

dymaxian28 September 2004#16
Hey Eric, I don't think he meant to use the metal building as part of the acoustic system, just as a weatherproof building in which to build the soundproof studio. In answer to the original question, yes this would work as long as you don't count the metal building system as part of your sound isolation. Depending on the metal building manufacturer, you might be able to drywall the inside of the system, and then just build up the individual rooms (structually isolated from each other and the metal skin) and drywall the inside. You may need to drywall both sides of the inner walls if you can't drywall the inside of the metal skin. As far as the rain goes, my friends from Arkansas just put old tires on the roof of their trailers to keep the noise from the rain down. Works like a charm.
