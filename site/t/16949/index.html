That still doesn't change the thickness of each INDIVIDUAL sheet! Yes, for MSM purposes you only take into account the total mass on each leaf, and that's it. But the individual sheets of whatever makes up that "mass" still also behave as individual sheets, as well as behaving as a single chunk of mass. So thin springy flexible light-weight drywall reacts to things that thick rigid heavy drywall doesn't react to. In other words, even though your total mass is the same in both cases, the effect won't be the same. Think of it this way: you could make up the same amount of mass by stacking a few old newspapers, a layer of bubble-plastic, some tin sheeting, some copper foil, a piece of carpet, some cardboard, a thin pane of glass, and a thin sheet of wood veneer. Obviously, that bunch would not react the same way as if you made up the same amount of mass from a single sheet of steel plate, or a layer of concrete. Each material has its own characteristics that affect the sum total, in one way or another. There's the issue of stiffness, for example, and the issue of coincidence dip. So in general, thin, light, flexible things are not so good for isolation. If that were my wall / ceiling, I'd much rather go with two layers of 16 mm drywall than 3 layers of 12 mm, despite the extra mass in the former. - Stuart -
