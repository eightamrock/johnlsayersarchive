- Use RW45 or equivalent rigid acoustic slabs, friction fitted halfway up the floor joists to provide more air gaps.
You don't want MORE air gaps! You want FEWER air gaps! Every air gap inside your floor is a resonant cavity. The more you have, and the smaller they are, the higher your overall resonant frequency will be, thus reducing your low-frequency isolation. What you describe is a multi-leaf system, which is not only very complicated to calculate and predict, but also provides less isolation than a very simple system that has only one air cavity, with a leaf of mass on either side. By all means, add more mass to both leaves, yes! That's a good idea. But do not create additional air spaces inside the floor. One air space, to leaves, is the best isolation, least complexity, and most economical. Here are the equations for two-leaf and three-leaf resonant systems.
