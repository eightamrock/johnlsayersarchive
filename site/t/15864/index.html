gullfo24 April 2011#8
use a 4" thick absorber - 4" or so spaced off the wall if possible - as your reflection point treatment. this will handle the low-mid as well as HF. it's often the mids that become problematic when the HF is tempered and then you still need LF absorption as well so the thicker absorbers help get enough into your space.

gullfo25 April 2011#10
i recommend the hard backed clouds in your room - the hard back adds some wave guide effects which can shift the modal response a bit over the desk more than simply a porous absorber. the slat resonator uses a different principle when operating in front of a sealed air cavity than they do simply as reflector strips (like when widely spaced in front of an absorber). you can use John's slat patterns for creating a broadband effect - 25mm, 50mm, 100mm slats spaced on 5, 10, 15mm slots.
