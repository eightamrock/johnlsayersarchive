studio_drums14 October 2008#2
I recommend: 2" more airspace between the walls. 3 layers of drywall on the inside wall instead of 2. If you're going to this much trouble to pour a new slab, make it an isolated one(island). 24 OC works better than 16 OC and you save in materials. Portable A/C won't cut it- you need to get deeper into the HVAC aspect. You'll need fresh air as well. Not just to meet code- you also want to be comfortable! Have you checked out my build? The sound isolation is remarkable- the studio was a complete success in every way. When the studio is finished, the 2 things that will affect you the most will be your sound isolation and your comfort.

xSpace2 December 2008#12
People have been putting extra water in concrete for decades to make it flow from the chute into the form and still be able to rake or shovel it around as needed. Plasticizers make it so less water has to be used in your mix and the workability is still there. Less water results in a higher strength slab. If you have experienced personal handling your pour, ask their preference. It is recommended that wire mesh be used in a slab on grade. Fiber was introduced as a means to control the thin cracking that can happen with concrete, that was formerly controlled by mesh. I have poured sidewalks and driveways with fiber in the pour and no mesh, but used close spaced control joints. Because cracks are the enemy of isolation, both, wire mesh and fiber, would be a good idea if the cost is in your budget. Especially since you will not be placing control joints in this floor. If not, wire mesh is a requirement, at least @Exit 4. An experienced finisher is a good idea to have on hand.

xSpace3 December 2008#14
ajoantmug wrote:
I am planning on doing the #3 rebar every 1' 6" in the top 3rd of the slab, which will be 6" thick. Do you believe I should still use the mesh as well? Would the fibers be enough with the rebar spaced as such to not worry about mesh or control joints? Thanks for your input
Using rebar is labor intensive and runs the cost up. If I were doing this, mesh and fiber would be it, no rebar >except< in the footers. And that would be 1/2" rebar. Tied joints in the rebar overlaps need to be tight to be of any use. Tight. You can use the 3/8" rebar, but maybe a tighter grid of 6-8 inches would help along with the fiber. But you wouldn't need the mesh and the rebar mat both. The one single biggest thing you can do to make a solid pour is to have a well compacted base to pour on. You are installing gravel of some kind, iir, so make certain it has that well compacted base. This is the area that will give way from erosion and what have you over the years and bring bad results to an otherwise good pour. Me personally, I would abide by whatever is current building procedure in your area as it relates to this concrete.
