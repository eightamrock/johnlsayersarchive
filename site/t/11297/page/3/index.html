So the gypsum has all been hung and caulked and the mudding is beginning today! It feels so close to completion. So much happening all of a sudden after months of slowness. I would like some feedback on a concern of mine. I have specked all along to use hardie board siding for the simple reason of increased mass. As the time to side comes near and money really low I am wondering if there are any other low cost options. I am assuming nothing is going to add the mass benefit of the hardie board, but what cost to isolation might another choice be. Vinyl siding, while it would be worthless as far as isolation goes, would be cheap and easy to install. I've got two layers of 1/2" osb on the outside, a total gap of 10" and two layers of 5/8" drywall with green glue between on the inside. I don't want to shoot myself in the foot after all of this hard work, but I don't want to overextend myself financially any more than I already have.
Soundman202012 June 2009#62
What about the insulation in the air gap? How much did you pt in there? That's an often-overlooked factor that can make a big difference in isolation. Did you just put a couple of inches of fiberglass / rock wool between the studs on one leaf? On both leaves? Or did you fill the cavity? I guess the real question is: how much isolation do you need, and how much will you lose by dropping that extra mass? Reducing mass will also affect your resonant frequency, and since you are going to play drums in there, that might be an issue. You once figured your resonant frequency at 29 Hz, IIRC, which means you start getting some isolation around 40 Hz, and good isolation around 58 Hz. A kick drum is somewhere in the general region of 80 Hz, give or take a dozen or so: so you would probably not want to let your resonant frequency go above 32 to 34 Hz, max. Would dropping the Hardie siding do that, or would you still be OK? This is all just theoretical anyway: Are you at the stage where you can actually measure how much isolation you have already, and see if you need more? Reality is always better than theory for learning the truth!. I guess I didn't answer your question very well, did I? But maybe the comments will help you take a decision. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 June 2009#64
One thing I don't completely understand is that if with all of the exterior sheathing and gb needing to be attached without voids or gaps, why can you have plank siding that has little gaps/voids all over and have that mass still be effective?
I'd say that the mass is still effective since it is directly coupled to the rest of the leaf. The air gaps might do some strange stuff with resonance, but the major point is the siding is directly coupled to the rest of that leaf, since it is in direct contact along its edges, and also directly nailed or screwed in place. - Stuart -
