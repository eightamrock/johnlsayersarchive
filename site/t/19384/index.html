FriFlo6 April 2014#4
Hi Dave. I was referring to the missing location, which is now in your profile! ;-) I think this is the most basic way of showing respect by having read the rules ... Anyway, with 30 - 40 sq meter room separation would be possible of course. The question is, what you wanna achieve. The space you need to have the musicians in a room is one thing, the sound you get from it something entirely different! Don't imagine, you will get your favorite drum sound in. 15sq m room, as no reverb can get you the sound of a good recording room with lively acoustics. The size of your whole studio dictates that it will be a mixing room with a small tracking room. Both of those will have to have a very short reverb time, to get good recordings and proper mixing. If you know that, you can start looking for good dimensions of a room to rent. Things to look for: - Dimensions in order to get two rooms with a good ration of length, width and height - good room height (at least 2.5m, more is better) - isolation/neighbors ... But actually, this is kind of impossible. Only if you know exactly what you want and can show a room and what you want to make of it (exactly), anybody could help you. For this it is important to get into studio acoustics, read one or two books about it and from there your plans will eventually evolve. If you are not willing to spend this time, you won't have a good studio, unless you can pay for someone to design it for you. I don't want to sound harsh, but that is what I had to learn as well. I started off with just wanting to do a little improvement, but that is just not how building a studio works. I could still learn a lot more in acoustics, as it is an endless subject, but now I feel quite comfortable to design, with a few questions asked here.

FriFlo8 April 2014#6
Well, that room is almost a square. The height is ok, but the dimensions are not good. But please! Get yourself a good book about acoustics (Rod Gervais is a good one for a start), learn the basics and come back here, once you have. You have to choices: 1) you wanna be quick and start working. Then you won't get much from this forum, because nobody has the time to make all your decisions - and even if someone did, you won't know what information is relevant to provide. 2) you wanna get somewhere with your studio? Then you have the take the time and a step back first. Learn what it is all about, choose a place and design something to show us, we can help you with. Anything in between is a waste of your time and money. Depending on your ability to learn stuff like that, this will take some time! And don't get me wrong: option 1 is a valid one, because you can easily get lost in building your studio. However, the work you put into learning will also be beneficial, once you realize, you will NOT get, what you were after with the first place.
