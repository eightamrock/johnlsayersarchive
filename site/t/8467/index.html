n8nod9it24 March 2007#4
groff wrote:
Hi, AFAIK it must be clear air gap. If it’s not than you have one leaf with different materials.
Actually i disagree, fiberglass is considered the "air" part of the MAM formula as it can pass air through it. Whether it is touching or has a gap of air between it, it doesn't make a difference to the sound wave. Only if you overc ompress the fiberglass to the point it acts as a "mass" partition, will you reduce your leaf isolation. Code in certain areas dictates that some builds must use a full fill of fiberglass for fire resistance, and that means no open space at all between the inner and outer leaves.

sharward25 March 2007#10
You also need vertical and horizontal draft stops, almost certainly required by code in your jurisdiction, which can entail complete fill of insulation every ten feet vertically and horizontally. For example, on 16" o.c. stud spacing along a long wall, every eighth stud bay would be completely filled with insulation without a gap between the walls. This helps to prevent a fire that breaks out in a wall from engulfing the entire area. --Keith :mrgreen:
drfrankencopter25 March 2007#11
An air gap is unnecessary IMO...and, as Sharward says, there needs to be fire-stops every so often, which mean either a complete fill with insulation or wood cross braces. The reason why an air gap is suggested is that it does not allow for sound to mass from one leaf to another via mechanical conduction (i.e. vibration). But, isulation is not a material that supports mechanical vibration, so it's not a problem....EXCEPT if you stuff it too tightly. Too much insulalation density can support vibration. Cheers, Kris
