sharward17 September 2005#29
Extension cord on a window A/C unit? No, no, no, no, no. It needs its own outlet, and possibly its own circuit run from the service panel. Do not take your chances with this. Your risk of fire due to an overheated extension cord is severe, and it is even worse in an attic. Please don't do this!!! :!: :!: :!: :!: :!: Window air conditioner inside attic sticking out of exterior wall? I don't know. The fact that the unit will breathe (and, if set to fresh air mode, supply) fresh outside air is a big plus. However, the temperature in an attic can reach 130-140 degrees Fahrenheit at times, and I suspect that would be very bad for the unit. Although I'm impressed with The Wise One's drawing and personal experience with the window A/C and custom-built ducting deal, I for one am not comfortable with this plan. While I agree that we sometimes need to be creative to solve our challenges and not to sacrifice the TL that we work so hard to achieve, I think there are better ways to do this. An idea that comes to mind is the chiller space concept that Steve recommended to dietcookie in the popular from shed to studio? thread. But even that doesn't necessarily guarantee the requisite amount of mechanical ventilation in terms of ACH (Air Changes per Hour) that you'll need in order to meet code for a habitable room without a window. You probably need at least 2 ACM (i.e., all of the room's air exchanged every 30 minutes) on demand -- meaning, it doesn't have to run all the time, just when occupants turn it on. Think of it as a mechanical pseudo-window fresh air breeze-making machine. ;-) Sorry to be a damper... I just want to see everyone safe and sound(proofed). --Keith :mrgreen:

Just a note on the attic installation, remember that AC units condense.. so there will be water. If you are using a window unit, they have to tilt so water can run out of them. Not doing this is extremely dangerous. Not only does it mess up the AC unit itself but it could promote mold and disease growth in the units standing water.
