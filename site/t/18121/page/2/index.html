ken_ardency29 September 2013#34
I think it would be better if you check 180° to make sure that RFZ cover mid to low because The monitor radiates omni-directionally at low frequencies. At higher frequencies, the energy radiated becomes increasingly directional. Between mid range to low could be 180°. So I always check 180° just to make sure
Image not preserved: Monitor radiation.png
At design stage, you can save sometime by checking only where angle's changing Example every 5° (This is just quick draw for example. it might be better idea if RFZ cover people standing around engineer as well)
Image not preserved: tracing example.jpg
Acoustic axis 1.2m or 4 feet above the floor. (It's standard height but a bit too low for Sketchup's 3D people model) Dot lines are direct sound, Normal lines are first reflection and lite blue area is Reflection-Free Zone As shown in diagram not all reflections change RFZ. Only where angle's changing will effect the area of Reflection free Zone So just check only angle changes and get same result. Less tracing time More designing time

gullfo1 October 2013#40
my recommendation - decide if your workflow defines the side window and a front window for iso room, or just the side room and large monitor in front (maybe using video between cr and iso room) etc. these things will dictate a lot of the wall features and so on. also, angled walls are not something to fear because a mode calculator cannot solve it easily - there are tools to render room responses based on complex shapes - but there is also the possibility of over-thinking it - the reality is, your room will have stuff in it - some of the stuff will reflect, some absorb, and your ears will change with time, etc. so creating a comfortable work space plus having its overall response be as neutral as possible to make the best possible mix decisions should be a significant part of the goals... the final details on room geometry etc will be subservient to your working preferences and experience...

Soundman20209 October 2013#43
I guess turning sideways I may not be able to do a pure RFZ design as a result of dimensions and/or needing a door from the CR to the LR
When you say "If I turn turn sideways", I guess you mean if you turn the CR to face the LR head-on? If that's the case, then there doesn't have to be a problem with RFZ or with access: You could make the center section of the front wall (between the soffits) into the access door, and use a lot of glass in it for visibility. If you design that carefully, it won't affect RFZ, and would give you the advantages of facing the CR directly. I do agree that turning the room to face the CR is a big plus, and worth doing, and I don't think you'd have problems with that, either for RFZ or for access. - Stuart -
