Hi John – As you know, I’m still no way qualified to help on the air-con front, but looking back through your thread, I did spot something that I hope you’re aware of, don’t misunderstand me mate just making sure. :oops: You mentioned:
That was tricky. I wanted kiln dried sand but that was just way too expensive. So i ended up with sharp sand driedout as best as i culd get it.
No probs John we can all relate to cost my friend. :twisted: However, if it aint kiln dried, it aint dry. Now again – no big deal, but looking at your outer blockwork, you’ll doubtless have a 75mm or 100mm cavity, filled with 50mm or 75mm dry-therm wall bats or similar, air gap, etc whatever, but I get the distinct feeling that you are at some point going to render the outside or something to make it look nice, and of course you’ll want to be cracking on with fitting out the studio etc. :wink: OK – here’s the point, your blockwork will surely feel dry to the touch both sides by now, but trust me dude, the sand inside will be wet through (in building terms) from all the moisture that would have been in it after you dried it! and from the moisture in the mortar seeping out in all directions. Again – totally normal, but I would urge you to be a little patient and maybe prioritise your tasks so that you don’t seal any moisture in. It will cause you no end of problems if you do. :roll: :roll: Could you, for example, live with sealing the inside first, (thompsons waterseal, then aqua-proof) or similar, and leave the outside till the spring at least? Winter is coming, and although we’ve had great weather, you and I both know it’ll be chucking it down soon, but so what, if your building is sealed from the inside, you can crack on, and more importantly your sharp sand and hollow concrete blocks can dry out in there own good time. (Providing of course you don’t yet seal the outside) Naturally, you could do it the other way around, but that would of course delay the progress of the studio! Just a thought. I’m sure you were aware, and if you’re not planning any of the above, apologies for sticking my nose in, just want to make sure. :oops: Cheers John – looking great buddy, :wink: Regards, Lou. 8)

Lou2 November 2007#22
OK John – cool. If that’s the case, this would be my suggested ‘modus operandi’ :wink: Is there any way you can ‘waterseal’ the entire outside today? Since you have concrete blocks and not hard faced bricks, the product will literally vanish before your eyes, so get plenty more than you need. Once you’ve done that you’ll still be able to render at the weekend, but more importantly, any moisture in the block work and sand will then only be able to go one way – inwards. (Moisture in the render can then of course only come outwards). To help this, I’d recommend you call TP this afternoon, and organise an industrial space heater for the week. Don’t forget to off-hire it at 11.55am next Saturday! That way, they won’t have time to pick it up till the following Monday morning. :wink: :wink: Once your render is on, or as soon as that heater arrives, prop open that door, open both of those windows as far as possible, and set that heater to provide a nice comfortable warmth to the room when you walk in at night, ie not baking. Leave the building in this state for the duration of the hire, ie don’t close the door or windows, and don’t turn off the heater. This will ‘draw out’ a ton of moisture that can then simply evaporate. Any that passes up through your ‘1 way breathable’ (I hope) felt, can’t come back down into the room once it’s left it, and the rest can fly out of the door and windows! You’ll be able to watch your walls change in appearance almost every half a day! :roll: Hope this helps mate, Regards, Lou. 8) (PS – TP’s waterseal equivalent is fine, and a lot cheaper!)
