SUNSET STUDIO Hello to the Forum and Members: Background: Aim to build a very isolated studio inside some outbuildings [Internal dimensions: 8500mm long by 3800mm wide by 2600mm high rising to 4500mm at the ridge] PROFILE; I'm trying to edit it right now but the location is in Cumbria, UK. Pro musician, studio number 4. REQUIREMENTS: To be able to play/practise drums/bass at full volume [approx 110+ dB] at anytime of day or night. The internal sound will be profiled by Bass traps and tuned false walls. The main requirement is the sound leakage outside the studio. It must be virtually zero. This studio is more for playing than recording so acoustic properties are secondary to soundproofing, although a control room with recording gear will also be built. http://www.demon-guitarist.com PRELIMINARY DESIGN: [i] Strip out supporting wall in outbuilding and support roof with steels. This leaves a large space with dimensions above. [ii] Dig for England so the floor level becomes around 300/400mm lower, down to the top of the drains basically. [iii] Strip Foundation laid around the perimeter of the space approx. 50mm from the foot of the surrounding structure. NOTE: the outbuilding is for weatherproofing only-I do not intend to include the outbuildings as any part of the soundproofing calculations. [iv] Lay Polystyrene/neoprene isolation strips underneath the poured foundation strip. [v] Build a dense block 100mm wide wall with a 100mm gap and another 100mm block wall. Fill the gap with dry sharp sand. The gap will be lined with Visqueen [In the UK its the name given for plastic which is used as a damp-proofing membrane] [vi] No windows and only one door opening. Loads more details to follow. My intention of starting this thread is to allow others to view progress and maybe some of the better informed members to chip in and make suggestions. If possible I'll post photographs but will also keep a daily 'blog' website going if I can't figure out how to post photos in this forum. Thanks for even reading this bit! JB

Jan Beck18 September 2009#6
Hello X, I've built 3 studios over the last 15 years. [i] The first was a Room-in-a Room in a residential home: The walls were Stud partitions separated by dense Rockwool that was freely suspended. It soundproofed a full drumkit so that neighbours 30ft away couldn't hear a thing. There were initial problems with some 'noggins' being attached through the studs into the host building masonry. [ii] Second build was never finished - it was in an isolated, rural barn - the main walls were Block-Air-Block [Mass-Spring-Mass]. It performed terribly as the ceiling was never finished off. The acoustics were acceptable but the soundproofing was awful - not really worth the effort. [iii] Third Build: A much bigger build inside a large industrial unit. Four large rooms, reception etc. The individual rooms were Masonry-Sand-Masonry with a sand ceiling. Excellent isolation. The acoustics were fine for rehearsals and practicing. It was used a lot by drummers to practise but there were a few recording sessions and a lot of song-writing sessions. JB

Jan Beck23 September 2009#10
Zero Progress on the site. Design started on CAD program. Started costing of Broadband treatment and Bass Traps [Helmoltz Resonators and RockWool Hangers] On Acoustic Hangers or Freely suspended Rockwool slabs: My understanding of acoustic Hangers is that they destroy sound waves by using the sound pressure waves to create movement in a mass. If that mass is attached at the top and bottom, the movement is more akin to vibration [resonating]. If the mass is attached only at the top then the mass is free to move further and absorb more sound energy. The eigenvalue of resonance and the sound wave will, theoretically, build up - similar to building up waves of water in the bath - and sum, reducing the bass absorption. By placing eccentric 'stoppers' [in the London studio these are actually variable in their position] the mass is knocked off kilter when the swing/movement is too great and the profile of the standing wave/resonance/eigenvalue is temporarily destroyed. This kind of bass absorption can be constructed with 200kg/m3 density Rockwool [or RockSilk, equivalent to Rockwool] Bass traps can be constructed that are 150mm deep. I'll be running some test units up before committing to building the full sized ones. If anyone reading this fully understands how hangers work, please post here or add a link to the thread/resource. Thanks. JB COSTS TO DATE: £21.00 - Rod Gervais Book "Home Recording Studio: Build it like the pros"

The following post is copied from another thread. I've put it here to keep the info at hand. JB Subject: Sheetblok alternative?
Eric_Desart wrote:
Jan Beck wrote:
Eric:
The springiness of neoprene (elastic) is no advantage over the dead weight (viscous) of roofing, for this kind of application. Elastic: stores energy and gives it back Viscous: absorbs energy
Where would you rate dry sand as either Elastic or Viscous?
Viscous. Sand (fine and dry, it must flow) works as a damper. Not necessarily as efficient as other synthetic materials but it damps. Shearing forces come into play (are needed) which aren't that high for sand. But it has it advantage of its high cheap mass of course. Sand is easy to use on strong horizontal surfaces, but wall filling (I mean normal cavities) is an unpractical idea since the sideways forces of sand filling in function of time become extreme (you blow up your wall = get shape of a drop = a lot of reinforcement or strength must be provided to withstand these forces). That's different of course if you use it to fill hollow concrete blocks. And sand is no magic. It's (in most cases) still better and more logic just to use traditional double leaf systems with absorption (whether lightweight or heavy walls). There are cases where I used sand with success because it was either cheap good mass (+ additional damping) or in special industrial cases (damping the steel (3 to 6mm) of HUGE silencers by welding an additional steel casing around them filled with sand). At FN http://www.fnherstal.com/ I once improved shooting ranges by spreading a thick layer of sand on the concrete roofs (was the easiest and cheapest manner in that situation). Brian Ravnaas once tested/measured the effect (vibration levels) of filling speaker stands with sand (and with leadshot and a mix of both I believe, not sure of the latter) with good success. The difference between viscous and elastic is easy to check. Drop a piece of elastic material on the ground (e.g. a dried ball of silicone) and it jumps up again as a ball (silicone deforms, stores the energy and gives it back). It restores to its original shape. Drop a lump of children's modeling clay or plumbers caulk on the ground, it deforms and falls dead. It does NOT restore to it's original shape (deforms, but absorbs/dissipates this energy).
