knightfly14 March 2006#23
If you've already built your frames, I'd start with that size; but if your basement has concrete walls (most do, unless it's really old and made from separate rocks) then you're going to need quite a bit of absorption to make the room usable. I normally recommend frames that are more like a meter wide; I know it's kind of a waste of insulation material, and if there's room you can even do corner traps that are 4 feet wide; but your 2 foot wide ones will be a good start. There's really no major thought required for first steps in a room, even yours with its L-shape - you just put absorbent diagonally across ALL the vertical corners, find a way to hang more of it a few inches off the ceiling, and maybe a couple of them on any bare walls - it's the total amount of surface area that's important, but I'd also (if you have enough) put TWO layers of your 60 kG stuff in each corner. If you can't, one layer is much better than nothing. In a pinch, you can just lean the stuff up in your corners til you get time to build frames - it doesn't need to be airtight or anything, just THERE. If your room is STILL in need of more treatment after all the vertical corners, partial walls, and ceiling, we may need to figure out other things to add; but these first steps are necessary for EVERY room that's less than 2 or three times the size of yours. HTH... Steve
