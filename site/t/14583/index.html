Closed cell foam is ineffective for acoustical purposes. That said, I suspect the reason the contractors are pushing this is because the structure may have a tendency to sweat and/or produce high levels of moisture which would ruin the open cell insulation and render it useless due to saturation. But it may only be simply because it is easier to use...who knows @ this point. To answer your question, yes.

xSpace16 July 2010#5
Very good observation Stuart. To add, there was not enough information for anyone to really answer this, except with more questions but as is often the case, this project has already moved along and we are getting the "hindsight" portion of it.. For example: "This room has one exterior wall, plus two walls which are concrete block..." This line suggests that there are walls of different materials, two of concrete and one of an unknown composition on the exterior. And this line: "...with approx 1.5 inches of closed cell insulation is best (We already did it to the ceiling a while back). " Closed cell foam performs like a vapor barrier. To install a vapor barrier in the ceiling requires some thought about the existing building, the roof / ceiling design, HVAC, and where geographically a person resides for the simple reason that moving from the higher pressure to the lower pressure, usually from the warm side to the cooler side, a vapor barrier in the ceiling will keep the vapor and potential condensation on the inside of the structure. Other consideration for the concrete block are just as pertinent, has it been bonded/sealed? To simply install a spray foam directly over it is inviting problems.
