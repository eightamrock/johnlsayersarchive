rod gervais1 November 2009#28
Osse, I don't know about Sweeden, but here in the state a glazier will typically use a glazing tape for the installation of a single piece of large glass in a wooden framed opening. This is actually a butyl tape not a foam. This gievs an excellent seal between the wood stop and the glass. I always recommend that people then caulk between the glass and the frame a secondary seal. Rod

rod gervais2 November 2009#31
The 1st (top most) picture. install the inner stops on one side - then the butyl tape against the stops - then setting blocks for the bottom of the window (to hold the glass off the frame with an even gap all the way around the window) then a couple of temp stops on the top sides of the frame - caulk the top over the window - install the top butyl tape against the glass - install the uppermost window stop - then finish all of the remaining 3 stops using the same proceedure..... Repeat for the glass on the opposite side of the window frame.......... Rod

Soundman20209 February 2010#34
First, I'd suggest that you read the "stickies" (the fixed posts on the first page of this forum). Then, you really should start your own thread, rather than post questions about your build in someone else's thread. Having said that, a couple of comments:
Two 12' x 12' rooms,
Ouch! Your rooms are square! That is pretty bad, acoustically. You will probably want to do something about that. If you have 8 foot ceilings, then your problems are even more complex...
divided by a double wall ( as seen on the site)
There are an awful lot of things that could be called "double walls" in this site! :shock: Which particular one did you mean? What construction method are you talking about? Photos or a SketchUp model to explain it would help.
I don't have the money to get the proper glass, especially not that big.
Then make the hole smaller! :) Decrease the size until it gets small enough that you can afford it...
I have accesability to acrylic and was wondering if it's stc rating was any good.
Depends on what you mean by "acrylic". It has some acoustic uses, but needs to be very thick. And it probably won't work very well for what you have in mind. It would have to be VERY thick to match the mass of the glass you should put in there. But you didn't say what it is that you are trying to accomplish, so it might work. But probably not.
I couldn't find an stc for acrylic anywhere and read different reviews on the sound transmission of it.
I guess you are aware that STC isn't a good rating for studios? It does not tell you anything useful about the part of the spectrum that is of the most interest. - Stuart -
