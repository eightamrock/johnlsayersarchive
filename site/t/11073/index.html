mkropfbe7 August 2008#1
Hi guys, I’m going to refurbish my control room and live room and I want to do it right this time :( So this time, I would like to ask the right people for their good input! :) First of all, to meet the codex, here are some details about me: I've read quite a lot in this forum, went thru a couple of books on this topic, so I hope, I did get a good impression, what's good and what's not... let's see... Location: Europe/Austria/Carinthia, where I constructed my house three years ago Music to be recorded/mixed: from local folk to jazz to classics, once in a while rock/pop Noise level: I live in the countryside, so insulation is not an issue Money to spend: about 3,000 EURO Please find a sketchup from the current rooms, pretty perfect in scale, only the joists are pretty random, since I don’t know the exact position because of gypsum hiding them :) Room Basics: Two rooms with about 11 m2 (CR) and 21 m2 (live) usable floor size and a height of 2,34 meters. All floors are finished with hard ceramic tiles. There are some specialities: yes, the brick wall is not straightly in an 90 degree angle and I can’t change its position, because it’s part of the floor foundation (not ceiling!). Still, I’m planning to cut it down to half height to get rid of the small window and the door, and then I want to build it half up again with a new, bigger and centered window with 8mm thickness, using the same-sized bricks. From the old (too dry acoustics) days, there are some panels of acoustic foam (BASOTECT) available and to be used wherever they fit: 6 pcs of 45cm x 45cm x 10cm pyramid gray 16 pcs of 45cm x 45cm x 7cm pyramid gray 14 pcs of 100cm x 50cm x 10cm flat white The other walls are specially built huge concrete blocks with concrete joists with gypsum on top. Every joist has a 2cm pad of wood, so it’s easy to apply gypsum or wooden panels. Note, that the joists have holes in the vertical, so it’s easy to get cabeling thru (so --> about 20cm deep and mostly 55cm wide cavities, but not air tight!) I thought of ripping off the gypsum to get room volume for free…. Just covering the cavities with fabric will obviously damp too much, so I thought of mainly use slats for reflection of higher frequencies. Knowing that no air seal will render the Helmholtz-concept fairly useless, I wondered if it would still be an acceptable way to go?!? Please have a look at the current plan of how the new studio could look like. Control Room: The big window and the entrance door is fixed and will have to stay as is. There is a hole in the east (left) wall (shown in pink), which is used for cabeling of the computer, which is silently positioned in the attached “hardware room”. I have KRK VXT6 nearfields, which are not edegly but smoothly shaped, so I assume, soffiting will be complicated. So I’d put them on the table or on stands behind the table. An extra double wall with wooden framing, double gypsum and insulation, but with a 5cm air gap to the brick wall, should be set up. The thick glass on the CR side could be obtained for nearly no money (15 EUR!)… it’s laminated glass with 2x10mm, so it’s 20mm thick and sized 100x100cm. Well, obviously there is a downside: there are four holes with a 2cm diameter punched along one edge, but I would just try to seal them and build the framing a little more overlapping, so the window would be only 90x100cm then. Bass Super-traps in the corners and a slanted wall left and right. Note, that on the west (right) wall, the slanted wall can only be built up to a height of 95 cm, since above that, the window is mounted :( Also below the CR window, I’d mount some broadband absorber. I could reuse my acoustic foam panels on the ceiling to have an acoustic cloud over the mixing position. Live Room: The big window, the entrance door and the terrace door is fixed and will have to stay as is. Again, I’d place corner traps and absorber pikes to break flutter echoes. Further, I’d glue the acoustic foam panels to the ceiling. Possible Problems: 1. I haven’t placed tuned low/mid absorbers anywhere (real Helmholtz or eg. Swinging plates). 2. In the CR, the big window will break symmetry. 3. My mixing position is more of 50% then 38% from the front wall. 4. Building slat absorbers is easy, but the cavities are not air tight. 5. The dirt-cheap 20mm window has holes punched on one edge. 6. The double wall between CR and live room are not parallel. […] Please help me and point out, how critical the above topics really are?!? What would you change, also in the layout of the absorber or mixing positions? thanks! Mike
