Gash wrote:
So, my questions are: 1. Should I kill both rooms or make an attempt at keeping one or both alive?
Make them dead. Rooms that small have such strong coloration that there is no value to their live sound.
2. I intend to build rooms within the existing rooms. Should I make an effort to give the inner room walls unparallel features in both rooms or just the control room?
There is no intrinsic value to angling walls or ceilings. If I was you I would not. For a more in depth discussion about splaying surfaces read this thread. Andre

Your welcome. There are few absolutes in design, and the elements interact. When a poster asks for help and is not clear on what they want or giving requested information, it is very frustrating. You mentioned in another thread that you are new to this studio design thing. I hope you noticed how a seemingly simple question's response touched upon room modes, early reflection control, room size and height, budget considerations, studio designs around the world, and Christmas! After all that, the OP understood the best answer is "it depends." Doing acoustics properly is world of multiple factors and decisions. Very few things can be answered simply without knowing several other details. You know this already, but for other readers, good studio building is 90% design and 10% construction. Andre
