BradJacob16 February 2008#3
I used old-recycled (from a salvage yard) sliding glass doors and mine work real well. When a drummer whacks the hell out of the drums, I can hold a VERY normal conversation only 8 feet away. My rooms are a bit on the small side: live room - 10x12, and my control room is: 10x11 The only reason I can hear anything really at all is because the stripping where the fixed door and the slider meet (when closed) is worn and there's a noticeable gap of about 1/32 or 1/16. But remember, there is the door (which is double insulated), and 3.5 inch air gap and then another door. I couldn't be more happy - given the size of my rooms. To be able to talk softly in the control room while a drummer plays (in such close proximity) is more than I expected. Plus, I can hear what the mic hears - and not any of the noise. I tried to buy new ones from Lowes - the standard, no frills white sliding doors for $300 each - BUT, there was no way to get them into my basement using the inside doorway, or the billco-doors. One method suggested on this forum was to actually cut the frame (across horizontally - half way up on each side). Then I could have brought them downstairs and installed it into the framing. This would have worked 100% perfect, but the post came too late and I was too far into it with my old doors. So, in closing, the regular no-frills sliding patio doors from Lowes ($300) will work fantastic because the stripping is really good. Even old junky ones like mine are doing the job better than I thought they would. Check my sliding-glass-door post: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... highlight= And my updated (and current) post: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... highlight= Hope this helps. Please let us know how you make out.
