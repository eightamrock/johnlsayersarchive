Hi I live in Ecuador and have the opportunity to build a studio in what has been until now a poorly constructed storage space. The structure is built from cinder blocks, using mud as grout. The roof is a tin roof and is sort of resting on the walls and isn´t actually connected. I am going to have to tear the whole thing down and build it back up. I would like to use the same cinder blocks and then cover them with cement (that is the most common building method here). The current structure is 26.5´ by 11.5´, but my wife has given me permisson to expand the second wall to 18´. As I have to rebuild the structure from scratch maybe that puts me in a position to be able to build in such a way that the structure of the building itself lends itself towards recording. Maybe tear down the front wall and make it sort of octagonal or circular? my restricions are the property lines, as it is sitting in the corner of the property. Bad things: I live in a rural area where the farmers have big parties almost every weekend and pay a dj to bring a pa system and the music often goes into the next day, sometimes through the whole weekend. during the week there is very little noise, but i would like to be able to record on weekends as well. Most of the neighbors who play the music are on hills that are above me and sometimes the music is so loud that it sounds like the pa is in my house. I want to record various arrangements with mostly acoustic instruments: lots of handdrums and misc. percussion, standup bass, marimba, guitars, brass, wind, strings, and voice. I dont know how to best divide the space. I imagine one big room would be good for recording live, but that would comprimise the voice and the drums? (i assume this to be the case becuase in most of the plans i have seen these are recorded in seperate booths). any ideas will be much appreciated. thank you
