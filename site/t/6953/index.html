len-morgan31 August 2006#5
When you said "cardboard" did you mean MDF? What is the gap between the two sides of the wall? If I remember correctly, I don't think you're going to get any better isolation with two panes if the wall is not separate. You'd be better off going with one thicker sheet of glass. You might also consider laminated glass since you do get better performance than you would with the same thickness of plate (float) glass. len
rod gervais31 August 2006#6
len-morgan wrote:
What is the gap between the two sides of the wall? If I remember correctly, I don't think you're going to get any better isolation with two panes if the wall is not separate.
Len, You are apparently remembering incorrectly. :wink: The value of the doublepanes of glass is exactly the same as is the layers of drywall on each face - thus (with the light guage metal framing which acts the same as wood with resilient channel from the perspective of decoupling) you have a MAM system - and want to maintain this through the window opening.
You might also consider laminated glass since you do get better performance than you would with the same thickness of plate (float) glass.
In this you are correct. To the original poster - you were given excellent advice by Eric at Studio Tips- follow it. Sincerely, Rod
