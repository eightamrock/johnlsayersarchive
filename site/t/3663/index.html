the dreamer3 May 2005#2
As I see it choose the height of the speaker first and then build the desk in a way, you don't get early reflections to your ears. You can easily check that with the mirror trick (if you don't know do a search). It's all over. Put them on stands (or soffit) behind the desk, it's easier to controll ER then. The bridge or 2 computerscreens in the proper hight can do a lot here. Certainly it depends on how you want to build the new desk. If you put your speakers at earlevel you're better of cause you have a flat angle in comparison of puting them higher. Refering to your other thread: If you put the speakers higher than earlevel I would angle them down. ---------- Florian

the dreamer3 May 2005#4
The mirror trick works whenever you want to raytrace the path of the sound after it hit a surface. No matter if it's a wall or a desk. What you should think about in context of speaker position is the modal behaviour at the listening position. This means that a soundsource excites certain roommodes, which may be bad at the mix spot. If you move the speaker the modal behavour may change for the better at the mix position. I'm not experienced in this, I will have to check it out myself in my new room too. Knightfly as the man to ask. I remember a thread, where he explained how to calculate this on paper with some excel spreadsheet to calculate the modes. You gotta search.
Any opinions on hollow core material for the computer monitor stand? THink it will resonate?
If you want to decouple the whole thing you have to make it heavy (sand, concrete, marble, gold :wink: ). On top of the mass sits the speaker and below the mass some kind of rubber/elastomer.
