Noumenon, I might be ale to give some help here. Its been twenty years since I practiced with a band but what we ended up doing was building a raised stage that the cabinets could fit under. Put the PA speakers up on that platform behind the drums and mic'd everything. Kinda like you would on stage. It saved a lot of floor space this way and it helped control the volume. If you have a mixed sound it is more likely the guitar player wont complain about the drum. and so on. Maybe :lol: I've been to many practice rooms since that time and most of them are pointing their instruments at one another much like yours and it sounds like a big mess meeting in the middle. jwl15 February 2007#4
Hi Noumenon (what a Kantian name.... LOL) In general, I gravitate toward putting instruments with a lot of low end (ie, bass and drums) in the corners. That way, it takes advantage of the acoustics of the room to make things heard. Then the vocal PA and the guitar amps can be placed accordingly. Most guitarists (I certainly include myself in this...LOL) should have their amps facing them so that they can hear themselves play well. Plus you won't drown out everyone else in the room. I'm also a big fan of having the musicians stand several feet away from their amps, with the amps facing them. In a room this size you should be able to get good placement.

gullfo3 March 2007#6
another option - put the drums on one wall, the amps on another and have people face each other with the PA mics 90 degrees to the kit and the amps. put the monitors up high enough and aimed to the side of the mics so people can hear themselves. what this does is enable the drummer to hear everything - people can get to their amps to adjust, and the mics don't pick up quite as much from either amps or kit. ideally if you can tip the amps a bit, the guitars and bassist will hear it more directly (and tend to reduce the volume a bit) and the drummer won't have to compete as hard... get some bass trapping in the corners to tighten things up. some absorption to keep the highs under control. FWIW, depending on your options, electronic drums help immensely for rehearsals. I just spent the last few weekends up in CT and having my electronic kit enabled us to rehearse and jam until 3am... the guitar and bass amps volume were pretty low (and with effects had the necessary sounds) and we could talk to each other and sing without mics. even had an acoustic guitar in the room on a large condenser coming through the PA nice and clean... good mix, no fried ear drums, no police... from outside it sounded like a moderately loud stereo... even the 250bpm parts... :shock:
