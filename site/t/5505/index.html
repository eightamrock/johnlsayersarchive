Foam or any acoustic treatment will not isolate the room. It may get rid of some nasty standing waves, make the room dead and so forth. Isolaltion requires a room inside a room build with use of exterior doors and a lot of other stuff, which would also make the isolated room 8 to 12 inches smaller then it already is. You should go here 1st: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3231 Follow it to the end, then come back with actual dimensions of your room. Also read: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html I'm a complete learning novice at this point but I hope this helps you out. Marc

In addition to contributing to a cluttered, non-professional look and impression, it's possible that these items may introduce mechanical noises (rattles, jiggling) depending on how loud the exterior bass levels are that penetrate the closet walls. They will not, however, contribute to any positive absorbtion or sonic qualities. May I sugggest that you remove them and store them in another location.

Or in a tiled pattern along the inside of the wall? I am unsure if there should be foam in front of the vocalist or behind him? Should we record facing a corner?
This thread addresses some of the foam/vocal in the corner issues: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic ... ocal+booth While recently lining my speaker soffits with 3" rockwool (2.5 pcf), I noticed how dead the space inside the soffit became, giving me the idea of lining my future vocal booth with rockwool, then covering with some visually appealing acoustical cloth (as in the Guilford of Maine product line) and being sure to provide adequate ventilation. Using a similar approach in your situation, you may achieve that "dead" sound you're looking for, but without the proper mass-air-mass construction, you may find that your low levels of isolation may require you to take this into consideration when monitoring at loud volumes, especially in the bass frequencies. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
