knightfly12 February 2006#4
Your average dim's for the room are about 3.7 M wide, 4 M long, 3.1 M high; these are averages since splayed walls are involved. Using those averages the Bonello distribution of modes is quite favorable, with no potential problems indicated. This is NOT to say you won't still need treatments in the room, only that they won't be in vain. On your outer walls, you do NOT want another leaf of mass besides the outermost one and the innermost one; air space and insulation between these will make up your mass-air-mass barrier for best isolation. If your outer shell isn't already heavy mass, you may want to increase that before building the inner shells; check out Sharward's thread in Construction for more, probably around page 5 to 10. Also, you only want ONE vapor barrier in a building and in your climate it goes to the inside; so you will want to replace that foil-faced insulation with UN-faced, and put the foil faced stuff with the foil against your first layer of INNER wallboard. The door problem I referred to is just that your existing door doesn't seem to be in the right location for the plan you've adopted... Steve

John Sayers16 March 2006#17
OK - there are two factors here. The first is the directivity of speakers. The midrange and tweeter speakers in a standard system are directional. In other words they don't radiate in 360 degrees (unlike the woofers) The main power output of these speakers is generally recognised as being around 30 degrees, after that they roll of. look at the angles emanating from the speakers in this plan and you can see what I mean by the 30 degree radiation angles.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/images/Basic%20Plan%202.gif
The second factor is the rear reflections that are going to come off the rear slot resonators in your cr reflexion.gif. If these reflections are under 20 msec from the original the two are going to mix together and confuse the ear. If they are greater than 20msec the ear will separate them and treat them as two individual sources, no confusion. I'm not sure of your exact measurements but it looks to me like these rear reflections will be under 20msec thus causing imaging confusion. BTW sound travels at approx 1ft/msec. cheers john
