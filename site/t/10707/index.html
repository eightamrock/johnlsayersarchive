when you look at the graph, is this response something that can be adressed with a sub or a bigger speaker or is this something more trapping should solve?
How low (-3 dB frequency) are your speakers rated to? I see less output below 60 Hz, and IMO speakers need to get down to at least 50 Hz or lower.
how do you read these graph properly anyway, i find very little useful info on that online..
That graph is not so useful, but you have the data so maybe you can display it in different ways? I prefer 1/3 octave smoothing for 300 Hz and above, and higher resolution than 1/12th octave below 300 Hz. You also need to display a waterfall plot to see the modal ringing. What program do you use? Regardless, this will help: Using ETF --Ethan

jwl12 June 2008#12
A few thoughts to add.... Yes, bass traps will definitely help you. Room EQ Wizard is another good program to try.... and it's free. It's possible that in your previous, smaller room you got used to hearing some artificial peaks introduced by the room; in the larger room these peaks aren't as noticeable, so the bass sounds "weaker" to you. Apart from being "weaker," how is the accuracy? Are your mixes translating well? Once you get some high-res graphs posted, we'll be able to help more. My suspicion is that once you have bass traps in the room, you may not need a sub, depending of course on your preference and what style(s) of music you work with.
