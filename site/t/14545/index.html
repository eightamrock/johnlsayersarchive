Soundman20209 July 2010#4
You're saying that even tho the airspace between the 2nd and 3rd leaf is so small (1 1/2") compared to the space between the 1st and 2nd...
To my mind, it isn't a matter of "comparing" the air spaces, so much as the fact that the thin one exists at all. You have a reasonably massive leaf over a thin airspace: that implies a rather high resonant frequency for that leaf. However, I don't even see where you get that 1 1/2" figure from. Your diagram shows the "stucco lath and felt" over "structural ply" attached to a 2x6 frame, but doesn't show the "1 1/2" gap. Where is it exactly? Also, you say that the studs are 2x4, but the plans show them as 2x6. Which is it? Also, the "mess of materials on the inner leaf" that Andre refers to sure is a mess, and the OSB nailing surface isn't the problem. You show it as "5/8" Drywall | Green Glue | 5/8" Drywall | 1/2" OSB | Soundboard | 2x4 studs", but the plans show it as: "5/8 drywall - 1/2" sound board - 1/2" OSB - 5/8 drywall - 1/2" OSB". No mention of GG. I'd say forget the sound board: it isn't doing much for you. Forget the extra layer of OSB: you only need one nailing surface, and it should be right up against the studs, not in between two layers of drywall. I'd go with something like: "5/8 - GG - 5/8 - OSB - studs". You might also need more mass on your outer leaf. My 0.02 - Stuart -

Apparently there are 2 very different conditions - the lower pic indicates the details he is referring to - which is an easy fix.... it can stay just the way it is and install 1 1/2" dow board insulation prior to the exterior wall finish - don't gain mass - but it does solve the 3rd leaf issue.. To the OP - this is a total cluster-f**k......... where in the world is your architect getting his design info from? There are a slew of things screwy with all of this..... starting with the base of the iner wall. If he is making you go through all of the expense to create an isolated slab assembly - then why in the world would you need to try to isolate the wall from that assembly? Either it works (in which case the wall can couple directly to it) or it doesn't - in which case floating the wall and creating structural stability via the clips can offer some assistance......... but this would also mean that you were throwing your money away on the floor assembly...... The wall assembly itself is (as mentioned above by both Sound and Andre) a total waste of money and energy and will do little to help your isolation......... To begin with - I would need to understand this a little better - like why is the architect building exterior walls using 2x4 in some locations and 2x6 in others (or is this an addition to an existing older home?) Inside I would increase the 1/2" space between walls to 1"......... I would add mass (as explained above as well) to the outside wall leaf - and I would be using (again as mentioned above) multiple layers of 5/8" gyp DW with GG over the OSB....... I would also point out that if you were using OSB on all surfaces inside the inner room there would be no need for the iso-clips because the room would be structurally sound in and of itself - which is much better isolation that you can achieve with the clips alone..... But the is a lot of speculation here because I cannot "get" the entire picture....... I would need to see much MUCH more of the plans before I could completely work through this - there may well be other even larger problems that exist that you are not aware of - personally I am not impressed with your architect's ability to design isolating systems - probably be even less impressed with his studio design........ but on that I might be wrong - never know unless I see it......... Rod
