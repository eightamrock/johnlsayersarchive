Soundman202028 July 2011#9
do you think a layer of mass loaded vinyl would help with better isolation?
It will help ... to take money out of your pocket! Mass is mass, and the sound waves don't care how much you paid for it: they only react to the physical amount of mass they encounter, regardless of the price tag. So choose the cheapest mass you can, such as plain old drywall. Much more cost effective.
drywall green glue drywall sandwich
That's the best way to do it, yes.
or drywall mlv drywall?
MLV is not designed to act as constrained layer damping, so it cannot substitute for Green Glue. Adding an extra layer of drywall is much cheaper (and more effective) than adding a layer of MLV of the same mass. Besides, who knows how MLV will behave between two layers of drywall? Do you have any research studies showing what the effects will be? In other words, MLV is mostly hype, and has few real uses in studio construction. Yes, it has SOME uses, but not as a cost effective substitute for drywall, and certainly not as a substitute for Green Glue. - Stuart -

Just a point about the two chairs in the back corners - this is the absolute worst position for them to be, the stereo imaging is nonexistant, being in the corner the bass will be overly emphasized - if you will have clients listening here, they will not be happy. BriHar14 August 2011#18
What I did for Pipes and duct is: -I drew a line along what would be the center of the pipe using the ARC tool to get your bends. -Next draw a circle the diameter of the pipe at one end of the line with the line being the centre point and the circumference perpendicular to the line. -Finally use the "Follow Me" tool to guide the face of the circle along the length of the line. You just have to take care with your bends - especially in relationship to the diameter.

Soundman202024 October 2011#22
i am not sure if the air gap between the control room and outside wall will be too much,
Air gaps in the wall is like money: you can never have too much! :) Seriously, it's not a problem. The basic effect that increasing your air gap has, is to lower the MSM resonant frequency, which is a good thing. What you may have read is that there's a point at which, in a three-leaf wall, the gap becomes large enough that the third leaf doesn't matter any more. However, you do seem to have a basic design problem: Your staircase is in your air gap! :shock: That needs to be fixed. - Stuart -

Soundman202031 October 2011#24
How is the stair case a problem?
It's a problem because it is inside your air gap, which is supposed to be absolutely sealed, air-tight.
I am thinking that because it is an enclosed stair case, and the enclosing wall will reach from floor to ceiling, it should be treated like any other wall
That's not what it looks like on the diagram! There seems to be a partial wall between the CR and the stairs, of unknown purpose, but certainly not sealed. Then there's another wall on the other side of the staircase, which I'm assuming is part of the outer-leaf, and is totally sealed on all edges, as well as on the face, with no perforations at all (light switches, electrical outlets, etc.). But the biggest issue is the stairs themselves, and the door at the other end of the stairs. 1) How are you going to seal each of those step? Do you have access to the underside of the staircase? Can you be certain that you attain at least two complete seals on each step and riser, all around all edges, as well as all the other woodwork on those steps? Are you certain? 2) Then the door at the bottom will need to be replaced with a suitable isolation door (solid, massive, heavy duty hinges, no perforations, etc.) and with two complete independent perimeter seals. Can you accomplish all of that? Can you guarantee full sealing of the leaves like that, with zero cracks where air can get through? It just seems like so much work and expense to do all that extra stuff, when a simple sound lock door on the studio itself (with extensions of the walls) would accomplish the same thing, much more reliably and much cheaper. - Stuart -
