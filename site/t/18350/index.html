We've got the side walls on 3.56 meters and the front, back wall on 3.42 meters and a ceiling height of 2.72 meters. On the back wall we've got the door, it's pretty much centered on that wall. On the right side of the door (looking out from the room), we have a air intake for fresh air and on the left side of the door (again looking out from the room), we have a air outtake. So we're fortunate to have good airconditioning in the room, but we do think that it sounds too much so we need to fix that. We've got the air out-pipe going inside our room from the back wall along the right side wall and on the front wall. This pipe is going out 28cm from the walls. For the ceiling I'm thinking of making John's Cloud Panel out of cloth and for the floor just some kind of rug. A big question: Should I flush mount the speakers? I think I've got it all down now and with that said: Here's some pictures of the room as it is right now and 2 "blueprints" of my thoughts so far! How I've laid it out in my head is that ....
You seem to be wasting a lot of space with your angled walls. Generally, even for a full RFZ design, you only need to angle the front part of the wall, maybe 1/3 to 1/2 of the room. The rest can be parallel. Also at the back: those cut-off angles are wasting space where your bass traps need to be.
The back wall will have bass traps in the corners starting from the door (I'm thinking of hanging absorbers, yes or no?
Hangers are good, but you don't seem to have enough space to get many big ones in, so it might be better to just do superchunks. They are also very effective.
And should I hang these guys behind the side walls too and even in the front corners?).
Side walls: no. Front corners? Possibly, but here again it depends on your room design concept and the available space. Superchunks might be good there too, or maybe if you use John's soffit design, then hangers underneath would be good.
And the front wall will be heavily treated, maybe 20-25cm, 10cm air between concrete wall and then 10-15cm insulation and then cover it up with cloth. Should I do it in another way?
Whoooaaa! I think you might be confusing "isolation" and "treatment"! The gap between the outer-leaf (concrete) wall and the inner-leaf (???) is part of your MSM isolation system. The insulation that comes after that, inside the room, is treatment.
A big question: Should I flush mount the speakers? I'm thinking no just because I think it looks ridiculous flush mounting a pair of Genelec 8040A's
Not a problem! :) http://www.genelec.com/learning-center/ ... -mounting/ www.genelec.fi/documents/catalogues/Gen ... ochure.pdf (check out page 12...) https://www.google.cl/search?q=genelec+ ... 13&bih=585 Actually, that's not quite the way I would do it, but it sure can be done! And I would do it in your case, as you have the space, and the benefits are excellent...
And finally I'm thinking that we should put up some kind of heavy cloth over the door that you just can pull to the side when entering/exiting the studio.
Or maybe a very thick panel of absorption mounted on the back of the door... - Stuart -
