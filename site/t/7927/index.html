sharward13 January 2007#3
For beef-up: Using cleats screwed to the sides of the studs holds the drywall firmly in place. Using some screws into the OSB is good to get a temporary hold, but drywall screwed only to OSB is no good I think... :roll: Sorry, n8not9it, but the method you described isn't kosher in my opinion. :( kindohm, for your first layer of 5/8" gypsum wallboard, assuming there's no RC or anything between the studs and drywall, and assuming wood framing, you'll want to use 2" coarse thread drywall screws so that you get 1 3/8" penetration into the studs and joists. For your second layer of 5/8" gypsum wallboard, you'll need 2 1/2" coarse thread drywall screws so that you get 1 1/4" (the bare minimum) penetration into the studs and joists. Fine thread screws are for steel framing... And in that case I think you can get away with a little less penetration. You didn't say what kind of framing you're using... I assume timber, but I'm including the steel info for the benefit of others who may be using steel. Of course, I'm going to highly recommend that you check with your local building department on the screw schedule you need to be using in accordance with the model codes that are in effect in your jurisdiction... I wouldn't be me if I didn't. 8) And never EVER rely on Home Depot or any supplier to provide code advice. Their word means nothing! ;-) I realize there are posts in the forum here that suggest using fewer screws on your first layer, but my building department gave me an adamant "NO" to that idea... And in subsequent discussions here and learning more about the role that screws play in holding drywall in the "not crashing down" position, I can't help but to agree with my local authority. Hope that helps... --Keith :mrgreen:

knightfly13 January 2007#10
For RC, you use the same fine thread screws designed for steel framing - they should be self-drilling types. You just hold the screw gun against the material (spinning, of course) til the screw bites into the metal and pulls up tight. If you keep a good pressure on the screw gun with your hand until the screw is properly sunk into the drywall, it doesn't make the screw threads do ALL the work of pulling the screw tight; this takes a bit of practice, you will probably want to play with some scraps first. Remember, RC is a "spring" - so if you intend to put more layers on it, the channels should be spaced closer (and vice versa) in order to maintain the proper spring rate... Steve
