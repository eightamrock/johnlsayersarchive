I live in San Diego C.A., and the room will primarily be used to record vocals/guitars although if possible I will cram a drum set in there. I will be using this to track others and not necessarily for recording myself. I'm trying to keep it under 2k. I believe based on my reading I can get about 50 STC with this setup (please let me know if I am wrong). I will not be putting in a window because of of cost, but I still need to account for HVAC. I plan on using a portable I can get used (looking at a Sharp that is supposed to go down to 38dBa) and a fan driven duct to get in fresh air (and keep air pressure normal). Please look at my pictures and let me know if what I am planning is safe and good. Like I mentioned this is the framing for now, however I will be providing a full drawing with walls, lighting, electrical, doors later. Jack

A few quick thoughts: -Forget about the floor. It won't get you any additional isolation. -Frame the ceiling 24" OC just like the walls. -You need a header above the doorway. -Use drywall for the exterior. Cheaper/easier. -Green Glue may be overkill.

xaMdaM2 September 2009#4
Jack, I would seriously consider buying an isolation booth like a Whisper Room (prefab) unit. The reason I say this is because at least you can disassemble one of these and take it with you when you move. When you take into account the construction methods you'll need to do to be able to take it with you... it's going to take the better part of a couple of months to build it right and co$t you a good chunk of change just for the assembly hardware. Even then, it's going to take a couple of weeks to do the design (at best) and then you'll need to plan on it taking you at least a week to disassemble it when you move. All tolled, I'd guestimate that you're looking at a total of 8-12 weeks, minimum. Granted that leaves you with about 30 weeks of use, but that could easily turn out to be less than 20 if you have a full-time day gig. Either that, or you'll need to accept the fact that you're going to forgo the reuse of materials and likely only salvage some of the framing and the door, then scrap the rest... which sure seems like a shame to do... especially when you can purchase a decent iso booth that you CAN take with you, and incorporate in a new studio design should you purchase a new piece of property, where you can permanently build. Just my feeble .02 worth Max
