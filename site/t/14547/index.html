Soundman20208 July 2010#2
There is a wooden beam running down the room which is 5 1/4" (.16m) wide by 7 1/2" high (.22m). There are then 3 support beams that go down that piece of wood.
Those are going to be your biggest problem. You'll need to figure a way to build some kind of decoupled "sleeve" around them, since they are part of the outer leaf.
There are already 3 walls with studs and are pictured.
It looks like all three of those are coupled to the outer leaf! Is that maybe just due to the camera angle, or are they really attached to the joists above?
I was thinking of doing either a floating floor or a tile not sure which would be best.
You don't need a floating floor, and you certainly don't have the budget for it, so I'd go with tile, or perhaps laminate flooring. Floating your floor is really hard to do right, and really expensive. viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173
Also, as far as the ceiling goes since I will not be able to tilt it due to the height restrictions would a drywall or cloth ceiling be better or is there something I am not thinking of?
If it is "cloth", then it isn't a ceiling at all! Isolation will be nil through that. You are tight on height, and you say you don't need too much isolation, so maybe just RC and a layer or two of 5/8" drywall would do.
I am not quite sure what to do whether to just finish each room and then buy corresponding bass traps and diffusers or try and change the design away from the rectangular.
Regardless of wall angles you will still need a lot of bass trapping! And you won't need any diffusers: it is a small room. Bass traps are needed without any doubt, and the room is too small for diffusers. I'd go for a typical John Sayers design, I think, with soffited speakers, a reflection free zone around your mix position, absorption on the first reflection points, absorption on the rear wall, plenty of bass trapping, and slot walls to keep it lively. - Stuart -

Soundman202031 July 2010#5
The walls are attached to the joists
Let me just clarify: You are saying that the new inner-leaf walls are directly attached to the joists of the floor above you? Is that correct? If so, then you have to fix that, or you will never get good isolation. That is a direct flanking path!
Would I have to build the "sleeve" for the wooden beam and the three poles or just the poles?
Beam and poles. You'll need to build that sleeve such that it does not touch the beam or the poles at any point. You cannot attache the sleeve to the poles or the beam anywhere. Two separate things. Also, the beam must not touch the inner-leaf walls anywhere. No physical contact at all.
My next question is how much would everything suffer if I only put in a single sliding door instead of how John posted in the "small studio" build with two sliding doors?
You would lose most of your isolation but just using a single door. The two doors are part of the MSM system. Each door has mass, and the air between them is the spring: Together they form a tuned system, which is what actually creates the isolation. If you take away either of the doors, then you no longer have a tuned system, and your only isolation comes from the mass-law associated with the single remaining door, which isn't very much...
I will be doing a laminated floor.
Great! Good move. Easy to install, looks good, good acoustically, and not expensive.
Also, as far as the ceiling goes since I will not be able to tilt it due to the height restrictions I will do a drywall ceiling and to cover the radon tube I was planning on constructing another tilted false ceiling that will cover that do the same thing in the live room.
That should work, as long as you take care to prevent the ceiling from touching anything else, such as the joists above, or the radon tube, or the beams / posts. The most important point is that everything must remain decoupled. But it looks like your big problem at present is to fix the issue where you have your inner-leaf walls attached to your outer-leaf ceiling (the joists). Unless you fix that, everything else is pointless. - Stuart -
