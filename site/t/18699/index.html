To beef or not to beef - that is the question

Started by andy_eade on 16 December 2013. 4 replies, 2013–2014.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 18699.

andy_eade16 December 2013#1
In considering different approaches for my second build I have been thinking lately about how much labor went into the beef-up process (between existing ceiling Joists) in my last studio build, and whether the isolation to effort ratio would be increased by adding the extra two layers of mass to the inside leaf of the ceiling. I know that in a lot of basement scenarios this would not be realistic given the limited ceiling height available, but in my case I'm more concerned about a) adding extra weight to the engineered joists, and b) the increased speed with which one could install the extra layers, and perhaps c) the ability to avoid mistakes and also compromise where there are pipes or ductwork existing in the joist bay. I have about 94" available and am planning an independently framed ceiling. I spent a couple of hours looking around the forum elsewhere on the web and could find no discussion on this so thought I would open it up. Please feel free to point me to existing content if you are aware of any. I have to imagine that there is good reason for not doing this else everyone would be doing this. Perhaps the imbalance in the mass of the two leaves, or not having enough mass in the upper leaf to minimize the impact / foot noise from above. Many thanks for your thoughts... Btw my thread can be found over here on the design forum.

Soundman20203 January 2014#3
Happy New Year Andy!!!! :) OK, the principle behind MSM isolation is that there are two leaves with roughly the same amount of mass on each leaf: That gives you the optimum, in terms of isolation. If there is substantially less mass on one leaf, then you no longer have optimum conditions. That's not to say you can't do it that way: just that it isn't optimum. The other issue is the seal: If the floor above you is already very well sealed, with no cracks between the sub-flooring panels, and no gaps around the joists or around the edges, and no other cracks, gaps, nooks, crannies, etc., then you are probably OK. Do you know how thick the OSB sub-floor is? One layer or two? And what is the final flooring on top of that? If you already have a two layers of 5/8" OSB for the sub floor, and something reasonably massive on top of that, then you are probably in the ball-park for decent isolation. - Stuart -
