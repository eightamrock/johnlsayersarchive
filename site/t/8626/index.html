drfrankencopter16 April 2007#2
I think your choices are the following: 1. Vent the return directly to the main return line and install a silencing baffle in-line. 2. Disconnect your studio from the main AC and use a seperate system (and seperate fresh air source) 3. Vent into a small-ish second room (which will also need to be built to a failrly high STC) which contains a direct line to the retrun. In this case the room is acting like a plenum chamber which will reduce sound transmission into (and from) the rest of the system. I'm not sure what the building code would say about this approach...but if your AC guy suggested it maybe he knows. I'm not sure if you saw my thread from a few weeks ago....it was regarding HVAC return noise: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8481 Cheers Kris
guitardad7216 April 2007#3
renegade, I've done a bit of posting back and forth with Rod on HVAC topic on my thread starting on this page: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... &start=465 You may want for Rod to chime in if I recommend something wrong, but I want to take a crack at it just for the exercise... Your Return should not vent into another room, it should tie in to a return duct (plenum) on your existing system. Want to use the same size ducts on return(s) and supply(s) so the pull and push (in CFM and velocity) stays the same. Who knows if the static pressure of venting into another room in house would be able to draw enough air out of return duct from studio room(s). You are going to need fresh air from the outside to go to your room(s), in code (at least in USA) sealed rooms like we build which do not have windows need mechanical ventilation... maybe you do have a window in the room to outside BUT if you don't, recommendation in 15 cfm of fresh air per person. A fresh air duct with fan could be tied into supply. Tied into the return, a fresh air exhaust duct with barometric damper, everything stays balanced. This maybe be the cheapest route which has been suggested to me... and I did fight over the idea. If you current air handler and duct work will allow (cost effectively?) you may want to create zones for your HVAC... one zone for your house and one for your studio. There are many retro fit HVAC zoning kits available online. You can also do a HRV or a ERV, Google search those. Take a look at Sharwards thread, if you can find the page Sharward basically got slammed by a code officer when submitting a plan for permit which did not include fresh air. Sharward is doing HRV. Also you have to make your current HVAC system can handle the new load. According to others cooling is the thing contractors will screw most, heating takes care of itself. I'm going to stop myself here BUT will ask: What is your current HVAC system? Does your heat or cool more in a year? How big is your house in sqft or cubic AND how big is the addition of studio in sqft and cubic? AND OR is your HVAC guy saying your system us big enough to take on additional load. Marc
