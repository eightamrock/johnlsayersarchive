dbluefield12 May 2004#3
This comes up allot it seems. One thing that nobody mentions at first is their need for isolation from the upstairs, as well as the need for quiet upstairs -- do your care if noise leaks upstairs? Can people be quiet if you are recording? If you don't have that great a need for isolation from upstairs - you can save quite a bit of money. Simply fill the joists with fiberglass, cover with fabric and be done with it -- or put in an acoustic tile ceiling with 703 on top. As for the floor - I floated my basement studio floor over poly house sheathing - it provides some de-coupling; it is cheap; it is water proof; you can then add wood floor or make an inexpensive checkerboard pattern floor from two layers of plywood -- the top layer made from 4x8 sheets cut in half. Entire thickness maybe an inch or so? Best, Dave

dymaxian14 May 2004#7
Back to the floating floor question... It could be done, and it'll give you a bunch of extra isolation- especially in the low freqs. This is probably your biggest concern anyway. You could probably float the floor on 2x4s laid flat. If 3/4" OSB floor deck can span 24" then just space the 2x4s that far apart. You'll need to use more neoprene than usual, but it'll save you a little headroom. Then you can build up walls off that floor, and the ceiling off of that. Set the dropped ceiling joists between your existing floor joists and you won't lose more than an inch there. Then just treat it like any other ceiling. It'll be low, but it'll be quiet.
