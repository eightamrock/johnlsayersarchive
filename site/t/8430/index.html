drfrankencopter17 March 2007#2
My understanding is it's a building code thing... Your 'permanent' installations should be fire resistant. That said, if you were to consider your traps/absorbers as decorative wall hangings there is no hard requirement to make them fire resistant (e.g. people hang tapistries and large painings on the wall all the time). So, in a home studio they reside (my opinion only here, and a city inspector may well see it differently) in a grey area of the code. Do consider the effect of fire though...fabric placed vertically can burn quite spectacularly. There are spray on fire retardants, and may even be DIY options out there. Oh, and FWIW, insulation is fire resistant...it will slow the progress of the spread of fire. Cheers Kris

Cram23 March 2007#4
My family is full of carpenters and a few firefighters... This stuff is drilled into my head: the most common use for fire retardant material or a fire block (combination of drywall and condensed fireproof material layered) is an essential part of construction for walls that are not flush against a surface. If you have a fire start and spread quickly through that wall, no matter if all were safe - insurance company will say that the fire wouldn't have spread and they would have less damage to cover. IE: no money for you... Read some of the reference section of this forum that supports this. Look at diagrams. it's common sense actually.

drfrankencopter27 March 2007#11
Here's some useful information... from this site: http://www.geocities.com/jonrisch/a1.htm
Burlap can be made fire retardant either by using a commercial fire-retardant chemical treatment, or for DIY, a 1/2 saturated solution of borax or calcium chloride will provide good fire retardancy. Note that dark colored burlap will probably show white streaks from these treatments. For those who are using burlap and wish to fireproof it there is a company called Flameseal (http://www.flameseal.com) in Texas which makes a spray-on product to fireproof fabrics, its approx. $30.00 per gallon.
