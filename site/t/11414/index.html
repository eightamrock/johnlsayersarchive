AVare20 November 2008#2
The door is the weak spot in the isolation. It will be the point of most sound getting through. what are you hearing? High mid frequencies or low frequencies?
I have another door to build. Is it possible to substitue Auralex sheet block for the sheet lead? They report that it works as well (or better) than sheet lead.
1/8" sheet lead (the same thickness as Sheetblock) is 8 lb/ft^2. It is 15 db better than sheetblock, using the mass law. Ask Auralex for the lab test results of Sheetblock and lead of the same thickness. Andre

rod gervais23 November 2008#5
The trick to getting this right is patience........... Tke off all of the deals and trims. Begin with the door completely closed - and gently place the first seal agains the door - starting at the top on one side - and using a stapler to lock the trim in place. Work both sides of the door and then the top. The rubber should just gently press against the door - too much pressure at any one point will push the door out of position. Once you have a nice even seal - apply the wood trim that locks the bubber inplace. Again - so not press too hard against the door - just a steady even GENLT pressure is all that's required. NOW - work a light outside of the door and not any spots where the light penetrates........ at those spots a small amount of silicone caulk can be used to back the rubber and press it smoothly to the door surface. Allow 24 hours for any caulk to set up. The re-do the entire operation for the 2nd seal.......... the only difference is that this time you will inspect the seat against the door with a light on the inside. Do the same thing with the caulk for a spot backing is you need it. LAstly - install the magnetic seal......... install a steel received on the inside face of the door surface - you can get 1/8" x 1/2 steel strips at Lowes or Home Despot - drill and countersink the surface so the screw heads will be flush. The install the mag strip by allowing it to set against the metal strip and slightly spring it back to allow for just a little pressure. Now that this is all done - you install your door closure - you want a very slow acting latching speed on this so you don't loose people fingers over time. A final door latch (on the inside) of a throw bolt (even better a top and bottom pair) that is installed so you have to slightly pull on the door to latch will make for a perfrect seal. See - easy as pie - but critical that you don't force anything (which is obviously what yu have done.). Rod
