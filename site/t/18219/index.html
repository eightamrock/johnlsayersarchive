And can I mount near fields in the bass traps like the diagram?
Those are not bass traps... they are soffits. And yes, you can mount near fields in them, and that would probably be the single best thing you could to to improve the overall acoustics in the room, given the small size. Soffits do very, very good things to rooms, since they eliminate the artifacts that plague speakers located close to walls in small rooms.
I’m thinking of getting some Adam A5X…
:D :!: Smart man! :) Great choice. They should work fine in that room, although you might want to add a Sub-8 to go with them, to give them a bit more bass extension, especially if you plan to do film work.
Should I build a stud wall to split the garage or can I use the Corner traps,
Both! The stud walls around your room are the isolation shell, and the corner traps inside the final room are treatment. Confusing isolation with treatment is a very common mistake: they are two entirely different things. Acoustic treatment does not isolate, and acoustic isolation does not treat. In fact, they are diametrically opposed, and use extreme opposite materials. So first you isolate the room with hard, rigid, massive materials, then you treat the interior with soft, fluffy, light materials. Very different. - Stuart -

Is it feasible to isolate the sound using thick marine ply on the outside and then another inner leaf?
Yep, it sure is! That's actually a good way of doing it, for isolation.
From out to in> Marine Ply, Insulation, plasterboard, Gap (1/2 inch) plasterboard, Insulation, Plasterboard.
Nope, that would be a 4-leaf wall! :shock: It would be far better to do it as: Marine ply, marine ply, Insulation, Gap (1/2 inch) Insulation, plasterboard, plasterboard. In other words, the same materials but arranged as only two leaves, not four, on the same two stud frames. Vastly better isolation for low frequencies. The only difference is that I replaced one layer of plasterboard with an extra layer of plywood, since that is on the outside of the building. However, apart from that it is the same set of materials, same wall thickness, same mass, but with much better isolation.

Soundman202018 March 2013#13
these materials laid right on one another?
Correct. No air at all between them, and no glue either (unless it is Green Glue, which isn't glue at all... :) )
Was wondering I'd chipboard would work as well for isolation. It's a fair bit cheaper.
It all depends on the density and thickness. Fire-rated drywall (plasterboard) has a density of roughly 725mg/m3, so a 16mm panel weighs about 12 kg/m2. Marine plywood has a density of about 560 kg/m3, so a 16mm panel weighs about 9kg/m2. OSB density is roughly 600 kg/m3, so 16mm panel is about 9 kg/m2. MDF is more like 820 kg/m3, so a 16mm panel is 13 kg/m2. Put another way, to get the same surface density as a sheet of 16mm fire-rated drywall, you'd need 21mm marine plywood, 20mm of OSB, or 15mm of MDF There are different types of chipboard, with different densities, so you need to find out the numbers for the type of chipboard you have in mind, then figure out how thick it would have to be to get the surface density that you need. In turn, the surface density that you need can be determined from the amount of isolation that you need (in decibels), the lowest frequency that you need to isolate, and the maximum permissible thickness of the entire MSM wall, including the air gap and materials. - Stuart -

My insulation rockwool. has to be 75mm or less, should it run behind the noggins?
No, just in the bays between the studs, and above and below the cross-bracing. Just push it in for a snug fit: don't compress it.
What is a good rock wool density to use?
For mineral wool: about 50 kg/m3. For fiberglass: about 30 kg/m3
Should the corner areas/soffets that the speakers will fit into be part of the wall? or separate units that fit into the corners?
Make them part of the wall: it simplifies construction.
Weed membrane 12 square meters. attached to back of frame to create the gap by keeping the insulation in place.
You probably didn't really need that, as the insulation won't create a flanking path unless it is compressed. But what you did certainly won't do any harm, and is actually a good idea for some other applications. - Stuart -
