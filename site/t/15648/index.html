Soundman202025 February 2011#4
Hi Darryl, and welcome! To be honest, I don't think I'd try to do a corner layout in such a small space. My first approach would be to have the desk facing the window to the "attached shed", and arranging the panels on each side, more or less symmetrically. It won't be perfect by any means, but it will give you much more room to work with, and will allow you to get your speakers a bit further apart. Question: Is that a door into the bedroom? Can you close that door, to give you a bit more symmetry? If so, aim to put your desk in the middle of the "shed" wall, about equidistant between the bedroom door and the wall on the other side (top of diagram).
i had not noticed the "partition walls" needed to be angled at 12 degrees
Not an issue. Your panels wont do much to isolate your area form the rest of the room, so it really isn't worth wasting that space on angling things that won't have much effect. You don't give any details on how you are going to build your "baffles". All you mention is a frame with some mineral wool and covered with burlap, but that won't work as a baffle, so I guess you forget to include something in there! Maybe you can provide some more details? I reckon you'll also need some type of movable pass traps for your corners. The other issue is that your room is practically square, so you are going to have modal issues with it. Not much you can do about that on a tight budget. For your clouds, you say: "I was planning on taking the single 3inch 2x4 panels and suspending them from the ceiling ". Panels of WHAT???? :) About your questions:
1. Will my “acoustic baffles” work to create a symmetrical space within my cabin, or will the sound waves just continue to reflect between the existing walls? If they are not going to work as I currently plan, will plywood or similar on the back help solve this problem?
They aren't baffles if they don't have a back on them! In other words, yes you need plywood, MDF or something like that in the back. They should basically be large gobos. And even then they are not going to make a huge difference, but they will help a bit. As you suspected, the sound will still carry on "bouncing around" the rest of the room.
2. Is there too much dampening in this current design? Should slats be added to some of the baffles?
Probably not necessary to add slats. Build them first, and see how it goes. You will still have plenty of "diffusion" going on from the rest of the room, so I doubt you will be absorbing anywhere enough to kill your highs.
4. Are the separate panels for my cloud design acceptable, or do I need join them and make one large cloud?
Separate is fine. - Stuart -
