More complicated, that's funny:) I have seen it discussed in several areas of the Internet using several methods but it all refers to the same principal. Mass air mass. Density air density. Layer void layer. This is achieved in the same way no matter if it is an interior wall, a floor or an exterior wall. A layer of material, a void, another layer of material. An interior wall has a layer (sheetrock) a void(air created by framing members thickness) and a layer (sheetrock). An exterior wall will have these same properties. Doesn't matter that the exterior of a house may have brick and the inside is sheetrock. Still it is density created by brick and a void created by framing member thickness and lastly density from the interior layer of sheetrock. This idea is not restricted to studio wall building but is used in many areas of building in general. Even the celebrated "room in a room" design is made with this basic idea in mind. Looking at a regular snare drum you can see this idea at work. A skin on top then air created by the frame and another skin on the bottom. Same principal, different results as required. You can look around your house and see this working everywhere. Regular interior walls have mass air mass. But the mass is seldom enough to push down the frequencies in the hunt for isolation. This can be rectified, if need be, by installing more mass on one or both sides. Even a simple hollow core door is constructed using a layer a void and a layer. It's everywhere, kinda like Elvis :)

Only thing I can say for certain is this. Slow down. The rush to get these builds done with so many unanswered questions is not helping your cause. One of the most often looked over items in developing a recording environment is "details." Folks rush like its gonna get built in a few days or weeks. It doesn't and if it does it is full of shoulda coulda woulda's. Is six weeks to much to ask of your time? I understand that Rods book is a treasure source for a lot of people in respect to making dreams of recording areas come true. Read it...slowly. Hell read it fast! But read it until you can make since of it. Chances are construction is a foreign language to you as it is with many diy'ers. That reason alone is enough to slow down and get a plan together. It isn't a job that just any beer drinking long haired yahoo can do. Although I am all of those things, I study everyday to understand better what it is that I am trying to do in respect to construction for acoustic space. You are not asking stupid questions. You are asking lazy questions :) If you have a book specif to doing what you are trying to do, read it. Your answers will most likely be found there. Good luck.

The firm I work with has a residential remodel project going in Mobile. A brick exterior, concrete slab foundation and everything that I have seen on the inside is double sheetrock. It is also double rocked on the ceilings as well! Bedrooms, hallways, living, anywhere we cut into the walls has double rock. That is a first for me! Big rectangular rooms with 9 feet ceilings and a long 46ish inch wide hallway. It probable helps that the roof is tall and you can stand up in it and walk on the wood decked/ insulated attic floor. How much? I dunno. Not a ring in the house. Guys banging out the tile in the m/bath at the end of the hall and separated from the ear by two of these walls, it was like a muffled sound. Like it was farther away from us then it really was? Even the flanking through the pipes was mostly noticeable only in either of the 5 bathrooms. The rock, whatever we have taken off so far, was glued with what may be constructive adhesive. Too hard to tell saving it(adhesive) is hardened and brittle. Construction glues that become rigid after exposure to air are mostly frowned upon these days. It has been proven that an STC rating is only as good as the field construction of the wall(s). A lot of worry goes into this aspect of a build. You can figure losing a square foot of floor space per wall run, per wall, interior or exterior with decoupled framing. If you don't lose it there, you'll lose it in the treatment side from what I have seen. Hope I didn't bore you with my recounting of the days events, it some how seemed relevant.
