camistan wrote:
When we say to "oversize" the register or grill, is there a rule of thumb for how much to "oversize"? Would the "oversize" be based on the duct size, air handler cfm or fpm?
Cami, you are a very confused young man........ first ff - there would be no value oversizing a register. You oversize duct - which automatically makes the register larger( you need to cover the duct - and a 10x10 register will not cover a 12x12 duct). Duct size has everything to do with the speed of the air (FPM) as well as the back pressure to the air handler unit....... So you size the duct based on the desired CFM at the desired static and the desired velocity (FPM). It tkes all 3 together. The balancing of the system (using balancing dampers) is what adjusts the CFM to meet the design parameters - and the rest (static pressure and velocity ) fall into place automatically........ some peoplelooking for a cheapway out will increase duct size right at the wall/ceiling - in an atempt to slow down the velocity 0 and then for that they need a larger grill than the ductwork feeding the room would suggest - but never the grille itself.
As an example if the register was 5"x10" (based on it's cfm value) would we possibly move up to the very next register size that also delivers the same cfm? Possibly go up 2 sizes that deliver the same cfm?
What you describe above is not possible. First off - a larger grille is always dsigned to have a capability of deleving more air than a smaller one- so there is not (anywhere in existence) a particular manufacturer who manufactures a 6x10 grille that in designed to deliever the same air as their 5x10 grille does. Rod

Camistan, I will not try to intervene for Rod. He's certainly more experienced than I am, but I'll try to help you anyway. I've had a bit of experience in the A/C field so I'll at least lend a little insight. I am going to understand that noise is your problem, not heating/cooling capacity, so all that you really aim to change is the noise from the air flowing from the register. I'm also not going to get into the tech details and just stick to some practical suggestions. First, Rod is somewhat correct, most registers are "standard" sizes. That means that for the most part getting an oversized register to fit your duct may not be possible. However, you still have choices. 1. Have a register custom made at a sheet metal shop. Actually you'll buy a register/grill of the size you want and have the "boot", the actual transition for the duct to the "grill" custom made. It's not overly expensive, at least not here. 2. You can leave the grill off. It will not distribute air as well, but it will make less noise and it will not hurt the system. 3. You can get a bigger register, whatever size you think will reduce the air noise (which basically comes from the air flowing past the fins of the grill) and get a reducer to fit between the new register and the duct. 4. Depending on your ductwork, how much space you have to work with, etc. You can get a larger register and a duct of a reasonable length (3-6 ft) and to attach to it. Then attach the larger duct to the smaller one. If it's flex duct, it's really easy. The longer length of larger duct will slow the air as it leaves the smaller duct and the larger register will quieten the noise even more.Get someone to show you how or e-mail me directly. My address is in my profile. I'm not on the forum a lot. 5. Again, if you have the space, you can "Y" off of your duct and split the airflow into two places. Cut another register in your wall/ceiling/floor and you get better distributed air and less noise. 6. Get technical: Your furnace/air handler has an output CFM rating. Get in touch with the manufacturer. Unless it's an older unit, they should have that information. Have the model and serial number for the furnace/air handler when you call. Also, get in touch with someone that does ductwork regularly and designs A/C systems. They can tell you how much volume and velocity will work for you. Good luck, Scott
