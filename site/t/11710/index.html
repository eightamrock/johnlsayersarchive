xSpace28 February 2009#3
By the design, these engineered joists should not be loaded in an effort to mass the underside of a subfloor. The OSB webbing, spacing, top and bottom flange, it's all part of the design to spec out the loads the company recommends. On page 16 of the brochure you linked above it shows a typical acoustic ceiling using the type/kind of truss you have. It is also a common example of what has been recommended around here on occasion. As with most construction related components, everything matters. Depending on if your area is just a simple span(as explained in the pdf file) or uses multiple spans, these things have to be taken into consideration. The deflection of the floor system is a high consideration and is just another part of the concern. It is not recommended to have lower level walls abutt this kind of engineered joist. So the brochure reads that at least, RC and 5/8 inch sheetrock can be used on the underside. Outside of that, maybe it would be better to get in touch with GP. Brien

xSpace4 March 2009#6
Did he say either, or... or did you confirm both? If it is a matter of one option, then the ceiling on the bottom side of the wood I joist would be better overall with the air provided by that install. If both places are acceptable then you would want to do the math on what the distance leftover is from the bottom of the new material you will be using, to the bottomside of the top 2X4 cord. Once you know that measurement, then you will have to get a piece of lumber of sufficient size and cut a portion out using a table saw, to build an "L" shaped board(running the length of the entire new board) that you can screw into the bottom-most side of this top cord. Or, as an alternative, using the same measurement you could notch both ends of a 2X4, cut to fit the distance from 1/8" shy of the plywood webbing, and allow it to press up against the new mass, to be screwed off to the underside of the 2X4 top cord. I can't draw pictures conveniently, this is about as good as it gets short of me driving over there and doing it for ya ;) Brien
