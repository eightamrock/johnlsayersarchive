Soundman202020 November 2012#35
here is the sample of the drums within the room ...
... thud ... BOOOOOM .... thud ... BOOOOOOM! :) OK, that pretty much confirms what was evident on the graphs: You have some major modal stuff going on there. And it seems to be exactly where predicted, according to room mode calculators. You first three axial modes are predicted to be at 46.8 Hz, 61.1 Hz, and 70.3 Hz, and on my DAW it sure looks like you are hitting all of those, and a few more besides. Solution? Bass trapping, and LOTS of it. What you have is clearly insufficient, and needs some pretty hefty upgrading. On the image you show above, I would go for the two vertical corners on the left of the room: Superchunks, floor to ceiling, and as wide as possible. Hopefully you can get them 90cm across the front face, but make them 60 cm at the very least. I'd also go for all four horizontal corners (wall-ceiling), with corner absorbers: Simple panels of 4" 703 placed diagonally across those corners, once again as big as possible. You also seem to have some flutter echo going on, which is especially evident on the side stick and toms, so I'd suggest some absorption on the open patch of wall on the left of the image, and a large, hard-backed, angled cloud hung directly over the drum kit. It's a small room, and will always have a "small-room" sound to it, but the above should at least help to tame the terrible modal ringing, the flutter, and the overall acoustics. You never did upload the REW file so we can take a look at that, so please do so, and once you have installed the treatment, please repeat the REW analysis and upload the new version too, so we can see what effect it had, and what additional treatment might still be needed. - Stuart -

Soundman20204 December 2012#48
does it matter if the corner superchunks aren't all the same size? If I couldn't get two of them as big as the others is it better to have all 4 the same size or 2 of them one size and then the other two slightly bigger?
They don't really need to be the same size, and if you are able to make some of them bigger, then go for it! Bigger is better. The gap behind the side panels isn't critical: it just helps to extend the range a little lower. Some folks say that the gap should be the same as the thickness of the insulation, but there's no theoretical support for that, as far as I'm aware. Any gap is good, so just make it as big as you can afford, in terms of space. - Stuart -
