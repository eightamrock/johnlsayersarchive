jwl7 July 2008#2
The most common material is FRK, which is the standard foil/paper facing that comes on some models of Owens Corning rigid fiberglass. You could glue it on with standard spray contact adhesive, on the front end of the trap facing into the room. It will restore some reflectivity at mids and high frequencies, as well as give you a boost in your bass trapping. It looks like your wall/ceiling corners don't have traps in them, it's a good place if you are still having bass issues. Also, any flat panels (ie, not straddling corners) you should space out from the wall a few inches. It will give you better overall absorption, as well as absorb to a lower frequency. This is especially important on the rear wall.
