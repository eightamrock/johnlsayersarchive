Soundman202023 September 2010#6
Can you please tell me a bit more on why particle board is not good idea,
well, you CAN use it if you want to, but you don't NEED to use it. It won't be as effective as gypsum board for isolation, and will cost more. In order to isolate, you need mass. Gypsum board is much more dense than particle board, and cheaper (in most parts of the world). So comparing kilogram for kilogram, thick gypsum board is the best and cheapest way to do it. Two layers of 15mm gypsum board is about the same total mass as what you had in your original plan, but since it is more dense, it uses up less space (total thickness), and costs less. The person who told you to put the particle board on the ceiling: Did he say WHY he thinks you need it? Perhaps it was for structural reasons, not for acoustic isolation reasons?
many people are trying to convince that there are some other "equal in effect" glues/liquids/whatever, but i was always sceptical...
Yeah, there seem to be a lot of people that just do not understand what Green Glue actually is, and they think you can substitute other things instead! But there really is nothing else like it. It is NOT glue at all: it is a very specialized acoustic damping material, so you cannot use any kind of glue to replace it.
i was just not sure if it is ok to leave layers like they are while, ofcourse, tapp and "close" all joints and screwholes.
Sealing everything is VERY important. Seal each layer, each joint, very carefully. Even a small gap can damage your isolation.
i am affraid that vibrations from drum playing are possibly going to make impact on a concrete structure.
A good way of dealing with impact noise form drum kits, is to build a "drum riser", which is basically just an isolated platform for the drums. Much easier and much cheaper than trying to build a proper floating floor.
i thought of putting hydroisolation next on a concrete (i need this!) then stone wool of 160kg/m3, then estrih and finally real oak parquet.
What is "estrih"?
my logic is telling me i should do it
Unfortunately, there are many things about acoustics that are not "logical"! Sound just does not behave the way most people think it does. It isn't logical at all, until you start understanding the basics of how it all works. Most people don't even realize that, from the point of view of a sound wave, even air is pretty hard and stiff, and also bouncy.... :)
as it was said, this way i eliminate reflections problem from a start and then later tune the room to desired effect.
You cannot eliminate reflections by adding extra solid walls: All that you do like that is to change the angle of the reflections. They are still there, but they just go in a different direction. There are several types of reflections that you need to know about, but probably you were told to angle your walls to eliminate flutter echo, which is one type of reflection. Don't worry too much about that: There are other ways of dealing with flutter echo. But there is only ONE way of dealing with the modal problems that all small rooms have: The ONLY way of dealing with those, is with bass traps. Bass traps have to be very large, and the best place you can put them is in the corners. If you cut off the corners of your room, then you have no place to put your bass traps, and your room will sound bad: The bass will not be smooth and tight. It will sound dull, muddy, honky, cavey, and unpleasant. Bass traps are very important in small rooms, so use that corner space for your traps, not for trying to chase reflections. That's not the best way to do it.
my base concrete is already shaped for the hexagon and elevation and difference in hight is already done.
Why did you shape the "base concrete", and why do you have "differences in height"? That is not necessary at all!!!! That is not the way to build a studio in a basement! If you have poured extra concrete on top of the existing concrete floor, then you have wasted money and also wasted room height. For good acoustics you need as much height in the room as possible: if you raise the floor at all, then you have reduced the height. You need as much height as you can get! This might sound harsh, but whoever told you that you need to shape your foundations on top of an existing concrete floor, and that you have to cut the corners of the room to deal with "reflections", does not understand acoustics or studio design. Sorry, but they just don't have a clue.
i like to hear what would you do with a space that is left on a top left corner on a top plan? right next to that wall is a road and i am ot sure wheter to fill that "triangle" with something.
I think you need to slow down, stop all your construction, and plan your studio better before you do anything else! If you have a busy road next to your studio, then "putting something in the triangle" is not going to help at all! You need proper isolation around your room, and you do not have that on your plan. Looking at your plan, I can see several MAJOR issues. They can probably be fixed, but not if you carry on building the way you were planning to. It might be possible to use what you already have built, but we will need to see photos of what you have done already, and much more information on what you are trying to do. For example, in order to decide how much isolation you need, we need to know how loud you are inside that room, and how quite you need that to be outside. We also need to know how loud the noise from that road is, and what kind of noise it is. Plus we need to know what kind of work you will be doing in that studio: will you be recording live instruments at midnight? Or just mixing during the day only? Or just mastering? Etc. Please take some photos of what you have right now, and post them here so we can see where you are and what can be done to fix the problems caused by the bad advice you have been given.
and then later tune the room to desired effect.
There are some things you cannot fix after the room is already built: You need to START by choosing good dimensions, based on one of the known good ratios (Sepmeyer, Louden, Boner, etc.). Then you need to isolate your room properly, but you do not show any isolation on your plans. Then you need to plan the basic layout of your room, showing the correct geometry for the speakers and mix position. Then you need to plan the basic treatment, which starts with major bass trapping in the corners, absorption on all first reflection points, designing a reflection free zone around the mic position, etc. Then you need to consider where you will have your electrical system, and your HVAC system. And once you have all that in place, you need to figure out doors and windows, and how you are going to keep all of those structures isolated and decoupled. You don't have any of that on your plans, and from what you are saying, then things that you have done already are no use at all for what you need to do. So please, stop building and start planning. You cannot build a successful studio if it is not planned correctly. You need to think of everything in advance, and only start building once the entire design works together. Post the photos here, and we'll see what can be done to fix the problems, and design your room correctly. And you DO need to fill in your location if you want continued help on this forum. - Stuart -
