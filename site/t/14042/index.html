jbassino10 February 2010#11
Stuart, Maybe I didnt do it the right way, but at least it worked for me in the Drum room I've built. Basically I didnt bother with the rubber seal but I applied a reasonable amount of caulk before, during and after placing the glass. I dont have any weak points through the window and it isolates perfectly. Our 146 noise evaluation resulted "null" meaning the noise was less than the ambient noise outside. Basically what I did is after you finish your inner and outer framings (inside out), you place the inner stops, that can be a 1x2 strip or whatsoever. Then apply a NICE amount of caulk all over the perimeter for sealing any gaps.. then, apply some more caulk "over" the stop where the glass is gonna rest on. . Then place your glass (the caulk is gonna compress and seal completely). After you set your glass, apply some more caulk now between the glass and the window frame.. and place your outer stop (that can be another 1x2 strip, stained, or those "quarter rounds" that also look cool). It worked for me, i dont know if thats the right methor or not, but it worked my 0.2 cents

This is correct for the most part. As long as the temperature is the same on both sides of the glass, that is, the environment is static from one room to the other, then you will not have any issues with condensation. Glass is a barrier so vapor can be extracted from the air (H2O) between the two pieces if the temperature where allowed to moderate on different sides.
