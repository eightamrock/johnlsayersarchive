Hi everybody! I'm putting together a mix room in my house in Chicago, IL. I posted about this room a while ago. I got the following drawings from Ready Acoustics as a suggested setup (they offer a low cost service fr this purpose), and wanted to get a second opinion. I'm unable to do construction in this room, as it's in a rental house, so I'm limited to what treatment can be done. I've currently got 8 bass traps made from Johns Manville 814 (3.0 PCF; equivalent to OC703) and 9 HF panels of JM 817 (6.0 PCF; equivalent to OC705). The Bass Traps are constructed with three sheets (6" total) of 814 with a small wood frame behind the panels and burlap covering. The HF panels are 1 sheet (2") of 817 with a small wood frame behind the panels and burlap covering. I've already put superchunks in the corners as indicated in the drawing. I'd like to know if you all think this is a good way to go about setting this room up, or if you have other suggestions. In case it's not evident from the drawings, the proposed listening position is 66% off the wall behind the speakers. (33% off the wall behind the listening position). I do realize that standard procedure is to set the room up with speakers firing down the long dimension of the room. When I mentioned this, Joel at ready acoustics indicated that this arrangement would be better because it would be more symmetrical. He also indicated that treatment behind the listening position probably wouldn't be necessary because of the narrow strip of wall that would produce specular reflections to the listening position. In the drawing, the red panels are 6" bass traps, the white panels are 6" bass traps that are "optional for this location", the blue panels are 2" HF panels. The corners are filled with superchunks (34" face, made of JM814). Any information or opinions are sincerely appreciated! Thanks much! Mike Taylor

jhbrandt3 October 2010#3
Agreed. Actually, I would recommend 'filling' that cubby with absorption. Also, you should check side wall reflection points. Use a mirror. With all that absorption in the room, the decay will be very fast at mid and high frequencies & I would recommend using slats across the front corner traps and anywhere the slats will not cause reflections into the RFZ. Cheers, John

gullfo4 October 2010#9
for proper Helmholtz action, the air cavity has to be sealed. there are several options, one is put some widely spaced slats to improve high frequency reflections off the absorber with little impact to its low frequency absorption. you could decide to build a proper slot resonator but your absorber response will be completely different than your current design. this is due the the range that a slot resonator will work over versus the cloth covered insulation. the formula you show is used to define a targeted frequency range. to create a more broadband effect, the changing depth cavity and some alteration to slat wide and slot opening will create a slightly wider range, and adding cloth behind the slats will flatten the Q of the absorber and reduce its efficiency. you could simply put some widely space slats over the middle section of the corner absorber to add some reflections and leave the top and bottom (effectively the tricorners) as plain absorber.
