I had one 10' x 10' shear wall that I applied a total of four layers of drywall onto, over 3/8" OSB. This is my "middle leaf." (Unfortunately it has to be a three leaf wall.) I actually did not bother taping. I only used one layer of joint compound (mud) and I did not sand it down. Am I worried about cracks over time? No... I can't see it mattering. I was careful to stagger my seams so that no two adjacent layers had seams aligned. --Keith :mrgreen:

Aaronw30 January 2007#6
My control room walls are and ceiling are all 2 layers of 5/8" drywall. All RC that the drywall is attached to IS RC1. The only stuff that looks like RC2 is the hat channel I used between the walls to hold in the insulation. ALL CORNERS are caulked. Especially where they stagger in the corners, there's a bead of caulk on each layer where they overlap. All layers are sealed at the bottom as well as the top. SEAL EVERYTHING. Including door jams and window frames when the time comes. I taped and mudded each joint on each layer. I did not sand...wasn't necessary. But you must seal them at the least. And most important...stagger your joints from layer to layer. Hang one vertical, and one horizontal.
