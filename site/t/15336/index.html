1) use hangers. built using Homasote or similar and wrapped with insulation. put them on a 15° angle relative to the front opening. 2) you could go either way. i'd would tend to think using that space for more bass trapping would be good and then add the diffusion on top of that. 3) i would go with all slats but consider dividing the cavity space in quarters - split in the middle both horizontally and vertically to improve efficiency of the unit on the right side. matching slats on the door side. 4) overall looks like some nice work. remember the ceiling plays a very big role and you could go with a number of clouds and various angles and sizes to the clouds to manipulate that space. in the live room, some of John's slat boxes or some polys along with bass trapping and/or slats across the corners. what are your plans for ventilation?

gullfo4 December 2010#4
If not homasote, then some other low density fiber board should be ok. The hangers should occupy as much space as possible in a given section. So full height or half height depends on the space available. You could try the bench idea and if it turns out you need more it should be straightforward to replace it. You need to seal the slat cavities really well. Yes, you don't always see what happens behind the slat walls but in this case (or really any long slat wall) you want to partition it to improve efficiencies.

you can build out boxes in the wall to create the sealed cavities, or use the walls to do it. either way the key concept is you need sealed air spaces behind the slats to make them function correctly. the simplest way is to add plywood panels that extend from the slat frame to the isolation wall, top to bottom, to divide the cavity and caulk it.
