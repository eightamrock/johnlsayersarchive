The further away from the wall the better. Wall reflections can have a very negative impact on your speaker response. Check out my Wall Bounce Calculator. Mounting the speakers on stands behind the desk is also preferable to sitting them on the bridge. Reflections from the console surface can also cause comb filtering problems. Thomas

dymaxian24 September 2004#6
Remember that high-freq sound doesn't come out of the back of the speakers. This means the wall behind them can be reflective to highs if you want, but try to put as much low and low-mid absorption as you can there. This may help balance out the overall live-ness of your room (don't want it too dead). If you put slat resonators, or insulation spaced away from the wall, you can have your speakers a little closer to that than if it was just the bare wall. Try to give them at least a foot from the wall surfaces. I'm kinda in the same situation- trying to find the best front-end setup, between speaker placement and wall treatment there's a million things you can do. If the wall is pretty much the way it's going to be, the best thing to do is just move the speakers around until you find a spot that really works. And remember that no matter how good your speaker placement is, the percieved sound will change as you move around the room anyway. ;) Hope this helps!

dymaxian28 September 2004#8
I don't think diffusors or high-freq absorbers will do you much good mounted on the wall behind stand-mounted speakers. Might deaden the room if that's what you want. If the stuff that you have up on the front wall absorbs lows, and your room sounds good to you as it is, don't worry about it. On the back wall, try to absorb as much of the spectrum as you can.
