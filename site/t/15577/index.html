xSpace5 February 2011#11
Amazingly enough with all this discussion, I see no measurements for this/these rooms anywhere? Your existing height, whatever that may be, will dictate if installing a vaulted type ceiling is in the design or not. If height distance is already low, then you want to get a flat ceiling to maximize the volume for your room(s) Ratios are not scalable, per se, so if you don't have 10 feet high walls to start with, it isn't something to get worked up over. Still, keeping a good rectangular room , should you go that way, is always prescribed. People tend to want to make angled walls/treatments at this forum, since that is the form and practice that Mr. Sayers designs with. As to the distance, anything that immediately decouples the two assemblies is required, so the distance isn't such a factor in that respect. But if you have the room/foot print to add more space between walls, then more is always better, if you can maintain that separation across the entire build.
gullfo5 February 2011#12
Agreed go with the flat ceiling and add angles via clouds and /or false ceiling rather than make the isolation layer concave like shown. If you do decide to make the ceiling angled, use a single angle from the front (lowest) to the back (highest). Use 6 degrees minimum angle.
