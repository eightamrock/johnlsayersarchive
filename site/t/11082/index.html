AVare12 August 2008#5
Where to start with what you have shown... If you believe a floating floor is necessary, which I do not, then use 50" of 48 kg/m^3 density glass based mineral wool with no stringers or neoprene. The closest testing of a similar wall in IR 586 (see figure 4, pdf page 9) show a TL greater than 28 dB from 63 Hz up and TL over 60 dB from 500 Hz up. With no coupling between the concrete and studs except for the floor and double plasterboard, I would estimate the TL would be over 66 dB from 400 Hz up. Reduce the space to just enough to assure no mechanical touching. Use two layers on the inside, not three. Green Glue is expensive (~$35 per sheet of drywall sandwich), you don't need it. The outside skin acts a third leaf reducing TL at low frequencies. USe stucco or material adhered directly to the concrete block. Remember you are saving a lot of money by not using Green Glue. The weak point is the roof. Consider a concrete roof to somewhat match the concrete wall isolation. Obviously it will not be as thick, but it is the cheapest way get mass. These changes may seem daunting, but you will get better isolation, more interior space, and spend less money. I think that is enough surprises for starters. Andre
