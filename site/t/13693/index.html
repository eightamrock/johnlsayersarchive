Hi Jhon, and welcome! (Is that really how your name is spelled?) I see several red flags in there. The ones that came to mind first are:
6) Exterior walls to be insulated with the most dense fiberglass insulation that will fit inside a 2x4 wall 7) All interior walls to be insulated with the most dense fiberglass insulation that will fit in a 2x6 wall
The normal recommendation here is for insulation density to be around 48 kg/m3 (about 3 lb/ft3). If you go much less than that and it isn't very effective acoustically. And if you go much more than that, then you are just paying more money for little extra benefit. And if you go with extremely dense stuff, then you run the risk of flanking. So you might want to change that to specify a density of 48 kg/m3, give or take a bit. It doesn't have to be fiberglass, either: Mineral wool is also good. Either will do just fine. Also, even though the studs might be 2x4 or 2x6, the actual depth of the air gap (cavity) will be larger: The air gap should be at least 4", and much more if you can afford the space. Also, some studies show that completely filling the cavity (leaf to leaf, and floor to ceiling) has a positive effect on isolation (improves it), as long as you don't cram it in too tight. So you might want to specify that that the entire wall cavity should be filled with insulation, not just the space between the studs, as for a normal wall.
8) All walls, Save the exterior walls backing to concrete, interior and exterior shall have ½” sound board installed then z channel, then 1/2 ” sheetrock
Six issue right there! #1) 1/2" isn't very much. Go for 5/8. It does several good things for you acoustically. #2) Soundboard? well, O guess that's OK, but ordinary common drywall is just as good (if not better), and much cheaper. Mass is mass. Get the cheapest mass you can. ( Sound waves don't care how much you paid for it. ) #3) "z-channel" is not what you want. Normal z-channel does not decouple, so it is useless. "Resilient Channel" (RC) is what you would want. Only proper RC decouples acoustically. Hat channel does not, ordinary z-channel does not. And you probably don't even need any channel it at all, if you build the wall right! Because... #4) A layer of 1/2" drywall (or soundboard) with a thin air gap and then another layer of 1/2" drywall is a REALLY BAD idea! The air gap, or cavity, will only be the same depth as the Z-channel, which is nowhere near enough! That thin cavity and thin drywall will cause the wall to have a rather high resonant frequency, probably way up there where you don't want it, in the very audible range. It also wont isolate very well. You need more mass, a much larger gap (at least 4 inches), and insulation in the cavity. #5) That's a three leaf wall, if I understand the description correctly: concrete wall -- air gap -- soundboard -- air gap -- drywall. Three leaf is a BAD idea. It should only be 2 leaves. #6) Even that small air gap should have been filled with isolation. (Of course, you won't actually have that in the end (I hope), but they way it was specified should have had insulation in the cavity.) The above makes me suspect that your builder doesn't understand acoustics, nor what is involved in building a studio. He's in for a steep learning curve! And you will need to be supervising a great deal. There are may things that he needs to "un-learn", and many precautions he has to take, to avoid trashing your isolation and acoustics.
9) Exterior door shall be 3’ steel insulated door without glass
For a studio, all doors should be sold core wood, very thick, very massive, with heavy duty hinges, door closers, etc. If you want a steel door, then get a proper acoustic steel door. And be prepared to pay big bucks for it! Otherwise get wood. All doors need multiple seals around them, too. At least two seals per door, preferably three.
10) Door between live room and control room shall be 3’ steel insulated door with full glass.
Glass in the door? It can be done, but you need to specify thick laminate glass, not just any old glass. And you need to specify how it is to be mounted, sealed, etc. And you need to calculate the mass density of the door, glass and wall to work together as one. They all need to be roughly the same.
17) It shall be wired to code with lights and receptacle placed at the owner’s direction
You need to specify the separation of circuits, the isolation transformer (if permitted by code) and most important of all the grounding (earthing). It must have star grounding, and very good (low resistance) grounding. Very important. Also stuff like specifying how much power you need, where you want it, distribution panels, etc.
23) The rafters shall be 2x10 and shall be packed with maximum allowable insulation and shall receive the same sound board, z-channel, sheetrock treatment as the walls.
Is 2x10 enough? Did you get a structural engineer to check the plans, spans, loads, and stuff. I guess you did, but it does no harm to check.... There will be an awful lot of mass up there, hanging from those joists: thy need to be able to support the load.
24) The walls inside may be framed slightly out of square to allow for optimal recording acoustics…. Owner’s choice.
What does "slightly" mean? The spec doesn't mention the angles, nor that the framing is two separate decoupled frames, nor that that the inner-leaf walls and ceiling can not, under any circumstances, touch the outer-leaf walls/ceiling. I'd suggest that you make that far more specific, such as specifying a minimum splay of 6 degrees on walls, and minimum angle of 12 degrees on the ceiling, etc. In fact, I'd suggest that you need actual PLANS in mind before specifying any of this.
1) Two new 3x3 windows shall be installed per plan… owner will supply up to two more windows one of which will be installed between the live room and the control room… the other may be installed in the loft.
A single window between CR and LR isn't much, if you plan to record in there! In order to isolate, that window needs to be two separate panes, each one being thick laminate glass, placed with at least 8 inches of air gap between them, correctly mounted, sealed, etc. Installing studio windows is a lot harder than installing normal house windows, if you want them to isolate correctly. Attention to detail is very important here. Even simple things, like cleaning before installing, because once they are installed, you cannot EVER clean the other side! I didn't seen any provision for HVAC in those specs, except for this: "16) One 13 seer heat pump with 3 speed fan installed, sized by HVAC contractor." that isn't much to go on! HVAC is a large part of the entire build, and requires careful layout of the ducts, silencers, etc., which probably needs to be coordinated with the builder. Anyway, that's the stuff I noticed at a first quick reading. Maybe others can comment too. - Stuart - (Edited for spelling and other errors)
