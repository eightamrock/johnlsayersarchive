Soundman20204 May 2012#8
Actually, it isn't really the density as such that matters. What matters for acoustic purposes is a property called "gas flow resistivity", which basically refers to how the material reacts to air moving through it. That's the important characteristic, and that varies according to the type of insulation. The problem is, most manufacturers of insulation never bother measuring that, since it doesn't matter if you just want thermal insulation, so it's next to impossible to find that number for most ordinary materials. You only normally find it for things designed specifically for acoustic applications. Fortunately, there's a very rough relationship between the density of some materials, an their gas flow resistivity, so if you know the type of material and the density, then you can estimate the gas flow resistivity. For fiberglass insulation, the optimum range is about 30 kg/m3 density. For mineral wool, it's around 50 kg/m3. So any insulation you can find in roughly those ranges will work. You don't need to be anal about it, so if you find mineral wool at 45 or 55 kg/m3, that's great. Same if you find fiberglass at 25 or 35. But if you find fiberglass at 120 kg/m3, well, that's waaaaay too dense. That won't work well. The gas flow resistivity is just too high. Also, contrary to popular belief, higher density is NOT better for bass trapping: lower density is! That's not intuitive, but then again many things about acoustics are not intuitive, until you understand the science behind them. Bass = Low frequencies = long wavelengths. Means you need more space around the fibers for them to move and thus convert sound energy into low-grade heat energy. If the fibers are packed too tightly together, then they sort of "shield" each other and get in each other's way. So higher density insulation is better for high frequencies, but not for lows. It's actually a bit more complex than that, of course, but that helps you to get a mental picture of what is going on, and why certain densities are good for certain things. - Stuart -
