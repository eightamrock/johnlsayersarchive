dymaxian24 June 2004#2
Greetings and welcome! The third, interior wall would pretty much be a false wall. The "3-leaf" wall system you're trying to avoid would only happen if you covered that interior wall with drywall and sealed it tightly. When we build false walls, they're usually just rigid fiberglass insulation with fabric covering, and perhaps wood slats as you mentioned. You could build slat resonators into the walls like this if you wanted, but even just having an angled wall with fiberglass blanket insulation in it would do a lot to soak up low frequency sound- the false wall would space the insulation away from the hard drywall wall on the other side. And if there's enough space behind the false wall, you could install hangers to absorb even more low freqs. There's lots you could do with those walls- just don't seal 'em. Your hunch was correct.

knightfly26 June 2004#9
Rick, you could of course do it that way - but there are a few reasons NOT to, if you can afford the room and extra materials - first, any trapping/absorption that's done as part of a wall tends to weaken the isolation of the wall at the trapped frequencies - (it's gotta go somewhere) and designing a wall is basically the same as designing a bass trap - it's all about mass-air-mass resonance. For best isolation, you want a m-a-m resonance that's at least an octave lower than the lowest note you want to stop - this takes quite a bit of mass to accomplish. If the inner leaf of your isolation system is also trying to be a bass trap, it (by definition) has to let some bass through (counter-productive to good isolation) - So, generally if you can it's best to over-build your isolation system (mass-air-mass barrier) for better isolation than you think you'll need, then do your acoustic treatment as a third (non-hermetic) inner shell. This can make the room into an RFZ by using splayed sections, it can hide bass hangers, parts of it can be made into slat resonators, diffusors, etc, depending on placement and size of the room - and, by over-building the isolation part (the two outer leaves) you compensate for the losses in isolation that traps create. And yes, Helmholtz traps need to be sealed - but that seal can be the inner leaf of the isolation shell, and the air gap can be variable - the general concensus is that a splayed slat resonator without individual septums between slots would tend to average out according to the median depth, with possibly some broadening of the response peak caused by the varying depth. Personally, I think I'd put separators in about every 4 or 5 slats if the air cavity was tapered - that way, I'd get more than one "average depth" and a wider band trap effect. There's a discussion on the acoustics forum about this part, it's pretty old - don't remember the title, though, I'm lucky to remember my name at this point in time (12 12-hour night shifts in a row, finally escaped the zoo for a few days, yuchh) Speaking of Slat resonators, I've been working on a new upgrade for the SAE slat calculator - this one's in inches, and also calculates the actual Sabins of absorption to expect at resonance, based on overall size of the trap and the slat/slot ratio - should have it posted for download in a day or two.
