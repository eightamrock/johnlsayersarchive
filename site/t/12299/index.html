  • Purpose: Hobby, no clients, mixing, recording synths, vocals
  • Loudness: Doesn't matter, only annoying my wife and she already agreed to ignore any noise, as I don't plan to record drums or electric guitar the sound level will be reasonable
  • Surrounding: Studio is in the basement of my own family home, adjacent rooms are a corridor, a big empty storage room (maybe future studio?) and the kitchen one floor above
  • Budget: ~1000 € for construction, another 500-1000 € for acoustic treatment
  • Room size: 3,47 m x 3,80 m
  • Room height: 2,17 m from existing laminate flooring
  • Walls: 15 cm Prefab concrete
  • Ceiling: Prefab concrete
  • Floor: Floating concrete pavement with floor heating and laminate flooring (not glued to concrete)

  • Do I need a vapour barrier on the outer wall or special slab walls with insulation to stop moisture? The wall is insulated with 80mm insulation on the outside.
  • How best to close the hole in the door wall where the tubing goes into the corridor?
I've posted some pics of the setup of the basement and a sketchup file for all interested in the detailed room dimensions. You can also follow the building process in my flickr set. I would be very happy to hear from you and keep you informed about the progress. Many TIA Dok:Z

Soundman202017 April 2009#3
Hi "dok:z" Could you post a SketchUp model of your room, with accurate dimensions? That would make it much easier.
Are angled walls needed? Nearly all designs shown in this forum have these. As I don't have much space I don't want to sacrifice much room.
You don't have to splay (angle) the walls. It helps to create a reflection free zone, and reduce flutter echo, and with other stuff too, but it is not 100% necessary. Many successful rooms have parallel walls, and then extra treatment to deal with those issues.
Is the template design like in this thread (scene 2) possible?
Yep! That studio was actually built, and worked out fine as far as I know.
Should I build slab walls or can I go with direct acoustic treatment on the concrete
What do you mean by "slab walls"? If you are totally certain that you do not need isolation (meaning that there is NOTHING noisy in your house or your neighborhood that could interfere with your recordings, such as vacuum cleaners, people, radios, TVs, dogs, cars, lawn mowers, aircraft, rain, wind, etc.) then you don't need to bother building inner walls at all, and you can treat your concrete directly. However, if you have any of the above in your home, then you probably do want at least a little bit of isolation, and the cheapest way of doing that is with simple stud frames with drywall on one side. So if you are certain that you will never need isolation then just go ahead and treat. but if you think you might need isolation, then do that first. However, you might also need walls for other reasons, such as making your room symmetrical, or adjusting the room ratio to be a good one (or at least to get it away from a bad one).
Should or can I integrate my speakers into the wall? I know that the bass response is better when integrated, but my monitors (Alesis Monitor II) have rear bass reflex holes.
Why don't you call Alesis, or send them an e-mail, and ask if your model of speaker can be "soffit mounted", and if so, how to do it properly. Soffit mounting (more correctly called "flush mounting" is good, but I don't think it would be easy to get right with a rear-ported speaker. I don't know how you'd handle that.
How best to close the hole in the door wall where the tubing goes into the corridor?
Where? I don't see that in the photos. Post some close up shots of that, along with your SketchUp model. - Stuart -
