Recently while using this sites (members posts) to research the best method and materials to use to sound proof my proposed studio design. I came across a link that took me to an amazingly informative site that contained STC tests for many types of walls. While browsing through the many walls tested, I noticed one wall construction that I found particularly interesting. I found one wooden framed double wall contruction that exhibited higher than average stc level for lower frequencies. (page 351 of the ir761.pdf) When I examined the building materials used for this construction, I noticed that one of the materials used was cellulose fibre spray. As this material and construction method seems to improve sound isolation, I was wondering why people did not use this material / method to sound proof their studio's. I understand that rockwool in slab or loose form is easier, quicker and cleaner to work with, however, if sprayed insulation treatment offers superior sound isolation, then why not use it. I thought that it may even be a good idea to treat the insides of a double walls constructed frame in one go, in order to seal the frame to the highest level possible. (with the exception of perhaps allowing yourself a method of walking around the inside of the double frame in order to do the job). I also notised that the wall mentioned had insulation spray only on one of the double walls. I also wondered that if both sides of the wall were treated with the same or even more insulation spray, and a greater air gap was given than the 25 mm noted in the spec, that perhaps this double wall could reach even further stc levels. Anyway, just a question for those who are far wiser and experienced than I. Thanks to those who give help. info on cellulose fibre spray http://www.ehow.com/how_2140531_buy-cel ... ation.html
John Sayers8 August 2009#2
WE used cellulose fiber in the construction of Studio Oasis. The original building had standard trusses which we modified to scissor trusses which we lined with a double drywall layer. We then removed a couple of sheets of roofing iron and pumped cellulose fiber into the cavity, much easier than laying insulation batts throughout the ceiling space.

xSpace8 August 2009#4
"I also wondered that if both sides of the wall were treated with the same or even more insulation spray, and a greater air gap was given than the 25 mm noted in the spec, that perhaps this double wall could reach even further stc levels." Yes it could. Getting the framing farther apart thereby creating a greater air gap is the easy part and is automatically beneficial. The minimum of mass-air-decoupling-insulation, either one of the four of these will produce better isolation with a combination of the four being the most helpful. The spray on cellulose is easy enough to do on the exterior partition. The problem with attempting to do the other side is that usually there is not enough room nor access into or out of the cavity for man and mechanical effects. You would need at least a 24 inch space to put an > equipped body< into that open area with gear to blow the back side of the wall. There is much data that recommends that mass-air-decoupling is the best starting place with insulation being beneficial but not so much that you would want to jump through all those hoops to install it to that extreme. As a thermal barrier on the exterior of the structure, it is a wise idea but beyond that, you would be better off putting your energy into other areas.
