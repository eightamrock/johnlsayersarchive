Soundman202029 November 2012#5
Hi "ts-12", and welcome! :) Just some quick answers and general comments on your build: 1) Photos of the area would help a lot so we can better understand what you are dealing with. 2) You apparently only have 6'6" of ceiling height to play with: that is VERY low. By the time you install your isolation ceiling and treatment, that is going to be down below 6 feet. I hope you aren't very tall! Also, it is not going to sound very good in there, with such a low ceiling. Therefore, I would DEFINITELY go with option "D": "use option C as temporary studio, and Build an addition to the house (see second sketch):" That certainly is the smart thing to do. It is not with spending much money on this place, with such a low ceiling.
1)Should I angle the CR/tracking room walls, or should I make it a rectangle?
You can splay your walls if you want, but to be honest I would not bother here: this place is not going to sound good anyway, no matter what you do, and splaying walls just complicates things and makes it slower / more expensive to build. So save the splayed walls for the future outbuilding studio, and just go rectangular here.
2)What Size would be best in this situation for the control room and tracking room?
For what purpose? What would be a typical recording session for you? A single musician with a midi instrument, or a full 6-man rock group with acoustic drums, keyboards, guitars, bass, and vocals?
3)How Should I acoustically isolate the studio from the furnace and upstairs noise?
With such a low ceiling, your only real option is to beef up the floor above you, seal it airtight, and hang a single layer of 5/8 drywall from resilient channel fixed across the joists, with fiberglass in the cavity. You don't have enough ceiling height to do any more. that takes care of the ceiling. For the walls, just follow the usual procedure for a fully decoupled two-leaf "room in a room". In other words, stud frames with drywall on only ONE side. That would be my basic approach. - Stuart -

Soundman20203 December 2012#8
I Carpet, removed ducts and will have hvac tech install new ducts so they wouldn't take up studio space.
I hope you meant to say that you REMOVED the carpet, not that you carpeted! Carpet is really lousy for small-room acoustics. If you were thinking of using carpet in there, then forget that idea completely. Carpet will harm the acoustics badly.
So I'm about to build walls
Where's your plan? What room ratio are you using? What is your isolation goal in decibels? How will you decouple the walls and ceiling? What is your MSM tuned frequency for the walls / ceiling? How much mass on each leaf? How big is the air gap? How much insulation? What type of doors? How will you seal them? Where's the design for the HVAC silencers? That's just a few of the questions that need serious, well-though out answers before you start building anything. - Stuart -

While Liquid Nails Glue is drying that I glued the ceiling joists and boards,
Liquid Nails is probably not what you should be using for that. It dries hard and might crack, and probably doesn't have enough mass. The best stuff is proper acoustic sealant, and second best is ordinary non-hardening bathroom caulk (the type that stays flexible and rubbery, even after it has fully cured).
I'm trying to figure out how to supply Air circulation into live and control rooms without compromising Sound "proofing".
Silencer boxes at all leaf penetrations. Search the forum for examples of how to do that. - Stuart -
