How much isolation ("soundproofing") do you need? That's the key to determining what materials and techniques you need. But in general, 1/2" drywall is too thin, to light, and too flexible to be good at isolating. 5/8" drywall is much better. Also, when you say there will be a gap between, 1" is not enough. The gap between the outer drywall and inner drywall needs to be at least 4". If not, then the resonant frequency of the wall will be too high, and it won't isolate low frequencies well. Also, that gap must be air, only air, and nothing else but air (or rather, air with insulation in it). In other words, there can be no hard, mechanical connects between the inner and outer drywall. For moderate isolation, you need either proper Resilient Channel, or RSIC clips plus hat channel. For higher levels of isolation, you need separate stud frames for each.
if i can afford it i'd like to cover the whole room in 1" 703 (covered with cloth)... is this a good or bad idea?
You can do that, yes: It is possible, but it will make the room overly "dead", and you will need other types of treatment to deal with that. In fact, there is a type of construction called "inside out" walls, which you could use. But your basic treatment plan looks reasonable, provided that those mineral wool panels are thick enough, the correct density, and placed at the right locations. And you will need a lot more of them! You will also need lots of bass trapping in that room, and additional absorption in the rear ceiling and rear wall.

Don't be embarrassed! It's important to post photos, so we can take a look and see if there's anything you might have missed, and could be improved. Nobody cares what the drywall and framing LOOKS like, as long as it is built correctly and sealed correctly: The goal is not to win prizes for aesthetically beautiful architecture!
