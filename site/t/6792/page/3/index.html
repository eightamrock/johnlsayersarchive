Things are going great, beside an aching elbow and the 25cm of snow :shock: ! This is way too early ! there I was shoveling, nailing, shoveling again. Anyway, got the first half of the ceiling (the slanted section) almost finished with framing, beginning the second half today. I had to use pulleys and cables cursing here and there :) , took me more time to set-up than to actually putting the joist into place. I had some help with the rafters wich was more than welcome. They were all nailed into place before putting the joist hanger. It was a great experience cutting the side ledger board at 6 degres vertical and 15 degres lateral in one operation and putting it perfectly into place. Man that was cool. 8) I had a sliding compound mitter saw of course. And above all I and knew exactly what were the angles, no trigo works, thanks to CAD. Got to continue.

I could have rented a real elevator but at 30bucks a day, I though I could use that money for even more drywall ! Gotta go, Got to work !

orangenumerik6 December 2006#61
Folks, now in the bad news departement... the so-called acoustic caulk i've used is cracking ! :evil: after only 5 months ! :cry: The caulk is cracking where it is more than 1/4 wide and exposed to air. I did a test a couple of months ago and in the back corner of the CR i've cover the caulk with a polyethylene film and it did not crack since then. But almost everywhere else it cracked. That may be a temporary solution. Anyway i'm glad i made a routine check before i'll be finishing all walls perimeters 8) . The stuff i used is made by Mulco it's gray and it comes in a blue tube with a white bold "acoustic" name on it. Don't buy it ! Around here the only acoustic caulk i could find was Mulco or bulldog grip. I'm going to try bulldog but my guts tell me i may get the same result as Mulco. Anyone knows where i could get USG acoustic caulk in Quebec Canada ?from what i've read about it seems like the real thing, not just something i'd rather call a vapour barrier adhesive.

orangenumerik26 March 2007#72
Back to work ! I was bumped to page 4 :shock: , That is lots of posts since my last update. The mixing project I was working on is almost over, client is happy :D , going to mastering soon. So, that means I have more time to finish the damn thing :twisted: . Here’s a couples of pict of the work I have done recently. The front wall framing is almost done, that includes the possibility of soffiting the monitors and having two slat resonators ( one above and the other under the CR window). For now the “soffits” will just be used as corner bass trap and the monitors will be placed on stands in front. I’ll give it a shot like that and see ( I should say hear) the result. The supermegaridiculouslybig superchunk bass trap (OK I am exaggerating a bit…) is under construction with the 6” of RW lined against the corner walls. I have also put the rear walls 4’X8’ high Freq absorber in place each side of the superchunk (no pic sorry). Those are centered at ear height when seating Now the room with all those traps in place sounds a lot better but still, it is not dead sounding, wich is, as far as I’m concern a good thing. One thing I’d like to share here is the difference the slanted ceiling make concerning flutter echo. When making a sharp sounds at the listeing point or where the monitors will sits, all I have is the actual sound plus a short rev. tail coming from the back of the room. But if I do the same thing near the rear corner superchunk, where the ceiling is flat, you can clearly hear an ugly flutter going between the floor and ceiling.(the only parallel sufaces in that aera). I knew I would have to have a cloud there, this is no big deal, but my point is : I have slanted the front portion of my ceiling the bare minimum (a little more than 6 degres) and I am surprised how much it is efficient. It is probably not that efficient at the whole spectrum with that angle but nevertheless it is doing something, without having to put Hi freq traps all over the ceiling 8) . It is surely old news for some or an obvious fact for others, but actually hearing the difference it make was a revelation for me. We all know the math and theories, but real life experiences is just priceless me think. My 2 cents.
