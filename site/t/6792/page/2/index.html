knightfly30 September 2006#39
Eric, it's about 3:30 am here and I'm about to drag my carcass off to bed, but I'll try to cover a couple of things - one, you're still confused about loading - dead load is ALL structural parts of the floor or ceiling - framing, paneling, insulation, everything that's ALWAYS there. Live load is things like people, furniture, appliances, pianos, etc - if you hang 2 layers of 5/8 wallboard that's about 4.8 PSF - 2x6 framing (necessary, as Mark pointed out) on 24" centers, weighs approx. 1.2 PSF - and 3" rockwool will add about 1 more PSF (assuming 3 PCF material) for a total of around 7 PSF. This means you need a dead load rating of 10 PSF, and doug fir 2x6's, #2 grade, 24" centers will span 10'4" - you have a bit of leeway here because you're UNDER 10 PSF, so this will meet span requirements. I'm also glad you're beefing up the center beam, since it will hold half the total weight of your ceiling. (The inner half of EACH half, approximately) Acoustically, whether you support your ceiling on ledger boards fastened to the walls or support it on TOP of the wall plates should make no difference. Structurally, I'd get a local engineer to look at what you want to do rather than take a chance on having the ceiling fall. Personally I'd NOT want to take the chance, although a 2x6 ledger board fastened to each and every stud with 16d nails (3 per stud) isn't going to go anywhere in this situation. Isolation-wise, the thing you do NOT want to do is tie any of this to your OUTER leaf mechanically... Steve

knightfly5 October 2006#42
Ceiling question - inner ceiling can tie to inner walls, difference in isolation between this and no hard contact will be nearly immeasurable. Outer ceiling can tie to outer walls, same thing - no problem. Inner ceiling frame connected to BOTH inner and outer wall frames - BAD BOY!!! NO NO !!! All the above are assuming that these two walls and two ceilings are your inner and outer leaves for isolation. If your inner ceiling is a THIRD leaf, it will detract from isolation BUT will also work well as a large bass trap, if you VENT in several places around the EDGE of the false ceiling and FILL above the false ceiling with fluffy fiberglass insulation - if there is enough space, you could also put bass hangers up there but it would be more work. Slats - you can guess what your modal problems will be, but that's ALL it will be with that shape of control room - better to measure after it's up. Also, calculating these using the AVERAGE depth is really just an educated guess - in actuality, unless you put a DIVIDER between each and every slat so that each slat becomes its own separate helmholtz, such a trap will have a strong tendency to AVERAGE its depth due to cross-talk between slots - this means that the end result will NOT be as wide a frequency range as if you had used separate dividers between slats, but it should be pretty smooth so it won't cause "bumps" in the room's response. Another thing - been reading quite a bit of this http://www.moultonlabs.com/main/ specifically this one http://www.moultonlabs.com/more/nick_ba ... interview/ And I've decided that this premise is a lot of why John's rooms sound good with slats on the (splayed) side walls- keeps the immersion thing going but "pulls out" some of the low mids to reduce mud in the sound. I've since decided that if you want to absorb early reflection points (on side walls) you should make these absorbers REMOVABLE and do a lot of A/B testing... Steve

I don't see any real problems there, I'm assuming that the single layer of wallboard at trusses will be your outer leaf and the new inner ceiling will be the inner leaf? I'd thought you had both leaves of the ceiling already there, and that the new innermost ceiling was to be a "false" ceiling which could then be vented along perimeter and filled with fiberglass for more bass trapping. IF this is to be your inner leaf, then don't vent it. The steel outer roofing (third leaf) will have some negative effect on isolation, but probably not a lot. No way to compute this that I'm aware of, just be aware of it. I'd also thought you were going to slope this inner ceiling - that would have helped with flutter echo between floor and ceiling wherever there is NOT a "cloud". Also, when reading the Moulton articles keep in mind that he's referring to a RECTANGLE - your rear corner MUST be treated as you intend, or at least covered with solid material if not rockwool - this, because a square corner will reflect any sound (above 500 hZ or so) back out of the corner PARALLEL to the entry angle - so draw a line from each speaker to your rear corner, "reflect" it across the corner and back toward the other speaker, and you'll see the problem - MASSIVE amounts of phasing/cancellation wavefronts in close proximity to the mix position, which can only serve to drive you closer to the brink of madness... I think I'd use that rear corner as a good place to store a MASSIVE amount of mineral wool (like a super-mega-uber-chunk) - in other words, fill the entire thing. IF this will be cost prohibitive, then at least line the walls and across the face with minimum 4" RW, and fill the rest with unfaced loose fiberglass. HTH, gotta run... Steve

With V3 and V4 you have the rear room ceiling angled down/splayed. I do not think this is a good idea. It decreases the available area for rear bass trap/absorber, for bass trapping back there the bigger the better. This decreases over all cubic feet of room making the room modes harder to deal with. The general recommendation when dealing with small rooms is to kind of forget about ideal dimension ratios, and make the room as big as you can. Angling the ceiling in front of room away from listening position as you show in V3 is a much better idea, as it helps kill flutter echo. Most CR's I've looked at get bigger towards the back, like a properly designed theatre. This makes sense to me. How ever if it were me I would build V5, regular level ceiling and add clouds with or with out hangers under false ceiling/cloud, and I think others would agree. Why? - Reflections between ceiling and floor are not as important as between side walls. - Cloud absorber will kill those reflections. - Level ceiling will be much easier to build. Figuring out all the (multi)angle cuts in combo with speaker soffits and splayed side walls & rear walls will be a small math project in itself. Then you have to actually make the cuts. - You could put some varied size hangers above your cloud, you could get a bunch up there. - It seems a cloud by itself rather then angling the ceiling should have just as good an affect if not better on room acoustics. I would do at least a 1:10 sloped HARD inner ceiling up to the cross-beam, leaving ceiling height at full behind the beam; that will spread out modal stuff a bit in the front half of the room, and make it so you don't HAVE to use absorbent over your entire ceiling - this will leave you the option of tuning the room a bit more to taste, instead of having no choice but deaden it with full ceiling absorption to kill flutter.
