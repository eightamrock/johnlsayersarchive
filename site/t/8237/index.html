Govinda20 February 2007#1
Hey guys! Ok here we go, I bought a house around a year ago, spent 6 months renovating the damn thing an exciting experience indeed! The cool thing about this house is that it has plenty of room underneath for a mixing room and a vocal booth but that's another chapter... It also has is a large round deck and underneath that deck is a very large water tank 4.6 internal length and a 3 meter internal height, 6 inch thick concrete!!! The tank's also sunk nearly a meter into the ground, and its next to the mixing room location! Because we have town water I decided it "could" be a good live room but we all know water tanks are full of bad reverb or good depending on which side of the fence you sit on haha! So where i'm at, the water was drained I then used a concrete cutter to cut a doorway in.. I have had a rough plan for a while so I knew where it had to go...it was very hard to cut took over 4 hours because of the concrete being so hard from having (i'm told) the water activate the lime for 40 years, then I shoveled out 2 feet high of mud from 40 years of it not being cleant out, once cleant it dried, then me and a builder friend john put a support beam in for good measure, engineered specified (the tank actually does'nt need it but we took the precaution). Then there was the rising damp and water proofing factor I wont go into it in full detail, but I have dug around the tank put plastic against the outside of the tank, agline then blue stone over it so the water can drain away to a lower point. The ceiling ever so slightly leaks but thats around 90% fixed thanks to a product called mr crystal which make concrete grow crystals in the cracks.. you paint it on, amazing stuff.. Then I hired a concrete grinder to level the floor off a bit and get some of the crusted gunk off.. OH THE FUN haha! next step was to lay some thick black plastic on the floor up to where the ground height on the outside would be, I duct taped it all together! step 2 was laying the floor battens down and making sure they are level etc... dynabolting etc...I did not use rubber as I dont care much for the feel and i'm not sure flanking with be a huge issue through the floor when its on 8 inch thick slab 3 feet under the ground.. SO that's where i'm at today! I have the insulation for under the floorboards so I have to deal with some electrics etc then lay the insulation in..I'm NOT building a room within a room as there would really be no space left by the end (currently its 20m2, 12m2 after internal walls if I did room within room) so i'm going to see how it goes for sound volume outside then if need be put an extra wall around of bricks with a 12 inch airgap etc. :roll: So what i'm really hoping I can get help is with acoustics!!! this is not a noraml calculation situation the room im wanting to sound live-ish but not quite bonham it's already bigger than bonham now haha! I have an acoustics plan so I will post pictures then write something more about my idea and if anyone would like to share or help out that would be Great! Thanks, Govinda

haha yes yes! the ceiling: I have a good idea but 1st I have to build bulkheads for the air ducts and lights, ceiling acoustics will comprise of angle panels, as of now inbetween the bulkheads.. i will try and draw this up in archicad to show my concepta.. as for the baffles there are only 2x1200mm wide absorbtion baffles for behind the drums, the rest are timber hermoltz slat resonators at 1200mm wide, they also diffuse, the use of rocks is to get rid of all concave walls in the room and disperse reverb evenly... it will be brightish sounding! concave is what gives this water tank the focusing 5 second reverb (concave focusses convex diffuses) its actually a great reverb kinda like a plate reverb..only usefull for one sound not versatile enough...I put some panels and panels in just to experiment one day and it is amazing how much the acoustics change, just a couple of absorbtion panels made the sound quite pleasant...something funny however is when you go to the centre of the room and speak or play guitar all the concave "focusing" comes at you at once its like surround sound reverb with short flutter echoes, you kinda hit a zone and its such an ununusual sound...my bigest challenge in this room will be low end and even reverb dispersion.. I may even have to do things slowly, measuring the room as I go... Painful! WOW hehe
