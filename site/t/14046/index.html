jhbrandt10 February 2010#2
George, Are the 2 X 6s structural? If so, I suggest (to increase room size) adding 2 X 2s to the bottom and top plates & using 2 X 4s stagger-stud in the wall. This will give you panel strength for the interior shell and the airspace needed and you will only lose 2" in the process. (I'll post a drawing if you need...) You will have flanking paths through the floor and ceiling joists anyway, so an independent wall on it's own floor plate would be over-kill. I would recommend against using the interior wall studs flat. This would be approximately the same as using 2 X 2s as far as wall flex is concerned. - More rigidity = more LF containment. What are your TL goals?? (TL=transmission loss) Have you analyzed your location and determined how much attenuation is needed from the wall construction? both ways... Cheers, John

Yes. However, if the interior framing is connected any anyway to the exterior shell, your isolation will be compromised. A staggered stud wall inherently has flanking paths. The design as drawn below will eliminate flanking paths through the wall and ceiling. A floating floor needs to have a resonant frequency of 10Hz and below to be effective and that requires quite a lot of mass. 8) Have you looked into spring isolators? Still, a structural engineer will need to check the building for that weight load... My suggestions to increase the room volume will compromise the TL of your original drawing/design. It depends on your design requirements and what compromises you and your clients are willing to agree upon. :wink: Cheers, John

soundgun wrote:
If you were to provide an accurate response, what information would I need to provide? Details on how the structure is constructed? Thickness of the floor joists?
The second one, which includes the third question. The main question remains of the desired TL. From what I can gather from the thread: - the first floor is a garage, - it will be used mostly as a play room, - low fequency (below vocal range) isolation is not significant for the recorded product, although it can impact the environment that is conducive to good performance, - limiting factor in overall NR is the large windows to the outside. Developing some numbers to the above data, recording levels may be as low as 70 dBA, noise from in the floor below may be as high as 85 dBA from children. Vehicles will not be running engines in the garage during recording. Background level target is 30 dBA. This starts to give specific design targets to work with. From the above numbers, 55+ dB TL is required in the range of children's voices. This peak's around 1 kHz. So what kind of floor construction meets that criteria and has 1.5" concrete in it, as I understnd is part of the current floor? The floor construction shown in TLF-02-045a pdf page 81 in NRC RR 169. That has TLs greater than 55 dB from 200 Hz up. What you have described is planned for the floor may already be good. Details are needed to confirm. Andre
