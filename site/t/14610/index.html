What laboratory test publication are they referring to, that shows that this will work? What are the published acoustic properties of this "self-adhesive rubber membrane" that they are talking about? How did they arrive at the frequency of 8 Hz for your wall? I'm not seeing that at all! Theoretically, the MSM resonance of your wall built like that is 28.87 Hz without insulation in the air gap, and 20.69 Hz with the insulation. Nowhere near 8 Hz! To get down to 8 Hz, you'd need to go to four layers of drywall over a 50 cm air gap, with insulation. It sounds like they are just guessing, and don't have much of a clue... It sounds like they are tying to do a poor man's substitute for constrained layer damping, but without any published data on the stuff they are talking about, its impossible to say if it stands a chance of working or not. While that rubber stuff MIGHT work, it also might not. a lot depends on the characteristics of the rubber. And in any case, adding a constrained damping layers does NOT work by lowering the resonant frequency of the entire MSM system: it improves isolation at the resonant frequency of the surface by damping the the resonance, but it doesn't affect the actual resonant frequency much. It lower the Q of the resonance, basically. So the claim by your drywall guys that it will "lower the plasterboard resonance frequency to 8 Hz" is pure garbage. The resonant frequency of drywall is way higher than that, and has very little effect on the MSM frequency of the wall itself. They seem to be VERY confused about how a wall isolates! Adding a thin layer of rubber to a sheet of drywall is NOT going to bring the resonant frequency of the drywall down to 8 Hz! :shock: Some people say that you can use normal roofing felt between layers as CLD, but I don't recall seeing any published figures for that either. So I'd say either go with Green Glue, which has been tested and is known to work, or don't do it at all, or just put on an extra layer of drywall and increase the air gap, if you are concerned about total isolation or MSM resonance. And I'd also say you can safely ignore whatever other "advise" your drywall guys might have to offer you, since what they are saying just doesn't make sense, acoustically.

Terracota with 2cm thick plaster is denser than tree layers of plaster stagered mounts and another tree layers.
Actually, thickness does not affect density (density is a property of the material itself), and there isn't all that much difference: density of terracotta is around 1900 kg/m3, and density of drywall is around 1200 kg/m3. So those six layers of 5/8 drywall are roughly the same mass as 3" of terracotta. That's a pretty good balance. But what is your point? There's plenty of mass and distance in there to get the resonant frequency down low enough.
One wouldnt believe the traction power the drywall would sustain once glued, milion more than nailed.
Glued to insulation batts???? :shock: And how exactly will "traction power" (whatever that is) manage to support the ceiling on only drywall and insulation, with no studs? The huge weight of that ceiling would simply crush the walls.
But if u want to outcome my sugestion post bether one's
Sure, no problem: Build it as a proper decoupled two-leaf MSM wall, using the beefed-up terracotta as one leaf, then build a stud frame a few inches away and put two or three layers of 5/8 drywall (as needed) on only one side of that frame, nailed or screwed and NOT glued (never!), put fiberglass insulation in the cavity (around 30 kg/m3), and do the MSM ceiling likewise, with correctly dimensioned joists spanning the inner-leaf top plates and supporting two or three layers of 5/8 drywall for the ceiling, with insulation on top.
drywall must no tuch floor so it can vibrate
Garbage! The entire inner-leaf will vibrate, including stud frame and drywall, and it will flank to the floor. The purpose of caulking the gap is to seal the wall airtight, not to decouple it from touching the floor!
the density of foam should be maybe 60,
No, it should be around 35 kg/m3 if it is fiberglass, or 48 kg/m3 if it is mineral wool.
insist in 100mm thikness

Putting insulation in the cavity does not prevent it from being a resonant cavity: it just damps the resonance, reducing the Q, lowering the resonant frequency, and improving the isolation.
