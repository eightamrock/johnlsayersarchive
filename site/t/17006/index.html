Whats the best way of jointing Gyproc sheets for multilayer studio plasterboarding- I'm assuming Joint cement: http://www.british-gypsum.com/products/ ... ement.aspx And Acoustic Mastic in the corners with Backer rod?
Soundman202010 September 2011#2
The normal recommended method here is to leave a slight gap between the drywall sheets, insert backer rod in the gap, then caulk over that. That gives you two independent flexible seals, and also keeps the surface density constant across the entire wall. - Stuart -

Soundman202011 September 2011#4
I was always led to assume taping and mudding
Tape and mud does not create a flexible joint: it is rigid, and therefore can crack, thus destroying the seal on that layer. Even a tiny crack will do that. Backer rod and flexible caulk remove this issue, creating two seals for each layer of drywall, and BOTH seals are flexible. So if there is any movement in the drywall after it is finished, the backer rod plus flexible caulk approach will not lose the overall seal. - Stuart -

Soundman2020 wrote:
I was always led to assume taping and mudding
Tape and mud does not create a flexible joint: it is rigid, and therefore can crack, thus destroying the seal on that layer. Even a tiny crack will do that. Backer rod and flexible caulk remove this issue, creating two seals for each layer of drywall, and BOTH seals are flexible. So if there is any movement in the drywall after it is finished, the backer rod plus flexible caulk approach will not lose the overall seal. - Stuart -
Stuart, With all due respect I have to disagree with this. Properly taped drywall joints are extremely stable - they do not crack....... this due to the covering for the joint used in the base coat. If cracking (which would allow the passage of air) was really an issue it would not be suitable for fire wall construction - where not only is the flame an issue - but the passage of hot gasses through the fire barrier. And this is true with even a single layer of drywall. Now - with a double layer of drywall all of the joints are supposed to be lapped when the 2nd drywall layer is applied. That means that in addition to the joint compound and tape covering the initial joint - you have the solid body of the 2nd sheet. There really is no reason for joints in the body of the wall need to be left open for backer rod and caulk. Rod
