Can anyone help with any issues I may run into by making the room narrower by a couple of feet? I would think that the space gained in the live room would help more than the space lost in the control room would hurt, but want to make sure.
Well, you can adjust your dimensions any way you want! But you do have to keep in mind two things (at least). 1) Room ratios. Make sure that your new dimensions give you a good ratio 2) Room size. The smaller the control room is, the worse it gets acoustically. EBU recommends 30 m2 floor area for a "high-quality sound control room", and 40 m2 for a reference listening room!

Soundman202024 January 2009#9
Yeah, I know what you mean about SketchUp! It's a blessing and a curse together! It took me weeks to tame the beast, and even then it still turns around and bites me every now and then! I wouldn't worry too much about the individual framing members: I made that mistake already. I'd suggest that, until you get your final positions for everything pretty much fixed, I'd just go with simple "boxes" to represent your walls. Be accurate, by all means, with the dimensions! Make sure that your "box" has the precise dimensions that your finished wall will have, but don't worry too much about the actual 2x4s or bricks or whatever until you are totally satisfied with the basic layout. Otherwise you'll get bogged down in carefully positioning timber, only to have to move it all 10 minutes later... Been there! Done that! So just try to get the overall dimensions and walls correct, including windows, doorways, etc., then later flesh it out with actual drywall sheets and framing members. - Stuart -

Maybe a view in my thread may help a little: look here and scroll a couple of lines down... Though I'm def a noob I think there are several good links concerning your questions about HVAC, silencers... To your questions "what is used": In Rod's book there's an example with an inline vent sucking the exhausted air into the exchange chamber. Due to the pressure difference (low pressure where you suck the air from(rec room or control room) and the high pressure created by the vent blowing more air into the exchange chamber) the circulation starts. In this example you don't need a thermostat/sensor. If you'd use two fans you'd need a sensor in order to "trim" both fans to work accordingly. Well...that is my understanding. But I'm not completely sure. Maybe this is at least a little helpful. Good luck. Carsten
