Soundman20206 March 2009#2
From the web site: "When Icynene® is sprayed into walls, ceilings and floors, building occupants are uninterrupted by airborne sound (human speech, stereos, and plumbing runs) and flanking sound (sound transmitted through the building structure)." How on earth is spraying foam into a cavity going to stop FLANKING noise? That makes no sense! To stop flanking noise, you have to decouple, break the flanking path. Spray-on foam cannot do that. There is also not one single bit of acoustical data in their technical specification sheet. They give plenty of data on the thermal properties (which may or may not be true), but nothing at all on the acoustical properties, and no explanation at all as to how it might manage to magically stop flanking noise! I think I'd be highly skeptical of those claims. When manufacturers make amazing claims that defy the laws of physics, then provide no data at all to support those claims, it's a sure sign that the product probably does not actually perform as advertised. If it did, they would proudly publish the figures. Since they don't, it makes you wonder what they are afraid of revealing... - Stuart -
