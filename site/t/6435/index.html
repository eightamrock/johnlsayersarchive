knightfly14 June 2006#3
To steal from one of my older posts - I file away (in my head) catchy little phrases all the time, to be later re-gurgitated as I see fit - the one that comes to mind here is "Good, Fast, Cheap - pick any TWO - you can NEVER have all THREE..." Where this applies is that decent sound isolation takes serious thought and quite a bit of mass - the more mass, the less portable. I've looked at the specs on the Whisperroom's and am NOT impressed - not that I could do better, it's just that "portable" and "soundproof" don't belong in the same sentence, essentially - neither does "temporary"... Now, if you can build your booth so it's basic dimensions are 3x5x6.5 feet inside, that will be about the best modal distribution you can get in that size booth. Muddiness shouldn't be a problem if you don't just foam the whole inside - Instead, you should start out with two adjacent walls covered from about midpoint up to the ceiling in 2 or 3" 3 PCF mineral or glass wool (Owens Corning 703 or equivalent) and another piece suspended at a slight angle from the ceiling. You can add more absorbent if that's too "live" - mic placement will also affect the sound; experiment here til you find what works. Placing one or more of the insulation batts across a corner diagonally can also help the sound quite a bit. As far as actual isolation goes, all the basics talked about in the REFERENCE section apply the same to booths as to other rooms, and a floor or ceiling or window or door is no different than a wall when it comes to mass-air-mass effectiveness. IF you have a lot of LOW frequency rumble coming in from the street, good luck; one way around that is to filter out any LF you don't need in the recording, this will help on vox tracks and a little on acoustic guitar. I use Samplitude, and have had fair luck killing noise with it's built in noise reduction algorithm with minimal artifacts - in most cases, can't even tell it was done. Gotta run, HTH... Steve

knightfly15 June 2006#5
PCF stands for Pounds per Cubic Foot, and 3 PCF is the density of the material - it works by all the little fibers inside trying to slow down the air movement, which converts some of the sound energy into heat. You can (and should) cover this with cloth, but everything you use needs to be able to pass air or it won't work the same. The angled ceiling pieces should be suspended (there are several methods of doing this, such as lightweight metal angle http://www.recording.org/ftopict-26889.html and more involved/aesthetic ways http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... 1&start=90 In a booth, the first should work fine. The idea here is to increase the distance from the FACE of the absorbent to the solid surface behind it, so it absorbs sound to a lower frequency - this makes the sound more balanced inside. As to danger, (from the REFERENCE section in this forum, which you need to check again :wink: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1742 YOu can also get more on acoustics at (you guessed it) the Acoustics forum - browsing thru all the stuff here will (1) answer at least a few of your questions, and (b) cause you to ask a lot more, both of which will lead to a combination of enlightenment and confusion :?
