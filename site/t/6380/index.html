knightfly16 June 2006#11
Keith is suffering from being beaten over the head by yours truly, regarding the "evil" effects of more than two leaves of mass in any sound containment structure - and he's right, ideally a SERIOUS sound containment structure would have two, and only two, leaves of mass separated by an insulation-absorbed air gap that's as wide as can be had - Sorry for the delay getting back to this, twixt all the alligators in my life some kind soul with strep throat apparently decided to breathe on me a couple weeks ago, so I've been pretty useless (OK, maybe not pretty but definitely USELESS) for a while... If your concrete slab is thick/strong enough, and if you need serious sound isolation, you can build two MORE leaves of mass INSIDE the steel skin; basically, you would put your outer mass as close to the skin as possible and make it as heavy as possible, then move in at least 1/3 meter and do it again. This for the ceiling as well, the object being (exclusive of the tin shell) two heavy masses separated by an air gap, with the inner mass damped by thick insulation batts. Outer and inner of these should have NO CONTACT with each other - any deviation from this will seriously reduce the isolation ability. There will still be a resonance (weak isolation at a particular frequency) caused by the tin/air/mass (going from outside to inside) but if this can't be helped, placing the outer mass as close to the tin as possible will raise this frequency so your OTHER, REAL sound walls can more easily contain it. (The lower the frequency, the harder it is to stop) To further compensate for the extra leaf, using more mass in either or both NEW walls will help. (Don't confuse the term "wall" with a typical house wall, which is already normally a mass-air-mass construct by itself) REad the first 2-3 topics in the REFERENCE section again and this should become clearer; HTH...
