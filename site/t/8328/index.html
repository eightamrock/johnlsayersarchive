You dont need backer rod on the beef up stage (between studs) unless your measurements are very innacurate or your studs are out of whack which makes it difficult to get the edges close. If you have gaps, put some backer in there, and caulk. Small gaps (less than 1/8") can be filled with caulk but check back on them after time and re-fill them completely as the caulk may shrink or pull away from the drywall. camistan wrote:
Any other opinions on question #1 above?
Don't know how 'out-of-line' this is, but I've been thinking if something like cementboard was used, there would be no need of worrying about crumbling of drywall -- in the event that IF it does become wet... Being on the outside of the wall -- even though it 'shouldn't' be exposed to direct moisture -- it is still possible that it could get wet if some sort of breach in the outside siding or sheathing occurs... or even if water makes its way between the wall from a different location/source (like, say, a leaky roof or leaky plumbing, etc...) Cementboard can get totally wet and dry in almost endless cycle (I believe), and of course, it is used often itself as (part of) sheathing in some cases. It's one thing to have to replace drywall that is visible; but it's another thing altogether to have to replace something deep inside of a wall system... Although there seems to have been some negative talk about cementboard, I can't see why it shouldn't be an 'at least' equivalent mass 'beef' as drywall is -- in fact, it's much denser than drywall is. The only likely sounding thing of possible question seems to be porosity... But poured concrete or blocks are also porous, and they appear to have very good ratings in use in M-A-M systems.... (the 'pores' are probably too small to pass appreciable amounts of higher frequencies, and are probably irrelevant for low frequencies). If anyone has more information about cementboard used in soundproofing -- especially to the contrary of above -- please post about it.... In fact, there's at least one company that uses cementboard (or Durock) in the manufacture of sound curtains that block highway noise from getting to adjacent residential areas... (naturally, they couldn't use drywall outdoors!) A downside of cementboard is cutting it, and fastening it (but I don't think you'd need special fasteners, since it's likely cleats will be used to hold it in place, no?)
