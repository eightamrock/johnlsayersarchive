ABOUT THE BUILDING: The studio is located in the centre of the city in a commercial/light industrial/residential zone, about 50 m (164 ft) from a busy street.. The house has two storeys and was built sometime in the eighties. It's really solid. There is a window in the studio area, and we really want to "cinderblock" it, partly because of sequrity issues, partly because a soundproof window (yep, there's no such thing) will cost a lot. And its a large window. The ceiling has 2 fake ventilation pipes going up to the attic. We tested them-they don't work :) POWER: The house is connected to industrial power (380V). We haven't done any measuring, but we were thinking about hiring an acoustics firm to take some measurements and maybe help us out a little. Don't know the about the cost, though. The studio area has no doors, so maybe things will improve a bit once we install proper doors. MUSIC: I'd say about 50% contemporary urban, 30% commercial rock music, 20% whatever we get our hands on :) OTHER WORK: Songwriting, arranging, voice-overs, jingles, cut-and paste mixing (for dance groups etc). We also might rent out the space for others to mix in, for example. LOUDNESS: I like to “crank it up” sometimes when I do my drum programming, so things get a little loud at times. SETUP: All we have at the moment is a top-notch PC, a pair of Yamaha HS80's, a largeYamaha keyboard (the one used for live gigs) and that's pretty much it :) I figured, we get the studio right first and worry about the gear later. CONSTRUCTION EXPERIENCE: We do have some experience and will be doing most of the work. We will only employ a construction worker to do the “tricky” stuff, heh. I cut it down to 4 but it's still too large. I can send it to anyone who's interested. Let's do this!

jwl5 March 2007#5
hello Kaido I agree with gullfo, the 2 rooms you drew were too similar in size and shape. I'd make a smaller booth and a larger room. If it were me I'd keep a vocal booth with full isolation between all rooms, but it doesn't have to be very big. Looks like a nice space you have there. You will get some isolation with gobos, but at the end of the day if you are looking to isolate your vocalist you will need a vocal booth. Gobos reduce sound somewhat, but with a rock band the vocals will still appear in the drum overheads, and everything will appear in the vocal mic (large diaphragm condensers are sensitive... lots of bleed, though you can play with the null points to increase isolation). Don't forget you can also isolate someone in the control room as well....
