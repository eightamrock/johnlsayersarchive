gullfo30 May 2008#8
sorry, then i think of angles i'm thinking off the front plane - 30°, but its really a 60° triangle all told. that said, these are recommendations because you have to check the monitor dispersal patterns as well as deal with your room space, baffle extensions to support the speaker cutoff response, and other factors. if you check the Genelec site, they have some very nice documents related to baffle mounting their monitors, acoustic center diagrams, and even CAD (DWG) files for their products. on isofloc - if i'm reading it right is blown cellulose insulation. you'll need something to bundle it up a bit like mesh or the light open fabric used on upholstery work. the framing could be metal or wood - ~2x4 (50mm x 100mm) w/ 2x 16mm drywall. the baffles can be 25mm MDF or some other dense rigid wood.

gullfo9 June 2008#10
normanbeats wrote:
1. the space above the Speaker box shall be filled loosely with insulation. In a post John wrote that this could be old cloth but can u also use mineral wool or would this stop the air flow?
you could use pink insulation or mineral wool (supported to avoid blocking the opening) but it depends on your budget. the lightweight insulation is likely cheaper.
normanbeats wrote:
2. what exactly is meant by "cloth Front"? Is this really just a piece of cloth like a thin carpet covering the front?
you want cloth which breathes. not a carpet.
normanbeats wrote:
3. john wrote that the acoustic hangers are "wrapped in insulation". what kind of insulation shall be used here? Mineral wool again?
could be dacron, pink, or could be mineral wool.
normanbeats wrote:
4. the front under the speaker shall be absorptive (especially for high frequencies reflected by the rack or console i think), so why is there a rigid insulation or 703?
absorption on mids as well as highs to ensure balance.
