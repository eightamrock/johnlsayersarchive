I took with me my sound lever meter set for A weighting slow response. Hitting a snare drum would result in a 70dB outside the container at 1m distance. With test tones from 100hz to 800hz and 105dB inside the container i measured around 70-75 dB outside depending on position.
If you had your meter set to "A" and "Slow", and you used a snare as a sound source, then you are not actually capturing the real sound levels of the snare. Snares (and the drum kit in general) have very large transients, with fast attack to very high levels, then a rapid drop to the longer, sustained resonant level. The real transient peak of a snare can easily be 120 dB, but measuring that on "slow" will only show maybe 90-something to 100-something. And since most of the energy of a snare is in the lows and low mids, measuring on "A" weighting is not taking that into account. You should set the meter to "C" weighting and "fast" response to see a more realistic measurement of the actual level of a snare drum. "A" weighting and "slow" response is usually what noise regulations specify, since it is better at picking up average sustained levels of typical low-level residential and light industrial sounds, but it doesn't give a good reading for nearby, loud music, which is what you need to check for studios. So I'd suggest that you re-check your measurements, using not just a snare and tones, but also a good studio monitor or other full-range speaker, playing typical rock music at about 100 dB (measured 1m away from the speaker), as your sound source, and with the meter set on "C" and "Fast" to capture transients, as well as "C" and "Slow" for getting a good idea of how people will typical hear the sound. But anyway, it seems that your shipping containers are giving you around 20 to 30 dB of isolation, and you need around 50 to 60 dB, realistically, based on your explanations.

I have never asked John, and I'm not even sure if he ever did sound level tests on Spark 1, but from the way it is built I would estimate that it probably gets about 50 dB of isolation, perhaps more. - steel studs (wood for floor)
Are you sure you need to float your floors? Might not be needed... How will the containers be set down on the ground? Will the go on concrete slabs? Concrete pads? Just rest on the dirt? Rest on runners? Something else? That's important.
- 1 layer 70kg/m3 5cm rockwool
For good low-frequency isolation, I'd suggest going with something that is less dense: around 45 to 50 kg/m3 would be good. That should be about 10cm thick, at least.
- 12mm and 15mm drywall sheets
Use only 15mm: 12mm is too thin, too flexible, the mass is too low, etc. So just do two layers of 15mm.
(MDF for floor)
Plywood would be much better. Use 3/4" (19mm)
- Auralex sheetbloc in between drywall panels
Not necessary. If you need high levels of isoaltion for low frequencies, then use Green Glue in between the layers (it is not actually glue at all! that's just the name...), but if you don't need extreme isolation, then you can leave that out too, and just use the two layers of 15mm drywall.
