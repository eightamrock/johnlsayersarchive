piginzen8 January 2007#1
Hello, Attached you will find my latest studio floor plan as it is right now. I started a studio in Washington DC and asked for your help here and everyone here was wonderful in helping us with the design. We've since moved to Baltimore and are starting another studio. This time with a budget and plans to actually take down and build new walls, if possible and if the budget allows. Right now any of the internal horizontal walls can come down, however to keep costs at a minimum we'd like to try and keep the walls by simply making them all non-parallel. The kitchen is up in the air and will probably double as more of an office. We'd also like to have a lounge as well in the front of the building. In addition to recording all sorts of music (avant, indie, R&B, hip hop) we'll be recording a percentage for broadcast (radio). I'd love to know what people think we can do with the space? The idea was to use the front room as the studio and the middle room as the control room, but now we are thinking the other way around as there is a lot of street noise at the front of the building and we'd like to keep the windows intact to it's historic quality. Also, we have 10' ceilings. And it's a 3 story row house office. The space above us is not occupied and will probably never be. There are tenants on each side of the building. We estimate the building was built in 1898. And the walls appear to be 12"-18" thick between buildings of solid brick and stone. And our mixing board is 7' x 3'3". And our budget is around $15-20,000 for parts and labor. I look forward to any insight people have and hope everyone had a good New Year! Mat Baltimore, MD PS. If you need or want pdf or adobe illustrator files, just ask.

Aloha Mat, Sorry, but I had to flip your plan. It was a bit wide, eh? You may find that your control room is a bit too shallow and that:
because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension. i.e. 6m x 5m. - John Sayers SAE website http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
Aloha 8)

John Sayers28 January 2007#18
What Ken said :) Import the jpg into sketchup and use the tape measure to measure a wall of known length. In the input box in the lower right corner you will see a measurement, type in the actual measurement and hit enter - it will ask you if you want to rescale the whole model and you say yes. Now the floorplan will be to scale. cheers john
