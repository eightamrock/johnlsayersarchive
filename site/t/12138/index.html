703 or Rockwool in the new walls? I should be most concerned with low-freq TL, I assume (kick drum, bass cab, etc). I know there's data printed, I've seen it, but I was wondering if I could get a user response?
It won't make a lot of difference either way, since the purpose of insulation in the cavity is to act as a damper in the MSM system. The insulation itself in an MSM system does not really absorb any particular frequency: it is only there to damp the cavity resonance, and it does that really well: Fluffy pink insulation or mineral wool work well in there. You don't need to go for expensive stuff: as long as it is open-cell insulation and the density is around 48 kg/m3, it will work fine.
Can I further decouple the 2 new walls by first resting the metal track for the studs on neoprene? Beneficial? Pointless? Dangerous?
That will help to seal the gap under the wall, but it won't really "float" your wall. For two reasons: 1) You still have to attach the sole plate to the floor somehow, and that largely short-circuits the neoprene. 2) Even if you don't attach the sole plate to the floor ( :shock: ) the chances are that your wall sill still not float, unless you do all the complicated mathematics and choose the exact right material for YOUR specific case, to make it float. This forum is littered with very short threads started by folks who came along to boast about "Gee. look how I floated my floor!", only to be told that there floor isn't actually floating at all, and that they just made things far worse by trying and failing... After hearing that, they normally don't have the intestinal fortitude to return and ask how to fix it. Because they know that the answer will be "Rip it all out and start again." Summary: Floating walls and floors is a major deal, needs careful calculations, careful selection of materials, and careful construction to make it work. The chances of getting it right by pure luck, just be shoving some rubber under the wall, are practically zero.
