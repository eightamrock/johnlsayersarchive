camistan7 April 2006#2
Welcome to the forum! I'm sure one of the moderators or Senior members can probably guide you better than me, but in building any type room you need to have mass for better isolation. What's mass you might ask, well for most of us here it's using drywall or some other material to stop or reduce sound transmission. Building a frame may also be necessary, but until you give additional information it's just hard to say. One of the first things I'd recommend is you try to draw up some type of diagram of the vocal booth itself. For instance how wide and tall will it be? Where will it be located? What will be surrounding it? What do you want to put in it? (mic connectors (XLR), 1/4" phone jack connectors, a light, etc etc). Also, are you in a house or apartment? All these things are important. Now, as to the fact you don't want ventilation...Hmmmmmm, I really wouldn't recommend that. You know, years ago I remember a guy that built a vocal booth in his bathroom. When he got ready to record vocals he would have to turn the air off because of noise concerns. His clients would sure need a lot of breaks coming out of that bathroom sweating and needing some "fresh" air. And what about if you need to record 2 people by chance? I know yours isn't in a bathroom, but the fact that it will be hot and stuffy in there still applies in your situation, plus it's unhealthy. (Just think if somebody passes gas in there... :shock: ahhh, the LINGERING aroma!). Believe me, it's one of those "fatigue" factors I've read about that makes for uncomfortable recording sessions. If nothing else, you need some type of exhaust plan to remove the stale air OUT. No ventilation? Think that one over R-E-A-L careful my friend. :wink: Here are some links I found in my files concerning vocal booths. I'm not sure they will help you, but maybe there's something in a couple of them that might give you suggestions. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... ocal+booth http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=12 http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=9 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5557 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... ocal+booth http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3164 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5557 Hope your project turns out well!

Canibus It would be real helpful for you to give more info to some of the questions asked in my other post. Believe me, you WILL be asked to do that by someone else trying to help you. Off the top of my head I wouldn't use a "blanket" in trying to reduce sound transmission, but as I said it's hard to say because more details are needed. For example, what kind of budget are you working with? ($100? $300? $500?) Suggestions are hard to give without knowing what you have to work with in dollars. Assuming you don't do the "blanket" thing, would you want a permanent booth or one that can be moved? Depending on which one you want, some building materials and how it's designed will most likely be different for a permanent booth as opposed to a "temporary-moveable" booth. Your 1st post said you live in a house. Ok, now picture this... Let's say you have a person who has P-O-W-E-R in their vocals and your recording session is running "HOT" with everyone in a groove. The time is getting LATE. Depending on how big your house is, the singers' voice will start to get pretty irritating to the others in the house if you have a blanket or a flimsy built vocal booth trying to stop sound. Have you considered this? I'm not trying to be "nasty", but I'm telling you this because I've seen a lot of people ask a question and give very few details which = S-L-O-W or N-O response. Hey, help us to help YOU...that's what we try to do on here! :D Go ahead and draw up an idea you have along with giving more details!
