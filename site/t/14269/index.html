Soundman202012 April 2010#2
He says to build the walls and leave the inside drywall (leaf) out and just install insulation and fabric over that. This sounds like it would be good for acoustical reasons but im concerned about isolation.
That is not an inside-out wall. In fact, it isn't a wall at all! It's just a large absorber... An inside-out wall just means that you put the drywall on the other side of the studs: it goes on the side facing the cavity, not the side facing the room. That is the only difference. You came to the right place: As far as I know, John Sayers is the guy who invented the inside-out wall, and this forum is the "home of the inside-out wall", so it would be hard to find a better place to learn how to do it right! The advice that your friend gave you is totally wrong. Just put your two layers of drywall on the "other" side of the studs. Make sure you have a large enough air gap (at least 4"). The studs will be exposed to the room, so you can use the stud space for whatever acoustic treatment your room happens to need: absorption, traps, slot walls, whatever.
My biggest concern about the way he was advising me was the wall facing outside (the wall with the window). Im not sure enough sound containment would be achieved. Should I just build a full wall there and leave the unfinished wall alone?
Your room has to be built the same on all six sides (or at least, five sides plus a thick concrete slab floor. Not building all walls the same is akin to building all of them like the weakest wall. Think of it like an aquarium: If you replace the glass in one wall of the aquarium with a piece of carpet, or a piece of cardboard, how well will it hold water? :) The same with your room: the isolation in the entire room, as a unit, is only as good as the isolation the you get from your weakest wall, door or window. The entire structure has to be built to the same level.
My floor: its concrete and im thinking about installing laminate with a throw rug. Would i be better off with carpet?
If you are going with inside-out walls all around, including the ceiling, then forget the throw-rug, and just leave a bare concrete floor, or bare laminate floor. And no matter which way you build. forget the carpet completely: Carpet on a studio floor is a bad idea pretty much always (unless you know the specific acoustic properties of the carpet the you will use, and they match exactly what you need in your room....) - Stuart -
