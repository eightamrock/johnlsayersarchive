If I recall correctly form one of Rod's recent posts, the vapor barrier always goes up against the warmest surface. So if you live in a cool climate where you normally have heating indoors, the vapor barrier goes up against your inner drywall. And if you live in a hot climate, where you normally have the air conditioning full blast indoors, then the vapor barrier goes up against the outer wall. I think! That's just what I recall. xSpace4 March 2009#13
The instructions on the kraft faced insulation clearly state the paper facing goes to the warm side, which is always considered to be the inside of the structure, even @ Exit4. Which is also why Rod said it. It doesn't change because it is a recording studio. What it does is it makes an "exploded" view. Where once you had a single wall with a thermal envelope, you now have an exploded version where the same thing, in this case, applies. Unfaced insulation on the exterior, kraft(retarder) on the inside. Plastic is a barrier, as the post mentioned with Rods input up topic was about a plastic barrier.
