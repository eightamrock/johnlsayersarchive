myfipie26 August 2008#2
How about if I just hang some 703s up in the ceiling, so it would be hung with wires, like clouds, with an amount of distance from the ceiling, maybe varying the distance between each 703s? Will it good enough to absorb bass and lo mid frequencies, or still I have to use cavity box?
Putting a frame around the panel (cavity box) has nothing to do with how it performs. It is the rigid fiberglass that is doing the work, so hanging from a wire will work fine. Keep in mind though that just spacing panels off the wall will NOT be enough. You really want to straddle corners also with the OC703, making the panel 4" or more thick.
And more question, I assume from what i've read from the realtrap website, is that the more bass trap you put, it won't make the low being absorbed too much, instead it will make the low frequencies more even in the frequency range. Is that true, or am I missing something?
Well to say you could never have enough bass trapping would not be true, but in most small rooms you would run out of corner space before you installed to much low frequency absorption. I would not worry to much about over doing it. You can though over absorb the high end and that is why products like Real Traps and ours are designed to absorb more low end then high end. Glenn :D

bolehnggak26 August 2008#6
So that means, if I use 4" rockwool, the distance between the ceiling and the rockwool is also 4", is that right? Also, another question, what if I angled the rockwool so it won't be parallel to the ceiling and floor? Is it a good idea?
jwl27 August 2008#7
Yes, a good rule-of-thumb is to have the panel spaced from the wall/ceiling the same distance as the panel is thick. So a 4" panel should be 4" down from the wall/ceiling. Angling shouldn't make much difference either way. Simplest is to just keep the panel at its optimum spacing throughout its hang.
