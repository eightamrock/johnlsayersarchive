FPR wrote:
- What converters you used during the test?
M-Audio Delta 66.
Why you choose Mackie HR824 ? Since they aren't the most precise ones you can find for this job.
Not so! Mackies are very flat - much flatter than many more expensive speakers. But the main reason I used an 824 is because it was readily available. I could have done this test in my living room where I have a killer sub that's flat to 18 Hz. But then the microphone would not be on-axis with a single speaker cabinet. More to the point, what really matters is not how flat my room or speaker are, but how well the inexpensive microphones track the expensive microphones that are known to be accurate. The whole point of this test was to answer the people who ask me all the time if the ECM8000 is any good. So I decided to buy an ECM8000, then someone pointed out the Nady, then I found the dbx. Before I knew it, a bunch of friends volunteered their own microphones too. :) --Ethan PS: Thanks Bert, that means a lot coming from you.

Anyone know any phantom protection circuits that could be utilized with these cheap mikes?
Phantom power was designed so that non-condenser mics would not get damaged. something was wrong in the circuitry if it got damaged by phantom power. Answering your question directly, just put a couple of 60V capacitors in the signal lines. Andre
