Hi all - I recently came across this site and have been reading some of the material here which is very informative. I used to work as a live sound engineer in my early 20's about 13 years ago, but have done very little sound work since. I work in IT now and have decided to get into recording, and so build a little studio in my garden. I don't have much studio experience so will be learning about the whole recording side as well as the studio construction side. I initially intend to maybe record some bands that friends are in, with the possibility of doing it more seriously and getting some money if I get good enough and my facility is good enough. The following is my current situation and I'd be grateful for any advice given. I have a semi-detached house on a residential street on the northside of Dublin city (Ireland) which has a back garden approx 14 metres long by 9 metres wide. I'm hoping to build a small studio with a toilet/shower and living space, ie small living /chill out area with couch and tv. I have neighbours on each side and also at the end of the garden, so sound-proofing is very important. I envisage the music played being standard electric guitar/bass/drums/keys. I've enlisted a friend who's an architectural technician and he's going to do up some plans, however he doesn't have any experience in soundproofing so he's doing as much reading up as I am. My budget would ideally be 30k euro, but could probably push to 35k max if necessary. We're going to attempt to come up with a few different layouts and try to estimate what they might cost. So the (very) basic requirements are: - studio: live room, control room, and drum/isolation booth. - living space: lounge area, toilet with shower. (Toilet to be accessible from recording studio also) Our first option is to build across the bottom of the garden, so a rectangular structure 9 metres wide and 4 - 5 metres deep incorporating the control room, live room, and living space. Another layout would be an L shape with the studio across the bottom using the full width and the other part as the living space. For the first rectangular option across the bottom of the garden I've uploaded a 3D model of how it might look from the outside. I also uploaded a picture of the garden taken from the kitchen looking out, a picture of the back of the house from near the end of the garden, and a (very) basic sketch of how I see the layout of the studio (I intend to knock both sheds shown in the pictures). I've downloaded Sketchup but I'm pretty useless at drawing and need to spend more time at it, so apologies for the poor quality of the drawing. Also I should add that I'm looking for ideas on the layout as well - this layout may or may not be a good way to go so I'm very open to suggestions. Hopefully I've provided enough detail. Some questions: - Is a block build the best way to go, or would it be possible to soundproof a wooden/log cabin type building (which are cheaper to buy)? - What's the best construction method...will an outer block wall with inner stud wall type construction suffice, or will I need a double block construction with cavity? (A double block will use up quite a bit of space, and I don't have a lot to begin with). - If I go with block build should I avoid parallel walls ? - Are windows out of the question regarding soundproofing, or will it be possible to have some? (Don't like the thought of no natural light) Cheers, Ciaran.

clunk5 June 2008#9
Hi Ciaran, first thing block would be better than wood, sound isolation of low frequencies requires mass. Second you need to go the room in room route to maximise isolation so basically build your shell from block and then stud walls inside with wood or metal studs and gypboard on one side only, you should also look to a design where the rooms have their own independant ceilings. You have to consider the roof and get as much mass here as reasonable. Stick to a M/S/M( mass/ spring(air+insulation) / mass ) system throughout to achieve the best isolation. You might also be better posting your designs in the design section of the forum, you might get more help there. Good luck
