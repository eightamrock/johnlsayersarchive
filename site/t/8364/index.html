Furring channel on ceiling?

baranstoll7 March 2007#1
We've built a free-standing 12' x 9' room-in-room on basement slab. Worst noise is low frequency footfalls from above. Ceiling framing is 18 gauge sistered pairs of steel 2"x4" (screwed back to back) 24" OC on 9' span. We're going to use 3 layers of 5/8" sheetrock under the joists and 3 layers of 2" Owens Corning 703 in and above joists. We were going to screw sheetrock directly into joists, to achieve a stiff mass. Then we hired a sheetrock sub because we're exhausted, and he wants to attach 3/4 " deep U-channel 16" OC perpendicular to joists and hang rock on that. (Ceiling height is an issue, too.) Questions: 1. Will the channel help or hurt preventing low frequency transmission from above? 2. Is hanging this much rock on channels safe? Sorry to ask since RC has been discussed so much here. I have to say I'm still too confused to apply what I've read and reread to this case. Thanks very much.

sharward12 March 2007#4
Very short on time here... Family emergency going on. The critical question is... What are these new ceiling joists attached to? Are they attached directly and firmly to the floor joists from the living space above? If so, that's not going to improve your footfall problem very much, if at all. You have to decouple upstairs from downstairs. The best way to do this is to have separate inner leaf framing attached to the basement floor, and then firmly attach new joists to the top plates of the new walls, having no firm connections between the new room and the existing structure except for the floor slab. You only described the room's width and length, but not its height, so at this point we don't even know if what I have proposed is practical or not. Buy Rod's book -- I would actually highly recommend buying it and being completely certain of everything you need to do before construction begins again. You are at a critical point in your build where mistakes are virtually unrecoverable. You'd be wise not to paint yourself into a corner, as one bad move (or a conspiracy of many) could lead to a disasterous result. --Keith :mrgreen:
