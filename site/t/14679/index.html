Soundman202011 August 2010#2
Hi Lukas, welcome! Well, your basic layout looks OK, but with such a small room you are going to need some serious bass trapping, and especially so, considering the square shape! The modal analysis for those dimensions is not good at all. I'd suggest you try to add more bass trapping, thick and deep, especially in the room corners. Another thing I'd do is to get the speakers off the desk, and mount them on massive stands, right up against the front wall. You don't have enough space in there to do much else. - Stuart -

Most likely that wont do much: it is probably closed-cell urethane based foam, which isn't a lot of use, acoustically. Soundman202012 August 2010#7
Do basstraps have to be in an angle of 45°?
No, not necessarily. Put them at whatever angle allows you to make them biggest and deepest. It's the total volume, depth and area that matter. Angle isn't very important.
but the traps there are only 25cm thick, If I could change the angle I could broaden them.
Then change the angle. 25cm is not bad, but more is always better.
I now also put a basstrap behind the bed and the desk.
Excellent!
Is it good to hang traps above the listening position?
Yes, but they are not really called "bass traps" if you put them there, since they accomplish a different purpose: The control your first reflections, which is good. In addition to bass trapping in the corners, you should also put absorption at all first reflection points in the room. That will help to improve your stereo image. - Stuart -
