drfrankencopter28 March 2007#2
We're talking about an above ground installation here, right? The advantages of a seperate room within a room type construction are twofold. First, there is no direct coupling path between the inner wall and outter wall...ie.. vibration must be converted into sound, and then vibration again to get to the outside. and second, the air gap has a profound effect on the low frequency transmission loss. Will there be a huge difference between these two types of construction...not at high frequencies (voice range), but at low frequencies the seperate wall construction will be significantly better. Cheers Kris
cjbdrm28 March 2007#3
I think it all depends on your noise reduction requirements. I would think more information regarding your situation would be needed. where are your neighbors, how loud will you be, etc. If you live in a neighborhood with houses ten feet from your studio, build the room. If you live on a farm, don't build it. If you're recording live drums the 'room within a room' is the only way to block the lower frequencies to the level you need, assuming you don't live on a farm somewhere. Similarly, I'll probably be beefing up my interior garage walls with a layer of Green Glued drywall. Then building a 'nothing fancy' single 2x4 stud frame with 2 layers of 5/8" sheetrock each side bolted to the concrete floor 'within' the room...(but far enough away as to not create a 'third leaf' effect.) Maybe using Green Glue again between the interior drywall sheets. This might be overkill, but I'm not willing to take the chance.

If I might jump in here......... the bottom line is this - especially if you have neighbors 20' away...... single wall construction will ALWAYS lose when it comes down to low frequency and flanking - that's just the nature of the beast. I don't care about mass (even single concrete walls won't get the job done - that's why in basements you still have to build inner walls - otherwise you could just mass up the ceiling and be done with it). and the green glue helps miles - but not enough on single stud walls....... Look at the green glue walls - single stud - and picture your bass player wailing away at 100 db - with a TL value of 23 @ 80 HZ - that puts sound levels outside your house at 77dB and your neighbors only 20'away.. and you still have drums to add in - (just LF for now) and it get's worse as the frequency levels drop...... now let's compare that to a double 2x4 wall - 24" centers - double 5/8" before any green glue application - this is taken from IR-761 - Test ID #TL-93-283........ AT that same 80Hz you now have a TL value of 36.6dB..... that's an increase in isolation of 13.6dB - so (just to get a perspective..... the 1st 6dB cut the volume in 1/2 - the next 6dB - in 1/2 again - and the last 3.6 cut's it by another roughly 30% Over a 200% improvement in isolation just by seperate walls - and ou haven't even added green glue into the equation yet...... I hope that helps........ Sincerely, Rod
