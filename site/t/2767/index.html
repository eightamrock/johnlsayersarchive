Hi John, I've got a bit of time at the moment so I'll try to help you.Please note that I'm no expert at this, but can offer some advice, so certainly consider what others have to say :)
1) Where is a good place to go online to study soffit mounting speakers? Once you set the size of the space for the speakers, are you stuck with that size – so you can’t upgrade monitors? Also, where do the wires go through? I’m still reading up on soffit mounting.
http://www.genelec.com/support/faq/faq19.php http://www.genelec.com/support/faq/faq20.php http://www.genelec.com/support/soffit.php Also check Paul's studio build diary here: http://forum.studiotips.com/ He's just recently been building his soffits.
2) Would building amp and drum platforms decrease the amount of noise leaving the room, or would the difference be very small?
It depends on what you're planning to do with the whole floor I guess.Sorry if you told us earlier what you're doing with it and if I missed it,,..but...If you're floating the floor in your recording room (where you'll play drums and your amp-ed stuff) then platforms shouldn't be necessary.If you're not floating the floor they would probably help a to isolate the low frequencies a bit.
3) Instead of building a hanging ceiling, would treating the floor above be effective? I was thinking of treating the floors above instead of doing the hanging ceiling, to avoid the difficulty of building the ceiling and also losing the room height, as well as saving costs (possibly, though I haven't budgeted yet). I don’t know if I’m able to handle building a suspended ceiling, time-wise and skill wise. But I could do some work to the level above the studio
well it depends on what you mean by "treating the floor above"? How would you treat it?If you think it would be easier to work on a floor than on a ceiling then you could maybe consider floating the floor above and isolating up and down-stairs.
4) Is a carpet floor to be avoided? I have read about problems with carpets absorbing mid-frequencies only. I would like to build drum and amp platforms, but for the whole room, would eliminating the carpet be a good idea?
Again depends on what your other surfaces will be covered with.Carpet I would think absorbs more of high and high-mid frequencies than just mid, but yeah you've got the right idea of its effect. Timber flooring is probably nicer (looking and "sounding") but again it might be expensive...So i guess this one depends on a few things. I hope this helps a little :) cheers

It looks like once you soffit mount the speakers, you are locked into that size.
No John you don't have to be "locked".When you go back and read some more (if you do :) ) of Paul's thread you'll notice that he's provided for his upgrade in future.You can just build your soffits with some bigger speakers in mind (check dimensions of some potential upgrades for you) and then build a smaller box to house your current speakers and put it inside the soffits.The air gap between the "2 boxes" would probably be filled with insulation to kill any possible resonances between them.If you build a stiff enough wall, you'll be fine :)
Also, is another benefit of soffit mounting increased soundproofing of the speakers? If the speakers are decoupled from the walls, and the soffit structure is filled with mineral fiber, it makes sense that a lot of noise transmission would be cut down.
Yes the transmission would be greatly reduced with a firm soffit wall.Also if you have a massive stiff wall, no sound energy (or very little) will be lost in those vibrations.So you'll be able to hear your speakers' lower freequencies without any energy beeing lost on the way (into the soffit wall) Ideal soffit wall would probably made out of concrete or bricks, but for a lot of ppl it's impossible to build in small home/project studio setups.
The main benefit would be for recording purposes, not necessarily sound isolation purposes – or would it?
John, the drum/amp risers or platforms are usually built exclusively for isolation purposes.I don't think your quality of recorded sound would improve or degrade because of them.Instead, if you're not concerned with isolation, spend the money and effort into acoustical treatment, which will surely improve the sound quality.
Would putting a slight angle on the wall with the window (see picture above) make enough of a difference to be worthwhile?
Seeing how much room you have to work with you'd lose some valuable space.If breaking up the parallel surfaces is what you have in mind, I would just try to break up that "window wall" with some diffusion or something.You could also heavily absorb it :? ...maybe... :) cheers
