Soundman202018 November 2013#3
Hi Harmen, and welcome to the forum! :) It sounds like you have a lot of good things going for you in that new space: the concrete floor and high ceilings, for example, are very useful. Regarding your basic question, I certainly agree that option 1 "stairs up then stairs down" is not really a good idea at all, and you would very quickly get tired of that! It also makes your control room squarish, and wider than long, which isn't so good for a small room. So I would forget that option. I would also forget option #2: kid running through your studio is not an option. That leaves you with option #2: leave a corridor for access to the kitchen, with a slightly narrower control room. That' the best of your options, in my opinion.
I read a post from John that the optimal size for a controlroom is about 6m from front to back and 5m wide with enough height to accomodate decent ceiling treatment.
True, but there's a LOT more to it than just that! In fact, the EBU recommends a floor area of 30m2 "for a high–quality sound control room", but the ITU recommends "20 m2 to 60 m2". Neither specifies shape, width, length, or height (except to say that the room should be symmetrical, left to right), because all of those need to be calculated for each specific room, based on the design concept (RFZ, LEDE, CID, etc.), the room ratio, and several other factors. Having said all that, those recommendations are just guidelines, and it is entirely possible to build very good rooms that are much smaller then the ITU or EBU recommendations. For example, John has built an entire studio inside a shipping container, with excellent results. So the actual design of the acoustic layout and treatment can compensate quite a bit for a room that is not "optimum" size.
A studio that is 7m wide and 4.2m deep is an option… maybe.
Or turn it sideways, and make it 7m long and 4.2 wide! That would be a much better option. In general, it is better to have your speakers firing down the long axis of the room, rather than across the short axis (unless you have a large room). So I would suggest using a layout that has you facing the live room directly, and with your control room laid out lengthways, not sideways.
2a. If I turn the whole thing around: can a room that is sufficiently deep but only 4.2m wide give me a great mixing environment?
Yes! 7m long, 4.2m wide gives you about 44 m2, which is much more than the minimum specified by both the ITU and EBU. And since you also have high ceilings, you should be able to get a good total volume in there too.
2b. Is there a way to get daylight in this space?
There are probably several options.... :) - Stuart -
