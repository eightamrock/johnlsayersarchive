sharward15 February 2007#2
Welcome to the forum and congrats on the home buy. :-) Truly a plaster wall, as in plaster and lath? If so, you're probably best off leaving it intact and building a single leaf three layer drywall wall parallel to it a foot or so in... Same with the ceiling. Yes, it's three leaves that way. but given your isolation requirements and the assumption that you're looking at plaster and lath, that seems to make the most sense. We'll see if others agree. Good thinking on the slab... My thoughts exactly. Keep us posted with progress and questions. --Keith :mrgreen:

jwl15 February 2007#4
I replied to this same thread in the Design forum..... not much to add to what Sharward wrote, just remember that "Leafs" are defined by Mass separated by Air... if there is airspace between the layers of mass, then you have multiple leaves. If all the mass is touching/connected, it's a single leaf....
sharward15 February 2007#5
Looking forward to seeing the pictures. FYI, I know people usually call them "roll-up" doors, but is it truly a "roll-up" door like this? Or is it a sectional door like this? The reason I ask: If it is a roll-up door, it would be somewhat easy for you to do a three leaf "big door wall system" using two new walls parallel to the roll-up door opening. The roll-up door could be left open when the studio is in use, which would eliminate the third leaf effect. It would be good for security when the studio is not in use. If it's a sectional door, you're going to have a harder time of it, because the tracks will extend over 7 feet into the garage and only be 7 or 8 feet high. :roll: I look forward to seeing the photos... They'll be worth thousands of words. --Keith :mrgreen:
