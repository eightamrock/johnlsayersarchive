The ducts in my current studio make a whooshing noise when the fans are turned up above about halfway. I've always presumed that that was because of the airflow itself, but it may be that it's just the mechanical noise of the fans being transmitted. They ARE very loud fans. But why not insulate inside a duct? I have seen three types of product that go under names such as "insulated ducting" or "acoustic ducting". The first is (usually flexible) ducting with an inch of rockwool or similar wrapped around the outside, followed by a layer of aluminium to hold it on. The second is like the first, but the inner layer of ducting has perforations in it. I've only seen this once, through an Ebay shop - and they were careful to call this "acoustic ducting" and claim that it gives greater soundproofing than the kind without perforations (which may be bullshit). I presume the perforations are so that the air passing through actually makes contact with the insulation. The third is solid ducting with foam stuck to the inside. This is basically what I'm talking about making, only for the reason that they don't seem to make it in wider duct sizes. It seems to me there are two different issues here: One is the amount of noise MADE by the air moving through the duct. Airflow against a rockwool or foam surface is likely to make less noise than airflow against a plastic surface, isn't it? The other issue is preventing the noise from escaping once it has been made, which would be achieved by the insulation around the outside of the duct in the first type of product, or Ted's idea of boxing it in. But it seems like if you can prevent the air from making as much noise in the first place, that's gotta be good.

bassman10 August 2009#6
I would also add that you can effectively create "silencer" boxes through careful planning of your supply and return paths. If you make it so that adjacent rooms have supply paths that are far enough away from each other, that makes a silencer box via all the turns in the pathway. This avoids the efficiency loss created by a silencer box. Every turn in a duct creates resistance in airflow that will reduce the CFM into that room and build up static pressure in the system that the blower has to overcome. Also, once the duct is at or in the room, double its size to slow the air down and quiet the airflow itself. Then box the duct in a soffit and run it inside the room a bit before venting out. Use real good vents and big ones! -ashley
