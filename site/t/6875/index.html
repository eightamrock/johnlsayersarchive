knightfly23 August 2006#3
Rockwool and rigid fiberglass are acoustically interchangeable by density; so 3 PCF is optimum for acoustic treatments. In most areas fiberglass of that density is more expensive than rockwool/mineral wool/slag wool. For inside a wall, 2.5 PCF (like Thermafiber SAFB) is ideal for music, but standard weight (around 0.8 PCF) fluffy fiberglass will only lose 1-2 dB at most, and is quite a bit cheaper usually. For outside walls, paper faced fluffy fiberglass should go paper out for hot zones (more air conditioning than heat) and paper in for cool zones (more heat than air conditioning) For inside walls, (between rooms) use NON-faced insulation. For double frame walls, it's best to use fiberglass strapping across the inner edges of both frames and a slight compression of insulation when the first layer of wallboard goes up; this keeps some air flow possible within the wall for any drying that may be necessary, it keeps the insulation from sliding down in the stud cavities, and it helps keep the insulation against the panel faces for vibration damping which improves isolation a few dB. HTH... Steve

knightfly3 September 2006#11
Calling anything Johns Manville makes "OC 703 might get them sued; OC stands for Owens-Corning, a completely different company. Acoustically tho, nearly any mineral wool, mineral fiber, rigid fiberglass, slag wool, rock wool, sheep wool, cotton wool, etc, have so close to the same characteristics that they are interchangeable. Any of these in the SAME DENSITY will give very close to identical results, with possible SLIGHT differences in absorption characteristics - but if you follow the accepted practices of mounting, placement, etc, seen on these and most OTHER acoustic related (NON-commercial) sites, you'll be fine. One exception - products like "duct board" usually have some sort of coating applied to minimise shedding of fibers, since they're intended for use inside of DUCTS - this will change at least higher frequency absorption, so this should be taken into account when calculating TOTAL room absorption at all frequency bands... Steve
