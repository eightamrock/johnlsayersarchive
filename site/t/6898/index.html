Control ceiling designs?

Started by donrice on 25 August 2006. 1 reply.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6898.

donrice25 August 2006#1
The standard options seem to be either flat or raked upward from front to back. I was considering angling the ceiling in an attempt to reduce standing waves and flutter. I also believed that angling the ceiling away from the mix position would reduce early reflections at the mix position. Since the control room in question is 22 feet long, anything other than a slight angle creates a large drop in ceiling height. I considered an "A" style design (Front to Back) acknowledging that the area beneath the peak may require some additional treatment. A designer and a couple of recording engineers have weighed in and say that an "A" is the worst thing one could do. I have a hard time understanding why angles in the lateral dimensions is desirable, but skewing a surface in the vertical dimension is not. What gives? Any thoughts? The unfinished height is 12 feet. My initial design would have resulted in an angle of just under 8 degrees on each side of the peak. Depending on who you talk to, 6 to 12 degrees is necessary to achieve any benefit. I concede that room response and treatment calculations are far easier if the room is rectangular. The walls are already constructed and are "A" shaped on each side with an angle of 6 degrees on each resulting in a 12 degree flare. Thanks in advance for any clarity that can be offered.

Anything over 6 degrees will help with flutter echo; for RFZ without absorption you need geometry.
