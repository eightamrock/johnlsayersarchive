xSpace20 May 2009#31
Pure L wrote:
What I'd like to know is what would be the best way to fill the gap in the middle?
It depends on if you intend to paint the door or sand and reseal. Making a plug to fill the void will require some finesse with @ minimum, a table saw and mitre saw. MDF is a good product for what it does, but it would not fill the hole in this beautiful hardwood door and allow for a quality finish...unless you painted it. The door is probably some where in the area of 1 7/8 inches thick. Based on this measurement you would use two pieces of 3/4 inch material with an inner layer of 3/8 inch to get this 1-7/8 inch thickness. (It could be thicker depending on the existing fire rating of the door.) The difficult part will be trying to match the grain of the veneered wood species if the exact wood product is either not available or unknown. If all the above conditions fell in line, then cutting out the widest width and height of the mentioned wood products and glue/clamp the parts together would be next. After a good 24 hour set up you can establish what the rabbited dimensions are from the existing hole and set the table saw rip fence to allow you to cut first the depth of the cut (on the side of the new panel) and second the width(on the face of the panel). I would glue the plug into the hole and clamp it in place, no nails. If this is too complicated you may want to enlist the services of some one local to apply this patch or even do a complete veneer. It can be done, Brien

TheFrenchVoice26 June 2009#43
I concur with Ro: do them yourself! Standard MDF density is 0.72 (720kg/m3), the density of many hardwoods. There is also HDF, with a density of about 0.95 (close to an average ebony density). A door made with 4 sheets of standard 25mm MDF is 150kg. This, combined with a good multi-seal system, should be not too shabby. Well, I hyope, since this is what I'll have! You'll need three specialised hinges per door (they each cost a small fortune but that's what you need) and a reinforced steel frame to hold them in place as strandard door frame and drywall structure will bend and not behave very well when you open the doors - make sure you place the hinges properly, too. Only thing with MDF is that although it's stable it will bend if you don't keep your stock properly, partly because of its weight, partly because it behave like a sponge with ambient humidity. Good luck!

One of the issues you will have is that the hollow core door becomes thicker then the jamb when you start adding layers of mass to it. Also it may not stay seated on the hinge leaf due to the inner hollow core door not being thick enough and the screws passing through the wood into the cavity, not getting enough of a "bite" of wood to secure the heavier door. A wooden frame is better for your jamb as far as continued mass. But these things all hinge(pardon that!) on if you have the ability to build a door and a frame. It is tedious and exacting work.
