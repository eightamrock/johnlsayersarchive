Ro8 May 2009#2
good question. Consider the door a part of your wall; so it needs MASS as well. You could make a door by sandwiching MDF/GB/MDF for example. Insert a sheet of lead if you got the budget.. GB and MDF are best mass withing a small budget. Steel, lead and other metals are more expensive, but also heavier. Doors are funny cuz they make a whole in your wall when they open. Damn, we don't want no holes. Sealing of the door jamb is a must and, since you use multiple sheets of mass, easy to make. If I find the time this weekend, I'll post a sketch-up drawing. Check the build diaries (note the "finished builds" sticky) for doors.

Ro9 May 2009#6
Here's a super-heavy door I designed earlier. The mass should atleast be equal to the wall the door's going to be in. Using a 3x12mm GB wall and hanging this door in looks like overkill. That is, if yer're gonna use 2 doors (make an airlock) This door is more like a super door when only using 1 door. You see the front, back and the detail (from above). On the inside of the door I placed an absorbing panel. It uses a pushplate and a doorhandle, both are mounted on the surface of the door (so no holes penetrating the mass). The plywood is where your hinges goin' to be installed on. As you can see its a very massive door. The 22mm sheets of MDF are heavy! You need atleast 4 strong hinges. You can adjust the thickness, and mass, ofcourse. I will see if I can sketchup the doorframing as well, to give a better idea on mounting the darn theng!
