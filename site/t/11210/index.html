super effective wrote:
1.Do I need to tie the two leaves together in any way or should they be completely unconnected?
Completely unconnected.
2.What size of air space should I use between the leaves? I understand that a larger gap is better but space is tight, so what would be a reasonable gap to leave whilst avoiding any nasty wall resonance?
There will always be a MAM resonance between the leafs. you doulble framing to avoid mechanical coupling. 25 mm between the frames will be fine.
3. For walls should I be using 3x2 or 4x2 studs?
From your drawings the walls will load bearing (the ceilings), so 2x4s.
4.Should I use GG between every layer of plasterboard?
That is the best thing to do isolation wise. As for the shape of the rooms yes this is a rough idea, can anyone point me towards some helpful information about room design and non-paralell walls. Better still if anyone has idea of a design that they could post, that would be exellent!
Also should I pitch the ceiling of the rooms?
Why would you? If you don't have a reason to do it, don't. Your last drawing shows a triple leaf between the rooms. forget the middle leaf.

elektrasoul wrote:
dunno if this is correct, but, just fill it with insulation / expanding foam / caulk (you can get 'acoustic' types but i just went to B&Q. lol) and get some heavy duty rubber door seals. youre threshold will be connecting the two leafs here anyway.
Most of this is incorrect. Doors used for isolation are solid core and are very heavy. I think it comes down to the lessor of two evils. The air space has to be terminated with something so as to have it perform the "spring" action derived from mass/air/mass and the doors need something to hang on that will support the weight. It isn't uncommon to have a jamb as wide as the wall-to-wall construction, from the outside of the one wall, to the outside of the opposing wall to hang doors on.
