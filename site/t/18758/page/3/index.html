Soundman202019 March 2014#56
(I think I'll avoid the lead sheet though. I hear this is not too good for health.)
As long as you don't eat your door, you should be fine. :) Lead is only a problem if it gets into your stomach or lungs somehow, so provided that you don't chew on the lead sheeting while you are installing it, and take reasonable precautions which you should be taking anyway, with or without lead, then I wouldn't be too worried. By "reasonable precautions" I mean: wearing gloves and eye protection all the time while building, washing your hands when you leave the work area, and using a simple respirator (mask) while doing anything that generates dust. You should ALWAYS do that, even of there is no lead around! Here's some advice from people who know: http://www.britishlead.co.uk/health-and-safety.htm :) - Stuart -

what's better: Add a sheet of lead between the door and a plywood sheet (or MDF)? or Add green Glue between the door and a plywood sheet (or MDF)?

Soundman202022 May 2014#69
Yeah, sorry about that! :) I was trying to be funny... (Maybe I shouldn't switch careers to become a comedian? :) ) What I was trying to say is that you probably need both mass and damping. I'm not sure what your door "sandwich" is looking like right now, but I'd guess that you at least have your basic solid-core door plus a couple of layers of MDF: If so, then use GG between the layers of MDF, and add your layer of lead between the solid-core and the first layer of MDF, without GG. That gives you most mass close to the core (and therefore close to the hinge fulcrum, reducing the moment arm that is trying to tear the door off the hinges), and some damping on the MDF, which is where the sound first hits the door surface. You still need to screw the two layers of MDF together, with the GG in between, so the damping isn't perfect, but it helps. Every little thing you can do helps a bit, and adding up all those "little things" can get you a "big thing". - Stuart -

Soundman202030 May 2014#74
Out of curiosity… how does ⅛" vinyl sheet block for walls compare to Green Glue? Any preferences?
It sounds like you are talking about "MLV" (Mass Loaded Vinyl)? If so, those are two very different things that accomplish different purposes: For a wall; I would go with the Green Glue, as it really does improve the low frequency damping considerably. But if you are talking about building up the layers of a massive door, then probably the MLV would be the better choice. MLV is just mass, but it is thin mass, and is also limp mass, which is good for doors. However, for walls, it is damned expensive mass! It doesn't do anything that another layer of drywall wouldn't do the same for half the price. And it doesn't do what Green Glue does either. So for walls, I would either add an extra layer of drywall, or add Green Glue. - Stuart -
