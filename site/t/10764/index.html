Glasgow wrote:
Are the double walls necessary, could I get away with RC on interior with single frame?
Considering your budget, you are probably going to have to cut corners here and there to fit it all in under $5K. For most of your applications, (singing, guitar), the RC will probably be fine. For the drums and bass (and for a Marshall amp cranked to "11") however, you are probably going to have to restrict your hours and/or volume if you go with the RC-only construction. Even with double-walled construction, you are going to get some leakage. Watch the windows for leakage as well.

A couple of hints from my build experience: 1) It will always cost you 2X - 3X what you expect, and take 2-3X as long to do! 2) Get things inspected EARLY, and be prepared to make changes!!! 3) Your control room can never be too big. 4) Your ceilings in the live room can never be too high. 5) You can never put it too many wire ties. 6) The drywall part of the build out will last forever! 7) It is all worth it in the end! Some things about roots. Live trees have them and as long as the tree is alive, the roots will expand/contract. Due to the slow growth of roots they can do damage later in your life that you cannot conceive right now. Building directly on them is going to come back to you sooner or later. So I wouldn't do it, me personally. I have no way to know what it is you mean when you say excavate in reference to the roots. If the roots remain they can bust whatever slab that you build. If you cut the roots, good chance the tree dies and the neighbor starts eyeballing you :)

Cram24 June 2008#14
I don't think you specified whether you'd be doing a slab or footings (tubes) for this, but if you're trying to deal with heave from root growth, you can keep the roots there and dig for tubes/footings that would not completely cut off the roots for that tree. What I'm describing is an alternative to a foundation or slab footing which would be a complete wall sunk into the ground. Another factor is the elevation your town is in oregon (an area of the country I know and love) - If you're by the seacoast the frost line is not as much of a concern as it rarely gets below freezing at all. But if you're over the pass or in a climate that gets a good amount of winter season, you'll have to dig deeper to avoid the frost heave. The local planning and development department for your town could inform you of this measurement. As an example - in NH where I live we have a 4' frost line in terms of code so when I built a deck, we had to sink tubes deeper than 4'. If I travel 3 hours north, I've got friends that have to sink 6' all the way up to places like Jackman ME where I know they have to sink 8' and opt for drilling into the ground rather than digging for tubes. The point is - with a foundation you'll be putting a wall in the ground, but with a elevated structure, you'd be digging 15'' holes to fit a 12'' or 10'' tube which would have a lesser impact.
