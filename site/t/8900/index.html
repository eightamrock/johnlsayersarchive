guitardad724 June 2007#2
Welcome to the forum Nikodemos. Yes you can and should use your existing concrete walls as the outer leafs. Basically you can add the "ground outside" to the equation of the mass of your 30cm walls. Even if the ground outside does not come to the top of the walls AND whether the concrete walls are solid concrete or hollow concrete blocks does not really matter... they will make a great outer leaf... that is presuming you've described and I understand correctly. I don't know your sound isolation goals however if this is a basement and there is ground underneath the existing concrete floor building a floating concrete floor may NOT be needed. It's expensive. You will loose height in the rooms and have a harder time with acoustics... we always want as much cubic feet/meters in the room as we can get. You will have a harder time getting building permits. You will make much more of a mess building. Most floating floors (not all) are best suited for studios not on the ground floor where sound isolation to spaces below studio is critical. Also you will (I think) have to examine your existing concrete floor to make sure it can take the weight of all materials you want to put on it. With no floating floor life will be easier. Marc

Nikodemos wrote:
well actually that's not a Corridor :oops: :oops: :oops: I just thought tha leaving some air between the 2 rooms would be a good idea...so it is just "dead space".
If that's the case - then you would be better off with a 2 leaf system - a 3 foot dead space will (itself) give you almost an additional 6dB of isolation. The 3' with a 4 leaf will actuallky decrease it slightly. BUT - if you need a corridor anyway - then I always tell people to live with what they end up with - and then it's a 4 leaf system - just the nature of the beast and all that. Sincerely, Rod
