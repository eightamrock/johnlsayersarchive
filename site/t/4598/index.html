knightfly2 November 2005#5
Only thing I see is I've never known a building official to allow gravel under a wall footing; maybe if it's compacted to surrounding earth characteristics, but generally they want solid, undisturbed earth or compacted and tested for all footings. The rest of the slab can have gravel as shown. Rigid insulation (EPS) is what to use, 1" thick minimum. Keep in mind when deciding on space taken up by walls, that this will also affect isolation by changing the air space between inner and outer masses. If you post desired dimensions and materials, I can give you an approximate isolation value... Steve

knightfly24 November 2005#10
If you're talking about the isolated slab, then the floor isn't likely to be the weak link; especially if you build an inner ceilng frame and keep the construction fully room-in-room. If you want better low end TL, and you're building NEW construction, it's much easier to use an added layer of sheathing (OSB, 3/4") to the outside of the stud frame than it is to cut and paste chunks of gypsum between studs. Yes, the tapered gaps caused by shingling the hardiplank would constitute an air gap, but I've no way of calculating just what the damage would be; it's a fairly narrow gap even at its widest point, so would have a fairly high resonant frequency - likely around 220-280 hZ. An added 3/4" layer of OSB on the outside frame should more than compensate for that, and a wider air gap as Keith mentioned would improve low end TL by around 4-5 dB. If you list a couple of wall scenarios, each with no more than two differing materials on each leaf, I can re-do the Wild-Assed Guess. I'm not aware of any material that would work well to fill the gaps behind the hardiplank - fiber insulation is still effectively air, spray foam is too hard to control, and you'll need a house wrap in there somewhere. Also, the effect of the extra mass will only be where it's the THINNEST, so it's probably better to just realize the possible negatives and compensate for them in order to get the look you're after. Keep in mind that field STC is ALWAYS less than computed by at least a couple dB, and that's if you're CAREFUL in every detail. As to maximizing square footage - understood. But keep in mind that we're talking about 3" thicker walls - if that makes the difference between actually being able to USE the space and NOT, then that also needs to be factored in... Steve
