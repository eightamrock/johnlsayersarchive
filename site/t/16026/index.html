Sir, Glenn knows what he is talking about. "I am getting conflicting advice. Starting with the walls. One says blow insolation between the existing walls, and apply 5/8 drywall over existing drywall, and that should do it. The other says, build a room within a room using staggered 2 x 4's that don't contact the existing walls, stuff with insulation, and add 5/8 drywall. Float the floor over existing carpet, re-pad and re-carpet? " It isn't conflicting information. In order to be conflicting it would assume that you have a fundamental understanding of the theories at present. You do not have that understanding. What you require is correct information and a few days/weeks here will get that for you at a low level I assure you and I can tell you first hand Glenn will be at the forefront of this education. "build a room within a room using staggered 2 x 4'" This is a morphing of two different procedures. It is either a fully decoupled room in a room or it is staggered studs...cannot be both. "Float the floor over existing carpet," Why? What purpose does the carpet serve? And this floating we have to assume is lightweight wood framing, most likely coupled directly back to the concrete. A floating floor is not in your future...maybe a decoupled/damped floor, but not a floating floor. Concrete in a highrise may have a PSI of 3.4 to 5K but that has nothing to do with the engineered liveload and the deadload. These are answers that do not come "quick" and will not come from this forum in any event. If you can get permission to place point loads upwards of 800 pounds and wall runs in the neighborhood of several hundred pounds with the overall weight possibly coming in at 4 to 5 tons...then you are in good shape. You will talk to someone involved with that building...there will be NO fast nor quick answer.
