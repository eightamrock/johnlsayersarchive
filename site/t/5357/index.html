tmix31 December 2005#2
It is pretty common in lower ceilinged drum rooms to completely absorb the ceiling so that you dont hear the reflections from the low ceiling. Sometimes thin acoustic tiles will tame some of the highs but leave the woofy mids and lows. Some 2" Compressed fibreglass above the kit helps immensely. If the floor is also dead the snare and toms will sound lifeless. Setting the drums on some plywood helps a lot. Hope that points in the right direction. I am a recording drummer and have OFTEN recorded in low ceilinged rooms and sounded fine. The trick is to absorb above the kit to give an acoustic picture that the ceiling is taller. Tom

tmix31 December 2005#4
Did you want to chop up the main room and make a drum booth? I find drums are happiest in the largest room possible. So unless you really need to track multiple instruments where you would need the isolation , I would track in the large room. I guess you could set aside about a 7 x 9 ft or 9 x 11 ft room for a vocal booth. If you made it pretty dead on a few walls and the ceiling you could use it for a small drum kit or amplifiers in a pinch. The smallest drum booth I every sucessfully recorded in was a really dead, 5 ft by 6 ft booth. The floor was live but all ealse was killed so you would not hear the "smallness " of the room. Please don't missunderstand. I am NOT a proponent of dead drum rooms. I like a lively controlled sound. But so often it is not possible for drums because they need room to breathe. So short of having a big live room, kill all the reflections you can except the floor. Tom

tmix2 January 2006#6
bart wrote:
I'm still wondering, how good a room can be created with these low ceilings, can I make it sound like a nice size room if care is taken to absorb reflections from the ceilings?
Yes! I have made at least 10 albums in my existing studio room where the ceiling is only 8 foot. If the walls are far enough (5-6 feet or more) from the kit and the ceiling is treated to be acoustically transparent. You should have a perfectly usable room. The mics cant tell if the ceiling is 12 feet up or 8 feet up if the sound is not reflecting from the ceiling to tell them so.
