sharward27 October 2006#7
MDF and drywall are very different in terms of their ingredients and fitness for purpose, but they are very similar in terms of their density. Acoustically, my understanding is that they are very interchangable. However, MDF is more durable than drywall, which is why it's a much more appropriate choice for speaker enclosures, shelving, some types of furniture, etc. However, what drywall has going for it is its relative lower cost in most parts of the world, and its speed of installation. Cutting drywall with a knife and a straightedge is a lot quicker than cutting MDF with a circular or table saw. Nobody's saying they're "the same," but in terms of their ability to stop sound from getting from "Point A" to "Point B," they're peas in a pod. Paul woodlock used MDF as one of his layers in his seven- layer walls so that he could hang shelves and whatnot pretty much anywhere without worrying about having to hit a stud. --Keith :mrgreen:
