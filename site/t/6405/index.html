knightfly14 June 2006#2
Don't have much time ATM, but a few things - don't use that heavy mineral wool, and don't use it within your ceiling at all. For acoustic purposes and for duct lining, 3 PCF (48 kG) weight is ideal. Within ducts, (I've not tried this) I would think that nearly any paint sprayed onto the surface would contain any fibers from wandering through the ducts and into the air; but too heavy and it would decrease absorption. For sound barriers, mass-air-mass construction is by far the most effective and the "air" gap should have no heavier than 3 PCF weight - for lower frequencies, it's been proven that even LIGHTER insulation works best. ONe of the best choices for this is the "fluffy stuff" or normal weight household fiberglass insulation, slightly compressed between the two leaves of mass for a degree of damping of the panels. Moisture-wise - I'd seriously consider running a dehumidifier in the space every moment you're not using it - for one thing, virtually ALL wooden instruments (even drum shells of wood) prefer an environment that runs between 40-45% relative humidity, and no self-respecting English cellar would EVER be that low by accident :wink: Condensation only happens when moisture/temperature differences exist, so if you keep your cellar and the upstairs at nearly the same temperatures no moisture will be driven through the floor in either direction... I'd put some poly strapping across the backs of your frame for the walls, http://www.uline.com/Browse_Listing_275 ... +Strapping (something like the yellow highlited one) - this can be stapled, or if you can build your frame on the floor and tip it into position it's better to use roofing tacks (25-30mm) - you just run the strapping perpendicular to the vertical studs at about 20-24 inch intervals, then tip up the wall frame and fasten in place, then add your insulation and wallboard. IF your wall frames do NOT touch the concrete wall, you won't need resilient mounts for the wall; but if your wall frame DOES contact the ceiling joists, then resilient mounts WOULD be necessary; also for the ceiling wallboard.
