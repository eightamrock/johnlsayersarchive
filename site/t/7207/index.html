Isolation loss via 3 leaves happens to a degree with almost any type of acoustic treatment you add inside the room. More loss would be with tuned slot walls and soffit walls as apposed to very little with corner bass trap just filled with insulation, the more solid mass in the acoustic treatment the more isolation loss. The way around this is to over compensate, adding mass and or air to your MAM wall system around the acoustic treatment. From Steve on an old thread of my design:
Still, any time you add ANY traps to inside walls you create a multi-leaf construct, and should allow for that by using MORE mass in the OTHER leaves or a wider air gap between them. Otherwise you'll end up with less isolation than you bargained for... Steve
Brian Ravnaas, from the Green Glue Company is currently posting test results about isolation loss with a panel trap hear: panel trap experiment outline Johns soffit wall could be built within the Air of MAM IF you’re not concerned about isolation. In other situations where other studio walls are behind the soffit, isolation could be built accordingly. But with your current Design from John if you desire a level of isolation your going to have to build your MAM, over compensate with Mass around the soffits IF you want to maintain and even level of TL/STC/isolation from all walls.
using Risc and hat channel
Use RC/resilient channel. Hat Channel is of no benefit for isolation.
Like I stated earlier brick walls separate me from my neighbors
Is it solid brick or hollow CB (concrete block)? Just checking, CB walls should be filled to avoid triple leaf.

Not sure what you mean with that, you got 2 frame walls? You can add/attach insulation directly to brick wall. Using different thickness of drywall will give a gain in isolation in the mids but performs worse in the lows, TL is what we are after so it’s better to stick with all 5/8” drywall and forget the ½”. You can build one super door, but by the time you’ve built it correctly it made be more expensive then 2 doors. Several forum members have found solid wood, used doors with no frames cheap on eBay and in surplus stores. Keep your eye out.
Can I build some walls outside in and some walls inside out?
Yup sure can. This was once in my design, I may or may not bring it back. For me it’s a battle considering isolation, acoustics and square footage loss. Increasing Mass or Air gives more isolation, decreasing Mass or Air gives less. Turning a wall inside out decreases the AIR... so a wall 2x 5/8” drywall, 2x4 studs w RC13 insulation, 1” air, 2x4 studs w RC13 and 2x 5/8” drywall = about 65 STC. The air in this equation includes 3.5” x 2 of insulation, 7+1 = 8” of air. Turning one wall inside out decreases the air and can’t remember of the top of my head how much less STC you would have, but I’m sure Steve has posted it somewhere. The inside out wall is difficult to build> frame, put in position, square and brace it, take it down, add drywall, lift back up with help from 2 or 3 grunting friends, then how do you stuff backer rod and caulk around perimeter? Could place a large bead of caulk on floor before setting it down BUT for sure it won’t be as tight of a seal as building the regular way AND you’ll have isolation loss. Gaining the 3.5” for acoustic treatment within wall cavity is valuable when you’ve only got a 10’ wide room. When combining outside and inside walls care must be taken to not create reflection points from within stud cavities. Should be easy…
