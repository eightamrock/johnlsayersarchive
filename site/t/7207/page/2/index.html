knightfly15 October 2006#43
I just deleted your cross-post in Acoustics, it's a toss-up but I felt that continuity might work better just keeping it here for now - Gotta get back into other things soon (need to find about 100 hours to use between now and 4 days from now, unless my math is flooey I'm already short 4 (even if I don't sleep) First, please FORGET about STC; R&B'ers and Rappers should be SHOT for even USING that term :twisted: - I'm surprised Keith hasn't already jumped you on that one... design your isolation for LOW frequency, and the rest will fall into place. This is called TL, short for Transmission Loss, and is spec'd for each individual frequency band - so Low Frequency Transmission Loss (LF TL for short) is what you're after. If you've not already bought Rod's book, you should (check the stickies in the Construction forum) - he explains how to do basement walls, keep insulation from getting/staying wet, etc - If your basement walls are really brick, and not concrete block, you should have them rendered (basically, buttered with about 1/2" of mortar) before you do anything else (other than remove the existing drywall) - this will help the outer leaf by a few dB, then you put up inner frames NOT touching the block - these need to (first) be framed on the floor, fasten pallet strapping across the backs of the studs every 2 feet, then frames tipped up and drywall added to the face. If you want inside out walls (BTW, the opposite of this would be "normal", not "outside-in", which is exactly the same as "inside-out" :wink: ) then you should plan on your frame being further away from the block walls or you'll end up with less LF TL; not good for your style of music. In fact, if TL is important to you then the only advantage of building inside-out walls is a slight savings in framing materials, at a cost of being a large PITA to get properly sealed (and be SURE) John's soffit design is NOT to be considered one of your leaves in a m-a-m construct, nor was it intended; it's meant to extend the speaker baffle plane in order to force the speaker woofers into 2 pi space, minimising a whole raft of detrimental reflections and making your "sweet spot" actually sweet. As long as you keep any "vents" in those soffits at least 2-3 feet away from your woofers, filling these cavities with wool and venting the bottom will help bass trapping quite a bit. You can, if using active monitors, also build in a "chimney" that runs from the floor, up behind the speakers, and out near the ceiling, so your monitors won't cook their power amps. This won't hurt trapping OR imaging, because the vents would be far enough away from speaker elements not to matter. If you do this, you can also put some holes in the sides of the "chimney" near floor and ceiling to further enhance bass trapping into your absorbent fill behind the soffits. Front faces of soffits should be heavy/solid - 1-1/4" flooring ply with your favorite veneer over the face is a good choice. Check out the speaker forum for more on soffit ideas. Although there will be some fairly small LF TL loss due to the third leaf effect of the soffits, there is growing evidence that this isn't the end of the world (Brian Raavnas' incredible contributions in particular) so, if you just add an extra layer (like 3 instead of 2) of 5/8 gypsum to your inner leaf I doubt you'll notice any degradation - Cloud - of course you need one. Everybody does, unless they're mixing OUTSIDE. IF you're low on ceiling height, at least 2" thick 3 PCF spaced a couple inches off the ceiling. Side absorbers - I'd make 'em optional/movable, do a search here for Moulton (dave) and check out the 2-3 most recent hits for a couple of links to interesting reading. Your splayed side walls (hopefully at least 1 foot in 10) will take care of wall-wall flutter, your "cloud" will do that for floor-ceiling, and rear traps will kill it for front-back. The absorbent John's drawing shows below the speakers is there to help kill reflections off the side of the desk that's toward the speakers, and to keep the first part of your mix environment "dry" - you may also want/need absorbent mounted to this part of the desk itself. Now I REALLY gotta run, HTH... Steve

Depending on when your house was built and local codes at that time: For sure every 4' one of those blocks is solid filled concrete, current code (but not what is in my block walls) is that every corner and every last cavity of wall should be filled with concrete.
