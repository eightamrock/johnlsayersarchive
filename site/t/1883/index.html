dymaxian2 July 2004#2
Need more input! That looks like a big enough space to make into a pretty decent studio. From just glancing at it, my first impression is that you could use the larger space on the left of your drawing as the tracking room, the lower right corner as the control room, and what's left between tracking and the door to the rest of the house could hold an iso booth. Or, if there's enough space there, you could use the lower right corner as a drum booth, and the whole left half of the plan as the control room. *shrug* But it all depends on what you want to do in there. Are you going to be recording full bands? Just voice-over work? How much sound isolation do you want to have in there- do you need to have a metal band playing full-volume at 3:00am without your wife even hearing a whisper? A big part of your plan will be the location of that center beam- it doesn't look centered on the plan, so how far from each wall is it? And if your ceiling is only 8'4 (to drywall, I'm assuming) would you be able/willing to pull that drywall down so you could stick new ceiling joists in between the existing floor joists? This would give you a lot more headroom if you decide to build a room within a room... The more info you give us, the more help we'll be.

I agree with you regarding that beam. It'd be easy to put doorways under it, but you should probably let that beam-line become one of your walls. I didn't mean you should move any joists- just set new ones between the existing joists.
Maybe radiant floor heat over pergo flooring?
Well, that'll blow your budget right there... Here's some thinking for you... get a good HVAC guy on-board as soon as you can. Ask him if the existing furnace there will be strong enough to add the garage heat system to it. If so, you can do this. If not, my advice would be to build the system so that in the future you can change out the furnace without too much hassle- then when you can afford it you can just connect the new one to the ducts.
