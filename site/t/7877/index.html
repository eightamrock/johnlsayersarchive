That'll make a nice sized room.
1. Will ceramic tile work for the floors?
It's pretty hard on the feet/legs/back after those long sessions, (don't drop your coffee cup!) and will create a really "live sound." You will need to tame those reflections with acoustic treatments. BTW, most of the treatments described here on the forum call for 2"- 3" semi rigid fiberglass or mineral wool @ 2.5 pcf. (Owens Corning 703, Roxul AFB, or equivalents)
2. What shape or use of the dimensions available are best to get a good tonal balance between tracking and mixing in the same room?
Tough call, as each room/application calls for a different design. (Any possibility of reconsidering a control room/live room/iso room approach? You've got the room...)
3. With the one neighbor that close, do we have a prayer with our budget at all? Most of the lumber we'll get for free, so I'm talking insulation, sheetrock, etc.
If you're taking that approach, here's a few links and diagrams that might be helpful in getting an idea of what your up against. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2125 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1168 Don't forget to address the sub floor TL requirements since you're not on a slab, and be sure that your flooring can support the extra weight of multiple layers of materials, as well as musicians, studio equipment, etc.
