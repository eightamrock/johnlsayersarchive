lilith_envy26 October 2009#3
My (yet to be finished) studio is a completely free standing structure. Ceiling rests on top plate, wall corners against walls. Haven't even needed a sway brace or overhead hanger. However I have 1 dyna bolt (concrete bolt) for every 3m of wall. Dyna bolts aren't the easiest things to remove and I doubt you would pass building regulations without something holding it down. Either bolt or concrete nails. Do some of your own research on that. your ceiling will be limited to a certain span depending on Kg/sq m. There are a lot of smart span products on the markert now that work much better than 8x4 or 10x4 timber.

Hi Steve! Welcome! Nice to see a newcomer who actually HAS read the stickies, and done what they say! So an extra good welcome for ya! Question by question:
Is it possible/feasible to construct a “room within a room” including the ceiling (which is my biggest concern) inside an existing household room WITHOUT flanking (or anchoring to, or damaging) any of the existing structure ?
Yes. Assuming that the outer leaf is only a single leaf. If it's a double leaf already, then I got some good news, and not-so-good news, and some bad news. The good news is that it still can be done, the not-so-good news is that it would be much better to attack that outer leaf and make it into a single leaf before proceeding, the bad news is that if you have no choice it still can be done, but things start getting complex. And expensive.
frame borders and resting on the existing floor via rubber pucks (more for protection than floating isolation).
You might want to be careful about that: Even though your intention isn't to float it, what you are doing actually is floating it! You might want to read this thread on floating floors: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173
So can I run the joists for the inner/new ceiling across the top of the framed walls or into hanging type brackets fixed to the upright/sides of the wall frames --- and is this entire structure capable of being freestanding within the existing room and without these joists being anchored to any existing part of the room?
Yes. You might need to brace it internally while you are building it, but if you think about it, that's basically the way a house is built anyway: framed walls, joists across the top, nothing else to hold it up except itself. Its the same basic principle. Not too sure about the "freestanding" part, though: it needs to be attached firmly to the existing floor somehow. I doubt that code would allow you to build something like that without it being attached to a foundation, directly or indirectly.
I would be inclined to assume that the biggest problem here is supporting the ceiling which will probably be 2 x layers of plasterboard,
You should really contract a structural engineer or contractor who knows about ceilings, to tell you how to do that part. You need to dimension the joists hefty enough to span those distances while supporting the weight of the drywall, insulation, any electrical or HVAC that might be up there, etc. You can't just slap up a couple of 2x4's and hope for the best.... CRUNCH!!!
Would I be correct in stating that although the existing ceiling is at the mercy of its’ current (unknown) construction elements, erecting a freestanding structure inside the room will pretty much create a 2 x leaf structure?
It might be 2-leaf, it might be 3, or it might be more. It all depends on "knowing" about the "unknown". You need to find out how it is built already.
Furthermore, is it in any way possible (even with extremely complex planning and detail) to construct this inner room in such a way that it could be modular to the point of being able to deconstruct it, move it to another premises and then reconstruct it at a new premises?
Possible? I guess it is, but I'm not sure how feasible it would be. Those walls / ceiling will be extremely heavy and hard to move. And if you do manage to move them, then you run the risk of damaging your seals, drywall cracking, etc. And you'd have to build it in sections small enough to fit through the door. Dunno.. Might be tough. So short answer? "Maybe, but...."
I do play drums (V-Drums),
Acoustic or electronic? If that's acoustic, then you are looking at levels up around 115 dB. That's a lot. how quiet do you need to be on the outside of your room? That's the big question here. Stone deaf granddad two floors up and at the other end of the house? No problem. Cranky baby sleeping right next door? That's a bit tougher! Add a couple more layers of drywall, and a bigger air gap, very thick laminate glass in all windows with large air gaps, Rod's double door design, or his "superdoors" everywhere that you need a door, with triple seals, extra mass. Etc. Do you have a rough outline of your existing room, and idea about your layout? One comment: that ceiling is already low. It's going to get a lot lower.... Another comment: Download SketchUp, and do all your design work in there. Much better than Paint! - Stuart -
