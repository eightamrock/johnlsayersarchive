Soundman20205 April 2009#2
Hey Chris. Good to see that you are still going on with the design! A couple of comments about the layout in your fourth image: 1) Which way are you going to orient your mix position in the CR? I imagine that you want to face the window to have good sight lines into the LR, but then your speakers will be firing down the short axis of the room: they should really be firing down the long axis. I guess you could do some really heavy treatment on the rear wall, though, if that's the way you need to face. 2) If that is the orientation, then your door into the room is probably going to be right at a first reflection point, which might make it hard to treat. You might want to think about moving that door a little further towards the back of the room. 3) You seem to be wasting a lot of space in the airlock. 4) It seems to me that your mix position is going to have to be more or less in the middle of that room, very close to the dreaded 50% front-back position! Do you have a diagram showing the positions of the speaker, console, and "your head", to check that? 5) Taking all of the above into account, have you thought about the following plan? Rotate your CR 90° to the left (anti-clockwise), and use the "airlock" space to add it the rear end of your control room. It will make your CR much bigger, your speakers will fire down the long axis, the mix position will be where it should be (around the 38% mark, instead of the 50% mark), you won't be wasting the airlock space, and you can even replace the window to the LR with a couple of John's legendary sliding glass doors! The only minor issue I see is that your main door into the CR would have to be close to the rear corner, which limits your options on bass trapping, but overall I reckon it would be a much more functional space. 6) Those sprinklers are going to be a major pain! What is your plan for dealing with those? What does code say about those? I guess you have no choice but to poke them down through the ceiling, right? Bummer. - Stuart -

Soundman20207 April 2009#6
OK, now I get it. It would probably be possible to work out some kind of CR design to fit that support column in, but it would be easier to leave that in your LR, where it symmetry is not critical at all. For an LR, you could maybe even use that column as part of a vocal booth or storage closet, or just wrap it with insulation. Lots os possibilities in an LR, but tougher in CR. Yes, your LR can be pretty much any shape that makes sense, and symmetry isn't important at all. In fact, many people build live rooms with non-parallel walls in order to prevent flutter echo. Your ceiling can also be flat, or angled if you wish, in your LR. Once again, it doesn't matter too much, as long as you try to keep it as high as possible and treat it as needed to get the sound that you want in there. So it really might be a good idea to think about switching the rooms: You could maybe slide the dividing wall between the rooms over a bit, to make your CR slight smaller and your LR slightly larger, or to get your CR dimensions to match one of the good room ratios, which is important too. - Stuart -

stuntbutt8 April 2009#9
I would get an accurate measure on the distance between the bottom of the steel trusses and the sprinkler heads. Your ceiling joists will have to run parallel to the steel trusses. Because your ceiling can't touch the trusses, the bottom of your ceiling joists will be lower than the bottom of the trusses. If you set your joists a half inch lower and add two layers of 5/8" drywall below that, you have made your ceiling 1 3/4" below the steel trusses. I don't know how far sprinkler heads have to hang below the ceiling but I think it's going to be tight. You may have to extend the sprinkler heads.
