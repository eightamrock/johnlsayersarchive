gullfo13 June 2011#2
3m high is nice - if you can afford to go to 4m that would be nicer. perhaps slope the ceiling in the CR from 3m to 4m (front to back)? in the live room you could do that side to side. on the front (window) wall in the CR - you could build out your treatments to block some of the window so that your treatment correctly balances the room. keeping in mind the general rule on double leaf walls - mass on one side or the other of walls which have a small air gap (< 1m), not on both sides - since you want the isolation between live and CR to be good. but on the back wall of the CR, since you have a hallway there, you could have the drywall over both sides of the frame.

gullfo14 June 2011#10
you put the absorption scattered around the walls and ceiling to break up reflections and distribute the absorption. this tends to balance the space and create a diffusive effect. super chunks are typically stacks of rigid insulation which span the corners (wall-wall, wall-ceiling, wall-floor - are all "corners") and from floor to ceiling (or as needed on the other corner types). you cut the 600mm x 1200mm x 100mm rigid insulation (48kg/m3) sheets into triangles or fill the corner with lighter insulation (15kg/m3) then put the rigid insulation across the face. use simply frames on each side to attach cloth to. use supports on a vertical corner to hold up the insulation so it does not compact or sag. do a search on this site and you'll see a number of examples.
