here are the dimensions as measured from the inside of the closet: L 59 1/2" x W 33" x H 94 1/2"
That's a REALLY small space! I'm sure you already suspect this, but it's so small that there really isn't anything you can do to make it usable. By the time you put enough treatment in there to make it even vaguely acceptable acoustically, there won't be enough room to even stand up in there. That's the issue. And if you put a door on it, then you'd probably last about 2 minutes in there before suffocating! I reckon I'd seriously consider doing something else. One thing that occurred to me is to build a frame around the closet door, to extend it a couple of feet into the room. Not sure if it would be worthwhile, but it would give you a bit more room, and MAYBE make the space usable.
My thoughts are to deaden and treat the room as much as I can with 703 dense fiberglass and put as much bass traps (2" thick) and mid traps (1" thick) as I can fit in there.
I doubt if that is going to work. Not thick enough. With the dimensions of the closet, you are going to have major problems in the range 113 Hz to 500 Hz, and the human vocal range goes down right into that area. But you can't treat those low frequencies with 1" and 2" 703 o the walls of the closet. I'm sure you don't want to hear this, but you cannot get around the laws of physics. There are no magical materials that would make that closet usable as a good vocal booth. Sorry.
or should I try to add little corner mounted triangle traps from floor to ceiling as seen in link below??????
They wouldn't fit! I couldn't find the dimensions on that web site, but in order to be effective those things would have to be at least 20" to 25" across the front face. You don't HAVE that much space in the closet! This is the entire issue. While those corner traps would probably help a lot, they just wont fit in such a tiny space.
I'm going to put on my own weather stripping and try to adjust the threshold as snug as possible or maybe order some sort of special adjustable rubber threshold.
You'd be sealing a tomb, basically, unless you also have a decent ventilation system in there. You have barely one cubic meter of air in there: not enough to keep you alive for very long...
I also thought about later when I can afford to add a layer of mass loaded vinyl to the entire inside of the booth just for a bit more isolation
Waste of money. MLV is just mass, and is very, very expensive mass. If you need more isolation, plain old drywall is far more effective and much cheaper. But even then, unless you decouple the two sides of each wall, it won't help much (if any).
cause vinyl is very small and and doesn't eat into my space at all really
Neither does drywall! But once again, it will not be effective unless you also decouple.
I have had decent results with vinyl before not great but decent ish, which might be just enough to turn up speakers a little more in room when tracking vocals.
If you got REALLY lucky, and the MLV was enough to double the mass of the entire wall, you'd be able to turn up your speakers an extra 6 dB. Maybe. Most likely more like 2 or 3. In other words, it would make no real difference at all. A 3 dB increase is barely noticeable, and even 6 doesn't help much.
Is it possible?
Short answer? No. You could put 703 on the walls and ceiling, sure, and even make mini-triangular "traps" for the corners, leaving just enough space to stand in, but my best guess is that it would sound like crap, and you'd be suffering greatly from lack of air within too-short a time to be able to record anything. It would be very uncomfortable. I never met a singer who could do a good job when they are feeling uncomfortable, hot, claustrophobic, and asphyxiated. Seriously, I would look for another place to use as a booth. I just can't see that working decently. Or even half-decently. - Stuart -

I've recorded in some pretty lousy spaces in my time, and I'm thinking that, if that room is all you have, then there might be some alternatives that would at least be better than what you have now, and would also fit in your budget. It's NOT going to be a real solution, but should help a bit: I'm thinking along the lines of building a couple of large gobos (8' x 4'), perhaps on wheels, with very thick absorption on them (6" 703 mounted over an air gap over a sheet of plywood), then arranging them in a "V" shape, touching each other along one vertical edge, and with the open end of the V facing towards the closet. Pack the back wall of the closet also with 6" of 703. Now stand in the middle of all this, with your mic somewhere located vaguely towards the sharp end of the "V". It's not perfect by any means, and you'll still get plenty of room sound in the mic, but with careful compression in your DAW you should be able to get something usable, maybe. (In a pinch I even once did something like that with rolls of carpet standing on end ( :shock: ). It worked well enough for what I needed, but I sure wouldn't recommend it. ) You could maybe even lay another sheet of 703 over the top of the two gobos, for a tiny bit extra absorption. But like I say, it won't be good, and you'll still need to do a lot of work in the mix to clean it up. However, it might just be enough. And you can even re-use the gobos later, once you get your studio going again! Just a suggestion, but I really can't think of much else you can do with that space on that budget. Sorry! I missed your response!
As far as overall treatment and direction of the vocal booth should I angle some walls offset like 6 inches to break up the parallels a bit??????
If you can afford the space and the expense, then sure. Angle two of them, and you'll be fine.
and fill the room with 2" thick 703 (which I happen to have a bunch) on every wall and ceiling with a 1" air gap behind traps?
Sounds like a plan!
should I try to put traps everywhere I possible can like above the window and below the window too etc...???
Yup! But you could also add some "slot resonators" on the walls, to not lose too much of the high end. You want a reasonably "dead" room for a booth, but it doesn't have to be slaughtered with a chain saw! Dead is good, massacre is not... :)
wanted to add a wood floor too for a little extra life or should I carpet it?
Wood floor, forget the carpet. Carpet is selective, and you need the floor to be bright. If the room is still too bright after you do everything (unlikely!) you could put in a small throw-rug to help, but I doubt that will be necessary.
Do I want to keep it 9 feet tall or should I lower ceiling a bit, maybe a drop ceiling with 1" thick 703?
You should put a couple of inches of 703 up there, definitely, but that does NOT lower the ceiling! Acoustically, it actually RAISES the ceiling, which is good. I'd go for 4" on the entire ceiling. Cover it with cloth, to hide the "ugly". It's a common misconception that putting absorption on the walls or ceiling actually changes the dimensions of the room: it does not. The dimensions are always measured to the hard surfaces of the walls, not to the treatment that you add afterwards. Sound waves terminate at the hard, solid surfaces, not at soft fluffy surfaces.
Is there any ideal dimensions I should shoot for to frame the room to then put the treatment on or should I shoot for certain dimensions with all the treatment installed and all = how exact do I got to be?
to be honest, I wouldn't make it any smaller than it is by adding drywall anywhere, but I would frame the 703 somehow: it needs to be framed to put the slot resonators up (search the forum for "slot wall" to find out how to do that), and also to cover up the ugly 703 with cloth in those areas where you don't put slots.
