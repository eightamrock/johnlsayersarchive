knightfly11 July 2006#2
If you're building double-framed walls as that drawing, glass thickness should be 1/3 as much as the total gypsum thickness for each side, with one side being slightly thicker if possible (different coincidence frequency) Sorry, black is 60 duro neoprene or equivalent, gray is closed cell foam (around 15 duro if it's specified) - the black is to keep the glass located and supported, the gray is to SEAL. GM's trunk seal rubber (boot seal if you're English :wink: ) works well for this; if you don't have Rod's book, it's available at JC Whitney (auto parts online) Steve BTW, do NOT use two double-pane windows; you want single, heavy panes as far apart as practical. Best is laminated, otherwise known as "safety", or "bullet-proof" - second best is Tempered, but you MUST order it in the size you need, and even NICKING it during installation can cause it to break into little pieces. Last is "float" which is ordinary window glass. These are given in order of both safety AND acoustics, which (for once) coincide with each other...

Ro13 July 2006#15
did a little digging.. it's not fermacell. It's what we call "gasbetonblok" in the Netherlands, dunno the English term :oops: It's something like a lightweight stone, open(cell) concrete blocks. Easy to work with, you can cut it with just a handsaw. lightweight stuff. It's not 2 leafs, but it's not very heavy either. It's a concrete type who is characterised by its light weight. The strenght of this light concrete is thus less than an ordinary concrete. The cause of the light weight is the large quantity bubble Len (warrants) in concrete. This bubble Len arises because during the production process a small quantity aluminium powder is added. The remaining material make be which necessary is warrant concrete, just like in an ordinary concrete, cement, lime, sandsand sand and water.
