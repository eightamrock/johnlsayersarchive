All this information can be relatively easily found on this forum, but I'll try and summarize it anyway. Should others detect any nonsense (which is very possible), feel free to debate or correct. As far as I know: when it comes to porous absorbers, it's all about density, thickness and spacing. All absorbing or reflecting qualities are strongly frequency dependent. Higher density (+-80kg/m3 or more) will contain lower frequencies better, but will reflect more high frequencies. Lower density (+- 40kg/m3) still has effect in the lows, but has its absorbing peak at mid/high frequencies Thickness is easy to understand. Considering the direction of a ray or wavefront of sound, the more material it encounters, the more it will loose it's energy trying to pass through it. Spacing is mainly a money saver. Spacing 10cm of insulation about 10cm from a reflecting surface will have similar effect as should you attach 20 cm of insulation to the wall itself without any air gap. When talking bout in-room treatment, paper backing is generally avoided due to its reflecting qualities in the higher frequencies.
