guitardad7219 October 2006#7
Jamie, Also be aware that when floating a floor chances are you would be better off not floating as far as isolation is concerned. The math to get the weights correct is difficult, everything to do with weight has to be preplanned. Many have floated when they should not have. Do a search for floating floor on this forum. Floating a floor to level out floor is an option, it's been done, but iso is still most likely going to be less.

knightfly28 October 2006#14
Don't panel those 45 degree corners, if those walls are part of your isolation shell you need to frame them square. If they are NOT part of a sealed isolation shell you should just fill those corners with 3 PCF (48 Kg/m63) mineral wool and cover with cloth. Corners are your best place for broadband trapping to smooth out room modes, don't box 'em in or you'll have a hard time making the room sound decent... Steve

Guilford of Maine FR701, muslin, burlap & fleece are some of the not many options. Fabric needs to be fire resistant when not installed directly against a wall like certains are BUT are acoustically transparent, sound passes right through.
