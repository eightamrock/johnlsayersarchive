OK, that's good -- if a structural engineer signed off on the thousands of pounds (please pardon our U.S. measurement system reference :roll:) of dead load you're adding to the existing structure, then at least we don't need to be worried about people dying in a catastrophic collapse. Yes, I'm serious -- it really can happen... which is why I brought it up. That being said, I think you're taking a big gamble with that floating floor design though. If you're prepared for the probability of your isolation outcome being worse than if you had not floated at all, then... More power to you. Had we been able to see your plan before you started building, I'm sure we'd have tried to talk you out of it and/or helped you come up with a plan that has a much higher probability of success. I wish I could provide some insights on how to make things better. Unfortunately, I don't know how possibly bad things are. There's not enough data to be constructive, and even if there were, things are moving so fast for you that I don't think there's any opportunity to improve, beyond perhaps a constrained dampening layer (such as Green Glue) under yet another layer of plywood in each room, which might help to control low end resonance, which I think will be your biggest challenge... :roll: I don't mean to be negative and discouraging, but we've put forth a great deal of effort over the past couple of years trying to squelch the myth that a floating floor/room that is not painstakingly engineered to just slightly compress its industry proven elastomers and to not actually amplify frequencies that are wished to be reduced by way of resonance of a relatively thin and lightweight wooden floor surface will somehow magically perform as we'd like. Rod discusses this rather frankly in his book... And I spent months when planning my project on the dreaded "to float or not to float" conundrum myself, and it's still rather painful for me to recall. I hope you don't think I'm beating you up just to be mean... This is a great thread and I'm sure it will be referenced by many who may have a desire to do something similar. That is one of the great things about this forum -- we learn so much from what others do really well, and in other cases, maybe not so. ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:
