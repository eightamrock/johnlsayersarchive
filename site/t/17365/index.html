Soundman20203 February 2012#4
Try searching the forum for terms related to moisture, dampness, and things like that, and you might find some ideas on how to deal with that. Brine is probably the best guy here to help you figure that out, but if you have moisture in your walls or floor, that that must be resolved first: A studio is a double-sealed, double-airtight box,so if you have dampness in the walls/floor before you start, it will have nowhere to go once the studio is in place, and you won't even realize that it is accumulating, until one day.... :shock: :cen: The reason your floor slopes is probably related to this, so you might want to consider that angle too: you most likely don't want water running under your studio! Regarding your layout: It's best to have the control room laid out symmetrically, with the speakers firing down the long axis of the room, not across it, and if the ceiling is going to be angled (tilted) then it needs to be lowest at the front, where the speakers are, and higher at the back. But with those HVAC ducts running down the middle, that will be hard to do. Also, those pillars will be an issue if you need good isolation, as you cannot have any penetrations through your isolation shell. Your idea of building them into the walls (between the two leaves) is a good one, and is the best way to go. So, considering all of the above, you might want to play around and try out a few other layouts, to see if there's one where the control room is better sized and oriented, and the pillars can go inside the walls. With a room that size, there should be several other ways of arranging it, I reckon. - Stuart -
