gullfo22 March 2011#27
the wide slats go over the cloth like regular slats except its primary role is to keep from HF alive off the face of an absorbptive backing. if you want to go with a slat resonator and use wide slots you could go there as well. in the way you assembled the last studio - rigid insulation for absorption treatment, hangers, clouds, etc. pink stuff between isolation wall studs and big areas needing insulation. for measuring, the actual parts - use normal framing rules except decouple the frames and drywall and seal everything where you need to create isolation. for slat resonators - make sure the cavity is sealed. then add the cloth and then the slats.

gullfo24 March 2011#29
c'mon! you built your last studio! :mrgreen: slats - the wood strips - 25mm, 50mm, 100mm slots - those gaps between the wood strips :wink: widely spaced = 1-3" (25mm - 75mm or more) apart regular spaced = 3mm, 5mm, 10mm spacing normal framing - 2x4 or 2x6 lumber with a bottom runner (plate) and one or two top runners (plates) with the vertical common studs spaced 16 or 24" (400mm or 600mm) apart. decoupled - use some form of foam/rubber/padding to keep the frame from hard contact on the exterior cement walls or ceiling, or floor. overall the goal is to use as many normal techniques for framing and construction as possible with the caveat that decoupling it good, angles are good, caulking is good, etc etc. same stuff we discuss here over and over again...

gullfo13 July 2011#32
you could leverage the existing walls as much as possible and thus you're really only treating the space - the exception is the isolation booth is actually surrounded by mass and separated from the CR with a single wall - thus reducing the materials there. the side wall slats are for the RFZ treatment. on the back wall the widely space slats are simply to preserve some of the HF in the space but you could either make it wholly absorptive or use heavier wood pieces you might have left.
