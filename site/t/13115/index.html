Is the attached picture so called decopuling? why will I benifit from this compared to just build a wall on the side towards the studio room?
If you just build a single wall between the rooms, it would have to be directly attached to the existing walls and ceiling, so therefore sound will flank around it. It will not isolate. In order to isolate, you have to decouple completely, which implies building a "room within a room", as in Lilith's model. Like Javier said: No mechanical contact at all between the inner "leaf" of the wall, and the outer leaf. The same applies to the ceiling. The inner ceiling rests only on the outer walls. No contact at all with the outer "leaf". Anything less wont work. - Stuart -

Soundman20203 June 2009#8
is the resonance going to be that big, making the new wall useless?
It's not the resonance that causes the problem: It's the coupling. The side wall and ceiling are in common to booth rooms, so there will be direct transmission of sound through the structure of the building itself, which will "flank" around your dividing wall. Put your ear up against that existing wall, close to one end of it, and get somebody to tap on it sharply with a hammer, close to the other. You will hear the tapping coming through the wall itself, not through the air. The vibration is conducted along the structural members of the wall. If you build a 2-leaf divider wall between the rooms, then the leaves will be directly coupled to each other bu the existing side walls, and the floor, and the ceiling. Sound waves will make one wall vibrate, that vibration will be directly transmitted through the structure to the other wall, which will vibrate too, thus transmitting the sound into the room. In order to prevent that from happening, you have to "de-couple" the walls completely, such that there is nothing in common between them. No mechanical links at all. In order to do that you need to build a "room within a room" for at least one side. Resonance won't even come into the equation until you decouple, since it will be insignificant compared to the flanking. And if you build your walls correctly, the resonant frequency will be so low that it won't matter, and it will be damped so well as to not be a problem anyway. Provided that you decouple everything, of course. - Stuart -

Listen up - the easiest way to picture what they mean by the coupling problem is this...... stand in a room - one foot from a wall and put your ear up to a glass - what do you hear through the glass? Squat - nadda - nothing....... Now - take that same glass - and hold it up to your ear - place it then up against a wall where you can barely hear 2 people talking on the other side - what do you hear? - Clean and clear voices that you can understand........ Don't touch the wall = uncloupled = no transmission - Touch the wall = coupled = everything passes through........ Easy enough to understand....... Rod
