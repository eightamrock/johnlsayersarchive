building a new studio in Cali, Colombia

Started by gabimonster on 12 March 2010. 3 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 14180.

gabimonster12 March 2010#1
We are planning the construction of a small recording studio in a house we have recently purchased. The house is situated in Cali, Colombia, S.A. The house is about 60 years old, the walls are about 21 cm in thickness and are made of brick and cement; there is a wall in the center of the room that arches, this wall is not a structural wall and can be removed; the floor is made of cement tiles; there is one window that faces the street; the entry is through a door that also faces the street; the ceiling is a type of drop ceiling made of wood paneling, above which is an open area and then the roof of clay shingles supported by wooden beams, there is no insulation to speak of (this is a great source of sound leakage); the studio faces a road with constant road traffic during the day and intermittent traffic at night, the road is about 6 meters from the front of the studio. I have attached a detailed sketch of the space. We are concerned about the sound that both enters and leaves the space; vibration from the traffic; and about achieving good acoustic results in such a small space. The space will be used to record vocals as well as a range of instruments including bass and drums. We will also mix and master, to the extent possible. I have done some research but find that I am still uncertain as to how to proceed. We are in the planning phase. We will be building this in stages do to budget considerations, and we plan to begin with the flooring in the recording area of the studio. We plan on floating the floor using u boats from auralex, is this a good option, or can I get away with using foam or neoprene, would that be cheaper? Philip Newell’s chapter on small studio design,suggests angled adjacent walls, is it worth my while to angle two adjacent walls in the recording area? I seem to recall having read somewhere that the two facing walls (not adjacent) should both be angled, and at equal angles. We have access to good materials here: plywood, sheetrock, drywall, mdf, osb, particle board, and there is a local distributor of a variety of items made from rock wool available in sheets and rolls of varying thickness' and densities, a "soundpanel" - which is a panel of either drywall, or cardboard with rock wool glued to one side. Lightweight materials like the uboats will be sent from the states. Our budget is about $1500 to begin the live room construction thinking of the floor first. We hope to make the most of that money, using effective reasonably priced materials. Thank you all in advanced for your cooperation Mario and Carolina
