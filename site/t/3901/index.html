sharward8 July 2005#13
carl_johnsn wrote:
I was going to put some kind of vent hole in the back of the closet for ventilation for the equipment. . .
Not unless you want to make things seriously dangerous by allowing carbon monoxide into the living space, not to mention compromising the integrity of the firewall that separates the garage from the living space! :shock: Ever seen a car fire? There's a reason (actually lots of them) there are a lot of building codes that govern attached garages. If you want to ventillate your closet, consider an exhaust fan like those in bathrooms and laundry rooms... You can vent out to the side (the same wall as the window) if you want, or up and through the attic and through the roof (don't just exhaust into the attic -- I don't think that will fly either).

carl_johnsn wrote:
My main issue is is is legal/code compliant to build an interior window wall that covers up the existing window? this seems like a non-starter since it cuts off the fire exit to a room that is considered a 'bedroom'.
Great point. Different municipalities adopt different "model codes." My city uses the "California" codes, which are derived from the "Universal" codes. You would need to find out what your city uses and then determine what the requirements are.
I'm sure there are millions of highly illegal studios that sound great but my wife is not going to let our house contain one of them :roll:
She's a smart gal. ;-) I would think that if you removed the window, you'd have to classify the room as "not a bedroom" from that point forward. Although I talk a lot about things meeting code or not, I'm by no means a "code expert." Issues of ventillation and "glazing" (i.e., windows) are, I have learned, taken quite seriously by the authorities. Unfortunately, I have not had much luck looking up code on the 'Net -- only bits and pieces, since the books themselves are copyrighted. :?
