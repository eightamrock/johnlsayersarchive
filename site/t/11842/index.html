John Sayers26 March 2009#6
If you built a completely sealed, single drywall, division between two rooms you would have a degree of isolation from room to room. If you made a nail hole in the drywall between the rooms, yes a nail hole, you immediately link the air and thus allow the sound (air) pressure waves into the other room. It becomes a speaker, as it were, and the bigger the hole, the bigger the speaker. Therefore a soundproof room is an airtight room but then unless you add fresh air, you'll eventually die in it! Most rooms in the home have spaces to allow the air to exchange or you have windows or some one opens an outside door and fresh air gets in - not so with closed up studio. A studio should be airtight and have excellent air conditioning. Ask your aircon guy to UP the fresh air input, you'll appreciate the difference.
xSpace27 March 2009#7
"What is the preferred exterior sealant?" Caulk for building exteriors should be compatible with the material you are using. Brick, vinyl or wood have caulks designed to be used for this material. Caulk for wood might be a siliconized paint-able mildew resistant type. This caulk may not have a high UV rating as the manufacturer expect it to be painted. With brick the caulking used is often a urethane type that is high UV and paint will not stick to it. But it does come in different colors so matching may not be an issue.
