xSpace26 November 2012#2
As long as it is not over packed the insulation is OK. If it is over packed it creates a bridge. In most all cases there is not a need to entirely pack the wall assembly with insulation. Personally I would err on the side of caution and not place so much insulation in the assembly. But this statement "The floor is decoupled as is the ceiling." could be the reason for some of your troubles as well. A decoupled floor is fine but it requires mass...not light weight building materials. So if you have that type of floor then you have more issues coming your way in the not so distant future since you could have developed a diaphragm, like the skin of a drum. The decoupled ceiling was a good idea depending on how you actually did this but the floor. Let us inspect this edited statement: "I went with a two wood frames system....Only the wall flanks were not decoupled, as they are massive concrete and should therefore carry hardly any vibration. If you have an exterior concrete structure and you build a double wall assembly inside of this you have effectively created a multiple air space assembly...the common three leaf known to reduce the TL at low frequency. So this and the other things mentioned have got to be giving you issues. But, if there is sufficient distance between the exterior structure and these interior framed wall assemblies, you may be alright...like three feet or more from the existing structure, more always being better.

This is, what i did following the instructions by the pro acoustic guid. So far, I couldn't reach him by phone. 1) I completely removed the new wood frame and removed the old drywall from the tracking room side. 2) let the builders cut through the floor between the old frame and where the new one is supposed to be. This gap should decouple vibrations of the floor between both rooms, as the acoustic professional suggested. The gap was filled by a special acoustic foam (he also suggested): Würth pure logic. This was done, that the gap would not close again over time. The floor decoupling was tested by hammering on one side and listening on the other side. It worked. 3) I closed the air gaps between hung ceiling and actual ceiling on both sides by fitting in wood pieces and foaming with the same würth stuff mentioned before. 4) I rebuild the new frame, fastening it to floor, ceiling and walls with big screws and duebel. I also did that with the old frame, whereas it was not attached to the floor before. 5) I cut the frame of the hung ceiling (and obviously the drywall there) between the old and the new frame, after screwing it to both frames. Now it is decoupled between both rooms. 6) I added two cable hoses and wooden boxes for light switches. Both do not create bridges between the walls. The cable Hoses are plastic foil held in shape by a light wire spring. 7) I tested by knocking with my fist and a hammer on both ceiling and frame on multiple locations and had some one els check, if any vibrations were transmitted to the other frame or ceiling. It worked at that point. This is, how everything looked then:
