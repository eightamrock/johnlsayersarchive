One word of caution: Sealing your room will make it air tight. That's great, for isolation, but not so great for you: You need to breath! You need to consider ventilation, and from what I remember of the last few times I was in Santo Domingo, you'll also need air conditioning! You can do that, yes. That's usually called a "window plug". If you search the forum, you'll find several examples of how to do that. It needs to be massive (very heavy), and needs to be sealed into the window frame, perhaps with rubber seals if you want to remove it, or caulk seals if you want it to be permanent. But I'd still replace those louvers with a pane of fixed glass if you do a plug.
I have an air conditioning in the room, I'll see how that goes...
Good! But you'll still need to get fresh air into the room, and exhaust stale air to the outside!!!! If you seal the room properly, it will be totally air-tight, with no place for air to come in or go out. That means that as you breathe, you'll use up the oxygen in the room, and increase the CO2 levels. After a while, you'll get a headache. If you stay longer, you'll pass out. And you could even die, if you stay in totally sealed room long enough. Split system air conditioners do not bring fresh air into the room, and they do not exhaust stale air outside. All they do is to cool the air, but they don't add oxygen, and they don't remove CO2. (And if you have a window-mounted air conditioner, instead of a split system, and it does have a fresh air vent, then there's not much point in sealing your room, since that vent breaches your isolation! ) - Stuart -

It's the same anywhere. Carpet absorbs high frequencies quite well, absorbs a little bit of mid frequencies, and nothing at all of low frequencies. That is the exact opposite of what a small room needs. Small rooms need a LOT of low frequency absorption, a fair amount in the mids, and not very much at all in the highs. I'm not surprised that your room sounds bad.

Yes, I realize that.
