Soundman202012 August 2013#6
I'm thinking of the effect of putting the inner-wall insulation on the inside and not in the cavity.
I'm not sure I understand the question: You ALWAYS need insulation in the cavity, regardless of how the wall is built, and you ALWAYS need treatment inside the room, which very often involves absorption (insulation). It doesn't matter where the studs go. The ONLY difference between a conventional wall and an inside-out wall, is that the studs are inside the cavity for a conventional wall, but they are inside the room for an inside-out wall. Apart from that, the theory and practice is exactly the same. - Stuart -
