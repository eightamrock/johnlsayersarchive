Just to get everyone caught up to speed viewtopic.php?f=2&t=17524&p=122456&hilit=texas#p122456 Needless to say I left the forum for a while (with my tail between my legs) and came up with a new plan on some stuff. Ill start at the beginning - Two car detached garage about 20ft from my house and 50ft from neighbor. First order of business wass the overhead garage door and window. Framed both out with 2x6's then 1/5in mdf then a layer of 1/4 particle board then a layer of 1/4 weather treated "wall board" on the outside. Then replaced the old cieling beams that were "sandwiched" 2x4 with 24ft long 2x10 and new 2x4 truses tying into the roof.

israelhands26 September 2013#5
then we were ready for walls. 2 layers of 1/2 with make poor green glue substitute. it was a 80% silicone based adhesive that dried really gummy kinda like green glue. it was called cellador i believe. i got it at lowes but the entire tube was in spanish so i don't really know much about it but it worked pretty good considering my budget. Also redid the exterior door. originally it was an in-swing. got a new frame and door and made it an out-swing with new knob and lock and pick proof locks. then when it came time for the interior door i had to improvise. even though i didn't do the "room within a room" design I still felt it important to have 2 doors with a fairly big gap between the two. So of course i didn't have enough room on the exterior door's frame so i made my own makeshift frame of 2x6's and 2x4's. worked out really well actually but ill get to that later. then after a big rainstorm there was some water creeping in UNDER the walls. at first i was freaking out thinking leaked from somewhere in the attic and ran down the walls. turned out that there was low spots on 3 of my walls on the outside. so when it would rain those low spots would fill up with water and come in between the foundation and my walls this was fixed my digging up a "trench" all the was around my exterior and filling it in with concrete and dirt. then i put up some rain gutters for good measure. havent had a problem since.

israelhands26 September 2013#6
as for AC and heat goes this was an internal debate inside of myself for months. originally i wanted to do a Mitsubishi mini split but later realized funds wouldn't allow it. so here is what i did... When I covered the window when I first started i ran a 4 in pvc pipe horitonally through where the window was. that would later come to be my exhaust. what I ended up using was a Portable AC. I draws in the stale air from the room and exhausts it through a 4 in hose while recycling some of the air back into the room (refrigerated). Now I just had to make a Silencer Box. my original design (pictured) was genius in my head but burnt up the motor in the AC within hours. So i had to open it back up and ran a 3 1/2in dryer hose through the "maze" of the box (not pictured). Works like a champ (so far) 4 months later

I know I did alot of things wrong and cut alot of corners but this was my first build and I dont plan on living in this house for more that 5 years anyway. This was my "practice" studio build. It came out to exactly 500 days (to the day) from when I first started to what I considered a "finished" usable space. And grand total so far was $5,700. All the sound "escapes" out the door NOT the AC exhaust which i was Kinda surprised at. ... Still debating what else I can do to help the sound escaping there
Most likely that is your door seals, and that can be fixed fairly easily by replacing the seals with better quality proper acoustic seals. Take a look at the Zero International web site: they have the stuff you need to fix that.
... or maybe something in between the two doors.
Facing the doors with acoustic absorption will help a bit too, perhaps gaining you 5 dB or even more. But I'd bet that fixing the seals properly on both doors is going to make the big difference. Door seals are very often the problem.
