Hello, I consulted Richard Oliver on the new facility here at Portland Community College, OR. I found him to be very knowledgable especially in regards to smaller rooms, innovative ideas and keeping within the budget. I know he can be a bit "New York" abrasive but he kicks the contractor's a** to keep to the designs & agreement. Any studio he designed that is still in business will take a call from you, do the research it's worth it. In my case however, our architects decided not to use a studio design consultant other than the industrial acoustic guy they usually use. I am now embarked on a 2 year corrective construction / treatment phase costing more than the original consult. You have to decide by putting together a performa. A lot of large scale studios (read expensive) only last until the seed money runs out. Then they either find new investors, to go another 3 years before bankruptcy, or they fold. Not that I am against this policy because I get to buy good microphones cheap when they go under. I recently corresponded with George Massenberg about coming to PCC for a forum and he remarked how much less money he makes now than just a couple of years ago. It seems everyone is feeling the bite of how the music business is changing. Keep it real in regards to overhead and expected income from your market demographic. My $.02 worth.
