len-morgan29 August 2006#7
If you are going to use "pot" lights (you don't mean "grow" lights do you? :-) ), make sure you get the kind that can have insulation all around them. In case you or someone else doesn't know, there are two types. One can have insulation all around and on top of them, and the other (usually cheaper) type, have to have a 3" minimum clearance around them to avoid a fire. This means you'd have to "box" them in and you'd then have a "hole" in your isolation. As to going overboard, it's a matter of power (more lights=higher electric bill and more heat). If it were me and I was going to put in that many lights, I'd try and group them into two three sets that can be turned on and off individually to set the mood. I've found that the amount of light that works well for mixing isn't nearly enough for construction purposes. One advantage you'd have by having at least two separate circuits is that you could power one set through a UPS in case the power goes out so you could safely get out of the room. I've found that if I loose power in my studio and I'm not in the reception area, I have to stop and think about where I am and where I just left everything on the floor to avoid breaking my neck. Unfortunately, I've got all my wiring covered up now so I'm going to be installing emergency lighting in all the rooms to avoid liability problems (and skinned knees) :-) len

Since almost every room I've seen here and elsewhere has corner absorbers/bass traps, why not make one that also covers this box? Hmmm... not many people cover there service panel. Reason, with out looking up code & like the link above from Sharward certain things have to be addressed. The electric service panel has to be easily accessible by all occupants of building and emergency workers. If it is in a closet, the closet needs to be at least 3' wide PLUS many other stipulations. An extreme situation to think about: If there is ever a fire in your building AND no life is being threatened in the building AND IF electric is on ON, there is no outside disconnect switch, your main switch is buried with something you've built and firemen fear there own safety with electric on, then they will let the building burn until the power company arrives and disconnects electric service. An emergency worker will not simply pull the meter can, which is dangerous, they'll wait for power company. If I were you I'd at least get an electrician to come and look. Maybe try to get a quote for moving the thing but at the same time find out your options. Getting a quote should not cost anything, you can get some answers to other electric questions you have and the electrician may suggest something that nobody else has thought of. Marc
