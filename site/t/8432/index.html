Hi, First off, let me say, this forum is great - I've been lurking here for a month or so and have found a lot of great information. Now I guess it's time to see if I pulled it all together properly. :) Project Details: I recently purchased a house in a standard residential area with a partially finished detached garage I'd like to convert to a studio space. Currently, I'm in the planning stage and have a ballpark construction budget of $10k. I have an experienced carpenter who's willing to swap his skills on the build for recording time when it's done (yay!), plus a crew of bandmates who all are experienced in basic renovation so I'm able to put it all into materials. I'd be using the space for editing and scoring (my main income), and would also like to have my band rehearse there as well as record other bands. The garage is suituated so it's on the furthest part of the lot from all the neighbors, but due to the types of bands I work with (loud, angry, metal and industrial), I'd like to ensure maximum isolation. If I can make so I can record drums at 3am without a noise complaint, I'll be in heaven. Isolation Details: City ordinances require 70dBA, so I'm shooting for 65dBA to have a safety margin. My band is peak 116dBA, average 110dBA (measured during rehearsal 3ft. from kick drum, 3 ft. from ground). This means I need a minimum of 51dB reduction, but more is better. Currently, there is a garage door, but I've decided to pretend it's not there, remove the opener and opener's mounting hardware, and wall right over it. Same for the two exterior windows. I plan on using cameras instead of putting in windows, to help interior isolation. Building Details: The building has a useable interior space of 23'4"x23'4"x9'7". The floor is a cement slab, with a sloping lip. The walls (as near as I can tell) are siding->sheathing->plywood-> 3"insulation->1/4" drywall using 2x4 studs 12"oc. The roof is standard sloped shingle/plywood roof. Celing joists are 2x4. I'll add some pictures to the thread tomorrow, when my wife shows me how to work the camera. I'd like to have the following areas: control room, drum booth, gtr/piano room, vocal booth. See the attached design. Since my focus is on editing I need to have a good sounding control room and made that the focus of my design. I used the ratio 1:1.28:1.54 from Rod's book, and the geometry for a quad room (since I'll be doing 5.1 eventually) from "Building a Recording Studio" - Jeff Cooper. I plan on making the ceiling peaks 9ft, 7ft at the walls, and based my calculations on that instead of the actual height. My questions are: 1) Am I trying to fit ten pounds of crap into a 5 pound bag with all these rooms? 2) Am I correct in assuming I should use Rod's wall construction #5 (STC57 pg.55) given what's already in place on the wall? I'd be removing the existing drywall and then adding another single leaf wall with an 8" air gap, correct? 3) I'm open to suggestions as far as what to do with the floors and ceiling. In the drum booth I'm planning on lifting the floor to 1', following the design available in Cooper's book. For the live room, control room, etc. I'm unsure what to put over the cement - or should I just leave it cement for a touch of liveness? I appreciate any insights and advice you guys might have for me. Thanks again for running such a great place. Peace and Groove, The Thump - owner Recluse Studios

According to the Rod Gervais book, you should be OK with isolation using the same concrete floor. You can gain a little more by ripping up your concrete and pouring seperate slabs, but that's a lot of work and money. If you don't have Rod's book, you really should get it before you build any further. I wish I had read it before I had my house built. At least I have it now beofre I start my studio build.

Thump, Looks good and like you are on track, Gut feel - remember you need "air" between your mics and kit and space for the stands etc so make sure you factor this in - measure up your mates kit in garage, him sitting there and place mic stands.....chalk it out - exactly with all angles perfect. Factor in the drums facing different walls too - mine has different treatment for each wall section so you have 4 x different combo's. ;) This will tell you a lot re what you need to do. YOu also need space for the couch and moving mix chair back into stereo field without bumping into someone strumming away behind you too. My larger booth covers this beautifully - BUT I would NOT want any less room in there for drums and "ambient" mics...... My 2c Out of time gotta run - will check back L8r Take Care Cheers Eliot
