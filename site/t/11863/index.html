I'm not the most qualified to comment on your duct question, but I'll try to at least get you pointed to a stating point... Obviously HVAC is a seriously complex issue that when done right, it's what will make your studio... and when done wrong... ooooof. First, the main trunks... Your trunks are typically a galvanized trunk, like you are already finding. Then they are lined with a rigid insulation like a Johns-Manville or Knauf duct liner. One thing I ran into was a county code wonk... Since my HVAC is in the attic space above the studio, our inspector's require that all galvanized duct be wrapped on the exterior with a minimum of R-4 insulation. Regardless of whether the duct is lined or not, this was a requirement that I had to meet. The reason is because of condensation that forms on the exterior of the ducts during the long cooling season, has a tendency to cause rust and degradation of joints. I mention this is because you're in GA, and may run into the same thing. Home Depot has flexible duct that at least you can start with looking at. It's an R-6 rated duct, from what I can tell. There's also QuietFlex and Thermaflex for starters. There are plenty of others out there to look at... almost too many. In my case, I already knew that the HVAC was well over my capabilities to handle. So, I contacted a bunch of contractors to try and get the best price. The contractor I settled on wasn't the cheapest, but the one who had the most experience in dealing with high-end home construction and at least knew what the goal was for getting the system as quiet as possible. e.g. long sweeping bends, the importance of keeping smooth transitions, etc. I'm still on saw service power, so I've not fired up either of the units yet, so I'm still crossing my fingers that we're going to have a quiet system, but at least I'm confident that if there is a problem, that the contractor WILL resolve the issue... and it's all at his expense from this point on. Low noise registers are something I'm going to start on VERY soon... like in the next few days. :) I'll actually be building my own. Essentially, they are simple slats that will direct the air straight down. (All of my supply boxes are ceiling penetrations.) The plan is to ease the edges of the slats to a 1/4" radius to reduce turbulence and thus noise. HTH, Max
