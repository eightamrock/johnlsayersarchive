Soundman202025 March 2011#11
What Glenn is saying is that you can improve the effectiveness of the diagonal traps across the corners by also adding ordinary pink fluffy fiberglass behind them, in the triangular space behind them. You can use the concept of the "superchunk" bass trap for that (use the search button on the forum to find out alllll about superchunks), and one good way of hiding the ugly fiberglass in the corner is with a simple frame made from 1" x 1" wood, which holds plastic sheeting over the fiberglass, and cloth over the plastic sheeting. (The plastic is to prevent the device from taking too much of the highs and mids out of the room: you want to treat the room, not kill it! Small rooms need a lot of bass trapping, but a side effect of bass traps is that they also kill the mids and highs, but if you cover some of them with plastic then that reflects the mids and highs back into the room where you want them, and only lets the bass through to be "trapped"). - Stuart -

This is the link for the design/construction that I followed. http://www.radford.edu/shelm/acoustics/ ... #Broadband This was my first time working with this material, and his guides were very helpful ! As you mentioned, it's like working with razor blades :shock: I did buy some useful tools to help: 1. Get a good pair of metal shear cutters. 2. I found a 'guide bracket' that helps maintain a right angle for the corners. 3. Bought a couple of small needle-nose vice grips. They were a couple bucks each, but they give you the extra fingers [hands] to hold everything while you rivet. 4. AND ... a good rivet gun! Do the double rivet/connection. File down any sharp edges. Wear GLOVES ! When you start building these things, they are flimsy as all get out ... but as you continue, you'll see them get better. Once you put the corner spacer on [mine were for 4" thick panels], a big improvement happens. When you finally add the 2 sheets of 2'x4'x2" panels ... then mount the top of the cage, using the needle-nose to hold them together ... get your rivets in ... if you do it properly, you'll find that you can roll these things around the room! They are surprisingly sturdy. I still have to cover mine with batting, and then the final fabric ... in the meantime, I have them leaning against the CR walls JUST so that I can stand being in the room :mrgreen: Once I get my walls finished, and then get the flooring put on ... I'm looking forward to adding the 'chunky corner traps'. I'm curious to hear what affect they'll have on the room. Wishing you the best! Sincerely
