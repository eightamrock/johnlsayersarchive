Thanks for the pic, it clears things up. Unfortunately you can not leave a gap and expect any significant sound isolation. My suggestion is to build the wall top plate right up to pipes cutting grooves in the drywall for the pipes, build the drywall right up to the ceiling and sealing the remaining spaces around the pipes with caulk. Good luck and keep asking questions if it isn't clear. Andre

Don't forget the requirement for fireblocking here. In the picture detailed here, you'd have to block up against the concrete below the piping and around where the 45 joint is. There are many pictures of fireblocking examples on the site. Rod's put up a bunch of these and I'd bet they are somewhere in the sticky lists. Basically - frame so your top plate is where you extend a thin plywood / 5/8 drywall / compressed rock wool sandwitched to brush up against the concrete and use fire rated caulking to bridge the slight gap where the rockwool touches. Consider your options for moving that pvc as well - what is it supporting? A basement pump system? Are you willing to do plumbing to re-route them for a more efficient frame & drywall detail for sound isolation? I did this in two spots for my basement build.
