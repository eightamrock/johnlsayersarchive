I guess the biggest question here is: How does the room sound right now? And how do you want it to sound? I mean, are there any really objectionable problems with the room, such as excessive reverberation, flutter echoes, bad resonance at certain frequencies, rattling, etc.? If not, then maybe it doesn't need much treatment. Have you done any measurements in there, to see what type of problems you might have? It's a beautiful room: It would be a shame to spoil the aesthetics with acoustic treatment if it isn't really needed.
AVare22 November 2008#9
It is a beautiful room. Are you doing the labor yourself? What are your neighbors and how close? All I can see are trees outside the window. You wrote you were going to start another thread on the windows. Attached is an skp with what dimensions I do know. It is 35k in size. Correct dimensions and add architectural details. DO NOT play with trying furniture etc, as once they are tried, copies of the components are kept in the file, bloating the file size. The room has about a 21,000 ft^3. A beautiful recoding space. This would need a reverb time of around 0.7 seconds. That requires ~1,400 Sabines of absorption. This is equivalent to 175 2'x4' absorbers. With the original budget of $3k that is about $17 per absorber. Guilford 703 is around $4/ft^@ last I checked. IOW you could not even cover the absorbers with that budget. An obvious start is to use fewer and listen to the room. If you are doing single instruments, have you considered making tube trap type absorbers and creating acoustiwalls and large goboes that you can adjust for different amounts of ambience? What does the room sound like right now? For mixing you will need some closer walls to provide the proper ambience. I am looking forward to your updated skp file. Andre

bassman30 November 2008#14
I think the gobo/false wall approach yields the most flexibility and best results for each application (ie Recording in a more live space with some isolation capacity with gobos, and mixing with complete false walls to enclose a "mix room" inside of the space) Having the room treated in one way for all of your purposes will be a compromise for each use. :shot: -ashley

bassman1 December 2008#16
Allaire did not have the same approach at all. They had dedicated control rooms which were treated very differently from the recording spaces. As a recording room, your church will be awesome! :yahoo: As a control room, it will be ultimately dissapointing, IMHO. :horse: The reason I'm chiming in on your thread is that I, too, own a church and worked in the main sanctuary space as a combined control room and mix space for over a year. What is most difficult is gauging how much reverb and ambience is in your final mix since the reverb from your room will mask the ambience in the recording. Depending on the type o music you do, that might not be a big issue. Who knows... I'm just giving you advice based on my experience in a similar space. -ashley
