Hi there Paulus, and welcome to the forum! :)
recent storms in the UK blew it down to the ground!
:shock: Ouch! But perhaps a blessing in disguise, as now you get a studio instead!
I may or may not construct the two iso booths in the live room, it will depend on budget and whether or not you guys see any design issues with them being there. The idea is to have many options while recording e.g. using the two iso booths for tracking guitar/bass with the drums in the main room.
Having a couple of iso booths is generally a good idea, if you expect to be tracking large groups, or if you like a lot of separation for your instruments. However, I would put those at the other end of the live room, where you'll be still be able to see them through your glass sliding door. I'll get to the reasons for that below.
There is also a third iso booth at the back of the control room which will be mainly used for vocals. I want to use the space to the best of it's potential so please help me if you feel I am wasting space.
That might not be such a good idea, since the rear of the control room is where your bass trapping and first-reflection trapping goes. If you could figure a way to work that in, then maybe it would be OK.
Access to the live room from the control room is via a central sliding door (it's not very clear on the drawing)
Where does it slid to? That space seems to be about 6' wide, and by the time you allow for frames, trim, stops, seals and all the rest, you'll end up with an actual opening of about about 30". Is that enough? It might be, if you don't need to drag kick drums through there, but if your facility has to be compliant with regulations about wheel-chair accessibility, then that probably isn't enough.
the soffit mounted speakers will be above some small windows in to each corresponding iso booth.
That's the part I said I'd get to, above. If your speakers are mounted correctly, then those windows will be in just the right place for you to look at the kneecaps of your musicians... :) The acoustic axis of the speaker has to be 1.2m above the floor (47-3/4"). It is allowable to raise it sightly, but then you need to tilt it down so that it is still aiming at your ears. But you can't tilt it much! If you tilt it more than about 10°, you start creating psycho-acoustic issues that can affect the way your brain determines direction. In other words, you could end up hearing things in the wrong place in the stereo image. But before you get to 10°, there can already be issues with reflections from the top of the desk and the surface of your console. So 7° is about the practical limit, and I wouldn't do more than 5° if I can help it. So unless you have some really tiny speakers, or a really ling room, there's not much chance you can get your speaker up high enough to have those windows down below. Yes, I know that there are numerous photos on the web of studios with speakers mounted much higher than that, and tilted at larger angles, but those were either designed by experts who know how to deal with the issues, or are designed to cover the rear client seating rather than the mix position, or are supplemented by other speakers mounted down lower. So that's why I would suggest moving your iso booths to the far end of the live room, and putting bigger windows in them, for a better view. That would allow you to do your soffits and speakers correctly, and still have iso booths. You could also make the iso booths bigger, since there would be no need to separate them around the CR door: In fact, they could actually butt up against each other, with each being half the width of the room.
There will be concrete block work for the external walls with a 4" air gap between the internal and external walls.
Excellent. But what will the inner-leaf walls be made from? Drywall on framing?
1. Regarding the dimensions of the rooms, does the general layout look ok or are there some major issues staring us in the face?
With those two extra rooms at the back of the CR, there is no place to put your very essential bass trapping in the rear corners. You also have the client couch set back into an alcove on the rear wall, but that is going to be problematic, acoustically, and there is no space at all for trapping behind it. I would re-think the rear of the control room. It's also not a good idea to have the bathroom opening directly into the control room, for several reasons. Odors, for one, and humidity for another. Also, did you check those dimensions in a room mode calculator, such as Bob Golds' or Andy Mel's? You should confirm that you have reasonably decent ratios, or at least that you are far away from the bad ones.
3. I am not sure whether the monitors mounted above small windows is a good idea or not?
:) See above...
4. I am planning to put bass trapping in the control room in the rear wall above the sofa (like an overhang, not seen in the drawing) and also in the front wall where the speaker soffits will be. Will this be the best I can do or can you suggest other solutions?
You will need a lot more than that. The smaller the room is, the more bass trapping it needs, proportionally. For that size room, you need something like large superchunks in both rear vertical corners, plus under the soffits, plus the entire rest of the rear wall with thick absorption, and probably some of the wall-ceiling corners too.

Soundman202014 April 2014#13
Isolation wise sliding glass door shouldn't be parallel as John wrote
That's a pretty old thread you are quoting, Ken! John no longer angles doors (or windows) since it isn't necessary: viewtopic.php?f=1&t=14215&start=4 MSM resonance doesn't depend on the shape of the cavity, just the depth at each point. If you angle a window or door, then one part of the cavity is not as deep as the rest, so at that point the resonance is higher, and the isolation is worse. It's best to go for the deepest cavity you can, to get the MSM resonance down as low as possible, which in turn means that the isolation is as good as it can be. - Stuart -

Soundman202015 April 2014#21
So, I don't have them to hand right now, and also they are available in different thicknesses. The ones I will use will be 450mm long x 215mm high x 100mm - 200mm wide.
Assuming you use the 100mm ones, and have only one course, that would give you about 73 kg/m2 surface density, which is about six or seven times higher than for a single sheet of 5/8" drywall. In other words, it is equivalent to about seven layers of drywall. That would give you overall transmission loss of about 40 dB, just from that. When you consider that in conjunction with your inner-leaf, you should be approaching 60 dB of isolation, which is pretty good. - Stuart -
