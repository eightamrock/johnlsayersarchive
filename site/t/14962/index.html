Soundman202020 October 2010#15
Agree absolutely with Brien. The problem is this: if air can get through it, then so can sound. With an ordinary HVAC duct there are things you can do safely to control that. With a flue, you can't. You cannot line the flue with duct liner, you cannot put multiple 90° bends in it, and you cannot put a silencer in it. I don't know much about flues, but I do know that hot air has to draw through it in a certain way, in order to control the burn correctly, and evacuate the exhaust gasses correctly. Anything you do to the flue will interfere with that. It seems to me that if you can't take it out, then you are stuck with the noise. I'm surprised that a wood burning stove is even legal in LA, with all the tough smog laws. I have a nice fireplace here at home in Santiago, and haven't been able to use it in over 25 years, due to the smog laws here, and I reckon that LA is even tighter on those than we are! - Stuart -

xSpace21 October 2010#17
"Sound isn't really going to go through the stove and up the chimney like it would in a fireplace. " It's a conduit basically so yea, sound/air goes in and out. It probably is only the tip of your iceberg in attenuating the sound that is coming into this environment and you may be back later, after this fails to rectify the condition. So, final answer and you didn't hear it from me. Build a cover out of James Hardi type material [cement based product] that can cover the exterior spark arrestor. That is the path, the leading edge, where sound is gaining access. Commercial grade glue and brad nails will be how you affix the parts. You do not want to use any wood on the inside of the cap, you want to make it as resistant to the heat as possible. Can't help ya with how to attache it as anything you do may well be a potential water trap or present a path for water. Like I could say use a piano hinge but then you have screws that can rust. But the cap will not be weatherproof unless you take great care in making it so. And if you do, we go right back to the smoke inside the room, heat in places you do not want it scenario. Most likely you will find that windows, doors and poor wall construction have a stake in this claim. Time will tell :)
