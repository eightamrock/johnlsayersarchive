Could you also assist me to understand your thought processes with the vocal booth. I see one in your designs. I'm not sure I'll actually include one anymore, so I'm just wondering what your thoughts on that are?
Depending on the "vocal sound" you're going after - whether it's a more "live" sound with a lot of room artifacts detectable or a "dryer, more intimate" sound with primarily the voice alone - and the amount of isolation you need from the ambient noise in your surrounding environment, a vocal booth can be very useful for obtaining a clean vocal track. It's important to remember that sometimes even a vocal booth itself can introduce unwanted coloration to your recordings. If you do build/use one, try to avoid having a "square box". Instead, a 4' x 5' x 7' inside dimensions work better. Also, in most cases, when using a smaller booth (like the above mentioned) a "deader approach" works better than a "livelier" one.
Also seeing as how I'm not sure about one, Vocal work will be done pretty much where the mic is. Should I put some other treatments on the two smaller walls either side of the lounge seat?? What type?
To begin with, I would suggest absorber panels as illustrated in the "Willo 1c" rendering. Keep in mind that even with these acoustic room treatments, your room may still be prone to picking up any ambient noises around you - whether it be birds/crickets chirping to passing traffic, the neighbor's kids playing, aircraft, etc. due to the open doorways, windows, etc. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

kendale21 July 2007#36
Aloha, I'll try to answer each question as best as I can. :wink:
What do I use 703? What is 703? As far as I can tell 703 is a rigid style fiberglass insulation.
"703" refers to Owens Corning 703 (OC 703) – Semi-rigid boards for use on equipment, vessels and air conditioning ductwork. 705 – A high strength rigid board for use on chillers, hot and cold equipment, and heating and air conditioning ductwork where high abuse resistance and good appearance are required. An alternative to OC 703 could be something like ROXUL AFB™ (Acoustical Fire Batt) a batt insulation product designed specifically for application in wall/floor systems where acoustical performance and fire resistance are the primary concerns. This non-combustible lightweight product has excellent acoustical dampening properties and is dimensionally stable which makes it ideal for friction fit into wall partitions and system applications from party walls to plant/manufacturing walls. The product is chemically inert which means that it will not promote corrosion. The AFB product comes in a number of thicknesses to meet the requirements of both retrofit and new construction applications. There are other companies that manufacture similar products that would work as well. What you need to look for is a “semi rigid insulation, with a density of 2.5 – 3 pcf (pounds per cubic foot). A semi rigid/rigid insulation will hold is shape better than say, the “pink fluffy type insulation” often used in insulating attics and walls.
Having not used it before do I affix it to the frame with a screw or glue.
There have been several approaches when installing the insulation using either of the following: Heavy duty cord/wire/nylon strapping fastened with screws/staples to support the insulation in place until the cloth facing, slats (when appropriate) and back panel can be assembled.
As for wood the only one I saw was to use pine. Can I coat the wood in Lacquer or Varnish or does that affect the absorption or resonation of the unit?
Pine works and is fairly easy to work with, although it does have a tendency for leaking a bit of sap from time to time as well as having being somewhat “knotty.” Basically, you can use what is available in your area and fits in your budget. Yes, you can use a light coat of lacquer or varnish finish. Be sure to inspect each piece of lumber for warp, crown, thickness, knots and potential sap issues, as they may hinder any finish you may choose to use.
What is green glue?
Green Glue is a damping material, it's purpose is to raise the damping of structures in which it is included. Here's their link for much more info - http://www.greengluecompany.com/impactOfGreenGlue.php
Is that what I use to seal these suckers cause I see everywhere these things need to be airtight? If green glue is not what I use to seal what do I use?
No, this would not be a good choice for sealing. What is recommended is "Acoustic Caulk" which is different from your garden variety bath and tub caulk. Here's a link to read up on it - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2045
What type of fabric do I use to cover the insulation in either the slat resonators or absorber panels? Does it matter what I use?
In several different ways, yes, it does matter. What you need to look for is an acoustically transparent material (as in, if you can blow air through it, it's probably okay) like say, burlap; and is treated with a flame resistant process. If the material is aesthetically pleasing as well, it may help the creative process. Here's one example- http://www.silentsource.com/acousticore ... hart1.html
2.5 - 3p/cf 35 -40 kg/m3 Rock wool or Fiberglass doesn't matter, semi -rigid or rigid is optimum.
Basically, yes. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

kendale21 July 2007#38
Aloha,
Do the treatments have to go the full height of the wall? Will it suffice to go to the top of the window frame for example?
Top of the window frames should be fine, provided that's at least a couple of feet above your ears @ mix position.
Can I use Construction Ply rather than MDF for Absorbers??
I'm not sure what "Construction Ply" is like in your area, but if you do go with ply, I would go with 3/4" (at least one side finish sanded) [rough side in] for a couple of reasons: - Both MDF & ply will resonate at some point/frequency [not desirable], but the thicker the material used, the lower the resonance.[better] - Since this will be a part of your studio's "furniture" so to speak, unless you like the "rustic look" a finish sanded, lacquered/varnished surface looks better/more professional. You may want to avoid pressure treated ply/lumber as they sometimes cause skin irritations in some individuals. Aloha 8)
