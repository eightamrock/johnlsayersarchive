Here is one perspective, a few excerpts from the SAE website and a few more links that may be helpful - http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m. This size room means a good sized working area with space for the musos, friends and hangers on. Also because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension. i.e. 6m x 5m. The studio, on the other hand, requires a different set of considerations. The first to consider is do you want just one room or more? The trend nowadays is for more than one studio so the you can get isolation between players and different acoustics for each room. The acoustics you want for a live drum sound is totally different than you want for a vocal for example. I recommend at least 2 rooms. You can put drums in one, guitars in the other, keys, vocals and bass (DI) in the control room or maybe the band in one and the vocalist in the other. Getting separation between guitars and drums is usually OK in a good sized room but keeping them out of the vocal track is hard if they are all in the same room. – John Sayers

I think an important point to note is that John stated "I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least..." Not only does the larger size (with best case ratios) accommodate the extra (albeit sometimes unwanted) bodies, but I think it's important to consider that the smaller the rooms get, the more the modal issues are going to be compounded by way of being so close together. While acoustic treatments help, in as much as is possible, try to start with a room size that has a better modal response. I think you'll find less work, frustrations and complications in applying those treatments to even out your room/s response. It certainly is a better starting point to begin with rather than having to apply tons of treatments to remedy a less than perfect situation. Happy reading, Aloha 8)
