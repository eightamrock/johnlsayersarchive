Soundman202011 February 2010#4
Ok, I've now made a picture of the room,
I think you didn't understand what I was saying: not a diagram, but actual photographs of the interior of your room: Several photos, taken from different places, and looking in different directions. Plus, a complete, accurate SketchUp model, in 3D, or your room, with the correct dimensions, and showing the exact location of your speakers. desk, mix position, windows, doors, construction materials, bass traps, etc. It is impossible to guess what your room looks like, how you have arranged your mix position and speakers, what the shape is, etc. That small "diagram" you posed only gives a rough idea of the size, and shows that the room is not symmetrical (which might be a big problem too!), but it does not show anything of the things that are critical for determining the acoustics of your room. Without all of the above, there really is no way that anyone can help you. We cannot see what you are seeing, unless you show it to us, in great detail!
I'm confused about the high freqs which of some reason lacks in the measurements,
A wild guess here, but you perhaps have too much absorption that is affecting your highs, and not enough that is affecting your lows. There are ways to deal with that, but first we'd have to see what you have in there already. Maybe it is even due to something simple like thin carpet on the floor or walls, sucking out the highs, or maybe curtains. Hard to say without seeing what you have....
So what about further treatment?
Further to WHAT? We don't know what you already have in there, so speaking about additional treatment is pointless. You might already have too much of one kind, or you might have too much of another kind, or not enough of both. Unless you show us, we'll never know... - Stuart -
