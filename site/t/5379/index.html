knightfly8 January 2006#3
Ceilings do indeed want to slope UP to the REAR, but speakers don't have to be so their tweeters are exactly at ear height - within reason, you can elevate speakers and then tilt them so the tweets still aim at your ears when at the desk. Some reasons NOT to go very far afield with this - if you have others listening at the rear of the room, too much tilt may cause a loss of high frequencies back there. Too much tilt can cause the direct sound to bounce off the desk, mixer, etc, and into your ears causing early reflections. And a third reason - if speakers are more than about a foot higher than the ears, especially in a small room, the angle becomes steep enough that your ears don't hear high frequencies the same as if they arrive in a level plane - this is due to the shape of the Pinnae, or the outer, visible part of the ear. Its shape is meant to give directional ability so you know where the danger is coming from (presumably so you can run the OTHER way :wink: ) - So, as a result you will likely mix too bright if your speakers are too far off axis vertically. In the Acoustics forum there is a topic, "are my speakers in a null", that should help you - go here http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2441 download the harmon spreadsheet I linked to, and set one of the dimensions to 100 feet 0" - that will show you peaks and nulls in percent of total dimension, which you can convert to your room height - read the rest of that link and it will help you figure out dimensions that will work for your situation. Your sloping ceiling plan is a good one, BTW - and not just for keeping your speakers/head out of modal problems... Steve
