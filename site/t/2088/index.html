knightfly11 August 2004#9
Wiz, "I put a glass slider in each buiilding , facing each other" - if you do this, your isolation between "pods" should be fairly good at lower frequencies, possibly getting worse at higher ones - this would depend on glass thickness and (somewhat) door sealing at the sliding joints. Using 6mm glass for one slider (NOT double-glazed) and 7mm glass for the other, with an air gap of 1 meter, your mass-air-mass resonance is around 21 hZ (meaning good isolation down to about 1.5X that, or 30 hZ) and as I predicted before running the #'s, higher frequency isn't as great as it would be with higher mass (no surprise there) - overall, looks like around STC 54 between pods, with about 32 dB Transmission Loss @ 50 hZ. Not too bad. If you don't need much iso between either pod and the rest of the world, this could work - however, when checking isolation between either pod and the rest of the world, you're down to STC 29, with only 14 dB isolation @ 50 hZ - this means that your neighbors will "enjoy" your drum solo much more than your wife in the other pod - possibly to the point of wanting to "share" it with the police... If you try to solve this last problem by enclosing the area between the pods and building good iso-walls to accomplish that, it could probably be done - you would, however, need to make sure that the joining to at least ONE of the pods is totally elastomeric, and NOT resonant at audio frequencies - otherwise, flanking would negate much of the advantage of the separate pods. Also, by closing off this "breeze-way", you couple more bass frequencies between the pods because the sound pressure between the pods isn't being "vented" through a VERY low impedance acoustic path anymore. Essentially, what you're describing is a 2-room studio with a WIDE double wall between rooms (good), and a MASSIVE sound leak to the outdoors. However, it brings up a valid point - when doing double sliders between rooms, if you can spare the floor space you will get better iso at lower frequencies with a wider gap... Steve
