Help needed with covering conduit trays in slab

Started by lobe on 28 December 2006. 4 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 7809.

lobe28 December 2006#1
I am nearly finished my first studio. It's got a 3.5x 4.8m control room and a 3 x 3.2 m booth attached. It's all in core-filled concrete. The soundproofing is great, but... it sounds like a cathedral at present (Haven't started the process of bass trapping and absorption.) I was going to put in wood laminate or cork to conserve a quite low ceiling height of only 1.95 metres. However, I got some form work done on the slab...suggested in "The Manual", so that I will always be able to change my studio cabling. There are three 195mmx 3000mmm conduit trays in the slab at present, with planks of wood which fit neatly into them. However, the very nature of having the wooden trays in the slab means I will always have slight variations in my floor level. Trying to laminate around them will be tricky I fear. However, covering the cable trays up sort of defeats the purpose of having them done in the first place, so I am keen to come up with a workable solution. A simple option might be to simply paint the slab and coat it a non-slip polyurethane and then polish the tray planks. Then I'd lay a bamboo mat or rug down for comfort at the back of the room. What do you think of this idea? Won't help my cathedral sound though...though...though. I am loathe to put too many rugs in, in case it eats up too many high frequencies and leaves me with a bassy, boxy sounding room. I've thought of cork too, but again, I will have trouble creating a nice even floor, with my transitions to the floor trays. Any ideas, suggestions, tips would be hugely apprecaited. Lobe :)
sharward28 December 2006#2
1.95 meters (6 feet, 4 inches) of vertical clearance? :shock: Wow... :roll: Is that even legal in your part of the world, mate? Consider an etched stain concrete. It looks a bit like marble 8), it may be cheaper than laminate (definitely cheaper in USA), and it's extremely durable. Area rugs are good because you can roll them up or move them around to change their acoustic affects. --Keith :mrgreen:
