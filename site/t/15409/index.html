fallenmunk22 December 2010#1
Hello all, I'm new to the group. I've been lurking a lot and learning a bunch. Working on the learning curve of sketchup but haven't gotten past the basics yet. I'm designing a basement studio to be built in summer of 2011. I have between $5,000 and $10,000 to spend. I'm experienced in carpentry, electrical, plumbing etc. and will be doing all of the work. My problem is creativity. I just can't come up with a design that doesn't look like crap, and I'm afraid it might sound even worse. I'm including a crude skp file, like I said, I'm still learning. You'll notice the space has a support post right in the middle of it. It might be abe to be incorporated into the design, but I'm not opposed to ordering an engineered beam to carry the load and eliminate the post entirely. Although that would be a lot of work to support the house to get the old beams out and replace with a new one. Ceiling height is not bad for an older basement @ 7' 10". Ceiling is uninterrupted except for a 6" X 8" beam that carries the load down the center. The studio will be used for recording my personal projects. I'm a guitar player, learning drums and might do some vocals if I had a vocal booth to practice where no one can hear me. :) So the studio should have a control room, live room for drums, and a vocal booth. Vocal booth can double for amp isolation when recording with drums. Main sound concern is to not hear anything upstairs. Thank you for providing such a great resource, and any advice I can get is very welcome. (edit) Just realized I forgot to attach an direction arrow on the stairs. The large square is the upper landing, the stairs go down from there. I also uploaded my latest file, still crude, but learning lots. Still have to build real stairs, but the footprint is shown. Thanks, Mike Salem, Oregon

fallenmunk24 December 2010#3
Basement walls are solid concrete from poured formwork, 8" thick, floor is smooth cement. Probably will fill in the snap-tie conical shaped holes in the wall surfaces before sealing the concrete. Ceiling is open floor joists. I have no obstructions other than that central beam with support posts. All wiring and piping was relocated to reside between the floor joists. The water heater and plumbing stack is inside the bath/laundry/utility area so will not be an issue. The only house stuff that is a very slight obstruction is the breaker panel on one of the walls. I'll add that to the drawing pronto. :) I've owned the house for 20 years, finally getting to do what I wanted when I moved in. Also realized that I don't "need" a vocal booth since I don't plan on tracking vocals at the same time as the instruments, but it might be nice to have one to double as an iso room for an amp. After playing around with sketchup, I realized that I do want to keep the stairs open so a small lounge near the bottom would be nice and it might keep the access to the bathroom clear. I might even put a small patch bay in the bathroom just in case I want to get creative. I should also mention that the bathroom/laundry/utility area is not yet framed so that can be a bit flexable in the door placement and framing for isolation. Learning a lot from browsing older projects but I've yet to find one close enough to work for mine. I've played around and still not having luck with a room ratio for my control room that leaves me with enough size for my live room. Mike

I wish I could go smaller on the bathroom, but there is a lot of stuff not shown in the drawing yet, like a large closet that will contain a large water heater, sump pump, plumbing stack and storage shelves. Then on the other side, a washer, dryer and a large soaking tub (think the size of a small hot tub). The main thing driving the bath size is the layout of the plumbing which is already in place. No way I'm taking a jackhammer to the floor to relocate that stuff. I'm willing to change just about anything for the right studio design, but that one is off the table because it's not just a few pipes that would need redone, there is also a drainage system under the floor with perf pipe that goes to the sump to keep the basement dry as a bone. The only reason I know all of this stuff is because the whole foundation/basement was replaced in 1996. I put most of it in myself and it all had to pass inspection to code. I've not had a drop of water down there since 1996 and the sump pump has only cycled a couple of times during heavy continued rain. I wish I would have done things a lot differently back then, but at the time never envisioned that I would finally get to this point. At the time it seemed like I had so much room down there after the work because the basement was originally only a half basement with 6' tall ceilings. It was like a small cave. Mike
