AVare wrote:
Xspace; Green Glue works as an inter-spatial damping material. Not as a sound absorber in the traditional sense. Therefore the backer rod and caulking are not required.
That is (of course) only in the body of the drywall - which I don't usually see any reason to caulk anyway - if you don't use green glue then just tape the seams like normal with joint compound. BUT - the perimiter of the ceiling (against the walls) still requires backer rod and caulk. As does the walls against the other vertical surfaces and the ceiling floor. Juat making sure there is no confusion here on the issue. Rod

rod gervais8 July 2008#42
rsmooth wrote:
well if the wood is directly to the concrete then it would be in contact with water..i guess the idea would be that it would be ok if the wood was in contact with water just as long as the water stayed under the 3.5" height of the 2x4..
Bad idea - let's suppose the water reached the 3" mark - so your floor above isn't wet - but all of the framing is - how long do you think it would take for the mold to begin forming in an environment where the wood couldn't breathe freely to dry out. 2 to 3 days would probably be about right,,,,,,,, And that wood in an enclosed area can stay wet for an awful long time, rvrn if you run as dehumidifier to try to help things along. I've had water problems in commercial properties - that it took about 2 weeks to get some professional humidity control companies into - and then - even with opening up the wall and using professional equiptment to dehumidify (along with plenty of fan action) have it take another 2 to 3 weeks before it was dry enough to close up the walls. You would be better off floating a slab than a wood deck. It isn't really all that much more money when you come right down to it. Rod
