Hi Zardoz. Welcome! (Better late than never...) I'm sorry you feel that this is a place for customers or trying to sell you something, since nothing could be further from the truth. Unlike many other acoustic places on the internet, nobody here is trying to sell you anything at all! In fact, there are people here from some major acoustic companies who are just here to help, and who never try to promote their own products. This place is a forum for people who are serious about learning how to design and build their own studios, period. Not about sales. If you want John or someone else to design it for you, then contact them off-line, privately, not here on the forum, because the forum is free. No cost. Available to everyone who wants to learn. But at the same time, it's not a place where anyone is going to design your entire studio for you for free! It's a place where YOU can learn how to do it yourself. There's actually a huge amount of information to help you do that, and folks here will be more than happy to help you if you get stuck, but really this is intended to be a "self-help" place. Having said that, there are several problems with your room that have already been pointed out to you, but let's recap and see if we can figure out some solutions: First, the floor: "It is a basement bedroom with carpeted floor" Carpet is not a good idea on studio floors. It is randomly selective absorption that does the exact opposite of what you need, so my first suggestion would be to take out the carpet and just use the bare concrete, as that's a great surface, acoustically. If it isn't in good shape, or you don't like the idea of concrete, then lay laminate flooring. How many pro studios have you seen with carpet on the floor? There's a reason for that.... Second is the ceiling "removable drop down light tiles overhead on the ceiling that rattle allot with bass": they have to go. No use at all, and you can never work successfully in an environment like that! Even if they are called "acoustic tiles", they are not doing much useful for the acoustics of your room: Get rid of them, take out the framework too, and use the extra ceiling space for proper acoustic treatment (we'll get to that). The next issue is symmetry: You don't have any. You need perfect symmetry in a control room, and your current layout shows that you have a 10" 'kink' on one side :!: :shock: That is hopeless. You will never be able to get a tight stereo image, nor a decent soundstage, nor even similar EQ or RT response. Your mixes will never translate well, as there are two different acoustic spaces, one on each side of your head. Until you can get the studio symmetrical, you don't even have a studio: you just have a room with speakers in it. It does not matter how well you treat the room, how many bass traps or clouds you put in, it will never work as a studio until you have symmetry. That should be your number one priority. Brien gave you great advice about an easy way to fix that: build splayed walls. That's the simplest, cheapest way to do what needs to be done. Another alternative is to get rid of whatever that 10" kink is hiding, and make the room into a rectangle. That would work very well too, but is going to be expensive, and may not even be possible, depending on the reason why the kink is there in the first place! A third solution would be to build a wall that continues the "kink" at least a couple more feet back behind you, so you at least get symmetry in the front half of the room. That's it: there are no more options. You have to solve that "kink" issue if you hope to have a usable studio.
My main concern is the effect that the jutting out area will have on reflections, perhaps it may not make much of a difference if does not intercept the angle of 1st reflection, I don’t know...
It's a biggie, for sure, and it has nothing to do with first reflections. It is about having identical acoustic spaces on each side of your head, with the same angles, path length, depth, materials, Eq, etc. It makes a huge difference. It makes your room unusable. It needs to be fixed, and it cannot be fixed with acoustic treatment. OK, on to your diagrams: Your basic layout is fine: The speakers should indeed be set up roughly like that, but using an equilateral triangle, with the speakers at two vertices and your head at the third. In a room that small, the speakers will most likely need to go right up against the front wall, not spaced away form it as you show them, and you will then need to adjust the bass roll-off accordingly. Put the speakers on very heavy, massive stands, and put an absorbent panel behind each one, not just one panel in the middle. You also mention "foam", but you don't say what type. Most types of common foam are useless for acoustics. Packing foam, mattress foam, upholstery foam, and things like that have no acoustic value. If you want to use foam, then ONLY buy it from a reputable acoustic company that publishes all the test data about their products. Do not get it from one of the typical "foam by mail" places. But proper acoustic foam is expensive. Much better is to use common building materials, such as mineral wool or fiberglass insulation (provided that you buy the correct density). Get a dozen panels of OC-703, and that will make quite a difference to your room (after you fix the kink, that is: installing treatment before you fix the kink is pointless). Now for some of your comments:
Basically what I am asking for are the bare essentials when it comes to improving room treatment.
The thing is, NO amount of treatment is going to make your room usable, until you fix that kink. That's what Brian and Brien have been trying to tell you. It's like if you take your car to the mechanic, and tell him that it's not running smoothly, so please change the oil and put in new spark plugs. But when the mechanic looks under the hood, he notices that you only have half an engine in there, and even that half is missing a piston and camshaft! Then you insist that all you want is an oil change and new plugs.... That's the type of issue we are look at here: you can change all the oil you want, and fit the best plugs in the world, but until you put the rest of the engine in, it just wont work.

OK, top five basic treatment steps for YOUR room: 1) Fix the kink, so that the front half of the room is perfectly symmetrical. This can either be by building side walls, or perhaps by re-arranging the layout, but I'm not convinced that there is any feasible layout without fixing the kink. 2) Get rid of the carpet and ceiling tiles. 3) Treat the rear of the room: Put large bass traps diagonally across both rear corners, floor to ceiling and at least 36" wide, and also at least 4" of OC-703 or similar across the rest of the rear wall, spaced away at least 4". 4) Get your speakers organized: mount them on very heavy, massive speaker stands, 1.2m above the floor, right up against the front wall, and adjust the bass roll-off accordingly (somewhere between -3 dB and -6 dB). Also, angle them correctly using the common equilateral triangle method, and decouple them from the stands. 5) Put thick absorption on all of your first reflection points, including the ceiling. Put it this way: if that were my room, and all I had was a couple of hundred dollars, that's what I would do. I can't give you better advice than what I'd do if it were my own room!
