Soundman20207 January 2010#3
The idea is to build the inner shell, then the outer shell, having a double wall system.
That would be a three-leaf system! Bad idea. Or maybe even four leaf (see below). Even WORSE idea. You already HAVE your outer shell: it is the existing garage wall, which hopefully is only one leaf at present (maybe not: see below). You only need to build ONE additional "shell", which will be your inner leaf. No more, no less. You can only have two-leaves in your MSM system. Three or more tunes the resoannt frequency way to high, and reduces insulation at the bottom end. However, it's really hard to see how you are going to seal that roller-door airtight and still have it operable, if you want it to be your outer leaf. That is going to be your number one issue.
We are close to a freeway which does provide some noise, but this is also about keeping sound in as most of my recording are direct.
It's pretty much the same thing. If sound goes one way through a wall, it will go the other way through the wall in pretty much the same way. As far as I know, there's no such things as one-way isolation. So whatever you do needs to be done with the worst offender in mind. If the noise level outside from the freeway is higher (as heard inside the room) than is the noise level from your playing/mixing activities in the room (when heard outside), then that will be the level that defines the amount of isolation you need. You need to make real measurements with a sound level meter, both inside and out: You also need to define what acceptable levels will be, both inside and out. The largest difference defines teh isolation you need, and therefore your wall structure.
1.The inner and outer walls will be soundcheck plaster board.
Waste of money. Go for plain old drywall (gypsum board, sheetrock, plaster board, etc.): Mass is mass: the sounds waves don't care how much you paid for that mass, so go with the cheapest high-density mass you can get, which is plain old drywall in most places. In any event, the outer wall already exists, and probably isn't soundcheck. You said it is brick, and that's good: brick is a good isolator to start with. However, you did say it is "double brick back wall and side wall". Do you mean that it is a single-leaf brick wall that is built from two layers of brick, or do you mean that it is a cavity wall, built as two separate brick walls with a gap between? If so, you might already have a two-leaf wall. Unless the two sides of that wall are tied together mechanically, in which case you only have a single leaf. Do you have any way of checking that wall, to see exactly how it is built? Can you see "inside" it (assuming it is a cavity wall)?
Is it worth adding timber sheets to the outside wall before applying plasterboard, or vise versa?
That all depends on what the existing outside wall is made of! And also what you plan to do inside the room / how loud it will be / how quite you need it to be outside the room / etc.
2.Where i have the staggered stud system in Diagram A.1 & A.2, the staggered studs are far enough apart that i could add a piece of timber board along the inside of the outer studs, also holding in the Rockwool bats. Worth doing for extra soundproofing?
That sounds like even MORE leaves, if I understand what you are saying! You can have two-leaves, and only two leaves in your wall. A one-leaf wall is limited by mass law, which means lousy isolation. A three leaf wall is limited by the low frequency response of the MSMSM system, which makes it a lousy isolator. You need two-leaf, and only two-leaf. So you don't need staggered studs, since the outer wall is already in place. You only need a single stud frame, with two or more layers of 16mm drywall on only ONE side of it, a 4" inch air gap (minimum) between the new leaf and the existing leaf, filled with rockwool (48 kg/m3 density). You can use wood panels instead of drywall if you really want to (OSB, MDF, plywood, etc), but they will have to be thicker to make up for the lost mass and lower density of the wood as compared to drywall, and they will most likely be more expensive. But basically, you'll need to provide a lot more of the details that are requested on the stickies at the top of this forum in order to get you more useful responses. Having said that, your single biggest issue is most likely going to be that garage door. If you have to keep it for aesthetics or for code, then I'd suggest that you split it in two vertically so that the other side can open while "your" side stays closed. You will have to seal your side in place permanently, make it airtight, and beef it up from the inside with mass, in order to use it as your outer leaf. If you don't do that, then you will never achieve decent isolation for your entire room.
i will be starting in a couple of weeks as i am finishing the electrical requirements on the brick walls, in case we sell one day i don't want electricals in these walls that may have to come down!
Your electrical system should NOT be on the outer leaf! It all needs to be totally and completely inside your INNER leaf (except, of course, for the one single point where you bring the main supply into your room). All internal electrical should be done with surface-mounted ducts and fixtures, in order to avoid making holes in your air.tight hermetically sealed inner-leaf. So any work you do on those existing brick walls is wasted. All you need on those is the one single line that will feed the entire room, which of course includes the earth conductor for your star-grounding system. One other thing you didn't mention is your HVAC system: What are your plans for that? - Stuart -

Firstly, the garage door operates on the outside of the build. That is why I have lowered half the ceiling, so the garage door will run along it's tracks and then over the section where i have lowered the ceiling. In no way will the garage door be seen from inside the build.
HOW it operates is not the issue! The problem is THAT it operates. Since it MOVES therefore it cannot be SEALED. It also has next to no useful mass. Therefore it is useless as your outer leaf. Therefore you have no isolation. Even worse, even though it is useless as your outer leaf, it IS still a leaf, so you cannot even put another leaf behind it, since that would give you a three leaf wall! In other words, there simply is no way around your dilemma. Either you have to give up operability of the garage door in order to seal it and add mass to it somehow. Or you have to give up the idea of getting the kind of isolation you want. You can't have both. And you most certainly cannot have them with such low mass leaves. I did warn you about this in my response to your first post, but you seem to have missed that entirely, because you never did mention what you were going to do about all of the rather large and glaring points that I brought up, and now you are back again, repeating more or less the same thing you said the first time. I am not going for absolute perfection, but am trying to limit sound leakage in and out as much as possible.
Fine, but you still have not defined NUMBERS. You absolutely do need to do that. You can't just guess about how loud you think you might be: you need to measure it, with a sound level meter, while your buddies are doing their best to make the biggest possible noise that they normally make, inside your garage. You can't just guess how quiet you need to be, either: you need to measure that too, at your neighbor's place. Play loud full-spectrum music inside your garage at 4 o'clock in the morning, and keep on turning it down and down until it is no longer audible at your neighbors place, and measure that level. The difference between the two is how much isolation you need. That will dictate the kind of construction you need. This isn't a guessing game: it is science. If it turns out that you need 45 dB of isolation, that requires one kind of construction. If it turns out that you need 75 dB, that requires another kind. You cannot know which kind you will need until you measure the real numbers with a real meter. Only THEN can you start deciding what ind of structure and mass you will need, and what kind of insulation, etc.
i can get a choice of Particle Board 16mm or 18mm, very cheap from a friend. i am thinking of layering this on the outside wall, covered with 13mm plaster,
Why would you want to do that? That makes no sense. You already have a lot of mass in your outside walls, since you say they are brick. Brick is many times more dense than wood. You'd need MANY layers of particle board just to double the mass of those bricks, and all that would buy you is 6dB of additional isolation. Not worth the effort. Most likely what you will need is a two leaf MSM system, all around, with plenty of mass on each leaf, and fully sealed, airtight. That wood makes no sense at all up against your existing brick walls, but would make a lot more sense as part of your inner leaf. - Stuart -

Soundman20206 February 2010#12
The garage door is not a leaf. It is not attached to the build .
Irrelevant. It is still a leaf. Being attached or not attached to the rest of the building has no effect, acoustically, on weather or not it is a leaf. What matters is that it is a large flat surface with appreciable mass, separated by an air gap from another large flat surface with appreciable mass. Period. So the only way that your door could NOT be a leaf is if you can figure out a way to make it mass-less, or figure out a way to eliminate the air gap between it and the partition. Like it or not, that door IS your outer leaf.
I am stopping just short of the garage door and having a second partition wall parallel with the garage door, but not linked to it in any way.
That's what I understood from your original post. It was not hard to follow. you made it clear the first time. That "second partition wall parallel with the garage door" will be your inner leaf. The garage door itself is the outer leaf. There just isn't any question about it. Even if you could figure a way to make the garage door hover in mid-air a few inches away from the partition, it would STILL be a leaf. It has a large surface with mass, and it is separated from another large surface with mass, therefore it is a leaf. it is part of an MSM system. Therefore it will resonate a a certain frequency, governed by the laws of physics, and it will amplify sound at that specific frequency, and it will not start to isolate sound until 1.4 times that frequency, and it will only start to isolate well at roughly twice that frequency. In other words, it will behave as any other MSM system in the known universe. The laws of physics will apply to it in the exact same manner as they apply to all other MSM systems
And this shows how the garage door moves without disturbing what i am building.
Once again, the manner in which it moves is irrelevant: What matter is simply that it does move. If it moves, then sealing it correctly to work as it should, (since it undeniably IS in fact your outer leaf), will be really, really hard. How does your builder plan to seal it hermetically? What products does he plan to use to seal the door itself, how does he plan to seal it to the runners? How does he plan to seal the door to the threshold? How does he plan to seal the roll to the frame above?
So the only place i need to add Particle board and gyprock is on the 2 new partition walls.
No, you need to add it on your ENTIRE inner leaf, AS WELL as the two new sides of your outer leaf. If you want to isolate well, then you CANNOT do it with a single-leaf fully-coupled wall, which is what you are planning at present (at least, you cannot do it without adding an unreasonable amount of mass). Your best and cheapest option is to do it with a fully decoupled complete MSM structure, which implies two full leaves all the way around, on all four walls and the ceiling. Your existing "double-brick" walls are most likely acting as single leaf anyway, since I doubt they were built correctly as MSM walls. So, once again, if you want acoustic isolation (and it seems that you do, or you would not be going to all this trouble) then you have no choice but to build a proper 2-leaf decoupled MSM system all the way around, for the exact same reason that if you want to build a fish tank, you have to put glass on all the sides and the bottom. A fish tank that has glass on only two sides and half the bottom will not hold water. A fish tank that has one wall made of two layers of cardboard and another wall made of sponge, will not hold water. A studio made the way you are planning to make it, is equivalent to a fish tank made like that, and it will not hold sound in exactly the same manner as the fish tank will not hold water.
