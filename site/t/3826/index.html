sharward4 June 2005#16
scalpshifter wrote:
. . . EPDM (I still have LOTS since I bought 10 lineal feet, 3 feet wide, and used about 3 inches!)
Ouch! So, you found the places selling the stuff with a 10 foot minimum, too, eh? I was lucky -- I was able to buy a "scrap" of EPDM sheet, 60 durometer, 3' x 2' x 1/2", for about $75 delivered. However, before you congratulate me, realize that I may end up not using any of it on my project! :oops: Also, Mike is absolutely correct. Think of sound traveling from inside to outside (left to right) and how many layers of mass it travels through in your current drawing:
    1. Inside layer(s) of wallboard on first frame 2. Outside layer(s) of wallboard on second frame 3. Inside layer of wallboard on original wall 4. Outside layer of "whatever" on original wall
Remember, a "leaf" is defined as a barrier of mass. The walls inside your home are two leaf -- typically one layer of gypsum wallboard on each side of the wall. (Notice the difference between the term "leaf" and "layer": one leaf can have many layers.) That's single wall, two leaves. It also happens to be a pathetic two leaf system -- each stud is a flanking path, because the outer leaf is not decoupled at all from the inner leaf, and each leaf is weak because it only has one layer of gypsum wallboard. Making sense?
