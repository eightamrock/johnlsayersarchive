Just in case that "E" in some of your signatures stands for "ephemeral", and just in case I goofed on the previous link (since I've yet to see any indication you've found it :=) here are a few "germain-ities" - Specifically, read the side-bar I added along with the screen shots. If you can get your outer leaf (like the siding and outer frame) beefed up by adding inserts (or complete extra layers outside, whichever works WITHOUT adding more than about a MILLIMETER of air space between layers, the next (and ONLY) thing is a second, ISOLATED, inner leaf with its attending support system. Where ceiling meets wall (outer or inner) if there are areas where framing is the only mass, consider that (hermetically sealed) wood framing is worth approx. equivalent of 2/3 that thickness of gypsum. Where headroom is limited, it gets trickier to maintain separation of inner/outer leaves; where not, just build an inner frame wall, rest the inner frame ceiling joists ONLY ON THIS INNER WALL FRAME, and sheet rock it. If you have LOTS of space, and need LOTS of isolation, you can up the size of ceiling/wall framing and put your gypsum on the OUTSIDE of the inner frame; for the ceiling especially, this will help keep the stuff in the air where it belongs, plus leave you with stud cavities you can fill with insulation and cloth-cover, or slat-cover, killing at least part of the "acoustic alligator" at the same time. knightfly8 June 2005#42
1. How many sheets of 5/8" drywall can one hang on one side of a single frame 2x4 wall before the leverage (of the gypsum mass extending away from the wall) causes significant wall distortion/buckling or other stability concerns? At least three; but if you're looking for high isolation, stiffness doesn't hurt either; I'd go up to 2x6 just on general principles. 2. Is RC necessary with single frame walls if the entire room is floated and isolated? How do you "float and isolated" with a single frame? If you mean, do I need RC when building a room-in-room when the inner leaf consists of multiple layers of gypsum all placed on one side of the inner frame, the answer is no. This construction is almost entirely governed by m-a-m phsyics, where the two masses and the air gap determine the overall resonance, at which frequency the isolation will be worst. (not zero necessarily, you do still have mass there; but definitely worst.) If your m-a-m resonance is at (say) 14 hZ, then it will be at least 1-1/2 octaves ABOVE that before the deleterious effects of that resonance won't cause a noticeable weakness in the isolation curve. So a low B on a 6-string bass (around 31 hZ) should be attenuated fairly well with that low a resonance. 3. How does RC compare to the ISOmax hangers in price, performance and ease of installation? I'll be careful, but I'd hate to spend time and cash using RC and have one errant screw hit the framing and screw the entire pooch flanking-wise. Haven't priced EITHER lately, not since steel rose in price by 300% - if you're planning on a full room-in-room, there may well be no need for either. The Isomax stuff is easier, maybe a dB or two better, and 'way more expensive (unless you buy your RC from an acoustic dealer on the web) - some web dealers want as much PER FOOT for the stuff as I've paid for a 12' stick :shock: BTW Everest shows some interesting alternatives including a 3 leaf system consisting of: drywall - framing (airspace 1) - drywall - RC (airspace 2) - drywall..... with an STC of 56 compared to an alternating stud system (STC 56) and a double framed system with the same amount of drywall (STC of 63) (Fig. 13-16, page 186). Rule # 13b - don't do ANYTHING the same way the British do it, unless you're MADE of money - some points to consider: The british seem to like 6-leaf walls, (which they refer to as "triple camdens") with each wall getting heavier and more expensive, and taking up more space - sure you can eventually get as good isolation that way, and no SINGLE leaf's failure will be catastrophic - but there are cheaper ways that take up less space and get the same results. Keep in mind that all Everest's numbers are STC, and that this doesn't even consider ANY frequency below 125 hZ. The man, great as his contributions, was nearly 90 when the SUB woofer was foisted upon us... - so any wall with phenomenal mid-range performance will look SUPER when using STC, but the wall may very well not even slow down a kick drum's primary thud. This is what happens with multi-leaf walls. Bottom sucks; mids ROCK. not exactly what modern music needs for isolation, not to mention tractors/trucks/jet planes, and other "rumblers" (trains for example) I'd like to get my hands on the program that does those calculations Sorry, it's an OLD demo - the newer demo is randomized so pretty useless, and the full program costs $1000 from Marshall-Day acoustics in NZ - I'm pushing it just doing what I do with it, I'm almost feeling guilty enough to plop down the $1k for the real deal, but probably not THIS week...
