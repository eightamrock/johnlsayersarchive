scalpshifter18 September 2005#68
I have finished the first wall and ceiling layer of drywall in the CR. An interesting experiment is the air delivery system. I lined two of the spaces between the ceiling joists with duct liner (see pix). These will be the conduits to deliver air to the room from four lateral vents. The air will be fed into these spaces via a central input from the attic. The flexiduct feeding the central input goes quite a ways through the attic to the heater/AC plenum. I also built an insulated box behind the rear wall that will function as the air return - spewing air to the open space there and then through a vent into the attic (or I may use ducting to take it directly to the attic - not sure yet). That drywall was hard to do - every piece had to be specially cut to fit. I sealed all gaps with Sheetrock brand acoustical sealant - not hard at all (use a plastic spoon to smooth it in - and that backer rod really helps stabilize the material when wet). Imade some audio routing compromises - running four 2-conductor lines to the back of the room in case I want a sub there that feeds to L/R speakers behind my head for that surround sound feel. I had to run them around the periphery because that was the only way to avoid the AC feeds in the room. This will go on my "what not to do next time" page when the project is complete! :) That is about it for now - I'll post more pix as the drywall goes up. then on to the finishing the walls, installing doors, a LOT of electrical wiring, and the bamboo floor! :lol:

scalpshifter1 December 2005#75
I AM pumped to see things making some sort of sense – and it is fun to have a paper project become tangible! There are some many things I might have done differently (and will on studio #3!). Many of my choices were economically driven (like doing all the labor myself….). Let’s see – some big “I wonder if I had…”s include: Doors – solid core with BETTER frames - I have developed a phobia related to hanging doors on frames made out of reconstituted toothpicks… Lumber – I bought several hundred 2x4s from a local yard that supplies contractors. I guess they saved the worst for me – and charged the most! Turns out that Lowe’s has much better lumber (straighter – does not still resemble a tree) at much better prices. Attic – I spent a lot of time and calories hoisting 3/4 inch T&G OSB into the attic to create a mass layer above the free-standing CR ceiling. I am not sure it was worth the trouble (acoustically or otherwise) and I probably will not do this above the live room. It is nice to have the top view access to wiring and stuff. On the other hand, if the sound leakage (in or out) is intolerable, then up goes the mass layer! Drywall – I just do not have the skill level of a pro and a lot of my drywall is less than perfect – thank goodness for acoustical caulk!!! On thing I am trying to do is leave options open despite the theoretical ideal of having planned everything perfectly from the beginning. I know that no matter how well things are planned most of us have to be somewhat nimble and flexible enough to make minor alterations for things we missed. So, I am comfortable with my approach of a good general plan with substantial room for experimentation and diversion when necessary. In other words stick to general principles and get started doing something without obsessing too much about the infinite details!! Last thing – the biggest challenge (at least right now) is my original question! I (again) need to frame up against a ceiling with a nice drywall treatment and would love to not rip out the drywall, but I probably should!! (this is because I decided to “bump out” an alcove for a drum set and a small iso room) – hence the “nimble and flexible” planning strategy gets put into action once more! :wink:
