Soundproofing a Soundstage?

Started by natzev_i on 1 December 2006. 4 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 7650.

natzev_i1 December 2006#1
Hi All, This is my first time posting here – was re-directed from the RAMPS forum. The film facilities where I'm working at as sound engineer were recently bought by bigger filmmaking company and the new owners want to build six new sound stages 30 m x 50 m large each. The best construction for the purpose would be to go with as thick as possible concrete walls but this will significant increase the construction work time and cost and is limiting somehow the total dimensions and structure of the stages. The solution is to use much lighter and versatile metallic constructions but then the sound problems come. The idea of the construction is to use heavy metal double-T elements as framework and sandwich-like metal panels made from PVC-coated galvanized sheet iron with polyurethane isolation between the metal irons used from both sides of the walls with air-gap between the panels. Now, I have shot in different metal warehouses and they sound terrible as expected but what about these new panels? - They don't ring as the usual sheet irons do. Do you have any experience with them? What if we put some mineral wool panels on the walls and ceilings and also between the metal panels in the air-gap? Will this construction (or suggest other, please) provide the needed sound proof for a sound stage? The location is in a suburb so, no big traffic noise just the production cars/trucks outside. Your advice will be highly appreciated! Regards, Ivo

John Sayers2 December 2006#4
Ivo - metal wall construction with double or triple drywall layers is the normal practice for shooting stages - the inner walls are then treated with a layer of rockwool (or equivalent) that is usually covered with a wire mesh to hold it in place. cheers john
