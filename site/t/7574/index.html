Deluks25 November 2006#6
You say that the building is listed, but doesn't mean it should be left to fall apart. If there are holes in the outer cladding that let water in then I'd say that was a pretty urgent repair. As it's listed you may even be fined or whatever for not maintaining it. That aside, rubber will not be the best or most cost effective material. You may have to take the cladding off, fix a waterproof barrier and then replace the cladding onto timber battens. (Replacing any damaged with identical stuff as you go along) The best product for the job would be breathable roofing felt. This stuff keeps water from penetrating but lets water vapour (ie condensation) out, timber structures MUST be able to 'breathe' Otherwise rot and mould can set in and that's a pretty expensive repair job! Tyvek being one of the market leaders of breathable felt. If this is a studio related question then currently you have timber cladding (with holes in!) and maybe a membrane of some kind. Therefore you have zero soundproofing. What's the construction plan from the walls inwards?
