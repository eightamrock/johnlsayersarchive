Hi Luke. Welcome. Please read and comply with the forum rules.
it's just about me trying to get a dead sound.
Not a good idea. A dead room is unpleasant to work in for very long: the human brain is not used to the sound of "dead", and doesn't like it much. The current leading concept in studio design is "RFZ", meaning "reflection free zone". Search the forum for this, and you'll find more stuff than you ever though existed on this concept. The basic idea is build the room such that there is an area around the mix position where no "first reflections" ever reach. The only sound that gets to this zone is the direct sound from the speakers, and the diffuse field ("resonant field") from the rest of the room. That's the theory, at least. But in order to have a truly natural, neutral and un-colored diffuse field, you need a non-dead room, such that there is a low level of reflection happening all around, with the frequency response being practically flat. If you do that right, your room will sound great and your mixes will translate well. So don't go for dead: go for just the right amount of "live".
but I am currently finding it hard to pinpoint specific areas I should manage/proof before I get money to do EVERY wall.
What is it that you want to do to these walls? You say that you understand soundproofing, yet you are talking about room treatment: They are two very different things. Opposites, actually. Isolating a room usually makes it sound really bad, and treating a room usually does nothing at all to isolate it. What treatment do you have already, where did you put it, and what result did you get? You say you have plenty of equipment so it would be really good if you could use it to measure the response of your room right now, and post the graphs here so we can see what your problems are, and suggest how you could go about fixing them.

Soundman20203 August 2010#6
Wow, I'm confused actually.
Yep! That's the way we all start out, when it comes to acoustics! There's not much about it that is intuitive or obvious.... But don't worry, it gets better: as you learn more, you'll become increasingly confused, more and more, ... :) Until eventually you'll be like the rest of us: totally stumped!
Soundproofing = Stopping sound Treating = Improving sound
More or less, yes. Except that there is no such thing as "stopping sound" on planet earth: A sufficiently loud sound will get through any conceivable barrier. You can't stop it: All you can do is attenuate sound enough to meet your needs. That's why one of the questions on the "Please read this before you post" message is about describing how loud you are in your room, and how quiet you need to be outside it (and vice versa, too: how loud is the road outside, and how quiet do you need it to be inside) in terms of decibels. Based on that you can figure out how to design the isolation (not soundproofing, but "isolation") that you need to attenuate the sound levels going in/out of your room. And "treatment" is as you say: make the room sound good, meaning that you want to achieve roughly flat response in your room with no echos, and decent low-level diffuse resonance... easy to say, hard to do! :) The problem is that the materials you use for isolation are generally very heavy (massive), rigid, large and flat. The way you isolate a room is to make the sound bounce back again, so that it cannot get out. The only way you can force very loud, low frequency sounds to "bounce back" is with mass. Serious amounts of mass. So isolation walls are massive, solid, and rigid. But that makes the room very "echoic" and resonant, which you don't want! So you have to treat the room. And the materials that you use to treat a room are normally fluffy, soft, light weight, thick, etc. Which means they are really lousy at isolation. The stuff you use to treat is bad at isolating, and the stuff you use to isolate is bad at treating... So two different concepts: Large massive flat rigid surfaces around your room to isolate it, all of them sealed airtight, then soft thick fluffy light materials inside it at strategic places and in the right proportions and thicknesses, to calm things down and control all the bad stuff created by the isolation. Yeah, it is confusing, isn't it? :)
