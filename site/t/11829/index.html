Why is it bad to have my monitors against either wall D or B?
In that orientation, your monitors are firing across the shortest dimension of the room. They should be firing down the longest dimension, for several reasons. The two most important ones are getting your mix position in a good location in the room, about 38% back from the front wall (it doesn't have to be exactly 38%: that's just a starting point, but somewhere around there), and getting the rear wall as far away from you as possible, so that the reflections that come back from that wall are as late and as low in level as possible. If you build a good , solid, acoustically well-designed wall at the red line, and then treat your room correctly for an RFZ design, then, it should turn out pretty good, I think. Make sure to keep the room symmetrical (left-right) and get all of those hard reflective surfaces out of there! Those shelves are probably doing all kinds of strange stuff to your acoustics. They have to go.
All I am concerned with is mixing in here, having an accurate room for mixing is my #1 goal. Accurate bass is a must as I do a lot of bass heavy dance and Hip-Hop music.
So if you want good accuracy and tight bass, then I'd suggest that you need to concentrate on room symmetry most of all, followed by lots of bass trapping in the corners, soffit-mount (flush mount) your speakers, place a large ceiling cloud over your mix position, deep absorbers at your first reflection points, get that carpet off the floor and replace it with laminate floor, and treat your rear wall and parts of your side walls with absorbers / broadband slot resonators / etc., as needed after you complete the other stuff and measure the room acoustically. - Stuart -
