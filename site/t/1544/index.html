Greetings & welcome to the site Jeremy. Yes, be sure to save some of that budget for the toys. :D It looks like you're doing it in the sub basement since the basement is complete? If I read this correctly, you have 20 foot ceilings in the sub basement. Is this to where the supports are or up to the concrete? What are your width & depth area of the room. Can you draw up a quick drawing of what you have (with dimensions), stairs, etc. Is there colums that need to be worked around, or is the room completely open? A lot of what will depend on the cost per square foot (besides the price hike of materials going up 15-30% per month right now) will depend on how much isolation you will require, how many rooms you have (control room, live room, iso rooms, etc.). Will you be doing floating floors? If you can give us some additional ideas of what you want to accomplish with the room/rooms, all this will have an impact on how much it will cost. If you plan on doing this all DIY, it won't cost you nearly as much. Most of those higher costs include labor for contractors to come in and do most of the work. (As I'm sure you know well from building the house). I'm currently building my studio in the basement, but it's just under 8 feet tall, and I've done 99.9% of the labor myself to date. Just had to buy the beer for friends to come over and help with an extra hand once in awhile. Right now I'm estimating somewhere between 6-8k for my control room area that is about 17x16 (a little wider in some area's). But I've been doing the floating floor (wood), which has cost me about $1200 for that area alone. Not including the hardwoods yet or framing. With this large of an area you have, and depending on how critical isolation is, you may consider having floating concrete floors since you haven't poured the concrete yet. Concrete here in Nashville is currently going for about $75.00 a cubic yard. So that should give some idea there. Once again, welcome to the site. John will probably have some ideas and questions for you as well later on. He's on Aussie time, so he usually pops in a little later. Aaron

Didn't mean to sound condescending there; that budget is way more than enough to finish out your studio in the manner that we'd recommend. By doing all the framing and drywall myself, building my own bass traps, and using rigid fiberglass for room treatment instead of Auralex or RPG diffusors I spent about $2000 on my little one-room studio at home.
