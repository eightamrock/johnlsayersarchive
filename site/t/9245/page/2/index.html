I'm so glad you "unwithdrew" from the discussion, Eric. :-) Aagin, you raise great points. I keep drawing parallels in my mind between the soundproofing industry and the nutritional supplement industry. As a rule, nutritional supplements are marketed here in the United States as miracles, often with lots of testimonials and moneyback guarantees. They rely on the placebo effect to propel sales. Rarely, if ever, is scientific evidence presented to support the claims. Eric's main point -- one we all would be wise to take away from all of this -- is that building for isolation is indeed a science, so when considering products or techniques, we should all insist on seeing the evidence that it works and mitigate the value of marketing and testimonials, regardless of how fancy the materials or the fame of the endorsers. That is not to say thay all things not proven to work do not work -- for everything proven to work was at one time not proven... But claims need credible evidence to be believed, and testimonials do not qualify as evidence. They are a distraction. I hope ASC's products are everything they say they are and that they can someday publicize the credible evidence to back their claims. Until then, however, their products, in my opinion, should not be recommended here. --Keith :mrgreen:
