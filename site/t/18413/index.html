robbie long19 June 2013#1
Hi - I recently posted here regarding construction of a floating timber floor, which received a very cold reception. I now realise that no one wants to touch it - and I understand why. However, there must be someone else who, like, wants a timber surface under them, for looks, feel and sound reflection to a point, without actually floating a floor on bearers & joists & creating resonant chambers? Surely there is a simple way to pin a tongue & groove flooring down onto concrete without chambers in a way that has minimal impact on sound? Its seems 90% of studios I see have timber floors - if they aren't floating floors, what are they? Obviously there is the option of a veneer floor, but they are not hardy enough in my opinion... I'd really like some help with this, as I'm getting nowhere with forums, talking to builders, suppliers etc etc...hours n hours... What other information do I need to post? Thanks! Rob
RJHollins19 June 2013#2
Hi Rob, That was basically my first design plan. A beautiful hardwood laminate over a concrete base. Had nothing to do with the sound ... it was for looks AND a way to cover. After the 6th store, I found a place that had this beautiful laminate laid out for show. It was placed over their concrete floor in the showroom. I do the walk on concrete ... then start walking on the laminate. Oh ... it felt so much better under the sneakers ... The bad part ... I was playing the worlds largest Marimba ... with every step :shock: Back and forth I went. There was no way I could walk on this without hearing this resonance :roll: I ended up buying this rubberized laminate flooring [like a fiber floor]. It's about a 1/4" thick. When you see it, it looks like a hardwood floor. It is very quiet to walk on. My chair doesn't make noise when I roll, and it's easy on the knees when I have to crawl around. Vacuuming is a breeze. Oh, and the cost was like 1/5 what a hardwood goes for. For me ... best move I made. Maybe there is some type of damping material that could go between the base and the wood floor ... but I never found any that worked. just my 1/2 cent.
