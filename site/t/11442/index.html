Hi everyone, just found this place after much searching on this subject, exactly what Ive been looking for! Here's my situation (yes Ive already read a lot on here): I just moved into a rented flat a few months ago, its a two bedroom so the spare room is a dedicated studio. Unfortunately, its sounds horrifically bad! Ive been using headphones since I moved in, its just unusable. The bass is just a boomy mess frankly, it overwhelms all the other frequencies. The worst place to sit in the room is also exactly where I work from. If I move back towards the rear wall the bass becomes less boomy (still not good though). The flat is a new build so the walls are pretty thin. The walls behind the speakers and behind me (both lead to other rooms in my flat) are made out of some plasterboard'ish material (I have no idea about materials), really hollow sounding. The walls to the sides are thicker, with a window in the right and a large built-in cupboard in the left. The floor is carpeted. I can feel the bass vibrating through the floor in my feet, at low volumes. It sounds to me like the major problem is the reflections on the front wall behind the monitors. The back of the monitors are 19cm away from the wall, I could move the desk further away from the wall (although less aesthetically pleasing!). You are in a square bedroom. That is the problem that you are hearing. Only thing to do is move to a better room or deaden the entire room. Also, I can't speak for anyone here, but I can do without the abusive language ;)
Soundman202028 November 2008#3
It sounds to me like you need bass traps: Lots of them. Big ones. In every single corner you can find. And maybe do something to make the room un-square. Perhaps move the rear wall? Build another wall? Square is bad. The bass trapping will help a bit, but it cannot make up for a square room. It would also help to get those speakers moved away from the walls a bit, and (I´m guessing here, hoping the experts can chip in:) de-couple them from the desk? Maybe put them on separate stands, filled with sand, and sitting on neoprene pads? I'm not an expert on speakers, but that seems like the logical thing to do. Is that a metal frame on the desk? Is the work surface also metal?
Also, I can't speak for anyone here, but I can do without the abusive language ;)
