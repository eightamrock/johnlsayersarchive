Jam, You have to use drywall screws made for metal studs - not wood studs - and be careful - they aren't metal just because they are fine thread - they make a fine and coarse thread for both metal and wood......... predrilling is insane.........
Also I am not a contractor and don't know the drywall "tricks"... is there an easy "trick" for cutting out the receptacle holes? Other then measuring for each and every one?
If you pick up a drywall router - you can always get a rough measurement for the iside of the box - and just hang the sheets - maring out the inside of the box on the front - a little circle would be fne - The you go back (before backscrewing all of the drywall) and use the router to plunge into the center of the box - go to an edge and then verycarefully go out to the box face and cross over to the outside of the box........... you then just route around the box outside............ Very easy compared to precutting the boxes.......... BUT - it's more difficult than it sounds to get a perfect cutout with the router - it walks way from the boxes real quick and easy if you let your guard down........... Rod
doublehelix4 September 2007#3
I just crossed this same bridge... I bought a drywall book to aid in my horrible drywalling skills, and found out that there are indeed type "W" (wood) and type "S" (sheet metal) drywall screws. The type S screws worked perfectly on my RC. Mine have a finer thread, but also have a different tip that more easily punctures the RC. Good luck!

Jam, Not a problem as long as you do this: Put up a full sheet that spans the door header and install it using standard penetration screws - then cut out the door - and then the short screws to finish up the header - and you'll only need a few along the perimiter. BTW - I never hold the RC 6" below the ceiling - perhaps 1" or so below the bottom sheet on the ceiling - but never 6". I know that's not in keeping with USG - but it's a cleaner installation. Rod
