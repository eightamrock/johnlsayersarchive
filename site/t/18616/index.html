Soundman202026 November 2013#2
3 layers of glued Gysum
What do you mean by "glued"? If you mean "Green Glue", then that's fine. But id you mean typical construction glue, that is very much NOT so fine.... You should not glue the layers of drywall together.
Would ceiling panels of woodchips and cement with a think underlay of insulation be a workable solution?
Workable for what? Is that for the outer leaf, or inner leaf? But that product does not look like it is any use for either. You did not provide the details of the rest of your build, so it's really hard to answer your question!
How thick ceiling would you do?
For what purpose? If this is the inner-leaf ceiling, then it should be the same surface density as the inner-leaf walls. If the "3 layers of gypsum" is your inner-leaf wall, then that's what you need for the ceiling too: 3 layers of gypsum.
I want a lot of low frequency absorbtion and keep as much floor area free as possible.
Low frequency absorption is normally accomplished with large bass traps inside the room, but that is not related to the isolation of the room, and it looks like you are talking about isolation. Low frequency absorption is part of the acoustic treatment that goes inside the finished room: treatment is not isolation, and isolation is not treatment. They are two very different aspects of acoustics. Isolation means "stopping the sound from getting in or out". Treatment means "making the room sound good inside". Two different things. Absorbing low frequencies is treatment, not isolation.
Then again, it´s nice to have a high celing when the room is done.
A high ceiling is very good, if you have the space to do that, and as long as the final room ratio is still good. - Stuart -
