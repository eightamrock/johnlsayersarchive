Soundman202017 March 2009#7
Hi, and welcome! Some answers to your questions. They might sound smart-alec, but they are honest.
1. How long can these projects take?
How much time do you have? It depends on what you want to do, and the resources you have available. Some folks here have done a rush job in a couple of months. Others have been going for 4 or 5 years, and are still not finished. I've been working on the my design for seven months, and I have not yet even bought a single nail to start building, because I am not yet ready to do so. Some other have built a couple of bass traps in a day or two, and are finished. How long it will take is simply a matter of what you want to achieve, and the resources that you can put to work to achieve it.
2. What can I put into this space?
What do you want to put into it? Do you need just a practice room, or do you need to make it into a full professional recording studio? Or something in between? We can't tell you what you can put in there: you need to figure out what your needs are first, then maybe we can help you fit that in.
3. What is the best sound insulation levels?
Best levels for what purpose? Another new poster here already has isolation of at least STC-85 in an underground bunker, and wants to improve it. Other folks are happy to get STC-60: Some are aiming for STC 60 but will be lucky to get STC-40, since they didn't research very well before starting to build, and don't want to believe that the laws of physics actually do apply to their studio, just like they apply to the rest of the universe. And of course STC isn't really a useful measure for studio isolation anyway. Once again, you need to decide how loud you are going to be inside that room, and how loud you want it to be on the outside. You also need to decide how loud it already is on the outside, from things around you, and how quite you want it to be on the inside.
4. My roof is slanted, what effects does this have?
Effect on what? It has no effect at all on isolation, unless you want it to. IT probably does have some effect on the room acoustics, but once again you are the one who is in the best position to judge that. What effect do you want it to have? Angled roofs can be good, or they can be terrible. It all depends what you want, how well you design it, and how well you build it.
As for designs, I'm a good artist, I may hand draw some designs also.
A much better plan is ti download "SkethUp" from Google, and learn to use it. It is a 3D modeling package. Build an accurate model of your space in SketchUp, and then play around with that to see what yo can fit in. But most of all, start reading this forum. Look at some of the threads on builds that are in progress, builds that are already complete, and builds that failed. Avoid the mistakes that others made, carefully follow the advice of the experts here, and yours should be successful. And listen to what Brien just said: Your foundations might not be able to support the weight of what you need to do. You probably should get an engineer to check that, before you do anything. - Stuart

Soundman202018 March 2009#14
Hi Jeremiah,
What exactly is hvac? I have heard it used before.
It means "Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning". You need to get fresh, cool air into the room without breaking your sound isolation, and you need to exhaust stale warm air to the outside. This is actually an important issue, since you have to seal your room totally airtight in order to get good isolation. And of course, if it is airtight, then people won't be able to breath for very long! You also need to surround your room with really thick insulation, which means that heat will build up in there very fast. So HVAC is important. - Stuart -
