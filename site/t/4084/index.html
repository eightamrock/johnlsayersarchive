DDev22 July 2005#6
I would think that you could at least get a halfway decent listening/mixing area with $2-300 worth of rigid fiberglass or mineral wool if applied in the right spots. If you rotate as John suggested (and as shown in the Bedroom Studio you picked off the SAE site), then you can begin to think about your treatments. First, you will need some bass trapping in the corners. This can be done effectively by just cutting and shaping the aforementioned insulation such that it bridges the vertical corners both by the large window and on the opposite wall by the kitchen. Some guys are completely filling the corners with rockwool and are pleased with the results. I would then recommend a decent sized cloud above the mixing position, hanging from the ceiling, and some absorption on the sidewalls. The treatment shown for the bedroom studio for the sidewalls would be great as well. Just trying to cut down on sidewall reflections. And don't think that John's suggestion of the garbage bagged insulation was just some kind of crazy ranting. Tightly packed insulation bundles (say R19 for instance) are pretty good low frequency absorbers. The garbage bags are there to keep the insulation fibers from becoming airborne. A stack of these in each corner does work. Good luck, Darryl.....

DDev27 July 2005#14
Funk, I think option 2 will be the preferred way to go, which will have you firing your speakers down the longer dimension. As Tomas suggested, stuff the corners with rockwool (those superchunks are a great concept), including the corner closest to the kitchen. As far as your comment about a temporary wall or heavy curtains to even things out, I don't think the curtains will get you much. As mentioned earlier, the biggest thing to try to do is to get the left and right side walls to be as close to the same as possible as far back as your mixing position. So, if you place your desk on the 10 ft wall facing the large window, how far into the large opening will your chair be sitting while mixing? This will be the area of greatest concern. Let's say that, hypothetically, your chair sits 2 ft into that opening. To get an area that is symmetrical with the opposite wall you will need a wall surface that goes floor to ceiling covering that much of the opening (a little bit further back is actually better). Probably the easiest way to do this would be to build a large gobo that can attach to the wall and rest on the floor. The hallway side of the gobo can be a solid surface material like plywood or MDF (painted or otherwise finished to be attractive). The inside surface (studio side) could be either a slat resonator design or could be an absorptive material like fabric-covered mineral wool or rigid fiberglass. The key will be to have the same type of treatment applied to the opposite wall the same distance from the 10 ft wall. Now you've got a symmetrical mix position. The back of the room (nearest the kitchen) will need a little attention, but because your room dimensions fit pretty closely to a "proper" ratio you might be able to get by without too much treatment there. I would definitely do another of the corner bass traps in the one floor to ceiling corner you've got, and possibly fit out the rest of that wall with absorption just to deaden it. I hope this helps. I'm not an expert like some of these guys around here, but my advice is based on what I've seen recommended to others in similar situations. Good luck, Darryl.....
