Soundman20207 November 2008#9
Regarding the angle for the speakers, the easiest to thing to do is use SketchUp. Just attach a line to your speaker, and rotate it! Draw a line that is perpendicular to your speaker face, and that passes through the acoustic center (or even geometric center) of your speaker, and then "group" it with your speaker. Now it will point whichever way your speaker points. place an object in your room at the exact position your head will be in the mix position (a sphere, maybe) to represent your head, and then simply angle your speaker down until the line is pointing just behind your head. That way you don't need to calculate anything, as you can directly see when you have the right angle. Here's a link to one of the designs for my studio, where you can see how I did it. http://www.digistar.cl/images/Studio-tr ... .Drums.skp Click on the "Behind Hd" tab or the "3/4 Ohd" tab, and you should be able to see my lines / speakers, then you can apply the same concept to your room. HOWEVER, that's not my final design! I only angled the speakers in that one. I have not yet angled the soffits to match the speakers, as I'm still fiddling with the details. It's still a work in progress, but it will at least give you an idea. In your case, you could attach the soffit to the same group as the line and speaker, so they all move at once when you rotate them. - Stuart -

If you do it right, then yes, your STC (or better yet, your TL across the entire spectrum) will be very good indeed. If you go with John's suggestion of four layers of gypsumboard on each side, you'll have very, very good isolation. Probably better than the flanking through your slab.
Or do i also construct a brick wall inbetween them, thus creating the greatly unwanted third surface. From what i've read here (and Rod's book) the third leaf will lower the STC value, making the room less ''soundproof''.
Exactly. That third leaf is a bad idea. It will be WORSE than two leaves in the low end of the spectrum, and will be about the same as ONE leaf at the very low end. Go with your first plan of two stud walls, just like John said. The only thing I'd add to John's post is to fill the entire air gap with rockwool or fiberglass, not just the space between the studs. If you can afford it, of course. It really does make a worthwhile difference, as it damps the entire cavity, which is what you want. Stuff it in to completely fill the gap, as much as you can. Just take care that you don't force it in so tightly that it acts as a flanking path between the leaves, but do fill the entire gap loosely. It sounds like you have a plan! Good luck with the build! - Stuart -

Soundman202017 November 2008#25
Guys does anyone know how far apart the wood frames should be?
Do you mean the center-to-center spacing for the studs within each wall, or do you mean the separation between the stud walls (air gap)? If its the stud spacing, then 24" OC seems to be the recommendation around here, although a lot of people have done it with 16" OC. But if you are asking about the gap between the walls (inner leaf to outer leaf), then the answer is "as much as you possibly can"! More air gap is good. You really start getting good results when the entire thickness of your wall is around 12 inches (measured from outside face to outside face), which means a gap of about 5 inches between the stud walls. But of course, most of us don't have the luxury of being able to go that thick! Some people do it with as little as one inch, and still get reasonable isolation. There was another thread a few days ago where I posted a graph that shows the amount of isolation for various wall thicknesses. You could try looking for that.
