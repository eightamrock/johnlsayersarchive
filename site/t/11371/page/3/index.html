Soundman202012 November 2010#75
Arguably the most common way of patching audio is with a TT bay (Bantam). Something like this: http://www.neutrik.com/fl/en/audio/204_ ... tlist.aspx Personally, I'd get a bay that allows you to select normalling on each pair, but you'll most likely end up with practically everything normalled. The general rule in video post where I live is that "signals flow downhill on the bay", which is a neat way of saying that the outputs from equipment are on the top row of each panel and the inputs to it are on the bottom row. Some might disagree, but whichever way you select, be consistent across the entire facility. The digital/analog issue is also taken care of: most good quality bays support AES/EBU signals. If it works for digital, then it will also work for analog. Just make sure you mark them clearly (maybe with a different color ink, or something) so you don't sit there for hours trying to figure out why it doesn't work when you plug the digital out from your DAW into an analog input for the headphone amp.... :) - Stuart -
