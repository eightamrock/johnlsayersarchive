Btw i've searched for bolts and still haven't foun anything, or maby i havent looked at the right place yet.
Well, if you were going to pour a new slab then you'd use anchor bolts that are set into the slab (they are left hanging in place from above while the concrete is poured), like these: http://www.anchorboltsource.com/ http://hardware.hardwarestore.com/27-10 ... bolts.aspx So after the concrete is dry, you end up with just the threaded end poking up out of the floor. You then drill holes in your bottom plates, drop them over the bolts, and put washers on, and tighten down the nuts, like this: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor_bolt However, in your case you already have the slab in place, so what you need is expansion bolts or wedge anchors: http://www.ameribestfasteners.com/expansion_bolts.htm http://bacoenterprises.thomasnet.com/vi ... &forward=1 With these, you drill a hole through your bottom plate and into the concrete. The hole in the bottom plate is a bit larger than the bolt, but the hole in the concrete is the same size as the bolt diameter, for a tight fit. Clean the debris out of the hole, drop the bolt down into the hole, and tap it down gently with a hammer until you have just a bit of thread left sticking above your bottom plate. Put the washer and nut on. Then as you tighten the nut the "wedge" part forces the bolt shank to expand outwards into the concrete, gripping it very, very tightly. I used those extensively in building a storage shed in my backyard, and also in building my garage roof, that goes over the place where my future studio will be. They work very well. I can post some photos of what I did with them, if that would help. You should be able to find them in your local Home Depot (or whatever the equivalent store is where you live). They are very, very common where I live, here in Chile, so my guess is you should be able to find them where you are. Worst case, I could ship you a box of them! They aren't that expensive (just a couple of dollars each) but the shipping cost might be expensive, as they are heavy. - Stuart -

Not preserved: 2Drain.JPG
For this situation, what was mentioned was that there is a wall that is either not properly water proofed OR the water proofing has broken down from erosion. For me, yes a drainage system would be part of the attempt to extract the water/moisture from at or below the floor level of this existing basement. It would be one part of a two part strategy. First, a ditch would have to be excavated to expose the exterior side of the basement wall. Then proper water proofing would be applied to the exterior side of the wall, after cracks or breaks in the concrete/block were mended. Once this was completed, then the drainage system would be installed in the excavated ditch. Anything that is done in an attempt to control an exterior water problem that does not go down to or below the bottom of the floor level or bottom of wall level with the water problem is not going to correct the problem.
