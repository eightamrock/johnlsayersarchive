Soundman202012 September 2009#15
In that case diffusion its achieved just "by chance" since you can't predict what kind of frecuencies will be diffused,
I don't think it is "by chance" at all, and it can be predicted. Acoustics is far more science and far less art than it used to be. I'm not really up to speed on the math (although I'm sure André is!) but it doesn't look like it would be too hard to figure out what happens, in terms of diffusion, on John's "slot wall" design. Hard surfaces with sharp edges, known dimensions, etc. For someone with better math skills than mine, it shouldn't be too hard to predict what happens. Then again, for math-challenged folks like me, we can just go listen in such a room, and hear what is going on! The proof that it works, and is broadband, is right there in your ears. Your own live room is a case in point, Javier: it sounds so good mostly because of the diffusion coming of John's slot wall design. If it wasn't for that diffusion, it would not sound anywhere near as good. I guarantee that if you took off a few of those slats, the sound would change dramatically, for the worse: It would end up muddy and dead. Probably just taking off less than half a dozen slats would already cause some serious damage to the diffusion. I don't think John achieved that design by chance. I'm just guessing here, but I reckon he came up with that design partly from his years of experience and knowledge of rooms, but mostly from calculation. There's a reason for those measurements, and it isn't just aesthetics, I imagine! - Stuart -
