gullfo6 March 2011#2
One option might be to open up the walls and ceiling around the pipe and make sure it is damped using a soft rubber wrapping then soft insulation then box it in make the pipe existing within a room within a room if you will. You might end up with some ceiling and wall soffits but that may be much less expensive that building an entire room. As with normal isolation you need MSM around the pipe to be effective. You also need to make sure the pipe is not in direct contact with anything structural and this may require some isolating pipe brackets etc to make that happen. You might have to ensure the toilet is decoupled as well to ensure the flanking isn't coming from that.

xSpace6 March 2011#4
"-there are 6 AC fan units attached to the outside wall that cause low hmm but it decently tolerable. Q. So I'm currently looking in to AC isolation brackets, Or would building another brick layer on that wall add enough STC reduction (45?)?" Pictures would be a great help in this reference. Also, your post was a fine starter but you did not mention the material involved in the roof that the offending pump is on. If it is a wooden roof, then get the pump off of the roof....since the wooden structure is the path of least resistance and flanking is difficult to overcome with isolation pucks. The building of a brick interior room is always a good idea in theory. Where it breaks up on you is if there is not enough air space between existing structure and new installation, your mortar can pile up and create the bridge that short circuits the whole assembly. One of the things I have seen folks do is to install a semi-rigid insulation panel around the perimeter as the brick goes up, to ensure that the gap is damped and the mortar does not plug up and short circuit. But directly to that, the weakest link will be the most offending orifice and that is most likely the overhead ceiling that hasn't been mentioned as of yet. To build a brick wall and then to have a ceiling that is not capable of reaching the brick walls apparent stc levels or the hoped for transmission loss levels is going to keep you wondering why you didn't get that pump off the roof, since you will most likely keep hearing it:)
