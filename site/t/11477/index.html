Soundman20208 December 2008#2
a dedicated jam, tracking and mixing room.
That sounds like three rooms to me. In which case isolation is an issue: If you have excellent isolation through the wall and window between your control room and your "jam" room (JR), but poor isolation to the outside, then you will have poor isolation overall, as the sound that gets out through the JR outside wall will come right back in through the CR outside wall. . . If you isolate rooms from each other, that includes isolating them from the outside world too, otherwise the "outside world" becomes a flanking path for sound, and is your weakest link. On the other hand, if you build just one room to be both jam, tracking and control room all-in-one, then you might find it hard to track well, since you'll be hearing the direct sound of the instruments rather than just what the mics are picking up, and you might also find it hard to mix well, unless you take the drums and other acoustic instruments outside, since they will resonate sympathetically as you mix, thus distorting what you hear in the room. Also, mixing rooms (control rooms) need to be absolutely symmetrical, while live rooms, tracking rooms, jam rooms, etc. are usually built asymmetrically. Yes, many people on a tight budget or with a very small space do build a single room to do everything, but if you do that then you need to be aware of the consequences. I'm not sure how expensive building materials are where you live, but AU$ 15k sounds like it should be enough to do something basic and decent. Maybe two rooms, instead of three? Perhaps just one control room, and one live / jam room? How much space to you have available for this project? What shape? What type of music / instruments? Is this a commercial venture, to make money out if in the future, or just a place for musicians to hang out, play and record for fun?

You want to build a solid structure using contemporary methods and materials available to your country. If you do not know what either of these are, talk to those that do. In the long run, folks who try to use one product in place of another will either spend more overall or weaken the structure, or both. There will be differences in how your build will sound based on what you use for the interiors. I do not see 15K being enough to build much when you put a concrete slab under it and add hvac. But there are other options, like modular buildings or containers and others I haven't even thought of yet:) Good luck and welcome.

Is a slab essential? Is it inappropriate to build a "house on stilts" type of setup? This may be a dumb question. Every time I ask something on this website I cringe at the anticipated flaming for not having read Rod G's book yet!
Well, if isolation is not an issue, then you can probably live without a slab, I would think. I'm sure there are plenty of studios around that are built over wooden floors. However, don't forget that whatever you build as your base, it needs to be very tough: You are going to have a LOT of mass sitting on top of it. Even if you don't go for excellent isolation initially, you'll still be loading up a lot of thick gypsum board, framing, etc.. Not to mention equipment, people, acoustic treatment, beer.... And if you do want to keep your options open for the future, to perhaps improve the isolation with extra layers, then you should plan for that now, by building a really tough base. - Stuart -
