dbluefield28 July 2004#5
Does it make any difference is it 6mm or 12,5mm?
I believe John designs with 6 or 12 degree angles. 12 would be preferable for a superior RFZ. Just by eyeball - drawn as I suggested - the slatted walls should be plenty thick. Simply use a carpenters square to determine the angles, and attach a refference string to position your studs. You could also make removable, portable boxes, and indicated on the SAE site for your side splayed walls.

giles11711 August 2004#7
Yes there is a difference. You lose the angle for one, which reflects the sound towards the back of your room and out of your mix field. You also lose the additional bass trapping benefit that the angles will provide for you. It is really... really easy to build the angled slat resonator. Much easier than you would believe. The hardest part is cutting all the slat boards. (More so time consuming) and then spacing them and nailing them up. Basically all you are doing is framing a wall at an angle, sealing the enclosure, placing the insulation, fabric and nailing your slats on. As far as the corner traps if they are to be permanently placed against the wall, then you must make sure the corner is selaed so no air escapes. In other words, frame the corner, seal all the places where the frame meets the concrete wall then place your insul, fabric and slats.
