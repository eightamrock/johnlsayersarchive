Well, we actually started. That's right, nails have been nailed...drywall has been placed...errr yeah. we started the "beef" process. I'm pretty sure we have put in about 10 or 12 hours of work for a total of 3 (out of 32) bays completed. Yikes. So my new goal is to finish this studio for my grandchildren...... In all seriousness..... we started the process of adding weight to the existing floor (we'll be using an independently framed ceiling, so this is to help with bass transmission/footfall). Wow....its a mess. First of all, for anyone who wants to do this in a basement.....there are TONS of tacks/nails, etc sticking through the floor. You have to get 4x8 sheets of 3/8" of "styrofoam" insulation to put up first to cover the nails (recommended by Rod Gervais in other posts on this forum....see the picture below so you know what we're talking about.) So you need tons of backer rod....other folks have recommended online places to buy this stuff...but you HAVE to get it in a giant industrial size roll. It requires TONS of this stuff. Also get a tool used to push screen into place. I took a picture of both so you know what to look for. It makes it into a very quick job. Ok...acoustic caulk. Blah...what a mess. (see picture). I have one of those "pro caulk" thingies from "as seen of tv"....it works to save extra caulk, but regardless its very messy and it takes a LOT of caulk to do this. If you push the backer rod in to far....it means more caulk to fill the space. We put put the styrofoam up with some adhesive and used backer rod on the edges. i didn't caulk that layer since it doesn't have any sound attenuation anyways. We used 1.25 inch finish nails to hold the styrofoam in place to allow the adhesive to set. I think probably the best thing (if i did it again) is to use 3M spray adhesive to hold that stuff up as you go. Ok so once that's up, you have to put the first layer of 5/8 drywall up, hold it in place, and caulk it. I have 3/8 backer rod, so we cut the drywall 1/4 inch short on each side. We made 2x4 T contraptions to jam them up hard to the deck so we could caulk. I used a 4" metal spatula to shift the drywall back and forth so you get an even space on each side (1/4 " or so) once its jammed into place. Backer rod, then caulk those damn edges and make a mess of yourself. I used surgical gloves (i'm in healthcare) since its so darn messy. Then Put some more 4D finish nails up to hold that in place, remove the 2x4 contraptions and pray it doesn't all fall down. Fantastic, it stayed! Repeat with 2nd layer of 5/8 drwall. No finish nails this time...we got 1x2 ledger board and placed it below the 2nd layer of drywall and screwed it in place (see rod's book). We found it works well for one person to use a piece of ledger board to push up on the ledger board you are going to use to hold the drywall in place. You can get it a lot more secure this way then trying to hold it up tight with one hand and drill with the other. So what i didn't do because it seems like a huge pain in my rear is remove the cross bridging. We are just leaving that place without drywall and hoping the increased mass of the whole floor will help, even though small sections don't have any beef added. Plus, we are putting an independently framed ceiling in anyways, and this is just a hobby studio. No reason to waste hours i guess. I added a picture so you can see where we stopped. Well 3 down, 29 to go. Sheesh. I'll post again when thats done. Probably 2012.

I prefer WIC or the RISC clips for support. On the fire stop, if you go with a rectangular wall structure for isolation it will be easier. Then build you inner treatments on angles. If you have less than the desired space, you can cut down the insulation to ensure you are not compressing it too much and end up coupling the walls. The caulking (fire caulk) is important as the insulation slows the flames but not the gases...

gullfo26 March 2011#21
If you have the option to use the corners, then do so. You could indent the corners and still leave the angle side walls past the soffits. This way behind the soffits you have enough depth for trapping and still have the angles you want on the side walls in the CR and the iso booth. In previous post on fire stop - the edge at the top of the inner wall is where you put it. A 5/8th inch type x drywall on the joists and on the top portion of the isolation wall with rigid insulation gently inserted and fire caulked will do it.
