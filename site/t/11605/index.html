Hi Michawave, and welcome! You certainly do seem to have a sad story there. I'm curious about one thing on your floor plan: You show some free-standing "slats" in the room with 20 cm of insulation behind them, but they are not sealed, there is no cavity containing the insulation. In other words, on your plan there is no "box" surrounding the insulation at the back: it appears to just be the slats, and then the insulation, and nothing else. Why is that? I'm trying to understand what effect that is supposed to have. Also, you have two large red triangular areas marked "3 x Helmholtz". What are they supposed to be doing, and what frequencies are they supposed to be tuned to? What are the dimensions of those? I'm no expert at all in this area, but to my eye, the layout of your treatment seems rather strange, and does not seem to follow any of the common principles, such as LEDE or DEDE. Did the acousticians tell you what they were basing their designs on? To me, it looks like you have reflective surfaces right behind your speakers (the slats), with no absorption, it seems like you have no absorption at your first reflection points, your entire back wall is dead, and the slats don't seem to be working as slats at all. Have you considered soffit-mounting your speakers? From what I've read, that really does help to tighten up the bass and improve imaging. Have you considered some form of diffusion on your rear wall? The studio isn't that big, but it looks like you have around 4m from your head to the back wall, so it might be worth a shot. What about the ceiling? What is it made of, and how is it treated (apart from the cloud)? But to me, the strangest thing is those slats standing up in the middle of the room, with no sealed cavities behind them. Maybe if you could post some photos of the room and close-ups of the treatment, so that folks here can get a better idea of what you have there. - Stuart -

Soundman202021 January 2009#6
I hope Ethan or Rod or Andre or someone like that can offer their opinion on what you should do here. I have some ideas, but they would just be educated guesses, whereas those guys really know what they are doing. But anyway, one comment:
Perhaps its a sulution to place the speaker near the frontwall and reduce the increased bass with an eq? or is this a bad idea?
For what it's worth, Genelec dos NOT recommend using any kind of EQ with their speakers, except for the EQ controls on the speaker itself (if any). They sent me a lot of very useful info when I asked, while I was designing my soffits, and among that was advise to never use EQ in you monitor speaker path, do to the possibility of introducing phasing issues. Genelec does sell soffit mount kits for some models of speakers (not sure about yours), and they told me that those speakers that can be soffit mounted already come with bass roll-off controls. Placing your speaker up against a SOLID wall is similar to (but not the same as) soffit mounting. However, I'm not sure what it would do with those slats of yours, since they are not a solid wall! I doubt they would work anything like an infinite baffle, or even an ordinary wall. I can't tell you what to do in your room, but I can tell you what I'm planning for my room, and maybe you can take some of that and apply it in your room: My Speakers will be mounted in concrete soffit panels, angled at nearly 45 degrees (towards the mix position) and tilted down by roughly 12 degrees. In my case I need those angles, as my room is much smaller than yours. In your case, you can probably use 60 degree angles and zero tilt (depending on your console, desk, etc.). The concrete might be covered with acoustic cloth, or I might just leave it bare. The space between the soffits will be deep absorption, several inches. The space under the soffit panels, below the desk will have bass traps in it, probably hangers. The side walls will be splayed, with slats on them, and deep absorption at first reflection points. The floor will be laminate over concrete, and the ceiling will be 100% absorption (several inches of mineral wool, with acoustic cloth for aesthetics). There will be two large bass traps in the rear corners, diagonal, floor to ceiling, and the rest of the rear wall will be absorption. I might cover the mineral wool with thin plastic of sort, after testing the room, to return some of the highs. I might add or remove slats on the walls, as needed. I might add extra bass trapping in the rear wall/ceiling corner and other corners, if needed. I might make a few other minor changes, as needed. That seems to be the general consensus here for a good-sounding room, and seems to have been implemented successfully in many studios built by folks here. That's about all I can suggest, with my limited knowledge. But it seems to me that your solution will be along the lines of removing a LOT of that stuff (perhaps all of it), since it obviously isn't working, then starting again from bare walls, using one single design philosophy, instead of the hodge-podge mixture of stuff that these "acousticians" have dumped on you. A question about your floor: What is it made of? Is it floated?
