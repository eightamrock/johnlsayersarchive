sami_sumea25 April 2006#1
Hi, all, We've started planning (and building) a new studio in Tampere, Finland. The studio will be around 100m2, built on a concrete/brick warehouse basement. The final studio will contain 3 control (and edit) rooms and one about 40m2 live room for drums, and a sauna. We're happy with most of our plans so far, but I'd like to hear your comments on the main control room design, as we currently have two options that we can go for, and choosing which one works better seems to be a bit hard to do by guesswork only. The control room is 4.7 meters long, 4.0 meters wide and 3.1 meters high (see the option_1_top shot and raw_space screenshots). The control room is symmetrical, there's a double glass window and double doors on both side walls. The side walls are two separate 2x4 frameworks, covered with 12mm gypsum board on both sides, and filled with rockwool. The front and rear walls are concrete and brick. Unfortunately we cannot build a full-height tilted front wall with flush mounted speakers, as there are a two windows on the front wall, and two heating/water pipes going through the room, and we need to make sure there's an access to the vents in those pipes if required (the space is rented). This forces us to either build the front wall of the control room from something very lightweight material (such as cloth covered 2x4 framework) and fill the front wall with a really thick layer of rockwool (something like 60cm), or build the front wall so low that we can access the vents in the roof without problems. I've attached the two design options here, and I'd like to hear your comments which one would be more likely to work. The option 2 shot only has the front wall design in place, so it looks a bit .. ugly :) Also, if you have any comments on the rear wall - should we just fill the rear wall with 20-40cm of rockwool + cloth cover, or build it more "live" sounding with wood slats, or try to design something with two tilted sides? br, Sami

John Sayers wrote:

Thanks, John! I actually did consider something like this, but it made me wonder about two things: Feel free to correct me if I'm wrong :-) First, did you mean that the traps are built as tuned traps (with wood slats, meaning they have a hard, reflecting surface), or just be completely filled with rockwool with soft fabric front (which would make more sense to me?) If you mean hard surface (wooden helmholz-type resonator), if I do it like this, the angled walls are actually acting as a "mirror" right towards the listening position (as the speakers are not inside the wall), which causes phase cancellation effects on low-mid frequencies. In my design, the speakers have over 1 m distance from any hard wall, which is something that Genelec recommends when using their 8000-series products with sub (they have a crossover freq of 85hz which means that the main speakers need to be 1.1 meters away from the wall to move the cancellation dip below that frequency). http://www.genelec.com/support/tutorials.php Or do the tilted angles have something to do with preventing standing waves in the room? br, Sami
