John Sayers28 October 2004#2
The first studio I worked in (1967) was a rectangle around 30 x 15. In that we would place: 6 strings - (4 violins, one viola and one cello) 3 trumpets 2 trombones 5 saxes two guitars 1 piano 1 bass 1 drums 1 percussion The mix was a balance of spill/bleed. :):) In those days there were no additional booths - everyone was in the same room. It was the power guitar music of the late 60s early 70s that created the need for the isolation booth where multi cab marshalls were overpowering drums and acoustic pianos/guitars etc. and the studios changed dramatically with the advent of the Hidely designed studios and the west coast tight dry sound emerged. Fortunately we've moved on from there and the modern studio is more live but still retains the isolation part. Last night I saw Mick Jagger singing in a studio and he was singing in a relatively live room (metropolis in London) but he was surrounded by 8ft dead gobbos to tame it down. cheers john

I've made some pretty formidable gobos and love the sh#t outta them. One side is a slat resonator and the other is dead (cloth covered 703 with air gap). Designed with help from this forum. Great spot folks. I use them to close my room down for mixing and use the slats for drums, acoustics, horns, you name it. the single most versatile and effective tool in the studio. Big sukkas too, 6'x3'x12". I'd love to have 6 more but then I couldn't walk around my studio anymore :( Time to build that new studio..... -bassman
