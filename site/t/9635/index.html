gullfo24 October 2007#2
put the insulation up in the joists and leave an air gap between the tiles and the insulation. the tiles will do better with the air gap. put insulation into the new stud walls to dampen the drywall. R13 for 2x4. for treatments - deal with corners with bass trapping - consider wall-ceiling and wall-floor corners if necessary. put 1"-2" thick absorbers on reflections points on front, side, ceiling, and back walls. for the live room - stagger 2" thick absorbers (mounted off the wall by 2"-4") on walls and ceiling - basically if you have a number of 2'x4' absorbers - leave at least 2' between them and alternate them on opposing walls. treat the corners with bass trapping. this should leave the room decently live and balanced. in the live room - if you need to build something - build some polycylindrical diffusers - you can put them over the corners to add trapping and diffusion in one treatment. the mirrors - hang some heavy drapes you can pull over when you need to dampen their (acoustic) reflections.

gullfo1 November 2007#11
the layout of absorbers and polys seem good. if you find after construction your measurements indicate more treatments are needed, you can add them as needed. use 1/8" ply on the polys. the ceiling being half-drywall should be ok, since you have limitations on what you can do, so you make the best choices you can. the reality is, insulation is NOT isolation in any form. you need mass and a lot of it. if you're not able to build a proper room-within-a-room or even apply drywall consistently across the entire space, then your isolation is pretty much limited by whatever absorption you might get and the basic sound isolation of common construction (call it 20-26db), and air spaces (such as the rest of your home). so you might get a total of 30db isolation from the inside to the outside as-is and not much more with the acoustic ceiling tiles etc.

gullfo11 December 2007#24
the wider slot opening is a treble trap, not bass... the slats close together work at lower frequencies... i was just suggesting an idea for the large absorber next to the chimney in place of a chair rail... if you're not putting drywall on the back, skip the hangers. the bigger space will do the work. the hangers work better either in the space or within a cavity that needs extra absorption surface. in this case, you have a big cavity which is separated by the dense rigid insulation which lets bass through and the cavity will do much of the rest. if the walls are intended to be mostly absorptive, then it doesn't matter EXCEPT that the slat resonator needs a sealed (relative to the room) cavity to be effective. otherwise its not really doing much in the way of Helmholtz absorption but more reflective and somewhat diffusive.
