Eric_Desart17 November 2007#2
Rod told in US and per USG, for cosmetic reasons (light reflection on seems) one mounts the last board horizontal. Here in Europe, in as far as I know and found on the net, but mainly my own experience we standard mount vertically (all of them). I saw UK documentation where the most inner board was mounted horizontally (hence that the reverse of the method described by Rod), but that was clearly caused by the type of board (called planks, but still gypsum board = thicker and smaller) with diverting width from the outer layers (hence they couldn't be placed vertically on the standard OC distance of the studs, positioned for the outer layers). I think there is no reason to find one method better than the other from an acoustical point of view, and since both methods are used, also mechanically I don't see a difference, other than I can imagine that one or the other method can be more efficient in function of board sizes versus to be covered measures. Also these but joints (board ends), where two boards meet, are a bit more difficult to finish for DIYers I assume. Summary: I think it doesn't matter how you place them, as long you do it good. But xSpace seems more of a practical expert than I am.

xSpace17 November 2007#5
I suspect, in single layer commercial sheetrock walls, the vertical installation of the sheetrock eliminates the horizontal joint that can bust loose from wall racking. This installation procedure may also aid in the walls ability to retard fire by placing all the joints on a stud which provides solid backing. Horizontal installing of the wall base panels in wood framing is preferred in respect to multi sheetrocked walls. The horizontal sheetrock ties more framing members together so a stronger wall is the result. As it applies to multi layers, changing the direction of the next layer will offset the joints. So this supports the fire retarding notion above. This also adds strength to the wall and reduces lumps bumps and an uneven surface from multiple mud joints. For multiple layer ceiling applications, installing the first layer perpendicular to framing members gives the same effect as if you were installing to a wall section. Especially when the ceiling is directly attached to the framing below, as is your case. You will get a stronger ceiling that is less likely to rack or twist. But if you talk to ten different installers you are subject to get ten different installation methods. This is my story and I'm stickin' to it:)
