knightfly15 September 2004#8
If you're only using one room at a time, and things you do in the shop won't contaminate your music gear, how about forgetting the wall and using the entire space for better acoustics? It's not like you're welding, grinding, or cutting plywood, right? Generally, you can place your mix area at the center of the short wall in a room and place absorbent at first reflection points - beyond that, your 10' ceiling and 15' dimensions are causing some modes to stack up - one way to lessen the negative effect of this would be to build some of John's slant-faced free-standing slot absorbers, shown here - I would change the design to make each section 4' wide, this would combat the 2/3 ratio of height to width more effectively - here's the page - http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm I'd give serious thought to making this entire space one acoustic entity, unless you've got a bunch of tools on pegboard or something elss that will rattle a lot... Steve

knightfly16 September 2004#12
Probably more like this - you don't want your head centered in any plane other than left/right, or you'll run into serious modal peaks and dips that will help you make crappy mixes - a good front/back head location is around 38% , then make sure your head and speakers are NOT centered height wise either - in an 8 foot ceilinged room, 42" to center of woofers and ears is about as good as you can get... Steve Oh, I narrowed the speakers because in most cases vertical orientation is better - less stringent horizontal head placement for minimal phasing problems (caused by varying distance of tweet vs woof and your ears)

knightfly20 September 2004#16
1 - likely both - one of John's rear wall traps centered in the back may be enough in addition to the traps shown; if not, the junctions between walls and ceiling near the corners can add more. 2 - 49-52 inches from floor - ears should NOT be within about 2" of 45" - either above or below that distance, and if speakers and ears aren't the same height then speakers should be slanted down accordingly, so they are aimed at the ear level. 3 - ceiling - low in front, high in rear - a steeper angle in the first 1/3 of the front will help first reflections... Steve
