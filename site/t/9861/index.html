rod gervais7 December 2007#2
Alex, Drywall is generally the cheapest mass you can buy - and therefor the best material you can use. I would not worry about loosing 15mm here - 15 mm there - it doesn't add up to a hill of beans when everything is said and done. Stick with double wall construction - a minimum of 2 layers of 15mm drywall each face (you don't gain anything of real value by mixing 12mm & 15mm thicknesses) and you will probably be fine, dependant on what you really need for isolation. That's something you have to decide on now........ Rod

rod gervais7 December 2007#4
AlexHoward wrote:
My main concern is over-spill from the drum booth, and soundproofing the Vocal booth as much as possible, because it’s next to the entrance. For the Drum Booth, do we need an air gap between the double walls?
Absolutely
I’m pretty sure the Control room isn’t going to make too much noise with the speakers, but would a loud cough or a real belly laugh get through…?
Now that is one bad assumption - a real bad one - I would be seriously concerned with the speakers if you plan on tracking live - with even an 86dB level in that space and single walls - IMHO isolation of the control room to the tracking spaces is critical........ I don't need my mics re-recording bleed-through that is making it from my control room back out to my tracking room.
Think as the designs getting closer to completion, I just really want to make sure we haven’t over looked anything that could be detrimental to the recording, or ruin that perfect take
I would say that the single biggest issue I pick up on when providing inspection services for clients is the vast number of areas that end up with something bridging from one space to another that essentially leave a series of acoustic "short circuits" creating flanking paths for sound - and the fixing of those errors are generally not that difficult. (which is to suggest that they never had to happen in the first place if someone had just been paying attention to detail). Sincerely, Rod
