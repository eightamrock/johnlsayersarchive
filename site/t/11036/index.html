drumroom24 wrote:
I think a 6'X6' room should be large enough.
Sorry - but that is not nearly large enough...... Even a beginners drum set - with double toms and a floor tom - and just one ride cymbal - one crash and a high hat will eat up all of that room - and not allow your son a chance to feel anything but trapped. It is also so small that it will be next to impossible to treat so as to acheive any sort of sound at all. I am a drummer - and I wouldn't be able to practice in a room that small for any length of time - and would eventually become frustrated enough to just quit playing the drums if I were a beginner. So if your goal is to encourage your son to play the drums and stick with it - then rethink your design...... if your goal is to frustrate him so he quits - then steady on in the way you're headed now - it will succeed. A drum room for a basic kit (my kit eats up about 8' in width by 6' in depth) should be at least 10' x 14............... the 12' ceilings help. You also have to think about the fact that he will eventually want to have some friends over so he can make actual music......... which will be very important if he is to become successful in this endevor........ at which time the space should be more like 14 x 16........ However - begining with the smaller space (10x14) is ok for a start. I really wouldn't go a whole lot smaller than that.
I want to build the walls out of 2x3 steel studs and double 1/2" sheetrock with green glue in between. What else does anyone suggest I can install in the room to deaden the sound greatly coming from the room into the rest of the space. Is green glue a good start?
The 2x3 studs are pretty much useless for this - you really should construct this with 2x6 walls (wood is best) with resilient channel (RC-1 if you are real careful to not short out by screwing into a stud) and then 2 layers of drywall (5/8") each face with green glue in between. Mass plus the larger air space is your friend here - these drums have a lot of low frequency transmissions that the 2x3 stud will never overcome. 2x3 walls like you described work real well in the frequency range of the human voice - we use them all the time in hotels for example - BUT they are lousy when it comes to low frequency transmissions. I know you asked for inexpensive solutions - but LF isolation and cheap solutions do not compute......... you can have one or the other - but not both. Doors and seals for them will be another challenge - as will caulking all penetrations through the walls and the perimiter of all wall panels. And then the question of fresh air so you maintain your son's brain cells also comes into play - sound isolation equals no air movement equals unconscious bodies after a short while in small rooms without fresh air. bu now you realize that this is much more than you envisioned - but it won't do you any good for me to sugar coat this...... Anyway - that is a start - room treatments come after - but first you must decide where you are going to head with this.......

Acoustic & MIDI drums have both advantages & disadvantages. In this case someone doesn't seems to have the budget & space to build up a dedicated booth. This is one of the possible alternatives. A beginner needs to learn the techniques first, that's true for almost all instruments. He doesn't have enough exercised ears before months of pratice (even years for some). I'm a drummer myself & use MIDI drums since 7 years now. It was pretty hard to imagine that a MIDI drums could compete with an acoustic one those days. Almost 3 years ago, all samples became realistic as they were recorded in high definition & in 11 velocities. But the weak point was the cymbals & crashes. Since 2or 3 years, new samples recorded in 13 different velocities powered by today's stronger machines are so realistic that even an experienced drummer can't hear the difference. If you knew how many acoustic tracks are released everyday with MIDI drums you would be surprised ! Of course a "WarLord" is better that a chinese MIDI drum, but it won't help you learn quicker or better. A beginner makes mistakes. So your WarLord will cost you 5 chinese drums before you can play your 1st track properly. A good instrument must be deserved !
