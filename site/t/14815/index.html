jhbrandt18 September 2010#5
Kalet, Window into the booth or tracking room should be out of the early reflection zone. I would recommend facing the wall at the top of the drawing and go from there. Start by setting the mix position about 54 inches from the wall in front of you and your speaker in a equilateral triangle. Trace the 'bounce' from the walls to the mix position with a mirror - do the same for the ceiling. Then position your viewing window out of that reflection zone. Cheers, John

Soundman202020 September 2010#13
Once i finish the 2nd wall.. whats better to finish it in sheetrock.. cause in the booth we were planning to cover it with fabric all around.. do you recommend this or not..
You seem to be confusing "isolation" with "treatment". In order to isolate the room, both leaves of the wall (inner leaf and outer leaf) MUST be made of sheetrock (or some other heavy, dense material). You cannot make a "leaf" out of cloth. Depending on how you build the frames to support your leaves, you might be left with the studs of the inner-leaf facing the room, in which case your construction is called "inside-out". In that case, you CANNOT put sheetrock on those studs, because if you did then you would have a three-leaf wall, which will actually damage your isolation (make it worse). So, if you are building inside-out, then you will have studs facing the room. You can now install treatment between those studs, and part of that treatment might include insulation between the studs, covered with cloth. Doing that in your booth, that would be a good idea, IF you are building it inside-out. Maybe you could draw a sketch of what you have in mind and post it here, to make sure that you are doing things right. - Stuart -

What you are showing in your diagram looks like it is the inner-leaf of your MSM wall. Is that correct? Since you are showing a 1" space between that stud frame and the outer-leaf, and teh "outer leaf" seems to be made of concrete or brick on that diagram, then you have to put sheetrock on that frame, on the side facing the room. That will give you enough air gap to have good isolation. Your diagram does not show the ceiling. For the ceiling you should do the same as for the walls: Lay joists across the tops of the walls, leaving a gap above. Fill the gap with insulation, and hang sheetrock underneath the joists. gullfo20 September 2010#17
it can get confusing but basically create the isolation walls/ceiling without regard for treatment and you'll end up with (mostly) rectangular rooms. if you can do normal safe construction then taking the next steps (the detailed ones) of properly sealing and insulating etc should be straight forward. as Stuart points out, if you're not used to doing proper construction, consider having someone else do the work because the isolation walls/ceiling are going to be much heavier than normal and mistakes could result in your studio collapsing on you or someone else. when in doubt, get a structural engineer to help. in fact, your building codes may require it. in general, you are creating 2 back to back walls which have the drywall layers on the inside of the room and you are leaving a gap between the frames. behind the drywall you are adding insulation to dampen the resonances of the drywall and to convert the acoustic energy being transferred via the air spring (air gap between the walls) into heat (via friction) before transferring to the other wall. you can find many many examples of this construction on the site.

gullfo20 September 2010#19
you should use wall isolation clips (PAC INTL, WIC, etc) to make sure it is held steady. you could also use isolation supports for the ceiling to take some load off the walls and maybe even get some decoupling between the walls and ceiling. yeah, once you have the isolation in place (windows, doors too), you can work out the treatment options. bear in mind you still want to finalize your room ratios before doing this work to make sure you have them as good as they can be given your needs and budget. spend some time with the mode calculators and try a number of different ratios in each space to see how you can balance the space versus best mode distribution. for every trade-off made for less balance you'll pay for it in space consumed by treatments...
