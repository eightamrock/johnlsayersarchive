Electrical Issues: One of the prospective electricians was by again today before finalizing his quote - he said that the electrical powers that be in Ontario are encouraging electricians to run separate 2 conductor wires for each outlet when isolated grounding is desired (rather than simply running a separate copper wire for the isolated ground). He explained that (and I hope I'm getting this right) it is because in some cases where separate ground wires were run there was an issue of heat build up on the neutral bar at the panel when equipment with switchable/switched mode power supplies were plugged into the outlets. But more of a concern was that he didn't think they would let him run the outlet grounds to a separate grounding bar at the panel (which would terminate at the new separate rod in the ground) while lights and HVAC grounded back to the house main panel and the original rod in the ground there. :shock: He was going to check with ESA though. I know codes here can be a little more strict than elsewhere - anyone in Ontario know whether this is the case or not? That would obviously be pretty major! He was also suggesting a grounding plate rather than a rod, which only goes a couple feet below the surface. I haven't read anything on those on the forum - thoughts? Its crazy that I can talk to multiple electricians and have each of them say something different. I had Mr. Electric in to give a quote and the guy said a 30 amp run (in addition to the existing 30 amp run which will go to HVAC) wouldn't be enough for the outlets I proposed to put in (4 20 amp and 6 15 amp). He said a 60 amp run would be required but quoted me both ways (a very high quote I might add).

I wasn't going to install my audio runs until after the electrical was inspected but since I'm waiting on my electrician I've decided to move ahead. I was going to run conduit up the wall at the front of the CR and then through the 2x6 ceiling joists to my equipment room. I needed to put in at least 1 1/2" conduit in order to fit a DigiLink connector through (the largest connector going through). So I bought all the stuff and then found that the 90s were just too big to go in like I had planned :oops: I could use plumbing piping instead with the small hard-turn 90s (I don't think the electrical inspector is going to be at all concerned with that) but then I may not be able to fish cables through in the future - and that's really the only reason I want to use conduit at all. I might just run all of my expected cabling through the ceiling joists without any conduit and then put in an empty plumbing conduit run with those hard 90s for the possibility of future runs. But in order to actually be able to fish cables through I might not be able to run them to the ideal locations - I might have to have the conduit exit at the ceiling and then have any cables run down the wall (exposed) to my desk.
