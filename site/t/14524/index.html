britune4 July 2010#2
It could probably be done, but you'd want to take extra care in your design to maintain a Mass Air Mass design. This may require something like a sheet of thick laminated glass on the inner leaf, followed by airspace, then the outer leaf (skylight). The design and construction of this would have to be done very carefully to avoid have a lot of sound escaping. Not to mention that skylights can be prone to water leaks if not installed properly. Do his skylights retain the same mass density as the roof leaves they are part of? I would suspect not. As long as you stick to the basic rule of keeping the mass constant throughout a leaf then I can't see why there would be any problem. Just because part of that mass is transparent to light should not have any effect on its ability to isolate sound. Isolation is a function of mass and air gap, not of transparency. So just make sure that the glass in your outer leaf roof has the same mass density as the surrounding roof itself, and the glass in the inner leaf ceiling has the same mass density as the surrounding ceiling, then you will be fine. Of course, I'm assuming that the rest of the roof is already designed such that rain noise will not penetrate! In other words, that you have already considered rain as an impact noise source in the design of your roof, and that you have enough mass, decoupling, air space, and damping that even heavy rain will not be audible inside. If that is the case, then replacing some small sections of the leaves with transparent material of the same mass should make no difference at all to the isolation. - Stuart -

It is not obvious, until pointed out... The "rain noise" is from the impact of the rain drops against the highly stressed glass/plastic of the outer pane of the window. In other words the sound is being created by pane, not transmitted through pane. Normal roof toppings have much less stress in them, so less noise is created. Andre

xSpace6 July 2010#7
Installing a skylight requires framing for the light shaft that will directly couple the roof (again) with the ceiling joist unless some time is spent to make stable shaft framing that does not touch the roof but can support itself plus the load you install on it mass wise and that the ceiling framing will also be able to support the consolidated mass as well. Another reason why this would have to be carefully planned out if pursued at all.
