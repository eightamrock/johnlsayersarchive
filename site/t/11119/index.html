One of the things you will deal with on a daily basis is heat trying to get inside that building. The other thing will be trapped moisture trying to get out, by any path necessary. To frustrate convective heat transfer you could look into a typical building exterior product like foam board insulation. Having no view of your barn I couldn't help much in how you would install it...but you require something, not sure densguard is the first option, for me. Radiating heat is something else a metal barn is going to be good at doing, another idea would be foil covered insulation, with the foil towards the metal. I know you want to run with your build, but condensation behind the metal and the sun from the 11 month summers we experience here in the South are going to be competing for which one can ruin your barn first!

rod gervais22 August 2008#8
xSpace wrote:
One of the things you will deal with on a daily basis is heat trying to get inside that building. The other thing will be trapped moisture trying to get out, by any path necessary.
Actually - in La I would be more concerned about ooutside moisture trying to get in - that's why you want a vapor barrier on the outside of the wall rather than the inside. The fear here is outside moisture condensing on the inner wall. I would not have any concern in this situation about using standard 5/8" drywall on the outside face - my bigger concern would be how to go about getting a vapor barrier to stop that moist outside air from making it's way into the building (remember what ever has the most moisture bexomes the high pressure zone - and in this case the outside is high pressure 90% of the year. (My son and his family live there - so I get to hear the complaints every time he calls). Rod
xSpace22 August 2008#9
rod gervais wrote:
- my bigger concern would be how to go about getting a vapor barrier to stop that moist outside air from making it's way into the building
Right. The blue foam is an exterior product designed for this specific purpose. Slow the heat, stop the moisture. But for it to be the effective, the envelope needs to exist with no breaks. As an alternative, I would consider moving the walls in a foot or so, so as not to even touch the exterior. Then lay out the room in a room design from there.
Slobbermonster23 August 2008#10
it might be out of you budget but you might want to see if you have any contractor in the area that use spray foam insulation.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.betterinsulation.com/UploadImage/Image/spray-foam_ceiling.jpg
Since you're in a humid area you have the opposite problem than those of us in the desert have - you're trying to keep the moisture from coming into the buildings and we're trying to let the moisture out of our buildings. as far as using gunnite or shotcrete that your friend says he can do for $80 -wow that's cheap but I'm not sure what benefit you'd get from that. If for some reason you pursue the idea, it's pretty simple to keep it attached to the walls . You can drill holes into the concrete floor then epoxy rebar into them - then build a lattice work of rebar - then wire tie something like chain link fencing to the rebar lattice work. Keep in mind you're adding a bunch of weight to the concrete floor and your pole barn by doing shotcrete, since it's only a slab and doesn't have a footer cuz the foundation of the barn is only on the poles, you'll have to calc the loads on the floor and the barn poles before pursuing shotcrete. If the metal was airtight it would be a great vapor barrier so another way to make the metal airtight would be to explore an elastomeric coating system. You'd basically foam and caulk all bigger voids like seams and where the metal meets the wood and "paint" the elastomeric - it's a bit like spraying chewing gum and take a big airless with a big tip but hey , what do I know
