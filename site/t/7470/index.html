Creature8211 November 2006#4
Hey DemoKing - I'm making some assumptions, like typical household loading. I'm pretty familiar with I-joists. 14 feet spanning 21 feet is a bit of a stretch. I realize that it may calc out for strength and deflection, but your span-to-depth ratio is 18... a little risky... especially if it's an active floor. I would encourage you to go 16" deep. Installing them at 12" o.c. will not help that much (stiffness/bounce-wise). I know that's counter-intuitive but it's true. If you have a couple walls that run perpendicular to the joists, that's probably no big deal. If you have a wall that is parallel to the joists I would add one extra joist under the wall. Creatch

Creature8214 November 2006#6
Hi DemoKing - Sorry... didn't mean to scare you. A couple things.. 1) When you say supporting the studio upstairs, I assume you mean that the joists are supporting the studio immediately above them... and that there is not another story above that. If that's the case then sounds like you're okay, structurally. 2) If you mean that there's a wall above the joists and the wall in turn holds up another floor above that, then you may have a problem.. or at least something that needs to be checked out by a professional. Hopefully you're in the item 1) category. The 14-inch joists are probably fine structurally. I'm just saying that if you have a choice, use 16-inch joists as a precaution. If you don't have a choice then you have to use 14-inch joists.. you may have a little bounce in the floor, and you may not... but going to a closer on-center spacing will not gain you much so don't waste your money doing that. You'd be better off spending your money on a heavier deck like 1-1/8 plywood. Regards, Creature82
