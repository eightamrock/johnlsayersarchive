knightfly8 September 2004#6
There's no question that acoustics and sound containment are at cross-purposes in most cases - Newell goes into this in some detail in his Recording Studio Design book; one of the instances he mentions is the recording of When the Levee Breaks by Led Zeppelin - a total fluke in acoustics that worked really well. The drum sound for that song was Bonham trying out a different drum kit on a break, the kit had been set up in a hallway in this big old house, Headley Grange, in Hampshire England - the rest of the guys were in the "recording" room, Bonham went out to the hallway to check out the new kit, forgot to close the door, started doing his thing, and Andy Johns heard this incredible sound (being picked up from mics in the OTHER ROOM) in the remote truck - the rest, as they say, is history... This particular house was 'way out in the quiet country, so no containment necessary - when we have to constrain the sound, it's a whole different set of conditions inside, and much more difficult (if even possible) to get "that sound". That, along with the obvious talent on both sides of the window, is something you can never get out of a "wonderbox" processor... Steve
