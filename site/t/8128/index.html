kendale4 February 2007#5
Aloha Steve, It's Q & A time! - Studio to be built on deck. You may want to have a structural engineer take a look at your deck construction to verify whether or not it can handle the extra weight required in studio construction. To give you a general idea of what this means, imagine every wall/ceiling in your studio consisting of 2-3 layers of drywall - 2 x 4 stud framing filled with 3" of either fiberglass (unfaced) or rock/mineral wool, and then 2-3 more layers of drywall. Toss in a couple of hundred lbs. of nails/screws/straps/caulking/etc., and then several more hundred lbs of musicians and equipment, and you begin to get the idea. You may also want to start reading up on how you're going to approach minimizing flanking and obtaining isolation using standard deck construction. Be sure to check out the "stickies" at the top of each forum category - lots of good stuff to be had there! - 6' x 6' restroom - Are you planning for ADA requirements? If so, I think it's closer to 6' x 7'. If not, 6' x 6' is a little generous, perhaps? (Unless you've got some large clients in your area, that is.)
The picture you see with the deck, has the door that right now leads into the house. That will all be closed off and a new wall put up. that is the only wall that REALLY needs to be sound proof because it is agaist the living room of the house.
- You may find that it's not possible to "spot treat" when soundproofing your wall just next to the livingroom. It's pretty much an "all or less than what you really needed" type thing. - How many people do you find yourself recording at one time vs in the control room at one time? Food for thought, Aloha 8)
