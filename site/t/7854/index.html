I was recommended the Audimute blankets by an associate of mine who I regularly hire to do sound for film and video work (field mixing, studio recording, etc.). He's got a studio in his house which is very small, and every now and then he needs to do a bigger setup there. He bought a bunch of the Audimute blankets and hung them from horizontal bars attached to C-stands (for those of you not in the video industry, a C-stand is a very heavy duty light stand). Basically created a 10x8 framework within his living room, and used the Audimute blankets to create a wall all the way around (a make-shift room within a room). It wasn't soundproof by any means, but it absorbed enough of the outside sounds and dampened any slap-back in the room that he was able to do ADR (advanced dialogue replacement, aka looping) for a movie soundtrack he was posting. They're not perfect, but they do absorb much more than ordinary moving blankets and most other similar products, and he often uses them in the field to build a make shift sound booth when on location on a video/film shoot, if there's a particularly important piece of quiet dialogue and extensive outside sounds. I'm about to use them to line the walls of my band practice room/recording studio, not because they're the best option, but they're quite affordable and work well to deaden sounds coming from the room. They'll get 703 fibreglass batts between the studs, a layer of drywall, Audimute Peacemaker MLV-type rubber sheeting, then another layer of drywall. The ceiling treatment is still yet to be determined. I'm not going out of my way to sing the praises of the Audimute blankets, as I haven't used them in my studio yet, but I've seen them in use at my associate's place, and we've used them on the set of several video shoots and they do what they say they're supposed to do - and much better than moving blankets and other competing products out there. They are definitely NOT snake oil. One of the main guys in the company is a drummer and he has a similar setup for his drum room at home. Check out their website, they sell many different sound proofing and absorption products - the blankets are just some of the "budget" products they sell. I will report back as to their effectiveness, after I've put them up. (I'll definitely be doing a sound test before and after).
