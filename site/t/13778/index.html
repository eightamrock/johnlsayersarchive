Soundman202012 June 2010#19
although someone told me the electrics on the tv and camera would cause a slight buzz or humm through the mic.......i dunno?!?.
If all your cables are properly screened and grounded, then it shouldn't be a problem. I often run live sound for events where I have to have the sound system, lighting system and video/multimedia system all in the same place, all fed from the same power source, and with the cables arranged roughly like a large bowl of spaghetti ... Very seldom do I get problems with hum, and even when it does happen it normally turns out to be something in the sound system, like an badly wired or poor quality instrument pickup or amp. I don't think I've ever had a hum problem caused by the video equipment. But I do take care to set up good grounding for everything, and that helps hugely. - Stuart -
dfeiszli12 June 2010#20
So, I guess it's time for an update.... I finally installed my system a couple months ago and it's working well. I got some fairly cheap Dynex 32" LCD TVs and these cameras: http://www.supercircuits.com/Security-C ... 3CHR4G-RTI The TVs look good; I'll be using one as a secondary screen for my protools rig but haven't hooked that part up yet. After adjusting my lighting to avoid glare and bright spots, the cameras work great, including in the complete darkness. They track motion very clearly with no issues, and there's no noticeable latency involved at all so the system works fine for watching the drummer for cues...etc. The only downside is the resolution of the cameras -- it's decent, but it's more like watching TV than HD video. There's some pixelation around the edges of objects, especially diagonal ones, and distant objects are a little fuzzy. In a few years I may upgrade if something better comes out that's still affordable, but for now it's working well and serving its purpose.
