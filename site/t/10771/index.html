TerryKullArchitect25 June 2008#2
Ben,, As you get ready to move forward with this, let me do a little research for you.. The issues are from my point of view as both an Architect and a Building Official (plans checker). What is the current 'use' of your space? is it your basic office / warehouse configuration? (from what I see, that would be my guess). And you will in effect be changing the 'occupancy' from storage use to probably business use (S to B). This will cause some ramifications as to the number of exits required, and the size and number of fixtures in your bathroom (s). You may be required to add restroom facilities. Is that figured into your T.I. budget? Things to consider:
    More toilet facilities Additonal HVAC Additional electrical, and the need for 'clean' electric circuits Exiting from the space, (may need 2, depending on number of people your new space can hold (per code) Disabled accessibility
Just to name a few... Not to scare you off, just better to be a "smart and informed client" Terry

gullfo26 June 2008#4
i might try something like this - small rooms on one side (or intermixed) with larger rooms. make them good ratios - use metal stud and 2x layer of drywall. maybe 10' high instead of 8', or mix and match. then treat at least one pair of walls with slats or porous absorption to break up flutter and add broadband treatment. have a separate hall. back to back solid exterior grade doors w/ magnetic seals. run the hvac duct and wiring over the top (per code of course) (this is a quick sketch and obviously needs a bit more playing around to get all the doors and such in the correct locations. depending on the doors, you might only need 1 per room or start out that way and add the second door as needed.)
