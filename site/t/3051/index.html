John Sayers20 January 2005#2
Lee - you just listed all the problems inherent in the typical US basement. the low ceiling height is the major problem. The raised floors you refer to are the floating floors that isolate each room but in your typical basement it's near impossible because of the limited ceiling height. Mind you IMO floated floors in a basement studio is overkill. Floated floors are more typically used to isolate one floor from the other - i.e. if you were to build on the second floor of an office block and didn't want your sound to get through to the other tenants. Floating floors also helps room to room isolation by stopping the flanking effect of the sound travelling through the floor from one room to another. In reality you rarely notice this effect and if you doo it's typically in the low frequencies only. why not look for a house with a good sized yard that you can build a new purpose built building for your studio rather than trying to get around all the problems associated with a basement design. I hope this helps cheers john
