marz18 May 2008#34
PreludetoJune, For the cavity walls, typically one needs to clean them out at the end of each day of bricklaying with water. This practise is often not followed in SA, but really should be done to avoid Water Capilliary action across the cement between the 2 leaves of the wall. I suspect that this will have some small impact along with the wire-ties and brick-force wire between the leaves, but the double brick wall on the inside is going to give you a lot of isolation if you can resolve the ceiling and roof with similar levels of isolation. For the concrete slab - this is typically how it is done. Though usually the slab will sit on the inner leaf. IE A single leaf brick wall should easily carry the concrete slab, as long as your foundations are up to it. 5m span is typical and should not be a problem. Note that this concrete slab will not be cheap and also needs to be engineered, though often the engineering can be done by the slab supplier / contractor in the price. regards Marz

gullfo2 June 2008#50
it would probably be useful if you could get some insulation in-between the brick walls. on the interior - decouple the drywall frame from the inner brick wall and use 3x 16mm drywall. this is a 3 leaf design but there's a couple of considerations - the drywall is less dense than the brick, the drywall with the insulation will act as a bass absorber, and sometimes you have to live with what you have. the other main consideration is your floors and ceilings - a lot of sound can get out from either of those places so plan on ensuring at least the same level of isolation for each.
