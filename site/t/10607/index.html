I think your main problem is those "beams" (rafters). They are way to light to carry anything serious. With a good foundation underneath your room and brickwalls it's a good option to place heavy beams ontop of the (inner) brickwall and place a descent roof on that. marz17 May 2008#24
PreludetoJune (May?) :wink: Typically Metal Sheet roofing (IBR) requires about half of the number of trusses as Tiled roofing. As a fellow South African and ex builder, normally SA tiles are Cement or Clay tiles and they are heavy. You will need to double the number of Trusses, along with battens approx every 30cm instead of the heavier purlins you have now, depending on size of the tiles. Perhaps as Ro suggests, you could even put a Concrete slab on top of your walls, though I could suggest a concrete slab (Tar sealed and options for extra rooms later!) on your outer walls and a timber inner ceiling on your inner walls. The tiles are going to be a weak leaf in the system, or potentially a 3rd leaf at the end of the day. I am pretty sure that you cannot put tiles on a roof with such a shallow angle, a Tiled roof needs a steeper pitch to aid water runoff, IE 30deg pitch or more. Again as Ro suggests, try to provide some diagram of the final roof system and you may save some time / pain and money. Where abouts are you in SA? Good luck Marz
