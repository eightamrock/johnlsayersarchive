rod gervais31 January 2010#5
Just use the span tables for ceiling/attic joist - you need to calculate the weight of the ceiling joists - plus the drywall = plus the green glue - PLUS lighting - a ceiling cloud and any duct work above the ceiling, which is BEING CARRIED BY THE CEILING - would be considered live loads - and would have to be included in the calculations. As long as all of your loads were well below the bottom - then you know that the smallest member listed would be more than adequate for your purposes. For design purposes L/360 would be fine. Rod
KevWind1 February 2010#6
If you are willing to go to the slightly added expense of using BCI's from Boise Cascade http://www.bc.com/wood/ewp/simpleFraming/bciJoist.html or TJI's from Truss Joist. Give them a simple scale drawing of the span and detail the materials to be used in sheathing and they will ( or used to) engineer the specs for free as part of the sale . The other advantage of these over conventional lumber is they all are within about a 1/16 of inch height of each other, with no crowing or twisting to speak of . Where regular framing 2X material is sometimes up to 1/2 inch variance as well as often crowned, bowed and twisted Also one person can easily set 20 ft joist by them selves. And last but not least. They can use more cut off wood in the lam. of these units so they don't require as much hart area of big trees ( More Green oriented.)

If you used the detail in figure 4.25a in my book you would only need 2x4's - if all you carry on the ceiling joist is drywall you should only loose a few inches (depending on your existing joist sizing - this assumes that your existing joist can handle the added load of new joist and drywall. I used this for a pro-home mixing studio (with a smallish iso-booth) for Diamond Godfrey - so he could take work home and not have to live his whole life in the studio. The bottom of the 2x4 joist in this case was exactly 1" below the bottom of the xisting floor joists. I can see no reason whatsoever why this would not work with inside out walls - i cannot see why you need an inside out ceiling - which would mean you loosing tremendous headroom - which would not be to your benefit. Rod
