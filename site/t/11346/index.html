xSpace wrote:
vakis wrote:
Can Monitors be soffit mounted in glass?????
It also shows that a three leaf can be used and no one dies.
Of course. The point with greater than 2 leafs is that the TL lower than what would be achieved with 2 and the amount of mass. It requires more material and proper design. Notice in the first image that one gap is ~twice as large as the other. This separates the MAM points by half an octave. In this specific case the isolation to the live room is from 2 sources, not just the control room. The first two leafs, going from live room out, are the isolation from the loudest source, the speakers. Andre

Soundman2020 wrote:
It looks to me like that is a three-leaf window! Why would they do that? It looks cool, but what about the acoustics?
I wrote about the three leaf design. I wrote why, two sources to isolate from. The two being the speakers and the control room itself. If are looking at doing this yourself, the design calculations for a triple leaf wall are in Long's Architectural Acousitcs and the originating document for the equations is Sharpe, WR 73-5R. you are getting the realm of stdio design where the design factors involved are practically unknown at the amateur studio design level. If Long and Sharpe are too technical for you to use, then a professional studio designer is a must, not an option. The calculations involved are too complex for the average studio builder. From reading your other posts, you are asking for help in designing a Formula 1 Ferrari while not familiar with Volkswagen design. Andre
