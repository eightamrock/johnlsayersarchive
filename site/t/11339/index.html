Soundman20201 November 2008#4
Hi Daiwa. I've been asking a similar question for a while, and it turns out that the best answer is simply: more concrete! Just lay more concrete directly on top of what you have already, and either finish the concrete neatly to be your new floor, or build your new wood floor directly on top of it, firmly attached. That said, however: Are you sure you actually need to beef it up? Have you tested to see how much transmission loss you are getting at present, with the existing concrete? For example, have you played loud music in your area, measured the level right there in your room, then measured the level in the room immediately underneath yours? You need to have those measurements in order to determine if / how much extra isolation you need. A thick concrete slab should be pretty good isolation already, so maybe you don't actually need anything else in the floor, and you just need to concentrate on the walls and ceiling. How thick is the slab at present? Also, have you checked with a structural engineer to see if the existing structure can actually handle the huge extra load you are going to place on it, with the new walls and ceiling, and maybe also with more concrete on the floor? Several layers of drywall all around and above is very heavy: You need to make sure that you are not going to overload the structure, especially if it turns out that you do need more concrete. But I'd say that your first priority is to get accurate measurements of the existing transmission, in order to decide if you need more isolation, and if so, how much more.

Soundman20205 December 2008#20
With a 10 inch+/- concrete floor, I do not know why you are even concerned with adding to it
Exactly. The math doesn't make any sense: Concrete weights about 2400 kg/m^3. Dry sand weighs about 1600 kg/m^3, or only about 67% as dense as concrete. So adding one inch of sand to 10 inches of concrete will increase the mass by less than 7%. To get a 6dB increase in isolation, you need to DOUBLE the mass. Increasing mass by 7% is not going to make any difference at all. Just a tiny fraction of one dB. I can't even be bothered to do the math on that, but you would not even be able to measure the difference. It makes no sense to lay an inch of sand on ten inches of concrete. To make a worthwhile difference, you'd need to lay FIFTEEN INCHES of sand. That would be over 600 kilograms of weight per square meter of floor space. I don't recall the dimensions of your floor spaces, but assuming just 10 x 10 m, if you DID put 15 inches of sand on it, you'd be adding around sixty thousand kilograms of weight to your floor. And all you would gain by that is maybe 6dB of isolation, theoretically. Like xSpace said: If you are concerned about sound transmission to the space below you, then do some real measurements.
