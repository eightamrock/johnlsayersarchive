knightfly17 July 2006#7
On drum risers, like floated floors, the thinner you make the top the more it will resonate. The less insulation you put in side, the more it will resonate. If you put a bottom on it, then the top, bottom, and your floor will create a 3-leaf construct which will wipe out any isolation value you may have gained by floating the riser in the first place. The bottom will also raise your resonant frequency, making it even MORE apparent in recordings. Maybe Giles liked the sound of a sealed riser because he likes boomy drums; I bet it would give somewhat of a "coliseum" sound to a kick drum, but what if you don't WANT that sound? :? Steve

Steve, Just a thought: If a riser is meant to 1) sit heigher, 2) decouple the drums from the ground. 3) Not to insulate airborn sound. I think a good manner should be to use top plates with holes or a perforated plate. I see this can also be unpractical (dust, holes for supports stands and chair). But isn't there a solution, one could figure out to solve or minimize this problem? Advantages: 1) Depending on the % holes one lowers MSM (or even prevent it to act as a panel on a spring) 2) A perforated panel can never sound as a drumskin (natural anti-noise). Question: what does do the Helmholtz effect? Note that industrial, the use of perforated panels to screen off dangerous things on machinery (e.g. belts) is a known practice in order to prevent such panels to radiate sound in themselves, normally caused by vibration picked up from the machinery by fixed mounting. Just a thought. Eric
