dk0126 August 2004#3
So much happens in the control room these days, that the barrier of the glass between artist and engineer and or producer is gone... heck, many people just do some modest isolation when even doing vocals and the vocalist is the control room... probably not the norm... but, every thing I read these days has the guitarist standing in front of the mains in the control room with the dial at 11 tracking to his rig in the live room, a bathroom or something or maybe its just amplitube'ed direct... Other instances to look up are articles with Daniel Lanois or Peter Gabriel, Real World, his main room there is really all about live playing and capturing the performance, screw isolation, do some, but get people to really feel each other, and with no separation with the engineer... I think the barriers need to come down - like every other barrier in this world... but lets not get too political.... Plus, its semi-complicated too build these glass partitions properly... I say use 2x sliding glass doors and have the option of opening up the space and minimizing the barrier... David
Aaronw26 August 2004#4
In most instances, the musicians prefer to see one another if they can. A lot of it has to do with visual ques, or just the vibe. As a lot of musicians tend to play off each other and need to see each other. However, as the cost of putting in camera's and tv monitors come down, it's getting more affordable to do this. Especially in a project studio or home studio where you don't always have the luxury of building from the ground up, and you have to deal w/ existing structure.
