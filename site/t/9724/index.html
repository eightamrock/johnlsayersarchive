Steve, Answer on your question here: (rather like to keep this together). http://www.soundonsound.com/forum/showf ... ber=546811 Louden: This is the only Copyright picture you'll find anywhere, rating them. Strictly theoretically as per the Louden approach: The perfect should should be: 0.00 The worst published Louden Ratio I pragmatically set to 1.00. Hence the lower the number the better. The best published Louden Ratio as per this scale = 0.4046 The best ratio I calculated over 3321 points = 0.3865. That are the darkest green areas. But in-between these points likely very small areas can go lower between 0.385 to 0.386 (too much work to investigate now and senseless). In fact these numbers improve to the center of these darkest green areas, but will likely nowhere go below this 0.385+ as minimum. What you can see that there is not such a thing as a perfect ratio. {0.00 equals best while scale starts at 0.38} Also note that the whole room ratio approach as per different Authors is still under discussion.

gullfo19 November 2007#12
Eric_Desart wrote:
This is the only Copyright picture you'll find anywhere, rating them.
i had it blown up and hung as a poster on my wall, er, next to my other poster of some of your other work, which is next to my Farah Fawcett poster. :twisted: for the walls? at least 6 degrees on each side and preferably more. in the sketch they're 12 degrees. however your constrained somewhat by the width of the room and achieving a good triangle between the monitors and the mix position. typically this would be an equilateral triangle on 60 degrees (or 30 degrees per leg). overhead, a cloud would help both in creating a proper reflective and absorption surface. this can be angled about 12-15 degrees. howver, you also need to consider the width of the soffit to ensure the baffle extends from the monitor correctly which likely means the soffit will have to be at least 4' wide. and so you would probably adjust things to pull the side walls out and away at a steeper angle in order to get the proper wide and still keep enough space for a window in front. another option - mount the monitors over the window but you'll still need to consider the soffit width, window below it to extend the baffle, and so on...
