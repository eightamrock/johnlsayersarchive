Soundman202011 July 2013#2
Hi Crushturtle. Please read the forum rules for posting (click here). You seem to be missing a couple of things! :)
Does the concrete slab need to be slightly above ground level?
Assuming you mean that you want to pour the slab on the ground, in a hole dug down a bit (with suitable footings), but also make it thick enough so it sticks up above ground level, then yes, I certainly would do that, given the option, for drainage and moisture issues mostly. But your local code might have something to say about that too.
Can the outer wall framing 2x4's be bolted direct to concrete slab?
It's not they they "can" be, but that the HAVE TO be bolted. All walls need to be attached to a firm base, either directly or through suitable isolation mounts. Especially where I live (!) but also everywhere else. Every now and then someone comes along arguing that it is perfectly fine to have walls just sitting on rubber pads, with no attachment to the sub-floor / foundations: it isn't. In my book, it is never fine to have a structure without proper support.
Is it possible to instead of creating a reinforced concrete slab to build a timber frame and suspend above ground,using steel feet concreted in the ground at various points to support the load.
I just answered another thread yesterday, where someone else was thinking along the same lines as you are, so I'll just post a link here to that thread (as I don't feel like re-typing it all! :) ) : viewtopic.php?f=2&t=17739&start=21
Then insulate , board etc as a floating floor?
If you float it correctly, yes. But that's not as easy as it sounds. This thread will give you the basis you need to figure out how to float your floor, if that's what you want to do: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 - Stuart -
