"so I will try to "float" the floor by using wooden pallets covered with thick carpeting, and maybe some material inside the empty space in-between. " Two bad ideas. The wooden pallets will not produce a floated floor and carpet especially thick is never a good idea since it tends to pull out mid to high frequency leaving you with a boomy sound. What kind of floor are you on, is it concrete ? The books can help to reduce the effect the tile walls are having and possibly add some diffusion. What about the window and doors? How are those constructed and why are you building a new ceiling that will be attached directly to the existing room?

gullfo6 June 2011#5
a damped membrane floor may be your best best - 1" rigid insulation, 2x 1/2" plywood (staggered seams, glue and screw to make a 1" layer), 3/8" flooring, small scatter carpet as needed. should help somewhat but your air borne sound into your walls will still transfer into your structure. on the ceiling - 4" cloth covered absorbers scattered to balance. the normal culprits for first reflections - side wall, ceiling, back wall, and a heavy drape for the window when needed.

Jabbaz16 June 2011#9
How much isolation do you need? Have a band play in the existing space and see how loud it is in areas of concern. Books have significant mass but a wall of books is not a continuous mass. You may have difficulty achieving significant sound isolation without framing and drywall. Is this not in the budget? The books I would use to build makeshift diffusers (http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bPL0or2x2ko). You wouldn't be able to create a perfect diffuser since books all vary in size, but you could approximate one with a little creativity.

BriHar20 June 2011#11
Regarding the use of books as a diffuser, granted we're not talking tuned systems here, in my previous studio a few large bookshelves were a given so I staggered the spines of the books on the shelves. This had a marked effect on the sound in the room as opposed to when the spines were all neatly aligned. Looks a bit odd at first but you get used to it, and when you explain to visitors why you've done this, they think you're a genius and often do the same at home. :wink:

xSpace4 July 2011#18
The ability to compare a bunch of books to a studied wall assembly of the same mass is going to be difficult to predict...the mass of the books have to be considered first. Then the lose of the spring in a mass/spring/mass type assembly changes it even more. Like said, the mass of the books will produce isolation, the gaps will produce leaks in isolation and since no mass/spring/mass really exists, you are hoping for the mass to produce isolation, which they will but to what end, we can only guess. This is what you have to work with, which we all understand as the limitations of the build for what ever reason it may be.
