sharward7 July 2005#10
Nestor Z. wrote:
I don't think that I need such extensive sound reduction since most of my work will be done on synths being heard thru monitors. I dont like to hear them toooo loud either. Moderate volume is fine for me.
Can you define this more clearly in scientific terms (i.e., decibels)? Your definition of "moderate" may not matter. Is it your intent to (1) keep your neighbor's happy, or (2) stay within legal limits, or (3) both? I ask because both standards vary greatly, and even interpretations of each vary depending on who's doing the suffering/measuring. Consider this: Suppose that whatever you decide to do is acceptable to your current neighbor. Then, your neighbor moves out, and someone else moves in. That new neighbor takes offense to the situation, even though you haven't changed anything. Was "old" neighbor simply more tolerant of noise that most reasonable people would have found objectionable? Or is "new" neighbor overly sensitive? In my own case, my city's noise ordinances have both a touchy-feely subjective standard and an science-based objective standard. Your city's noise ordinances may be quite similar, as my understanding is that this "double-edged sword" is a common model. Note that noise ordinaces for single-family residential (houses) often varies from multi-family residential (apartments, condominiums, townhouses -- basically anything with common walls or floors) -- typically the latter is more strict. You also may be prohibited from making any structural changes to the wall that you and your neighbor share without a lot of homeowners' associaton red tape. That may not be a concern to you right now, since it seems that you're averse to doing any major construction anyway... But just in case you end up exploring that path, know ahead of time that you're going to have those obstacles, in addition to any building permits your municipality will require. If you know the sound levels that you expect to generate, and you know the sound levels you aim to "allow" to escape, then the difference between those numbers (at all frequencies) is the target transmission loss (a.k.a. "TL") you're hoping to achieve. Then, and I believe only then, can you receive specific advice on what means will be necessary to achieve that. You will also need to be extremely detailed in the construction characteristics of your existing walls, even if you have no intention of changing them. That information will be necessary in order to estimate the current "soundproofworthiness" of the situation (which, I predict, will be quite insufficient).
