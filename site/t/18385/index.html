Soundman20203 June 2013#2
You seem to be using ETF for your measurements, but I'd suggest that you switch to REW. It's just a much better package all around, and is FREE! :) You can download it from the Home Theater Shack web site. I bought and used ETF for a few years, but since they never updated it again, I switched to REW. ETF does not show you everything that REW shows you, so it is quite possible that there are issues you just are not seeing in ETF.
After flushmounting i got a normal bass boost between 3-7db under 200hz. I tried different Eqs (Linearphase and nonlinearphase EQs) to lower the bass boost from the flushmounting with a shelf eq
It would have been better to use the built-in shelving filter on the speakers themselves. The circuitry is specifically designed for the exact baffle step response of that cabinet, so it makes more sense to use that than to try to get close with an external circuit. In general, I'm not a big fan of using EQ to "fix" room problems, because EQ cannot actually fix any room problems! After you download REW, I would suggest taking out the EQ from the signal path, and using only the built in controls on the speaker itself, then run a test like that. What mic are you using for the tests? There's also the issue with your geometry. For a room 7 meters long, you are way too close to the speaker. Your head should be about 2.5 m from the front wall, not 1.2m. You might very well be sitting in a bad location, acoustically. You also say that you don't think you have modal issues, yet the waterfall seems to be showing some rather marked modal issues.
The complete backwall has a 1m thick rockwool abdsorber installed.
OK, but what did you do to that to reduce high frequency absorption? And what about the rest of the treatment in the room? From the way you describe it, you might well have too much high frequency absorption in the room in general, and not enough diffusion / reflection. Please describe in detail how the rest of the room is treated, and also provide photos.
I´m not hoping about some acoustical solutions at the moment, i just would like to measure the wired bass problem.
They go together. It IS an acoustical problem, so it has to be viewed in terms of the entire room acoustic response, across the entire spectrum, not just a problem with the speakers and the bass. So my suggestion would be to remove everything from the signal path to your speakers such that it is as direct as possible: No EQ, compression, processing, bass management, or anything else: just a direct path from the DAW out to the speaker in, and with the speaker shelving set to -6 dB, the rest set flat. The measure with REW, using a suitable mic in the mix position at ear height. After you do that, measure the exact position of the mic in the room, in all 3 dimensions, so you can get the mic back to that precise location for all future measurements, accurate to within a few mm. This is very important, so that you can compare future measurements validly. If the mic is in a different position, even a few mm away, then you can no longer compare. Do three measurements with REW: Left speaker only, right speaker only, then both together. Then move the mic to a point about 2.5 m from the front wall, 1.2m above the floor, on the room center-line, and repeat the those three tests like that. Upload the MDAT data file to a sharing service (maybe Dropbox), then post the link here so we can download it and analyze it. Also post photos of the room, and a description of how it is treated. - Stuart -
