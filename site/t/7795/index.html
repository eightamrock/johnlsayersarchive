If you could build a studio from scratch. How big would you make your vocal/iso booth? Control room? Room designated for drums? And a main studio room?
From the SAE website - http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
So what size should a control room be? These days people are building bigger and bigger control rooms because so much happens in the control room now. Often the bass player and keyboard player actually sit in the control room. I often have the vocalist singing in the control room while the bed tracks go down, so a good size control room is a good idea. So many people think that you need a big studio and end up putting a poky little room at the end and call it a control room. I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m. This size room means a good sized working area with space for the musos, friends and hangers on. Also because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension. i.e. 6m x 5m. The studio, on the other hand, requires a different set of considerations. The first to consider is do you want just one room or more? The trend nowadays is for more than one studio so the you can get isolation between players and different acoustics for each room. The acoustics you want for a live drum sound is totally different than you want for a vocal for example. I recommend at least 2 rooms. You can put drums in one, guitars in the other, keys , vocals and bass (DI) in the control room or maybe the band in one and the vocalist in the other. Getting separation between guitars and drums is usually OK in a good sized room but keeping them out of the vocal track is hard if they are all in the same room.

One thing that can help you with your decisions on room sizes - knowing how you intend to work, and with whom/how many other bodies. Example - I mostly do "one-man-band" type projects these days, and so I find that a CR laid out like a "jet cockpit" works well for most things - meaning, I have everything I normally need in the course of laying out/finishing a project within reach of my (comfortable) chair, or at least within range of it's respective remote control - it's pretty tricky to achieve this and not muck up the acoustics, but with enough hair-pulling it can be done. Keeping racks, etc, at or below desk height is important for acoustic considerations. Depending on your needs, a larger CR and a vox booth may be all you need - or you may want (as John points out) additional iso rooms if you work with live bands and still want/need control over separate sounds. A typical setup for the "one man band" approach is to have the desk in normal location along with speakers, build low racks into each side under the desk's knee hole for often-used gear, then to one side of the desk a key stack (couple of midi keyboards kept lower than chest height while seated) - to the other side, an electric guitar and bass on floor stands, with floor processor in front, di'd into the board (or interface to computer) - further around (still in reach of your chair when rotated in place) an electronic drum kit (I use the Roland V-drums) and a boomed mic somewhere in this circle - I use such a setup for rough tracks, then replace tracks as necessary once the "feel" of the song(or in my case, usually video) is established. For music beds in industrial videos, I may never progress beyond electronic drums - otherwise I'll play maybe 8 or 12 bars of a pattern on the electronic kit, clean it up/quantize it if necessary and loop it to use as a "click track" - this works better for me because it gives the "feel" I want for that song much better than "tick tock tock tock tick tock tock tock" - then, when the rest of the song's pretty close to what I want I'll re-record live drums and ditch the electronic drum track. Guitar - you can set up remote amps in an acoustic space and still play from the CR if you want - if you're isolated enough, this allows you to hear what your setup will sound like "off tape", in context within the mix. Same with bass, either DI'd or thru a bass amp within the acoustic space. I hope this gives a general idea of one way to work - your chosen methods will dictate how you set up your own studio. Obviously if you're doing "for hire" work, you'll need more rooms/more options/larger spaces... Steve
