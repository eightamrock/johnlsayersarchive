RichardM2 January 2008#5
It may not necessarily be a big deal. Concrete shrinks as it cures, and most of the time a hairline crack with be due to that - they're actually called "shrinkage cracks." That's basically why concrete slabs on grade contain reinforcing steel or mesh - to distribute the stress caused by shrinkage and keep the cracks small. For a slab on grade (sitting on the ground) there's no real structural reason to have reinforcing - strictly to control this minor cracking. How big is this slab? Is it a slab on grade?

Richard, All of the above is true - BUT - shrinkage cracks only occur during the shrinking stage of the curing process - and that is generally within the first day or 2. A crack that is an extreem hairline might not be noticable until dirt deposits build up in the crack itself. It is physically impossible to have a shrinkage crack turn up 5 months after a pour (that's 150 days +/-) and concrete reaches 100% strength in 54 days (+/-). If a crack has appeared this late in the game it would suggest to me movement in the concrete under loading. Thus a settlement crack as opposed to a shrinkage crack. BUT - depending on load- - that may not be a reallu huge problem - and if you really want it fixed and stucturally sound - you can always get a "structural epoxy crack repair" performed - it's costly - but the repair is stronger than the surrounding concrete that it's repairing. So they are not prone to failure. Rod
