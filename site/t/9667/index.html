nickfranklin wrote:
Anyways, my mate and I have begun leasing a commercial space, and we're building a studio over the next couple of months. Before we start, I have a couple of niggling questions...
Nick, believe me when I tell you this - a 1/2 way decent studio could take a couple of months for a professional studio architect to design and detail - and seeing as you and your mate don't even have the most basic understanding of the construction - and shouldn't begin building without a design - I think the 2 of you should take a deep breath along with a reality check. Unless you want to throw your money away building something that no one is going to enjoy recording in - and still be stuck paying for your rental agreement without any income - I suggest you spend some serious time reading and learning - I say this because the first questions you asked are more basic than we are used to encountering here.. From where you are right now - you could spend the next 6 months just figuring things out unless you hire a pro to do your design for you - and even then you would probably be 4 months from starting (never mind building it in the next couple months) by time you line everything up. Good luck, Rod
BradJacob31 October 2007#4
Rod is right. I'm a carpenter with plenty of building experience. But once you start thinking of building (even a VERY simple) studio, it takes a lot of time invested in researching, reading, planning, seeking pro opinions, asking questions, and repeating these steps about hundred times. Rod has put together a fantastic book that is a MUST have. Suggest you pick it up. This is not a sales push for Rod, but getting this book will save you time, money & aggravation. I've been planning (and I stress "planning") my studio for close to 10 years - as it all started with a drawing on a napkin. Then I joined this forum 3 years ago and have been reading. I thought I knew what I was doing - with my construction experience and all. Then it came time to start. I wanted a VERY SIMPLE (almost "rule-breaking") design. This quickly was abandoned in favor of doing it the right way. My budget tripled, I had to take apart things, hire people and wait until I got more money. BUT - I was not about to waste my time and complete something only to find out that sound leaks from every possible place imaginable. And forget sound leakage, you NEED to be concerned with codes. They're there to protect you against fire, structural failure, etc... So there's alot to think about in beginning a project - especially a project this "specialized". I would start by reading those basic FAQ's that Steve ("Knightfly") put together, and buy Rod's book. Then I would start reading through EVERY "build diary" you can find on this site. I can't begin to tell you how much I've learned from reading these. Hope this helps - and the best of luck.
