jwl29 June 2007#2
HI Dingo You're talking about 4 different designs there. I'd not bother with the first one. Other designs will be more effective overall, especially in small rooms. This design is most useful for treating specific problem frequencies once you have most of the room under control. The second design is probably the most cost effective, provided your absorption is at least 4" thick. The third design is quite good as well; it will absorb a narrower band of frequencies, but the frequencies in which it works will see more absorption than the second design. If you design them carefully and do the math so you know which frequencies they work at, they can be extremely effective. THey are also reflective at high frequencies, which can be good, again if you plan the shapes carefully. The fourth design isn't really a hybrid, in that it's not a sealed chamber, so it's not really a slot resonator. It's basically option 2 with some boards in front, so it will reduce the absorption and add some reflection. Though it looks nice, I'm not sure I see the value of that design over option 2 or 3. A fifth design I would consider is superchunks. I have played around with a hybrid design, basically superchunks in the wall/ceiling and wall/floor corners, with a (probably angled) slot resonator in the middle of the wall. Lots of possibilities..... JWL

jwl2 July 2007#4
I've not done a side-by-side comparison of Realtraps vs. Superchunks. For that matter, I've never actually seen a Realtrap in the flesh (or in the membrane, as it were....) The superchunks design puts more mass of absorption in the corners, over the 4"-straddling-the-corner design. This means it will absorb more, and to a lower frequency. Not a huge difference, but a difference.
gullfo3 July 2007#5
if your room has well distributed modes and is balanced between live and absoptive surfaces, you might not hear a significant difference. otoh, if you have low frequency mode problems, the superchunks may likely help more, and if you have low-mid problems, the realtraps may likely work nicely. slats also work across a variety of problem areas as well as add some diffusiveness and a nice appearance. slats can also shape the space to reduce flutter echos and reflections without making things too dead (something purely absorptive surfaces cannot do easily). best bet is to make some measurements of the space(s) before beginning to decide what treatments will work...
