Nice mass in the floor. Nice decoupling in the ceiling below. Given the neighbor concern, the isolation in walls and ceiling will be the issue. You have roof vents, soffit vents, etc I assume. xSpace30 July 2009#5
The issue with the upstairs and the proposed floor is that it develops a drum head with the method you are considering. Even if you can install the lightweight concrete, the sleepers alone will raise the floor boundary creating air cavities that will resonate, and a potential three leaf depending on if you hang a ceiling below or IF there is one existing already. Whatever mass you can give to the floor I wouldn't add air gaps. I would also have some degree of certainty that whatever load was about to be placed on this upper floor, the structure below could support it. Good luck

QUOTE: "I do planning on hanging a ceiling below the second floor (garage ceiling) - there is not one now. I was going to hang with two layers of drywall with green glue between. It sounds like you are saying not to do this because of creating a 3 leaf system. Is this correct?" If there's no drywall ceiling in garage now, then no triple leaf, assuming you do not raise the floor of the room over the attic (thereby creating a new air cavity). If you could decouple the ceiling drywall ( the "hanging ceiling" you mentioned) from those original joists, you have gone a long way to lowering the LF resonance point of that floor ceiling assembly. Further lower the resonance point by adding moderate insulation and mass (double drywall).

jpricekc wrote:
I am adding another stud wall (2x4) to the garage walls to support the upper floor.
After looking at your pdf I think what you really require is more time and better information for your design. Your current plans show a staggered stud wall that is coupled together by the roof basically shooting yourself in the foot as far as isolation is going. Your window detail is coupling interior exterior walls as well, so no matter who's window you may purchase the isolation quality of the environment is being reduced further. Your plan is showing Wood I-Beams, this is not a place you want to just build a wall and stick it under the joists if the wall is to come into contact with the wood I beam. One more point of interest is the slab. If you are doing this as a new build then you want to incorporate sand into this slab not crushed rock. It's these kinds of things that can be the difference between building a quality recording environment and just building. It seems to be a fair start for a plan but the detail required to get your monies worth has been omitted and will hurt you if you move forward without better attention to these points.
