Soundman202026 April 2010#32
Shall I build with a inside/out structure instead (basically the other way around)? : - brick wall - air gap (10 cm) - 16mm plasterboard (with possibly another MDF layer of 12mm glued to it) - stud frame with insulation in the cavity - cloth stretched on top to cover insulation
Well, that sounds like you are forgetting to put insulation in the air gap, between the two leaves. that would cost you several dB of isolation. And you may or may not need the insulation between the studs, which would be exposed to the room, depending on what kind of treatment your room is going to need. Perhaps you are confusing the use of insulation as a damper in the MSM system, and insulation as acoustic treatment inside the room?
Is there any advantage of one of the two compared to the other?
If done properly, inside-out construction buys you a couple of inches of extra space inside your room, which is very useful for small rooms. Every inch counts. - Stuart -

Soundman20207 May 2010#35
1) Can I use resilient bars in a inside-out wall ?
Why would you want to do that? If you are building an inside-out wall, then by definition it is going to be decoupled. so why do you want to use RC as well, since the real purpose of RC is to decouple? Decoupling twice won't gain you anything: it will just waste time and money. Unless you have a really good reason for doing that, I'd just attache the drywall direct to the stud frame, on the floor, then lift the wall into position. Soundman20207 May 2010#37
I'd go with "Option C", for sure. But there are two problems on your diagram: 1) The drywall ("plasterboard") should not stop where the frame starts! It should go down all the way to the floor. So should the mineral wool. 2) If you can afford it, filling the entire 100mm "gap" with mineral wool will help your isolation a bit. Your diagram shows only 50mm in there, but 100mm would be better, if your budget will allow it. Just make sure that you don't stuff it in tightly! It should be just enough to fill the gap, but not compressed at all. Also, your diagram says "acoustic mineral wool". You probably don't need to buy anything special for that! Plain old ordinary mineral wool will work fine, as long as the density is somewhere around 48 kg/m3. And don't forget to seal the brick wall before you build the inner leaf! A good coat of paint should do the trick. Brick and mortar are porous, and need to be sealed. - Stuart -
