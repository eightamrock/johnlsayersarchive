dymaxian24 May 2004#3
It can be done. Especially if you're not too worried about isolation. Try to set your speakers up to fire down the long axis of the room, and make the back wall as absorptive as you can. If you need to record really loud electric guitar, you can stuff it in the closet. No matter how bad, almost any room can be made useable. And no matter how good, any room will require you to spend some time learning how it sounds before you can really mix in it confidently. Until you get to that point, check your mixes on as many different stereo systems as you can. Hope this helps!

dymaxian25 May 2004#5
The window on the front wall won't hurt your sound too much- in my opinion it's best to have hard, reflective stuff like that in front of you (since high freq's won't be coming out of the back of your speakers to reflect off of it so much) as opposed to having it behind you or on one side... The door behind you won't be too bad- if it is, cover it with rigid fiberglass panels. Try to make your front wall absorptive to the low-freq stuff- panel traps or rigid fiberglass spaced away from the wall a few inches. The electronic drums will still be a source of noise for anyone below you- if this is a problem, you may need to build a platform or something under the pedals. It's basically just the thumping of your foot on the ground. If you stood in the room and stomped repeatedly, without the drums there at all, you'd have the same effect. If that wouldn't bother anyone, you're all good. As far as bed placement, try to put it behind your mixing position, and don't expect it to soak up too much sound. It might help you out a little, but I'd largely ignore it. If you find your room is too dead because of it's presence, you can liven up the front end of the room a little, but try to keep the back wall in particular (back as in you face away when you mix) as absorptive as you can, across as much of the sound spectrum as you can. Bookshelves can make good diffusors, but dressers really won't do much for you. Try to put them behind you.
