delusion8 September 2007#1
I'm in a situation where I have a 25x11 garage available to me to turn into a recording studio. The garage is detached from the house and was probably built in the30's or 40's but was rebuilt/remodeled as a video edit room about 5 years ago. The guy who did the rebuilding was a hack to say the least. Air tight, plumb and level were not in this guys vocabulary. So needles to say it leaves some things to be desired, but it has good electric, existing cables, and it's in a good location in Hollywood. Isolation wise, much improvement could be made. The structure is almost “good enough” for normal control room volumes and most instruments other then acoustic drums. However, drums are loud outside and need to be contained to not run into problems with the neighbors 20' away. To save money, the leading plan so far is to divide the room up and make a 9x11 drum iso room. The plan was to to do the mass-air-mass 2 independent wall 2 leaf system just for the 9x11 “live room”. Well, at least that was the plan until I ripped into the wall and found out that there is NO external wall other then the siding! The Wall Sandwich break down: -Exterior fiber cement siding (poorly installed AKA-not air tight-caulked only in spots) -Black tar paper (No actual exterior wall!!) -92mm Steel studs (with original wood studs AND the new steel studs quasi-staggered pattern it appears. For example it looks like the tarpaper/siding is in contact with both types of studs, while the interior drywall is only in contact with the wider 92mm steel stud. I hope that makes sense!) -4” Johns Manville “Thermal and Sound Control” (with paper backing “mostly” toward interior wall) -Steel mesh fence (P.I.T.A!!!) -2 layers of 5/8” Drywall The Ceiling on the interior is vaulted (81”' to 104”) and looks to consist of: -Exterior shingled roof -4” Johns Manville “Thermal and Sound Control” (with paper backing toward interior drywall) -1 layer 5/8” Drywall The Floor: -Laminate over concrete slab Due to the difficult shape of the ceiling and the steel mesh in the internal walls, I want to avoid ripping out the existing 2 layers of drywall if at all possible….or at least make it the last resort. Thus the Options: 1. Rip off the siding and put “beef” on the exterior of the steel studs where there is ONLY black tar paper now. Leaving the interior wall as is. 2. Build a “room within a room” on the inside and make the new wall air tight and lined with mineral wool. This would create the dreaded third leaf effect unfortunately. The question is would the tar paper and poorly installed siding be so leaky and wimpy as to not be TOO much of a problem as a third leaf? 3. Suck it up and realize that it's going to be more trouble then it's worth and save up for a long time to build a new free standing isolated drum room from the ground up. Please tell me if more information is needed, and I’ll do everything I can to get that info. Thanks!

It is already a 3rd leaf........... and it will act in that manner - the amount of air leaks you are talking abuout are minimal compared to the overall - But - in your case I think this is one of those times where I would not remove the inner face - as a matter of fact I would add to it - and then build my new wall in front of it.
