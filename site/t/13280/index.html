Ro16 July 2009#2
If you've got a "floating" floor, then it should indeed not tough your walls preferably. Caulk it. You need caulking (around the perimeters) anyway, so a mixture of both ceilingplans will do. It can "tough" the wall since walls and ceiling are one construction (no decoupling, as per your drawings)
tiltrite16 July 2009#3
Thank you! Unfortunately the room is all pre-existing traditional house construction; nothing floating. Nothing decoupled. The floor touches the walls which touch the cieling. I'm just adding mass to the inner leaves of this existing situation, and making it as airtight as possible. Cieling: so I will let the added sheetrock on walls and ceiling touch; then caulk the seams as usual. Floor: would it make sense to have a gap around the newly added plywood floor and caulk it? It's not floating but would there be an advantage to doing that? There will be loud/vibrating guitar amps on the floor so maybe there is an upside to doing that. Or maybe because the floor is not floating it won't make a difference if the floor and the walls meet without a gap: Basically the structure is still all coupled but just more massive.
