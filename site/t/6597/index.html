knightfly8 July 2006#2
One reason for staining individual pieces is the prevention of warping after it's installed; take a piece of cardboard (like the back of a writing tablet), lay it on top of a water glass, and spray the upper side (lightly) with any liquid (preferably water though) - notice that the edges immediately droop? This isn't just that the cardboard is wet and floppy, it's because one side is damp and expands, causing the piece to warp. The same happens with applying a finish; if you only apply on one side, there is a tendency for that side to become convex due to swelling. OTOH, if you intend to somehow hide any screws or nails, prefinishing can make this harder, unless this is ONLY cosmetic trim; in which case, (since not much strength would be required) colored finish nails can be bought that match almost any finish; these can be nailset and a small dab of wood-specific plasticised filler applied (the color of the nails isn't important when doing it this way, of course) If you ever intend to remove the glass to facilitate cleaning, then screws make more sense; I think that before I could give a very useful answer on this though, that I'd need to see a sketch of what you intend to do for trim, and maybe a pic of what's already there... Steve

sharward10 July 2006#10
No, you can't. I have a 3.5 ton air conditioner in my house. The air handler (and furnace) in that attic sucks exhaust air from the hallway, blows it over cold coils, and then pushes it back into the house through the vents. The outdoor unit's job is to keep the coils in the air handler cold. It dissipates heat and keeps the refrigerant flowing. That's my very basic and probably very flawed layman's attempt to describe a typical air conditioner. More than likely, the way you get "fresh" air into your house is to open windows. Since studios typically don't have windows to the outside world, one must use mechanical ventilation to supply the room(s) with fresh air. Some air conditioning systems do have fresh air provisions, but from the description you gave us, I doubt yours has one. --Keith :mrgreen:

len-morgan10 July 2006#12
The air they are talking about is CIRCULATING, not exhanging. If you don't KNOW it has a fresh air supply, it more than likely doesn't. It's very rare in a normal installation. What I suggest you do is to use an ERV (or HRV depending on where you live) which will exaust the bad air but heat or cool the incoming fresh air on the way out. Some of these are 90+% efficient so you won't be throwing out cold (expensive) air. You'll cool the fresh air first so the energy you used to cool it in the first place isn't wasted. Kieth (aka Sharward) is using a FanTech model for his ventalation system so you might look through his thread for more info. len
