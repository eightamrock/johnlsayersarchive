knightfly31 March 2006#5
Andre's right about angles; in less than optimum space, it's not practical to splay more than 6 degrees each side; with that, you'll not have to worry about flutter between sidewalls so you can use less absorption than would be necessary with straight parallel walls. You'd still need to create your (sorta) RFZ with patches of absorption, same as with a parallel pair of walls. You'd also still need to put at least a cloud, and sidewall first reflection points, and either a fixed or movable deep rear trap for a tight mix position. 1 foot splay for every 10 feet of wall length is the simplest way to get your 6 degrees per side. If you already feel you're cramped for space, you can use Andre's suggestion of "sawtooth" walls - you'd need to do at least 4 foot long sections or it will tend to "average out" and won't do as good a job on flutter. IT's also a PITA to build, and if you build the walls normal and add the "teeth" later you will get some 3-leaf effects on your isolation - Also, the splayed wall thing will leave slightly more width between walls at the rear of the room where you're likely to have more people - it's a tough choice when space is limited, but I would do the splay if it were my space; I don't like deadening an entire room to fix things that can be fixed while still leaving options (like degree of liveness) - remember, you'll also have parallel floor/ceiling to treat so that'll be more absorption as well. In your case, you also need to find a way to kill that boomy stuff from your floor - that will also color the sound of the room. If you don't have something like ETF, there is a freebie that can help you figure out what's going on in your room as is; http://homepage.ntlworld.com/john.mulcahy/roomeq/ One thing I'd do is to "shoot" your room as is, check out all the lower frequency peaks, then drill about a 5/8" hole in one of the boomier places and force as much of this (the 29 oz tubes, not the squeeze tube) http://www.quikrete.com/catalog/GrayConcreteRepair.html into the hole (don't stop til it squeezes back out the hole, then strike it off with a trowel or putty knife) You should first tap the spot with a rubber or plastic hammer and see how loud it is; then re-check AFTER the hole is filled and treatment has cured. Then re-shoot the room and see what's happened to the peaks; most will still be there, because they're room modes - you'll probably only see one peak that changes before and after. I've not used this stuff nor have I had to do the type of repair you need, that's why I'm telling you what I would TRY, and only one spot til you find out if it helps. You might try PM'ing Rod Gervais to see if he's got any better ideas... Steve

AVare27 April 2006#14
Okay here goes:
1. What’s the best degree of splay for side walls?
There is no genereic "best angle." Walls splayed to prevent flutter echo t 6 degrees minimum. That is only universal accurate constant. Angles for creating RFZs are determined by ray tracing, on a specific room by room basis. The numbers you see for these angles are "typical averages." Think of the "typical human" with one ovary and one testicle.
2. In a varied depth broad band slot resonator what’s the minimum depth the deepest section should have?
There is no minimum depth, other thatn 0. The depth is determined by the desired absorption frequency range.
3. What effect does having a varied depth slot resonator on only one side wall OR having 2 different size slot resonators or absorbers on the 2 side walls have on the acoustic symmetry of the room?
It reduces it. How much depends on how the different the two sides are, the frequencies involved etc.
5. How far back in the room should the splayed walls go?
As in one, it depends ON THE PURPOSE of the splays. If flutter echo reduction then it is reasonable to splay yhe entire wall. IF for RFZ creation, then as determinde by ray tracing.
Summarizing what my delima is about : whether to use splayed slot resonator walls (and how even or unevely sized each side wall could or should not be) OR just splay the walls and use DIY panel absorbers.
With the room size you have, and not seeing the detailed plans (if you are good), or chicken scratches (if you are like me), I would: splay the side walls between the monitors and the listening position; put lf absorbers behind the listening postion and below the monitors; after making initial room performance calculations for required absorption. You did not mention it, but to make it explicit, with a room that size, diffusion is not appropriate.
