First I think having a square room is very bad from an acoustic perspective, I'd rethink the shape, 13x13 sounds very bad (not to mention unlucky :twisted: )
The rub is that there is a roll up garage door that will bring approximately 5 ft of the ceiling height down to 7 ft.
Does this mean that you'll be building into the space under the garage door when it's opened? If that's the case then what use is the garage door to you? Just keep it closed and build right up to it, losing no headroom at all (low ceilings are a pita)
Dan Fitzpatrick7 July 2005#3
cajonezzz, this is a similar situation to the one i am in. it sounds like you are trying to retain function of the garage door, leaving about a 5 ft corridor alongside your studio. is that right? one thing to consider, since you are going "under" the rails, is the thickness not only of the ceiling beneath the garage door rails but also the thickness of any interior ceiling absorption (for acoustical purposes) you'll be using. that can take away several inches. hopefully your garage door rails are high enough that your headroom won't be a problem. although i guess if your desk faces the garage door, then headroom on that side won't be as much of an issue. if you are interested, you can check out my much messier garage pix elsewhere on the forum. i'm going for iso though, since i want to drum in there. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3876 in my case (just to compare notes, no suggestion implied), i've decided to cut the rails and block (read: virtually destroy) the roll up door. but i've got a side door to the outside that i can access the garage thru. my wife wasn't too excited about that but i cried, so she gave in. :twisted: er, i mean, i angrily demanded my "man space." (new baby coming as well!!) good luck, and congrats on the new kid :) dan
