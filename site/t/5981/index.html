sharward12 April 2006#8
Actually, there's a footnote on the "metal is better" situation. Metal is better (isolationwise) than wood in single frame assemblies without resilient channel. But metal and wood are in a horserace (i.e., very close competitively speaking) when frames are separate. The reason for this is that light guage steel studs flex and have less of a transfer path from leaf to leaf when both leaves are on the same studs. When the leaves are on different studs, it doesn't matter. Steve has posted numerous times that wood has an advantage terms of water tolerance. (Removed erroneous statement about corrosion) Steel has the advantage in terms of straightness, (perhaps) ease of installation, and wood destroying pest infestations. It's not a cut-and-dried "steel framing is always better than wood framing" situation. I chose to use wood framing in my project because my house is already framed with wood. I didn't want to mix and match my framing methodologies... :roll: The answer is, as always, "it depends." --Keith :mrgreen:
