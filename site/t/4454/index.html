Paul3 October 2005#2
shady area. as far as i know a room can be as small as you like. Problem is, the larger the surface area of the wall and the bigger the size, the less chance of getting unwanted bounce back of frequencies and more chances these are absorbed or dissapate. Technically speaking if someone can get in the room with a guitar then it's big enough, but acoustic dampening smaller spaces is a lot harder than large ones. I see people put loads of old CD's on the wall randomly and LP's on the cieling in smaller rooms like vocal booths as the last layer of a good multi layered construction, that's cool and it's supposed to work well. The BBC guidelines however say they need v.large rooms, i'm not sure of the exact reasons but probably because of audio quality and work space. Here are some recommended room sizes i found.
