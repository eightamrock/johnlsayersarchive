Hokuto24 October 2008#35
In case anyone was wondering or going to ask, these panels should be ok to remove/move around (rented house). I have used small nails to attach panels to walls. These nails shouldn't leave much of a sign and if holes are too visible I can easily fill them or use a quick coat of paint. I didn't use any glue or stuff like that. For the bass traps I have used some Velcro Strap as well to help the small nails. Actually the two back-wall bass traps and the bottom ones on the front wall are not attached to the wall at all, they are touching the walls but simply standing over other stuff. Panels on ceiling I have used Velcro Straps but I am not sure they will still be there tomorrow morning... If the drop down I'll do what I had to do with the Front top Right Bass Trap; simply add some strong glue (e.g. loctite) on the sticky side of the velcro strap (glue side) then attach to foam. The stronger Loctitie glue did the trick, the glue at the back of the Velcro Strap was not sticking well enough to the Foam. I suspect I will have to add Loctite glue to the straps for the panels on the ceiling Anyway guys, suggestions, answers to question included with photos all welcome. Using all these panels did get rid of reverb/resonance that was initially present (clapping hands). I also had to get rid of the glass-doors that where in front of the larger cabinets (back corners), the glass was vibrating and emitting a glass/metal sound so they had to go :) Thank you for your time and help guys (Update): yep, two of the panels from the ceiling went down :). Velcro not enough... so I added some glue and I HAD to use some small nail as well. There are a few nails per panel so hopefully it will last longer (more than 2 hours :)) this time around.

All the standard things apply - as much corner bass trapping as possible, plus absorption at the side-wall and ceiling reflections points. What your speakers sit on matters much less. But...

tomislav26 October 2008#39
hi, i have the same problem with my room being a square. the biggest problem with that is that on some frequency (for me 47, 94, 141 Hz) we have huge problems. For me it ment 40 dB off difference between 47 and 56 Hz. It sounded soooo bad. After lots off reading i came to conclusion that normal products wont work for me and that i need to build really big basstraps, and i did build two for now, and that difference between 47 and 56 is now still 20 dB. Still long way till happiness :) ...so my point is, i am afraid that your current auralex(?) basstraps and tiles wont have much effect on controlling the low end. You will most probably have to build some yourself. I am not expert on this but i would just like to give you some off my experience dealing with my own room. Best regards and good luck, Tomislav
