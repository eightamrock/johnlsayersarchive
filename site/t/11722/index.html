Sprayed fireproofing as additional wall mass?

Started by Cold on 23 February 2009. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 11722.

Cold23 February 2009#1
Hi, Has anyone had any experience using spray applied fireproofing as additional mass in a wall system? I spray fireproofing as my day job and have never seen any official numbers as to how effective this would be as part of a m-a-m system. Sound transfer "seems" to drop after spraying the walls of a building and the fireproofing we spray does have high mass and density. But this is absolutely unscientific and untested. The specific product I am referring to is Cafco Blazeshield II link: http://www.isolatek.com/product_search.asp?productID=51 This product is much denser and heavier than blow-in wall insulation such as cellulose or fiberglass. Hypothetically: 1st Leaf- Garage with stud walls,sheathing,exterior finish. Sealed with caulking and sprayed with 2-3" of fireproofing between the studs from the inside. -air spring stuffed with fiberglass- 2nd Leaf- Double drywall stud wall (room within room construction) also sprayed with 2-3" of fireproofing. Any thoughts on this? If no testing has been done with fireproofing would it be worth doing some testing? I could do some basic testing (have a test mic, software, and an spl meter as well) if there is interest or opinion this may be worth pursuing. If I go ahead with testing,I would be open to suggestions as to how I set it up for the most usable results. Caveat: I would not test with a big structure. The test structure would have to be done as panels, sprayed on a job site, brought home in the back of my truck, assembled at home, tested. Open to all comments or suggestions. Thanks, Steve

rod gervais23 February 2009#4
I am very familiar with spray fireproofing products - work on projects that utilize them quite often. My only concern would be on relating to long term bonding between the fireproofing and the sub-strate it is applied to. These products are designed to be applied to either raw structural steel or to galvanized steel decking - not to drywall or plywood. I would feel pretty confident stating that there have never been any long term studies done (as regards bonding to materials on other than steel) - and would not feel anywhere near confident as to taking the experiment on myself. Sincerely, Rod

Testing this yourself would be less than useless..... and I am not trying to be either funny nor insulting. Testing labs have special rooms set up - with special openings - in order to perform testing of this nature. They know the limitations of their existing assemblies - know exactly when flanking begins to affect the readings........ at which point they have gone as far as they can. You obviously are not a testing lab. If you really want to know - then make up a sample and pay to have it tested at a real lab - otherwise don't waste your time..... the results really will be meaningless. Rod
