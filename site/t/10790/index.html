How does 2nd layer of walls/ceiling not fall over?

Started by Lunatique on 30 June 2008. 9 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 10790.

Lunatique30 June 2008#1
One question I have when I read about adding an isolating layer of wall and ceiling to a studio is how to fasten them securely to the original walls/ceiling without compromising the isolation? I understand that for the floor, you use neoprene pads to help decouple the new floor from the original floor, but you can't really do that with walls/ceiling since gravity isn't working in their favor like it does for the floor. Walls - I read over and over that you must not let the new leaf of wall touch the original wall in any way. So my question is, if it isn't secured to something, what's to stop the new leaf of walls from just falling over? Them attached to each other at the corner is enough security? I understand that the bottom of the frames for the 2nd leaf walls will be resting on some kind of damping pad, like sponge rubber or neoprene, so I understand it is decoupled that way. Ceiling - It may be hard for me to find any kind of isolating hanging accessories in the city I live, so how else can I decouple my isolating ceiling from the original ceiling. Can I put wooden beams on the walls and then rest joists for the isolating ceiling on them with neoprene pad as cushion? Would that be enough to decouple the isolating ceiling?

People would look...they would find. REFERENCE AREA - Useful threads/links http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2125 These kinds of questions have simple answers. The simple answer requires the builder to have some knowledge of construction. After that, it becomes a long answer, trying to speak in terms that may seem foreign to someone with no knowledge or limited knowledge of the terms, procedure, etc. But I'll give it a shot. The isolation does not exist with one wall. It takes the entire project to begin to get an idea of where your isolation is at. With a ceiling, a typical procedure is to use RC. For example, this material is installed perpendicular to the existing joists using screws. Then the sheet-rock is attached to the RC. It's a lot of little things that eventually turn into a big thing:) As for a framed wall, it is what it is. The rock or what-have-you is installed directly to the studs, most often. Not always, as some cases may require RC to be used here too.
