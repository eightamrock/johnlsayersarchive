SonicClang30 August 2006#4
There are different types of solid core doors. The main three are solid wood, partical wood, and foam. Solid wood, like if it's a solid oak door, is very expensive. Not necessarily a whole lot better, but a lot more expensive. Partical wood is a much more economical choice. I've seen a few different styles of fill, but the ones I just bought for my studio are filled with a sort of chunky looking saw dust. They're very solid and stop quite a bit of sound. I bought them as solid pieces, not pre-hung and no hole for a door knob (very important in a studio) and they were only $47 a piece at Home Depot. I already have a router and bit for the hinges, so I walked away not spending very much at all. Foam core is more for insulation than sound isolation. Of course 99% of construction guys and about 99.9% of the guys at the stores will tell you the foam is for sound proofing... but we all know how much most people know about sound and foam. :) Those are more for exterior installations, but I'm sure you could find plenty of interior doors with foam on the inside.

len-morgan31 August 2006#10
Just off the top of my head, I don't know that I've ever seen pre-hung (which it sounds like you got) solid core doors. If I were a bettin' man, I'm thinking they just screwed up and didn't give you what you ordered (and paid for). On the other hand, you could do what I did on 3 of my 6 doors and that's make a "Knightfly sandwich" door. You can search for "doors" and find Steve's post about it. Basically, you take a hollow core door and glue a layer of MDF (I used 3/4" but 1" would probably be better) on each side. You can only screw around the perimeter (because there's nothing to hold the screws in the middle and you don't want to "short out" the isolation by screwing from one MDF sheet into the other. According to Steve's calculations, you even end up with a slightly better door in the process. It's VERY important to note that this won't work if you have double doors (like I have going from my main room to my vocal booth) because you end up with a quad leaf system. As a last point, I paid about $100 for my solid core doors (and I know they're solid because I had to cut them to size so I saw the innards), and the same size hollow core door panels were going for about $25 at Lowes and HD so for what you paid, you should have gotten the real thing. len
rod gervais31 August 2006#11
len-morgan wrote:
It's VERY important to note that this won't work if you have double doors (like I have going from my main room to my vocal booth) because you end up with a quad leaf system.
Len, this is a mistake....... the manner in which hollow core doors a manufactured - (i.e.: with a cardboard honeycomb interior that's bonded to both surfaces) does not create a 2 leaf assembly - and thus 2 doors can't create a 4 leaf system. The honeycomb is too small to allow the door leafs to act as single units. Sincerely, Rod

Not only that, but in certain existing situations where asbestos has been, it is sometimes not recommended to remove it... For example, there has been this recent squak about my neighborhood's elementary school having asbestos flooring tiles in a few areas... But it seems if asbestos tiles are solid and attached, it would be more of a hazard to remove them if there is no other need to (i.e., if there is no remodelling going on, etc.)... Removing them could possibly cause the asbestos to become friable, which WOULD then create a hazard. On the other hand, if the asbestos tiles come off the floor in one piece, there is really not much problem with them (but getting the mastic off the floor -- which can also contain asbestos -- may be more of a problem).
