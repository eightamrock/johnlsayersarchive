AVare20 August 2010#7
binarybloke wrote:
Yes, a separate gorund is a no no.
Andre, could you briefly explain why this is a bad idea?
Grounding, as in the connection to the earth, is for lightning safety reasons. Having multiple grounds creates potentially multiple levels. Also these multiple levels are sources of hum from ground-loops. It is a common misconception from the confusion of bonding and grounding that problems start.. As a case of the lack of need for grounding and clean sound, how much hum do you get in your car's sound system, and how large is the car's ground rod, and the extension cord going to the ground rod? It is covered in detail in the document I linked already... Andre

Day 15 - Beefed up door in place and ready for seals and hardware. I may have slipped up on the 3 leaf rule here, I'm using a firedoor with 18mm and 12.5mm mdf board either side plus green glue. We've included a timber lining deep enough to cover the gap between the outer and inner walls but stopped short by 2mm to allow a caulk seal. We're also trying to allow for expansion/contraction of the timber as it settles, so it may need adjusting later. All credit to joiner Carlo's accuracy given how far out the brick walls and concrete floor were! I'm still experimenting with finishing the gap around the south window, I've tried the insulation wrapped with a black weed suppressant membrane, and also white sheeting over insulation with a layer of mdf for rigidity - but I'm not happy with either results.

binarybloke6 September 2010#23
Day 16 - south window fitted and more caulking. According to the "window = 1/3 mass of walls" guideline, we should be using a 10mm pane of laminate. The closest I could get was 10.8mm and it was ridiculously expensive, so we went with 8.8mm which was much cheaper and still fairly close to the ideal. We decided to sit the window on a length of neoprene strip with a layer of glazing tape either side which felt like a tight fit. Tip: clean BOTH sides of the glass thoroughly before fitting cause that speck of dirt you thought was on the accessible side will turn out to be on the inaccessible side and you'll be doomed to stare at it for eternity...as I am. Also discovering that the beef-up layer of gyproc eats up much more caulk than the first layer, so it's important to cut them as tight a fit as possible between the joists. Question: I need to build some kind of gobo to reduce the possible first reflections from the south window which will be behind my monitoring position. Am I right in thinking this absorber wouldn't need a rigid panel in order to work?
