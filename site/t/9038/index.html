This was sent to me by 4runner4sale who asked me to post it for him as he wanted all photos posted and the 6 limit hindered him from doing it - so here it is - great job mate :) ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Hello Everyone, I have spent countless hours gaining knowledge on this forum, and decided it’s time for me to give back. Below you will find photos of a 12’ X 19.5” room. 3 walls are 9” thick concrete, and one is drywall. The ceilings are 12’ tall with 1’ wide steel joists. All rooms are a challenge, especially small ones. This was no different. The key to building a “home” studio, for the do it yourself person, is to take what you’ve got, and make it work the best you can…that’s it. 80% of this studio’s design was learned from John’s site, and this forum. Hiring an Acoustician was not an option for me. So information on most everything you see is within this forum. Time hinders me from answering any questions, but I’ll try to do what I can when possible, if @ all. Bass traps are John’s design, room design is mine, ceiling was on this forum, the rest was trial and error. The room has been tested the old fashion way…ears. And I’m happy to say it sounds awesome! The live room is “live”, and the control room is “controlled”. A very slight build up in the mid range, but not enough to effect anything. So all in all, a success. Decoupling was key, as were bass traps. The divider wall is a double wall. The door is a steel acoustical door, and the window, the same. Both are from Specialty Doors + Automation, [removed] El Segundo CA 90245, [removed]. Insulation is 2” Owens Corning 703, from the fine people @ CWCI Insulation of L.A. 727 S. Ninth Avenue City of Industry, CA 91745 Phone: [removed]. Fabric from Gilford of Maine. We used “Sheet Block” under the drywall/cement board combo. It’s available all over the net, but don’t buy it through Auralex…too expensive. Everything else, builder supply. A quick note on “sound proofing”. There is a product called Quiet Rock. It sells for about $90.00 a sheet. It’s a great product, but don’t waste your money. We looked @ this product and realized that it’s ½” drywall, glued to a piece of ¼” Hardie backer (cement board). You can purchase both of these products @ Home Depot for less than half that price. Then glue them together with Sekaflex. Don’t forget to caulk all your seems. Make everything water tight. You’ll see the gray Sekaflex on all the walls in the photos. I can’t even imagine how many tubes we went through, but it paid off. I hope this info, along with photos, helps someone as much as this forum helped me. Best case scenario, if you can, call John. If not, read, read, read. ENJOY!

I can't say for sure of course, because we'd need more information. Hey Keith, If you're referring to the floor, this was the best we could do to separate the wood "live room" floor, from the control room floor. Every little bit helps, believe me. If you're referring to the sheetblok used to de-couple the wood, this works, and very well. Also, being that sheetblok is so dense, it's very secure. We just slipped it in between every joint where wood touched wood, before nailing it securely in place. You can hear the difference immediately by just tapping it with a hammer. When wood touches wood, it obviously transfers more sound than a "buffer" slipped in between. Again, it all adds up. I might be throwing the word "de-coupling" around too loosely. Maybe I should say "buffering". Thanks for the kind words!
