Nikodemos4 June 2007#4
So here is the details: We're planing to build a studio with a centered control room of about 22,5m2 and two live/recording rooms(a big one about 50m2 and a smaller kind of booth about 15m2) plus a rehearsal room. Our main isolation goal is to be able to work in the recording studio while a noisy band is practising in the rehearsal space. We also want to be able to use the rehearsal as an aditional live room so we are going to have all possible wiring compinations between the rooms. Our main designing problem is the 2 concrete walls right in the middle of the space....in my design idea there can be no optical connection between the control room and the rehearsal room but that's not a big deal and after all a window would bring some extra noise to the control room. The control in this starting design has a "clear" shape because from what i've read is easier that way to understand and calculate the acoustical problems of the room and then deal with them(or i got it wrong?). I'm planning on using the space between the control room, live booth and the outside basement wall as a sound lock and as an entry to the existing studio(not pictured here). The big live room and the booth will have a glass door(sliding or part opening) covering most of their separating wall. I'm thinking the same for the control room - live room combination for easy access through the rooms but the option of a big window is here too. As i mentioned this is a basement build with reinforced concrete and i'm thinking to use the existing concrete walls as the first leaf of a Mass-Air-Mass wall system. I still have a lot of questions on that so i'll wait until i have a final plan on my mind to throw them on the table. The reception room is quite small but since we already have a lounge room in the existing studio we only need this space as a reception plus a place for showcasing our cd releases since we operate a small record label too. As i mentioned above our main sound isollation challenge is to completely seperate the rehearsal room from the rest of the studio. I thought that the best way would be to have 2 completely seperated wall systems ....my main question here is how big should be the space between the 2 wall systems in order not to act as a 4 leaf wall but as 2 completely different walls? in this design i have a 80cm of air space betwwen the rehearsal's outter wall leaf and the studio wall.Is this enough or it would still be a 4 leaf system with all the dissadvantages i 've read here and in Rod's book? Well i'll get back on this as i'm still searching for all the available info in this forum and anywhere else i can... I hope you'll find the time to check my project and i would love to hear lots of ideas and proposals.....if i'm not describing something right please let me know...and i'll do my best to fix it!!! Thanks again....i'm waiting for your replies Nikodemos

jwl8 June 2007#11
the control room, the live room, and the drum booth all still have 2 parallel walls. I'd put an angle on the back wall of the control room, basically making a 5-sided room that is still symmetrical at the mix position. You could also easily enough angle one of the parallel walls in the drum booth easily enough. The live room might be a bit trickier, you'd have to pivot the control room a few degrees to make that wall nonparallel. You are getting close with your layout, though. Looks like a good design overall that just needs some tweaks. Though admittedly I'd be very curious about what John would design for this space.... :twisted:
