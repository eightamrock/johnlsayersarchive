I have described my studio project elsewhere (viewtopic.php?f=2&t=12330 and viewtopic.php?f=2&t=12326) but in the spirit of absolute clarity and consistency I thought I'd restate it here before asking new questions. I'm in Bristol (UK) and my house is a 1930s brick-built (280mm brick cavity walls: inner and outer layers of brick with foam insulation in between) bungalow that looks like drawing number one. I'm planning to build a studio which will comprise of a live room, which will be used to record and rehearse my rock bands (with live acoustic drums and guitar and bass amps, so pretty loud) and a control room which will be used for tracking and mixing, but realistically about 50% of the time used for composition, programming and teaching preparation (I'm a university lecturer in Creative Music Technology.) The constraints are: - isolation from the nearest neighbour (2m away but no windows whatsoever on that side of my house) - isolation from the street opposite; on the ground floor are shops only (so not really a problem) but there are flat on the 1st floor. It's also a moderately busy street so there is a fair amount of noise (which I don't mind, it plays in my favour if a moderate amount of noise leaks out from my studio.) - isolation from the bedroom and living room in the house; they are both some way away on the left side of the house, with the bedroom upstairs. I haven't started any work on it yet (still in the planning stages) but will start the build pretty soon (in a matter of weeks.) The live room is a concrete floor already. I have removed the existing floor from the control room; it's now to the bare foundations (concrete.) I have figured out a layout which is drawing 2. In this scenario, I'm using the outer wall as one-leaf of a two-leaf mass-spring-mass system. Difficult to see in the drawing but I'm assuming a 25mm gap, then a 100mm frame with two layers of plasterboard with green glue. In the lower room, I will build the control room, and to its left a WC and small entrance lobby to the control room (indicated in drawing 2) This should give me inner dimensions for the control room of: 2.6m x 4.065m x 4.73m which translates to the ratios: 1: 1.56:1.82 which I understand to be reasonably ok (judging by what I understand from Eric Desart's excellent "Acceptable Room Ratios" article.) I could nudge a little here or there but I'd rather not lose much width, and the length is near maximum because of the need for the WC. I'm planning to have air ducts for ventilation in the loft space above both the CR and the LR. There is a window currently in the CR but I will block it (I don't care losing the use of the window, but would rather keep it for aesthetics on the outside.) The LR has a double-door which I will retain for access. It also has a single door going outside. I'm planning to have two sets of doors for each, plus a set of double doors separating the LR and CR. Now for the new questions: Question1: I had my builder visit today and we need to start work on the studio shortly (to make a long story short: because much work needs to happen in other parts of the house, and I need the studio as temporary space.) We talked about the floor that needs building in the CR. He raised two concerns: - there are central heating system pipes in the LR that would be very difficult to redirect - bringing concrete inside the house would be difficult because of the access. So: 1) would the following scenario be any good: over the existing concrete (15inches away from intended floor): sand, then Kingspan Celotex, then IsoRubber HP6, then screed, then flooring? This scenario has the advantage that the heating system pipes can just be buried in the sand, and the respective heights of sand, Celotex and screed can be varied to suit. 2) Otherwise: given that my inner room will be (I anticipate) a single frame with two layers of plasterboard, plus the ceiling it will support, how much re-inforcing will the concrete need? And what thicknesses of sand, concrete and screed will I need, considering that the bottom is already concrete? Question2: The LR will be built into a newer extension to the house. It has a pitched (on both sides) roof that is about 4 feet high at its highest. Right now there is a loft ladder that gives me access to it for storage. Is there a way to retain this space as storage (not every day storage, but equipment cases and boxes, spare parts, that kind of stuff) while retaining sound isolation? Many thanks in advance. All advice very much welcome! Question3: I'm thinking of naming the studio "Electric Landlady" after the test pressing of Jimi's "Electric Ladyland". What does everyone think of the name? A tip of the hat or just a little too much? - martin

John Sayers10 May 2009#6
Martin - the simplest way to float a new slab is to use this method.
Image not preserved: concrete_slab.jpg
Basically - you lay down a plastic vapour barrier. On top of that you lay 1" (25mm) rigid fiberglass/rockwool. Then a layer of homosote or plywood or MDF followed by another layer of 1" (25mm) rigid fiberglass/rockwool also extending it up the wall. You then add another plastic barrier, add your reinforcing mesh and supports. Then you pour the slab on top of it all. The weight of the concrete will compress the fiberglass and the whole slab is then isolated from the rest of the building. You then build your internal walls directly onto the new slab.
