gullfo27 January 2011#2
steel framing has some isolation properties because it is much lighter than wood, but it has much less structural support so you have to go to much heavier steel which then mostly negates the isolation benefit and then also becomes roughly on par with wood anyways... since you have at your disposal a master carpenter, you should be in good shape. if you have not already obtained a copy of Rod Gervais' book http://www.amazon.com/Home-Recording-St ... 143545717X then now is the time to get a copy as it will provide many many of the details you will need and is a reference many of us can readily work from during your construction. on the design - since this is a greenfield project, consider what your priorities are - mixing or live room? both? isolation - if you plan on having loud amps and live drums, then you need isolation. this will require an exterior shell and an inner shell to accomplish, a simple metal shed will not do that. you need space for people to play... you need space for acoustics... something like a 12x20 shed would seem to be a good size for this overall. let's assume you built a 12'x18' (outside dimensions) shed 8' high on the outer walls, 13'h in the peak. you could then build about a 10'6x16'6x7'6h room inside.

xSpace2 February 2011#5
"8 feet high as per town code before I need to get permits and I cannot afford that. " That height limit is most likely a restriction from your community that you reside in, code is more interested in dollar figures when a permit is or is not required. A permit would cost you in the neighborhood of 50-100 (USD) bucks, which will prove to be less than a percent of your overall cost to build this thing correctly, even with a modest budget. "As for the metal studs I only intend to use them on the interior shell walls which will only really be supporting there own weight. The exterior frame and support of the shed will be wood. " If you build an actual room in a room (box in a box) then the interior metal studs will be supporting a ceiling. The only surface that this room will have in common/share will be the concrete floor. If you do not intend to add mass directly to the exterior walls, you are shooting yourself in the foot, since this is your rooms "first line of attack" against air-borne sound. You have over-stressed 8 feet as your height limit...what kind of roof are you planing for this structure...built-up? roll roofing?
