knightfly27 January 2008#2
Husky, welcome - first off, let me warn you about "cheap" "acoustic" foam - there's no such animal. IF it's cheap, it's marginal at best, and it's NOT fire retardant - I can 99% guarantee this. If you insist on going that way, you won't get the effect you're looking for acoustically and may end up sorry you ever built the room. There's a reason why the Illbruck (Sonex) and Auralex products cost more - they're higher density and fire retardant. Most of the other stuff is just bed foam wrapped in LIES. One such outfit actually just COPIED Auralex' specifications to their site even tho their product is NOTHING like Auralex. For about the same money as the "wanna-be" crap, you should be able to build your own MUCH better performing treatments using rockwool, rigid fiberglass and similar products - take a look at some of these member-built projects for example http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5930 And a general idea of where things would go in a "normal" room- http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm Hopefully this will help you get started... Steve

knightfly27 January 2008#4
Checked out their site, and their "acoustic" foam claims to be class B fire retardant (good thing) but any company that calls their foam "sound proofing" is no one I'd do business with. If you check out this company's closest location to you http://www.spi-co.com/servicecenterdirectory.cfm and ask about either rigid fiberglass or rockwool in 3 to 6 PCF (Pounds per Cubic Foot) density, you should be able to get something in a 2" or 3" thickness that'll cost maybe a little more but do TWICE as much for your acoustics in the same space - their offerings vary from place to place and day to day, so call and verify price and availability (and ask us) BEFORE making a trip. Trust me on this - you will like the way your room sounds a TON more going this route... Steve
