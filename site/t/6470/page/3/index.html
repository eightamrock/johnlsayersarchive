First of all, I would go down to the building department with your pictures and measurements and determine, assuming weight is not a factor (we know better), what limitations you will have in terms of floor space, given your sloped ceilings. I strongly suspect you will be told that the angled ceilings will limit your floor space to a tiny area and that such a "pup tent shaped" attic-type room is too small to legally build habitable space (and, yes, it is considered habitable, even if it won't be a sleeping room). I suspect your limited vertical clearance will prevent this job from getting off the ground (no pun intended), let alone the weight issues (which really shouldn't be let alone, of course). That "room" is intended for storage. There's a reason they didn't make a real room out of it. I'm sorry to be such a downer, but it hasn't been proven to me yet that you can legally pursue this project. --Keith :mrgreen:

Jack A.7 July 2006#53
Pardon me for butting in, but I am facing similar decisions for my over-the-garage space, and there are a couple of points that could be addressed further here. Apart from the weight issue, wouldn't a floating floor in this case also be a third leaf, given the ceiling in the garage below? I gather that this is one of Rod's reasons for disapproving of floating wood floors in general, apart from the resonance issue. Adding some mass to the existing floor, as Steve suggested, seems like the better approach; but I guess Robn and I both need some more assurance about this. There's another thing that hasn't really been discussed here, but has come up in other threads about attic studios: the continuous soffit vents which Robn mentioned early on. I seem to recall Rod saying that they present one case where it is better, all things considered (including the need for a ventilated roof), to have what are technically three leaves. Here that could mean putting sheetrock on the inner faces of the roof joists as your "real" outer leaf and ading an inner non-coupled sloping ceiling/wall as your "real" inner leaf. It would also validate Robn's original idea of 2-leaf knee walls, where the inner sloping ceiling would stop. This may sound like heresy, but how can the roof act effectively as a second leaf with all that venting open? It would be good to hear about anyone's actual experience with this.
sharward7 July 2006#54
robn wrote:
There are thousands of houses here in town with the "pup tent" design.....my neighbor to my east has one....I know because their house was built the same time ours was. The only difference in what I'm doing is that it's knee-walled on only one side.
But are the other houses with this "pup tent" shaped room over the garage habitable? Or are they just convenient storage spaces, as is the case with yours right now?
The weight issue is what has me wondering.....
It should have you wondering -- but what should have you wondering first, in my opinion, is whether or not the dimensions of the room dictate that the project is unstartable, and only your building department can advise you on that. In other words, assuming weight is not an issue (i.e., pretend you can build it with magic weightless materials), are you even allowed to build anything useful in there, given the roof lines? I predict the answer is "no" -- or "yes, but the floor space would be so tiny as to make it impractical." When I was a teenager, we rented a house for a year, and my bedroom over the garage was the coolest ever. It was over the garage and it had the coolest shape: all sorts of funny angles -- walls that went straight up a few feet, then into the room at a 45-degree angle. But here's the thing -- I still had a lot of flat ceiling. The majority of the room had a flat ceiling. How much ceiling in your room would be flat? I suspect none or very little. I predict you will find that your floor space will have to be a multiple of your flat ceiling area. If you can squeeze out a tiny strip of flat ceiling, and then maybe double that to come up with your floor area, will you have anything larger than a closet when you're done? I'm trying to save you some time here. Rather than getting bogged down on the weight issue -- a very important issue, of course -- find out if this thing can be legally built at any weight first. --Keith :mrgreen:

I'm sorry -- I didn't realize you had a permit already. I also didn't realize you'd have 2 1/2 feet of flat ceiling running the length of the room. It's not much, but it might be enough. Just so you know, though -- the permit gives you permission to start building. If you end up building something that is not code compliant, you won't pass your inspections and you won't get your occupancy certificate. You mentioned previously that other houses nearby have different walls than you plan to build (i.e., the floor space would be different, right?). If that is the case, then you can probably assume that their floor spaces are the maximum amount allowed, given the roofline constraints. Otherwise, they would have added the square footage and sold the houses for more money. --Keith :mrgreen:
