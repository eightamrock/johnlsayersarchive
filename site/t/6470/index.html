AndreasB22 June 2006#10
"What are the differences between a staggered stud and not? Does the wider spring space give the non-staggered the advantage?" Yes the non staggered is the best, but the staggered is not taking up the same space so if your room is very small the staggered is the way to go. "If I went with a 2 layer leaf and 1 layer leaf, would it be better to have the 2 layer leaf on the inside or outside frame?" for you it would be the inside, as you have to add the other layer to your existing outer leaf. "On the 2 layer leaf, is it best to glue them or nail them? I've read conflicting opinions on this." GreenGlue.. If you have trouble getting the right pressure on the second leaf you can Glue and then screw it tight. Then when the glue has dried you remove the screws. I read that the gap between the floor and the wall should be about 1/8" and caulked. Does this apply to the outside wall as well as the inside? hmm, as far as I can tell from your pictures, your outer leaf would be your existing ceiling. IF you put another wall there with a leaf facing outwards you'll get 3 leaf's = BAD. So generally you'll have to make sure that your existing outer leaf is airtight, if not you can beef it up with another layer of gypsum and caulk and THEN fill the cavisties between the existing studs with rockwool. That's your outer leaf! The you build the new frame inside your room and try to build it in a way that it never touches the outer leaf, preferrably an airgap of a couple of "'s. then just fill it up and put your 2 layers of gypsum on the inside. :D well... that's the "light" explanation.. and I'll guess I'd get corrected by the ones that know more than I do. Just wanted to give you an idea what you have to do. best andreas

sharward23 June 2006#13
Congratulations for getting your hands on Rod's book, and doing it so quickly. You are beginning to appreciate the magnitude of what you are hoping to accomplish. Your drawings are bad -- they're showing a three-leaf situation. Your outer leaf would be the roof and its sheathing. Then you're describing adding two leaves. That's bad. You want (ideally) only 2 leaves with as wide of an air gap as you can afford in terms of loss of square footage. Staggered stud? Nonsense... That would be acceptable if you maybe wanted a little more privacy between bedrooms. Double framing is your only hope. I would personally not recommend the "studio in the bonus room" scenario. Instead I would consider using some space downstairs (assuming you're "slab on grade") and perhaps finish out the bonus room and use it for whatever you displace downstairs for the studio. If isolation is important, I can't see it any other way. By the way, your builder is probably not qualified to answer your "can I do this safely?" question. You need a structural engineer for that. How much space do you have on your lot? If you have room for an accessory building out back, that's what I would recommend. You can build a nice two-leaf structure from scratch. You avoid so many headaches associated with retrofitting an existing building that in no way was designed with the demanding requirements of an isolated studio structure. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward23 June 2006#25
"Living space" is, I think, what my agent asked me. In building code speak, it's "habitable space." If you have the angled wall/ceiling thing going on, they probably also have restrictions on that. Generally a certain percentage of floor space must have a certain amount of headroom. It doesn't surprise me at all that the structural engineer would want to physically see the wood members. His deliverable would be a printed document with his certification stamp on it. He's exposing himself to a lot of liability. Lives are on the line, literally. Yes, your taxes might increase slightly by increasing the square footage of your home and making it more valuable. That's life. If you sold your house and moved across the street into a larger one, you'd pay more in taxes. Don't miss the "Permits, Codes, Licenses -- and WHY YOU SHOULD CARE!" thread. --Keith :mrgreen:
