Vintage1 May 2011#1
Hello to everybody. I have been following this fantastic forum for some time, and got inspired by it. And the time has come for me to submit my first post. First of all, I am from Croatia (near Zagreb) and I intend to build a small studio on a very tight budget. Any advices or guidelines would be much appreciated. OVERVIEW The space I can work with (a garage, ground floor, not attached to other buildings) has the following internal measurements: L 630cm, W 310cm, H 218cm. The walls are made of hollow concrete blocks (approx 22cm thick), and both the floor and the ceiling are made of concrete. There is a window on one of the walls (W 182cm, H 143cm) which has double-glazed glass. The top of the window is 24cm below the ceiling. The door (W 85cm, H 200cm) can be placed anywhere on a shorter wall, as this wall is yet to be built. One option, that I would like to keep open for the future, budget permitted, is to make an extension of the space through one of the longer walls is. However, this will be out of the question for a while. As this is such a small space, the heating will be done with hydronic central heating (one or two radiators) . Air-conditioning will be needed during summers. PURPOSE The studio will be used for recording and mixing (maybe mastering as well, in the future), and I am going for 'vintage sound' as much as I can (some vintage mics, Neve pre-amps, Hammond organ, Rhodes piano, etc.). LOUDNESS Good sound-proofing is preferred here in order to 'protect' my neighbours (other houses around me), as well as to stop outside noise coming in, as the property is next to a main road. The space needs to be divided to control room and a recording room (for vocals, guitars, drums, etc.). The Leslie cabinet would normally be kept in the control room and would be moved to the recording room only when needed for recording. EQUIPMENT The control room will, for now, be based around Yamaha 02R (hoping for this to grow into something bigger in the future). The rest of the equipment would be: ADAM S2.5 and Avantone Mix Cubes speakers, 1 to 2 computer monitors, 2 outboard racks (10U each), Hammond C3, Rhodes Stage 73, and a couple of extra keyboards. Not sure id dimensions of the equipment would help, but here they are anyway: Yamaha 02r – 66 (W) x 22(H) x 68(D) cm Hammond c3 – 98(H) x 124(w)x74(D) cm Leslie 122 - 102 (H) x 72 (W) x 40 (D) cm Rhodes Stage 73 – 88 (H) x 114 (W) x 57 (D) cm Adam S2.5A – 45 (H) x 28 (W) x 30 (D) cm (each) Master keyboard AKAI MX73 - 11 (H) x 15 (W) x 35 (D) cm

gullfo14 June 2011#8
the window in the CR - you could put in a set of very heavy drapes to cover it during mix down. or have some absorbers as "shutters" or slide over to cover as needed. however, you could flip the design to face the other way and have the window in front of you. the speakers should be decoupled from the soffit - foam/rubber inside the speaker box to keep it in place and yet separate, or a separate frame to support the speaker box - depends on the size of the monitors. plus the walls and air cavities in the soffit will have a lot of absorption to dampen it.
