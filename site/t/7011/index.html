John Sayers8 September 2006#4
I remember when they put some panel absorbers into Rhinoceros Studios in Sydney back in the early 80s. They were large plywood panels, maybe 6' x 3' x 3/8"and were attached over a rubber seal around the perimeter of the frame and held with securing screws at around 18"' spacing. They then attached a contact mike to the panel and set up an oscillator through a speaker. As they changed the frequency they hit the resonant frequency of the panel and the contact mike mike registered it. Apparently the frequency could be tuned by changing the tightness of the securing screws. cheers john

Be really careful there; one of the FEW helmholtz/slat calculators on the web that does NOT use the WRONG formula is posted here in the acoustics forum - and even THAT one has been questioned by greater math minds than mine. z6061112 September 2006#18
nickdahl
Contracarpet is used to mitigate the negative effects of carpet on reverberation time. So, ceilings are the place to locate contracarpet?
Contracarpet can be used in the same room as carpet, but you can use other things, and you can put them anywhere in the room you want. I'd prefer to put them in a place to defeat the loudest axial modes rather than opposite the carpet. In your case, it might be that the loudest axial mode is the floor-ceiling, and if you also have carpet on the floor, then the ceiling is good place for contracarpet. Remember my rule a) use RT60 to estimate how much absorption and what kind of absorption to put in the room b) and then use other acoustic ideas (SBIR, comb filtering, axial modes, early reflections, desk reflections, spaciousness, etc) to figure out where to place them
I'm still trying to figure out absorption coefficients for my walls. The front wall is 1/2" gypsum, in front of unpainted concrete blocks, and the front yard. The right wall is 1/2" gypsum, with a 2x4 wall space, and another 1/2" gypsum, which separate my room from the garage. The rear wall is 1/2" gypsum with a 2x4 wall space. This wall separates my room from the laundry room (8'x16'). My left wall is 1/2" gypsum, with a 2x4 wall space, which separates my room from my house's stairwell. How would I calculate absorption?
The book has two examples for different kinds of walls. A single layer of gypsum wall, and a double layer of gypsum wall. Use those two make an educated guess about what values you want to use. When in doubt, lower the absorption coefficients to play it safe.
When I use my absorbers (4" OC703) at a 45-degree angle, diagonally across the wall and ceiling, how do I allocate Sabins between the wall and ceiling?
Don't do it that way. Ignore the wall percentage. Think of it as sabins in the room. You can also ignore absorption coefficients and use sabins per unit directly (unit = 4'x8'). That's what I did in my spreadsheet for you. Here's some more absorption coefficients: http://www.bobgolds.com/Sabin.htm (start with "Sabin Data text file") http://www.bobgolds.com/AbsorptionCoefficients.htm and of course, for DIY Theoretical values you can try: http://www.whealy.com/acoustics/Porous.html BTW, your target RT60 doesn't have to be flat at that one value I mentioned. There's an acceptable range by frequency. If you tell me the volume of your room, I can give you the acceptable ranges. Flat RT60 is ok too (i.e. same RT60 from 30hz to 20khz). Also BTW, corner traps are great in an otherwise untreated room. But now that you're doing RT60 calcs, you have more options.
