Basically, the information in the above linked post stops being true the minute you either use separate frames or insert Resilient Channel in a wall or use light gauge steel studs - and the PITA of using "soundboard" in a wall system (and full-sheet lamination, for that matter) is just not worth the effort. For the most recent study of wall construction and performance, go here http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/fulltext/ir761/ and download the entire PDF (around 6 megs) - it shows constructions, STC ratings, and TL for each band separately, for nearly every combination of wall materials that's practical. To tell where the stud are after you put up the 1st sheet - just put a mark on the floor and on the ceiling with a pencil where the studs are and then you can snap a chalk line down the wall when the 2nd sheet goes up. I screwed both layers into the studs and put joint compund between the 2 layers. The 2nd layer I put in less sctews.

Thanks -Andrew gave the method for locating studs, etc - what I do is put strips of masking tape about an inch away from frames, before any paneling goes up - then I mark (with a Sharpie permanent marker) the location (and description - stud, RC, Screw, etc) of each and every component of the wall/ceiling/floor - After the first layer is up, I repeat for individual screw locations per layer (so you don't hit layer one's screws with those for layer two or three) - if you use the wide tape, you can get all this info on one (sometimes two) strips of tape PER wall intersection. It's critical that you get Studs perpendicular and RC level and straight (for runs longer than one stick) because you need to mark BOTH edges of the usable fastening surface so you can be sure you're fastening in the middle of that space. Every time you miss is another STC point lost... Under layers will only need half as many fasteners in each direction, this lessens any flanking into the cavity and saves on screws. The final layer will have a full schedule fastening, which goes through ALL layers and into the RC (or stud, if using full double framed walls) Also, when using a screw gun, first use some scraps of wallboard and framing and get your depth setting right before doing actual wallboard - you want to adjust the depth cone so that the screw just barely sinks below the surface - much more, and you'll tear the surface paper. This isn't good on ANY layer, it weakens the construction. On the surface layer it also makes things harder to get smooth for final taping. Gotta run for now, more later... Steve
