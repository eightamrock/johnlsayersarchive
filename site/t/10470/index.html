You a nice size space to work with. What is your budget? Are you doing the work yourself? How close will the mowers get to the building? Do want 5.1 capability now or to be ready for it in the control room? I strongly suggest at least the latter. Is the ceiling of the garage designed for the weight loads? Have a look at ALL the studios in the Stdios Under Construction section of the site for ideas. Congratulations on doings right. Too often I read about people who have started building without planing properly and having to tear down what they had done. For an excellent documented example of this read Lou's Studio Build. Yes, it is long, but knowing what he went through may save you tens of thousands of dollars. Good studio building is 90% planning and 10% construction. Constructively, Andre

AVare13 April 2008#10
I'm being diplomatic trying to save face. Yes you answered questions. I did not read your answers properly. Working on space, how many people do you want to have in the control room at once? Do you want a drum/vocal isolation booth? My first thought are all in one iso booth from where the stairs come up, with doors to the washroom, control room, and studio. Size dependent on what is wanted for control room and studio. One of the great things you have in your advantage is the ceiling height. The control room is the only space where working on symmetry is important. One of the best investments you can make is to buy Rod Gervais's book Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros. In addition to being an excellent text, Rod is a regular here. Keep asking! Andre
