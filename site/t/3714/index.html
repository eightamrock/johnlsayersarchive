John Sayers13 May 2005#4
Adding a sheet of plywood over the front of your ceiling clouds will make them reflective, angling them will help create angled walls to your rectangular shapes. The added reflective surface will brighten up the room in the high frequencies making it sound not so dead. The clouds will still absorb as the back of them is still insulation. Yes - I'd consider slots on the side walls but to brighten up the room (highs) but also if you angle them you'll also be breaking up the rectangular dimensions in the room. cheers john

John Sayers15 May 2005#11
What you are looking for in the slats is absorbers that they will take over the absorption of the 703 which is rolling off around the low mids 300hz. This is also the major resonance frequency in small rooms like yours. It's also the frequency area that can mask what's actualy happening in the frequencies below that. So often low end problems can be resolved by solving the inherant low-mid frequency problem. If you check out the reverb calculator at the SAE site .http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/pages/Reverberation%20Time%20Calculator.htm#verbcalc and put in a room with gypsum board walls and ceiling and wooden floor, your standard bedroom, you'll see that the reverb time is highest around 500 - 1khz. The low end is down - that's because gypsum board acts as a low frequency panel absorber and the low lows go straight through it. Now do it with brick walls and concrete ceiling and floor and you'll see the low end reverbtime is almost equal to the 500 -1khz reverb time. That's because brick and concrete has more mass and reflects the low end and holds it in the room. With regard to the slat widths etc. I would need to know the dimensions of your slat frames before I could answer that one for you but as you can see from above I'd be aiming at the low-mid frequency range. cheers john
