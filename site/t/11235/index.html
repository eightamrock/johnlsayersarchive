Marz Studio Build – Cape Town 2008 I am mainly a guitarist interested in recording side of music with a smattering of piano training in my younger days. I recently built a house with a fairly large basement room 7.5m x 3.6m x 2.4m but without a Studio in mind. This space is made of 220mm solid cement bricks with a further 300mm solid rock face in the front and earth on the back side of room (Built into the mountain), a 100 mm re-enforced concrete floor on grade and a 300mm concrete beam and block ceiling. Fortunately I had made some allowance for air in and out ( 2 x 110mm pipes each).

The Aim is to have a well Isolated room for practicing and recording in. Plan is to build essentially a room within a room design in timber frame using 2 x 4’s or 32mm x 110mm planed and CCA pressure treated Pine timbers set inside the concrete room. The front wall which will interface from Studio to Machine room and Vocal booth will be a double 2 x 4 timber wall to add isolation to the Booth. Due to lack of available room height, I decided to use Steel Angle Iron 50 x 50 x 10mm for the Ceiling beams to maximize room height. Walls and ceiling were filled with OC 703 Rigid Fiberglass sheets 100mm thick (2 Layers of 50mm sheets). Walls and ceiling were covered with 2 x layers of 13mm Gypsum boards staggered and each joint filled with backer rod (Caulk saver) and Flexible caulk. (Could not easily source Acoustic sealant, and use a product called Flexothane which has 25% elasticity - I recently had to undo some after 2 months and it was still flexible and soft) Doors would be single “Super Doors” with rubber seals and a single Frame passing through both walls – as per Rod Gervais’ suggestions. Frame is heavy duty 330mm x 50mm Pine fastened with compressed Fiberglass between Frame and wall framing. To start off, we made the bottom floor rails with CCA Pressure treated pine, fastened on top of plastic DPM on top of wet leveling cement with Hammer nails into concrete floor. The frames were made up inside the room and installed on the bottom floor rails with a 110mm wide strip of 10mm Neoprene rubber all the way around. This may not have been the best thing to do - Many argue to use just the correct amount of rubber to allow for proper M-S-M spring action to occur. The method I used is recommended in several places in Studio design literature. As of 15 September, we put the Drum kit in with Guitar and Bass Cabs and closed the two entrance doors and not much can be heard on the floor above. It was extremely loud inside this space!!! This is without fine-tuning the rubber sealing systems on the doors, or fitting the Silencer boxes on Air Supply systems or even finishing the plasterboard on outside of wall interfacing Studio to Machine room. I guess starting with a solid concrete shell helped tremendously with the Isolation. Now it can only get better! I have spent a small fortune on this studio build (Don’t know exactly, but close to $12000), but aim to have a lot of fun and hopefully make some cool recordings. I don’t plan to sell the house – its taken me 12 years to build and is in a fantastic part of South Africa in the Deep South of Cape Town, close to the Atlantic ocean on the way to Cape Point. We have a separate flat and workshop on the property as well. If I could do it all again : I would have made the room much taller – IE closer to 3m in height, made more volume allowance for Air supply and planned to use the current Water Room as a Live / Drum room. Otherwise I am happy with how it has turned out thus far. Still need to finalize…… The Monitor mounting system and soffits (KRK Rokit 8s) / Finish off all the wiring terminations / Make the Desk / Properly install the windows in the frames / Fine tune the Door Rubber sealing systems / Add more mass to the Vocal booth door / Treatments – Wall panel absorbers and Clouds / Lighting systems / Electrical hook up to Independent Switch Board in Machine room / Sort out Air Supply ducts with Sonic Liner / Find a High power portable AC (13000 BTU) / Finish off the Entrance Way \ Machine room / Make a set of Bass trapping Seats for Guitar \ Bass \ Producer to trap on Floor wall interface at sides and back of the room. Thanx Ro! Yup, we poured a 70mm cement screed on top of the 100mm concrete and hid the conduits in this screed. I took the many peoples advice on the various Studio fora (?) and decided against the initial idea of a sand filled timber deck on pucks and opted for solid concrete / cement floor. From what I have found so far, this was good advice and there is no need for an expenisive floated floor in a basement studio. I was also limited by existing room height. The screed was isolated from the timber wall framing with a 10mm x 80mm deep foam strip all around the round, just visible as a white strip below the bottom rails in pictures below.
