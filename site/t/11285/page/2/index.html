orangenumerik19 November 2008#29
Ro wrote
Well, there's not enough volume in a small room (or wall volume) for the diffuser to develop a useable reverb and adds unwanted coloration. Especially in close range of the listening point, it'll only distort the sound image.
Put another way to answer your question about why diffuser are no good in small rooms. Our brain cannot decode reflexion that are too close from each other, if the said diffuser is too close, then your brain will just mix the signal comming from the diffuser (reflected) with the signal comming from the speakers (direct) , and create comb filtering Fx wich would just be percieved as being the signal comming from the speakers (bad). To be usefull diffuser must have at leats 16 ft behind the listeing point. At that distance our brain hear two different things , the signal from the speakers and a diffused "room" sound, or tail, or reverb call it what you want. I hope this is not too confused.

gullfo23 February 2011#37
the dip is likely caused by a mode due to your room height. putting a cloud over your mix station is a good start, if that only starts to correct it, rebuild the cloud using a backing panel of 3/4" (19mm) MDF and plywood (for attaching screws easier and some stability) with the absorber side down. hang it on a 15 degree angle (front low- back high) if possible. have the cloud extend from the front of the mix desk to about 16-24" (400-600mm) behind you. leave at least 12" (300mm) all around the edge of the cloud to the walls. in your case, consider build several smaller clouds.
