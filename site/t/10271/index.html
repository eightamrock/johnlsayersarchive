awilki012 March 2008#10
Here's the new design. I figured I could build the panel out of 1/2" MDF or something else inexpensive and place them in a decorative box built out of 3/4" MDF with a cherry veneer on the outside to match my desk. Each panel would fit in the spaces in the surrounding box. I gave an additional 1/8" on the decorative box so as to make sure the panels fit with the fabric on them. I have corner supports in the panels so when I screw down the decorative panel to the acoustic panels, there should be no need for added support to the whole unit. I could have just created the decorative box and put the panels in them, but then the fabric would not have anything sturdy to pull against other than the rock wool/insulation itself. I think this will probably take more time than most people would spend on these, but I want them to look really nice. There will be about a 1" airspace gap behind the rock wool/insulation and the wall it will be hanging on. Should I have holes in the decorative frames to allow for airflow? I would think not, but I"m not sure..... Any ideas on how to hang these on the walls? This can all be used for panels on the walls, a cloud, and bass traps that straddle wall to wall and wall to ceiling interconnects. Please provide feedback if I need to change these in anyway. I've also added the Google Sketchup file....

The frames with no holes should be fine. I am somewhat confused about your remark about the 1" gap. If you have a gap, you will have to put something there to hold the absorbent material in place so that it does shift and change the acoustics of the room over time. My suggestion is to look for the best material for the money and build the frame to match the thickness. Please resize your pic so that it follows the guidelines.
