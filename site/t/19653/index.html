Practice room in an apartment building

Started by mihkelz on 23 October 2014. 3 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 19653.

mihkelz23 October 2014#1
I'm looking for a bit of sound isolation advice and general thoughts on my own plan thus far: I'm turning a 6.5m x 4.4m (21ft x 14ft) living room on the 4th floor of a 1920s brick house into a music practice room (classical instruments - mainly strings, singers, winds, piano). The house itself is pretty solid, typical early 20th century Amsterdam brick house. Only adjacent neighbours are below us, side walls are staircases. Currently the sound isolation is pretty decent, we hardly ever hear our neighbours. Regardless, we'd like to improve on the situation within a reasonable budget (and we're fully aware true isolation costs a fortune). I've done some reading up regarding various products and options and have decided to give Green Glue a go, but am doubtful about some of the other components. My current plan is something along these lines: Rip up old laminate + underlay (layer of foam and a layer of some sort of soft fibreboard). Underneath is the actual subfloor, pine planks. On top of the planks I'm thinking of installing first a high density rubber mat, then 18mm (3/4") OSB, green glue, 18mm OSB. Then put screws through the OSB sandwich that won't reach the rubber mat. The floor finish will be glue down cork. For the walls - a layer of green glue + drywall on top of the existing walls. As I mentioned above, I'm not looking for any miracles here, just a reasonable improvement over the current situation. I've read a few forum posts here, at Gearslutz and at AVS forums and have seen similar configurations used a number of times with what appears to be a reasonable degree of success. There's a few issues though: Suitable rubber matting seems to be almost impossible to source in the Netherlands and international shipping costs for something that heavy make this option a little less appealing. A couple of times I've seen people recommend 'soundboard' for a similar decoupling effect and after some research figured out that this material is basically cellulose fibreboard, but again I've been unable to find any for sale in the Netherlands. Are there any other alternatives that could work for this purpose? I've seen a few people suggest a layer of rockwool or similar, but others seem to be strongly opposed to this approach.. Anything else I'm missing or getting wrong? Any kind of input would be appreciated! Thanks in advance!

You seem to be missing a couple of things! :) It sounds like you are trying to isolate or "float" your floor on a rubber mat, but in reality what you are proposing won't work. Here's why: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 Despite what you see on the internet (especially the poor misguided many on YouTube!), that simply does not work. There are ways of isolating a floor, but not like that.
I've seen a few people suggest a layer of rockwool or similar
Exactly. That's the "drum riser" approach, and does work reasonably, if done properly. It works MUCH better than trying to float the floor on rubber... - Stuart -
