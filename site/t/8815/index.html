paul14227 wrote:
I just finished checking out the Green Glue website, and I am still scratching my head. Will Green Glue work like acoustic caulk as well as a sound barrier between surfaces?
Paul, Green glue, neither works as caulck, nor is it a sound barrier. It's meant to glue (or stick) board layers together thereby increasing damping and increasing insulation of the whole wall combination by surpressing vibration, mainly at resonance. It uses CLD (constraint layer damping). But in fact the site self gives the best explanation possible, supported by graphs, pictures, measurements, comparisons, whatever. In order to feel what it does: If you knock on a normal drywall it sounds a bit like a drum skin. If you knock on a Green Glue treated wall it sounds dead. Have you ever heard the difference by knocking on a standard window pane versus a laminated pane? The laminated pane sounds dead as well. That's what Green Glue does, but better since they don't need to worry about the transparency needing to be preserved in window panes. That pvb foil in laminated glass is no sound barrier either. It just makes the pane better.

Eric_Desart23 May 2007#7
paul14227 wrote:
That would make it a 'soundproofing' material correct?
Paul, No, it helps soundproofing, it's no soundproofing in itself. It's a damping material. If you put the same Green Glue on the outside instead of between 2 gypsum boards, the effect will be close to nothing ( :) with the exception that you get an incredibly sticky wall). The gypsum boards are the soundproofing materials. The Green Gloe surpresses the nagative effects in these gypsum boards, making them more efficient resulting in higher soundproofing. Acoustically: It impoves the transmission loss value of the sandwich panel by increasing the internal loss value of that sandwich. And that extents towards the resonance in the wall itself. If you put low density fibreglass in the cavity of a gypsum board wall it will significantly improve soundproofing of that wall. Take it out that wall and the soundproofing effect of that fibreglass will be very minor. This fiberglass is absorption material, no soundproofing material. Still it contributes to the soundproofing of that wall. If you feel safe calling it soundproofing material it's OK for me, but not distinguishing between these concepts will further block you if you want a more in-depth understanding. And if you call it towards others soundproofing, it only helps distributing wrong concepts. Brian Ravnaas the designer of the product and the Author of the technical content of that site, spent a lot of his time, certainly in the beginning to put these concepts, very often wrongly used in forums in the right context. It's as wrong as calling standard fibreglass soundproofing or acoustic isolation rather than absorption. This is really well meant.
