Hello all. This isn't my very first post here - but I'm still brand new to this site. At Rick F's suggestion (over at homerec) I decided I would make a thread here with as much info as I have about my studio ideas and progress. With that being said - this is my studio thread: Me: - I work in Audio (recording and editing broadcast speech), and have done some level of homerec in my spare time since I was 14. I'm a guitarist, drummer, singer, songwriter, and I have all the tools of my trades in my basement. I have a decent amount of homerec gear (mostly budget) and desperately need a space to use it. - I'm 29 and I live in Virginia Situation: At my last house - I had a space to record, mix and edit (it was just a room - and I was happy with it for the most part), but since we've moved - all my gear's been boxed up ...and it's killing me. Since we bought the house - I've done nothing but dream of plans to build a studio in the basement. My House: - Just bought the house last year. The basement is nice and big. Built in the 1950's, 2 story brick house, passed every level of VA Home Inspection. - My House is on a hill, the front of the basement is underground for about 10 feet before the ground slopes down (on both sides of the house) and the rest of the basement is level with the back yard. After a year - I cannot detect any moisture issues in the basement - although the part of the basement that is underground does smell a bit damp after a big rain. Purpose: - I would like a somewhat neutral sounding space to mix, edit and record. - I don't need to record drums in this room, and probably will not be recording bass amps (I usually go DI). Guitar amps are a probability, but mostly it will just be me with my MIDI, Guitars and Vox. Budget: Ridiculously small. It's possibly so small that I shouldn't even be posting this here - but I'm conflicted with the desire to do it right, so I keep reading and researching, and this seems like the best place to find the right information. Anyway - I'm looking at a couple hundred dollars a month at the most......so this may take years (or longer?). I can do all the labor myself, and I have friends who can help in specialized area's. Basement: Concrete Slab floors and load bearing walls with cinderblock walls inbetween. Issues so far: - The room I want to use is in the very back of the basement (Back wall is also the back of the house). The floor has a slope to it. - There's a drainage pipe running through the room (From the downstairs shower, toilet, washing machine). - No Ventilation What I've done so far: - In attempt to level out the slope - I started pouring some concrete. That is the 3.5" step you see at the back of the room (Which is perfectly level now - to the tune of 25x80lb bags of concrete). I realize now that without a building permit/structural engineer to look at this - I should have waited, and I'm going to get someone down there to see if I can even do what I planned on doing. So where I'm at now: - I just schooled myself on Sketchup and I'm downloading Smartdraw as we speak. Hopefully I'll have some more visually informative drawings for everyone shortly. - I've read Rod's Book once through and I go back to it alot, and I've been reading up on Sound Isolation and Acoustic Treatment for years. I still don't understand a lot of stuff. What I've been thinking - Non-parallel walls. Possibly slant the ceiling as well. - Eventually I think a desk in the front of the room - with my PC, mixer, outboard gear, monitors, etc. - Back of the room used for tracking - maybe with some Gobos to separate the space when necessary. - Bass traps in all corners. 703 panels scattered throughout the room. Wood floors perhaps? Anyway. I realize I need more illustrations (and possibly more info). Please let me know if I'm missing something here. Thanks in advance to anyone who takes the time to read this and post a response (good/bad/constructive). More on the way! Thanks, Todd *EDIT ADDITION: Isolation: -Isolation from neighbors is not an issue. I play drums in basement all the time and they don't mind (Ive talked to them about it). - I need isolation from my upstairs. Currently I can hear when someone's walking upstairs (hardwood floors through out the house). When my kids start running/jumping/rollerskating upstairs is when it gets loud down there.

gullfo2 August 2007#14
i think you should either keep the walls parallel and add some absorbers at key reflection points. the back wall can be multiple angles to allow for adding absorption or slat resonators. i also think you should level the floor either with poured cement or sanded filled flooring. the 3 1/2 step would be odd for most people to deal with in the middle of the room but less problematic in a door way.
