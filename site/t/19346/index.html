Soundman202020 March 2014#2
since green glue is a sound dampening glue
That isn't really correct. Despite the name, Green Glue isn't really glue at all: it does not stick things together: It also is not a general-purpose sound damping product: Rather it is designed for one specific type of damping, which is known as "constrained layer damping", or CLD. What that means is that it has to be held in between to large flat sheets or layers of some type of massive, flexible material, such as drywall, plywood, OSB, NDF or something similar. It works by damping a specific type of sound wave that would otherwise occur between the layers. Technically, it works by damping the sheer forces in the surfaces of the layers that constrain it. And it works really well at doing that, when used properly. But if you tried to use it between two studs that are nailed or screwed to each other, then there is nothing for it to work on! There are only very minimal sheer forces on the surface of a stud, practically non-existent, so there just isn't anything much that Green Glue would be able to do there. It isn't rubber, so it won't act like a resilient clip of some sort. It won't act like a spring, so it won't do what you are hoping it will do. It was never meant for that. What you need for that job is proper RSIC clips and hat channel, or alternatively proper resilient channel. Those are designed to do the job that you want done. - Stuart -
