Soundman20208 September 2009#33
Is it still important to have your ceiling between the speakers and the listening position angled?
Personally, I think it is. The angled ceiling also helps control flutter echo (floor-ceiling) and spreads out vertical room modes, not just creating an RFZ. In any event, you shouldn't really tilt your speakers more than about 9 or 10 degrees, but you need at least 12 degrees angle on your ceiling to make it worthwhile, so you'd still theoretically need 2 or 3 degrees on the ceiling anyway. Besides, tilting your speakers down at high angles increases the risk for nasty reflections off the console / desk / other equipment. Don't forget to plan for that! On the other hand, some people prefer not angle their ceilings at all, and to just leave it flat and treat it heavily instead. Personal preference. But personally, I still like the concept and aesthetics of an angled ceiling. - Stuart -

Soundman202020 October 2009#41
Regarding the ceiling question at the top of this page, I've pretty much decided on a ceiling parallel to the floor for the front half of the CR. Not ideal, I know, but if I'd left it sloping then I would have had to choose between soffits and a window - and I want both! By leaving the ceiling flat in that area, I have room for a window with speaker soffits above.
I'm not sure that I understand what you are saying: maybe its confusion about the "soffits"? The speakers don't go ABOVE the window: they go NEXT to it, on either side! Yeah, "soffit" is a misnomer: They aren't really "soffits" at all, in the strict sense of the word. To be more accurate, "soffit mounting" should be called "flush mounting" to avoid this kind of confusion. Let me explain. The speakers need to be at your ear height when you are seated at the mix position, or maybe slightly above, but never at the top of the window, right under the ceiling! The correct height is 1.2 m above the floor, which is roughly 4 feet. (That's about half way up the wall, for a normal 8 foot ceiling. A bit less than half way, for your ceiling, I reckon.). So, your "soffits" are actually wide vertical panels on each side of your front window, but the window in your model is way too wide at present: You will have to make it narrower (and taller, if you want), in order to leave sufficient space on each side for the "soffits"-which-aren't-really-soffits-at-all-but-often-still-get-called-that-incorrectly. In reality these soffits are vertical panels that run from the ceiling almost all the way to the floor, with the speakers mounted roughly in the center of the panel. The panels need to be massive, rigid, wide, and exactly flush with the front surface of your speaker, since they are supposed to act like something called an "infinite baffle". The panels need to be as wide as possible, and the entire panel/speaker assembly is usually set across the corner at an angle of anywhere from 30 to 45 degrees, so that the speakers are both aimed at a point just behind your head, roughly 38% of the distance from the front wall to the rear wall of your room. If you want to have the speakers a bit higher than 1.2 m, (to avoid interference from video monitors, racks, equipment, etc.), then you can do that and tilt the entire speaker / panel assembly down a bit, to keep the speakers aimed at that point just behind your head. But you should never have the speakers so high that you have to tilt them more than about 10 degrees! If you tilt more than 10°, then you run into several serious issues, such as messing up the frequency response of your ears, producing comb filtering off your desk / console / racks / equipment, and other not-nice things. So 10° is about as much as you want to go. Some manufacturers say up to 15° for their speakers, but the EBU recommends no more than 10. (It's hard to see how you could design room geometry to work for angles more than 10° anyway).
My new question is regarding the little side window I've added in.
Well, unfortunately that window is positioned exactly where your left hand "soffit" panel needs to be! So there isn't any place for it there. But it looks like you can move that little window backwards a few feet, towards the rear of the room, so that its roughly in line with the mix position. You'll still get a reasonably good sight line back there by the looks of it. But you'll be able to check that well on your SketchUp model, after you move the window and build your "soffits". Also, with the window in that position there should be no first order reflections from the speakers anyway, since they will be angled towards the mix position and away from the window, so any reflections off that window would go behind your head.
...those corners would have been ideal for either bass trapping or extended soffits, if possible....
BOTH! If you follow John's design for correctly soffit mounting speakers, then you get BOTH bass trapping AND ALSO soffit mounted speakers in those corners, plus they look great, and you'll have an excellent RFZ around your mix position.
d) Other. (Please elaborate)
The other option (which you won't need if you build the soffits correctly, but I'll mention anyway) is video. Just stick a camera in the live room, and a video monitor in the CR. You could even use a cheap webcam and view on your DAW screen, but that may not be a good idea. Anyway, as I said, the best option is to look at John's design for flush mounted speakers with built in hanger bass traps, and adapt that design to fit your space. then you can, indeed, also angle your ceiling correctly, if you choose to. Hope that helps! - Stuart -

Others might disagree, but in my book, acoustics come first in a studio, and things like windows, doors and sight lines take second place.
