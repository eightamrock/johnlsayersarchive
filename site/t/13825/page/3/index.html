Soundman202016 March 2010#52
I believe I read to fill 50% of the cavity space, between the inner & outer mass.
If you only fill 50% you will be compromising on isolation. Fill your entire cavity, or as near as you can get to filling it completely. A half filled cavity vs. a fully filled cavity can cost you anywhere up to about 8 dB of isolation, depending on how deep it is, etc. That said, it is also important to not stuff too much in. Just enough to fill it, but no m,ore. If you start jamming it in too tight, you will once again compromise isolation, since you could create a flanking path through the insulation itself.
If I have an 8" Spring, 4" of insulation against the wall etc.
The entire cavity is the spring, not just the part that is empty air. The insulation is also part of the spring, and is also the damper on the spring. It is a system: you can't really think of it as being separate parts. The masses on each side, the studs, the air, and the insulation are all part of one single system. Change one thing and you change the resonant frequency and the isolation of the entire system. - Stuart -

Arsenic wrote:
I'm afraid to have the VB against insulation, however, in fear that moisture will collect within the insulation, creating mildew and problems.
Condensation happens on the warmer side of the wall. The reason for a vapor barrier is to allow this vapor a membrane to condense on, to keep it from penetrating the wall and developing mold or wetting the insulation. You hvac has to be able to extract the moisture from the room(s) which is why it is in high order to have it sized correctly and return air paths are almost always required for each individual room. Probably, if you have a fear of something, you should get in touch with your local builders to make an attempt to understand what it is they would suggest. Still, they will say that the plastic membrane, the vapor barrier, goes to the inside of the room. What they may give you is information on if it is even required in your situation. This can be altered by the exterior siding, what kind of substrate was used under that siding and what kind of drainage plane was installed for that exterior wall. It never gets easy :) Brien
