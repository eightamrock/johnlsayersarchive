gn87berner11 March 2010#37
it is not necessary to use tape. I am a retired drywall finisher. As long as you leave a gap to be caulked. taped drywall is to be kept at (i always recommended) 58 degrees or it will crack just like an asphalt road can. freezing and thawing will "pop" your joints- butts and long joints. with caulk it can expand and contract without cracking. I'm not using tape but caulk, real good and clean, then spread some mud on the joints. less the better. the tape won't be able to hide a crack, if you get settling later. vapor barrier just before 1st layer of sheetrock, where you live.

gn87berner14 March 2010#41
That's where I was saying to put the barrier, as long as you get it figured out. Another good tip; use a fresh blade in your utility knife, and carefully, almost surgeon like, cut a v shape in your butt joints and along your angles (inside corners)then dust. Easier to caulk and superior quality. In the early 90's there was a big batch of drywall that had flaws in the manufacturing, where they cut the endo, the blades got dull or ??? anyway we had butt joints popping in alot of houses we had just finished. The reps came out, plus others had to have experienced the same thing. You could go to the end of the sheet and run your hand and pull the face paper back. A factory cut was inferior to one you scribed and cut. Cutting a v cured this but it is a lot more work. I have cut every butt since then to insure the finish holds up. Alot of people can make drywall look good but, looking good years down the road takes skill and understanding that fewer posses. When you cut an angle on the end of the sheet it increases the purchase area of your caulk or mud or whatever. Where is it going to crack? At the gap? or at the surface of the paper? It doesn't know. IT DOESN'T CRACK! You could say I'm a crack specialist. :) While you have your blade out cut out any little imperfection, down to gyp or white. If you leave any brown paper showing from the layers.And all it is layers and layers. Each time a layer gets wet it will lift and separate. When you paint, it will be the last thing you do, there in getting it wet and it will lift. so cut it out at the beginning. IF you do get something that lifts or blisters(after paint), get an large or vet syringe, with thinned elmers glue. Make a SMALL insertion, squirt a little then roll it out with wall paper roller. Best to cut it out before hand. Hope this helps someone. :D
