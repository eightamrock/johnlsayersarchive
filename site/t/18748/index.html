Hi there Pete, and Welcome! :) I was about to answer your initial post, but then I saw your second one:
Looks like my goals have changed somewhat and I have to be realistic about the fact that isolation is not going to happen on my budget (<$2000 AUD). Oh well, I still love reading the threads on here - maybe one day when I'm a rich man
Don't give up so easily! Have you looked at other alternatives? While 16mm fire-rated drywall is generally the cheapest, that's not true in all places on the planet. It might turn out that 16mm OSB, or 16mm plywood, or 16mm MDF, or 12mm fiber-cement board, or even something else is cheaper where you live. Look around, and see what you can find. Also look at the "waste" section of your hardware store: I one picked up an entire pallet of drywall at less than half price, simply because a careless fork lift operator had banged one corner into a wall, so every sheet had a broken corner. Who cares? Cut the edge off every sheet, used a few more sheets, a bit more work, but saved a bundle! I have also heard of people going to construction sites and picking up "overs": the excess drywall sheets left over that can't be returned because there aren't enough to make it worthwhile, or damaged, or dirty, or whatever. No use to them, so they usually just dump them :shock: They'll be happy to have someone cart them away... So be creative! Look for alternative materials that have the mass and rigidity you need, and look for alternative sources / options. - Stuart -

The colourbond shed won't really act as mass but it will act as a leaf, so I'd be looking to beef that up somehow if it's possible. Then you'd be building a stud wall inside of that and fully decoupled from it, with you plasterboard on the stud wall. I know the fyrcheck is expensive but it's really the best way to go in OZ. If you're planning on wailing the elec guitar at 1am after a skinfull and you're on a 1/4 acre block without a lot of studio-isolation then you'll have the neighours calling the cops instantly. stevev7 July 2014#9
petesguitar1 wrote:
so I think I'm down to single stud with resilient channel (16-90-30-16)
if i'm reading that correctly you'll be having a layer of 16mm fyrchek on one side of a 90mm stud, 30mm resilient channel and 16mm plaster on the other side. All of this inside your colourbond garage? I'm pretty sure you'll get better isolation if you change your layout to a 90mm stud wall (decoupled from your colourbond) and stick the two layers of 16mm on the inside. Thus creating a two-leaf system between your plaster and colourbond and eliminating the resilient channel. Make sure you fill the gap between the plaster and colourbond with insulation of around 30 kg/cubic meter for fibreglass and 50 kg/cubic meter for rockwool.
petesguitar1 wrote:
I found some insulation that had been used (yellow fibreglass)
I'm pretty sure that stuff comes in at around 10kg/cubic meter and won't be of any use to you.

petesguitar1 wrote:

correct. and GG will really help your isolation if done correctly and in the right quantities. your ceiling needs to be the same construction as well.
petesguitar1 wrote:
1) What sort of distance should I have between the Colourbond and the studs?
this is a cas of bigger is better, but if you had over 100-120mm in which you could loosely sit your insulation without compressing it then that would be great.
petesguitar1 wrote:
2) Any thoughts on the best insulation (brand) for this purpose? Is it possible to get polyester?
I used bradford soundscreen in 100 (or maybe it's 110mm) batts. You'll get them from Skinners in ballarat (which is where I got mine). I believe you can get polyester in higher densities, but i'm not sure if anyone has the gas flow resistivity numbers for them (which is what makes them work for isolation purposes). I also believe you pay an arm or a leg or maybe both for them in the higher density. (not that soundscreen is cheap either) :shock:
petesguitar1 wrote:
Got my plasterboard today (16mm fire rated) but I only have enough for 1 layer.
Well, that rules out green glue as well. :( The advantage is that you can do a single layer of 16mm with your insulation in the cavity and see what sort of transmission loss you come up with once you're done and sealed. If that gets you over the line with the TL you need then you're done :D If not, it's still pretty easy to put another layer of 16mm over the top. The only decision you'd have to make with putting a second layer on is whether or not to use GG. good luck with the framing and keep the pics coming :D
