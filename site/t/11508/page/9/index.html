Dissimilar materials such as 5/8 inch gypsum and 1/2 drywall is said to block more effectively than equivalent thickness of drywall alone. More energy is lost as sound must change it's speed for each different material thickness. In reality it isn't enough of any loss to justify so mass is always recommended, the more the merrier. To the sheetrock caulking, the top picture would be more accurate. Keeping a 1/4 inch gap between panel ends so your ceiling sheetrock would be 1/4 inch from the stud and the first sheet installed on the wall would be directly beneath the ceiling but would also have a 1/4 inch gap.

Pre-fab is always good. I counted 7 different mitre and/or bevel cuts on the top panel alone :) I'm just saying with actual saw in hand, these kind of things can be tricky. Sure I can guess at all of them, let's see a 10 degree top and bottom cut on the upper studs, the top plate would be something like a ten degree rip on the face of it. Depending on the actual desired degree of the soffit in the room, 45 degree on the ends unless 30 degree orientation is used then you would have a top plate with a 30 degree cut on the one end and a 60 degree on the opposite end, each mitre having a bevel equal to what ever the flare is from top to bottom on the sides. The flare on the ends would either require an offset stud on either flared side (moved to keep the plane flat) or a stud on the 30 degree side would require a 30 degree ripped stud and the opposite side would be 60 degree requiring a 60 degree ripped stud. Figure the lengths of the bottom and build that since it will be less trouble but dictates the height of the speaker panel. I guess it cold be pre-fabed with little to no trouble :0
