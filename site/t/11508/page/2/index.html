Well, if you promise not to have the dog for lunch if the inspectors get there early.... .) Actually, for non-rectangular rooms, I really wouldn't have a clue how to calculate the ratios. If it is roughly rectangular with just a couple of splayed walls then I've seen that you approximate ratios by take the average dimensions, but in your case with that diamond-shaped room, I really don't know how you would go about it. I suspect that the math becomes rather complex for that kind of shape, but on the positive side I also suspect that your modes are going to be fairly well spread out, mainly because there are so many paths that a sound wave can take, and eve a slight variation in the angle is going to have a large variation in path length. So, without being able to tell you why, or even if I am on the right track, I'm guessing that your shape of room should not have too many issues with badly spaced modes. Soundman20202 March 2009#49
I assumed since I want MSM, my drum room leaf must run at least the same distance as the control room leaf,
The walls don't have to be the same length, and don't even have to be parallel. What is important is the sealed air space, and de-coupling. So if you don't like that angle, you could change the angle of the wall marked 8'2" in your diagram, so that it is not parallel to the other wall. (In fact, increasing the gap between walls will IMPROVE isolation). The other thing you could do is to "chop that angle off" by building another small wall diagonally across that tight angle, so you will have 2 larger angles, both of which will be greater than 90 degrees, instead of one small angle which is less than 90. - Stuart -
