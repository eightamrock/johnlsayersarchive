gullfo24 October 2010#226
i'd just add additional 2x4 blocking towards the top and use 2 pieces of 1" MDF. use the full width on the bottom of the angled face and the smaller piece up top near the vent. optionally connect them together from behind so the panel cleats sits between the supporting frame members. i would leave a gap and bead and caulk it. you will need to secure it from tipping and reduce any vibration movement though so consider isolation brackets. inside the speaker box use 1/2" carpet padding or better Sorbothane and make sure the speaker fits snuggly but is still allowed some flexibility. NOTE: carpet padding - use the heavy polyethylene closed cell type, not the lightweight padding.

gullfo28 November 2010#229
the soffit assembly is actually thought of as a single unit. the next step is to erect it and keep it in place. several options exist: one is the entire assembly is self-supporting, two it's attached securely to the walls and ceiling. in the latter case, you could directly attach it to the walls or using a variety of isolation clips (WIC, RISC, etc) to secure it. first lag bolting the necessary framing members onto the walls and ceiling and then either joining the front portion of the soffit framing directly or via the isolation mounts. if you go the direct route, another consideration is using decoupled anchor bolts to attach them to the walls. using a layer of neoprene or equiv. helps decouple and seal (when necessary). adding the drywall i think has a similar issue that the MDF has - limited sizes for the sheet material means seams somewhere. maybe several MDF layers with overlapping seams to create (effectively) a single massive panel. gluing together 3x 1/2" MDF with overlapping seams would provide a substantial panel.
