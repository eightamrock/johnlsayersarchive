Soundman202030 March 2009#115
Sounds like a plan! I know that you already realize that you'll have some sort of three-leaf effect from the roof deck, and that you are prepared to live with it, but I just noticed that your roof is pretty steeply angled, which puts it always a long way away from the outer leaf MDF, so I reckon any three-leaf effects will be minimal. (Finally, some good news! )
although the 2x8's or 2x10 rafters would be touching the insulation from the upper 2x4 rafters, but I don't know if this would couple them.
Not a problem. It won't couple, since the insulation doesn't have enough mass to do that very well, and in any case it is "fluffy", so it doesn't couple well to start with. Unless you really stuff it in there very tightly, of course, in which case it will couple to some extent. But if you just place it on top of the lower joists, thick enough so that it only just touches the underside of the MDF, then you should be fine. In fact, it doesn't even need to touch the MDF above: If it is resting on the lower joists, then you could choose a thickness that would leave it just shy of the MDF. EG, if you have a 3 1/2 inch gap (between the bottom of the MDF and the top of the lower joists), then use 3 inches of insulation, leaving a 1/2 inch air gap above (between insulation and MDF). That will work fine. That's not really necessary, but if you are concerned about even slight flanking, then you could do that. It will probably sag away from the MFD slightly over time anyway, so you'll end up with the same effect naturally. A couple of questions that you might not have considered: How are you going to seal the edge of the MDF, which is your outer leaf for the ceiling, to the outer leaf for the walls? There isn't much space for you to get your hands and caulking guns in there, at the eaves, either from inside or from outside. Do you have a plan for that? Will just one layer of MDF for the outer leaf ceiling be enough? I seem to recall that you said you wanted to match the ceiling construction to the wall construction, for consistency, and I'm betting that your outer leaf wall has more than just one layer of MDF! :) Also, if any joint on that MDF fails, then your entire ceiling fails. If you add another layer on top (either more MDF, or maybe drywall), then you can stagger the joints so that a single failure in a joint won't kill your entire room. - Stuart -

Soundman20201 April 2009#119
I like the idea of MDF better. It is much more dense than drywall a
Actually, that's not true. MDF is less dense than gypsum board. To be more exact, MDF is around 700 to 800 kg/m3, and drywall varies a bit more, but starts around 800 kg/m3, goes up to about 1,200 kg/m3, as far as I can tell. Although it seems that most drywall these days is closer to the low end of the scale, around 800, which is similar to MDF. But MDF is not denser than drywall. HDF is more dense (at around 900 kg/m3), but not MDF. In any event, in most places drywall is cheaper per kilogram than than MDF, which makes it more attractive as a studio construction material. However, I sure do agree with you that MDF does make more sense in this case, as you pointed out, since you can walk on it. I can tell you from personal experience (the fist time I tried installing drywall on a ceiling, many many years ago), that trying to walk on top of a drywall ceiling is not a good idea.... :) - Stuart -
