NativeLuv17 wrote:
Thanks for the quick responses Andre and Soundman, so I'm guessing I should just drop the soundboard and db-Block and roll with another layer of sheet rock right? Pref with green glue.
Yep! You'll save yourself a lot of money, and get a better wall.
Excuse my newbyness to framing structures as I recently got into it, but what would be the isolation difference between a double stud wall and my staggered 2x6 frame with 3 inch air gap air paralleling a 2x4 frame?
If you do it right, you can get around 10dB gain in STC, by going with double studs plus an air gap full of fiberglass or rock wool, as compared to staggered studs with fiberglass or rock wool. If you search the forum or the internet, you should be able to find several examples. Look at this one, for instance: http://www.nzwood.co.nz/images/uploads/ ... P-Wall.pdf "A wall assembled using staggered studs will compromise transmission loss because sound will travel along the studs on one side of the wall through the common plates and along the stud connected to the other side of the wall. The transmission loss of such a wall can be significantly improved by having two completely separate wall frames with a small gap between – a double-stud wall. The STC and Weighted Sound Reduction Index (Rw) values of a double-stud wall can be 10 dB greater than an otherwise similarly staggered stud wall." However, beware of STC ratings: They don't tell you the whole truth about your wall, as STC is really meant for speech, not for full-spectrum music. STC only considers sound transmission down to 125 Hz., but you really need to have good isolation going down at least another couple of octaves. You need to know what is happening at 60 Hz too, and even lower. STC ratings cannot tell you anything at all about that. STC also cannot tell you much about transmission loss at the critical frequency for the wall, nor about transmission loss at another important point, called the "coincidence dip". To get the full picture of how good a wall is, you need a complete graph of transmission loss across the entire spectrum. Double stud walls 24"OC with a 4 inch gap between, the gap filled with rock wool, three layers of sheetrock on the outsides (ONLY the outsides) of each set of studs, and green glue between the layers of sheetrock, will give you a really, really good wall. Provided that you build it carefully, and seal it airtight. In fact, depending on your needs and your budget, you might even be able to get away with just two layers of 5/8 sheetrock on each side if you use 100% coverage with your Green Glue. Of course, the big question is: How much isolation do you need? - Stuart -

Soundman20209 January 2009#21
will the middle frame be creating a triple leaf?
If I understand your question, then no it won't be a triple leaf. Unless it has sheetrock or something else massive on it, then it isn't a leaf. If it is just a stud frame, then it's no big deal. The studs might still vibrate sympathetically with something, since they are not damped or stiffened by anything, but I don't think that would be much of an issue. Of course, it wouldn't hurt to take out that frame and use the wood elsewhere! Why waste it, if you can use it? Also, that sheetrock you are taking out: If you remove it carefully, you might be able to re-use some of it to beef up other walls, ceilings, etc., (EG., between joists, studs). I'm not sure if you need to do that, but if you do need beefing up, then old sheetrock is a good way of doing it, and its FREE! So work carefully and save yourself some money. Ditto any insulation you recover from those walls. Everything can be re-used if it is in decent condition, and the price sure is right! - Stuart -
