specialkm9 December 2009#1
New to the forum and have been lurking for the last few days. Lot of great info here and my thanks up front for anyone contributing on my new endeavor. I'm taking over the lease on a two story building in Burbank, Ca. The building has existed as a post audio facility since the 90's and has about 10 rooms with various video edit bays, two ADR/VO studios, offices and a really nice mix room where they've been posting movies and TV shows. My plans are to take over the main mix room and turn an adjoining lounge area into a tracking room. I've built 4 studios before, my last in 2000 and I have a pretty good idea of the design concepts behind building a decent room. I'll try to document the progress and post here if anyone might be interested. I've been looking over some of the designs here with great interest. Since the space I'll be turning into a tracking area is directly under a couple of office/production rooms, isolation is of extreme importance. I'm definitely going with a room in room design and since space is somewhat of a limited commodity, especially ceiling height, the designs with drywall layers on the outside of the framed walls looks to be a great space saver while still having the same amount of layers and isolation. Anyways, just a couple of questions as I start my planning. 1. Do I float a new framed floor? Seems like there are enough opinions here questioning the real value gained by floating floors. The existing floor is concrete slab. Since I only have 9' 11" from floor to the bottom of the 2nd floor floor joists, every inch not wasted in floor to ceiling height will be important. 2. What about MDF/particleboard? Looks like all the designs use drywall in their STC ratings. One of the studios I built utilized a MDF/Soundboard/MDF sandwich and it worked out pretty well. Seems like the MDF really stopped the low end How much more isolation can I gain with a couple of layers of MDF as opposed to multiple drywall layers? Thanks, km
