knightfly14 March 2006#3
Looking at the pix, I wonder if it's stiff enough not to need different methods of support than the rockwool types; also, they comment about it being "resistant" to microbes, etc - and they refer to the 3.5" stuff as being R13, whereas the rigid stuff only requires 1" thickness to achieve R13 IIRC - not saying the stuff isn't any good, only that I'd look closely at some of these differences before leaping into it. Also, the mineral fiber products are fire resistant enough not to just "pass" a 1-hour fire test, but to be used as fire BLOCKING in some code interpretations - not sure whether this is a big difference or not. I agree that the acoustic tests look good, and since it's being billed as a "green" product it may become more available than the alternatives - but before I'd recommend it for acoustic treatments (outside a wall) I'd want to "fondle" a chunk of it to see how hard it'd be to keep it flat, etc... Has anyone found a price? That'd be another possible deal-breaker if it's $3 a SF or something... Steve
gibong14 March 2006#4
dversion posted this link in response to my Offgassing and Green products question in the Building Products forum: http://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/ I just talked to them on the phone. They use Bonded Logic cotton for all their value-added products: http://www.proaudioacoustics.com/ (this is a sister site) The pricing for 1" and 2" 3 pcf raw panels is $2.50 sqft. The 6 pcf 1", 2" and the 4" "Bass Buster" is $4.50 sqft. The 4" stuff is what I would select for broadband absorbers (0.97 NRC at 125 Hz) ...the 2" stuff ("Echo Eliminator", 0.35 NRC at 125 Hz) doesn't look like it will do the job. They don't have a Canadian distributor, unfortunately.
