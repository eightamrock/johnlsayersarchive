leecow31 October 2006#1
Hi folks, I know it's been said many times before - what a great site and great bunch of people!! The amount of information available here is staggering. Having a respectable home project studio has been a dream for some time. It’s been great reading the information presented here learning that something of quality is possible and darn near practical! I’ve been chewing on a ton o’ information from this site and a number of books (primarily Rod’s, Mitch Gallagher’s “Acoustic Design for the Home Studio” and Everest’s “Sound Studio Construction on a Budget”) and a vague idea of what’s what is beginning to form in my head. Project Overview/Goals - My space is the partially above ground end of a daylight basement. Rough dimensions are 22 x 12 x 7.5 feet (attached skp has fairly accurate measurements). The final multi-use, single room to be used for: • Personal ‘cave’ • Tracking • Mixing • Personal and small group rehearsal Remodel status – Overall project just crossed the halfway point. Money and calendar almost line up ;-) Plumbing is done. Radiant heating crew is at bat. Electrical and low voltage on deck. How loud? - The hand waving guesstimates would be 95db without upsetting neighbors or anyone in the house during daylight hours and be able to play or mix at 80db after everyone is in bed. Some details - The ‘core’ house is about 60 years old but the remodel includes replacement of all plumbing and electrical. As mentioned above, the studio space will be in the mostly above ground portion of a daylight basement. Below grade walls are cement. 3 walls are exterior and 1 is along the stairs. Rooms above are the kitchen and dining rooms. The ceiling is where I anticipate the most trouble. The heavily used rooms above, precious little headroom and in-floor radiant heating preventing direct application of sheetrock to the deck make this an assembly where ideas and advice will be greatly appreciated. Ceiling joists are the original 16” oc 2x10s running the width of the room. Additionally, plumbing is running through one of the channels chewing up all but the bottom 4”. Couple o’ questions: 1)Ceiling system – Given the restrictions (radiant heating pipes attached directly to deck) and weaknesses (plumbing run that cannot be moved), what are the best isolation options? Answer: Ceiling system covered at http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=7423 . No magic bullets :wink: 2)Electrical – It’s a fair distance from the basement to the garage panel and the electrician has suggested running a subpanel for the studio. Are there any special considerations for such a configuration if star grounding is to be employed? Budget – Whatever it takes to do it right as economically as possible. Thanks for looking!! Lee

sharward10 November 2006#15
I think I see a problem with the design from an isolation perspective, assuming isolation is a priority. (I'm assuming it is, since you're doing double framing.) So, your closet door is part of the inner leaf. Got that. But is the entire closet surface, including the part under the stairs, part of your outer leaf? If yes, then you don't want your inner leaf framing extending into the closet. There's no purpose for it and it will only be in your way, chewing up square footage. If no, then you'll need to complete your outer leaf, and that means another door that would have to open into the closet. Another option would be to make the closet a part of the outer leaf, but not the part under the stairs. You could then make an access door for the area under the stairs that would keep up with the rest of the outer leaf. I started to quickly sketch this up in Paint, but I'm really short on time and can't afford to play around with it too much... Sorry. You'll also probably need to do something for ventilation or cooling in that closet if it's going to be a machine room. Overheating your gear would not be good. :( --Keith :mrgreen:
