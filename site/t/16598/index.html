Soundman20206 August 2011#4
Hi there "x00011011", and welcome! "x00011011"? Hard to pronounce! Or maybe that's a Hex 1B? A decimal 27? An Ascii ESC? You sure are a hard fellow to pin down! Mysterious handle... :) Anyway, two things stand out in your design: One is your budget: It seems to be missing a zero! :shock: For a studio, you should normally figure about the same cost per square foot as for actually building a house, using the average building rates for your area. US$ 20k for 1,800 square feet works out to about US$ 11 per square foot. Call a couple of local contractors in your area, and ask them what it costs per square foot to build a nice house: I reckon the figures you will hear will be a bit more than US$ 11 per square foot. You also say that the actual studio space is about 747 sq.ft, and that changes things a bit, but even then it is still only around US$ 25/sq.ft. Even assuming that most of the walls,windows, floors and doors are already in place, and that this is more of a renovation and refit than an actual build, your budget still seems rather on the low side for that amount of space. I'm not saying you can't do it: If you have a source of free labor, and access to big discounts n materials, and do all the design work yourself, you can save a lot of money. But you might want to re-consider your budget. The second point is the control room: It seems to be squashed up in a rather tight space, and is kind of "short and fat". In general, you want to have the speakers firing down the long axis of the room, not across the short axis. There is no space to properly soffit mount your speakers with that design, and there also seems to be no space for bass trapping, which you certainly will need since it is a small room. For example, at the left front corner, exactly where you should have either deep bass trapping or your speaker soffit, is the window to the vocal booth! You might want to consider re-arranging things to give yourself a bigger CR, that is oriented the "right" way.
I understand that doors are the weak point and the vocal booth has a large number of them.
Yup! And you seem to have at least NINE acoustic doors that you need! Figure a couple of hundred dollars each, and there goes ten percent of you budget, just on doors. Then you have four double-pane windows, which adds up to another large percentage.
I did not float the floor because I understand minimal flanking will take place in a basement studio on top of a slab.
True! You very likely do not need to float your floor, and the other issue is budget: floating that floor correctly would mist likely eat up your entire budget, or at best a very large chunk of it.
Rooms, flooring, lighting, HVAC, electrial, in-wall cableing,
HVAC will take up another 20% of your budget, at least. So, in general I'd suggest that you maybe try moving things around so that you get a better shape and size for your CR, with better positions of doors and windows so you can install the treatment that it will need, in the correct locations, and also re-think your budget. That's just me, though. Others might have different opinions... - Stuart -

Is soffit mounting the speakers better than having them on a shelf on the wall?
Hell yes! That's like asking "Is eating grade-A prime fillet steak better than eating cow dung?" :)
I will have bass traps down the front corners, I will have to adjust the design (shrink the vocal booth window) to reflect that.
You can't have both soffit mount and bass traps in the front corners. One or the other. At best you can have hangers under the lower part of the soffit. That would help somewhat, but you still don't have enough bass trapping for that room.
double pane windows. If you have a double wall, both windows would be double pane, correct??
Nope! That would be a 4-leaf system, and would give you really lousy isolation in the low end (drums, bass, etc.) you only ever want two leaves in an isolation wall. Never one, never three, never four. Two and only two.
I know it's not ideal atm. If I flip the audio desk and re-skew the walls, there will be like <1.5' on either side of the desk for a walkway. I could make a <3' walkway but then the desk wouldn't be centered. See my dilema. Suggestions?
Move the CR to the bottom left, close to where it says "Audio Storage", and re-arrange the LR and vocal both around it, including the area that is currently the CR. It will give you much better layout, and better sight lines too! The only drawback would be that you'd have to go through the LR to get to the CR, but IMHO that would still be way better than having a small, cramped CR with poor geometry and acoustics. - Stuart -
