Soundman20204 July 2009#9
A small room in my house anout 8 x 12 or 12 x 15.
Like Javier said: That's a very small room. It will need lots of bass trapping, which is best done with large volumes of thick absorption, such as fiberglass or mineral wool. Diffusion in small rooms is not useful. It works well in large rooms, but not small ones. Also: 8x12 would probably not be a good ratio. I guess the height is also 8?

eruss wrote:
The question is which would you do if you had the choice.
If those were really the only two options, I'd get a good set of headphones and a super-size prescription for industrial-strength antidepressants. :shot: Thicker panels of denser material (the oft-recommended 4" or thicker panels of rigid fiberglass or mineral wool covered in breathable, fire-resistant fabric spaced 2" or more off the wall, beginning by straddling wall-wall and wall-ceiling corners and at 'first reflection points' on the walls and ceiling, and then spot-treating other surfaces to combat parallel-wall flutter echoes and standing waves) would be so much better than foam or cardboard. Auralex will just muffle the highs and exacerbate the other problems, and 'barrel diffusers' aren't as in vogue in larger designs as they once were, and aren't too useful in rooms that small (and more than anything, would just take up space on the walls that you need to use for the other types of treatment). And try to win whatever argument you have to to get the bigger of those two rooms - especially if the ceiling height is also 8'. :mrgreen:
