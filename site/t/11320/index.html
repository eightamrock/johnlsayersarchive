Soundman202030 October 2008#23
rontal wrote:
I have a question about airtight?? Does airtight means sealed between the 2 walls where air has no where to go or each room should not have any voids in it that would allow sound to travel the path of air?
"Airtight" means that you seal everything, caulking all joints carefully, on all of your walls and the ceiling, plus the door frames. Basically, you want each leaf of your "room within a room" to be totally hermetic.
Now Iso on left of CR inner wall would be tied to the drop ceiling and now is where I am scratching my head. Do I take the walls up to the deck or WTF because one either way I go the one wall inner would be one walls outer? I am confusing myself! But you guys get what I am saying.
Ummm.... actually, not! :) Maybe a diagram or SkethUp model would help to show what you are trying to explain? It's hard to picture it just from words.
So in this case there is no way that I could totally seal the walls in between the 3 rooms because these wall supposed to not touch each other.
Not really. Each of the three inner rooms (ISO, CR, LR) has to be hermetically sealed by itself, and all three of them are sealed within the outer leaf, which is also hermetic. Think of it this way: Imagine that you were going to fill up one of the rooms with water. You want the inner leaf of that room to be so tight that no water can leak out of that room into the gaps between that room and the other room, or that room and the outer leaf. So no matter which room you fill with water, only that room will get wet. Also, if you filled the gaps between the rooms and the outer leaf with water, then only the gaps would get wet: nothing would leak in to any of the three rooms. No substitute "air" for "water" in that mental picture, and you have the idea of how to seal.
I really really need to get my sketchup file going but I get so damn fustrated trying to figure it out!
Yup! Definitely you need to do that! At first SketchUp does seem a little quirky, but once you get going you'll find it is extremely powerful, and extremely useful. And you definitely need to take xSpace's advice on the drop ceiling tracks: Those things just cannot safely support the kind of weight you are going to need here. I'd suggest that you take it out completely, beef up whatever is above you (if it needs it for good isolation), and use the space where the drop ceiling was to build a proper "inner leaf" ceiling over each room, with at least a couple of layers of drywall, firmly screwed to a solid wood or metal framing structure that is properly designed to handle the weight of many hundreds of pounds of drywall. - Stuart -
