The Space13 June 2006#1
So, it's my first time actually posting, and i've got a pretty ambitious project coming up. So here goes - I hope i can give enough information and don't break any of the rules. Our warehouse/studio building and land were just purchased, and since we're on a month to month lease, the new owner's plan doesn't include us. Now we're being relocated and we've found a great new building with twice as much space and it's just better built. Now the only problem is constructing a brand new studio from scratch in an empty space. Here's what we've got to work with: Location - Upstate NY just outside of Rochester. New Dimensions Size - 43' wide by 42' 6" long by 17' tall. This is the area that is going to be dedicated to the studio. The constraints within this are a couple of doors on the exterior walls that we need to fit into the plan - That's it. It's free open space - no wiring, no pipes, no poles, no columns, no nothing - just clear area. The floor is thick poured concrete that is smooth as glass. For cost issues, we're talking about staining the concrete short term before we go full bore with floor treatments of carpet and hardwoods. The long walls are interior walls that separate this planned studio area from warehouse space. The whole building is roughly 60' deep and 200' in length. The long wall that contains an opening is our entrance into our warehouse. The facing long wall with no openings in it is not currently built. It is going to separate our area from the building owners personal space. If it needs to move a foot or 2, it's probably not the end of the world, but the building owner might get angry if we keep muscling into his space. The short wall that contains an opening is the entrance into the studio from the parking lot. In the rear of this space are 2 bathrooms, a utility area with water heater that will be turned into a kitchenette, and a loft office area. This portion of the building will remain unchanged. We talked about running a 5' wide hallway from the bathrooms, around to the utility area, and then down the "warehouse wall" to the front corner, and finishing just to the side of the entry door. Kind of like a big rediculous S shape. The only thing we don't like about this is the amount of lost space to hallways - but we don't want to have to walk through multiple rooms to get places. We have a couple floor plans, but nothing that we're very happy with so far. Goals: We would like to fit a really nice control room, primary tracking room, 2 isolation booths, and a lounge area within this open space. If not possible to fit 2 isolation booths, than one large iso will probably suffice. Traffic flow and isolation within the structure are important - sound leakage to the outside world is not necessarily a concern. The entire building is post frame on a poured 6" concrete floor. Lots of insulation throughout the structure. We're talking about starting construction within the next few months to have a studio that will be ready to track bands by late 2006. Most of the bands we deal with are local metal and rock acts. The budget is up in the air - it's kind of going to be a "whatever it takes" kind of thing. We've roughly budgeted $10,000 USD for materials and doing construction ourselves. If i need more information, let me know - I've included a sketchup and a picture for those that just want to look. We're looking for anyone to give us some input to push us in the direction of what to do. The layouts we have come up with aren't bad, but again, we're not really happy with any of them and we're looking for fresh eyes to approach the project. If anyone wants, i'll include sketchups of our latest "design", but i'd rather get new input instead of trying to rehash what we've thrown around. Thanks in advance, Brian[/b]

The Space31 December 2006#7
We recently were able to get more space than originally intended for our studio which we hope to start construction of in the next few weeks. The larger space has allowed for a lot of modifications to the original plan, including moving the band lounge to a space that makes more sense and will be quieter. All of the recording spaces can be moved around and we think we came up with a better plan. I've included some sketchups and a jpg of the newer space. The real problem that we have run into at this point is control room dimensions. we were working with a model that was 22 feet long, 14 feet wide at the window end, 18 feet wide at the rear wall, and a ceiling that varied from 10 feet to 12 sloping up from the mix position. Quite honestly, we're not sure what dimensions really work best - from looking at recording studios around the world, it seems that personal taste weighs in more than any real design. Can you achieve good sound from any practical space? In any event, We're stumped. We need some help from the regulars on the forum. It looks like my control room is too big in the sketchup, compared to the other rooms. but perhaps it isnt. I'd also like to fit in one more iso booth - just wasn't sure where. The new dimensions of the overall space that is open is currently: Width: 43 Feet Depth: 42 1/2 Feet Height: 17 Feet In the sketchups and jpg we've allowed a five foot hallway running from entry to lounge - the dead space between lounge and drum room will become either storage for media or equipment, or a machine room. If you need any other information that wasn't in my original post, please ask. I think i included all the forum basics in that one. Oh - just noticed - it looks like i forgot to blow out a window in the control room - just imagine the window facing the drum room... We like this plan, but we think some of you professionals might have better insight. We're gearheads and audio engineers - not design enthusiasts. And we're grateful for any help or suggestions you might be able to provide. Thanks in advance
