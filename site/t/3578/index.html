the scenario is simple really: location: suburban USA, East Coast structure: rented attached 2 storey townhouse space available: second bedroom now office quality of structure: basic 1986 building code followed (ie it SUCKS!) studio kind: digital recording of synths & vocals, some guitar work check out the basic schematic i posted on the idiotic verizon webspace I have: http://mysite.verizon.net/vze8caar :oops: :oops: yes I know the imagery is shameful, but rather than wasting my time signing up for yet another free-trial software program or even buying one, I always thought that working with what you have sometimes has to do. the idea fore the studio is to have 2 iso booths in mirror position in the room and have them act, if possible, as bass traps. Either by virtue of them being iso booths, or by virtue of adding a adjustwed design of basetrap into 1 wall of each booth facing the mix position. Both booths would be resting on a sliding track, that runs parallel to the wall facing the mix position. Function of this would be so that I can get to my closet and window if need be. Attached to the iso booths, but not permanent would be portable slat resonators: 3 to 4 panels attachable and detachable via hinges, plus with ability to thus fold like a harmonica. (are you laughing yet, or do you think I am high as a kite or the dumbest MF? neither of which is true!!) behind the mix position I would position a more permanent resonator build per specs found on forum, including angling it, so that it is 1'2" away from the wall at ceiling height. In the attic I can hang more traps, plus just below the celing a 703 cloud may be possible. I would really like to engage in some healthy discussions about this idea. Note that I live in a quiet area, my neighbours are hardly EVER home and I do not have the need to be able to jam at loud frequencies. Most important to me is the quality of vocal/ acoustic recording tracks, and if possible a maximized mixing environment giving the circumstance.
