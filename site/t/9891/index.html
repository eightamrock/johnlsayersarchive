Her man24 December 2007#7
You're right, there are bluegrass bands that do record in the same space. And I believe a larger space with proper gobos would work fine. What concerns me is the size of my studio, mic bleed would be hard to overcome. Unless everyone recorded on the same mic. Even in this situation getting everyone to work the mic just right can be very problematic and time consuming. In the past I recorded a bluegrass group in a smaller room, but not by much. When one musician made a mistake, the other musicians had to go back and record the song again, and punching in and out changed the sound noticeably, all the background noise was gone in the punch in. Trying to get the proper blend was almost impossible. The banjo got into all the other mics. The low end upright muddied up the sound since it wasn't isolated properly, etc. It just seemed to me it made more since, if the music was on separate tracks I would have much more control in the mix. When I tried to "effect" the vocal, it not only changed the dynamics of the vocal but the banjo, working with individual instruments was a nightmare. Anyway, that's my logic on iso booths. After saying all that, I am not by any stretch of the imagination an expert on studio design...I'm like a lot of folks who desire to take on such a project as building a recording studio and since my budget is relatively limited I can't hire an expert but I try to read every book I can and learn from recording websites such as this one and most importantly "listen" and respect what you guys are saying on this forum. Believe me, your help and suggestions are very welcome..Thanks, Jackie
