Hi John, and welcome! There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post. Actually, several people, who are experts on this forum, will not reply if you do not do what is written in the post. Many others who are very helpful, will not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. However, two quick comments:
1. Should I build double walls for the control room or is there another way of constructing walls that is effective, less bulky, and inexpensive?
Nope! Sorry. Two-leaf MAM wall is the cheapest most effective way to get good isolation. The only other way is with a ten-inch reinforced concrete wall. You can't cheat the laws of physics. To stop sound, you need mass (or a vacuum). The cheapest way of getting mass is with sheetrock. The bulk is part of the MAM system: You have to have a decent air gap between the leaves, or you don't get decent isolation.
How will the concrete floor affect the sound?
Great! The general consensus here is to have a live floor and an absorbent ceiling. Concrete is good for a live floor. Or you could glue laminate floor to it. to ,make it look better. If you want it deader, scatter some thick throw rugs.
What amount of absorption, diffusion is necessary for a room this size?
Absorption? Lots! Large bass traps in all corners, to start with. Diffusion? In the control room, none. It's too small for diffusion to be effective. In the live room? To taste. - Stuart -

Don't worry, John! I understand you perfectly. Several months back, I was where you are now, bemuddled and confused. It takes a while to get your head around some of this stuff, since it isn't intuitive. Sound does not act the way we tend to think it does. Once you get the basics in place in your mind, it starts to make more sense. Hang in there!
From what I've gathered so far, it seems I need to build a double wall, insulated and with 2 layers of sheetrock on the outsides of each wall.
Exactly. That is "MAM". Mass - Air - Mass. Or more correctly "MSM", Mass - Spring - Mass. The sheetrock on each side is the mass. The air trapped in the middle plus the insulation acts as a damped spring.
I'm getting there.
Yup!
Once I find the design. check the cost of materials, I have to figure out how wide these walls are going to be exactly. This is a small control room to begin with. The width of these wall may force me to take another approach.
To get the best isolation, your entire wall will be around 12 inches thick. That includes a 4-inch air gap, two 2x4 frames, and the thickness of the double sheetrock on each side. You can do it for less, but you sacrifice isolation. You can do it in about 8 inches by using staggered studs on a 2x6 base, plus double sheetrock on each side. That will cost you up to about 10 dB of isolation, depending on how you build it. You can even do it in about 6 inches, using a single 2x4 stud wall, with resilient channel on one side, plus double sheetrock on each side. That will cost you another bunch of dB in isolation. If you absolutely must have a thinner wall with better isolation, then that too is possible, but the cost goes way up. Now you have to start thinking of extremely dense materials instead of sheetrock. Things like concrete, steel plate and lead plate. It can be done, but you better have a big budget: sheet lead is just a tad more expensive than sheet rock!!!! Unfortunately, you cannot beat the laws of physics. There are only a couple of ways to stop sound. And they require lots of space, lots of mass, or lots of money. There is no such things as a cheap, thin, lightweight wall that is effective at stopping sound from getting through! - Stuart -
