Ceiling question - whats the best solution?

Started by Byra on 3 November 2009. 8 replies.

Byra3 November 2009#1
Im working on finalizing the design for my house addition. Its going to be 22'x25', connected to 2 existing walls on my house. Im planning on room within a room construction, and for the walls its pretty straightforward. However, for the ceiling, im still a bit unsure what the best way to do it is. Here are the possibilities as I see them: 1. Hang the outer leaf from the truss joists, and then hang the inner leaf off of joists sitting on the inner walls. This seems like the most decoupled scenario, but I wonder if the joists will be able to handle the load. The span would be 22', with one inner wall that could help support the joists, but is that enough? 2. Build the ceiling 'inside out', as I have read here. I think this means putting drywall both above and below the joists and filing the cavity with insulation. Is that right? It seems like it would be a pain to cut the drywall for the side above the joists. And I think I would still have to decouple the inner leaf with rsic clips or something similar. 3. Hang the outer leaf from the bottom of the truss joists, and then use some kind of decoupling hanger for the inner leaf. This would eat into the headroom, but is feasable. Which is the best way to do the ceiling? Thank you for your help. --Byra

rod gervais4 November 2009#3
Byra time to think outside the box here...... why do you need an outer "ceiling" ? If you use a sissors truss - not only would you cover all your tracks for the outside building frame - but you would create a high spot in the center of the room where you could then install an LVL beam - your inside ceiling could have pitch to it so as to maximize ceiling height and increase room volume. Your inner wall height would only have to be 1" below the outer wall height - just offset your inner joist from the trusses themselves - there is no action on the walls other than the gravity load because an LVL beam will carry the ceiling load easily. Thus no need for collar ties to hold the walls in place. A win win situation........ Rod
