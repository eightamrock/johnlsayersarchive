I want to make my room less 'reverby'. I am not after making the room soundproof at this moment. Just want to make it acoustically better. I want to get such on the walls as shown on the picture(foam).
While applying foam products may decrease the high frequencies, more often than not, it leaves the room sounding somewhat "boxy." This is in part due to the fact the problematic low & mid-low frequencies are mostly unabsorbed by the foam and are still bouncing around the room. Excerpts from the SAE Recording Manual: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
For an instrument to sound good you must hear a balance of its high and low frequencies. If your room is too dead in the high frequencies the instrument sounds lifeless and tubby, whereas if it's too live in the high frequencies the instrument sounds distant and has no presence. To balance a room you must have a true balance of the high frequencies and the low frequencies with the right amount of deadness to liveness.
By using different construction techniques it is possible to treat the walls so they absorb sound at various frequencies.
Most home studio enthusiasts only seem to treat the high frequencies in the room yet it is the mid and low frequencies that cause all the room problems.
Attached are a couple of pics from the SAE site. The first pic is what I was listening to (and more than likely, so are you) before going with soffits and absorbers. Very tiring to listen to, let alone trying to mix in. The second pic is kind of what I'm shooting for, albeit in a modular sort of way. I opted for a front wall absorber instead of the window as suggested by Steve (Kinghtfly). Granted, the added expense and work involved may be somewhat daunting, but in my case at least, the end results more than justify the investment. I would encourage you to keep reading, asking and saving up for your project as best you can. Looking forward to your progress and pics as you get started. Aloha 8)
