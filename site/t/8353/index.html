Studio Will Be Next to a Classroom

Matt Rouch5 March 2007#1
Okay, this is my first post here. I’ve read quite a bit of the stuff on the REFERENCE sticky as well as the basic explanation of terms used. Here’s the situation (and I’m sorry if I have to be a bit ambiguous about some of the details, but certain things aren’t known yet): I teach at a university and, after many years of trying to get a space for a recording studio, some has finally been allocated. The performance room will be about 17 X 15, and there will be a control room that’s about 10 X 15. We can probably shave a couple feet off the width of the control room to make the length of the performance area 19 X 15 (and then the control room would be about 8 X 15). The height of the ceiling is unclear. This is because the second floor is about 18 feet above the first floor (and we’re on the first floor), but the building is a library that was renovated to be an academic building (classrooms and offices) about two decades ago so the ceilings on both floors were set at 9 feet with drop acoustical tile ceilings. We know we will be able to go higher than 9 feet, but we’re not certain exactly how high right now. Anyway, the studio is in a corner of the building and, unfortunately, there is a classroom that runs parallel to one of the longer walls. It is essential that we block as much sound as possible from going into the classroom, and it would be great to actually be able to record a drum kit in there while class was in session. Budget is another thing that’s unclear at the moment, but the folks who are managing the renovation (about a fifth of the building is being renovated – the studio is a relatively small part of that) are aware that a recording studio is going to have some special needs as far as soundproofing materials and construction. I don’t really have to worry about labor costs because the university’s construction team will take care of the project. Materials, however, are a different story. I’m quite confident the university would be willing to pick up the tab for AT LEAST 3 thousand (dollars) in specialized materials, but I plan to ask for much more (lower five figure range). I’ll have other questions as well as time goes by, but by Monday March 12 I have to present a list of materials needed for proper construction of the room, and the main dilemma right now is how to stop as much sound as possible from going to the classroom. I was wondering about construction techniques and materials, and I was particularly wondering about Auralex SheetBlok. What do people think of it? Are there cheaper alternatives? I know it would need to be used in combination with other layers, but how effective is it? Any insights would help. Sorry I can’t be more specific about ceiling height and budget. I’ll have more on those as time goes by. Matt
