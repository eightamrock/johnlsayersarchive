Soundman20201 March 2011#4
I imagine they would be good windows in CR.
Maybe not, as Glenn already pointed out: There isn't much mass in those panels. It is only equivalent to 1/2" of ordinary window glass. The idea with windows is that the glass must at least match the surface density of the rest of the wall. What are your walls made of? That is the factor that will decide what type of glass you need in your windows. If you use something that has less surface density, then your windows become the weak point, and all the sound will leak through there: Thus, you will have wasted a lot of money making good walls if you put bad windows in them.
Two of them separated by 2 x 2 timber. ... All together, 2 inch of perspex with 2 inch of air inbetween... fitted air tight of course.
Bad idea! A 2" air gap is nowhere near enough! Even if you had proper laminate glass of the same surface density as the rest of the wall, you would STILL need at least 8 inches of air gap between the two panes to get decent isolation.
How would this idea compare with double glazing? Sound Isolation wise
It does not compare at all. You will not get good isolation at all out of that plan: you don't have anywhere near enough mass in the perspex, and you don't have anywhere near enough air gap between them. The MSM resonance of that would probably be way up in the low-mids, so your wall would isolate terribly for the entire lower end of the spectrum. (I didn't do the math: just "guesstimating". You can do it yourself to confirm).
Rest of wall would be 2x4 (leaving 1inch for perspex).
It sounds to me like you are planing to build a fully coupled wall only 4 inches thick, with drywall on each side? If so, that will not give you decent isolation at all! Maybe 20-something dB, or perhaps 30-something at the very best. Even speech will be audible through that. Not what you need for a studio. Maybe you could post a diagram of the cross section of your wall? - Stuart -
