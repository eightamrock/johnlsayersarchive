gullfo19 October 2007#2
best bet to help us with design ideas - besides text answers - is to create a very accurate 2D floor plan. if you have enough skill to make it 3D with proper placement of windows, door etc... thats fine. you don't need to add a lot of materials and colors to it. your proposed control room looks square... this will be a problem for any number of reasons you can find by searching this site. given your budget you might find that taking some photos of the place, and using a Radio Shack sound level meter to measure the noise levels will help us pinpoint the right adjustments. things like the subway and other tenant noises, log it and try to determine the sources - windows, ceiling, etc.

gullfo8 November 2007#7
for non-rectangular rooms, the simpliest method for ratios is simply averaging the dimensions. since the modes will be skewed, you don't have sufficient control over the mode details by just calculating because your room boundaries (the hard walls) aren't rectangular and so you can only approximate. you could use SU and export to CATT and perhaps get some additional information but its only likely to lead to you use broadband treatments until you complete the build and then measure the actual room response and treat accordingly.
