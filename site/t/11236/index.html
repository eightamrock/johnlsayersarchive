So the first step was to dig out the old concrete slab, and replace with something thicker and of a different mix. Once that was done, all the walls were tarred and damp-proofed. While this stage was been completed, the small kitchen and toilet (off of the CR) were finished.

Ozzy21 September 2008#4
Once the new concrete was down and the walls tarred, the ceiling was seriously beefed up. We tried to short the two leaves (flooring/sub flooring - joist - plasterboard) with rigid fiberglass so we could still work with a 2 leaf system. All in all 5 layers of plasterboard was hung from the ceiling joists. We needed maximum mass as directly above the LR theres a shop, so we needed the best TL we could achieve. Along with the plasterboard on the ceiling, we boxed in the support pole with some fiberglass and 2 layers of plasterboard (fire regs apparently, but not sure it needed to be so big?!) In this pic, you can see the new floor, tarred walls and boxed in pole and support joist.
Image not preserved: PICT2143.JPG
Ozzy21 September 2008#5
The next step was to start the 2 floating floors for the LR and CR. The materials were spec'ed by a studio company, but it basically rubber strips with multiple layers of subfloor (of differing densities) on top. Between the 2" or so rubber, insulation was layed to damped any resonance etc. Not great pics, but if you look closely, you can see the gap around the edges of the floating floor with insulation poking out. Also note how low the ceiling sits in relation to the support joist now. Thats what 5 layers looks like lol!

Ozzy22 September 2008#13
Doors going in! Also, you can see the gap between the windows have been boxed in. A 10-15mm gap was left between everything, and the same between the airspace between the doors. When the rooms were plastered, the gaps were left (and checked, double checked etc). And once the plaster had dried, caulk (and in places backer-rod) was used to seal the gap without shorting any leaves). The doors were prehung presealed units that were custom made, rediculously expensive and even more rediculously heavy! One of the builders said he used to install security doors in bookies (betting shops), and this was the heaviest doors he ever felt! The door to the CR took 4 men to lift. In one pic, you can see that the door has shims holding it in to the wall. Obviously the doors shouldve been installed before plasterboard, but nothings perfect! We filled the space with perpendicular pieces of plasterboard, caulk, and then plastered over the gaps for a smooth finish.

Ozzy22 September 2008#22
Ok so once the rooms were finished up being decorated, having all the fittings finished and having a bloody good clean, they were measured. They were primarily measured for STC as the acoustics werent completed, although the RTC 60 was measured, but not really relavant. This is how it faired. CR to LR; STC - 55dB TL - 40dB LR to shop (the most important!) STC - 91dB TL - 75dB CR to shop same as above CR to street unmeasurable above ambient noise Service Noise NR10 8) Pretty happy with those! I wired the multicore, then the desk, monitors and (rediculously heavy) racks went in.
