knightfly7 September 2006#6
I'm still not clear on what you have so far; you mention outer steel walls and inner 2-layer walls. Is that it? Is the steel well-sealed, or just so-so? How much sound isolation do you need? The paper is probably going to be the LEAST of your problems, but would have some measurable effect on isolation IF the rest of your construction would comprise an actual mass-air-mass system; just not MUCH of an effect. However, a lack of DAMPING of your wall panels (from medium contact with the insulation) WILL be noticeable. We probably also need to discuss ceilings; apparently your wall frames rest on the main floor instead of on the inner floated one - but a more detailed sketch would sure help visualize what's going on here. Posting pix - not too tricky. DRaw something in Paint, save it as .jpg so it's small enough file size, click the Browse... button, navigate to your file and double-click it - then click the Preview button to see if it's what you expected. For multiple pix, navigate to the LAST one you want and work your way back to the FIRST one (5 maximum) - Browse to each, select it, then click the Add Attachment button and repeat... Steve

knightfly7 September 2006#8
If you're just using Paint, here are a few tips - Pick a color scheme and stick with it - I use the light brown for framing, light blue for gypsum, black for rubber (and borders of solids), light or dark gray for concrete or steel, green for fasteners or rebar, red for pointer lines, usually black for text, light or dark brown for dirt... You can build bordered solids by choosing the middle option in rectangle, ellipse, etc - then right click on the color you want for the fill, left click on the color you want for the border. IF you pick the LINE first, you can thicken your borders if you want. Then choose rectangle, and you'll get a thicker bordered, filled rectangle. Holding the Shift key while drawing a line forces it to be horizontal, vertical, or 45 degrees; useful in a long line to make it accurate. I usually set the background "soft" - click on the "A" or on the dotted rectangle, then choose the lower of the two colored block images that appear on the left. IF you want several items all alike, just draw ONE - then select it with the dotted rectangle tool, then [ctrl]c (for copy), [ctrl]v (paste) - while it's still got the dotted line around it you can move it anywhere on the drawing. IF you watch the coordinate display on the lower right, you can line up with another item and note the coordinates - then move to where you want, and one or the other coordinates will be the same when you're in the right position; then unclick the object and you're done. Note that these numbers will change each time you copy, pick up and "deliver" an object. There's a lot more, but I gotta go dig in my back yard... Steve
