knightfly16 August 2004#2
John, first off can you click on your profile and put a location down? I get tired of trying to remember where everyone is located, and answers aren't the same in all parts of the world - Second, is that the TOTAL amount of material you will be floating on rubber? Here is most of the info you need to figure out your specific application, except for some of the weights - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=839 There is both a method and some weights there - for your 23mm wallboard, just double the weight of the 1/2" gypsum. For the lumber - 460 cubic inches of wood weighs roughly 8 pounds, since its specific gravity runs between .50 and .55 for most species - so a 2x4, which is really 1.5" x 3.5", runs 206.6 cubic inches per meter - dividing 460 by 206 shows that a meter length of 2x4 would weigh roughly 2.2 pounds, or almost exactly 1 kG. Check out that link, and if it gets too difficult to figure out come on back - Regardless of what size and spacing you end up with, 1/2" EPDM rubber (60 Durometer hardness) is your best (not cheapest) bet for floor floating - typical life of 25 years compared to about 10 years for neoprene. Re-read the rest of that thread I linked to if you get time, there's quite a bit more there than just floors... Steve

Aaberg19 August 2004#4
If you can't pay regular high prices for EPDM, you can go to your local roofer and get scraps for a lot less. We did that, then cut the stuff into appropriate strips with a small pair of metal snips, stacked them up until we got the requisite half inch, and put them where needed. Aside from price, the advantage is that you can cut them to fit the situation. e.g. Under wall plates, we cut them the width of the plates so they don't wobble.
