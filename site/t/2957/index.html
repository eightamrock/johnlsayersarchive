knightfly15 January 2005#4
what next? The room drawing would be a good start; that way I can make some semi-informed blathering instead of total guesswork :? Are the front & rear wall absorbers in your diagram exactly the same? Which insulation should I use? Side wall & rear wall = 703? Corner traps = 703 & 705? They can be; the rears could also be slatted, with wide spacing between slats; but better NOT if the room is this small, and especially if you will end up putting your speakers on the LONG wall. In that case just plain 703 covered with cloth will be best. I would use this exclusively, and not worry about the slight differences with 705 - these are mainly for direct incidence, which rarely happens in a studio - for other angles, the 703 will catch up with and pass the 705 on a more practical basis. As far as the "not being nasty" part - It's taken me over 20 years messing with acoustics/sound proofing to get this stupid, so I don't expect others just getting into it to have either enough answers or enough questions; that's the point of this site, and I don't allow flames or other crap; this is a LEARNING place, not a war zone. Ask your questions, and all you better get is respectful answers; (some of them possibly even useful :=) BTW, welcome to the board... Steve

knightfly21 January 2005#6
No draft needed if you're moving :roll: If you want portable, I would build units no taller than 6'6" tall; standard doors are 6'8", this would give enough room to get them through most doors. Those units can be used in nearly any room, you could even put them on casters if the room you're using has doors in wierd places; then, when you're mixing and want an even sound field, you could just roll an absorber in front of any acoustic obstacle and "remove it" from the equation (mostly) - If you end up in the basement, a drawing would be one of the first things to do so we can visualize what needs done easier... Steve
