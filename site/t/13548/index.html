xSpace30 August 2009#9
A bedroom is limited for several reasons not the least in that it does not have the cubic footage to get the acoustic feel of a spacious area. You wind up being limited in what you can do, but it is the same thing that will be suggested for any room in general, it is mostly an "all you can do" proposition for a small room. Since so much was left out of your introduction, dimensions of the area, how much you will spend, who is doing the work, where you live geographically speaking etc, I will skip isolation (sound proofing) since it is more costly overall and requires the use of products specific to a region of the Earth, based on where you may live :) Your monitoring position will usually be in the narrow side of the room. If your room is 8 feet by 12 feet then you would have your monitors backed up to the 8 feet long dimension. Theoretically you would locate a place somewhere at 38 percent of the 12 feet back from the hard boundary your speakers are backed up to and attempt to establish an equilateral triangle with your two monitors and your head. This is mostly not even worth the effort in a small room but at least is a reference to let a person know not to get crowded up against the wall :) You have to locate the first reflection points in this acoustic environment and build broad band panels to be placed at these First Reflection Points to stop the reflections that the parallel walls are creating. I know they are parallel because it is a typical American bedroom and I am a typical American who has been in a lot of these bedrooms. You can easily establish these points in theory by installing a ceiling to floor 3 foot wide X 3 inch deep panel directly in front of you in the center of the wall and directly behind you in the center of the wall. Do the same thing to the left and right of your sitting position centered on your ears and a little toward the rear of the room. Build an overhead cloud, take up the carpet on the floor. It's there...don't argue with me ;) You can build corner bass absorption panels and install them in your, at the very least, front corners directly behind those monitors we talked about up topic. Maybe a few in the rear of the room will help, but I have no proof in a small room this is really worth the effort. Typical building insulation that you see at Lowe's or Home Depot will work for what you are doing.
