gullfo27 January 2008#11
now all you need is some of the Funk Brothers and your Motown room is complete :wink: "Standing in the Shadow of Motown" DVD - required viewing... twasn't the room to be sure... when you consider the 2nd floor - i'd make sure that no quiet spaces (like bedrooms) or high impact spaces (like exercise rooms) are over the live room. same for plumbing over the CR etc... this way you avoid disturbing folks in their sleep, or having thuds from dropped wights or running machines or dance practice from interrupting the next Yesterday recording. or having sewage overflow or burst water line wipe out your desk and racks... also fire protection from the downstairs... didn't realize you have to have in interior garage to qualify for a second floor... in the US many places need a minimum sq ft area for parking which is dependent on community bylaws (most times) to determine if a garage is needed and if it can be detached or not... if this is a commercial facility, then i'd also take into consideration any disabilities requirements for the floor plan, access, bathrooms etc. safety for occupants upstairs - separate egress routes if possible. i think the idea of having additional "control rooms" (like post prod or video edit suites) is a good idea and having the ability to split off the 2 live rooms/control rooms (i'd make the Motown room more capable than just Motown using reversible or changeable treatments) so you could have 2 projects going on at the same time without interference... that said, lot's of things to consider between 2 floors, commercial vs residence, and the odd shape of the building imposing itself on your layout...

gullfo2 February 2008#14
i like your latest one. seems very practical. nothing wrong with parallel walls, you just need to understand the options from construction, space utilization, treatments, etc. i see a lot of designs with non-parallel walls which work because of space restrictions but the trade off is often less treatment options and in small spaces that can be a hard choice. if there is space, i opt for parallel boundary walls to simplify construction and design analysis, then plan the treatments. if smaller spaces, angled walls can work to help some of the potential treatment issues (like flutter echoes) but room modes, rt60, etc all still need to be treated and so one way or another you should expect that treatments will occupy space, its often just a matter of where... if you have overhead space, then that can go a long ways towards alleviating issues relating to the floor plan...
