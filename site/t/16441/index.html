xSpace27 July 2011#6
I think I would glue it in place before I ran screws into it. An overhead cloud is not a generic piece and will be slotted or not depending on the rooms behaviour. The slotting reduces the hard reflective area and allows the absorption to be more effective. But typically and overhead cloud wants to be absorptive...not hard, to begin with. Sometimes a hard back is used in an attempt to break up room modes.
gullfo31 July 2011#7

Soundman20204 August 2011#12
Just curious, but why do you have the room (CR) oriented that way? Would it not be better to follow the more common method, and have the speakers firing down the long axis of the room, instead of the short axis? As it is now, your head is pretty much exactly in the geometric center of the room, on all three axes (length, width, height). That's about the worst possible place for it to be. It also means your speakers are going to be way out on the edges somewhere, in order to be far enough away from your head to be useful, and that probably puts them beyond a 90° intersect angle, which means a very, very tight sweet spot, and it even looks like your right ear wont be able to "see" the left speaker at all, and vice versa. So I guess there must be a really major overriding reason why the room is set up like that. Is this just meant to be only a basic tracking room, not intended for mixing, mastering or critical listening at all? - Stuart -

xSpace13 August 2011#19
You could use wire but the hardware required brings in more moving parts that could, over time, weaken the eyelets in either the joist above or the cloud frame below, to become a hazard. I want to say that most folks, that I have seen and I did mine this way as well, in a confined area, use heave duty (1/4 inch to 3/8 inch ) crown bolt turnbuckle hook / eye hardware. This bracket will maintain a strong connection and allows to "level" the cloud with the turnbuckle, while reducing moving parts and increasing load capacity.
