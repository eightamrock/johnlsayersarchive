The air space isn't the deciding factor of what is determined to be a leaf. It is when no connection is made, that decoupling is used between the one mass and the other mass that determines if a leaf exists or not. Although it is common to have walls sitting on the same floor, it[the floor] is not considered in the determining of what is a 2 leaf(mass/spring/mass) and what is not 2 leaf. It is often that the perimeter of existing buildings is used as part of the build, the first leaf in a two leaf design. If I had to guess at your existing brick facade, I would expect that it is basically coupled in many places via brick ties between the bricks and from the brick to the existing structure, so it would be looked at as one leaf due to be coupled in so many places. Most likely a very dense, heavy, really good start for something of this nature, so that is a good thing. Your ceiling is a matter of your design specifications so no one can say with any authority, do this or do that. If you had a budget that could support it, building a frame tall enough to accommodate an overhead framed ceiling with added substantial mass is, again, common place and not in the least bit unusual for a recording studio build. It always helps to give some dimensions and at least an idea of what you are wanting to do, even if it is scribbled on a napkin ;) Having some sound level reference is another piece of the puzzle that goes a long way in helping you to better understand what kind of noise is involved in your environment. It is also helpful with this sound level reference data that you can better explain to someone else what you are up against and there by get a better feel for how much sound has to be isolated against. It has been my experience that if your HVAC (heating and air) is not taken into consideration for your build from the very start, it will create problems that could have been avoided. It is certainly one thing among many, electrical feeds, audio cable runs, etc. but can devastate a budget or make the blind see ;)
