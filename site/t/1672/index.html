Greetings! Couple of observations...
in both rooms i have tried to deaden the cieling with insulation and 2" thick styrophome.
Whats that styro doing there? Chances are, not much for your sound. If you can replace it with rigid fiberglass insulation, you'll do much better. Closed cell insulation (which extruded styrofoam is) doesn't do much for acoustics.
is it a problem to have my monitors set sideways in the room so the angle of the cieling is going lowest from my left to highest at the right when mixing,(basically sideways in the room)
You'd have to have your ceiling awfully dead for it to not affect the sound. Even so, you'll have better sound with the speakers firing down the long axis of the room (just because first reflections off the back wall will take longer to get back to you, and be weaker when they get there). If they ARE firing down the long way, but the ceiling is sloped sideward, then you're stuck with a choice of evils. Having your ceiling deadend to high and mid freqs doesn't mean it's dead to lows. These won't affect your stereo imaging quite as much, but they will. You could mix the whole thing in mono, then create the stereo spread and check it on headphones, I suppose... As far as your live room, I wouldn't worry too much about golden dimensions. Chances are you're close enough. If you want to cut down flutter echoes without deadening the room, what you've proposed will work. There's dozens of solutions for that kind of thing rolling around on these boards, so pick the one that works best for you. I personally would look for the one that takes up the least floor space, but that's 'cause I have a small studio... Hope this helps! Kase www.minemusic.net
