Soundman202015 November 2012#5
there is a girder sustained by two walls. I already planned to get rid of these
:shock: :!: DON'T! That is a structural support girder. I'm not a structural engineer, but it seems pretty obvious that a huge girder like that is holding up more than just the bit of wall above it. I suspect ot is holding up the entire roof! If so, and if you try to remove that girder and the wall, then the roof will come down on top of you. Do NOT do any such modifications unless you first get the building checked by a qualified structural engineer, who approves your modifications in writing. - Stuart -

Soundman202026 November 2012#9
I think you're not getting many replies because you have such an unusual situation! As you already know, that curved ceiling is basically a lens that focuses sound, so that is going to be really hard to deal with. I don't think it can be done just with treatment: If that were my room, I think what I would do is to build a frame to somehow suspend a new ceiling below that, something like a very large hard-backed cloud, that runs from one side of the room to the other. The question is at what height do you put it? If you put it down low enough so that it covers a large part of the curve, then it might make the room too low. But if you put it up high then it wont be wide enough, and part of the ceiling/wall will still be curved. You will also need a lot of bass trapping in there, and with most rooms the bass trapping goes in the vertical corners, but you don't have any vertical corners! That's a real challenge to get right! I'm having a hard time imaging how it might be treated to get it to sound good. Do you know the radius of that curve? Is it circular or oval? - Stuart -
