AVare2 January 2009#9
Soundman2020 wrote:
How do you calculate the resonant frequency for the entire wall system? I know how to do it for a single leaf, but how do you do it for the entire 2-leaf system: I guess it involves masses of the walls, density, air gap volume, and fill material?
The equation looks much simpler in traditional form, but here it for those interested: Fc=c[(m1+m2)/(m1m2d)]^.5 where: Fc=resonance frequency (Hz) c=constant (60 for empty gaps and 43 for with insulation) m1=mass of first leaf (kg/m^2) m2 mass of second leaf (kg/m^2 d=interleaf spacing (m) For imperial units the constants are 170 and 120. The reason for two different constants is that no insulation, the air acts adiabatically. With insulation isothermally. Sharp ha several pages devoted to it starting on pdf page 35. Interestingly Sharp uses the physics phrase "mass-spring-mass" while Warnock uses the acoustics phrase "mass-air-mass" in IR 586. You might recall the thread where someone wrote that they could not find either in search of acoustics documents. I found it amusing that both showed doing search for a good text for you read more on it. Andre

Soundman202013 January 2009#15
Will vacuum act as a spring?
Interesting question! I don't think so, since basically "vacuum" means that there just isn't anything there at all! The reason sound cannot cross a vacuum is because there is no medium to carry it.
And will the 2 Sides of mass on each side of the vacuum act as one leaf or 2 leafs?
It is a different principle, so I don't think you can talk about "leaves" at all in this case. The two sides of the container are just that: two sides of a container. Glass block bricks are good isolators for this reason: they contain a near-vacuum. The only path for sound is through thick glass envelope around the "vacuum", but glass itself is a good insulator: massive, dense, etc. I hope you are not seriously considering evacuating your walls, Mika! Soundman202014 January 2009#17
Instead of 2 heavy thick glassboards i can use 2 of these "light" vacuum Glassboards.
Light vacuum glass? Can you show a link to a web pages that shows what you are thinking of? I've never heard of that. Are you thinking of double-pane glass, also called "thermopanel" in some places, as well as other names? Basically, two sheets of thin glass separated by a small gap, manufactured as one unit? If that's what you are thinking of, then there there is no vacuum in between. The pressure might be reduced a little, and they might be filled with an inert gas, but it is not enough of a vacuum to make a big difference for sound. Those windows are good thermally, and they do stop a fair bit of sound, but not as much as you need. In any case, thin glass vibrates way too easily, and the resonant frequency will be up in the range where you don't want it! To get a good acoustic studio window, you need the same as for your wall: Mass - Air - Mass. In other words, thick heavy laminate glass, a large air gap, and more thick heavy laminate glass. - Stuart -
