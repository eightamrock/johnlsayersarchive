MagnusP29 August 2006#1
Hi everyone! I'm thinking about building a side wall absorber in each side of little vocal booth with the dimensions: lenght: 1,5 m, width: 0,7 m and height: 2,1 m. The little room is at this moment isolated 100mm rockwool insulation, wallboards and pyramid foam, but that makes my sound very dark, since this construction only keeps down the high frequencies - not something I prefeer. So I need a new construction, that can help me keep down all the frequencies and give me good acoustic for recording vocals. I have three questions pertaining to this: 1. Is it even a good idea to use this construction(side wall absorber) in such a small room? 2. Is it okey of the wall is only displaced 100mm instead of 200mm, since I do not have that much space? 3. How do I make sure that the unit is air sealed correctly? I should might add that I have no possibility of changing the dimensions of the room.. :( Hope that some of you can help me with this, and that way help me to get a better studio :D - Magnus
len-morgan31 August 2006#2
Would it be possible to remove the pyramid foam and wallboard from the inside walls? If isolation is not a problem, you could then frame up a "thickness" extender so your total wall thickness would be 200mm (100mm where the rock wool is now and 100mm with the new wall. Then stuff the entire cavity you've created with rockwool and cover the walls with cloth. If isolation IS a problem, you could also remove the rockwool and "beef up" betwen the studs of the outside walls (from the inside) like Sharward did on his garage (maybe two additional layers of 5/8" drywall calked against the studs), build out the new extender framing, and put the rockwool back and continue as above. Just a thought. len

knightfly7 September 2006#4
With that narrow a booth, if you don't need much isolation but DO need better sound, I'd be tempted to remove the inner wallboard altogether; then put your insulation flush with the frame and cover with cloth. Before covering, try out the sound and if it's too dark you can first cover parts of your insulation with light gauge plastic (about .05mm) until you like the sound, then cover with cloth. If you're concerned with damaging the cloth or insulation in such cramped quarters, you might want to find some fairly open metal mesh to fasten across the face of your frames... Steve
