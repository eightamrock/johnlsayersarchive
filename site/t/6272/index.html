tmix21 May 2006#2
We will probably need more info from you. If your object is to make it quieter inside to record vocals and soft acoustic sources, then yes , putting insulation in the walls and a second sheet of 5/8ths will help with sound transmission some. but if you are asking if it will do anything for supressing the sound transmission of a guitar amp through the wall or the sound of a lawnmower outside migrating through the wall, then I say it won't help very much. As for your question as to whether it will make a big difference in the way the room sounds... no it wont. To change the "sound of a room, you need to be looking into sound absorption ideas . Beefing up the walls basically serves to "Sound Proof" your room , meaning it will deal with the sound trying to get in and out of the room. The acoustics of the room sound are dealt with by wall design and sound absorption. Let us know more of what your goals are for the room. Tom

knightfly21 May 2006#5
In addition to following the forum guidelines linked by Keith, a correction - Tom, actually changing your walls WILL change the room sound - single 1/2" wallboard on studs acts as a panel bass absorber. Either adding more mass to its surface OR filling with blown in insulation will CHANGE that panel absorber - the insulation will widen the "Q" of the trap as well as lessen the effect, and additional mass on the studs will lower the effective trap frequency... Steve
