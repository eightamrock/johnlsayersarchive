lovecow5 February 2004#11
KK,
midiman123 wrote:
What about this then: 1: Pyramide foam on pegboard up in the ceiling, slightly angeled. (But how deep down from ceiling?)
Minimum spacing from the ceiling should be about 1". Maximum would be about 6". Angling should be such that the "front" edge of the panel (closest to the corner) is the lowest (biggest gap from the ceiling) and the "back" edge (furthest from corner) is closest to the ceiling.
2. Build/Buy 2 Basstraps, one behind desk, and one in the back of the room(covered with pyramide foam?)
Assuming you just want passive traps, those should work well. Though, you can always use more if you find extra space in the room. :D
3. I have already built a 3 frame on wheels(150x170cm) that I plan to cover with Pyramide foam, so that I can move it around and use it for vokal booth. Maybe when mixing, I can put it like this on the drawing?
Mobile treatment is never a bad thing. You will probably find good spots and not so good spots for such a device.
4. Cover some walls with strategically placed 4" pyramide foam.
OK. But don't go overboard. As I've said before: "Foam doesn't kill rooms; people kill rooms."
(By the way, the wardrobe´s is made from a thin, spraypainted reflective wood material(Peg board?). should I cover it?)
It can't hurt. But I wouldn't worry about covering all of it. Just the parts that might reflect sound back to the mix position. Also, you might be able to position your "gobo" just right so that you don't have to cover it at all.
-And, Is it ok to spray paint acoustic foam?
Most definitely not. Paint of any sort fills the pores of absorbers, reducing its acoustical effectiveness dramatically. Even painting absorbers that claim they can be painted is not a good idea. Best regards, Jeff D. Szymanski Chief Acoustical Engineer Auralex Acoustics, Inc.
