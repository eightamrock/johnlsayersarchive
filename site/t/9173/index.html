gullfo2 August 2007#2
you might want to review some of the projects in the construction forum where folks have been beefing up rooms to achieve better isolation. also Rod Gervais has an excellent book on home studio construction which deals significantly with cost effective isolation techniques. besides Rod's book, the other important bit is to get yourself a sound level meter and determine what level of isolation you need by getting your drummer and bassist to kick it and do some sound level checks inside and out. since its a stripped out garage, you have plenty of options to build up the outer walls, roof, and wall off the existing garage door as a good start. you will also need to consider ventilation and cooling/heating as part of the design...

gullfo12 August 2007#5
i'd make sure the walls are properly bolted into something substantial and not just resting on blocks... assuming the structure is sound enough (contact a structural engineer to review) then you could potentially add 5/8 drywall between the rafters to beef the exterior up top as well as the walls. then adding a new interior ceiling (you could use the overhead space for AC and air exchange equipment) should give you decent isolation. if you skip the in-between drywall and just add 5/8 over the rafters, then you potentially have a third leaf but it may be your only option and you may have to live with it... if you can remove the garage door and frame it with a single door that would be best. otherwise just build an isolating wall inside it and leave it in place - with the amount of air gaps and lower mass its not likely to contribute too much to third leaf but you would want to add insulation to dampen any resonances...

gullfo14 August 2007#10
victimeiced wrote:
thanks gulfo, all walls appear to be bolted down into the blocks. do you think i should fill in those block gaps with something? or will it not matter, since it'll be in the air gap space between outer and inner walls?
if you're building an inner wall then you can probably leave the blocks the way they are since they're not contributing to isolation or support for the inner walls. as far as isolation - if building an inner room, don't add a layer of drywall to the outside studs as you'll end up with a third leaf - you could put drywall between the studs to beef up the wall but not an air gap there.
