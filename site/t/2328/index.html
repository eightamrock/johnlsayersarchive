knightfly15 September 2004#5
Yeah, I know - that's part of why I didn't think these would work very well at lower frequencies - I noticed they are very careful to ONLY use STC, and not give actual TL values at different frequencies - even though these are rated at STC 46 or so, they wouldn't perform as well at lower frequencies as the same system with two single panes, making a true mass-air-mass barrier - if you put two panes of single glass in a window with 90mm space between, using 6mm glass on one side and 9mm on the other, you also end up with STC 46 - the difference is, instead of your TL dropping to about 18 or 19 at 50 hZ, it's up around 25-26 dB. That triple leaf thing just isn't as efficient at lower frequencies, and can't be. OTOH, Lee's happy with his - I can't help wondering how much more he'd like them if they had one pane of thicker glass instead of that insulated pane... Steve
rod gervais16 September 2004#6
My guess would be that you are not going to be happy with these for control room windows. A window used to isolate the outside world is a different animal than that used for your control room. Although you may get your STC 45 - you are going to get very little in the low frequencies. So if your bass and drums (Toms and Bass) are combined at somewhere around 95to 100 db....... the 80 to 85 db (rough estimate) you hear in your control room is going to sound pretty loud. Personally I would save my money and do it the conventional way. It's got to be a whole lot cheaper with better results for the same s.f. of window area. Rod

I wouldn't splay the glasses unless glare from lights or early reflections back to the mix position will be affected - and look for a source of EPDM rubber, NOT neoprene - the difference is about 10 year life vs. 25 years. - 1/4" is good for windows - too thick and you may actually amplify instead of decouple the glasses...
