Green Glue vs. Soundboard

Started by kindohm on 9 November 2006. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 7468.

kindohm9 November 2006#1
I'm in the process of getting estimates from drywall contractors to hang drywall in my basement - including my studio room. I explained to the guy coming to do the estimate that I wanted to use Green Glue between two layers of drywall on the walls in the studio. He's trying to get me to use soundboard (one layer of soundboard and one layer of drywall) instead because that's what he and his crew are familiar with. Plus, for me it'd be a lot cheaper than using Green Glue. GreenGlueCompany.com shows that Green Glue performs better than soundboard... by about 5 STC units if I remember correctly. Have any of you had experience with both Green Glue and soundboard? Any thoughts/opinions on which you'd rather use? I know I can't let this guy tell me what to do because I need to make sure I make the right decision for my studio... but I'm just curious about what anyone else thinks.
sharward9 November 2006#2
Your contractor's lack of familiarity with Green Glue doesn't make up for the crappiness of the product for which he is familiar. Print the instructions from the Green Glue Web site (there's a PDF version that is very print-friendly) and make him not only read it but commit in writing to following it. It's very easy to do. I've done it. Also, while you're there, read their article on STC and what it means -- and most importantly, what it doesn't mean. In a studio context, it doesn't mean squat. --Keith :mrgreen:
Creature8210 November 2006#3
kindohm - Most contractors do not want to change their ways. . "We always do it this way." . "You don't need that." <- my personal favorite . "I've never seen anybody do that way in twenty years." I was reading Rod's book and he had a small part on contractors and attitudes. Basically it says that if they don't buy into your project and doing it the right way, then don't use that contractor! I couldn't agree with him more. You will probably alienate at least two-thirds of the contractors you talk to by insisting on using the Green Glue (or whatever special requirement you have).. and it will probably cost you more money to get the right contractor. But if you stick to your guns, you will be doing the right thing. I'm not as technically knowledgeable as some here, but soundboard is not as dense as drywall; therefore I think a dampening layer like Green Glue and more drywall makes more sense to me. I believe that when soundboard is used it is usually applied directly to studs and before the drywall. It does two things: 1) adds more mass than if it weren't there, 2) acts a little like a slight shock absorber / decoupler for certain frequencies (I think the higher ones). I believe it would be redundant in a system that uses decoupling (e.g. staggered studs, or RC channel, or double wall, etc.) Creatch
