Self evaluation: Studio design knowledge 3/10 Understanding of acoustics 3/10, but I'm learning Construction skills 7.5/10 Willingness to try new things, even unproven 8/10 (I could tell you all about my house, but that's for another day...) My average loudness while recording in the space, maybe 4/10-7/10 (?) Some details, then questions. Shell: The outer shell of the studio is 17' 6" X 24'. The walls are 8' high, standard 2 X 4 construction, 24" O.C. The inside of the roof is a 6:12 pitch, rising from 8'2" to 12'4 1/2" On the bottom left corner is the door to the studio. It goes to a set of stairs down one flight. The room is connected to the house via the wall on the RIGHT side of the drawing Floor: 3/4" douglas fir T and G flooring, on 2 X 12 joists, 16" O.C. on 3 1/2" X 12" glue lam beams, on posts 12' apart. Part of the floor is cantelevered, so the actual span on the joists is 15'. Room 1: Likely used as the control room, as well as a recording space, is symmetrical, and is 13'3" X 16', with an average ceiling of 10' 2", for about 2100cubic feet(I'm guessing on the loss of those short angled walls at the back of the room) 1:1.3:1.57 Room 2: The other, smaller room, is 9'2" X 16' 1", with the same average ceiling of 10'2" for a volume of about 1500 Cubic feet. Two skewed walls.1:1.1:2.59 The "main" room, when opened up: My intention is to be able to open up the double doors, and have it behave much like one large room. Then the dimensions of that large room become about 16' X 23' X 10' 2" Ratios I've calculated all the room modes and there does not seem to me to be any really bad frequency pile ups. Soundproofing, neighbours: I live way out in the country, 1000 feet from a dead end road. The nearest neighbour is 1000 feet away. So I probably don't need to go nuts on the soundproofing to the outside world. Uses: I record mostly just me for now, creating drums and other instruments in Reason, I then play guitar and record vocals. I have been told that the very best solution to my space, acoustically, is to keep it as one giant room. But I would like to expand to doing small projects for others, and I would like to be able to bring in, say, a drum kit, duo, or trio, or even have my amp wailing away in the next room and be able to more or less tell what the mic is actually picking up, if you know what I mean i would likely have the double doors open most of the time, and use it more or less as one large room, but then if necessary, I could close them, and separate them. I understand that I'm not going to get really great transmission loss wiht the double doors, especially because there will be glass involved, but I think it will be a decent compromise. Aloha and welcome to the forum Keith :D A couple of initial thoughts if I may: 1 - There are several threads around here regarding building a studio above a garage, and more specifically, on an upper level floor. One of the main concerns is in regard to the increased weight of a studio build (2-3 layers of drywall/sheetrock/gypsum per leaf, acoustical treatments, insulation, studio equipment, instruments, musicians, etc) that may exceed the existing structure design. It would be a good idea to consider evaluating your structure's ability to support the additional weight. 2 - In regard to the front wall - will you be soffit mounting your monitors? There are numerous threads detailing the advantages of soffit mounting. 3 - There have been designs using sliding glass doors between the control room/live room that would facilitate your visual and open up the rooms. Perhaps a "search" on the above topics will prove fruitful in your design stages. Be sure to check out the "Stickies" in the Reference areas of the Design, Construction & Acoustic forums, as well as the "Other Studios" section for lots of great info. Looking forward to your design process and build! Aloha 8)
