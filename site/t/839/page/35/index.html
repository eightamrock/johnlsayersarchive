Aaronw, Eh, repeating what every one else has said, looks good. 8) All this talk of conduit thought I throw this in... Last night a guitar player friend on mine was on the same gig location, we got to talking electrical stuff and about properly bending conduit. He's been working part time doing commercial installations. What I found real interesting of what he said about conduit runs: Apparently you can not have more then 360 degrees of turn/bend in a single conduit run, if you need to do more than 360 degrees of turn then a J box has to be installed (in an accessible location), wires spliced in box and you then can bend another 360 degrees in 2nd run of conduit past J box. Reason is to not damage the wire when pulling through to many bends and ease of pulling. I know I’m going to sort through the NEC to verify this one for residential installs, BUT my friend said it does not matter what voltage line you’re running, no more then 360 degrees of bend for safety sake. :roll: Marc

Aaronw29 September 2006#863
First, is what prompted you to use steel framing instead of wood?
Several reasons. 1: When I started the project, I was getting a lot of it free, and it was also cheaper than wood. (3 years ago). 2: Moisture. The studio is in a basement. 3: It's a heck of a lot easier to work with than wood. 4: Sound transmission. 5: And a few others I can't think of at the moment.
Second, is that I've heard using RC on steel, or with separate leaf framing is not necessary... Am I seeing that use here, or is there something I'm missing?
With 25 quage you don't need it. It's flimsy enough. With 20 AWG (beginning of structural studs) it's recommended.

Most of the control framing, I got free. With the new construction I've done, I'm really not too concerned with moisture (or mold for that matter), for three reasons: 1: The new construction sits 4 1/2" away from the block wall all the way around the entire basement. 2: I run a dehumidifier 24/7. Part of the design I included into this with the 4 1/2" away, and the location of the dehumidifier, it pulls the moisture out from all the way around the structure. 3: I'm using mineral fiber, which, supposedly, doesn't promote mold growth.
