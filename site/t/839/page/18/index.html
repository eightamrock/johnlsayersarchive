Pantalaimon, glad your construction has been helped by this; now, if you could do US a favor by following as many of the highlighted points http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3231 as possible, particularly editing your profile to include a general location? Thanks. As to replacing the fiberboard with neoprene, NO-NO - around a window, you need to let it BREATHE into/out of the wall cavity insulation - this helps damp the glass panes, and allows moisture to escape as well. The fiberboard mentioned is much more permeable than neoprene, which does NOT pass air OR moisture. Also, I would minimise splaying the glasses to what you need for reflection control (sound reflections that is) - because the narrower you make either the bottom or top gap in a window, the more you reduce the isolation for sound. See the following - http://www.audio-muziek.nl/audiotechniek/acoustics.pdf Hope this helps... Steve

Aaronw30 June 2005#441
Hi Hugo, There were some reason's (after thought) as to why and the construction ended being built. If I had the chance to do over, There would be a couple things I would do differently. Here is how things are constructed now: The floating floor is under the entire control room (walls, soffits, treatment, etc.). I then built the walls on top of the floating floor. The top of the walls are supported by brackets I made to support the walls and to help decouple or isolate as much as possible any hard connections to the existing structure of the house. I then installed the ceiling above on RC. The entire front section (the bass traps & soffits) are completely self supporting; in that it is only connected to the floating floor. There is no contact at all w/ any of the framing to the side walls or ceiling. At this point in the construction, I decided to set up a couple of stands w/ my monitors to see what was happening in the room. I found there was some structural resonance w/ the control room, so I asked Steve (Knightfly) for some suggestions. After telling him the details of the floor contruction, he felt I needed to add some weight to the control room. So this is partially the reason for the concrete block stands. ( I wish I could have had them go all the way down to the slab below instead of the floor). The stands provide a few solutions for my scenario. It adds weight to the floor so it achieves the needed "spring" for isolation, it helps the monitors by being on a "solid" base (which by the way should add 3db from what I learned in college), and in theory should help w/ the bass trap (that's my guess). Construction of the stands include: 8x8x16 concrete block (hollow in the middle). Mortered. Filled w/ sand, then topped off w/ 4x8x16 solid concrete blocks. Some of them I had to make a frame around and top them off w/ concrete mix to make them all the same height. I cut a piece of 3/4" MDF (basically for a shelf) for the monitor to sit on. Since the block was sitting back too far, I needed it to come out for the monitor to be flush w/ the front wall. On the MDF, I used a paddle bit (1") or so, and drilled out part of the MDF so the screws could be "countersunk" below the level of the speaker. I used concrete screws (Tapcon's) to attach the MDF to the block. But, first I added a small piece of 1/4" rubber where the screws would go through between the concrete and the MDF. This was mostly to help w/ some added isolation and reduce structural resonance. There are 4 mounting points on each piece (screws that hold the MDF down). I also used washers that already had neoprene attached to them (purchased that way) for the top side of the MDF. So basically you have the following order: Concrete screw, neoprene/steel washer, MDF, 1/4" rubber, and then the block. Then I have straps attached to the sides of the block. I attached these by burning a hole in the end of it w/ a soldering iron, used fender washers and screwed it to the sides. The construction of the front wall frame is: 2x4 framing except the front section the plywood attached to. It is 2x6 construction. The entire framing is filled w/ Roxul Mineral Fiber. I made the hangers and hung them inside. Then put the 2 layers of 3/4" plywood. Cut out the ports (that I forgot to cut before hand :oops: ) for ventilation. The monitors I'm using are powered and rear ported, so I didn't create a box for them. I need them to breathe as much as possible. I may create a plenum return (or similar) above the ceiling treatment and this will suck the hot air out of the soffits. :D
