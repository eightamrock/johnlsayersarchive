knightfly23 January 2004#101
Yeah, the tee nuts are what I drew in the last pic. The two smaller pieces of wood on either side are "nailers" that would nail into the joist. Reason being that if you drill holes in the joist itself, it will change the span that's possible with that size lumber by weakening the joist. Using "nailers" on each side would still center the load but not weaken the joist. An alternative might be to alternate sides every few feet to cut down on parts count, but I'm not sure that's a good idea. That price is hard to beat for the levelers - you could probably just glue them onto your pucks and be set... Steve

knightfly29 January 2004#113
Right - the main function of the "perimeter board" is to keep a floated floor from shifting enough to contact the walls and "short out". Not sure, but I think it's more important when floating a concrete floor, since wood construction would be easier to keep from accidental contact - 703 would be too porous to also act as a seal though - so if you need sealing, you'd be better using something like homosote/celotex and caulking well... Steve
