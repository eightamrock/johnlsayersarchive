Hi everyone, i just found this forum 2 days ago and have been reading like crazy over all the knowledge that's available. Hence i've been motivated to look at treating my room properly so i can use it as my studio/bedroom. I'm 19 and i'm from Australia, I've been recording for about a year but haven't been too excited about the results. Like you all say the room is the starting point. And i really want to get it sounding good so i can get on to making music. So here goes nothing. Room Usage Like i said, one room will be used for recording and mixing Noise Built a new computer about 3 months ago specifically for recording, no other ambient noise, so it's almost silent. The Room The room measures 276cmx360cm (LxW) with ceilings at 240cm and carpeted floor. I don't have the most trained ears but there is certainly a boom quality when playing the guitar in any direction. Budget Budget is $500Aus. (Roughly $430 US) I'm really not confident enough to go DIY so i'm just looking at premade stuff i can access here in Australia. Fonic is currently front runner, specifically their wall tiles and low/mid traps. Here and Here. I've uploaded two sets of plans i drew up, all the furniture is to scale, can't really spin the room around because the wardrobe is too reflective and can't be treated. My thinking is i need to minimise reflection from the monitors, hence the two sets of six tiles opposite. I also face the computer when recording so i suppose the panels in front and behind of a mic will reduce unwanted room reflections/echoes. Now the corner traps aren't really true bass traps cause, A) they're much more expensive and B) obviously larger. which isn't going to work for this small room. By the looks of things these are still going to be somewhat effective. Some data from Fonic Here Questions 1) are the tiles better of bunched up like the picture or some spacing? 2) do i need to consider treating the ceiling in any way? As i say my budget is fairly restricted, and the amount i've used on the plans is basically what i can afford and access in Australia. Thanks for reading, I appreciate any input anyone can offer.

gullfo20 September 2007#2
i'd put one of the corner traps over the mix wall-ceiling corner, and space out the tiles and put some overhead on the ceiling over the mix position. the windows may cause reflections - so maybe some heavy drapes to pull over them when mixing/recording. another option is building the traps/absrobers yourself - compare and determine.
