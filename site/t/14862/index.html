gullfo27 September 2010#2
since you might not have any options to do any serious level of construction, you might want to do a few things before signing a lease: 1) find out explicitly what you can or cannot do with the walls and ceiling 2) do some sound level measurements to see if the isolation is any good. if you're limited in #1 and the isolation turns out to be no good enough, then you may be stuck with a lease and a unusable space...

gullfo27 September 2010#4
keep in mind the conference room isolation may have been defined for the normal STC range (125hz and up - about half of the frequency range in your spreadsheet...) and for drums and bass this could be problematic. at a practical limit, unless the floor as been correctly floated and decoupled from the building, you probably would not be able to get more than 40db isolation before flanking becomes the predominate level. this may be more of concern for sound going in than out but it also depends on your neighbors in the building as well... i'm assuming the area with the doors and tile floor is the hallway and the space you can use if the 12x32 space?
