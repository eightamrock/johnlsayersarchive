I'm fairly certain that conventional wisdom here is that the best results are achieved by floating the room. Imagine gravity not being an issue, with a smaller box within a larger box, with no part of the inner box touching the outer box. That's the ideal. Unfortunately, that pesky gravity thing always ruins the fun. So, while it's "somewhat" easy to keep the inner ceiling from touching the outer ceiling, and the inner wall from touching the outer wall, everything has to "touch down" to something, and hopefully that gravity point of contact is cushioned with something resilient, such as EPDM rubber. Now, imagine floating a floor on rubber pucks, firmly attaching the inner walls to the floating floor, and attaching the inner ceiling to the inner walls. That, I believe, is pretty much the closest thing to the levitating box I rambled about in the preceding paragraph. Bolting your inner walls to the outer floor would be in the category of "not ideal" I think. Bolting your inner walls to the floor with rubber cusions and washers would also probably not be ideal. knightfly2 April 2005#37
I think I'm actually getting LESS confused by all this; sounds like you're getting close to figuring everything out more or less. On the "leaf" thing - even 4 feet of air space won't eliminate the extra leaf's negative effect on isolation, just lessen it a bit. Sometimes this just can't be helped; in those cases, all I can suggest is to put an extra layer or two of drywall on the two OUTSIDE leaves, that will lessen the 3-leaf "badness" by a few dB; I've no way to calculate how much. If your inner, OLD, garage door framed wall has to have paneling on it, AND it is in contact with your OUTER framing, then that paneling should have RC under it; there's no other way to isolate that paneling so it doesn't flank sound directly to the outer framing and outside. Of course hermetic sealing with good acoustic rated caulk is part of it. That front section, if you panel parts of it, can make a resonant chamber if part is open to the rest of the space and part is paneled (like the upper section) - this could work to advantage if you were to absorb the living s*** out of it and make it into a bass trap. Otherwise not so good. Anyplace you have two opposing surfaces, even if there is nothing sealing or even connecting them to each other, you WILL have a resonant system. This is still true even if you bore 4" diameter HOLES in one or both of the panels; just not as bad as if it were solid. Generally; if a frame is hard connected to both the inner AND the outer leaf of your "containment shell", or mass-air-mass envelope, then you MUST decouple the wallboard on at least one side - if both sides of this non-decoupled frame will be clad, and BOTh sides will be wall surfaces of adjoining rooms, then this would be a rare case where BOTH sides of the frame should have RC or RSIC clips/hat channel; the idea here is to have NO inner wallboard that is hard-coupled to anything that is ALSO hard-coupled to the OUTER leaf. Period. Floating floors - anything LESS than concrete will be a compromise. That's about as simple as I can put it; for effective, FULL FREQUENCY isolation, you need LOTS of MASS and LOTS of insulated AIR space and proper decoupling between that mass and the surroundings. At today's prices for sheet goods, concrete is a BARGAIN - I recently costed out two floated floor systems (theoretical) - the one using wood floor cost more than the concrete version, and had about 3 OCTAVES less isolation on the low end, and less (calculated) isolation throughout the audio band. 3.5" concrete floor, for just the concrete, will cost just under $1 per square foot; MDF or OSB in the thicker sheets costs about $.70 a square foot, and you need THREE LAYERS to be 1/3 as good as the concrete so the concrete will cost about HALF as much for MUCH BETTER isolation. Replace Keith's bricks with a PT 2x4 perimeter frame on 3/4 OSB on PT 2x6's on 1" EPDM, fill that 2x4 perimeter with concrete, (stuff the 2x6 joist cavities with insulation first), and you have a floor that will isolate any frequency you can hear, for almost a buck less per square foot than a "wooden wannabe" - Hope this helped some; lemme know if I missed much, or just confused the issue more :roll: Steve
