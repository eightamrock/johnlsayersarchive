sharward10 April 2005#53
Your garage is pretty old and was (and, for the moment, still is) "unfinished" inside: all the timber was exposed for you to see. As a result, there is no "attic" per se. (Right?) If you do the enclosures you're talking about, you'll have an attic of sorts. The attic will need to be ventillated, just as all attics are (or should be). Ventillation is pretty much the opposite of sealed, and in order for a leaf to be effective in a soundproofing situation, the leaf must be sealed. You're kind of stuck between a rock and a hard place: stuff needs to be air-tight, yet it needs to be ventillated. Thus, you look for a solution that gives you ventillation without compromising your soundproofing too much. In my design, I have an inner leaf ceiling (which I haven't built yet), an outer leaf ceiling (which is the current garage ceiling that I intend to "bulk up" with an extra layer of rock), and a roof. The roof is, essentially, a third leaf. However, the fact that the attic is ventillated mitigates the third leaf effect as far as resonance goes and may dampen sound somewhat as far as TL is concerned. (At least, that's what I recall from my conversation with Steve -- I may be wrong on some of the details, but the conclusion is the same.) In your design, it appears that you have an inner ceiling (which you haven't built yet) and a roof. If the roof is ventillated (such as a turbine vent on top, or soffit vents around the outside), then the roof may not be an effective outer leaf. If the roof is not ventillated, then you have an illegal unventillated attic that would probably cause major problems for you down in the not too distant future. So far you haven't indicated how you'd gain access to the attic (perhaps there may be a way to have an access door in the "nook" area). I know, it's a major buzzkill, but I'm going to say it: Permit. I'm dealing with the headaches of complying with building code myself as I type this. Have you considered hiring John to design your studio for you? I wonder if all of these factors would be taken into consideration in that effort. Hope that helps. :mrgreen:
knightfly12 April 2005#54
Mike, no you don't have trusses; you have separate, "stick built", rafters - every second rafter has a "collar tie", those are the horizontals near the peak. Running full length of the building at the peak, is a Ridge Board - into which each rafter is fastened. This is how older construction was done; if done right, it held up pretty good, but trusses are stronger with smaller lumber required. These are trusses; the entire assembly is called a Truss, and each piece of wood within the assembly is called a Chord; the uppermost chords on each side are also usually called Rafters. The truss's strength comes from engineered geometry such that each section of the truss makes a triangle; as long as no fasteners come loose, one of the sections of the triangle would have to break before the shape can change much. Notice that each truss, although different, has that "triangle thing" goin' on. Here are a few more terms. Thought some definitions of terms might help clear some of the confusion... Steve

Mike, as usual my s**t-box house has the last laugh; still working on the crapper, just got all the retarded excuses for construction out of the way so I could figure out how to put things back, takin' a quick break before the trek to town for parts (ever seen a toilet flange put in in such a way it required an air chisel and a cut-off wheel on an air die grinder to get the damn thing out? had to chisel off the nails idiot-stick used instead of bolts to hold the flange in place (filled with wax from the wax ring of course), then used the die grinder to cut the existing flange into sections to remove it; swear if I'm ever diagnosed with an inoperable tumor I'm gonna track this guy(who cobbled this place together) down and torture him to death slowly (with the wrong tools for the job) :twisted: OK, as to attics and venting; the ONLY way I know of you can get away with NOT venting an attic, is if you leave it open to living space and condition (head/cool) the entire space. If you SEAL it so it can work as a 2-leaf sound barrier (roof, air/insulation, inner ceiling) then it can't BREATHE - if it can't breathe, it rots. So, someway or another you will need to get that puppy VENTED if you want the roof to last more than maybe 10 years or so, sometimes it happens even quicker. What happens when habitable space is used, is that the expelled air/carbon dioxide from people's breaths contains moisture - so does the exterior air that comes into the room every time you open the door. Now, the place gets heated; either intentionally, or by human body heat. This warmer air tries to get to the cooler place on the other side of the wall or ceiling - air infiltration allows this to happen in most cases, so now you have moisture in your wall (or in with your ceiling insulation) - this MUST have a way to dry, or you're SCREWED. That's where VENTILATION comes in - the moving air carries the moisture away, the relative humidity in the attic goes back down, mold stops growing, and everybody (except the mold) is happy.
