knightfly21 September 2005#182
Mike, you're making me nervous again; you've already moved your collar ties toward the roof peak, which weakens the rafters halfway between where the collar tie fastens and your outer wall; then, you want to replace them with straps, which will ONLY work for TENSION; unfortunately, it's likely that putting extra weight on the roof at that point halfway between collar tie and eave, will cause the collar ties to go into COMPRESSION; this means that a STRAP WON'T DO SQUAT. Finally, after weakening the roof framing you want to add as much weight as is already there (doubling the weight effectively) - The only way you' d get away with beefing up between rafters is if you AT LEAST add the MISSING collar ties, fastening them for both compression and tension (some nails toe-nailed one way, some the opposite) - better if you ALSO use liquid nails and clamp the ties while you're nailing. Once you have your ORIGINAL roof framing beefed by that minimal amount, you should be able to add ONE, and ONLY ONE, layer of sheet rock between rafters (same method as walls) - THEN, you can DUPLICATE your original roof frame halfway BETWEEN the old rafters, only using 2x8's - that frame's collar ties should be as far DOWN as you can stand it, the closer to rafter centers they attach the better. Collar tie each of the NEW rafters, maybe even add a 2x4 from under the (new)ridge board (don't short out between inner and outer ceiling) so your new rafters are kind of a half-ass truss. Do this with EVERY rafter. As you go, you'll need some sort of diagonal bracing across the rafters til you get the wallboard up; you can remove it as you add wallboard. What's a HRV? No time to look back... Steve
