I grew up in a house with an evaporative cooling system (also known as a "swamp cooler") for the first 12 years of my life. They're pretty common in California because most of the warm climates here are dry. I don't think they would work well in your/our application for a number of reasons though. First, the ducts are huge. Second, they cool air by blowing it over cool water, which does cool the air somewhat but it adds humidity in the process. (This is why they're more common here, because we Californians can usually afford to add some humidity during the cooling process.) I would think that with airtight rooms, multiple warm bodies exhaling, and sensitive equipment, making the air more humid is the last thing you'd want to do. Finally, I don't think they can keep up with the heat we experience here in our part of California, where summer temperatures regularly exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit. To top it off, I just don't like them. You can learn more about evaporative coolers on this cool page at the California Energy Commission's Consumer Energy Center. As for the hydronic, I really don't know. :? --Keith

sharward wrote:
rather than risk gaps and "little third leaf effects," why not just add a whole lot more mass to the inner leaf walls?? :?:
i think this is putting too much weight on the third leaf thing and missing the big picture. i think the general principle is that you want two substantial leaves separated by an airspace. true, having little gaps between layers in the outer leaf is going to hurt performance. but it's going to perform a HECK of a lot better than not beefing up at all. sometimes this gets confusing, because we talk about resonant spaces amplifying. the performance will not be as good as it could have been given the materials used in adding layers. but it will be better than it would have been had you done nothing. which brings to mind an old saying: "Better to have beefed up, and created resonant cavities compromising optimal performance, than never to have beefed up at all."
sharward wrote:
I think the answer might be: outer leaf resonance frequency would be too high. (Right? :?)
leaves by themselves don't have resonance frequencies ... only mass-air-mass systems do. if you mean that the outer leaf would suck, then i agree. one layer of stucco isn't the worst thing in the world, but i think adding a few layers of drywall would be much better. dan
