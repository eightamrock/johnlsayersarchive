knightfly28 October 2005#244
Mike, there are several reasons why you can't use that first link as an argument to attempt suicide (which is what you're doing whether you realize it or not) - if you read "peach"s comment, he said that "In vaulted ceilings, you can't really use rafter ties, so you need to design the ridge board as a beam." He's right; your ridge board is NOT a "beam", it's nowhere NEAR rigid enough for that. It would need to be more like a 4 x 16 glu-lam heavily framed into your end gables with about twice the "beef" you have on your end gables before it could be called a "beam". Then, each rafter would be resting on a wall cap and a much more rigid "beam" - because the beam is then a stiff, well-supported surface in its OWN right, each rafter would be more able to "stand on its own" - in which case your puny 2x6 rafters would STILL come up short on span for anything but a light (single) roof covering and no drywall. The terms "collar tie" and "rafter tie" seem to be a bit localized; I've heard "collar tie" used for both, but the distinction makes more sense. Another point - if you read THOROUGHLY the comments by NJ-inspector (instead of reading just the parts that appear to support your "wishful thinking", you'll find things that support my cautions on your proposed weakening of your structhre - comments like "Raising the ceiling joist to form a vaulted ceiling requires a larger rafter size", etc - Also, at the height you've placed your "rafter ties", they are STILL called rafter ties and are STILL NECESSARY on EVERY rafter (with your weak 2x6 rafters) - in order to be considered a "collar tie", they would need to be within 1/3 of the distance between wall and ridge board from the PEAK. In that position, all they'd do is keep the peak from lifting during high winds.
