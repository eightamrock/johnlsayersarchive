Got it. Well, this information does present some new questions from me. First of all, let's talk about the closet in the lower left corner, which is obvious that you must live with since that wall is very load bearing. I see you're going with double-wall construction all the way around, which is good. However, it looks like your inner left wall is connected to your original outer wall between the door and the left outer wall. If that's firmly connected, flanking will occur there. An idea that I have is taking that original entry door (far left) off its hinges, and putting your inner leaf door in that general area, a few inches from that doorway. You'd lose your closet, and you'd also lose your "straight shot" entry into the space. However, you would gain uninterrupted wall space and, I would think, better isolation. Also, if you'll be running this studio as a business, even some of the time, don't miss this post about ADA requirements that may further dictate what you can do with your entrance design, among other things. It may be time to provide more detailed illustrations of your existing space, photos, and as many details about your constraints and the existing construction materials as possible. I'm only guessing at this point what your questions are or might be, beyond just providing the initial "feedback" you asked for. Ha! See what happens in the wee hours of the morning when your mind gets churning? You see all kinds of possibilities! :-) I'm kind of leaning towards your final iteration myself... However, there are others whose opinions would be of more value, particularly in terms of the acoustic qualities of the space. In any event, I believe you have successfully resolved your decoupling problem. At least, that is true in terms of the two dimensional world. I expect that you'll have challenges with your ceilings. Do you intend to have a new inner leaf ceiling framing structure sit atop your inner walls, as I have done in my design? If so, then you'll have major issues with your ceilings as they "duck under" your original doorways, including the 16x7 vehicle entrance. For this reason, I would think that your first illustration in the new series would be the easiest to construct. For relative simplicity's sake, you may want to go with a soffit-type ceiling in the 16x7 vehicle entrance area, rather than attempt to go up once you clear the header. Or, maybe you can vault it, starting at about 8' a couple feet away from the opening, angling downwards at 45 degrees towards your newer outer wall. That would be a cool place for a drum kit, at least aesthetically -- not sure about acoustically though. I still don't see any doors or pizza slots in that control room -- maybe dual sliding glass doors would be your ticket. There are lots of illustrations of those on the main site here. Hmmm... I'm not sure what you're trying to accomplish with that, but it doesn't seem good. You do realize that you're aiming for exactly two leaves, not simply an "even" number of leaves -- correct? And you realize that if you have two leaves, plus some space, plus another two leaves, you have four leaves -- right? Consider sound traveling from point A to point B and how many leaves it must travel through. That's how you count the leaves. I'm not sure if this is news to you, but if it is, then rereading the posts to which I previously linked would be in order. (Well, it's never not in order, actually. ;)
