First off, greetings and heart-felt felicitations to ALL of the originators, moderators and participants of this marvelous resource. This is truly a fine example of what the internet could/should be. I am a newbie who has been poking around in this forum for nearly a year and am still not yet ready to present my first plan to your collective perusal for comment, but have been encountering recurring issues relating to my involvement in other construction projects and would like to ask a question that i have not (yet) seen addressed elsewhere in the forum with any specificity.... From all that I have read thus far, it seems that gypsum board (doubled using GG on both sides of a double wall, for example) can be very effective in cutting down on sound transmission ABOVE 125Hz, but that it basically sux for low frequencies. All I deal with is reggae and there is ALWAYS a really heavy bass component involved. Has anyone published studies involving use of concrete-based tile backer board and/or hardi-panel in comparison to the use of plain gypsum? Some of those backer boards contain a styrofoam component and seem somewhat porous, but the hardi panel stuff is more like laminated layers of paperboard somehow impregnated with concrete. I did note where someone agreed that it might be useful as an exterior home siding in his NYC application, but I am more interested in it's possibilities for isolating a voice booth or a mixing/control room.... thanx in advance for your consideration, pablo p.s. -- if i can get my "plate" cleared off sometime in the next six months, I'll be planning my own space in 2008!

sharward9 September 2007#5
...and the depth of the gap between the leaves is just as important as the amount of mass the leaves have, especially for low end transmission loss (TL). In other words, two massive leaves with a small air gap will perform poorly in TL, while two leaves of the same mass with a large gap will perform much better in TL. --Keith :mrgreen:
