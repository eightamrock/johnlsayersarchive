About Me My name is Greg Hill of Raleigh, NC (USA). I am a Student/Musician/Recording Engineer/Aspiring Acoustical Engineer I've been playing/recording music for the past 15 years of my life both solo and in various groups. I've always been in awe of the talent involved in the creation of the musical art form, and now find myself wanting to achieve a higher quality product via a well designed recording studio for both personal and external uses. (I had originally posted my design topic under the studio construction forum here- http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?f=2&t=14853 I apologize for having posted this twice, but I feel it is more appropriate to post it here.) My Goal To construct a 2-Story, 3-Car Garage, with a semi-professional studio on the second floor. Total budget for the project will be approximately $75k. The studio will include a Control Room, Live Room, Iso/Vocal Booth, and Bath. Instruments to be recorded include Electric/ Acoustic Guitar, Bass, Drums, Vocals, and various digital sources. Overall I'd like to have a studio that is both functional and comfortable, making the best use of the prospective 32' x 22' space. Where I'm At I am currently in the design phase of the project, and have developed a handful of ideas via Google Sketchup. (See previous thread) I have been in contact with a local contracting firm and have confirmed that the garage can be built on my property, but the contractors studio construction experience is very limited. I have read both John Sayers' "The Original Recording Manual" and Rod Gervais' "Home Recording Studio: Build It Like the Pro's". Both are excellent sources for the aspiring acoustician/ studio owner. I have also searched the forums non-stop for the past few months reading up on relevant projects. Volume I will be recording at relatively high volumes (115db +) when tracking drums or guitar/bass cabinets. (There's just something special about a Class-A Tube Amp and a 4x12 Cab when you begin to take advantage of some sweet JJ Power Tubes) Although the majority of the time this will not be the case. I would have to say the majority of the recordings I do involve acoustic guitar and vocals, while drums come in a close second. I have very friendly neighbors who have never complained about the sound creeping off my property, even when hosting gigs in the driveway way back when. We share reasonably sized lots, approximately 50' between our houses. I would like to keep the sound level reasonable between our properties, but in no way is it necessary to totally isolate the created sound from the adjacent dwellings. Questions/Concerns Is this layout the best way to use the allotted space for this studio? Being that I'm tracking drums in the live room I'd like to have it be the largest of the three studio rooms. Also, the sight lines of this design seemed to be a little skewed, where there is no direct line of sight between the CR and LR. Another issue I've run into with this design is the Iso/Vocal booth being almost entirely consumed by the angled ceiling, it will work, but will be tight, no to mention the complications with the angled wall facing into the live room meeting the angled ceiling. Any recommendations to an alternative plan or links to similar floor area studio designs would be greatly appreciated. What is the best method for isolating the ceiling while conserving room height? Soundman202011 October 2010#3
My first question would be why you chose such a complex shaped studio layout. Would it not be easier to go with simple more rectangular shapes? Why not just put the control room in the middle, facing out the window, with the booth on the right (next to the bathroom), and the really big live room on the left? Seems like much better use of space to me. I don't have time to sketch that out right now, but I hope the explanation is clear enough that you can do it yourself. Also, this is going to be on the second floor: I imagine that you are aware that this will most likely require some major beefing up of the structure to be able to support the huge amount of extra weight that you will go into that construction? You say that you are not concerned about isolation to the outside world, but you also say that you want to track live drums and amps at blistering levels: this implies that you need excellent isolation
between
your rooms, which in turn implies that all of your isolation must be very good. Without good isolation between rooms you cannot track well, since you won't be able to hear clearly what is coming from the speakers, as it will be muddied up by the bleed coming through the walls. And that level of isolation, of course, implies very large amounts of mass, which means that you need a structure underneath it all that is capable of safely supporting all of that mass. So I'd suggest that a major point in your design will be modifying the existing structure to handle the mass.
Where I've planned to use John's inside out method for the interior walls, I'd like to be able to do the same with the ceilings.
You can and should do the same. Inside out all around the room makes sense, and will give you better isolation than just using that for the walls then hanging your ceiling from RC. Especially considering the strange angles that you'll have to deal with in some places on the ceiling. So my suggestion would be to support your inner-leaf ceiling on the inner-leaf walls, in true "room-in-a-room" fashion. - Stuart -

gullfo27 October 2010#11
Correct, you're adding another stud adjacent to the king studs. For the ceiling you want continuous mass and can do this by hanging drywall under the ceiling joists or over them (inside out). Either eay, you will need to support a heavier than normal dead load including ventilation components. Given the span, you may need a beam added or at least 2x10. Maybe (since the building is still on the drawing board) is to raise the overall structure to accommodate the necessary structure, or beef up the exterior roof to handle suspending the ceiling.
