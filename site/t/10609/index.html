rod gervais24 May 2008#8
First of all - the green glue is great for sound - but if have direct connections through all of this to structure - your weak link is going to be flanking - not passage through the surface - Forget carpet pad - it's useless for what you're doing - and it creates a perfect home for dust mites -, etc. SO if OC Acoustic Mat - Add up what you want to do (per OC installation instructions) OC QuietZone acoustic floor mat 3/8" 2 layers 3/8" exterior grade plywood - 3/4" (you can't use OSB because it will never hold together with screws that short - they cannot penetrate the floor pad. That's 1 1/8" - floating - but the 2 sheets of 3/8" plywood don't have a whole lot of mass - so they aren't going to do a whole lot to help you with airborne sounds. Adding green glue will help quite a bit - but not a much as more mass would (along with it). Also, the acoustic mat is pretty much a simple decoupling mechanism - it will stop foot fall noise, etc. - which is exactly what it says in it's literature:
"Noise Control Floor Construction Installed beneath a double layer of exterior-grade plywood or 1 1/2" gypsum concrete and finished flooring, Quiet Zone acoustic floor mat isolates and minimizes structure-borne noise from transferring to adjacent areas or rooms"
Do what you want - just make sure you understand exactly what you're getting in return. Sincerely, Rod

the compressive strength of 705 is 200 psf (pounds per square foot) you'll be fine no matter what you put on it.. The green glue is well worth the investment. Rod
