gn87berner21 April 2010#5
I just went thru the same process. 5 gal buckets and easy loader. The BEST way is to use a panel lift for the ceiling. Put sheet on lift, load up with GG and raise into position. For walls I recommend "standing up" your layer with the GG. Stand up, means sheets run vertically. Put sheet into position on wall, then lower to the floor, apply GG then raise into position. YOU DO NOT WANT TO HANDLE SHEETS VERY MUCH ONCE LOADED. Follow instructions and leave space around the edge for handling. Treat GG as hazardous material in the respect of ; wipe up every drop as you go (it will not just dry up like caulk.) It will remain a possible mess the next day and the next. It is not hazardous, just treat it as such. You can squirt on wall, but they don't recommend it. Alternatively put sheets on saw horses, load and hang like instructions recommend handling carefully. My advice is best method for ease and cleanliness, maybe not fastest, unless you add all the cleaning time you will have, moving loaded sheets around.

From the greenglue.ca website: http://www.greenglue.ca/installation.htm Implication is that installation can be done in two ways: 1) Apply glue to first layer of drywall, add second layer of drywall 2) Apply glue to second layer of drywall, attach second layer to first layer They indicate that either way works, but method #2 is a "neater and cleaner" solution. My 2cents for what its worth. Andy
gn87berner21 April 2010#8
Byra, tell your hangers to stand up the sheets like I already advised. The reason you load the sheet to be hung with GG rather than load the already hung sheets is because you will get drips. Now drips shouldn't seem that bad, but it should be DRIPS :shock: Because when you get a DRIP you can't just wipe it up. You have to carefully wipe it up as to not spread it and then use a wet paper towel and dispose of the paper towel or it will transfer from the garbage. You have to contain this product. If your hangers insist on manhandling it (rather than a lift) they need to be told how to handle the stuff. Have a warm bucket of soapy water, tons of paper towels and a big garbage bag and when ever it gets on anything wipe it, wet towel wipe it up, dispose of it or it will be hell. Now you can clean it after it dries a bit (it doesn't dry for days so when you don't wipe up, 3 days later it can bite you again. Get your hangers a few cans of wd-40 when it does get sticky and it will,screw guns tape ect they can again use wd, paper towel and dispose. THE BEST THING IS NOT TO GET IT WHERE YOU DON'T WANT IT NOOOOO DRIPS That's why they advise atleast putting the sheet on saw horses and applying GG If your hangers insist on applying to already hung layer, definitely video tape it and post on you tube. :D
Ted White21 April 2010#9
I agree. #1 The Green Glue will not flow once on a board. The consistency was designed for this specific purpose. #2 It is definately much easier to install the "Glue" to the back of the second layer. The risk of wearing the material on your shoes is too great to apply directly to the wall. #3 Leave a 3" Green-Free perimeter around the board so you have a clean place to handle the board. This also eliminates the opportunity to have the glue squeeze out and on to the drywall surface. #4 Clean up any spots as soon as possible using soap & water or common isopropyl alcohol.
