Soundman202012 March 2013#3
apart from the diffusors on the ceiling!
Good call! That would be a big mistake. As far as I know, they don't publish the actual frequency range for those things, nor the polar plots, but based on normal diffuser theory, you'd have to have a REALLY high ceiling to be able to use those. Theory says that you need a minimum distance of ten feet between a diffuser like that and your ears, and the distance could be even greater depending on the lowest cutoff frequency. Three full wavelengths for half of the lowest frequency is the theoretical minimum distance...
so i will be doing everything they tell me too in this image
I would not do that: they are basing that diagram on one thing only: the dimensions and layout of your room. That might or might not be what your room ACTUALLY needs, and the only way you can tell what it actually needs, is by running an acoustical analysis of the room, to see how it is behaving in reality, not based on their guesses. So before you do anything, I'd suggest that you should download REW, run that on your room, and post the results here so they can be analyzed. Based on that, and that alone, you can then decide what treatment is needed. If you go to a manufacturer of acoustic products for recommendations, guess what they will recommend? :) Maybe you noticed that they only recommended their OWN products for your room? :shock: I wonder why?... :) It seems doubtful that they would recommend something made by another manufacturer, even if that would be better for your room! And even more doubtful that they would tell you about all the things you could do yourself that would just as effective (or even more effective), and would cost you less.... So my approach would be to first test the room with REW and see how it is actually behaving in real life, rather than guessing at how it might be behaving based just on numbers, then design the treatment that will treat the real problems, not the imagined ones. - Stuart -
