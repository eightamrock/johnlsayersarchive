xaMdaM5 March 2005#3
Russ, While not a moderator, I too welcome you to our little corner of the studio build family of crazies! I can't actually address your acoutic questions, but I do want to address your idea/concept of sending the Hammond direct. WHY!?!? The WHOLE premis of what makes that Hammond sound IS the Leslie! Without the actual doppler effect of the rotors, you loose, IMO, about 75 percent of what the sound of a Hammond is. It's obviously your studio and you can do what you darned well please, but in your design/build I would seriously consider either an isolation booth for a Leslie or scaling back and making an isolation booth for something like a Motion Sound. (In my case I plan on being able to use the larger iso if I track a performance.) Either way, welcome and the best of luck in securing a facility big enough to build your project in! Max

laptoppop8 March 2005#6
Hi Russ, First, please update your profile to include your location - it really helps to know where you are in terms of recomendations. I had never heard of 1500 cubic feet per person for a choir - that sound like overkill for this situation. I totally agree with John about having two rooms, if not three (put drums in the third for even more seperation). More seperation means more options later while mixing. I actually would consider using a ganged headphone system instead of speakers (sorry, John!) Again, I LOVE getting more seperation. If you say 50 people - figure 3x3 for each one, that's 9 square feet per person - 450 square feet -- allow double that for a space around the choir -- about 1,000 square floor feet. You'd want HIGH ceilings if at all possible - 20' sounds about right.... that would be 20,000 cubic feet. Is this being built for a particular group? You're talking a pretty major facility here -- it might be a LOT cheaper to rent a studio, or even just use a good church and remote recording equipment. -or- You might want to have a smaller studio for the band, and just rent time in a big studio to overdub the choir in. Just some thoughts. Studio building costs are expensive, and bigger is more expensive. -lee-
