According to the span calculator I use, using Doug Fir #2 2x6 16" o.c. with 20psf live and 10psf dead (i.e., not especially strong), the maximum span should be 12.25 feet. The longest span I see in your sketch shows about a 16 foot span. This probably means that the species and/or grade of lumber used in your house is stronger than the one I specified. However, it does prove a point that you may be overloading your joists, even adding just one layer! I therefore recommend you stop what you are doing immediately and consult a professional to verify the strength of those joists can withstand what you are putting on it! Although it may not collapse under normal conditions, it may collapse the next time someone crawls into the attic over that space! Or, worst case, it may collapse while you and your all-time favorite people are under it! Adding literally hundreds of pounds to a ceiling is significant and must be taken seriously!

knightfly26 September 2005#44
While you're doing that, (from your earlier post) this is storage space for mics and cables what not , the 2x4 stud wall is angled at 17 degrees. now if I slat the front of this wall and seal the back with drywall and isulation would it work or should I do it another way? You can do it that way; if you put heavy gypsum or MDF on the mic locker side of the frame, then rip 2x stock to 1-1/2" square for cleats, place the cleats at the rear (away from the main room) to keep insulation toward the slats - then 2" rockwool in cavities, dark cloth cover stapled to studs, and if you use 1x6 slats (actual 3/4" x 5-1/2") spaced 3/8" apart, you'll get a center freq of approx. 300 hZ which works well for drums (primary mode of kick drum shell for 22 or 24" diameters) Keep in mind that any absorption will partially WEAKEN the wall behind at that frequency; wouldn't hurt to put an extra layer of 5/8 gyp on the walls/ceiling of the mic locker to compensate for this. HTH... Steve BTW, for that purpose I'd NOT do the angled gypsum ceiling; while it's true that would reduce flutter, it will also reduce isolation and add weight. Instead, I'd add layers to the MDF on TOP of those joists, fill the cavities with rockwool and cover with garbage bags (or similar weight plastic) and ADD that beam you mentioned across the room, shortening your spans to 8 feet or less. You can cover the beam with more rockwool/cloth to kill reflections. The thin plastic will contain any shedding from the rockwool AND increase the brightness in the room by a few dB; stay thin with the plastic though; too thick and you may get a bit of flutter. If, after you're done with the room, you still have some flutter - you can add slats to the ceiling joists (all or part) - same dimensions, the extra depth will lower frequency center to around 240 hZ. If you did a LARGE area I'd vary slat spacing to broaden the absorption frequency a bit... Steve
