I’ve been talking with an acoustic engineer familiar with the products available in New Zealand and he had a couple of comments on what I was proposing – I thought I’d share these and post a couple of sketches of proposed details in the hope of receiving some feedback. 1. He has recommended using steel studs (90mm) rather than resilient channel against the block walls. Apparently there can be a problem with certain frequencies resonating when RC is used because of the small dimension between the block and the gib. This problem is less of an issue the bigger the gap because the resonant frequency drops. If 90mm steel stud is used it drops to around 60 HZ. This also seems to tie into graphs contained in a Canadian document I’ve seen referenced in this forum, describing tests on block walls. In NZ there is a ‘Rondo Quiet Stud’ available which assists to give even more isolation between the block and the gib, so this is what I’m planning on going with. 2. Also available here are ‘Rondo Quiet Clips’. These can be used to clip steel ceiling battens to the underside of floor joists. I understand these incorporate neoprene grommets which help provide isolation between the joists and the ceiling battens. Again, I’m intending to go with this, unless anybody suggests any reason not to? One other thing I’m wondering if I should do is provide neoprene pads beneath the bottom track (refer the first detail below). The reason I’m proposing this is that initially I want to try and get away without a floating floor, except for a small floating platform for the drums. If I do decide to put one in at some stage, I would like to make sure the inner leaf of my walls is well isolated from the floor, otherwise this could reduce the benefit of adding a floating floor. If I provide the neoprene it means I can get a decent seal between a future floating floor and the inner leaf of the wall, without shorting out because the wall connects to the floor below. Any thoughts on whether or not this is worth doing?

Be sure to check the rubber material you will be using. I would think you would want to have a material with good "dampning" qualities. (unless it's butyl sheeting...and even if it is, I'm not sure if there are different "kinds" of butyl sheeting. I was planning on doing some research in this area and asking questions as I got nearer to finalizing my exact floor plans) Also, be sure to do the "test" Knightfly suggested concerning moisture. As far as how to secure the particle board I'm not sure. Maybe someone on here has a suggestion. If not, maybe Steve (Knightfly) or even John himself may tune in and offer a suggestion. I'm going to look into this because I'll be doing this same thing. I'll let you know what I find out. One thing that may help you...From my reading on here, building a room for sound isolation and for acoustics are 2 different things. I would think to some degree they work together though. Once you are done buiding your inner and outer walls to reduce sound/noise from entering/exiting your room, it's then time to control the frequencies travelling all around your room from your speakers which is what the acoustic treatments of traps and absorbers do. In the case of a slat resonator wall, I would think it wouldn't matter if the inner walls were rectangle (in your case) as long as each side wall slat resonator was angled 6 degrees or more to control/deflect standing waves and there were other treatments in the room to absorb or reflect as needed. I have no doubt if this is a wrong assumption I will be corrected! Hey, I'm still learning myself. :) Hope some of this helps...
