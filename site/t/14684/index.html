There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post (click here). Actually, several people, who are experts on this forum, will most likely not reply if you don't do what is written in that post. Many others who are very helpful, will most likely not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. Regarding the advice you were given: It is basically correct, but incomplete. It is only half of the story. It is called "beefing up" here, and is a small part of the full treatment that you need to do. If you want to isolate your room (sometimes incorrectly called "soundproofing") then adding mass to the sub-floor above you will help, but only if it is part of a properly designed and built "room-in-a-room", based on the MSM principle. So what you'd have to do in addition to adding that mass to the floor above, is firstly to seal it all airtight with caulk (the gaps around the edges), and then to add another ceiling below that, fully decoupled from that floor, plus you'd build another set of walls around the room, a few inches away from the existing ones, but taking care that you do not create any three-leaf walls. That would be the basic idea, but its a bit more complicated than that.

Soundman202014 August 2010#7
How many layers of gypsum (on the top leaf and bottom leaf) can this sustain with no problem?
That depends on many factors, such as the span, type of wood, and how much load the joists are already carrying. Probably your best bet is to get a structural engineer to look at it, and tell you if your "beef-up" plans are feasible or not.
(on the top leaf and bottom leaf)
If yo build a fully decoupled MSM room-in-a-room, then you don't have to consider the weight of the inner leaf, since it would be supported by the new inner-leaf walls, not by the existing floor above you. In that situation, you only need to consider the mass of the "beef". But of you go the other route, and hand your inner-leaf on RC, clips, springs, or whatever, then yes, the inner-leaf ceiling would be part of the load on the floor above.
The two surfaces that I cannot adjust are the two that have almost the same dimensions: Pardon my going into metric - 218cm (w) x 211cm (h) - I can extend the length of the room by a meter (from 343cm to around 450cm) but will that fix the problem?
Metric is fine! In fact, I prefer it! In any case, your only real option is to loose height, or make the room very narrow. Neither of those is very good. I doubt that making the room longer is going to solve anything, since the issue is with the two other dimensions, which are practically the same. - Stuart -
