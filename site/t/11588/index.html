Hi G-slam, and welcome!
These stones do 54db of reduction.
Do you mean they have an STC rating of 54, or do you mean that they reduce transmission at a particular frequency by 54 dB, or are you referring to some other rating? I'm assuming that you mean STC?
If I know, that, I can calculate the overall reduction of the new wall. ...
Actually, it's not that easy! You can't just add up the STC values of two independent walls or building materials, and assume that the sum total is what you are going to get if you build a single MSM wall using those two! For starters, you are building a two-leaf resonant system, so it will no longer be mass law that governs Transmission Loss. TL will now be governed by the characteristics of the MSM system. You will get much better TL at high frequencies, and you will get WORSE TL in the low frequency range. The frequency, width and depth of that low frequency dip depend on a number of factors, mostly related to how you build the combined MSM wall. That alone will skew the STC rating one way or another. The dip may or may not be important in your case, but that depends on many other factors. There are equations that you can use to predict the TL and other characteristics of your MSM wall, but it is not as simple as saying "My blocks get me 54 dB STC, my bricks get me 43 dB (or whatever), so I have a total of 97 dB STC". It just doesn't work like that, unfortunately. I wish it did! You say that you want to isolate the room so that you can use it "for recording and concerts", but that doesn't say much. What are you recording? A single piccolo, a heavy metal rock band, a folk group, or an entire symphony orchestra? And regarding your concerts, do you mean harmonica concerts, or bass drum concerts? Are we talking Pink Floyd or are we talking Chopin? You also don't say how much you need to reduce the level by, on the other side of the wall. What is out there? Is it a nursery for a newborn baby, or is there a mile of empty landscape to you nearest neighbor? All of the above are wildly different scenarios, and nobody here can help you if you don't proved a bare minimum of information to work with. You might be overbuilding or you might be underbuilding, and either way is sad. You may not need a wall anywhere near as tough as you imagine, in which case you'll be wasting lots of money building it, and you may need something even more substantial, in which case you'll have to tear it down afterwards and build something better (or just live with a mediocre wall). There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post. Actually, several people who are experts on this forum will not reply at all if you do not do what is written in the post. Many others who are very helpful, will not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. Please read that and try to provide all of the information that you can.
I need this to choose the best windows, to prevent noise leaks.
If you are going to put a window in there, then that changes things hugely. The window will be your weak link, and needs very careful design. There isn't much point in having a wonderful wall with, for example, an STC rating of 72, if your very best window only gives you an STC of 52 (I'm just picking numbers out of thin air here: I have no idea if those numbers are representative of your wall or window). The overall TL of your wall is going to governed by the window, not the wall. It doesn't make sense to build a wall that is seriously better than the best window you can afford to put in it. Neither does it make sense to build the window much better than the wall. They should be designed for roughly the same TL. For the kind of wall you seem to have in mind, that window will have to be constructed with two panes of very thick laminate glass (different thicknesses), separated by a very large air gap, and extremely well sealed. That is expensive. And you didn't even mention doors, ceilings, or HVAC. Three other potential major issues. Anyway, please read over that link I gave you, and fill in the gaps so that folks here can have a better shot at helping you. - Stuart -
