jwl31 July 2008#8
FRK panels can work fine. Point the FRK into the room for corner-mounted panels, or any panel where you want better bass trapping, and high frequency reflections aren't a concern. The FRK will have 2 effects: it will be semi-reflective at higher frequencies (good for not over-deadening a room), and it will give you a membrane effect, with more bass trapping below 100Hz. For panels at reflection points, point the FRK away from the room. If the 2" panels are FRK, and you are stacking them for thicker panels, then you'll probably want to peel off the FRK before stacking them together (possibly keeping the FRK on the top panel pointing into the room, if applicable). If it were me, I'd only peel off the FRK ONLY for panels at first reflection points, on the side walls, ceiling, and rear wall. Possibly the ones on the side walls at the rear, if you want a deader recording space. All other panels (including all corner mounted panels, even at the front of the room), I'd leave on the FRK for the increased bass trapping. Also, I'd use 2 panels on the rear wall above the keyboard, not just 1. Possibly move the panel at the center of the front wall (leaving the 2 panels behind the speakers), so you can put 2 panels on the front and rear walls. There is often a bass buildup at the rear wall, so more panels will help with that.

You will only get the membrane effect if the FRK is facing into the room. That alone is reason enough to do it, it will increase your bass trapping under 100Hz, noticeably. I wouldn't worry about the room being too reflective. With the number of panels you are installing, it will be MUCH deader than what it is now. I was looking at the last photo you posted, the birds-eye view, with 3 panels flat on the front wall and 1 on the rear wall. I'd go 2 on each wall, personally.
