Aloha, Perhaps some of the reasonings "why" might be helpful. In Rod Gervais’ book “Build it Like the Pros,” he points out both L.W.Sepmeyer’s & M.M. Louden’s formulas most widely used in professional studios worldwide. He also notes that the orders listed are (top to bottom) the first to third best ratios in both cases. M.M. Louden Height Width Length 1.00 1.40 1.90 7’10” 10’9” 14’8”
I thought a drop ceiling would make more sense so I can have access to wiring and pipes, etc. is there a reason I don't want to do that?
While offering access to pipes/wiring, drop ceilings have been known to rattle a bit on certain freq's from time to time. They also provide little to no TL benefits. This route would seem to result in wasted time, energy and $$$.
What about flooring? Carpet would give me a warmer cozier feel, but is that the right thing to do? Like I said, I want this to be a decent sounding room for mixing, but also want it to be very comfortable.
Carpets tend to absorb mostly hi-mid to high freqs, resulting in an uneven response. Most of the treatments described here (RSD) would lean towards a hardwood floor with wall/ceiling absorption, which can provide a smoother response overall (provided they're done properly). If needed, area rugs can be put down to further tailor your live to dead ratio. Another couple of benefits are that it's easier for your chair/equipment/racks to roll around on hardwood floors, and it's easier to clean. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

One question about soffit mounting speakers.. I'm not sure I want to do that. It sounds like something that needs to be perfect to be more effective than just having my standing nearfields on stands or on my desk? Maybe I can do both, but if I choose to not soffit mount, will that change the design? Would I still want to have the wall angles the same?
Your nearfields would be better on a set of stands if you chose not to soffit. Be sure to avoid placing them at the mid point of your floor/ceiling measurement. The side walls should be the same (@ 12 degree angles) and your monitors should be angled 30 degrees each, for a total of sixty degrees @ the mix position. Here's an excerpt of an "after-soffit" review - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... &start=240
Q. I just wanted to know how you like your Mackies soffit mounted. Have you had and issues with them overheating? A. Compared to what they sounded like before soffiting, you could almost say that I love it! (What I would love is a pair each of: Dynaudio Air 6's or 15's, Klein + Hummel 0300D's, Genelec 8040 monitors/7060A sub Wink) - bottom end is tighter - imaging is clearer - drums sound clearer, more defined in mixes - able to hear clearer at lower volumes than before - room sounds quieter without music playing, louder & tighter with less effort when music played - less fatigue/more enjoyment in listening to mixes! - mixdowns have been so much easier - mixdowns with considerably less fatigue than before - mixes transfer room to room much closer than before I have had ZERO problems with them overheating, even after several all day/night sessions. I will mention that the rear of the speakers are not enclosed in a box, and they have lots of air circulation. While it is true that Mackie does not recommend soffit mounting, I will say this though; they definitely sound better than not soffitted. There are also several others here that have also soffitted their Mackies with similar results and have thoroughly enjoyed the improvements to their studios as well. Given the option of soffit or not, and having heard and worked with both, I would definitely choose soffitting.

Aloha,
So... if I decide to tackle the complex project of soffit mounting, where would my work desk sit in this picture? I'm trying to visualize how things would layout. With soffit mounted monitors, does the desk then sit right up against the wall?
Depends on the dimensions of your workstation desk. You would set up your mix position (chair, ears) about 38% of the length of the room from the front wall (roughly 5'3") and then position your desk and see where it ends up. It might be prudent to figure this out on paper before buying/building to make sure that everyting fits. :wink:

Whether or not that matters to you is your choice, but it has been suggested otherwise elsewhere in the forum. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
