britune7 July 2010#2
Not to be a buzzkill, but I doubt that you'll get much sound isolation for $1000. You should spend some time reading this site to see how others have built sound isolation. And learn about the attached STC chart below. But you would need two stud walls (one on your side, one on the salon side) with no drywall in the middle. You'll also probably need your own ceiling structure and ceiling drywall inside your space that is decoupled from the other ceiling/roof structure. You almost need to build a sealed structure that is only connected to the rest of the building on the ground. You'll see that sound isolation techniques usually involve a Mass Air Mass design that you can learn about on this site, and in books like Rod Gervais', "Home Recording Studio: Build It Like the Pros". Posting pictures and drawings of what you have would help.

britune8 July 2010#4
The wall you are proposing would give decent isolation, not as good as two serarate stud walls, but decent. But that wall is only a part of the entire room structure. It doesn't make any sense to build a wall structure that gives better sound isolation than the ceiling components. If the ceiling is the weak link in terms of isolation, you'll let more sound escaping through the ceiling into the salon. When you are trying to do sound isolation, any one small weak link can defeat the entire system and cause all of your money and work to be wasted. Posting pictures of what you have, and posting drawings of what you want to build would help. Also, are you tracking acoustic drums in that live room?

The green walls are the walls that I had planned on using the resilient channel on.
You seem to be treating each wall differently! Once again, isolation is an "all or nothing" proposition. Just like you cannot build an aquarium with glass on some sides and cardboard on others, so you cannot isolate a room by only treating some walls, not others. You could put a two-foot thick high-density military-grade reinforced concrete wall where your green lines are, but it would be worth absolutely nothing in terms of isolation... unless you did the exact same to the other five sides of the room. Sound will simply flank around your fantastic wall, taking the easiest path. If the easy path happens to be through the attic space and tin roof, then that's the way it will go. Or if it happens to be out through the bathroom and down the passage, then that's where it will go. Or through the electrical work. Or through the HVAC ducts- Or through the doors or windows, if they are not built right and correctly sealed. Or.... In other words, if you want to isolate a room, you have to do the exact same thing on all six sides, or at the very least on the five walls plus the ceiling (provided that you already have a really good floor). Same mass, same air space, same isolation. Treating only one wall of a room is like trying to build that aquarium with only one piece of glass. All four walls, plus the ceiling, plus the doors, plus the windows, plus the HVAC system, plus the electrical instalation must all be built to the same level of isolation. If not, then you wasted time, money and effort on building the "good" parts, since they are worthless. An example. Build a fantastic room with brilliant STC-80 isolation all around, but only put an STC-40 door in. Guess what your isolation is for the entire room? Yup. Around STC 40. So unless you do the exact same on all sides of all your rooms, you might as well not bother. - Stuart -

Soundman202012 July 2010#9
What kind of isolation could I expect then? Would I be able to record drums while the salon is open?
Hard to say exactly, but my guess would be that you'd get isolation somewhere in the high 50's. Which means that drums would end up as something like 60 to 70 dB on the other side of that wall. Is that quiet enough? That all depends on what is going on in the salon. If it is a noisy salon already, with hair dryers, people talking loudly, music, etc., then it might be OK. But if that is a quite salon, then it might be too much. If you need more isolation than that, then you'd need to increase the mass of both leaves on the walls/ceiling, and/or increase the air gaps between the leaves. You will probably also need to forget the RC and go with separate studs frames to get better isolation. And since drums put out a lot of low frequency energy, which is the hardest of all to isolate, I'd also suggest that you use Green Glue between the layers of drywall, to give you a bit extra isolation in the low end. - Stuart -
