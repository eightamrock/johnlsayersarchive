we are trying to build a control room with an iso booth large enough for recording drums.

Cool! Then you are in the same boat as all of us here! recognizing that is the first step... :)
they arent sound guys and for the life of me I cant get them to see that you cant just throw stuff up as you go with a regular contractor who has never done any kind of studio work.
OUCH! and OUCH again! You really need to sit down with them and get them to understand that what they are doing now is very similar to just taking large wads of hundred dollar bills, and flushing them down the toilet. Maybe that will get through, because that is exactly what they are doing. It sounds like most of what they have done already needs to be torn out and done again, if you hope to actually have a workable studio in there.
They have already sheetrocked and put up insulation in the room,
Unless they did that as fully decoupled MSM walls, then they just flushed all that cash down the drain. It is most likely of no use at all, and at least some of it will have to come down. depending on how they did it, you might be able to get away with just taking the drywall off one side of the studs. Hopefully that will work.
They plan on using sheetrock they have already bought, as well as wooden studs, and I guess fiberglass.
The materials are fine, assuming that it is 2x4 studs, 5/8 sheetrock, and that the fiberglass has a density or around 3 psf. If so, then they can us that. If not, then send it back to the store and get the right stuff. The most important part is that the sheetrock must be 5/8. Anything thinner is worthless for good isolation.
So basically my main question is should they treat the walls that are already up as a single leaf already,
Nope. If that is normal construction (studs with drywall on each side) the it is already 2-leaf: Which is why it needs to come down, or at least to have one leaf taken off it. Money. Toilet. Fluuuushhhhh!
or do two more seperate leafs for the booth.
Bad idea! That would make FOUR leaves! :shock: That would most likely make your isolation WORSE than it is already in the low end.
we will just put up a bunch of walls, if it doesnt work, will just put up more."
They need to understand that putting up MORE walls will make the isolation WORSE not better! They need to understand that acoustics is NOT intuitive, and things like adding extra walls really can make things worse, while taking down stuff that is already there can actually make it BETTER. Until they get that idea, they will continue on the well-worn path from the bank teller's window to the toilet. FluussshhhH!!!
Plus its an asymmetrical room, so I am having issues with that too.
:shock: Then you don0t have a control room! By definition, a CR needs to symmetrical, at the very least for the front half. No symmetry means no control room. You can't do a good mix that translates well if you don't have a good sound stage, good stereo imaging, identical paths for both ears, etc. That should be your first priority: Plan to get the front half of your CR symmetrical. If not, then you already know where this is going.... fluuussshhh!!!! In your post, you show a picture "StudioPhase1-3 2.jpg" with a couple of windows in it. That looks like a really good starting point for a usable space. It has symmetry for the front half, and looks big enough to work for a CR. You have brick and concrete, and good height. I'd start with that one. Any chance you can get it? It would make a good CR. So where would the LR go, if that can be your CR? - Stuart -
