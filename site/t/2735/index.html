knightfly30 November 2004#2
"1. Is RC needed on walls since top plates are directly connected to ceiling joists? Are sway braces and no RC a better option? If I use RC, will I have 3 leaves - i.e. drywall, studs, parallel wall?" If you were to extend your inner frame so it bears on the joists, then you would need RC to decouple the room from the wall frame; but you're right, NOT using RC and instead using sway braces is a better way. And no, a leaf is defined as all the CONTINUOUS layers of mass in one location, with no division by air (or air-like material, such as insulation) - so, your frame would only count as an addition to the mass of the wall coverings - right now, your outer concrete is one leaf and your inner steel frame with layers of wallboard is the second leaf. The continuation of your outer leaf (in this case the concrete) should be your upper sheathing on those joists; as such, it should be one continuous sealed mass everywhere, with no hard contact to inner framing. If necessary for better isolation, you can help this outer leaf some by adding a couple of layers of wallboard between the joists - if you search on "between the joists" and look at the 2 or 3 latest posts that come up, you should find some description of this process. "2. HVAC. I'd like to tie into the existing house furnace. Will I be ok just running insulated flex duct? Superduct seems like a bear to work with. What kind of registers should I use?" I'm not very knowledgable on HVAC - I usually try to leave these questions to Rod Gervais, one of our members and a VERY knowledgable guy on anything involving construction - I do know that Rod doesn't like flex duct, because it tends to flatten and pinch off air flow - I do know that air noise caused by moving air is roughly proportional to somewhere between the 4th and 5th POWER of the velocity - so if you double the diameter of a round duct, you get 4 times the cross sectional area and therefore 1/4 the flow, which should lower your air noise to nil - this would leave you with finding good registers, which I've yet to research. I also know that there is a lot more to AC than just the above, but I've found that the HVAC community is very closed, not willing at all to be generous with information - I don't want to perpetrate any more "internet fact" here than I've already done. Rod's your man on this one... "3. Lighting. I am most concerned with leakage through the ceiling. Is surface mounted track lighting compatible with a resilient ceiling? I don't have much headroom." This would partially depend on the weight of the fixtures and track - to be safe, it would be better to include a layer of plywood behind the innermost layer of drywall, for the screws to bite into - I don't think most track lighting would be heavy enough to cause enough extra weight on the RSIC clips to worry about. You can gain a little bit of headroom by running your channel parallel to (and between) your joists, mounting the clips either on "L" brackets mounted to the sides of the joists (up in the cavity) or mounting them on short 2x4 blocking between the joists, with the blocks recessed up into the cavities enough so that your channel just clears the bottoms of the joists by 1/2" - I'm working on a re-do of your "EastLiveWallSection" that will improve your ceiling height by at least an inch, but need to get some sleep (4:30 am wake-up tomorrow) so I'll have to continue this tomorrow nite, along with #4 - "4. Short of a floating floor/total room within a room, have I done everything I could to isolate from upstairs? " More later... Steve

If there is currently HVAC supply to (and return from) the basement space you'll be building in, you can probably just set up a group of transfer grilles to move air in and out of the studio. The existing HVAC system was sized and balanced to provide air for that space- you're just enclosing some of it. Just make sure the intake and exhaust are sound-protected at the studio walls and their outlets are far enough away from each other that you're not getting exhaust air coming back into the intake vent, etc. There's a couple posts around here about plenums and duct silencers, but you might need to dig a little to get to them. And as Knightfly said above, Rod Gervais is the man when it comes to HVAC.

knightfly30 December 2004#20
Your live room span is the 11 foot one shown in the cad drawing? If so, then 24" centers using 2x6's won't quite give you 10# dead load, which is minimum for hanging two layers of 5/8 rock with insulation - you'll need to go with 16" centers which will span up to 13 feet, using Doug fir #2 and better, 2x6. If you have the headroom, you could go to 2x8's; all else being equal, they would span 13'6" with 24" centers. Yes, this would be better from an isolation standpoint; however, if you're limited in headroom then using RSIC clips and hat channel would likely give the best results with minimum headroom loss. Room to room isolation within the studio would be better if using separate ceilings, with wall extending up above the ceiling and sealed... Steve
