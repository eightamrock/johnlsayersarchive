dymaxian5 August 2004#2
I'm sure there's other people here who can help more with a layout than I can, so I'll just rattle off a couple things that come to mind... I think you should break the wall between the control room and the sound-lock to the office/bathroom area, mainly to cut down on structural sound transmission. It looks as if you've got double-walls between the control/tracking/iso and the neighbor's tenant space (to the right on your image) so that's good- if your isolation requirements make you think you won't be needing a double-wall between control and the office, that's fine. I personally would double-wall between tracking and control, just to make the isolation that much better when you're tracking drums or a full band. Will you be floating the floor? With this layout, doing just the tracking room should be enough. Perhaps the outer iso booth (on the right) to minimize noise getting to your neighbor when you put a full marshall stack in there and turn it up to 11. The building inspector may have a problem with double walls- he may instruct you to put draft-stopping at the top of the wall cavity to keep any fire that starts between the walls from spreading up and over them. Trouble is that this will make a structural connection that will negate the advantage of having a double wall. Ask the inspector about building the walls inside-out, with the drywall between the stud walls instead of outside- this will fire-protect the cavity and the inspector might think that would be sufficient. Whats your ceiling height in here?
