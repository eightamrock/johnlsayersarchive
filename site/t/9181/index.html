i51423 wrote:
Am I right in saying that it's ridiculous for a floor plan to not line up exactly? I have the floor plan the owner gave me and it's just not matching up. :?
Actually it's not that unusual- for example - I spent 2 days in an office building a short while back doing exactly that for one of my clients - they begin with an original building plan -- with tennant fit out - and then over the years make changes to suit new tennants - and you don't usually incorporate these into a new set of drawings.(or let's say nost don't - I always do it in cad - so my as-builts are always up yo date). But I would have to syay this is the norm - not unusual at all...... Now - if the major structural components were wrong - that would be different.
I need to get in there with some tape to get some real measurements or something. I'm not even sure how to go about that. :cry:
Awww........ that's easy - just take a clean sheet of paper - make a rough sketch of the space - it doesn't have to be to scale as lokg as all of the various walls and jogs are correct in general. The pop inside the room and measure all 4 walls - and from one wall to a door edge - and then write in the size of the door. The same thing with windows. do this one room at a time - pretty soon you will have all of the data..... you can then give that to a cad company that will turn it into an accurate drawing - for not all that much money. Rod
