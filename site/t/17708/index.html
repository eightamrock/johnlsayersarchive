OK, so you don't have much to isolate in order to not annoy the neighbors, but how about sound going the other way? You say you want a good quality pro-level studio, but your customers might not appreciate the sound of wind, rain, thunder, and helicopters flying overhead, getting into their recordings. Ditto for the sounds of cars and trucks arriving and leaving, or driving past, people walking / talking elsewhere in the building, phones ringing, radios playing TV, photocopiers, microwaves, HVAC, doors opening and closing, water running, toilets flushing, etc. Those things are often overlooked when considering how much isolation you need, to you might want to consider that!

Putting the client couch on a riser at the back is useful, yes: gives the client a better view over your shoulder, of what you are doing, and makes him feel a bit more important. Relatively easy to make, not expensive, and very useful, so yeah, you sure can have some risers in your live room, if that's what you want, and what your build needs.

Soundman202021 July 2012#5
I'll be climbing around the drop ceiling more, other than plumbing, wiring, and air ducts, is there anything i should be looking for?
Yup! take photos! Lots of them. It would be good if we can see what you are seeing, but the only way that can happen is if you post photos. Take lots of careful measurements, too. Of everything. Even if it seems insignificant, still measure it. Also, check for gaps, cracks, holes, and penetrations in your walls and ceiling up there, as even very small gaps can severely compromise your isolation. Take note of where they are, and take photos, so you can put together a plan about how to seal those. Don't forget to install duct liner inside all your HVAC ducts while you are up there. That's probably the single biggest thing you can do to control air noise inside the ducts.
take a peek and give me your thoughts.
The front wall of the control room should not be angled like that. Symmetry is critical for control room design: the front half of the room must be perfectly symmetrical about the room center-line. If the room is not symmetrical then you will never have an accurate sound stage or proper stereo imaging, so your mixes will all be "skewed" in one way or another, and will not translate well. So I would fix that first. The control room also seems very long and narrow: What ratio is that based on? You could probably chop off a section at the rear for a machine room, iso booth, or storage area. The two iso booths also have strange shapes: I would add an additional wall to each one, to make the trapezoid instead of triangular. Just some suggestions... - Stuart -
