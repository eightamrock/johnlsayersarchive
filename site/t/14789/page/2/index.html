Why "6 inches left"? It would probably be better if your head were on the center line.
I am aiming the axis of the speakers to a point 12 inches behind my head.
That sounds like a bit too much, unless you have your speakers spread very far apart (or you have a very large head! :)) The idea is that the acoustic axes from the speakers are aimed at your ears, which generally puts the intersection point just around 6 or 8 inches behind your head. The layout is looking much better now, IMHO. So it looks like you have a good basic plan for your room now. About the only thing I would do different is to soffit-mount the speakers ... :) ! - Stuart -

Soundman202020 September 2010#29
I thought it was common knowledge around here that the center of the room is a null? Am I not right?
Yup. It's a null. And it's also a peak. And also neither null nor peak. And also both at once. Depending on which mode, which frequency, and which harmonic you happen to be talking about, and whether it is axial, tangent or oblique, .... (It gets rather complex). But basically room symmetry is far more important, and you cannot have room symmetry if your head is not on the center line. Besides, your ears will not be on the center line! Your head will be, but your ears will be a few inches off to either side (unless you have a really, really thin head! :) ) In fact, offsetting your head six inches to one side will most likely place one of your ears pretty much directly ON the center line, right where your dreaded null is... Think about it... The other issue that few people realize is that when you are seated, your ears are pretty much at half of the height of the room anyway... roughly 1.2 m, when most ceilings are roughly 2.4 m high... It's not a big deal: just get your head on the center line so you can get correct symmetry. - Stuart -

mmyers1 December 2010#46
Next, I hung the triangle panels in all four of the top corners of the room. These are 36 inch triangles. I decided to do the hardest job first and get it out of the way, so I hung the ceiling panels (with the help of my trusty wife!) I carefully measured for first reflection positions for all of my speakers, positioned a couple of panels directly above where I sit to work at the computer and also where I move back to the "sweet spot" in the room which is 38% from the wall with the windows and also three inches to the left of center. To hang the panels, I am using a 4 inch L clamp from Lowe's which positions the panel exactly three inches from the wall. I mark each clamp at one inch and line the panel up with it and drill the screw into the wood frame. Notice in one picture there is my "frame template" that I use to mark where I want to line each panel up and then drill holes into the wall for the clamps.
