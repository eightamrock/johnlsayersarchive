Gregwor27 February 2018#2
I'm unable to give you a number here, but I have a few things to say: - set up your drums, buy a meter and get someone to go outside and measure how loud it is. From there, you know how well your current situation is working. - you've outright said that you won't do a two leaf system. That leaves you to 2 options. 1) seal it up the best you can and add mass 2) seal it up and use resilient channel and add mass. - RC is not good at isolating low frequencies. Those are the ones that are going to piss your neighbours off. It's failure rate is high and adds more headache and cost than it's probably worth. - Green glue can help a lot. You could add a few layers of drywall with green glue and probably improve your situation a fair bit. - You need a BEEEEEEFY window plug and door. - I don't fully follow your diagrams regarding ventilation. It's a major concern for sound leakage though. If this is a rental house and you don't want to spend lots of money and it's just for jamming, you might want to consider just circulating fresh air from within your home rather than risking your already poor isolation by tapping into the fresh air outside. I know this isn't ideal, but it might be the cheapest and most fool proof. Again, not ideal, but worth thinking about. Greg

Gregwor28 February 2018#4
Remember, each pane of glass counts as a leaf. Any more than 2 leafs = less isolation. For your silencer, you'd get the best results having 1 inside on your inner leaf and 1 outside on your outer leaf. Each with at least 3 baffles and built heavy. I'm not sure what kind of weather you get there, but MDF doesn't play well with moisture.
could I add mass by screwing the plasterboards to the wall (3 layers)? Would this added mass give any additional attenuation at all to these already heavy walls?
Certainly. But like I've said before, you wouldn't achieve leaps of improvement in doing so. I suppose, do the window and the door and take some measurements. At least then, you'll know what you're up against! Greg
