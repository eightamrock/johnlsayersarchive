Anyone heard of this? A place in town carries it and Tremco. Here's a pdf about pecora ac-20 ftr which includes 'technical data': http://www.pecora.com/downloads/specdata/AC_20FTR.pdf Here's a quote from it:
Used as an acoustical sealant, AC-20® FTR reduces sound transmission in partition systems to maintain specific STC values by sealing spaces around cut-outs and at perimeters of partitions. The sealant cures to a tough rubber to form a long-lasting acoustical seal.

gullfo29 December 2010#2
sounds more like you want the AIS-919 acoustic caulk although the other seems like a useful product for fire stops. if you're looking for just sealing via caulking - most non-hardening 25-year (or longer) caulks will work.
Pure L29 December 2010#3
I'm looking to seal gaps with this and backer rod, gullfo. In a first layer of beef type situation. I have a case and I'd rather not open it if it's deemed to be bogus. Thanks for any input.
gullfo29 December 2010#4
i don't think it's bogus, i think it's intended for fire stop or other more structural bonding than the stuff they sell as acoustic sealant which seems lighter and more flexible so it would be less likely to couple surfaces and be resilient enough to deal with expansion/contraction.
Pure L29 December 2010#5
OK. I've found a line on some OSI which seems to be the go-to brand around here. Would you advise I use the water based or oil based? Thanks.
gullfo30 December 2010#6
i prefer water based as they tend to have lower VOC.
Pure L31 December 2010#7
Thanks gullfo. I actually picked up a couple of cases (the OSI SC-175) and have been using it with much success. For the record, I also discovered that sticking a heating pad in the box (on the lowest setting) allows it to travel out of the tube quite easily during sub-freezing temps (only while I'm actively using it, of course.....not all the time). I read on the box that allowing this stuff to freeze is no good so I've been keeping it in my house when I'm not using it as well.
