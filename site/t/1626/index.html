dymaxian7 May 2004#3
Greetings and welcome! What kind of music will you be recording? Will there be any high-volume instruments played here? Do you need more isolation than is currently there? (eg are you sick of having your neighbor's dog show up on your recordings?) That room size has potential to be a pretty good sounding room. I don't know if the window will be too much trouble for you, but the door being in that position isn't helping you out too much. IMO you should build bass trapping into the alcove on the "left" end of the room. It's pretty hectic for me this morning, but I can sketch something up later on. But anyway, in my mind what would work really well would be to build a wood-frame wall to fill in the alcove between those closets. Not flat across, but angling back into the alcove so that the walls on each end of the room are not parallel. Stuff the area behind with acoustic hangers, fill the space between with fiberglass blanket insulation, cover the front with 1" rigid fiberglass panels, then fabric, then wood slats. Then set your mixing desk there, facing this wall. The door and window would be behind you in the room. Then I'd put a thick absorber on the "back" wall (right side looking at your drawing) per John Sayer's wall-units page. It's basically 4" rigid fiberglass spaced 4" off the wall, covered in fabric. The side walls could use a couple of 2'x4' panels of rigid fiberglass hung in the right spots, but too much would make your room dead. Just put them up on the walls (and ceiling) in the places that would reflect sound right from the speakers to the listening position. Once you're there, you'll have a pretty good sounding room. There's more you can do beyond this, but it's a good starting point. Hope this helps. Kase www.minemusic.net

dymaxian8 May 2004#6
That's exactly the kind of layout I was describing. But as John mentioned, soundproofing is going to be your big challenge. It doesn't seem like you have the space to double all your walls, so what I'd recommend doing would be to beef up the walls here. It's probably going to mean that you'll be taking down the existing drywall. Do you happen to know what kind of sheathing was used on the outside of the house? You can probably lay up strips of drywall between the studs, flat against the outside face of that wall. Then put RC up on all the walls and ceiling, and 2 layers of 5/8" drywall over that. Make sure every joint in every layer is caulked. Once you're there, you can worry about the real tough stuff- the openings. If any of the doors in the recording room are hollow-core, replace 'em with solid core doors and weather strip them as best you can. You can probably build a heavy inside shutter for the window, but sealing it will be just as important as the doors- maybe more. If it sounds like a lot, it is- I'd hate to have you take half-measures here and not get the isolation you need. Kase www.minemusic.net
