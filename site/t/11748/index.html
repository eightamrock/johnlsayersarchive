Soundman20202 March 2009#2
Hi Paul, and welcome! There is an announcement at the top of the forum about what to do to assure getting as many responses as possible. The announcement leads to this post. Actually, several people, who are experts on this forum, will not reply if you don't do what is written in that post. Many others who are very helpful, will not reply out of respect for the moderators' wishes. Also, I'd suggest you download "SketchUp" from Google (it's free!), and use that to create the plan of your room, since drawing it with dots and dashes makes it really hard to figure out. Anyway, to address the basics: Yes, you should start with bass traps in the corners. Make them big and deep. Use the "search" feature on this forum for terms like "bass trap" and "superchunk", and you'll find more than you ever wanted to know about the subject!
If I have resources, should bass traps in the corners formed by wall a ceiling be a good idea?
Yes! A bass trap close to one wall gains you 6 dB of absorption over the same trap in the middle of the room. If you put it in the corner where two walls meet, you get 12 dB boost. And if you put it in the corner where three walls meet ("tri-corner"), you get an 18 dB boost, so it is definitely a good idea to treat your tri-corners, if you can. You get more "bang for the buck" there. If you have to put a trap in front of your door, you could mount it on wheels so it can be move into place when you need it. Also, your room appears to not be symmetrical: this is probably another reason why your mix did not translate well. You might want to think about how you could make it symmetrical. Next, two of the dimensions of your room are the same: you have an 8 foot ceiling, and the room is 8 feet wide. This is bad, as it means the modes are identical for those two, so you will have huge dips and peaks in your response that is going to be hard to fix just with treatment. The third dimension is also a related multiple of the other two. Is there no other room that you could use instead of this one? If not, is there any way you can change these dimensions, maybe by moving a wall? Finally, you definitely do need to treat your first reflection points, including the window and the ceiling overhead. Once again, you could put your absorbers on wheels or stands, so that they can be moved into position when you need to do critical listening, and stored elsewhere at other times. - Stuart -
