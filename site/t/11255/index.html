marz24 September 2008#2
Daiwa, Option 1! As sound will travel along flooring, I suggest that you build the walls and make independent floors inside each defined room after that, which should minimize flanking along the floor. Keep the floors isolated from the wall structure and plasterboards as well by a gap of say 6 to 10mm and fill with backing rod (Caulk saver foam) and seal with flexible sealant. WRT your subsequent question - If the Cement screed (your glaze) is already in place on the concrete, then go straight on top the screed. If you can build the wall frames direct on the concrete, then rather do that. You want the frames fastened to the concrete if possible, but not overly fastened, unless you live in earthquake country. What is the depth of the screed? I made a 60 to 70mm screed on top of concrete floor and managed to fit conduit pipes in the screed to carry all studio wiring in the floor to the desk area. This screed will become heavy and may be a structural issue on your floor! Check ! Good luck Marz

marz24 September 2008#4
Daiwa, I suggest you keep one thread going to cover your build and keep it in one place, instead of many diverse threads. Possibly rename it with an informative title: eg - Daiwa's 2nd floor 2 room studio build? 70mm of cement screed is very heavy, and may be a structural problem, so you need to review that with a structural engineer to be safe! Depending on the size of your rooms? I personally suggest you do not use a rubber mat under your floor, but fasten the OSB direct to the cement screed and the parquet flooring glued to the OSB for a solid floor. There are many discussions on this forum about not making a floating floor and it is a heavy / expensive engineering exercise to do it right and worse to do it incorrectly. Can you improve the ceiling isolation of the room below in any way - IE Add more Plasterboard below your floor or even an isolated ceiling, separate from your floor? Possibly post a plan of your rooms and your idea of ceiling structure with dimensions. Preferably with reasonable scale - I found it difficult to follow your earlier wall diagram. The ideal is for it to be completely isolated from your outer shell and fastened on top of the new inner walls. Again it needs to be engineered for the dimensions of your rooms. Wider rooms equals deeper ceiling beams. I used heavy Steel Angle bar profile for a 3.6m span and carrying 2 layers of 12mm Plasterboard. cheers!

bassman25 September 2008#6
Don't put the rubber down. While you might dampen the impact noise from going into the slab, you will also be creating a resonant floor in the midrange frequencies. We laid a laminate floor down over concrete with a very thin layer of foam for the same purpose and created a much worse TL from the next room because any sound coming through the floor was amplified by the extra layer of foam. my 2 cents.... -ashley
