gullfo25 March 2008#2
for vox, you'll want to worry mostly about the quality of the room sound and that would likely tend to be dryer (deader) than when recording an acoustic guitar (for example). so overhead absorption as well as the side/angled ceiling/walls will need treatment. a good quality exterior door with magnetic seals would work to close off the space but you'll want to consider HVAC with fresh air exchange as part of the design... foot impact can be reduced several ways. a carpet with padding works. heavy plywood over opencell polyurethane foam (like carpet padding :-)) works and keeps the floor lively. from an attic perspective - inbound noise like rain, hail etc would be annoying so maybe some RC on the joists/rafters and 1-2 layers of drywall w/ or w/o greenglue as budget allows.

gullfo27 March 2008#4
the insulation itself won't help with isolation, a couple of layers of drywall and green glue (not an adhesive) would be do the trick. i would take the drywall all the way down the rafters to the floor and then use under the eaves for trapping or racks etc. the absorption planning sounds right - on the angled ceiling you will want to treat those for reflection points just like you would a side wall or ceiling. the rest of the space behind you can stagger the absorbers to break up the reflections and then concentrate some absorbers where you need reflection control for the vox.
