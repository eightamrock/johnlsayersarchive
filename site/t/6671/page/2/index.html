woodlakesound wrote:
You don't take into account diminishing returns based on personal needs.
Perhaps he doesn't - but I do. I have a saying which sometimes gets lost in the mix of things - and that is this - "when you have to live with something - live with it the best that you can". Sometimes we have to live with triple leaf construction - sometimes we have to live with flanking connections - sometimes we have to live without large air spaces - Well - when these things happen - live with it - and move on getting the best that you can under the conditions.
Are the three thin layers of DW with two layers of glue the same as two slighty thicker layers of DW with the one layer of glue?
The answer is yes. All things being equal: 1 - the mass in both scenerios are exactly the same, thus the mass law side of the equation doesn't change. 2 - You will get a slight (very slight) benefit in the high to mid range due to the different thicknesses of materials (BTW - I do not usually recommend this - I generally tell people to just stick with 5/8 - the added mass makes up for the difference in the long run - and mid to high frequencies are not generally our biggest problems) 3 - I would expect you to get a big advantage through the use of 2 layers of green glue versus a single layer.

woodlakesound wrote:
Mr. Cooper just called and put forth the proposal of filling the cavities of the outside wall with sand. I was dubious as to the effectiveness with regards to stopping direct transfer from the studs to drywall we have now.
I always adamantly oppose the thought of a sand filled drywall cavity - first off - sands weighs a lot - and the drywall isn't designed to carry that weight - remember that the load not only wants to spread out - but also wants to settle to the bottom - therefore you have the entire load trying to spread the drywall open at the bottom of each bay. Next - I always get nervous about the fact that sand soaks up moisture like a sponge - and damp sand will make drywall "dampwall" - and damp drywall likes loads lilke sand even less than it doesn when it's dry. SO nix the sand idea...... Besides - it still will not solve the problem with flanking through the studs - it would only damp the drywall and add some mass......... so effectively you would only have a one leaf system - not great for sound isolation.
So then at my request we thought of taking one of the two 5/8in layers of drywall off and Green Gluing it back on. Would that be as effective as my previous proposal of taking the two layers of 5/8in DW that is presenty on the studs, cutting it to size, GG against the sheathing, caulking ....then 4 layers of 5/8in drywall with GG in between.
Think about what you're asking here (for even a moment) and you'll realize all by yourself that it could not possibly be as effective........ Would it be enough? I doubt it - would the 2 layers relocated to the outside - with 2 layers inside be enough - I have no way of knowing for sure - although I think perhaps it might - you could always test it after the installation - and then add a 3rd (and possibly 4th) layer if you really need it.
Is the extra demo and reinstallation of all the DW with GG gonna substantially beat out the sand idea.
Absolutely.............
