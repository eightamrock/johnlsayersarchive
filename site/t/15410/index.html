Approach My approach when moving stuff, when I try to find the best placement for traps has been, starting from the bottom, I played the 76 hz tone (consider the 76 hz dip changed depending on the placement, it went from 73 to 78, I don't know maybe this info is useful for you) and walked around the room to find places where it was louder, using also an SPL meter But unfortunately only few places where it resonates loud were good to place a trap, in fact in many places the dip become deeper. The good places where mostly on the back wall, and on the right, where the amps are. The ceiling corner trap absorb a lot the 76hz dip, also the two over the left corner trap. And so on, I mean in general I used that method, where I found a dip, I played a sine on that freq and I walked in the room to find the best placement for traps, but considering many of them made things worse, I also tried just placing some traps around, and I discovered, for example, the heavy black trap under the bridge of the yamaha speaker stand, does a lot aligning the lows (the part you see more flat) and the traps under the library bridge does a lot too for the 76 dip and in general for the lows Listening Experience The room sounds controlled in the low end (I'm talking about decay), but working in there is quite fatiguing, probably I have to many highs and the lows are like "shelved". I tend to cut often too much low mids, mids, hi mids, and I think this is sometimes right (in frequency), sometimes influenced by the room, generally the amount of the cut is too much. When I go in the car I have planty of low end (my car hasn't a sub, but has big woofers, good mids and tweeters, I have a quite natural sound, not so defined in the low because it is always a car, but much better then all the cars I've been in). Speakers Part of the highs are because of speaker design, the PMC TB2+ have a lift on the highs, because the tweeter design has a wider dispersion and PMC suggest to use them not in perfect equilateral placement, but with the axes that cross 50 cm behind your head, to have a wider sweet spot because, they say, usually when you use outboard or in general when you mix, you move around and your head isn't always in the same position. So my room already has a lot of highs plus and specially the lows are a bit shelved, so I think I'll get a second amp to bi-amp them and take down the level of the tweeter or I'll take an eq. Why PMC? I've used Dynaudio BM5a, good overall, very hifi, poor transient response, not articulated lows, a bit boomy of their own, I mean the classic hi fi effect in the lows. I changed to KRK VXT6, great speakers, great transient response, I tried to fill the port with some foam because I like sealed speaker. Then I took just to try because I found them used (I was interested about the transmission line concept), those pair of PMC TB2+ and an Adcom 545, and wow wonderful image, they are very 3D, very articulated, ultra clean lows, very detailed mids, unbelievable transient response (a conjunction of the KRK transient response and NS10, Auratone), unfortunately too many highs. That's why I'd like to keep these speakers. Next step, I found a used TLE1 sub and I want to try with it, maybe even the highs will feel more pleasant having the lows more at highs level. And then I'll eq them or reduce them with an amp, if are still too much.
gullfo23 December 2010#3
empty the room completely - everything not nailed down. then set up your speakers and the omni mic in the listening position and measure. also so a single speaker in the low front corner and the mic in the opposite upper back corner. this will give you the baseline of you room both from a mode perspective and the monitors/listening position. start with the monitors @ 70% of the room width and 100mm off the front wall and turn them on 30° to the listening position. set the mic dead center of the room and 38% of the room length from the front wall. you might find you need to move the speakers and listening position to get it close. make a triangle with the two speakers and the mic. once you have that, it is then possible to begin to determine what you need. at the very least it's likely to be bass trapping across all corners of the room, side and top absorbers for first reflection points, and back of the room. basically start with 8 absorbers. re-measure. then you can determine if you need to add more or reposition etc. finally, bring in the library and desk and make them fit within the treatments. re-measure. if need be, consider moving the library out into another room or storage. offer to pay for the storage if need be. if you're serious about the sound, don't let the furniture dictate your treatment decisions. otherwise accept what you cannot change and alter your workflow to accommodate more A-B testing of mixes to accomplish what you want.

how well do your mixes translate to other systems? car? club? radio? home stereo? etc etc? if you're getting good translations across your material then you're good. if you find that your mixes don't come across as you expect, then you have a problem. maybe there is another way to layout the library so it's out of that spot but still in the room? if not, then you may need the sub and you may need to cut some HF to get the balance you need in order to achieve a good mix.
