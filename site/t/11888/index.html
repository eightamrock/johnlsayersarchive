Soundman202023 April 2009#11
Is using RC-1 before the next two layers going to offer a significant amount of extra isolation?
Absolutely! It will decouple your inner leaf from the studs, which is about as close as you can get to building a "room-within-a-room" without actually doing it. Of course, you need to attach the RC directly to the studs, NOT to any layers of drywall that might already be on them. The drywall has to come off. Then put your RC across the studs, fill the gap inside the wall (behind the RC) completely with fiberglass or mineral wool, then attach your two layers of drywall to the RC, taking EXTREME CARE to ensure that no screws go into the studs!!! Screws can ONLY go into the RC. If even one single drywall screw touches a stud, then you have short-circuited the RC, and it will not decouple any more.

Soundman202028 April 2009#19
Would it simply be possible to do a room within a room and make sure none of the walls are touching? If I am assuming correctly this will not put ANY added weight onto the existing walls...right?
Well, as far as I can see, that probably would be your best bet! Build new 2x4 (or 2x6) stud frames a couple of inches inside the existing frame. Put new joists across those new walls. Put drywall (at least two layers) on only ONE side of all those studs and joists (either the inside or the outside: it doesn't matter. But not both sides). Carefully seal it all air-tight, make sure that nothing from the new inner leaf touches the old outer leaf, and you should have a decent, and safe, room. You have a concrete slab on grade for your floor. That part, at least, is good news! You should check how thick that slab is, and find out if it is reinforced, to make sure that you can put all the extra weight of the new construction on it, but chances are you should be OK for that. However, I would still suggest that you get a structural engineer to take a look at what you have, and what you plan to do, and get his professional opinion. He is the only guy qualified to tell you if you are OK. Explain to him the above (2x4 or 2x6 walls and ceiling framing, with two layers of 5/8 drywall on only one side, plus glass, doors, equipment and people), and he'll be able to tell you if the concrete slab is OK for doing that. Like I said, it probably is OK, but DON'T take my word for it! I can't see what you have from here, I'm not a structural engineer anyway, and you DO need one to inspect what you have, and tell you for certain if you can do what you propose. The only issue I see is the possibility of your existing wall become a third leaf, but I think that is hte least of your worries, and you don't seem to be concerned about isolation too much anyway. I looked but couldn't see any place where you described exactly how that existing wall is built. Is it just something like MDF or OSB plus siding on the outside of those 2x2 studs, then drywall on the inside? Or is it something else? I'm still curious as to how a building like that can pass inspection if it only has 2x2 studs for the wall framing, and 2x4 joists for the roof. Are you CERTAIN that is what it is? That there is nothing else in there providing support? Maybe those 2x2's are just furring or something? Can you get some of the drywall off those walls, and take a good look at what you actually have? I'm getting more confused, because I just checked an on-line span calculator, and I cannot find ANY 2x4 wood that can safely span 11'6", even at the lightest loads, unless you relaly relax the deflection limits greatly. The only thing that fits the bill seems to be Douglas fir, but like I said, even then you have to assume very low loading and poor deflection limits. Most 2x4 woods will only span 7 to 9 feet using the default parameters on the span calculator. I don't really know what I'm doing with that thing, but I do see that the numbers just don't look good unless you relax the deflection limit to what seems (to me) rather flimsy! Are you sure there is nothing else holding up the ceiling? Do the joists really span all the way across 11'6", with no additional support? Are the really resting on 2x2 stud walls? I think you need to double and triple check how that place is built, because from what little I know of construction, it just isn't making much sense. Brien would be the guy to tell you for sure (xSpace). He's the expert on that kind of thing. But it doesn't look good to me. However, the place is there, and it is standing, and has been for some time, so SOMETHING is keeping it up! Anyway, your absolute best bet right now would be to get a structural engineer to come and take a close look at things. - Stuart -
