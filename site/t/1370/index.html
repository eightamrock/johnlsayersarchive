The evolution of acoustics has been quite rapid since the use of ideas first used by Chips Davis in what became known as LEDE control rooms and diffusers based upon the work of Manfred Schroeder. Initially the idea of the back wall was to help provide ambiance without effecting the sound character of the direct sound. By using diffusers the character of the late reflections was made more diffuse and hence spacious, for lack of a better word. To avoid coloring the direct sound the rear wall was placed so that there was about a 20 ms delay before the reflections from the rear wall arrived at the listeners position. These two factors (ambient space and no early reflections) are the commonly accepted reasons for using diffusers. If a sound is delayed by a small amount of time (less than around 20 -30 ms) and combined with original at a similar level there will be comb filtering caused by the phase difference between the two signals. Especially in small rooms, in order to try and get better sound quality, a lot of absorption is used. More than many people would like, because of the reduction of the ambiance. If the level of the reflected sound is reduced, then the comb filtering effect is reduced. This is why several technical documents (eg EBU t3276, available on their website) specify that early reflections be at least 10 dB lower than the direct sound. The diffused refections from diffusers are reduced in level. In effective ranges, at least by 10 dB. The back wall diffusers reduce the level of the early reflections, without adding more absorption to the room. This use of diffusers is often not understood by many people learning acoustics. Meaning people that know just enough to understand the basics, but not enough to know how much they do not know. The RFZ design concept is a direct evolution of the LEDE design. Some people might refer to it as the next generation. Rooms designed strictly to LEDE design concepts are almost never made these days. RFZ concepts, which are derived LEDE design concepts are the most usual design.
Are these diffusiors for LEDE different than the ones used on the back of Large RFZ rooms ? How do they lower the sound level ? They look like every other diffusior i saw
The diffusers are the same. RFZ is a refinement of LEDE. The scattered (note Steve :D ) reflection is spread across the plane or hemisphere in front of the diffuser. Since no energy is added and the sound wave is now spreading out over 180° the sound pressure level is lower because the same amount of energy is spread over a greater area. Andre
knightfly25 February 2004#6
I don't know of ANY recent LEDE rooms, they were found to be generally too dead. This makes them too exhausting to work in for more than an hour or two without a break, which is a lot of why things evolved. With the advent of surround, even RFZ won't cut the mustard in its pure form - the front reflective splayed walls/ceilings are fine for stereo, if the rear wall is close it is generally absorbed and if further away can be diffused. Some like diffusion even with short rear gap in the room. However, with the addition of surround speakers we get early reflections from the rear surround speakers bouncing off the front, splayed, live soffits/walls. Most 5.1 rooms are being built with absorption all around, then ambience is replaced digitally. There is almost no other way to do this that I'm aware of. I think that part of why this works is that the typical living room is pretty dead, so mixes translate between CR and living room fairly close... Steve
