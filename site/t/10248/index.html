Cram26 February 2008#3
Take from this what you will - If you're aim is support and having a sturdy staircase, (for standard construction methods) with walls on either side of this stairwell, try to use the walls for support against the outside stringers (sides of the actual staircase). I have fixed "creek" noises in a couple of staircases because the consruction only had nails (or nothing) that connected one or both sides to the frame of the walls they butted up against. For the most stable suport of stringers, I've used long screws to secure them against the wall. Vibrations of foot traffic through the years will develop problems if a staircase isn't done right. With isolation however - this would couple the stringer (through the nails/bolts going through the drywall) with the frame. I did not do this as I opted to keep the supports underneath the stairs there. I'm not sure if you want to follow this example, but in my basement I put a decent trim board [1X12 pine] down the side of the stairs along the wall. That was screwed to the stringer in place, then the outside layer of drywall was screwed to this (free standing), then the 2nd layer of drywall - then the framing was attached to the two layers of drywall.
