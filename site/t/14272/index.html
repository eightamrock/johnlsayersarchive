xSpace13 April 2010#2
Flanking is always an issue, but you cannot start designing a foundation that try's to minimize the flanking when you are actually weakening your structure. AND...the flanking that you are trying to reduce still exists! ;) Not allowing your bands to make a complete run to tie the entire wooden floor together is not a good idea. Like you said, the walls and the trusses are connected...and in fact, so is the floor system, even though it is cut up, the skid beams still transfer vibration. Build a strong foundation and you will have a better building. Also, if you take your "inner" wall and flip the sheetrock side so it is facing the sheetrock on the exterior wall, this would be better than what you have now.

xSpace14 April 2010#5
A vapor barrier has a specific place to be installed in a wall. Can it be mass? Yes it can be mass because it can stop sound. But the goal of a barrier has nothing to do with acoustics so your question has no ground to stand on at this moment.
