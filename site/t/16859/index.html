Soundman202029 August 2011#2
Hi Rob. Welcome! :) A couple of comments:
the only noise issues i believe we'll have is an occaisional lorry turning round outside.
No wind? No rain? Hail? Storms? Aircraft flying over? People talking? Car arriving/leaving? Animals? Radios? :) Lots of things outside make noise that you might not want inside. Lots of things inside, too: Telephones, radios, TVs, refrigerators, computers, printers, microwave ovens, bathrooms, toilets, people walking/talking, doors opening/closing... All of those should be considered in your isolation plan.
The building is a typical industrial building (for the UK at least), approximately ...
It would be useful to see photos of what you have, to get a better idea.
The top half is corrugated metal, this includes the roof.
A word comes to mind: rain! Rain falling on a tin roof makes a noise...
a false ceiling will have to be built over the whole studio section, leaving the warehouse open.
If you plan to achieve a decent level of isolation, then you are going to need rather more than just a false ceiling! For isolation, you need three things, basically: mass (lots of it), air-tight seals (at least two, all around you), and decoupling. A typical false ceiling has none of the above... Regarding your layout: you might want to keep both of your live rooms adjacent to your control room, even if you will usually only use the second one for rehearsals. When you DO use both at once, it would be good to have sight lines to both of them, and to have direct access from the CR, for things like setting up mics, etc. Having to run back and forth across the green room each time you adjust a mic position could get boring, real fast. I'm also wondering about the strange splay angles of the CR walls... They don't make sense, no matter which way you look at it. If you are after an RFZ design, the CR should start out narrow at the front and get wider as you go back, or at least half way back, after which it can get parallel again. But it should never get narrower again, at the rear! You might want to fix that. It would also be good if you could show the positions that you have in mind for speakers, console, racks, doors, windows and basic acoustic treatment, to see how it all works together. Finally, you also didn't mention your budget, but a project like this clear is not small potatoes, so I guess you have that under control? For a project of this size, you might want to consider hiring someone like John to do the design for you. - Stuart -

the budget is under control yes, although not massive i've been told it is big enough...tho not been told exactly what the budget is.
General rule of thumb: Building a studio will cost you about the same as building a high-end house in your area, per square meter: so call some builders in your area, and ask roughly how much they estimate per square meter for an up-scale home in your neighborhood. In your case, you already have the basic shell, so it's more like a remodeling job than a build from scratch, so ask them about costs per square meter for doing something like turning an unfinished bare garage into a guest bedroom or home theater. That will give you ball-park figures for the budget. You then also need to add in a bit more for things like HVAC and electrical, which aren't quite the same as for a house.

Good enough to get a basic idea! So it looks like you have a good solid concrete slab on which to build: That's a good start! What else is going to be inside that shell? Is it just the studio, or will you be sharing the space with something else? And the biggie: How much isolation do you need? That's where the whole design process starts. Try to put a real-world number to that, in terms of decibels. Based on that you can choose the type of construction you need to get that level of isolation, and that in turn dictates materials, techniques, and even part of the acoustic treatment that you will need down the line... - Stuart -
