As for your GG in a multilayer floated wood floor it is something i am aslo wondering if it's worth it, we're both in the same boat i guess, ill keep you posted if i found an answer.
I will be applying a 3/8" layer of plywood and a 1/2" layer of OSB to my subfloor using GreenGlue between each layer and will post the results. It's been 10 days since applying GreenGlue in between each of two layers of 7/16" OSB placed on top of a subfloor made of 10" thick SIPs (Structural Insulated Panels- 7/16" OSB panels sandwich with a styrene/foam insulation in between ). GreenGlue works. Very Well. SIPs are something I would strongly urge NOT to use for studio building. Great insulation, Poor noise isolation due to low mass. Bad choice on my part but I was lucky enough to find Rod Gervais' book and GreenGlue. GreenGlue is expensive but if one is in a situation where you could not or did not build it "right" in the first place GreenGlue can save the day. GreenGlue says that the full isolating properties of GreenGlue will not become apparent for 30 days while the moisture content of the GrreenGlue is outgassed/removed. I can say that every two days since I have applied the flooring layers I can hear the difference in the increasing noise isolation between the outside and the interior of the studio space (interior dimensions are 27' x 18' x 14.5' peak of cathedral ceiling) and also with the "reverb" of the room when shouting, clapping hands etc. Even though it is expensive, using GreenGlue can reduce the need for adding more layers of drywall etc if you need to conserve space. Ted at GreenGlue indicated that using 2 tubes of GreenGlue on a 5/8" x 4 x 8 drywall panel will add the equivalent mass of one additional layer of 5/8. I also used GreenGlue between each of 2 layers of 5/8" drywall applied to the interior walls and cathedral ceiling of my exterior shell (I am building an inner room). I have not had pervious experience using layers of drywall as part of a 'mass-air-mass' structure so I can only give my experience in this building. Prior to adding the double layers of drywall (with GreenGlue betrween each layer) I could hit any of the walls and the reverb of the room was demoralizing. I had a metal ladder leaning against one wall and I could make it rattle but hitting an adjacent wall. After the GreenGlue drywall layers were applied (setting apart each wall/floor/ceiling from each other using the Corner Framing Detail/Gypboard Sequence as specified on this website/in Rod's book) I could hit the wall 3'/1m away from the ladder and the ladder would not rattle. (Perhaps not as scientifically rigorous a test but it was pretty effective on me...) The "reverb" of the room is now very reasonable for this stage of construction. Again, GreenGlue is expensive but if you factor in the cost of labor to hire someone to hang 4 layers of 5/8" drywall versus 2 layers of 5/8 with GreenGlue between each layer - equivalent according to GreenGlue - the costs come very close. And then there is the Time it will take to hang 4 layers, including backer rod and caulk for each layerto consider. (I enjoy doing a lot of things DIY but I have "issues" hanging drywall.) Anyway, my 2 cents. This product works.
