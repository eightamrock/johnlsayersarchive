Soundman202021 September 2015#2
Hi there "Scissors61". Welcome! :) Looks like you got a "fun" shape there!
*Fire door on the bottom left of diagram needs to remain easily accessable at all times.
If that's the case, then when do you show it closed off in your suggested layout? If it cannot be closed off, then how come you are closing it off? And if it CAN be closed off, then that would solve your problem: that would allow you to set up your room facing that bottom wall.
*Narrower section at the bottom presents problems in terms of keeping the room symmetrical
... unless you orient your room to face into that section. Symmetry is only critical for the front half of the room. The rear half isn't so important.
This is an incredibly rough idea of the only way that I can think of to make this room both symmetrical and actually functional...
Except that it isn't symmetrical like that, because you cannot build the wall that you show on there (violates your first rule of not closing off the "Fire door on the bottom left of diagram"), and your head ends up pretty much exactly in the geometrical center of the room, in all of the first order modal nulls... It's also not a good idea to face your room into a corner, for acoustic reasons: you'll find it very hard to deal with your early reflection problems like that. The way I see it, you have three options: one is to face the room into the bottom narrow part, the second is is to build a wall across the "niche" at the top right to make the entire room symmetrical, and the third is to flip your suggestion 180°, so it faces into the convex corner, instead of the concave corner. None is a great option, but all three would work, to one degree or another. Your layout could work too, to a lesser extent, if you really were able to build a solid wall at the bottom, in the place where you absolutely cannot build a wall, and then put some major treatment in the room to try to reduce the modal situation. But that is out of the question, apparently... There's also the issue that building a wall across there (assuming that you are able to do that) introduces the possibility for creating a three-leaf situation, which could trash your isolation in the low end.
I haven't spent ANY time thinking about specific angles or distances, firstly because these measurements are not 100% accurate and also because I have very little knowledge on the subject. The 'proposed' shape is purely a "might something like this work?" I'm guessing probably not...
Well, it COULD work, minimally... assuming you really can violate your own first rule and build that wall, and assuming that it can be massive enough and/or far enough away from the existing wall and fire door to eliminate the three-leaf problem, then you would need to fix the geometry that you are showing for the speaker/mix position relationship, get your head out of the null, put suitable treatment on the first reflection points (which will be hard where the window is), and build a cloud. Once you have the exact dimensions of the room, and clarify the situation with the fire door, please post them here and we'll see if it might be possible to set things up in some way that works. However, give the small size and strange shape of that room, you should not expect that it will be a world-class control room. It can be "good", but it never can be "fantastic" with those limitations, unfortunately.
my question is more of "Is there any possible way that I can make this work?" rather than asking for particularly detailed plan/exact construction techniques, although I would also obviously very much appreciate help with this at a later date once I have further details.
As long as you realize that there will be limitations in what can be achieved in there, then the answer is yes, it can be made to work to a certain extent.
I hope this isn't too ridiculous an ask!
Not at all! Yours certainly isn't the toughest room that forum members have tried to fit studios into! At least you have no curved walls, support columns, extremely low ceiling, staircases in the middle of the room, two-level floors, or other fun stuff like that! With enough money and careful design, yours is feasible, but the acoustic design is going to be a little bit complex if you go with your layout or the "180° flipped" version, or it is going to be "boxy" if you go with either of the other two versions. Fun! :) - Stuart -
