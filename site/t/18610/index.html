That should be achievable. But it would be good if you could get a sound level meter, and measure how loud your music is, and how loud the ambient level is, both inside and outside your studio.
1. Will I be able to practice with my band in the open space (as seen in skg file) or will the acoustics be terrible and sound insulation be too demanding?
What other instruments are in the band? If you have drums, for example, then that makes it a lot more complicated. You will probably need to build an isolation wall around you, inside the existing room, to do that.
Band noise :Acoustic drum set, bass (Fender Rumble 350), my guitar amp, PA: 2xRCF Art 312, 2xBehringer 1800 subs, 3x Peavey PV12M floor monitors
OK, so that answers my question above! :) You have drums. So yes, you probably do need to build something in there to isolate the sound properly.
Partitions must be gypsum/wood, as I will be doing everything myself- I'm not good enough in masonry to make brick partitions.
That's fine! You can get very good isolation from just simple wood framing with gypsum board ("drywall"): And you already have a good outer shell around you, so it should be possible to get good isolation like that.
3. What to do to with the floor (right now its bare cement- no overlay), my final overlay later on will be sanded OSB screwed down and then varnished.
Bare cement is just fine! You don't need to do anything to that. You will probably need to build a "drum riser", to keep the drum kit off the floor, but that is easy to do. - Stuart -

Soundman20209 December 2013#6
Because of this, i plan to stucco the interior side of wall (cannot get permission from the neighbour for scafolding to get to the otherside), then add another drywall partition infront of the existing brick partition.
Right! That's what I would do: Make the stucco as thick as you can (within reason!), then once it is dry, paint it to ensure that the porous surface is sealed.
I will be using rock wool for both sound and thermal insulation. How far away from the existing wall should i start my framing, are there any sound proofing techniques I should apply on my framing. There will be at least 2 layers of 1.25cm thick gypsum sheathing.
The drywall (gypsum sheathing) is too thin. Use 16mm, instead of 12.5mm. Start the framing at least 3cm away from the stucco, and even more if you want better isolation. - Stuart -
