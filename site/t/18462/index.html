Johnnie27 July 2013#3
I think that you might regret having the isolation booth located behind the engineer. That's a fairly awkward location. Right now you'll also have the lobby and a recording space directly next to each other and separated only by a single wall. That seems likely to get noise onto recordings. Are you planning on doing doubled walls with an air gap? The width of that is going to impact how much room you have in some of your traffic flow patterns. Be very careful with floating a floor. It's a frequent suggestion but is often unnecessary and can even result in problems you wouldn't otherwise have had. Is there a particular reason it's felt necessary in your case? You may not be worried about volume levels coming out of your studio, but what about those going in? You mentioned a bus inspection station very near. To my mind that sounds like you're going to have low frequency rumble close to your studio, which you'll need to be careful to isolate for. That front door in the bottom left definitely sets the stage for what's got to happen next. I totally see how you'll want a lobby, but as shown it's looking awfully narrow, though I grant I'm having trouble with getting a good picture of the unshown dimensions. What if you keep the lobby where it is, but orient the control room facing upward on the picture instead of towards the side? Move towards a side view control room rather than a front view control room. That's a little bit awkward, but I kind of like them, and I'd guarantee that it's better than having to look backwards. I'd also ditch the angle at the back, as it's making things a bit awkward and it's not going to be that hard to get good absorption in on a flat backed control room. This would accomplish a couple of things. First you could extend your control room all the way across the width of your design. Second (and pretty important) The traffic flow coming through the control room would all be able to pass along the space in front of the sofa instead of further forward beside the mixing position. I really don't think you're going to want people carrying a drum set so close to your mix station as currently shown. Your priorities would then impact the placement of the iso room. How big do you want that lobby? The bigger, the tougher to get in a reasonable iso booth. The specifics of the spacing can be altered by sliding the control room to the left or right. I'd definitely want to be able to see the rest of the band while I was in the iso room so I'd get the iso room and the live room over together on the right side of the control room.

Johnnie30 July 2013#6
That's looking better already. I do have a couple of suggestions though (and some questions) First, if you turn the control room 180, I think you can get the narrow end into the area that currently looks like it's lost space. The bass trap that sticks out to the sides behind the front soffits isn't strictly necessary and it's costing you some space that I think you'll want. You can deal with the bass absorption by using John's bass hangar soffit design. You'd also be able to avoid that little cut out in the upper right of the ctrl room picture. Second the slightly different angled walls aren't really necessary and also lose a little space. As long as you've got a reasonable air gap (4 inches or more) you'll be ok with a consistent angle. (the amount of gap / mass you want will depend on the frequencies you need to isolate... I'm still thinking about that bus rumble :horse: ) You're correct though, in your recalling that you'd generally want your control room symmetrical (particularly around the listening position) and that in general higher ceilings are going to be better than lower. Third, it looks to me like you've got paired sliding glass doors. If they are standard patio doors, then you're hurting your isolation. If memory serves you'll only hit something like 42ish db with those in place. (how much are looking for?) If you buy real acoustic sliding doors, you're adding a lot of expense. Have you thought about just doing a regular pair of studio doors, and then using windows for your sight lines? Fourth, is there any way to move the bathroom over to the lobby or is that placement non-negotiable? It seems like it'd be awfully nice to put the bathroom over in the lobby space, and to put your iso booth where the bathroom currently is. (assuming the control room is turned around too so you can set up a sight line to the new iso placement.) To answer your question though, yes, you'd want to double wall the isolation booth if you want to get good isolation there.

Johnnie2 August 2013#9
That's definitely looking a lot more clean. I'm not sure on the current sight lines from the iso room to the control room (is that angle for a window?) but you'd definitely have good site lines from musicians between the two spaces, and if you have to pick one or the other, I'd always side with the musicians being able to see each other.... course I'm primarily a musician so I'm probably biased. :) The one other suggestion I'd have would be for the entry way. Right now the traffic flow there is a little awkward because it runs directly into a wall. That will tend to give new clients a "closed" feeling as their first impression on entering the studio and the spacing there might be tight for a large bass drum to turn the corner. If I recall correctly, you have some stairs there, so you can't just turn the door into the lobby from the existing walls, but what if you extend the entry way all the way to the control room wall. Right now the wall is on an angle, so you'd need to be sure to straighten out early enough that it doesn't get to narrow for smooth traffic flow. Then make a new door that opens directly into the lobby from what would now serve as a landing... i.e. the door is facing up instead of facing to the right (hope that's clear). The goal being to make a landing that feels generous and open (and might serve as a staging point for equipment being carried in) and the first real impression of the studio would be into the open space of the lobby instead of into the wall.
