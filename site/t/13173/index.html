Soundman202017 June 2009#5
Since I won't be doing any other walls in those rooms, would isolating just one wall with an extra insulated, .. .... be a reasonable solution....?
Short answer? No. It wouldn't help much. Isolation is an "all or nothing" proposition. If you don't do all six sides of the room, then your isolation will only be as good as the WORST side of the room. Think of an aquarium: it only holds water (and fish) if all the sides and the bottom are in place. Take out one side, and you don't have an aquarium any more. Same with isolation. If one side of your room has poor isolation, then that's how good your total isolation will be, no matter what you do to the other five sides.

Hi Ace. To me, your third option "CtrlRm facing Side.jpg" seems to be the best. The other two are going to give you strange issues with those angled ceilings.
- If I'm sideways in the room, the length is better but the ceilingand sides aren't symmetrical.....
Try to get it as symmetrical as you possibly can, then treat the non-symmetrical part to make it better, That would be my suggestion.
it's harder to make the back wall absorbent and basstrap heavy, since there need's to be a door in between the two rooms.
Put the door in the middle of that rear wall, and cover it with a sheet of 703. Put the bass traps on each side of the door, as big as you possibly can.
and I still don't quite know how much space I should leave in between the back of the speaker and the slant ceiling.
You either want your speakers right up against the wall, or at least 2 meters away. Anywhere in between will probably give you comb filtering effects off the wall.
And will a window ,behind a speaker, present much problems?
Yup! :) (Windows are transparent to light, but the are excellent reflectors for sound...) - Stuart - (Still trying to keep the answers short, so that Ro has a chance to give his usual LONG version...! :) )
