Soundman20206 April 2010#14
The internal rooms walls are 5 inches thick, consisting of (from inside to out) plasterboard, timber frame, rockwool, air gap, plasterboard.
You seem to be missing a timber frame for the second set of plasterboard! Your second leaf kind of does need something to hold it up.... :) Also, a total wall thickness of 5 inches doesn't make any sense! It implies that you only have an air gap of 2 1/2 inches! :shock: Minimum recommended air gap is 4". Any less than four inches and the resonant frequency of your MSM system getting too high for the wall to isolate properly. For a good isolation, you need a total wall thickness of about 10 inches, as follows: Leaf one: (two layers of 5/8 drywall) = 1 1/4" First stud frame = 3 1/2" Gap between stud frames= 1/2" Second stud frame = 3 1/2" Leaf Two (two layers of 5/8 drywall) = 1 1/4" By using the "beef up between the studs" technique on both leaves you can shave off a total of 1 1/4", getting you down to 8 3/4". Building one leaf inside-out would improve overall floor space by narrowing your air gap to exactly 4", but the "wall" would still be the same total thickness. Even if you do a staggered stud wall, with 2x4 studs on 2x6 plates, your total wall thickness is still going to be at least 7 3/8", and probably 8". Even if you went with only a sngle layer of drywall on each side of a staggered stud wall, it would still be 6 3/4" thick! I just don't see how you can possibly get a usable MSM wall down to just 5" thick! - Stuart -

Soundman202012 April 2010#19
You are not making any sense! first you say that your wall is only five inches thick, but now you say that it has a ten inch air gap inside it!!!! :shock: [edited to add:]
so you are saying we need to have a ten inch thick slab of rockwool between the front of one wall and back of the other?
No, I'm saying that for maximum isolation you should fill the gap between the LEAVES of your wall with rockwool. Not that you should fill the gap between two walls. You shouldn't even have two walls next to each other anyway: ("front of one wall to back of other wall") that would be a four-leaf system! :!: It seems to me that there are concepts here that you haven't grasped yet: Between one room and the next there is only ONE wall, not two walls. That single wall consists of two leaves. Each leaf consists of a one more layers of drywall attached to only ONE side of a stud frame. So there are TWO frames that make up the wall. Each frame has several layers of drywall on ONE side of it. The drywall itself is the leaf. The frame is just there to hold it up. The gap between the two leaves is filled with rockwool. The entire set of frames, drywall, gap and insulation, all together, makes up one single wall. - Stuart -
