Ethan Winer25 November 2005#7
Dobro, > Doable? < Sure! Problems from the low ceiling can be minimized with absorption, and the size may be small but it's not horribly small. The three main goals of acoustic treatment are: * Bass traps in as many corners as you can manage, including the wall-ceiling corners. * Mid/high frequency absorption at the first reflection points on the side walls and ceiling. * Some additional amount of mid/high absorption on any large areas of bare parallel surfaces, such as opposing walls or the ceiling if the floor is reflective. Once you've done this, you'll be in pretty good shape. --Ethan

Ethan Winer11 June 2006#13
dobro, > Why not have that flush panel cover the wall/ceiling angle? General principle, or a specific case? < Specific case, though I'd do it a little differently if I were adding those panels today. Note they are not flat on the wall, but spaced four inches away. This helps them absorb to a lower frequency, and also lets mids and highs get to the rear of the panel to be absorbed. Those portions of the walls are parallel and could foster flutter echo, so it's important that they absorb mids and highs as well as bass. The ceiling is 11 feet high at the top of that peak, so for someone like me who hates being on ladders climbing up there is not an attractive option at this point. :) In the "Room setup" video I recently added to my company's web site I describe this room and its treatment in detail. I also show what I'd do differently in those corners if I were doing it again today. If you have high-speed Internet you'll probably enjoy the video because it explains a lot about this stuff in detail. --Ethan
