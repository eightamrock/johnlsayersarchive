Now that i've finished buttering you up, here's my situation. ***scroll down for short version*** OVERVIEW I have been using the mother-in-law apartment above my detached garage as a "jam" room and (sorta)recording space. Permission has been granted by my wife to take this space to the next phase-building a studio. This will be initially for personal use, but who knows what the future holds. With that in mind, i want to build this space to it's fullest capacity. I've sold a few toys, so my starting budget is around $10,000. Nothing has been done to the space yet, and i don't intend to pick up a hammer until i have a solid plan. Ideally, i would like to have a decently sized control room, and a tracking room that can still be used as a practice area capable of fitting a set of drums, couple amps, P.A., and combo organ(which will be upgraded to a Hammond and Leslie when my skills are worthy). Come to think of it, an iso booth would be really cool(but not necessary if it isn't feasible). I know that this is a lot to fit into such a small space, but i've seen some large women stuffed into some minuscule pants, so i assume anything is possible. NOISE I have been using the space(untreated), for over a year without a complaint. The nearest neighbor is about 150 feet away, she called the cops on me when we were testing the p.a. system in my backyard for my wedding, but has yet to complain about the late night jam sessions. I want to increase my isolation from the outside world(lawn mowers, subwoofers on wheels, occasional small plane or helicopter), but i'm not trying to get super crazy with it. I am more concerned with sound control between control room and tracking room. STRUCTURE The detached garage is two floors, built on a slab, 2x4 exterior walls, T-111 exterior siding, drywall inside. The second floor is supported by 2"x10"x10ft joists(northern fir), on 16" centers carried by the wall on the outside, and a 3 1/2"x15" glu-lam beam running down the center of the garage. The floor is carpet over 3/4 plywood(except the bathroom which is tile), and the ceiling is plaster with a groovadelic swirl pattern. I haven't poked any holes, but i assume insulation in walls and ceiling is fiberglass. SKETCHUP The upstairs is 19'4"x21' with a bathroom in one corner,the untiled area is a shower. I could relocate the bathroom door around the corner from its current location if necessary. All ceilings are 7'9"(lame). The placement of windows and bathroom makes it difficult for me to make anything look decent(or maybe it's because i'm horrible at sketchup). The black area on the floor of my sketchup is where the stairs come up from below. I will be covering this opening, and building a porch with an outside door. The most probable location for this door would be on the wall with the bathroom and window, as close to the corner as possible. Ideally, this door would allow easy load in and out of instruments, without going through the control room. REVIEW(SHORT VERSION) -2nd floor mother-in-law apartment above detached garage 19'4"x21' -low ceilings 7'9" -existing bathroom in corner -want decent sized control room, and big enough tracking room for practice(iso booth would be super cool) -i am no good at sketchup, so i come to the experts for advice. -budget $10,000 to start -sketchup of current condition included -if i've omitted anything, please don't hesitate to tell me Thanks in advance for any advice, assistance, or anything else you have to offer.

gullfo10 March 2007#12
you can use John's inside-out wall design to get the isolation and much of the treatment in one construct. this will help in getting the rooms balanced and reduce the extra treatments. the corners should still get something to bass trap, and the rear of the CR is a big one of those. the exposed walls can all be slats because the interior walls (between CR and live room) are sheetrock. probably slightly less isolation because of the closeness of the space between the live room and CR walls but shouldn't be a major issue. in the CR, you'll also add a cloud overhead and a couple of 1" 703 type absorpers on the sides where you would get reflections from the sidewall. after that, you might have a couple of other spots to touch up in the live room with an hanging 703 type absorber here or there (overhead or whereever you find you need it)

gullfo24 March 2007#14
g-raj wrote:
I have been contemplating using inside out walls, but i'm assuming that i either have to go all inside out or all regular. If two wall join, can one be inside out, and the other one regular? Is there a way to seal the joint as effectively as a regular corner?
no reason you couldn't - the key would be sealing them at the joint and the face of the regular wall would effectively be level with the slats. its behind the slats and drywall are studs + caulk etc.. forming the seal. you could to the 9 degree splay with sliders or the smaller doors. if you can double the walls and door you'll get more isolation but it will cost more. generally speaking exterior grade - decent quality sliders seal pretty well but all that glass will be much less isolation than the walls. you could go with the smaller doors with windows in them which will mean less holes in the walls.
