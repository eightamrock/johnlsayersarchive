For which room? CR acoustics is usually very different from LR acoustics, and vocal booths are also different again. CR acoustics should be as neutral as possible, in both frequency and time. The specifications for control rooms are laid out in ITU-R BS1116-1 and ITU-R BS775-1, which are probably available on-line. EU-3276 are another alternative, that say pretty much the same thing. And for the USA, the equivalent is AESTD1001.0.01-05 (although that is more for 5.1 than plain stereo rooms). All of those lay out the basic requirements in terms of floor area, room volume, speaker geometry, frequency response, decay response, etc. But live rooms are a different thing: Live rooms are supposed to have some type of "character" that makes them attractive. They are supposed to have a "sound" of their own that makes them a place where people want to play and record, since the room "sounds good". One interesting option for live rooms is to make the acoustics variable, so that you can change the sound as needed by opening or closing panels to reveal or hide different types of treatment. And in general, isolation booths are made pretty "dead" acoustically, since they are so small to start with. For a better understanding of basic acoustics, I'd suggest "Master Handbook of Acoustics" by F. Alton Everest.

For maximum effect, superchunks should run the full height of the room, from floor to ceiling. Room modes all terminate in corners, so the best place to treat them all is in the corners. And the best of the best is where three surfaces meet: wall/wall/floor and wall/wall/ceiling.
I assume the distance between windows matters (talking about the street facing window). If i space them 15cm apart, and lay the space inbetween with glasswool or rockwool (which would benefit me more?) would that prevent even more sound from coming through?
A two-leaf isolation wall is a tuned system that resonates at a certain frequency. You need to tune that frequency as low as possible, and no higher than half of the lowest frequency that you need to isolate. Tuning is accomplished with two things: the mass of each leaf, and the size of the air gap between them. Since the windows are part of the wall, they have to maintain the tuning, so they need to be of the same surface density as the wall, and they need to be the same distance apart. In fact the mass needs to be a bit higher, or the air gap a bit bigger, since it is impossible to put damping (insulation) between the two panes of glass, the way you wold be tween the two leaves in the rest of the wall. Theoretically, the distance between the glass panes has to be 1.414 times greater than the distance between the leaves in the rest of the wall, to compensate minimally for the missing insulation.
