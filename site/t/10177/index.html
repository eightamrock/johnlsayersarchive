clunk9 February 2008#1
Hi my name is Brian O’Callaghan, I am writing for help and guidance in the construction of a new studio I am planning to build with a friend Steve Fearnley. The studio will be located in Warrenpoint, N Ireland and we hope to be able to operate the studio both as a commercial venture and as a base for our own music creation. We would welcome any advice anyone can offer and greatly appreciate your help. Its our intention to document or own efforts here as best we can. I’ve done my best here to give a rough idea of the buildings we will be creating the studio in and some of the areas we will be concentrating on . Our budget is small (about £ 8,000 or $16,000) but we’re hoping to make best use of what we have and will be doing most of the work ourselves. Ok some details about the space we will be using We have been offered the opportunity to rent 3 rooms in an old outhouse located in the grounds of a country estate. ( the first jpeg shows a rough floor plan with pics of the external surroundings of the rooms) We have permission to make room alterations , though some of the changes we will make need to be reversible ( for example we can block off some of the doorways, but they must be easily reopened if ever we leave the studio) . The building itself was built circa 1886 and falls under restrictions as a ‘listed’ building which means no alterations can be made to it’s physical appearance from the outside, but we are free to make alterations internally. The estate presently operates as a working farm with a variety of livestock and farm machinery being the main sources of noise pollution. There is a concrete yard to one side of the studio building and this is also up for rental, we don’t know what the likely noise pollution will be from this, but the yard has bays suitable for storing sand and stone and would possibly attract a builder. Our landlord is our nearest neighbour so we have no worries about complaints from the neighbours about noise coming from the studio ( unless we fail to pay the rent ). It’s hard to say what music will be played as it will vary from heavy drum n bass to rock, blues and folk. Our set-up includes equipment for mobile recording so we will probably be mixing some live sets in addition to any artists we record in house. About the rooms The rooms are basically empty shells at the moment so we have the ‘luxury’ of being able to make a number of choices on how we design various features. Both the external and existing internal walls are 0.45m thick and are coursed granite rubble with stepped brick dressings to openings and a single brick eaves course. The roof is an A frame timber construction with slates on the exterior and the rafters are exposed to the rooms. The timbers used are 5” * 1.5” at 16” spacings. All three rooms have recently laid concrete floors and we have been told the floors have 4 inches of concrete on hardcore, we don‘t know the mix ratio of concrete used. The landlord has put a drainage channel under the floor with outlets to each room. Room dimensions Room 1 Control Room : length 4.5m, width 4.15m, height to top of wall 3.2m, height to middle of roof 5.1m. Room 2 Live/Tracking room: length 6.6m, width 4.15m, height to top of wall 2.9m, height to middle of roof 4.55m Room 3 General purpose : length 8.9m, width 4.15m, height to top of wall 3.15m, height to middle of roof 4.8m. Doorways: Room 1 has 3 doorways, 1 door leads to a grass ‘courtyard area’ and is 1.24m wide, 2.47m high and .45m deep, 1 to a veranda area 1.0 m wide * 2.1m high, and the third into room 2 is 1m wide* 2.1m high. Room 2 has 4 doorways, 1 leads to a courtyard 1.2m * 2.1m,1 to a concrete yard 1.0m * 2.1m, the last to room 3 1.0m * 2.1 m. Room 3 has 3 doorways, the one leading to room 2, a double doorway to the courtyard area 1.5m * 2.1m + arch 0.4m and a door leading to part of the building we will not be renting. Windows: Room 1 has 1 window dimensions : 1.0m * 1.5m high Room 2 has 1 window dimensions : 1.1m * 1.45 high Room 3 has 2 windows .6m * 1.35m high (arched) Wall vents: There are a number of wall vents, each is approximately 0.3m * 0.3m and run through the wall to the yard area. We are proposing to use Room 1 as the control room and Room 2 as the live/tracking room, these will form the main basis of the studio. Room 3 will be subdivided to create a toilet, a small office, and an area for people to hang out in with kitchen facilities. We may also use the open areas here for occasional recording. At present there is no electric supply to the rooms and we will have to install all electrics, (we have ordered the electric supply). Our ideas/plans Our starting budget is £ 8,000 ( approx $ 16,000 USD ) some of which is needed to purchase equipment such as snakes etc… I know this is quite small but we feel that given the mass of the walls we should be able to achieve a high level of sound isolation for most applications without having to build rooms inside rooms, although we do intend to build a vocal booth in the tracking room. We have identified the roof, doorways, windows and wall vents as being our main vulnerable spots and our plan is to tackle these first, test the levels and then make further adjustments as necessary . For the doorways we intend installing double doors in each doorway, we will most likely use heavy firedoors for most of these but between the control room and tracking room the doors will need to have glass. For the windows we will install second double glazed windows. We will seek advice on the necessity of the wall vents, but assuming they aren’t needed it is our intention to close and insulate these. If room ventilation is needed then we may install an air-conditioning unit and use one of the vents to carry the ducting for this. The roof is our main worry at present, I have been reading through this forum and it lead me to our own local building control (permits, code type stuff) and this has raised some questions and worries about our plans to isolate the roof. Put simply can the present roof structure take the additional loads imposed by our plan and if not is there another way we can produce the desired result. I have included a simple plan of our proposed roof isolation strategy and would be grateful if anyone with good knowledge of these things could point us in the right direction. The rafters and cross bracing timbers are 38mm * 122mm, I'm not sure of the type of timber. One worry is that the cross bracing may not be capable of carrying the intended load of 2 sheets of drywall, the only calculations for safe spans I can find are for ceiling joists supported by a wall, as the cross bracing is simply nailed to the rafters I'm not sure if the safe spans I have identified are relevant. Is there a safe way to strengthen the support for the cross bracing or should I be looking for other ways of isolating the roof. Steve reckons that adding additional joists to the other side of the rafters using bolts will create a safe structure to carry the load required. Any advice here would be of great help. The floor has also become a source of concern, we had started out with the notion of building a floating floor, but as I read through the forum and the debate on to whether to float or not to float, I have become more and more confused. With the base of concrete we have do we need a floating floor or would we just be creating a new set of problems for ourselves? Could we possibly experience problems in the future with our present floor if for example a builder begins to use the yards beside us and regularly uses a scoop bucket to pick-up sand from the pit? Does a wooden floor set on rubber cradles qualify as a floating floor? If we build a wooden floor what problems might we encounter and how do we overcome these? ( a raised wooden floor would allow us the easiest way to run cabling from one room to another, our only other realistic solution for this will be to drill holes through the walls which may be quite problematic) At the moment we are thinking of laying a 2” screed floor to level the present floor and creating channels in this to run the cabling, what are your ideas? When we have completed the basic sound isolation of the rooms our plan is to build a vocal booth in the tracking room. We have plenty of space and are hoping to build the booth with an exterior dimension of 8’ * 8’ * 8‘ (approx), I will post our design for this later. When all this has been completed we will then begin to work on improving the acoustics of the rooms and in particular the acoustics of the control room. I will post more details on this later. I intend keeping a diary of our progress here and share our experience and hope to find answers to the some of questions we will face. I would welcome any views people have on our solutions and will do my best to provide the best information I can. Thank you in advance for your assistance.

frostgfx16 May 2008#8
Clunk etc.,.. I would consider doing something like JS Inside Out walls for the Vocal Booth if it's not to late... It it too late for that, then double up your sheetrock, first layer hung vertically, second horizontally. Don't forget to stgger the joints/corners and fill with backer rod and/or acoustic sealant. When you have gotten that far. Shine a bright light over the whole room and stand outside to check for light/air leaks. Leave about 4-6" air space between your rooms. Each room is it's own little 'house' in that the walls and ceilings don't touch the adjoining room. MASS / AIR / MASS is your friend. Insulate all the walls with Roxul SAFB or similar in your area of the world. Best of luck on your build, Jon Frost Easthampton, MA USA

clunk17 July 2008#20
Hi guys, thx for the compliments, means a lot. I really don't know where you would have seen such an original design before Lou :wink: Gash on the floor I have checked but it seems I have no pics of the floor during construction but here it is from ground up - The whole floor sits on 12 2" x 2" x 1/2" neoprene pucks. I simply phoned the company that supplied the neoprene and gave them the approximate weight of the room ( just over 2 tons) and asked them to calculate the amount of neoprene I should use to obtain a 7% compression and a few hours later they got back and told me. Not very scientific but looking through other threads the amount we have used seems fairly consistent with what others have done. We placed 8 of the pucks around the perimeter of the room at the corners and midway between the corners, and the remaining pucks towards the middle of the floor. Hopefully this will give our room the correct spring to create the desired effect. Between the pucks and the floor frame we have attached 2 layers of 1/4" ply, mainly to hold in the sand. There is a plastic membrane attached on top of the ply to help keep the sand dry. The floor frame is made using 2 x 4 timbers and is filled with 1/2 ton of kiln dried sand. We were a little short on the sand and used some plasterboard off cuts to help fill the compartments. Next there is a layer of 3/4" ply and finally a layer of 3/4" MDF. We have also put pipes in to run our electric and audio cabling into the booth. We had put the floor together prior to getting the sand to test it out and it acted as a great amplifier. Now with the sand in place the floor sounds totally different, nice and dead. The pics below show a little of the detail, best I can do I'm afraid. All the best, Clunk
