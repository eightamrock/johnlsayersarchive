Soundman202017 June 2009#9
Two comments: One to agree with Lil: Your room is not symmetrical. In order to have symmetry you need to make either: A) both side walls straight (not splayed) or B) both splayed. On splayed and one not splayed is going to mess up your stereo image and sound staging. The other about your speaker angles and mix position. To me, it looks like your speaker axes are intersecting at 90 degrees, when it should be 60 degrees. With a room that big, you certainly don't need to tighten your speaker angles so much! - Stuart -

Soundman202017 June 2009#12
Thinking tube traps around the mix position, speaker placement nearish the front wall, and maintaining as much open space for flexibility in the room.... However it will be me for the most hours, and it has to sound good!
You have the space, so I'd say get your speakers well away from the walls, to avoid nasty artifacts like comb-filtering. I'm trying to find my reference on that, but I seem to recall something like 2m (roughly 6'6") as being the minimum distance away from walls for that. If you can't afford 2m of separation, and you really want those speakers up close to the front wall, and you really want them to sound good, then how about soffit mounting them? (More correctly called "flush mounting", but everyone seems to call it "soffit mounting" these days....). That will tighten up your bass and imaging, and will look cool as well. It's a bit more work, but it will allow you to put the speakers practically right up against the front wall, and leave the entire rest of the room free (for playing hockey, football, golf, .... :) that's a big room! Envy! Envy!) What's the MAIN purpose of the room, by the way? I saw that you listed many purposes, but I guess there is one overriding one? Have you thought about maybe dividing it into a control room at one end, and a large live room at the other? You have the space. By the way, I guess your family must really love listening to what you do! :) And also that you don't mind ambient noises getting into your recordings: wind, rain, thunder, dogs barking, kids playing, cars arriving and leaving, people talking, radios, TV's, lawn mowers, phones ringing, washing machines, dish washers and hair dryers running, that kind of thing. The reason I say that is because I see from the plans that there isn't much isolation considered for the room: All that I see is RC on the existing wall, but that probably won't help you any, since the rest of the room is directly coupled to the entire house. Plus, you only have ordinary simple doors between room and house. Are you SURE that you don't need any isolation in that room? Can you really make recordings like that? Does your family really love hearing you work that much? :) - Stuart -
