Considering merrie auld England's fairly constant moist state and the propensity of brick to like moisture, you're probably right; however, this will raise hell with m-a-m performance (holes in one of your mass layers) Question 3 I need to seal the fireplace up - we're keeping it in for aesthetics for when we move eventually, but will sealing it cause damp problems - normally fireplaces have an air vent in them even when they've been sealed. Do you have a pic of the fireplace in question? You may be able to leave the chimney open (is there a cap on top?) and build something similar to a window plug. Question 4 The window is currently double glazed UPVC. I'd like to add some secondary glazing - have you got any tips on this? I'd prefer not to have to build a window, and I'd like one that opens. Everything is a compromise; if you can find a similar (or identical) window and mount it as far away from the existing one as possible, you'll improve m-a-m performance of the window with the wider air space between panes. Caulking up any weep holes will help isolation, but can cause damp problems. Question 5 How do I attach my acoustic treatment to my newly sealed plasterboard ceiling and walls without drilling through them and breaking the seals? You can't; if you've insulated and used resilient mounts, I'd mount any relatively heavy treatments so the screws penetrate your resilient bars (assuming you still know where they are) and if mounting lighter absorbers, you can just use drywall anchors (the little plastic cones you insert before driving a screw) - if you use the anchors, put a dab of caulk into the hole before inserting the anchor. The insulation inside the wall and the tiny (or non-existent) hole won't be noticeable in isolation. As to the RC question, the USG manual shows an extra RC where sheets of gypsum meet, so each has its own resilient bar to mount to; makes it much easier to mount the gyp to the resilient bars, since they tend to be quite narrow from some manufacturers - something like the attached sketch... here's a response frmo the SOS site, but I'd welcome anyone else's thoughts! (Knightfly are you around sir?): I tend to think it would be a waste of time. Mass law states that when you double the mass of a wall, you increase soundproofing by 6db, although in real world application it's probably slightly less than that. Now consider that: 1. You'd be increasing the mass of the stud wall by only 50%, which in and of itself would gain maybe 2-3db sound reduction. 2. The total isolation of the wall is combination of the effects of each leaf. This is not derived by simply adding the transmission loss of the two leaves, it's more complicated than that, but basically the TL of one leaf is "applied" to that of the other, using a formula, and the final result is somewhat better than that leaf would be on its own, though nothing like both leaves simply added together. So, that 2-3db becomes maybe 1-2db (rough guess - there's a formula around somewhere on the net for calculating this if you really want to). 3. As you say the ceiling (and the rest of the room) will only be two layers anyway, and sound will certainly radiate through the other surfaces into the airgap into your neighbour's wall. It's hard to say here whether just beefing up one wall will do anything at all. So that 1-2db becomes maybe 1db at very best. More likely less.
