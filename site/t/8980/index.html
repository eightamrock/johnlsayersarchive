Hi Nightmusic, I'm probably one of the most sensitive people on the forum when it comes to flood-related issues... Perhaps second to forum member Andrew McMaster, who sustained major flood damage from Hurricane Katrina a year and a half ago. I live in what is arguably the most at-risk floodplain in all of the United States. :evil: (Don't even get me started on how that came to be! :roll:) The product to which you are referring is ideal for some scenarios, like tile... But it's a real pain to work with as a total substitute for drywall. It's extremely heavy, is much more difficult to cut than regular drywall, and it's a lot more expensive. I personally would not recommend its use as a wholesale drywall substitute in a studio build. Others may disagree, but that's my take. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward21 June 2007#4
Actually, it might not be such a bad idea for you to use the product for the bottom two feet or so, if that's the extent of the water level you'd experience. However, you'd have to do more than that -- you'd somehow have to have framework that could withstand the water, a way to air out the moisture so that you don't end up with mold (sometimes toxic! :shock:) in the walls, and of course your wiring would havle to be kept safe... :roll: :!: I would actually recommend that you consult with a water damage remediation specialist/contractor on this issue. They deal with the situation day-in and day-out. They can give you a clearer picture of what typically has to be done after a flood to make the space habitable again. What you don't want to do is use the product, falsely thinking that you're assured of no damage, only to find out after you get hit by a flood that you'd still have to gut it all out and start over again. :roll: FYI, mineral wool insulation, I think, can withstand a lot of moisture and not get damaged. I don't know if it would survive a flood though... :roll: ...You do not want to put it in plastic bags though. It has to "breathe" in order to function as "air" in your mass-air-mass system. There are other reasons as well (e.g., trapping vapor inside the bags) that make it a bad idea. In a way, I'm jealous of you. ;-) If you get flooded out, you'll be under water by a foot or two? Dude, if the levees are compromised in my area, my entire house, peak of roof and all, is completely underwater within a day. :evil: Hopefully the half-billion dollars we will be spending on levee fortification over the next three or four years (or more :x) will make such a thing very unlikely to happen in my lifetime... :roll: Yes, we have flood insurance.... But it's expensive and it does not make you "whole" again. If it happens to us, we'll probably take a hit of about $100,000 to $150,000, out of pocket. (And, no, our pockets do not go that deep! :cry: By the way, I also bring to the discussion an experience of having a plumbing disaster in our home three years ago -- only about an inch of water throughout half our home, but it was a $75,000 hit (covered by the builder, thank goodness!), and we were forced to move out of the house for three weeks while the repairwork was done. :roll: What an experience. And that was an inch!! :shock: --Keith :mrgreen:
