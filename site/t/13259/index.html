xSpace17 July 2009#11
I think it has been called that, but I don't think that a typical residential wall is actually mass spring mass in the way we might strive for. In the same respect that a regular concrete block is not a two leaf because it has connections from front to rear and left to right and the air space is broken up at each block. I think there actually has to be no connection between the two panels in order to be at least mass-mass and a contiguous enclosed air cavity between the panels to be a spring. Still, there is an air spring at least at 16 inches on center that can create this effect isn't there? The flanking of the assembly because of the connections might be the answer I am looking for. Good question, good luck

xSpace17 July 2009#13
Every doubling of mass gives you a 3Db gain and potentially a 4-5 point increase in the STC rating. It is easy to see how just simply doubling the mass will require more material overall and gives limited increases. Your existing room dimensions are too small to decouple with another framed wall to justify the work involved...unless you are in serious pursuit of isolation:) . Adding mass will only get you so far, but is better then nothing. If you are not prepared to do the work required to establish some form of decoupling of the wall panels (the Insane approach), then based on what the GreenGlueCompany website information presents, their product will add additional performance to a wall like you mention. http://www.greengluecompany.com/upgradi ... gWalls.php "All walls are sheetrock, over studs 16" on center. Pink fluffy fiberglass behind all sheetrock. Block walls with a inch air gap (for ventilation purposes due to dampness)" Do you have a picture of this assembly yet? Is this a basement?
