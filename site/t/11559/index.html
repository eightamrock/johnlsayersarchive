chuckswenson wrote:
how do I actually take the EQ measurements?
This article explains how I use the ETF software, but the principles apply to all such programs.
For instance, is my large-diaphragm condenser mic (studio projects C2) appropriate for the job?
See this: Comparison of Ten Measuring Microphones
Should I have both speakers on?
Yes. The reason we measure a room's bass response is to know how things will sound at the listener's ears. In most pop music the bass and kick drum are panned to the center, meaning it comes from the left and right speakers equally. So from my perspective, at bass frequencies anyway, measuring a room with both speakers active more closely emulates how real music will be played. That said, I think it's good to also measure each speaker separately for additional confirmation.
Where do I position *myself* when taking the measurements?
Probably out of the way if possible. I'll also add that measuring a room is not strictly needed. The main reason I tell people to measure their room is so they'll understand how awful it really is. Most people have no idea how skewed the LF response is in their room. It's not like you need to identify problem frequencies to know how to approach treatment. Regardless of what is measured, you need as much bass trapping as possible, and absorption at the side-wall and ceiling reflection points. Just doing that will get you 90 percent of the way there for any room. --Ethan
