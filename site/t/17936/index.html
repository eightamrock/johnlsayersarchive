Excellent! That's the best time to find this forum. Gives you plenty of time to plan things properly and understand the principles of acoustics, before you start building. We get a lot of people coming here, saying things like "I start building tomorrow, and I'm going to carpet the walls and put egg crates on the ceiling because that's the best way to do it"... :) So you are being very wise in starting to plan years in advance of the build.
Is it important to follow room ratios?
To a certain extent, yes. But there is no need to go overboard. Room ratios are only one aspect of design, and all they tell you is that your room modes will be distributed evenly across the low end of the spectrum, which is the most important. As long as you stay away from the bad ratios and get close to one of the good ratios, you'll be fine. Despite what you read on some web sites, there is no such thing as a "perfect" ratio, or a "golden" ratio. There are only a whole bunch of bad ratios, and a small set of good ratios. Also, ratios are more important for the control room, and less important for the live room. The control room needs to be designed for neutral acoustics, in both the frequency domain and the time domain. There are a handful of recommendations and specs for control rooms, from organizations such as ITU, EBU, Dolby, and others, and they are pretty similar overall. As long as you get close to their recommendations in your design, you'll be fine.

I honestly have no clue what to expect price wise, and am trying to see what a realistic budget would be.
Building a studio costs about the same as building an upscale house. So call around contractors in your area and ask for their flat rate per square foot for building a quality home in your area. The cost for your studio will be a bit higher, so add 20% to that to get a rough ball-park figure.
I'm hoping I can build the following plan with somewhere around $20,000-$35,000.
That seems to be very much on the low side. I'm not sure what contractors charge in your area, but I very much doubt that they will do it for US$ 23 per square foot. (You show roughly 1500 square feet, and a max budget of US$ 35k). I would suspect that you'd need a budget of at least four times that. Or reduce the size of the facility to one quarter of what you show.
I'm going to use double wall construction and plan to pour separate concrete slab foundations for each room as recommended in Rod Gervais' Build it Like the Pros.
For separate slabs, add something extra to the budget: That will increase isolation, yes, but it also increases the complexity of the build, and thus the cost. Same thing applies to 16 foot ceilings: it can be done, but increases the cost. So the question is: Do you need that much isolation? You can reduce costs by simplifying the construction, and a large part of that depends on the actual number of decibels of isolation that you need. If you can relax your requirements in that area, then you can reduce the overall cost. So maybe your first order of business should be to determine how much isolation you need, in real objective terms (decibels), not just in subjective terms. - Stuart -
