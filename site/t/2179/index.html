dymaxian25 August 2004#8
You guys would be suprised how many architects- REGISTERED architects- don't have a clue about acoustics. They didn't give us any training on acoustics at all when I was in school- before I found this site and recording.org, all i knew about sound control I learned from designing animal hospitals (those dogs get LOUD when they wake up from surgical anaesthetic...) We dont mean to be jerks around here, psychotroll. We really don't. The trouble is we get a lot of people who just log on, post a question that we've answered a thousand times, and then don't bother reading thru the rest of the forum. There's not too many general design questions that haven't been answered on here before. It's a hazard of running a message board such as this. The basics of construction techniques are in the sticky threads of the Studio Construction part of this forum. Read them all if you haven't. The Studio Design part is mostly overall layouts. These two sections will probably answer 90% of your questions. As far as the size of a studio, it depends on a lot of stuff. If the studio only has one engineer (which will be 90% of what is done on this forum- it's mostly guys building their own studio) then there's little point in having more than one control room, and a single control room will probably only service one or two tracking rooms and a couple isolation booths. Much more than that, and you're talking about having another control room group. Which is fine if you're talking about a larger studio company that employs multiple engineers. Before you go too far with your thesis project, you'll need to nail down the particulars about the theoretical company you'd be designing this for. Hope this helps a little bit...
