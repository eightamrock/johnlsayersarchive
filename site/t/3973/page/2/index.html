in this construction (inside out ceiling), the inner ceiling probably must be installed in sections ... there is no way to attach the drywall to the inside of the inner leaf frame after that frame is in place, correct? just as with the walls, the walls must be built on the ground and then lifted into place. so, the ceiling must be built in modules with gypsum in place and then lifted into place on top of the walls and screwed to the support beams. i wonder how big a section it is practical to build, and how many people it takes to lift this into place and attach it. or is there another way? also, i'd anticipate difficulty in positioning the modules in corners ... because the corner module would have to be slightly (3.5 inches) bigger than the hole that it filled in TWO directions. if you can picture this: in a corner, a ceiling module has to be angled up and over the top plate on two walls at once, something that i expect to be impossible. i imagine one might have to add horizontal support cleats to the top edge of the wall to hold these modules up, rather than set them directly on the inner wall top plates. this is not a problem really, but it's the only way i can figure to do it. this would be a horizontal 2x4 in the top corner where the wall/ceiling meets. so the ceiling would not rest on the top plate of the wall but on this cleat. well there is one other way: put the ceiling up first with temporary supports (still in sections), and then tilt up the walls after. but this would be much too difficult i think. or maybe i'm missing something obvious :oops:

http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=1414 I'm pretty sure you have to be logged on to download - this will require you joining the forum. you want this http://forum.studiotips.com/download.php?id=1141 Don't wait too long; I'm not sure how long these files will be available since there's been a major emotional upset at that site. ONce you have the file, plug in your outer wall values and proposed inner wall mass, then adjust the air gap (not between frames, but between inner surfaces of panels) to get resonance - the lower the resonance the better the isolation, ideal is 1.5 to 2 OCTAVES below the lowest frequency you want to stop. I've yet to play with this particular calculator, but can probably help if you get lost... Steve
