stuntbutt21 April 2009#6
I haven't done it but I believe the idea is to temporarily hold each layer with finish nails. Backer rod and caulk each layer with the nails in place by cutting the backer rod to slip behind the nails. When you add the second layer of drywall the nails get pushed into the previous layer. The same is true when you add your 1x3 cleats to permanently hold everything. Obviously you want to use the smallest/skinniest finish nails feasible. Also, if you have nails protruding through the sub floor, there was a thread a while ago where Rod suggested using 1" or 1.5" foam board insulation as the first layer and just let the nails stick into the foam instead of trying to trim them flush with the underside of the sub floor.

xSpace26 April 2009#12
Determine the thickness of both pieces of drywall combined or determine the thickness of whatever material you will be using for this mass loading. Once you know this measurement, then measure down from the overhead subfloor and make a mark at this known distance. You will need to have this measurement available down the entire length of the joist as you will need it for this process. I would not use a chalk line. This would not maintain the distance from the subfloor down to your current mark(in the very real event of crowned joists). Install one side of your ledger to the entire length of the joist in question. After the ledger is installed, you can place your piece(s) into the lip created by the ledger. It may take three hands to do this, so be prepared. Have a short piece of ledger material available while you are doing this and you can screw it as a temp piece underneath the mass(opposite the ledger you have it resting on) you just installed until you are ready to install the full ledger piece.
