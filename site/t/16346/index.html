Bass hanger orientation

Started by Rhino on 27 June 2011. 11 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 16346.

Rhino27 June 2011#1
It seems bass hangers are a bit of a black art, but I've been searching the forum to try and find some accepted guidelines. Here's what I've come up with: - Each hanger should be constructed with a sheet of softboard/homasote sandwiched between 2 fibreglass batts. - Although some (Knightfly, Barefoot) recommend using a "limp and dense" material like linoleum instead of the softboard / homasote. - Hangers should be kept 8" - 12" apart. - The longer the length of hanger (including length of hanging mechanism (chain, cord, pipe strapping, etc)) the lower the frequency affected. - Hangers should be placed so as best to interrupt the flow of sound, such as a herringbone pattern. - Or, John's preferred method is to place hangers on modal points (continually dividing the space in half and placing a hanger on each point, until there's no room left). I have two ceiling cavities in which to place bass hangers, one at the front of the control room, and one at the back. Right now I'm focusing on the back of the room.

I plan on the pinkbatt-softboard-pinkbatt approach. The slope of the ceiling dictates that each hanger panel will be a different height, presumably allowing for a range of frequencies to be affected. Question: What do you think is the best orientation for bass hangers at the back of the room? Are either of these ideas on the right track?
