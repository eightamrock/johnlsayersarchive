camistan19 April 2006#1
Greetings! I need to get some suggestions on some type of material (acoustically friendly :) ) that comes in 1/8" size to use as a fill-in material for my beefed up ceiling. The upper and lower supports on my "I" joist is 1 & 3/8" in height each. If I put 2 sheets of 5/8" drywall below my subfloor, that will = 1 & 1/4" so I'm 1/8" shy to make it flush with the bottom of my upper "I" joist (see drawing 2 & 3) By having all the "mass" flush with the bottom of my upper "I" Joist support, I can support the weight of my drywall by having wood braces screwed into the upper "I" Joist support. (see 4th drawing) These strips will be spaced apart and spanning the cavity length. I have already had a structural engineer to tell me my "joist" will support 4 sheets of 5/8" drywall. (drawing 4) However, by knowing here that it's recommended to have the drywall "screwed" into the subfloor, I need to know that my subfloor can handle the extra weight. I may be "waiting a while" from my structural engineer to get a response concerning this issue, so I'm asking this now in the event my subfloor can't support the weight. I have to admit, I'm not too keen about screwing into my subfloor, but as long as there's ample room for a screw to "secure" itself in the subfloor (without coming close to coming through my floor! :shock: ) along with supporting the weight of the drywall, I'm ok. I'm going to check other houses they're constructing in my area to see just how thick the subfloors are. I also need suggestions for a "strong" thickness of wood for the braces supporting the drywall. 1/4"?? 1/2"?? 3/4"?? And also how far do the screws need to go into a subfloor to secure the weight of the drywall? (Sure hope my subfloor is thick enough!!) There may be other suggestions on how to do this, so if there are, I'm all EARS...er, I mean EYES... :D Thanx!

knightfly10 May 2006#7
Yeah, you did; I'd go for 5/8" penetration into your subfloor to be safe; I'd also use full schedule along both edges of EACH layer of "beef", (8" O.C.), and offset the second layer's screws so you have no "collisions" - so the first layer would get screws along each edge (and a couple across each end of each piece) spaced every 8", and the second layer would get the same spacing but offset by 4".
