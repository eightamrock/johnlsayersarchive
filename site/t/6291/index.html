Hi John and thanks for this site which is invaluable to a novice in studio design such as me. I'm just about to give up full time employment to dedicate my time to music, both playing and recording. My goal is to carry out some personal recording and eventually to record other performers. I'm at the stage of designing a studio which I intend to build in a robust corrugated iron shed which stands adjacent to my house. I'm in a very quiet location on the outskirts of Canberra, Australia. My nearest neighbour is about 1km away and I have very little noise pollution to concern me. Which brings me to my first point: because of the low noise risk, I am considering building a single frame lining inside the shed and concentrating my budget on good quality isolation between the different rooms of the studio. Does this sound sensible and are there any suggestions regarding materials to use in the lining to give the best acoustic response as opposed to noise protection? The shed dimensions are 30' x 20' with a pitched roof 8' in height sloping down to 7'. The amenities area is already there but the entrance to the right is something I will construct. It stands on a concrete base. To achieve the best isolation between the various studio rooms, is a floating floor essential - you will notice that I haven't got that much height to play with. I have attached a .png snapshot of my Sketchup design. Unfortunately I'm not expert enough in Sketchup to know if it's possible to reduce the 1.2Mb file that my design sits in. As for budget, I've got about $50K Aus to dedicate to this project but this will include purchasing the first fit of recording equipment. I suppose what I'm after in this first instalment is some assurance that my design has no obvious flaws and that my considerations on noise proofing aren't way off the mark. Any advice will be most gratefully received.
