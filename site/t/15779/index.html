gullfo25 March 2011#4
so the impact noises are more direct. so you probably want to create an inner shell to the room to decouple the walls and ceiling and then a damped membrane floor to reduce the transfer there. it will be tough on such a small budget. you might consider removing existing drywall/plaster (if its an option) and isolation clips and hat (furring) channel on the existing ceiling and walls. if not, maybe agglomerated foam (rebonded) on the existing plaster and then layers of drywall on isolation clips/channel to create as much of damped membrane on the walls and ceiling and avoid too much air space between the layers.

gullfo5 April 2011#6
speaker angles should be 30° if possible to form an (overall) 60° per leg triangle. you could build out the side walls using 15° treatments like a saw tooth to help - using slats on the faces going back, and absorption on the leading edges. triple leaf is never ideal but if you have limitations that push you into that, then you have to live with it. what are the isolation clips and channel attached to? the shared brick walls or an standalone frame on the floated floor?
