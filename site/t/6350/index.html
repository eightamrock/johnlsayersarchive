knightfly2 June 2006#4
Can you post a sketch showing the roof beams, plus whatever else you're proposing to add on top of them? As to radiant barrier ply, here's what one manufacturer says - Radiant Ply FAQ From that, it sounds like you'd need to put the radiant ply on the INNER layer of the outer leaf, with the silver side facing inward. They also say you should have at least an inch of air space next to the silver side, so you can't do a complete fill of insulation to improve damping of your roof/ceiling - this will cost you at least a couple of dB, possibly more. In addition, with solid coupling between inner mass and outer mass, you'll not have as good mid and high frequency isolation as you would with either resilient couplings or separate frames, but the lows will be about the same because they are primarily controlled by the air gap between inner and outer mass. For this reason, if the existing construction can handle the weight it would help you to use wider boards as spacers/rafters between inner and outer mass - Also, because the foil side of the radiant barrier needs to be to the inside on roofs any extra outer leaf mass would need to be plywood, which isn't as dense as drywall or mdf but is more durable. Something else that can help this outer mass is to use TWO layers of 30# felt under the shingles instead of ONE layer of 15# felt like most roofers tend to use - you'd need to pay a bit more for this, but it would help the outer mass layer somewhat as well as "self-seal" better around all those shingle nails. HTH... Steve (Oh, and yes this would be a 2-leaf barrier, unless you're adding another ceiling you didn't mention)
