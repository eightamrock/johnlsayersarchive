len-morgan22 May 2006#5
Are your slats going to go over this framing? If so, I think you should save yourself the time and trouble (not to mention money) unless you just want the LOOK of a slat wall. You will have virtually NO DEPTH and therefore the frequency is going to be very high. Did you use the slatted wall calculator on this site? It will tell you what frequency(ies) you can expect to get out of it. Just throwing wooden slats up on a wall won't do very much for you but it might look nice. len

Kelly S. wrote:
Keith - Your question about the outlets have been keeping me up at night :roll: . To be honest with you, I had considered drilling a hole through the sole plate and running a power strip through it, then caulking around both sides of the cord. I had also considered extending the outlet to a 2x4 on the resonator wall, similar to what I am considering doing with the wall plate in the booth. What would you suggest?
Deep breaths... Kelly, there's a reason this is keeping you up at night. Listen to that voice. You cannot cannot cannot simply plug an object (in this case, a power strip) into an outlet and then permanently or even semipermanently block access to the outlet. In fact, you can't do that with an outlet even with nothing connected to it. You can't even move the outlet to the inner wall frame and use the original outlet box as a junction box, because the junction box needs to be accessible too. All of these are serious electrical code violations that I don't even need to "look up."
Oh, and no I haven't gotten a construction permit yet. Someone told me I didn't need one since I was not affecting anything load bearing. (should I have ignored my friend :shock: )
Yes, you should have ignored your friend. That's B.S. Please, stop what you are doing and carefully review these two threads...
    Permits, Codes, Licenses -- and WHY YOU SHOULD CARE! HGTV has done the right thing!
Not to discourage you, but it took me about eight months of planning and research before I finally got my permit. Others have done it in less time, of course. You can too. I want to emphasize that my city building department was very helpful to me in my research. I'm sure they'll help you too if you ask them. If you have time, you can look at my monster thread to see the planning obstacles I faced and how I ultimately designed around them. It wasn't easy, but it was worth it. I'm now well on my way with construction. In fact, I passed my second inspection last week (or, truth be told, two halves of one inspection). I'm the first to admit that I've done things without permits in the past. That was then, and this is now. Today, knowing what I know, I would (and will) get a permit every time. Listen to that voice inside of you, Kelly. :) I think that deep down I'm telling you what you already know. --Keith :mrgreen:
