I've probally been browsing now for 4 straight evenings to the same thing, from the ground up construction. Most of the constructions I see happening here happen to be in allready build places. And if there is a topic about a new studio that's being build, then most of the time it works on wood construction, which is not really used a lot here in Holland, except for some garden sheds. With that in mind, it's really difficult searching for the "perfect" recipe for the wall constructions if you start from the ground up. I've read this from Rod Gervais on PowerStation wall construction, so I took that into option 1 & 3:
Power Station was constructed using an exterior wall consisting of 12" masonry block - sand filled (where ever structural grout and rebar were not required) - roughly 75% sand fill with 25% grout fill. Inside was isolated wood frame with multiple layers of gypsum.... you cannot hear anything outside of this building......... we did not use Green Glue as it had not been invented yet....
For now I'm leaning towards option 1, I only just want to be 100% sure before I start building! Then after this I need to get the ceiling design going, which will probally be a concrete ceiling... IMPORTANT NOTES: - This will be for a recording room for full bands from Gospel to Metal - There will also be a isolated slab - Neighbours on the left side: 5 meters - Neighbours on the right side: 2 meters

Like i mentioned before, I haven't finished the concrete roof yet. So I cannot commend on the isolation. But, if the construction can carry it, it won't hurt your isolation either. I did not finish 'cause I need a lot of help; carrying buckets full of concrete up the stairs. If you on the other hand build the inside 'bunker' first, you could use a truck with a concrete pump: fluitje van een cent (whistle of a penny... :idea: ) If I were to do it again, I would not rest the beams in the walls. I've encounter problems with the wood beams expanding (a little) causing some cracks in the concrete walls. I would make a (preferably) steel contruction that does not touch the walls. I would cut the beams, so that they fall in between the walls, leaving a small gap (maybe 1 cm on each side) to give the beams room to work.
