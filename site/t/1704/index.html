Dirk has a good layout. I personally would make the bigger room the drum room, have a smaller room for the rest of the guys to stand in (2 guitarists and a bass player can all fit into the space taken up by their drums if their amps are in an iso booth), and since the drums are the most affected by room sound, you want to give them the best sounding room. DirkB28 May 2004#6
Yep. I'm a drummer and do a lot of drumrecording. I won't call myself an expert, but people tend to like my results... I lust for the moment that I finish building my dreamstudio (exactly as the above picture) and can start to use some serious room mics on the drums! I don't care too much for Iso rooms. One or two smaller amp closets is cool though to be able to track drums in the main room together with bass and guitar. As a general guideline, I would say, make the control room as large as possible (within reason) and have one bigger tracking room. I see a lot of people here trying to fit a lot of smaller rooms into their space which I don't quite understand... I love the feel of a spacious control room and of a recording room where you can set up the drums, leave some stuff lying around and still be able to move without kicking mc stand. Furthermore, the larger spaces are easier to get sounding right. Good luck, Dirk

Having a bigger space and making it more dead is not that difficult. Some movable gobo's (rockwool in a frame on wheels) may come in handy anyway to adapt the acoustics for the purpose. You could even put the drums in a corner with some gobos around it to have a dry sound and record the opposite site of the room and use this as your reverb :wink: . Greetings, Dirk
