gullfo27 February 2011#35
so what are you willing to do here? (meaning your space) do you want a nice sounding bedroom, or do you want a proper mix room? what are your isolation requirements? i mix new age chimes at 65db isn't going to be a problem, versus i mix deth metal at 110db next to the local infirmary. if you want a proper mix room - lose the bedroom, take down the fan, seal off the window and doors, and do a lot of broadband treatment. for starters - please draw out a floor plan of your room - accurate to 1/4" (5mm) showing all the features - doors, windows, pipes, radiators, electrical outlets, closets, any odd protrusions. include details on the construction - floor, walls, ceiling. how thick are the windows? have you done any isolation testing? flanking testing? tapping with metal and rubber hammers will help determine how much flanking you have between your room and whatever is next door, above or below. use a loud PA or studio monitor to do some sweeps and bass/drum material and a sound level meter or your laptop with appropriate software to measure your isolation. how much noise do you have coming in?
xSpace27 February 2011#36
Young Shoota wrote:
what should i do about the windows?
Windows and doors are always causes for leaks and reduction in your isolation and need a different approach, materials and some attention to detail to achieve a proper seal. Gaskets and solid core doors with properly insulated door jambs and window plugs with gaskets or hard built to the structure with mass equal to or greater than the existing wall assembly. But be forwarned that installing additional framing in this room that is most likely an existing framed wall can defeat your intent. Your goal is to be on the ground level of whatever structure you are in so to have access to an earth damped concrete slab that can be of service....rather than a wooden elevated assembly that brings much trouble with it.
