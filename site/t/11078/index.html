azul3311 August 2008#1
Is a vocal booth for VOICE-OVERS (VO) build on the same acoustic treatment parameters as any other booth for musical instruments? For this discussion lets just call it Voice-Over Booth, instead of Vocal Booth Rephrasing the question: Should ALL the inside walls and ceiling of a Voice-Over Booth be covered by acoustic absorbers or only part of them? Going a step further: How to find out the right quantity and quality of absorber for this purpose? (Rather than just going by trial and error, as I have been adviced) I need advice on this matter (as probably many other VOs). Any advice based on professional experience will be quite welcome, and I would be able to pass this info to other VOs, in their forums and blogs. Reasons for the question: 1. A Voice-Over actor/talent uses most of the time a normal speaking volume voice and this produces different sound characteristics and challenges than musical instruments, even different to vocals (in volume at least) 2. To have or not to have a dead sound booth: Some acoustic designers argue that a vocal booth should have some sound color from the room itself; therefore they prefer not to use acoustic absorbers in all surfaces. Other designers prefer just the opposite covering all walls and floor. They argue that having this neutral or dead sound is better for later EQ and compress adjustments. I have seen this in some pro studios (where I do Voice-Over and where I feel like talking in outer space). I am building a vocal booth for only Voice-Over projects and I couldn't find any info on this matter. (I guess this is due to the fact that question & answers in many forums and also articles are only related to recording musical instruments). Vocal Booth size (Inside dimensions) and materials: Wall 1 - 3.41' ; opposite wall - 2.36' wall 3 - 5.54' opposite wall - 5.34' Height front wall 6.88' ; height back wall 7.70' Walls and ceiling: Gypsum >> mineral wool>> Gypsum. Floor: Floor cement (from the room)>> Gypsum >> wood (MDF 18mm) Walls will not be parallel (to avoid sound problems in a small place like this one) Acoustic material: • 3 Foam Polyurethane panels (1000 x 10000 x 35 mm) • 2 Mineral wool panels (1600 x 400 x 50 mm) • 2 Mineral wool panels (1000 x 600 x 50 mm) Questions: How to proceed on the acoustic treatment issue for this specific vocal booth? 1. Should I cover all walls and ceiling with absorber material (foam or mineral wool)? 2. The above acoustic panels would be enough for the booth size? 3. Any other way to find out the right quantity and type of absorber panels? (Rather than just doing by trial and error) If this question has been worked out in some other part of the forum or even in other article please let me know. Best regards, JC.Aramayo

Congratulations on the books. You are now asking questions that require books just to answer them, The short answer is in that small rooms, which is what a booth is, have such strong acoustics that anything recorded in them will sound like it was recorded in a small room, unless the room is dead. The sound of a small room is terrible. I recommended 100 mm thick, not 10 mm. Andre
