gullfo7 January 2011#2
have you made any acoustic measurements? have you any specific things that are troubling? in general work on the first reflection points and the front corners and put a cloud overhead not just ceiling absorption. this means something over some of those windows on the side and the wall opposite. get the front corner stacked floor to ceiling with the bass traps. a 4'x6' cloud hung down about 12-20" on an angle low in the front near the ceiling in the back. then work on taming the sounds from the back of the room. you might be served by building some free standing absorbers and sort of wall off behind your seating position.

if you are a starter engineer and maybe have not trained your ears, what are some ways to tell if your room is too dead?
gullfo22 February 2011#7
if the room sounds like you have pillows duct taped around your head, it is too dead. in general you want the room to be balanced all around so you can easily hear HF and its not just coming from the front speaker. a dead room has kind of a creepy sensation to it because your ears feel like there is completely empty space around you.
