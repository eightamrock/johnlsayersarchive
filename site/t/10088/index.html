gullfo26 January 2008#3
i'd just frame in the openings you don't need - to seal the rooms - then add a layer of drywall all around to increase the mass of the walls. ensure insulation is in all the walls (if possible), and build some porous absorbers to break up the wall surfaces in the live room, and treat first reflection points in the control room. depending on your needs, the small section above the bigger room could become an air lock/iso booth.

Ro28 January 2008#6
rowdy finn wrote:
i am also curious about using plexiglass as opposed to real glass, and how thick it should be. any opinions/suggestions.
YEah, forget plexi or any other plastics.. Glass is much heavier ('bout 3 time the weight of GB) and will do much better concerning sound isolation.
i was also thinking about using a staggerd stud approach for the reframing. i am actually doing a room in a room design, so the dimensions you see are inside the extra wall. any suggestions on what type of insulation to use with in the staggerd stud wall. thanks again.
If it's a single wall (one frame, one side faced with GB) then staggering studs is not even possible ;) Building a double faced wall (for separating a room)on one frame then you might stagger the studs. But better results will be gained with double framing (no staggering there) I'd advise to take some extra time of and read all the goods on this forum, there's much to be learned.
