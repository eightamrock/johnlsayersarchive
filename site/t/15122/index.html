jhbrandt13 November 2010#4
An air gap won't hurt it... We usually place the batts between the joists so they are held in place with string until the fabric is installed. I usually do not use the frk type for stuffing floor joists from a basement that is otherwise occupied/heated, etc. Using the frk may make installation easier by stapling it to the joists to hold it in place until the fabric is installed. Whatever is easier - it's not rocket science. ;) As for alternatives to O/C 703, -- the specs are not the same, Bob Golds Absorption coefficients page has a listing. http://www.bobgolds.com/AbsorptionCoefficients.htm Cheers, John

gullfo18 November 2010#9
you can use anchors (like the floor) to attach inner wall top plate to the joists or resilient braces like WIC or the Kinetics equiv. for the ceiling, you can use hat channel on resilient clips (Kinetics, RISC etc) and attach the inner ceiling drywall there. fill that space with pink insulation. you can then use the ceiling tile tracks to hide any ducting and wiring and flush mount lighting etc. plus some absorption from the tiles.

The secret is that the "open" ceilings may be inside-out ceilings or there's another isolation layer. So, you could try to beef up the ceiling with multiple layers of drywall to increase the mass but understand that the impact noise etc will likely not be resolved. But you would still have the option to add another ceiling and then achieve a pretty high level of isolation.
