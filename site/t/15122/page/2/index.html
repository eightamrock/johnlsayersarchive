gullfo4 December 2010#31
i would beef it up with layers of 5/8". Rod has a detailed explanation in the book but basically you're going to add 3x layers of 5/8" drywall and support it with cleats and seal. use some blue style foam insulation if you have nails coming through then add the drywall. caulk is your friend. then using the RC-2, add at least 2x 5/8" drywall off the joists. add any necessary framing around the beam. not as good as either suspended or fully decoupled but will be better than not using it.

xSpace4 January 2011#49
In reference to using the thermal sheathing panels up against the upper floor, if there is even the slightest protrusion of nails into the, or rather, that have penetrated through the upper floor sheathing, you should use the thermal sheathing. If you take and jamb sheetrock against the nails, you run the risk of pushing the nail back up into the above flooring. That can produce holes in vinyl, bumps in vct, cracks in ceramic tile and squeaks in wood flooring. It is one of those areas that you do not want to start tightening the money purse since there are so many other areas of little to no consequence further down the road which would be more suited to the restriction.
