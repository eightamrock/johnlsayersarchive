Soundman202028 December 2008#8
My one question regarding bass traps: Are they as effective at side and ceiling corners as floor corners? I would hate to lose floor space at the edges of the room.
Yup. Bass fills the room, and the nodes all meet in the room corners. It doesn't matter too much which corner. Any corner works. Theoretically, you could put bass trapping anywhere, even hanging out in the middle of the room, but the reason why you want to concentrate it against walls and in corners is because you get much more "bang for your buck" so to speak in the corners, because all the nodes meet there. If I recall the numbers correctly: Trapping up close to a wall will gain you 6 dB of absorption. Trapping in the corner between two walls gains you 12 dB, and trapping in the "tri-corner" where three walls meet gains you 18 dB. So obviously those "tri-corners" are an excellent place to trap. So you do want to use your corners as much as possible for trapping. The obvious candidates are the wall-wall corners at the front and rear of the room, and those usually get treated first simply because that's what you think of first one someone says "put it in the corner", but there are plenty of other corners. You might want to avoid treating the floor-wall corners, simply because someone might step on your trap! But if you can keep people away from it, then it's as good a corner as any other.

Perhaps heavy drapes during tracking, but can be pulled back for viewing?
-What is general strategy to add absorbtion/diffusion for this room?
The "general" strategy would be to add bass traps to corners of room (if more trapping is needed, wall/ceiling intersections also count as "corners"), eliminate parallel surfaces with absorption/slot resonators/clouds. How much absorption will depend on how "live" you prefer your recordings to sound, in addition to treating any abnormal amounts of low/low-mid freq buildup you may have.
