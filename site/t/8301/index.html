This is my first post so hello everyone. As with many, I've been lurking for a while now and have been checking out relevant posts and searching for answers to my particular issues. I've learned a lot, but have a few more questions and hopefully some people might be able to assist me with them. The project in question is a commercial space for band rehearsal rooms. I'm going to call it Scientific Laboratories, or Scilabs. An odd enough name, but I like it. I am about to sign a lease on a building and hope to quickly proceed with construction. It will be located in Brooklyn. Rent in the particular area is quite high, as it is in most of NY relative most everywhere else. The high rent means that maximizing the space in terms of number of rooms is a must. That's the New York was as far as affordable rehearsal rooms go. Fortunately, my prospective clients aren’t expecting and don’t want to pay for really high end soundproofing and acoustics, which is good, because the situation can’t provide it. The rooms are intended for standard guitar/bass/drum setups of varying loudness. Just so everyone has an idea of the overall space and the packing in of rooms, there’s a google sketchup mockup of a possible room configuration below. The space is almost 6,000 square feet and 50% bigger than my desired size. But availability of suitable spaces in this area is very low at the moment so I had to either go for it or possibly wait another 6 months to find a more suitable property. This one was too good to pass up in other respects though. It’s a single story building on grade with sufficient exits to comply with code. My neighbors are primarily heavy manufacturing companies, so keeping the sound in isn’t much of a priority, although I’d rather not deafen passers by and this will be 24 hour access, so I’d rather not attract attention at night. But the external walls are very thick, so I don’t see significant problems. I’ve attached a couple of pictures of the building itself. I realize, however, the sheer amount of rehearsal rooms mean that a good quality construction will be needed, even if the bands I hope to attract aren’t as discerning as some. And I need to be careful and efficient about the techniques I use. So with that in mind, here are my current construction specifications for the rooms. Rooms: My current plan calls for three different size of rooms, all being built with independent frames inside the existing structure. Here are the approximate sizes. 5 rooms: 10’ x 10’ 9 rooms: 10 x 15’ 8 rooms: 10 x 20’ The building has high ceilings, so I was planning on going for either 10’ or 12’ height in all rooms. Adjacent room studs are to have a 4” air gap, giving a total of 12” between rooms. Floors: My current floor appears to be very thick concrete and at grade. After reading the floating floor sticky, the various links to other threads and Rod Gervais’ writings on the topic, I decided to leave it be. Doors: My original intention was to use a single solid core door. After reading the Installing a door’ thread in the reference section, I figured I’d be better off with a hollow core door with 5/8” MDF on each side with veneer plywood. Probably doesn’t work out too much different in price either, hopefully. I’ve also changed my mind about the lock I was planning on using and have an idea for a surface mounted one. HVAC: This is the area I’m still most clueless in, especially considering the amount of rooms I’m dealing with. It was suggested to me that I use a flexible duct system to go from central insulated steel ducts. The flexible duct would have the appropriate angles So those are the basics of what I plan to do. In terms of budget, suffice it to say that I have costed the job and it is affordable to me. But I still have unresolved questions: 1. Is there any additional benefit to using different thickness drywall or is it minimal compared to the extra mass? If the latter, I guess I should move up to 5/8” drywall for inner and outer layers, although I have to cost this alteration. 2. Although the consensus seems to be that floating a floor when you’re on thick concrete at grade is unnecessary or even unhelpful in certain applications, most of the discussions seem to center on whether noise will reach upper levels of a building from a foundation through the walls or adjoining buildings through the shared concrete. Might this be a good option? Unfortunately I can’t find that thread anymore so I may have misremembered or not taken something into account. 5. Can anyone elaborate on the HVAC requirements? I’ve read a bunch of threads here, but most relate to smaller builds and much smaller units. I’m wondering if the sheer amount of rooms I’m linking is going to cause real issues. I wonder if there is any ducting configuration that might be more efficient for my purposes, sound-wise? 6. rod gervais29 March 2007#15
greenlander wrote:
5 rooms: 10’ x 10’ 9 rooms: 10 x 15’ 8 rooms: 10 x 20’ The building has high ceilings, so I was planning on going for either 10’ or 12’ height in all rooms. Adjacent room studs are to have a 4” air gap, giving a total of 12” between rooms.
square rooms - or rooms with dimensions that work on factors of 2 - are acoustic nightmares............. add a 10' ceiling to that 10x10 room and it's even worse. so your 10 x 10 and 10 x20 rooms should be redesigned......... By the way - a 4" air gap with 2x4 walls would leave an 11" air space between rooms - not 12".
Walls as per standard double stud construction, pic attached: 1/2” Sheetrock 5/8” Sheetrock 2 x 4 steel stud R13 Insulation 4” Air Gap R13 Insulation 2 X 4 Steel Stud 5/8” Sheetrock 1/2” Sheetrock
forget the 1/2" drywall - although you can get a little bit of bang using different sheets on differing sides - the gain is so small that it isn't worth the cost of the added labor you'll pay your contractor for mixing and matching.
Ceilings: My original intention was to replicate the walls in terms of the amount of sheetrock on either side of the ceiling studs with the appropriate insulation.
By this I assume you mean that you are planning to construct your ceilings bearing on your new walls - and then the same number of sheets of drywall as on the walls......... That being the case - yes that's ok.......
Floors: My current floor appears to be very thick concrete and at grade. After reading the floating floor sticky, the various links to other threads and Rod Gervais’ writings on the topic, I decided to leave it be.
Actually - I recommend isolated slabs at a minimum between adjacent rooms - otherwise the floor will become your weakest link..........
Doors: My original intention was to use a single solid core door. After reading the Installing a door’ thread in the reference section, I figured I’d be better off with a hollow core door with 5/8” MDF on each side with veneer plywood. Probably doesn’t work out too much different in price either, hopefully. I’ve also changed my mind about the lock I was planning on using and have an idea for a surface mounted one.
Sorry - but this would be a big mistake,,,,,,,, you would be much better off with solid core doors and properly gasketed for sound..... in addition - you have these doors dumping into a means of egress corridor - they will probably be required to be rates tested assemblies - and you can't achieve that with custom built doors - they have to come from a certified supplier - and you can't modify them once you have them or you void the certification.
HVAC: This is the area I’m still most clueless in, especially considering the amount of rooms I’m dealing with. It was suggested to me that I use a flexible duct system to go from central insulated steel ducts. The flexible duct would have the appropriate angles
I would suggest that this is an area where you should get a professional engineer involved in your design. In a building this large - you can actually have cooling required on the south side of the building - and heating on the north at certain times of the year (we deal with this all the time in hotels) - and thus you should probably have a minimum of 2 systems if dealing with split systems........ BUT - it might also make sense to have 3 pipe system supply a series of individual fan coil units for each space - that way you would avoid any problems with interconnecting the rooms via ducts - and you could satisfy the fresh air requirements with simple small individual fans to provide fresh air and exhaust - all piped with small 4" round duct. Another advantage to this approach (with these types of rooms) is that you don't have large systems running when rooms are not occupied........ remember - in here - in practice rooms - you don’t have the noise threshold you require in tracking rooms or control rooms - where any additional sound screws up a recording - so a fan running relatively quiet (about what you would hear in a hotel room) wouldn't bother anyone at practice - even though it would if recording.
