Soundman202023 February 2012#9
You say isolation is very important: That implies that you need to build a second "wall" all around that room, about 6 inches away from the existing wall, by building a stud frame and putting at least one sheet of 5/8" drywall on it. (You need to do the same for the ceiling: a large air gap, a frame, then drywall.) So when you do the wall framing, you can make the gap much larger in one direction than in the other, in order to deal with the rather important problem that Brien mentioned: modal behavior. So for example you could make the air gap five inches deep on two opposite walls, and 9 inches deep on the other two opposite walls. That would improve the situation a bit. Of course, it also makes the room much smaller. Then you need to add a your acoustic treatment inside the finished, isolated room, and that takes up a few more inches on each side. To be honest, I very much doubt that you can get a vocal booth in there too, in such a small space: The space you have might be OK for a control room, but I can't see you getting in a booth too. You have to isolate the booth in the same way as the room itself: stud frames with drywall, and you have to treat the booth interior even more than the room interior, plus you need space for the booth door to swing inside the booth, without interfering with the mic or the talent! So the booth would actually be rather larger than you show on your diagram. So if that were my room, I'd concentrate on doing it as just a control room, then record vocals or amps in the room itself. And of course, those HVAC ducts are a huge problem! No matter what you do, you are stuck with those. If you can't move them, then you only have two choices: build the room that much smaller, so they are fully outside it, or soffit them. Once final comment: I don't think your budget is realistic for doing what you say you want to do. - Stuart -
