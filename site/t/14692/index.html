Hey Sono! Looks like folks are busy with other stuff around here lately! Quick answers:
1) Recommended Fiberglass is still 50mm thick 40kg/m2 like in isolation (50kg/m2 for rockwool) ?
Yup. Anything in that ball-park should work. Don't go too dense if you are planning on dealing with lows, and don't go too light-weight if you need to deal with highs. The numbers you mentioned are a good compromise. (There is some in-depth info on that in IR-832, and also IR-693)
2) How do I determine widths and gaps for the slats
For a good, general-purpose broad-band slot wall, use the numbers that John gives on his diagrams and in his recording manual (SAE manual). OR if you have specific problem frequencies, then you can calculate the slot dimensions and cavity depth using the Helmholtz equation (but make sure you use the RIGHT one! There is a WRONG version of that equation floating around on the internet, and even published in some books, so take care that you don't get the wrong one...)
3) Can the slats be made of MDF or plywood instead of timber?
Yup. Anything fairly stiff and dense will do the job.
4) Can I paint/varnish the slats?
Yup. Jut don't put it on too thick, as that could change the characteristics of the slots. A couple of thin coats won't do any harm but if you really layer it up disgustingly thick, that might not be a good idea. - Stuart -

Soundman202024 August 2010#6
Back to Treatment, how about something along these lines?
Basic idea is OK, but it needs some work still. It looks like you scaled the treatment in one direction only, so the speakers and soffits are now distorted, and things are all wonky! The slot walls are also rather deep at the front: I doubt you'd need that much depth.
3) I intend to build a 12º inclination ceiling. From the center of the room at ceiling height the treatment will descend at 12º, and will be around 0.7 metres at 3m length. Is this correct? Does this need to be on both sides of the center of the room or only on the speakers' side?
Just the front- In other words, the ceiling is lowest right at the front wall, above the speakers, then rises to its maximum height about half way back, and stays at that height from there all the way to the rear wall. Do not angle it down again!
2) I have calculated the sweet spot at 38% of the Length of the room (that's roughly 2.16m from the wall). From there I drawed a triangle with a 60º angle at the sweet spot facing the wall. At what distance from the sweet spot should I place the speakers following those 2 lines at 60º?
The speakers and your head should be at the corners of an equilateral triangle. In other words, the distance between the speakers is the same as the distance from each speaker to your head (or rather, to the point just behind your head where the acoustic axes of the speakers intersect). Once again, the 38% position and the equilateral triangle are only GUIDELINES! They are not written in stone, and nobody will arrest you for violating sacred acoustic laws if you have to adjust the geometry slightly to make it fit your room better. :) - Stuart -
