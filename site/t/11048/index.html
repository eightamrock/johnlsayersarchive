delusion31 July 2008#2
Yes, the thicker the better, and laminated glass performs the best. Most often you would build your own frames as you want a two leaf system, but you don't want the frames coming in contact with each other. If you buy a prefab two leaf window, then the 2 pieces of glass will share the same frame causing a direct flanking path. That is a no-no for isolation purposes. Also, you may want to use a different thickness of glass for each of your two leaves. This will help combat the same frequency exciting them both and thus passing through as a result. There is lots of info here on the subject. You need to do a more detailed search.

delusion1 August 2008#5
Ted White wrote:
Don't know if there's some advantage to a pre-framed glass vs. raw that has to be framed and isolated.
There's no real advantage other then being a time saver. Both approaches would be achieving the same thing. You need one framed piece of glass per wall no matter if you go pre-fab or build it yourself. Pre-fab would be an expensive custom job but easier. Building it yourself would save you money big time and you would be able to assure that it is "studio grade" construction. I guess it just depends on the confidence you have in your carpentry skills. With all of the info available here a dedicated person should be able to pull it off.
