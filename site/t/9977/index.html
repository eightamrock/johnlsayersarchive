MY QUERRIES ARE ABOUT “MAM” AND “3 LEAF SYSTEMS”: FIRST: I intend to do my new construction “building shell” to inside space like this: New const. from outside in: 1. stucco 2. 11.25” concrete and rebar filled Styrofoam blocks- (so that’s a sandwich of 1.625” styro-8”concrete-1.625” styro) 3. 4” air space (no connection of wall to independent slabs either) 4. 2 layers 5/8” sheet rock on “inside out” 2”x4” stud wall filled with 703 insul. 5. then variations of acoustic cloth and slat resonators as needed for tracking room+/control room wall surfaces. SECOND: When I encase my new structure around the existing house, again I have 3 leaf questions. Existing house structure from inside out to new construction: 1. 5/8” painted sheetrock 2. plastic vapor barrier 3. 2”x6” stud walls 2’ OC filled with 6” standard fiberglass insulation 4. next is OSB (“wafer board”) sheathing 5. then there is Tyvec house wrap 6. and lastly vinyl siding The abutting/encasing new construction (control room and air lock/stairs) will have the 4” air space between the existing house wall (which will not have styro/concrete wall here, also no connect to new slab) and the new construction. {QUESTION #2: Will I need to remove the existing OSB to once again avoid the potential 3 leaf problem??} (I’m assuming I need to remove the vinyl siding and tyvec) If I do, the complete system structure would be would be as follows: From outside in: 1. 5/8” painted sheetrock 2. plastic vapor barrier 3. 2”x6” stud walls 2’ OC filled with 6” standard fiberglass insulation on it’s own footer with new footer below it supporting (because the new construction goes down to the ledge below for extra room height for studio space). 4. pourous cloth to cover the exposed insulation in entry area 5. 4” air gap with no connection between existing house footer walls and new construction independent slabs 6. 2 layers 5/8” sheetrock (on “inside-out” wall) 7. 2”x4” “insideout” stud walls filled with 703 insul. 8. cloth/slats If I remove the OSB I have to accommodate with “let-in/hurricane bracing” on the abutting house wall for structural integrity. Any thoughts are welcome from anyone!!!!

gullfo12 January 2008#2
if isolation isn't too much of a concern, the 3rd leaf is really only going to reduce isolation, not necessarily introduce unwanted resonances in the room (unless something is vibrating loosely or somehow you hit the right combination of things to cause it. by dampening the air cavities with insulation you'll reduce the chance the cavity resonances have any perceptible impact.

gullfo15 January 2008#4
in the case you describe with inside out walls, you shouldn't have a 3rd leaf problem since the interior wall of the inside out wall is treatment and not really a boundary per se. it would be different if the walls were drywall-frame-drywall-air-styro-cement-styro which is 3 leaf. instead you have treatment-frame-drywall-air-styro-cement-styro so for all intents 2 leafs.
