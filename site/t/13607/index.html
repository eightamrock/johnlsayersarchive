First, please read the stickies, or you won't get much response here. :)
It's a mainly roughcast structure ...
What do you mean by "roughcast"? Can you be more specific?
...so it's currently being bricked up form the inside
So you are building a brick wall just inside whatever is on the facade of the building? If that "roughcast" facade has much mass to it, then you will already have two leaves after you add the brick wall, so adding a third (drywall) leaf inside of that might not be a good idea, especially if low-frequency isolation is important to you! but you didn't say what you are trying to accomplish, nor how much isolation you need, so that's hard to judge...
i'm going to have (after brick and soundboard/insulation
Why use soundboard when drywall is so much cheaper, kilogram for kilogram, and just as effective in stopping sound? Sound waves don't care how much you paid for each kilogram of mass in your wall...
...instead of doing the normal Brick/frame/insulation/drywall, I was thinking of doing it this way - Brick/drywall/frame insulation and then covering the frame and insulation with fabric,
I'm not certain, but it SOUNDS like you are talking about building your inner-leaf inside out. Correct? If so, then yes, that will work very well, and this forum is probably "the home of the inside-out studio" anyway, so you are in the right place! However, you still need to leave an insulation-filled air gap between the brick and the drywall, of at least 4 inches. (Unless, of course, that drywall would have been your third leaf, in which case you don't need it at all, and your room will end up being brick on the inside, which WILL need lots of acoustic treatment).
in theory deadening the room and avoiding having to spend extra ££££ on bass traps etc.
Dead rooms are a bad idea. They sound... well ... errr... "dead"!!! :) So if you want to build inside-out, then use some of John's designs and ideas, such as his slot wall, so that the room is not too dead. You'll probably still need bass traps anyway, since that is a small room.
Also, does anyone know where in the UK I could locate a suitable fabric?
Pretty much any fabric would probably work for that purpose, as long as it allows free passage of air. If you can blow through it easily without feeling any resistance at all, then it should work. The acoustic properties of the fabric probably aren't too critical in that specific application.

I've been doing that for a year now, and I'm still going... But read the stickies first. - Stuart -
