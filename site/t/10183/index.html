AVare14 February 2008#3
The RFZ is created by using ray tracing. The design goal is no reflections for the first 15, or 20 (the latter is better) milliseconds. The ITU spec writes that reflections within that time period be at least 10 dB lower than the initial sound. The idea of nearfields is that they are so close to the listener(s) that room treatment, or lack of, does not affect the initial sound from the speakers. So no, you do not have to make the front wall absorptive for the nerafield monitors. Keep the questions coming! The more you know, the better your space will be. Andre

gullfo16 February 2008#6
in many cases, you'll see the walls use slats to provide absorption, diffusion, and the angles walls, as well as a face to the area which becomes the bass trapping. this way you're not only creating the RFZ but also maximizing the usages of space. in the case of 6°, its generally considered the bare minimum for elimination of flutter echoes. for really effective RFZ you need wider angles on the walls and ceiling.
