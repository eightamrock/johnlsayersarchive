gullfo22 April 2011#2
welcome! you should first and foremost do some level measurements to see where you are leaking the most sound. put some 3/4" plywood over the windows after the first test run to see if that make a difference. run sine sweeps and bass-drum music at 110db inside and use a sound level meter outside. it's very likely your roof is the main culprit. in which case, consider pulling down the drywall and adding isolation clips and hat channel with 2x (or 3x if your structure can support it - get a structural engineer on-site) 5/8" drywall. next - the floor you didn't document what's under it but that will also be a big emitter and sound leaking down the stairs and through the floor will be a problem. a big one. if you've read some of the other threads here on 2nd floor studios in wood structures, you will find it is not trivial to get very good isolation. beefing up the floor will also require your structural engineer to assess there as well.

gullfo23 April 2011#4
i would put the desk by the window and the practice area by the stairs. this way you can control the symmetry on that end better and if its a bit constrained it doesn't interfere with the band moving in and out etc. you will need the usual treatments on your first reflection points - side, ceiling and on the angled ceiling parts. behind you, think gobos to block off some portion of the room during mix down. if you had the option to turn it all sideways and perhaps make a wall then you may end up with the isolation for the control room and better visibility but at the cost of less open space.
