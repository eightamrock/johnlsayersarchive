ALl of this sounds pretty much by the book- BTW - you have to be carefull when dealing with neighboring party walls - If the wall is shared it is a party wall - and party walls have requirements to have and maintain particular fire ratings........ IN the states there are a couple of testing labs that certify these walls - one is UL (Underwriters LAb) and another is FM (Factory Mutual) - neither of those include any of the reversed walls that John uses in his designs - and to the best of my knowledge they do not esist in any test data anywhere. They are designed as acoustic assemblies to be used within a studio environment - but not as a part of a rated fire wall assembly. (John - please correct me if I am wrong on these being tested - perhaps in your country they have been). Anyway - the point I am trying to make is this - the removal of a fire rated assembly - replacing it with a non rated assembly - is a criminal offence in most states - and (if ever there was a fire) could be cause for criminal arrest including homicide charges if ever there was a death related to the failure of the wall to meet required ratings. In addition - the movement of the wall face from it's original location to another location inboard of it's original face (even if the wall design itself is able to be rated) has most probably compromised the rating at the wall/ceiling assembly - by creating a direct path into the wall cavity through the floor decking (which isn't rated) I would have to see some drawings on exactly what you did and what existed - and how the 2 dovetailed together.
Am I expecting too much by wanting no sound transfer at all?
Absolute - you are way beyond realistic......... I have measured just drums in a band room at 110 dB - it could easily be 120 - 125 for a full band - to expect perfect isolation is nowhere near realistic. Galaxy - the quietest srudio in the world doesn't meet the standard you are asking about - and that's when building it from sxcratch - not trying to set it inside of someone's house with a neighbor on an attached wall..........
How at this point should I go about reducing the transfer even more?
It all depends on what is the weak link - if it's flanking - then you can just throw money at it forever and never make it any better...........
Will adding another layer of sheet rock to the wall closest to our neighbor help any? How do I test to see if it is a possible short curcuit? Will adding a few more bass traps help? Auralux? I'm desperate...LOL. Any suggestions would be much appreciated.
At this point you don't test - because you don't have either the experience nor the equipment to pull of the testing. The way for you to handle it is to hire a pro to do some testing for you - and let them give you the best advice for the situation - anyone here would be making a blind guess - which may or may not be any better than yours......... and this will only work (the testing) if your neighbor is willing to give them access to their apartment as a part of the process - they can't do it from just one side....... BTW - just for future reference: BASS TRAPS DO NOT CREATE ISOLATION BETWEEN ROOMS.... get a good strong picture of that in your minds eyes - they only help to attenuate that which exists in the room due to the level of isolation you have.
