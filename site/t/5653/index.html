Considering the follow info, you may have to reconsider using them in that way, especially when you consider the acoustic treatments that should be up there.
Is the roof shape a benefit or a problem?
Here’s a post and excerpt that discusses a situation that’s similar in shape as yours: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4809
(a) Quonset huts are nearly parabolic, which makes them hard to soundproof very easily and (b) if you don't care about that, they also tend to FOCUS sound to one point in the room (or one line through the room) neither of which makes things easy for getting balanced recordings. If you don't care much about the soundproofing part, you might be able to build a different-shaped inner shell that gets you away from the focusing effect... Steve
Is there a better cheap starter solution?
Other than building a decent sized control room, you could probably get a lot of mileage out of some strategically placed bass traps, absorbers, and gobos. Perhaps even a drum riser if/when that happens. Hope this helps. Aloha 8)

kendale22 February 2006#7
Aloha Alistair,
I had originally thought that I could have a large main studio room... but am I thinking about this the wrong way? Should I be going for a larger control room and several smaller isolation rooms.
Depends on how you like to work and the kind of work you do. My space is about 20' long x 12' wide x 8'-10' high, before acoustic treatments. After which I am finding that it's comfortable enough for one person, cozy for two, and definitely needs a lounge/hangout area for more than three. Also, if you are planning to track 4 or 5 players at once, having a decent sized live room helps. It can also double as your iso/vocal booth as well. Ideally, in addition to a decent sized live room, having at least one iso booth would be nice, but not always possible for some of us with limited space.
If I'm not going to get a large studio room then what about going across the room rather than longways?
One point to remember is the cross section of your room. By orienting your room sideways (or longways as you call it) would give you a room with a 6' front and rear wall starting point according to your diagram. By positioning your room lengthwise and centering it as much as possible, you would be able to gain quite a bit for your ceiling height. Also, if you were to remove the curved portions of the existing structure as you mentioned, you would have close to a 14' deep x 20' wide x 10'-12' live room, which is large enough for a 5 piece band, and a 19' deep x 16' wide x 10'-12' high control room. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
