> b) The doors are placed asymmetrically as you can see on the picture. < That's fine too, but the panel placements should be symmetrical left and right if possible.
This may not always be the case. Sometimes, slight staggering can reduce flutter. If you notice an annoying "boing" between parallel areas of flat wall, you might wish to stagger a little bit. Shifting panels on one side forward slightly and on the other side backward slightly - "slightly" being less than a foot, typically - it should be enough to reduce flutter without sacrificing imaging.
Also, your mix position is not well placed. It looks like it's too close to the back of the room.
I would concur. But I see the doors are probably going to prevent you from moving forward in the room. Any chance you can consider rotating 180°?

Ethan Winer20 May 2007#6
z99 wrote:
the only other worthwhile choice would be placing it 38% from the "rear" wall
The most important point is to not be near the center of the room front to back.
should i cover most of the rear wall with absorbers?
Yes, but a room this size needs that anyway no matter where you sit. If the wall behind you is less than 10 feet away, those reflections are considered "early." Even when the wall is farther, bass reflections are a problem. See this short article and calculator program: http://www.realtraps.com/sbirlbir.htm
will there be more reflections from the ceiling and floor as well?
Yes.
is cloud above mixing position a must?
The ceiling reflections points are halfway between you and the speakers, not directly overhead, as explained in the Room Setup article I linked above. This is not to say that absorption over your head won't help in other ways. For example, if the cloud is four inches thick (or more), it can add useful bass trapping. --Ethan
