kendale19 April 2006#12
Aloha Kelly, Here's few bits of random info I've collected on the topic of vocal booths. Perhaps some of it may be helpful to you.
My understanding of small vocal booth/voice-over treatments tend to lean toward the "dead" side. During the insulating phase of my front soffits, in which I lined the cavity with 3" 2.5 pcf rockwool slabs, I noticed how nice and quiet it got with each 4' x 2' x 3" section of rockwool that was installed. Bryan Giles cautioned however, against building a square booth saying; "Be careful, a dead square booth will have low end issues (you'll hear the issues on your mic.) Steve (Knightfly) mentioned that "4' x 5' x 7' inside dim's is about the best modal compromise for a small booth." Mode-wise, a 5 x 6 x 7 (inside) booth looks very good. It will also fit into an 8 foot ceiling area if necessary, and is big enough for 3 (very) friendly vocalists or 1-2 with a guitar. Just do NOT make any two dimensions the same, or more than one dimension in even # of feet.
I used Bob Gold’s Room Mode Calculator to find some ideal dimensions for a Room within a Room construction I am designing for a vocal booth... or so i thought... The maximum internal dimensions I can achieve with my limited space are: 8'L x 5'W x 7'H. Remember this is a vocal room and will mostly be used just for vocals (so I figure relevant frequencies will maybe go down as low as 85hz to capture the harmonics of a very bassy voice). The "Ideal" dimensions I came up with are: 7'3"L x 4'7"W x 6'9"H I have 3 questions regarding the results of these dimensions when entered into this room mode calculator. 1. The resulting room ratio is 1 : 1.47 : 1.58, these numbers fall close but not exactly within the "Bolt Area" graph that i see in my Master Acoustics Handbook, I don’t think its that important but should i be concerned about this? 2. There is a dip around 100 Hz in the Bonello graph, I understand this is not desirable, but is it less desirable than using these dimensions: 7'3"L x 5'W x 6'7"H whereby the Bonello curve looks slightly more flat BUT there is a problem with the modes in the 113hz-116hz frequency group (as indicated by the Yellow warning color). These are Axial and Tangential mode problems so i figure they're treatable since I plan to treat the walls with wideband absorption anyhow. 3. EXTRA CREDIT Question: The test "Fails" the first formula under the Computed Information > R. Walker BBC 1996 heading but the corresponding linked document states that this formula is for a classical music "listening room" is this a problem for my vocal booth? Should I ignore this line altogether? I would guess the first dimensions I indicated (7'3"L x 4'7"W x 6'9"H) might be the best for my situation acoustically, but in terms of width I would also be loosing 5 inches of already cramped space. And if the problems caused by the second set of dimensions (7'3"L x 5'W x 6'7"H) are easy to correct, I'd rather have the extra width. Does it even matter that much? I'm driving myself nuts here trying to wrap my mind around this design.

knightfly25 April 2006#18
If you angle walls you can get close to what the modes will be by using the average distance between them; the good news is, the modes will be less pronounced due to the non-parallel walls. The bad news is that it's not practical to try and calculate, much easier to just measure with a program like ETF/R&D, etc. If you decide on using slat resonators, do NOT use the calculator at the SAE site; it has the wrong formula, as do 99% of the calculators on the web. Instead, use the one in the "stickies" in the Acoustics forum... Steve
