knightfly28 October 2006#3
That shouldn't hurt anything - I'd follow John's advice here, with maybe one addition - since your 703 is thicker AND heavier than what most have used for hangers, if you're gluing these to backer board I'd ALSO wrap wire around the entire hanger after the 703 is glued on, and twist the wire snug enough to bite into the insulation about 10-15% or so - the insulation will damp the wire so no ringing will happen, and the wire will help the glue do its job of keeping the insulation where you put it. A possible further benefit might be that since this will "convolute" the insulation surface somewhat, you might get a tiny improvement in overall absorption due to a more "tortuous" sound path within the ceiling trap - but that last part is probably just me "over-thinking". I agree 110% with John's statement about full absorb in your ceiling - that's essentially a parabolic reflector, and without absorption it will focus everything right back at your head... Steve

knightfly2 November 2006#5
With a variable dimensioned space like that, just make all the hangers as deep as space allows - width (side to side) should be at least half a meter, wider is fine too. Having a variety of depths/widths/weights will help to smooth out the absorption of the trap area so it's a good thing to do... Steve
