kendale19 February 2007#7
Aloha,
Some has been stuffed in from the top by the previous people who built the studio before. So let's go with the answer of NO to your question because what insulation that might be there is probagby not in correctly. Does it matter anyway?
Yes. Part of the function of properly installed insulation is to dampen the drywall from resonating. With no insulation, the drywall on both sides of that cavity will resonate at some freq. (usually a low one - say like traffic rumble) which will add low freq noise to your room & ultimately if your mics are picking them up, your recordings. If you are able to obtain permission to do so, (and if that currently exposed framed wall is not a load bearing wall!) you could carefully: - remove the exposed framing (to be reused) - remove one side of the drywall - caulk all the interior frame/ remaining office-side drywall seams - properly install the insulation (be sure to use some sort of support to hold insulation to studs/drywall - nylon strapping works well) - frame a new wall 2"- 4" apart from the now insulated office wall - insulate new wall using above mentioned insulation support - re-install control room side drywall with 2-3 layers, being sure to stagger & caulk the seams (very important here) - add 2-3 layers of drywall on the office side, staggering and caulking the seams. You would now have a properly installed 2 leaf wall system that should give you a STC of 63 or better. This may seem like a lot of work and expense, and it is. However, can you afford the time and expense further down the road to redo the construction, especially when you're already this close to being there at this point? Or are you willing to accept the noise as is with your current construction? Time, talent & resources being what they are, often determine our choices. Choose wisely, my friend. :wink: It's important to remember that this wall is just one part of a system. Your floors, ceilings and other walls/doors/windows all contribute to the whole. Any compromise in any part of the system will result in a compromise of "less than what could have been." Here's a diagram to give you an idea of what I'm trying to describe above. Hope this hleps, Aloha 8)

kendale21 February 2007#12
Aloha,
May I ask why?
There is a recent thread (try a search) covering some of the reasons why you want to build your exterior walls straight and then angle your acoustic treatments to break up the parallel surfaces. To my mind, the key words here are exterior walls, acoustic treatments, and rented office/business space. I too, have seen several of John's designs as well as professional purpose-built studios that incorporate angled walls in the structure, but these are/were primarily owner/purpose built facilities as opposed to rented office space. You may also want to consider it a courtesy to the property owner and/or any future tenants. Perhaps you might consider asking the owner if its okay with him for you to build permanently angled walls in his building. If its okay with him, and after reviewing the above mentioned post, and find that it is still a viable option and helps in achieving the isolation you need, then by all means. Again, this is just my point of view considering the circumstances, and you will have to ultimately make your own decisions based on your discernment and preferences, as well as what and how much you are allowed to do.
