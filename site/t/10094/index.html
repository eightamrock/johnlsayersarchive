rod gervais4 February 2008#5
Here is a couple of different ways of going about it. No matter what the situation is - just think about the goal....... you do not want the free passage of fire and hot gasses to pass from the wall asemblies up into the ceiling above - or from room to room. In the process you do not want to couple seperated structural assemblies together to create flanking issues. In addition - running vertically up the wall - you want a fire block in wall junctions - corners - and in distances as proscribed by your local building codes. The attached give you a number of simple details for firestopping at top of walls - and plan views for locations for vertical fireblocks.......... Rod
