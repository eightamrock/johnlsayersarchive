roman2 July 2006#1
Hello everybody :-) Goal: I would like to build a (relatively) sound-proof cabin (room-in-room construction) in order to be able to play drums without disturbing my neighbours too much. I would also like to use this room for drum recording. Situation/Location: The cabin will be situated in the basement of our house (no, it's not mine -- I just live in it! ) where the walls are fortunately very massive and dense due to the fact that the house is constructed for very low energy consumption (in German it's called a "Passivhaus"). Nevertheless my neighbour is living just above in the first floor, so noise exposure is a matter. The basement floor consists of painted concrete. After reading and researching a lot about acoustic treatment and studio design I've decided to make my plans public in the hope that anybody would commentate about it. Construction Details: Length: 3,00 m (9,8 ft) Width: 2,50 m (8,2 ft) Height: 2,20m (7,2 ft) I chose this measures for having evenly spread room modes. The outside of the frames will be one ply (maybe two, if it's not enough) of gypsum board (1/2"). Inside, two walls are plywood, and the other two walls are slot resonators used as low mid and bass absorbers. I would like to use Rockwool isolation for the frames and the slot resonaters (3 1/6" or 4") The ceiling will consist of Isover SSP2 (thread about Isover) rigid mineral wool held by a simple wooden slat construction, in order to be as less reflective as possible for the high frequencies and mids. The outer side of the ceiling will also be one ply of gypsum board having a distance of 4" to the Isover SSP2. First, the floor will be the floor of the basement. If I get into trouble with subsonic noise I will build a wooden podest without any contact to the walls of the cabin. Budget: The material should not cost more than 1000$, unless it's not realizable without spending some more money. The relatively low budget is also the reason why I planned to use the cabin walls directly as absorbers. Otherweise I would have aimed to sound-proof the walls first and then build some traps for acoustic treatment inside. I have some questions yet which I hope you can answer: 1. Do you think the slat resonators are enough or shoul I add another bass absorber, maybe in the right angle corner (in opposite to the slat resonators)? 2. Does it make sense to construct the plywood walls as panel absorbers? Should I use either thicker (1") or thinner plywood (1/2")? 3. Is it better to construct the slat resonators as two hermetic boxes (one for each wall) or as one big resonator, where the back of the box consist of the two adjacent walls as you can see on the drawing. 4. What do you think about the overall design? I hope you guys can give me some advice because I have never built something like that before! I'm looking forward to your comments :) Regards Roman

DirkB23 July 2006#5
Non-parallel walls do not remove standing waves, they just bend them a little (for lack of a better description). Flutter echo is removed with non-parallel and since it is mainly a high frequency issue, the slats from the helmholz trap will work fine. However, since there still will be a lot of sound hitting those reflective walls perpendicularly, there is a big chance for comb-filtering. For a pretty small room I would use 4-6" of fiberglass all around the walls and ceiling and perhaps fix some poly's in front of those walls or other type of diffusors if you want more high-end back into the room. Good luck, Dirk
