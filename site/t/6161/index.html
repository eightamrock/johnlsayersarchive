John Sayers5 May 2006#2
well you have to buld two buildings. The outer one should have one skin (i.e. outer layer) The ceiling should ideally span the whole building without the need of support poles, but we can get around them if it's too expensive to do it on one span. All moisture protection for that building is standard building code. you then build a second frame internally also with one skin - that's your two skin assembly. The internal frame should not be connected to the outer frame. Hope this helps cheers john

guitardad725 May 2006#5
Also you should be looking into to your local building codes and get a building permit, which will require you to build the exterior walls a certain way. Commom things are #3 grade 2x4 on exterior walls and load bearing walls 16" on center, PT 2x4 against concrete, you'll have a minimum sheathing depth & type, you be required to provide "X" amount of insulation.... then you'll have to figure out with you code's requirment's and options you have for material, where to place that materail which will best sound isolate. Moisture> Vapor Barrier in the right spot for your climate. You very well may be required to hire an architect. Stamped plans will have all the requirements. Make sure to go through the reference section on this forum...Steve (Kightfly) has posted some real good stuff relevant to exterior walls. Although many users of this forum are very familiar with building and construction, most are adding to an existing structure. I too am putting up new exterior walls and the info I need for that is not really on this forum or in Rod's book... it's in my local building code and other web sites dedicateded more to building construction. In the end I'm using a combination of info for my exterior walls from here and other sources. BTW I would choose solid pour concrete over brick. Good Luck Marc
