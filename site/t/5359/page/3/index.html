knightfly24 January 2006#52
Glass - what about using some of that tinting film you can buy for cars? Just get the darkest you can find, and it's DESIGNED to laminate to the glass. Once that's on, you can insulate using standard fluffy stuff (fill the entire window well with it) and maybe screw some fiberglass strapping to the metal window frame using short sheet metal screws and washers, so the insulation doesn't fall out of the window. This would damp the window pane. Then, if you use 3 layers of 5/8 gypsum with GG on the last one, you should do pretty good (especially if you leave a total air gap of around a foot) Duct enclosing - are you talking about RC as in Resilient Channel, or "clips" as in RSIC clips and hat channel? knightfly24 January 2006#54
Putty packs are high density and will seal cracks, plus they're intumescent (swell up in a fire rather than just going away) - so it's all good. Are you planning on RSIC's for just the ceiling, or the wall also? This will change how you should enclose your ducts. 2x2 framing would be absolute minimum for this, 2x4's on edge is better. spaced about 1/4" away from duct, they would leave enough room to lightly compress 2" 3 PCF insulation, which will damp the ductwork. OUter corners of the duct box - no problem, just stagger joints same as wall/ceiling joints with multiple layers, then use outside corner mold. You may be able to resiliently hang most or all of the box, need to know about your walls in the questions above... Steve

knightfly13 February 2006#71
On your angled walls, will there be a point at the front of the room where the wall is actually further from the concrete wall than your duct? Fastening RSIC's to concrete - per the first drawing (previous page) I mentioned either Ramset or lagging the hat channel to the concrete. Ramset is a trade name for a powder-actuated "nail gun" - it uses .22 or .38 caliber blanks to drive a special nail directly into concrete. This method is mostly for pro's, it's quick but can be dangerous, and can cause spalling (breaking out) of chunks of the concrete in some cases. Lagging is drilling a larger than usual hole into the concrete (a masonry drill is required, and a hammer drill makes it go faster than a regular drill) - you then insert a "shield", which is a 2-piece lead alloy, ribbed tube - you then tighten a lag bolt into this tube (with whatever you want fastened there already having the lag bolt thru a hole in the device you're mounting) - as you tighten the lag bolt (they're tapered) it spreads the two halves of the lag shield, cramming the ribbed parts up against the sides of your hole and making a very strong anchor to the wall. These are the same idea as the little plastic anchors used in drywall to fasten pictures, etc, only much stronger. You would use this to fasten the actual hat channel to the wall, then clip the RSIC's onto that, and the rest is shown pretty clearly in the two drawings. Your ringing floor - this is NOT good. voids can be caused by not compacting the underlying dirt if it's been disturbed during construction, or by water rushing thru under the slab. Short of drilling holes in the floor every couple of feet and injecting new concrete til it squirts back out the hole, I don't know what to suggest. Just injecting foam won't help much, because foam has almost no damping and no mass. NOT fixing this will leave the room subject to ringing, which is ALSO unacceptable... 2x6's for framing - unless needed for ceiling/floor strength, no. For sound control a lighter frame that lets the leaves "bob and weave" just a bit is more effective. This is why 24" centers are better than 16" centers. Extra 2x8's on ceiling - I'm not sure what you're trying for here; you'd need more support EVERYWHERE to add more weight. I'd be more inclined to spend the $$ on Green glue between layers in this case. Still trying to find time for your "picture story" - may have to settle for doing another sketch, but haven't had time even for that :cry: Steve
