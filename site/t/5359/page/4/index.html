guitardad7210 March 2006#100
Tom, There is no way that drainage pipe runs under your house, unless some one really took there time to install it incorrectly. :roll: I’m in the middle of building a drainage system for one of my down spouts and hopefully I’ll finish today… I’ll take pics and if you ask I’ll post them, although this is completely off topic of studio build. Typically houses (at least in Florida & Halifax Canada) are not built with gutters; they are put on by the home owner after the fact. You can not install a rain gutter till the roof is up anyways 8) … Also typically a drain field from the bottom of down spout is not installed by gutter contractor. The black pipe attached to your rain gutter looks like a drainage pipe mostly used in a septic tank system for drainage after the tank. You should be able to see holes in it. If it was installed correctly it should be buried in the ground, surrounded top and bottom by cheap rock and lead away from your house for X amount of feet depending on your soil type. The only problem(s) you should ever have with it is if it gets clogged or over flows. Typically by tree debris such as leaves or pine needles or depending on you roof shingles little pebble like pieces of the shingle. You should check it once a year, maybe install a clean out (place to pull it apart) and check to see if someone put wire mesh of some sort at the top of down spout to stop debris from entering. Another cause of over flow would be if it was not installed correctly with enough rocks to let the thing drain. Only adverse sound effect I could think of is noise when it rains… but rain hitting your window should be louder then water gushing through your drain field that you probably won’t hear through a concrete wall and dirt. How ever so slightly is my down spout drain field related to my studio build… the job was sparked by removing sprinkler manifold from studio wall and a chain reaction has lead to landscape work all over my house. :twisted: And Tom, you should jump up and down about having spending $350 on you electric panel move… my upgrade and move is going to cost me $2,300.
