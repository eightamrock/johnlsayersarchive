Sandersd15 March 2006#110
It is not a good idea to place loads off-center like this illustration depicts. It creates what is called an eccentric load that will try to twist the joist. You should try to find a way to attach to the bottom of the joist so it hangs from the centerline. Also, the web of this type of joist is very thin and therefore doesn't have a lot of holding power for screws or nails.

knightfly16 March 2006#116
Problem with that is that it doubles the spring rate, lessening isolation. If you mount 2x6's as blocks perpendicular to your joists and fasten the CWB's to those, it won't twist the blocks because they're too short to have that much flex; and it won't even TRY to twist your joists. Also, that lets you adjust the height of the lower bracket however far up into the joist cavity you like (provided NONE of the "L" frame or the clips touch your main structure (joists) - That way, your "L" frames can stick up high enough to make the corners between ceiling layers and HVAC box layers easy to alternate, caulk, mud and tape... knightfly16 March 2006#118
Right; and the other answer is - very accurate cutting, and a fair-sized hammer :wink: Seriously, you would mark the exact locations of your 2x6's on the web of each joist (keeping in mind that they will each be different, and should line up with each other but NOT with your outer side wall, because your duct is tapered) Each span between joists may be slightly different, so you'd need to cut each block to size. one way to do this (if you don't have a Stanley folding ruler with the optional sliding bar for inside measurements) is to use two 12" rulers and a small clamp or two - put one end of one ruler up against one surface, and the other end of the other one up against the second surface, clamp the two together, rotate til they're free and use that as your measurement to cut THAT block. Repeat til done. Snap a chalk line (across the bottoms of your joists) from one end of your tapered HVAC box to the other (assuming you're running a straight, angled line and NOT a "stepped" box, that is) and use that line as the center point for each block; then take a framing square, hold the short side parallel to each joist and slide it along the joist til first one chalk mark and then the other (on the neighboring joist) lines up with the square. The halfway point between the two marks on each joist will be where the OUTER edge of your 2x6 block goes for that cavity. Mark a straight vertical line on the web of the joist, put one end of your block on that line, and hold it tight while tapping the other end into place with the hammer. If you mark both sides of each cavity it's easier to line up; otherwise you'll need to check with the square many times in the process. Plan on putting each clip in the center of it's block (halfway between joists, that is) in order to maintain equal centers for finding the frames with your drywall screws. I'm almost outa time for now, you've got the right idea but if necessary I can sketch out my instructions above if they're not clear enough... Steve

knightfly28 March 2006#125
Those clips will work fine, you'll need to drill the concrete for small lag sheilds to fasten them. One every 4 feet is plenty, you're just keeping the wall from trying to fall over between corners, where it'll be supported by the walls that meet it at an angle. Frames of booth - I'm on a really slow connection ATM, so can't browse around quickly - do you have an ACCURATE drawing of how you intend to build this part? If so, please link to it - if not, try to draw EXACTLY how you will place each and every board, what connects to what, etc - from your description, you'll get flanking into your booth. Less so if you use RSIC's on all the wallboard, but that gets expensive and won't cure ALL flanking as well as separate surfaces/frames. Framing screws - normally nails are used - 16d for most framing, 10d for toenailing if the plate is on concrete. The only reason I showed using screws (those were DECK screws, basically just LONG drywall type screws) is because those "L" framing subassemblies are cut thinner and need to be fastened close to the end - so I drilled countersunk pilot holes and used screws and construction adhesive for maximum strength. I know those will work, but I'd not show them to an inspector if you can help it; I doubt they'd conform to code. And actually, as long as you were careful not to split out the boards, 16d nails would work just as well if you use construction adhesive too... Steve
