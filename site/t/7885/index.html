Does the painted surface (with thick oil paint) diffuse the high frequencies well?
The painted surface would reflect rather than diffuse, possibly contributing to any flutter echoes already existing.
And what foam/ insulation would you recommend for bass control in the back of them if so?
What you need to ask for is either rigid fiberglass, such as Owens Corning 703 (un-faced) or rockwool, mineral wool, somewhere between 2 to 4 pounds per cubic foot density; Johns Manville and Knauf also make similar products. Steve

knightfly8 January 2007#4
This is indeed a challenge - in over 40 years of marriage (and about 25 years of "acoustic awareness") I've come to the conclusion that, for the most part, "WAF" and decent room acoustics are diametrically opposed :? Your "modal nightmare" room ratios definitely need a lot of help - and covering your corner traps with anything as acoustically opaque as painted canvas will make these absorbers act more as bass (only) traps, making it more difficult to get your room semi-acceptable for a decent balanced listening position - the actual distribution of modal energy isn't all that bad, but it's bunched up at several frequencies due to your dimensions being either identical or at even multiples (3x either width or length = 2x the height) One possible way around some of this might be asking your wife to try a new "medium" - use a more open weave cloth to cover normal 3 PCF thick corner absorbers, then using thinner paints use this cloth as a "canvas" and create a new painting that does NOT have all the pores of the cloth saturated. It should be obvious by now that Picasso is NOT one of my previous identities, but this might be something to look into. Solid false "walls" can help some of the more obvious room faults such as flutter, if they are angled by at least 6 degrees per side (or 12 degrees if only used on one wall) but a mix position needs to be symmetrical or it will mislead you on stereo balance. Another idea for corner traps with heavy canvas paintings over them - if these aren't priceless masters, maybe finding a way to "micro-perforate" the canvas AFTER paint has dried would help acoustic absorption without causing the art to suffer - assuming people won't be taking a magnifying glass to examine the artwork, that is. One of the major manufacturers of acoustic products offer such a panel in acrylic sheet for places where absorption is needed without blocking lines of sight. Ideally, if there is ANY way you can dedicate a room to only ONE use it will be MUCH easier to make it useful to THAT purpose - especially if you can close the door and explain to your wife that if she doesn't like the way it looks there are OTHER rooms (good luck on THAT one; it only took me about 30 years to reach that point, and it's STILL sort of an "armed truce") HTH... Steve
