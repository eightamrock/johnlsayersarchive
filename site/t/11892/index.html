Soundman20208 April 2009#2
Second floor can be done, but it ain't easy or cheap, mostly because of the weight issue. The studio floor would need to be concrete, probably, in order to take the weight, and even though its just a garage below, you'd probably still need to isolate it with a second leaf ceiling of some sort, or else the entire garage will become part of your "air", and would basically be a giant resonant cavity. It's easier to build it at ground level, IMHO. - Stuart -

Soundman20209 April 2009#4
I know most metal buildings suck for sound, but these have insulation between two thick layers of metal. Would this work good?
I don't think that would work very well, to be honest. There isn't nearly enough mass in those walls to give you a useful level of acoustic isolation. My guess is that those panels are designed for thermal insulation, not for acoustic isolation. To stop sound, you need mass, and those panels don't have it. You also need to complete, independent "leaves" around you, without any mechanical connection between them. Those buildings don't have that either. You are probably much better off sticking to the tried-and-proven MSM method outlined all over this forum. It's the most economical way of getting good isolation using standard building materials. - Stuart -

Soundman20209 April 2009#7
So I will not need total sound isolation. I will, however need isolation between each room.
It's the exact same thing: You cannot have isolation between two rooms unless you also have isolation all around both rooms. In the exact same way, you cannot build an above-ground swimming pool by building only one wall of the pool, then trying to fill it up! Obviously, unless you have all of the walls in place, the water will simply spill out all over the place. Sound is the same. If you want to stop sound from leaving one room and getting into the other, you need to build a completely sealed "shell" around both of them (or at least around one of them). Tom is right: Get Rod's book, and carry on researching this forum. US$ 3100 will go a long way towards building a decent outer leaf for your studio, but building it the proper way. - Stuart -
