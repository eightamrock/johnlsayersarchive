rod gervais19 October 2007#9
Mac. if this is for a commercial facility in the US - the sliding glass doors do not meet the ADA requirements for accessibility - and are therefore not code compliant. If this is a personal studio that will never be available to the public - like the typical home studio - then it doesn't matter....... BTW - if clients are going to do business with you - just calling it a personal studio doesn’t change the fact that it's a private facility available for public use according to the requirements of the ADA....... Be aware that should a complaint be made to the ADA and should you be found to be not in compliance - penalties can run to as much as 10,000 per occurrence. Now to explain the problems with the doors themselves......... The ADA establishes a maximum threshold height of 1/2" for interior thresholds....... see here:
4.13.8* Thresholds at Doorways. Thresholds at doorways shall not exceed 3/4 in (19 mm) in height for exterior sliding doors or 1/2 in (13 mm) for other types of doors. Raised thresholds and floor level changes at accessible doorways shall be beveled with a slope no greater than 1:2 (see 4.5.2).
Creating the ramp, dropping the doors so that there is no raised threshold (which I have seen in some studios) creates a separate problem - which is the slot created in the floor - see here:
4.5.2 Changes in Level. Changes in level up to 1/4 in (6 mm) may be vertical and without edge treatment (see Fig. 7(c) ). Changes in level between 1/4 in and 1/2 in (6 mm and 13 mm)shall be beveled with a slope no greater than 1:2 (see Fig. 7(d) ). Changes in level greater than 1/2 in (13 mm) shall be accomplished by means of a ramp that complies with 4.7 or 4.8.
Now the question becomes - at what width does this become a change in level - which is actually answered under the section relating to Gratings:
4.5.4 Gratings. If gratings are located in walking surfaces, then they shall have spaces no greater than 1/2 in (13 mm) wide in one direction (see Fig. 8(g)). If gratings have elongated openings, then they shall be placed so that the long dimension is perpendicular to the dominant direction of travel (see Fig. 8(h)).
So they are looking for a slot a maximum of a 1/2" wide - (or series of slots) for accessibility - and you will have one slightly over 1 3/4" - the establish a maximum depth of 1/4" for no special treatment - from 1/4" to 1/2" sloped leading edges @ a max slope of 1: 2 and (for what you would have - which would exceed 1/2" = a ramp (which is not possible on the fixed side of the doorway....... Anyway - hate to be the bearer of bad news - but this is reality. There are commercial sliding walls that you can create doorways from - and these are legal - (which none of the residential or commercial standard exterior sliding doors are) but these are relatively expensive...... For an example of a code compliant assembly look at the Kawneer 1040 assembly.... http://www.kawneer.com/kawneer/north_am ... od_id=1848 I hope this has helped you. Sincerely, Rod
