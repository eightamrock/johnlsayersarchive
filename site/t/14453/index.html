... so this makes a total of 3 layers:
It also makes a three-leaf wall, which is generally not a good idea! Search the forum to find out why. That said, in your case you already have a pretty darn good two-leaf with your brick and block walls, so why do you think you need the third leaf? What are you expecting it to do for you?
Ceiling will be the same as the inside layer of the walls: 2 sheets of plasterboard mounted on wood frame with mineral wool inbetween.
So your ceiling will be your weak point, by far. Why bother with all the trouble of "bricks and blocks" for the walls, if you are just going to build a much lower isolation ceiling?
Does anyone know what STC rating I might get with the above?
You will get whatever the ceiling gives you, since it will be the weakest link in the chain, bar far. Not sure here, just guessing, but maybe around the mid 40's, or perhaps the very low 50's, if you get really lucky. Look up one of the IRC papers to get a better idea. There is probably something in there similar to what you are planning.
so it needs a damp proof course, heating and electric installed etc.
It also needs ventilation! It will be doubly-airtight, hermetically sealed. You will need to get air in and out, and do it without losing too much isolation.
I'd like to be able to have drums playing in the room, and not hear them 10 metres away. What STC rating do I need to achieve this?
STC isn't a good system for deciding on drum isolation, since it does not take low frequency sound into consideration, and a lot of energy from a drum kit is of course in the low end. So one wall rated at STC-70 might not do what you want, while another one rated at STC-65 might do the job just fine. It all depends on how well those walls isolate at the bottom end of the spectrum, which STC does not consider. You also need to define what you mean by "can't hear drums 10 m away". How quite is that, in real decibel terms? What is the average ambient level at that 10 m location, at the quietest time of day? You need to be quite a bit lower than that in order to be inaudible.
I have heard many people have soundproofed up their garage and managed this, so I'm hoping I should easily manage it with a dual skin dedicated building.
Yes, but you are proposing a three-leaf building, not a two-leaf building, so the low frequency isolation in your case could end up being WORSE than for those two-leaf buildings.

"It also makes a three-leaf wall, which is generally not a good idea! Search the forum to find out why. "
Already done that. Can you let me know why or give me a specific link to read?
Well, on this forum, there are literally dozens of explanations for three-leaf walls and why they are so bad for isolating sound. I know they are there, since I wrote many of them myself, and I've read many that others have written too. You really should be able to find them with the "search" feature at the top of this page. Look for things like "MSM" and "three-leaf".
"That said, in your case you already have a pretty darn good two-leaf with your brick and block walls, so why do you think you need the third leaf? "
Because I need top-notch isolation for recording quiet sounds.
In that case, a three-leaf wall would be a BAD idea, since it provide LESS isolation that a two-leaf wall, all other factors being equal. Simple physics. But not intuitive at all. (Practically nothing about acoustics is intuitive).
What would you specifically suggest for the ceiling? Thanks
Build it the same as the walls. Two-leaf, fully decoupled, no mechanical connections at all between leaves, both leaves totally airtight, lots of mass on both leaves, large air gap between, filled with mineral wool or fiberglass. You have to have the same construction all around your room, on all sides. Anything less wont work. Just like you cannot build an aquarium by using glass for the back wall, paper for the front, sponge on each side, and cardboard underneath. It has to be glass all around to work. Same for your studio: it has to have the same level of construction all around. Otherwise it will "leak" sound through the weaker sides, just like the aquarium would leak water.
As soon as I can find any designed for ventilation that will be the next step. Right now most important thing is to have the soundproof room design sorted.
The design of the ventilation system IS part of the soundproofing! Yo cannot do one without the other. It all goes together. To isolate, your room has to be airtight. Hermetic. Sealed. But the ventilation system is obviously a hole through that airtight seal. It has to be designed right, acoustically, or the entire isolation breaks down. A studio isn't a set of individual things that you can add up one by one: it is an entire system, where everything you do affects everything else. HVAC is a big part of it.
Thanks. What would you suggest?
TL (Transmission Loss) gives you a much better picture of how well a give structure isolates, for purposes of studios.
