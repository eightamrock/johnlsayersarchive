Dan Fitzpatrick28 April 2006#6
if you truly do have 2 1/8 inches of drywall on your current ceiling, then you are good to go. i don't think you need to add more. then do your walls to be about the equivalent mass. if you want better performance, make your inner leaf walls and ceiling extra thick. but again, if you have over 2 inches of gypsum or equivalent mass on each leaf that is pretty darn good. 5/8 is good because it is thicker so for the same amount of work you end up with more mass. your description of my beef up process is right on. i do use backing rod and caulk each layer, for redundancy in case any of the seams fail. this seems like a lot of work but actually backing rod and caulking are one of the easier parts of the beef up. the hard part is just cutting the odd widths of gypsum to fit, that is the biggest PITA. the gap that you end up with between the gypsum and stud can be anywhere from non-existent to over 1/2 an inch, just because everything is not straight i guess. so i have different sizes of backing rod on hand for different size gaps. the ceiling may be a weak link but then again the roof will provide some additional decrease in db. i had an idea i might throw some insulation ABOVE my ceiling outer leaf, not sure if it is worth it though. but insulation helps deaden sound and decrease resonances. it's more a question of cost/benefit.

well i shot for a 1/4 inch gap, but as i said the results varied some :) i tried to use 1/4 inch rod and it's easy to put in the crack with a window screen roller tool (looks like a pizza cutter wheel). the worst thing is actually to have no gap, because having a littel space to put caulk in is a good thing. you do not want to stick the panels together, it is better that they can vibrate independently instead of as a unit. doing the OSB layer sounds like a good idea. however one thing to watch out for is that if you do an OSB layer you may think you can just screw in teh next layer of drywall anywhere, but in reality you still want to hit the studs. if you use OSB you may take a small hit on performance because it is probably a little less massive than the equal thickness of gypsum. offhand i don't know what the difference in weight is, it mgiht not be that much. then again there is some theoretical benefit to using different materials in your wall so maybe that's a plus. also if you use OSB i think that was recommended in your other thread in design, that this will keep the walls from being able to "rack" ... which is actually a good thought and i may do that myself.
