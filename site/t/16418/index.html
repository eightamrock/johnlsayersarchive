Before paying money to any designer, I'd want to know about his track record! What has he already built? See plans, visit customers, check reputation, etc. Take a close look at his work, and see if it is any good. Talk to his customers when he is not in around to influence the conversation. etc. As a very rough ball park figure, it seems that studio design fees run in the same general range as house design (per square meter or square foot), and then some. So find out what an architect would charge you in your area for designing a good quality house (per square foot/meter), add a healthy percentage to that, and you'll have a rough idea of what a professional studio designer would charge you for designing your studio. Of course, it isn't quite that easy: there are many other factors that go into the studio designer's fee too! But it will give you a basic ball-park figure as a point of reference. So, compare that 1,500 to 2,000 figure against the size of your studio and the local cost of house design, to see if you have a reasonable figure. If the designer is charging substantially more or substantially less than the "ball-park", then you might want to ask why! But first and foremost, I'd look for track record: If they guy is good, and has done a few designs that turned out happily for his customers, then he should have no problem telling you about them, and the money will be well spent. But if he has nothing to show, or won't put you in contact with customers, then you might want to start wondering why... And when it comes to building costs, you can use the same rule: What does it cost per square meter to build an up-scale house in your area? It will cost roughly the same per square meter to build a studio. Well, that's my US$ 0.02, anyway. FWIW. - Stuart -
