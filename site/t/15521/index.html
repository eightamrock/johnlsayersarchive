gullfo28 January 2011#7
you will need to separate the rooms. you have some options for independently floating the walls and the floor so the isolation walls sit on the existing floor via isolation mounts, and the inner floor is floated on rigid insulation. you could also make the rooms rectangular (choose good ratios) and use treatments inside the room for the angles - this can help with creating more overall volume and possibly simplify the construction for you and your builders.

xSpace4 February 2011#9
"1: outer world-existing concrete wall-rockwool(which side the paper?)-air-again rockwool(which side the paper?)-lead sheet-plasterboard-my studio" Your finish room should resemble this picture that you posted of the existing walls configuration. This is a basic double wall assembly that everyone is talking about:) If you have lead sheets, and it reads like you do, this is a good thing. The paper on the insulation always goes to the warm side/interior of the building. When you are trying to isolate, that means stop sound, you want mass. Insulation does stop some mids and high frequencies, but mass stops the low frequency and taking care of the low end as a focus will eventually get a better overall isolation level on all the frequencies.
