Soundman202013 September 2011#55
I wouldn't sweat it too much. The backer rod is not life-or-death, as long as they have good seals on both layers, with correct size gaps filled with good quality flexible acoustic caulk, and they staggered the joints between layers, you should be fine. Backer rod is just extra peace of mind, but if the seals are good on both layers, and staggered, and flexible, then don't worry too much. What coverage did they use for the Green Glue? How many tubes per 4x8 sheet of drywall? (Just curious.)
So tomorrow morning at 8am we are meeting at Home Depot to get the door ( SOLID and heaviest I can find )
Make sure you get extra heavy-duty hinges, and really good seals! If you can, AVOID cutting holes through the door for mounting your latch: better to use a door-closer and plain old simple handles on each side (once again, heavy duty!). Cutting holes through the door reduces isolation a bit. If the door already has a hole cut, well, you can live with it, but it won't give maximum isolation like that. Are you going to "beef up" the door with extra layers of MDF or suchlike? It would be good if you can... You should have at least two full seals around the entire door perimeter (top, bottom and both sides. They should both be airtight. You might need to install some sort of threshold for the floor part, to get a good seal.
... and when I banged and pushed on the drywall the bounce was there.
The GG is not fully cured yet, but you should already notice that it doesn't sound like normal drywall if you bang on it: kind of a dull thud, with no "ringing" to it. Right? About the backer rod: I reckon they owe you a BIG discount for having screwed up like that! :) ( It ain't a huge deal, but they don't have to know that... :) ) - Stuart -

Soundman202013 September 2011#60
More pics! Great! So it DID happen! :) A couple of comments: The Green Glue spread looks a little light. Probably not a big deal, since even low coverage still does good things for you! It's hard to say for sure, but from IMG_2780.jpg and IMG_2779.jpg it looks like they supported the inner-leaf ceiling drywall on top of the top batten that is directly attached to the outer-leaf studs! They should not have done that. They should have left a gap there, so that the ceiling drywall is well clear of the outer leaf, even with a small gap to the inner-leaf drywall, which is then sealed with caulk. You might want to check that they did that correctly. If not, then they have created a direct flanking path all around the entire room, from the inner-leaf ceiling to the outer leaf. That needs to be fixed. IMG_2784.jpg seems to show the same thing: the wall drywall and ceiling drywall seem to be touching, and they applied the caulk along that edge. There should have been a gap between them (where the backer rod should have gone!) that was just filled out to the surface with caulk, but from the photos it seems that there was no gap, and the caulk is just sitting in the corner. If so, that needs to be fixed. There also seem to be MULTIPLE penetrations by electrical cables, in both the walls and the ceiling! :shock: To me, it looks like they just went ahead and drilled holes through your brand-new high-transmission loss wall, and poked the cable through, with no thought at all as to what that does to your isolation! That also needs to be fixed. That's what I noticed on the photos so far. Maybe you can take some close-ups, to clarify these points? Regarding the dual seals on the door: You need one seal that goes all the way around the edge of the door, between the door and the jamb, and is totally air tight when the door is closed. Then you need another seal that accomplishes the exact same thing, an inch or two behind the first seal. Normally, these doors are built up in layers in order to get the mass that you need, such as by adding a thick sheet of MDF on top of the existing solid core base. You make that MDF a bit smaller than the door itself, all the way around, and you also build a second jamb on your door frame, that is a bit larger than the original jamb. The MDF mates with the second, larger jamb. You put the second seal in between those two. See page 94 of Rod's book for details on how to do this. It's very easy, when you understand the concept, and very necessary, if you need good isolation. - Stuart -
