plus6vu17 March 2007#4
If your concerned about the AC working properly with two different kinds of ductwork, that's not a problem. As long as you can mechanically join the two and keep the air from escaping, the AC will work fine. What you want to make sure that you do however, is do the best you can to make the downflow seam so that it doesn't protrude into the duct, thus impeding airflow. If you are concerned with sound transmission issues with joining the two different types of ducts, I'm thinking that you're on the right track with the ductliner in that it will considerably help the metal part of the duct. Generally the metal duct has very little insulation on the inside. Sometimes it's just enough to keep it from sweating on hot days. Scott

The thing that bothers me with this pproach is that you are ow shrinking up the duct size - which means increasing the static pressure on the system - which might be already maxed out based on the motor/fan - when you use insulated duct the inside of the duct is the same size as the metal - when you use duct liner you increase the duct by the liner thickness so as to maintain the same design parameters. Thus -with 1" liner - for a 12x20 duct - you would construct a 14 x 22 galv shell. I would not proceed with liner until I had a mechanical engineer verify that my system could handle the added static pressure while still maintaining my building requirements. BTW - the answer to the original question is "yes - youcan transition from duct board to metal duct. The installation manual is clear on how to acheive this. Take care, Rod

Kris, I wouldn't put a whole lot of stock into what he has to say......... he is a salesman - sells product to make or line duct - that doesn't make him an expert in HVAC design - What matters is your existing fan coil system - what it's design limits are for static pressure - what the existing static pressure is - and what it would be if you shrink the duct size. Typically most residential systems do NOT have a lot of slop in the design...... this because they aren't usually designed by engineers - but rather by the contractors installing them - and they want to get your house cooled and heated for as little money as they possibly can as to improve their profit margins. So they do not plan for people adding onto these systems unless they are specifically told by the homeowner to do exactly that.. Your equipment has listings on it - and the actual static pressure can be measured at the unit to determine what is available (if any). Sincerely, Rod
