Hello all. I have been reading this forum for a day or two and am amazed at the pure wealth of information. I have used the search feature for this question but haven't totally found my answer so.... 1) I am located in Southern California. 2) I have a vocal booth that I just built and now need to treat the inside for room reflections. You can get dimensions from the attached picture. I am using an Exterior 2' wide steel door for the room (It has the built-in weatherproofing so I thought this would help with sound escaping/entering) and, at this time a 2'x2' fixed window from Lowe's. (Just a standard dual-pane vinyl window) I know, I know, I'll be replacing that soon enough with a custom built window. I know I'll need to cover the inside of the door as well as the entire room but with what? I am on a budget so... I'm not looking for pro-level treatment but something that will make this a usable space for vocals as well as for acoustic guitars, etc... Is carpet a good enough insulator for room reflections? Should I use fiberglass panels covered with "something"? The booth is built using standard 2x4's (not staggered) and a single layer of 1/2" drywall inside and outside the booth. I used R19 insulation inside the entire booth except for the rear two walls (the 6' 8" sides) as these were existing and I didn't want to open them up. (I know, I should have) My main goal is to have a room that will be mostly "dead" / "dry" but not too dead. Thoughts? Thanks in advance! Brad

AVare26 September 2008#2
You it as dead as possible. A room that size does have a desirable sound to it. Treatment is easy. Use 4" thick fiberglas and face it with an open cloth. Andre
luvtorekord26 September 2008#3
Thanks AVare. Is that to say that I should use a 4" rigid fiberglass? (Probably not since that's probably impossible to find) So I should put up some R13 insulation and then cover it? I'm guessing I should use an insulation that has no facing right? This way the sound can penetrate straight through the cover and then into the insulation. Anyone else have $.02 to say about this recommendation?
AVare26 September 2008#4
No facing. I wrote nothing about rigid. 3 lb/ft^3 glass or 4 lb/ft^3 mineral based insulation. Use bob's page for more product info. OC, Knauf, Fibrex, Roxul all make suitable material. Andre
