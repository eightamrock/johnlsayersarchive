gullfo20 June 2011#2
maybe just ensure the moisture barriers you apply allow you to finish off the interior as a regular room using RC-1 on the walls and RC-2 on the ceiling (or if you can use isolation clips and hat channel) and at least a single layer of 5/8" type x drywall or 2 layers. then use super chunk absorption in the corners, 4" thick absorbers on your first reflection points - walls and ceiling, reflection points on your back wall, and then some scattered absorption around the room to balance.

xSpace23 June 2011#11
"The shed is wrapped with this house wrap as a moisture barrier" See this is a common misconception. The exterior wrap is a drainage plane that provides protection in the exterior environment from air driven moisture. A vapor barrier or more correctly referred to as a vapor diffuser retarder will be placed on the interior side of the build, if it is required. The theory is that vapor moves from the warm side to the cold side. So my concern has nothing to do with isolation or even acoustics, it has to be with a proper building and how this is approached, which few can challenge me, if any, on how to go about this aspect...it is after all a structure that has nothing to do with acoustics. Good luck,

What I am saying is this. IF this building has a framed wall with an actually Class 2 vapor barrier (poly) on the interior side of the framed wall AND it has sheetrock on this interior side AND you add more interior framed wall assemblies then you run a REAL risk of trapping the potential for condensation INBETWEEN the walls, the very same walls that have insulation in them. Remember, I did not bring up the vapor barrier....I just jumped in since I have a little experience in this area...well, a lot, but who is paying attention? But since isolation is not a concern for you, what ever you do will most likely be alright in terms of acoustic treatments. It only changes when you alter the structure, like your mention of doing an inside out wall, which would be fine as long as the mass of the interior wall mated up directly against the interior side of the existing exterior sheathed wall, no air cavity. Once you introduce any air space between the sheathing of the wall assemblies, then (depending on the whole wall interior/exterior assembly sheathing) you run the risk of an incorrectly place VDR, there again, if the wall assembly requires it.
