In your research, you should try to find out what is within your walls and ceiling for framing, existing insulation and most importantly - what thickness of wallboard. If you just add more layers of wallboard to the ceilings and walls it will not be as effective as you could achieve. There will always be direct contact with the other side of the wall/ceiling through the studs/joists in the framing. the best scenario may be to pull all the wallboard out of the room, evaluate what you have and build upon that. You may be well served by inserting strips of wallboard within the wall cavity of the far side of the wall assembly (against the surface of the other room's wallboard. Most existing construction threads here contain some form of this practice and is termed as "beefing up the mass inbetween the joists" or the like.. Once you have a plan for that outside layer, you can think of options to de-couple the inner wall which is commonly referred to here as a "leaf". Each leaf (section of wall or ceiling) shouldn't touch the other leaves. You'll see a lot of work done with backer rod and acoustic caulking. Options you may end up considering are forms of buffered furring channels. RC (resilliant channel I think is the acronym..) with RC clips that are the actual buffer to the wall could work on your ceiling. There are other furring channels which can be used for the walls too. All wallboard is screwed to these mounts without contact made directly to the framing. now if you are willing to bring the demensions of the walls in and do additional framing you could frame interior walls with angles condusive to better acoustics (non perpendicular shape to the room). The things to learn about for this are - Fire blocking at the top of the wall frames. whether you're wiling to have an independantly framed ceiling and whether you can even do this based upon what utility runs are in the ceiling. Other weak, but neccessary parts of the room are windows and venting for air. These are strong concerns for any room and need to be planned out thoroughly or you're just wasting your time == money... At anyrate - that is all stuff that comes to mind after reading your post. I think others would appreciate more detail about the walls of your room. There can be much to learn about in these areas. Terms And Other Thoughts - not sound proofing - you're looking to Isolate the sound on either side of the room or outside world. You mentioned jagged walls - do you mean that you *are* interested in adding another interior frame? Or are you speaking of acoustic treatment? I've seen this term confused with those beginning threads. adding mass to the ceiling (layers) - you REALLY need to know what you have supporting the ceiling as you're adding 5/8'' drywall for example. It weighs over 70lbs per sheet and you need to know if the dead load rating of the frame can handle this.
