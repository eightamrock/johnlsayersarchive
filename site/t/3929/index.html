sharward22 June 2005#6
Hey DD, Retrofitting an existing space to increase transmission loss (known as "T.L.," or "adding soundproofing") is no easy task. It's typically major construction surgery to get any real results. There are products by manufacturers that claim they are "magical" (such as QuietSolution's stuff), but when you do the math (costwise and in low frequency TL), you're left facing the reality that there is no easy or inexpensive way to get good results. We don't mean to discourage you -- our goal is to keep you from following a path of frustration, buying or building things that have been proven not to work. For what it's worth, I have been researching and redesigning my space for six months now, and I have yet to put two pieces of wood together. It's 90% planning and 10% building. Part of the 90% planning is learning as much as you can about what works, what doesn't work, and why. Granted, we don't have any real information about your existing space... But I predict that you will discover that in order to get the results you're hoping for, you will have to develop a two-leaf, Mass-Air-Mass system. Keep reading and studying, and you'll get there. If there were a cheap and easy solution to the problem, I dare say that attendance of this forum would be far less than it is. Sorry... Laws of physics are pretty darned stubborn. :roll:
Dan Fitzpatrick22 June 2005#7
hey man, yeah we're not trying to be mean. but you really do have to do the reading and educate yourself. but i'll give you a quick and dirty answer to your question
My rooms built and i wanted to know what i can put on the walls that could reduce the noise transmition through the walls.
what you put on the walls is more drywall, as many layers as you can, and make sure it is sealed with caulk preferably special acoustic caulk that will not crack over time. preferably add drywall to both sides if you can but if not add more layers ot the inside. you don't say, but you mention walls of block, so if your probelm wall is block then make sure it is sealed painted well so you can't see any tiny holes/pores in the block -- on both sides. and then add more drywall to this as above. if you can, remove the existing wall board and put insulation int he walls first, because that will help a lot too. there is usually no insulation in interior walls. and make sure your doors seal airtight, even around the bottom. use some kind of weatherstripping and make sure the doors close tightly. hope that helps. if you want to help us help you give us more details, a sketch or even some photos would be nice.

Hey DD, I'm not picking on you (really!), but you do have some more studying ahead of you. There's a world of difference between absorbant materials that reduce sound reflections (i.e., decreasing echo inside the room) and mass materials that increase transmission loss (i.e., decreasing sound escaping from the room).
