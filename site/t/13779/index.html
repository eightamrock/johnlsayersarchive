1.) Anyone see any other design/layout opportunities that I may have overlooked?

2.) The newly constructed walls (b/t rooms & hallway wall) would be double stud, 2” airspace, 703, and double 5/8” drywall w/green glue on both sides. BUT on the existing walls, could any decent isolation could be achieved by only adding drywall and green glue layers to the existing drywall on the outer walls and the ceiling and treating with 703 and slot resonators (pseudo-inside out)? Or would this do next to nothing isolating the space from the living space upstairs? Do I need to get in between the studs and joists with mass layers to beef things up? Keep in mind I am obviously also trying to conserve square footage.
Considering the intended SPL, especially at Low Frequencies, I submit you will have to not only add extensive mass, but decouple it as well. Considering the small spaces, I would also submit you will have to plan in extensive Low Frequency absorption too, as Isolation equals little Transmission loss to the outside world. I would suggest a multi tiered strategy for small footprint Iso construction with different TL assemblies for walls that are part of the Exterior than those that are Partition walls. However, the biggest problems are your Slab, and existing ceiling. Since you will have a common slab between CR and Live room, and it is most likely is integeral with the foundation footings or at least poured within the building foundation, this is a direct flanking path for structural transmission. Not so much for airborn sounds, but impact transmission from drums. For this I would suggest a floating platform of 4" concrete, or at least a sandfilled framwork with 2 layers of drywall and top layer of 3/4 MDF. For ceiling, I would submit you indeed will have to line the joist cavitys with 2 layers of drywall, and build ceiling assemblies either supported by the interior Partition walls, or suspended via RSIC decouplers and Hat channel, or use RC fastened to the existing joists. For an overall strategy, I would suggest a complete decoupled inner envelope in each room. However, since(I asume)SOME of the walls below grade are probably concrete/concrete block, without knowing how high they are, among other things, at this point, it would be impossible to detemine exactly what TL strategy to apply to any given exterior wall, which in turn may determine other things, such as interface connections with cieling and partition wall assemblies. So untill I know some more information, I hesitate to further advise anything.
3.) Are the ceramic tile floors able to stay? Can the base plates of the framing be drilled through the existing tile floor? Anyone have experience with this? Or will I end up needing to replace all of the flooring?
Personally, I would cut away the tile where ever partion walls are placed. You may need to use some type of Partition wall floating devices, such as neoprene pads at bottom, and sway bar decoupling at the top. This still remains to be determined though. Existing joist locations, HVAC ducting, and other things will have a bearing on this too.
4.) The large closet in the live room is one of our only good-sized storage areas in the house, so it must stay…I suppose mass must also be added inside the closet?
I would assume so if it's open to the room. There may be another strategy though. I played with your layout a bit to illustrate. See below. BTW, see the jpg with the drums? I would move the window wall 16" to align the wall for a Soundlock door and make a wider Live room. The closet opening is 4’5” and extends under the stairway, so it is triangular, and about 2 ½’ deep. I was thinking of a track-mounted, sliding barn-door covered with 703? Stupid? Suggestions? I don’t think I can get away with hangers in there as we need easy access to the storage. Yes, you could add one layer perhaps, and or add a solid core door, as well as create a Soundlock with all solid core doors. Forget the 703, at least for TL considerations. It does nothing in that regard. As to hangers, I'm still unqualified to answer about those. Theres lots of disagreement on the net regarding LF performance. As far as the closet...well, I would think clothing would help with any resonance :mrgreen:

This has to do with the ultimate choices for TL assemblys. I have to play around with it for bit to see the advantages/disadvantages of any given strategy. Perhaps the use of Staggered Stud or 2x6 framing with RC for partition walls. I'll post some sections later.
