Soundman202012 December 2008#2
Wouldn't this make a single leaf system though?
Yes.
I'm wondering if it would be far more effective to have a space between the layer of drywall and quietrock about a half an inch instead?
That won't do anything about the joists! They will flank the sound directly to whatever you use as your lowest layer. And a half inch is not enough airspace to be useful: you need at least several inches (four or more). Unfortunately, if you want isolation you are going to have to sacrifice an inch or two of height. You still need to beef up the floor above anyway (just like you said, with a couple of layers of drywall attached to the floor between the joists, then rockwool) but you also need to have a decoupled second leaf underneath that. In such limited space, your best bet is probably to use RC directly on the joists, and hang your drywall from that. Depending on how much isolation you need, one sheet of 5/8 might be enough, but my guess is that you'll need layers two plus a layer of Green Glue in between. However, having said that: Are you absolutely certain that those joists can handle all the extra load? You are talking about adding many hundreds of kilograms of mass to that floor above you. Did you check with a structural engineer to make certain that you actually can do that safely? - Stuart -

Soundman202012 December 2008#4
Look at your line of "XX's", going vertically. They directly connect the floor above with your layer of "quietrock". This is called "flanking": So basically all of the rockwool in between is wasted: it does nothing useful, since the sound "flanks" around it, through the joists. So basically, you still only have one leaf, just more massive than before, and with several built in air-pockets.
So it should be able to support 2 layers of drywall no problem.
"Should be able to" isn't good enough. You must check with someone who actually knows what they are talking about with structures, and is qualified to to the anaylsis: You need to check with a structural engineer. And it isn't just supporting 2 layers of drywall: It is supporting the drywall, the "quietrock", the rockwool, the screws, the glue, and the fabric. It all adds up. You don't say how much area you need to treat, my guess is that you are talking at least a thousand kilograms of mass. Do you really think you could drive a car across that floor quite safely? Would you be happy to sit underneath that floor with a car parked on top? I wouldn't. Check with someone who can do the math and confirm in writing that those joists can take an extra few thousand pounds of load without any trouble.

WayneD14 December 2008#13
rossome, looking at the drawing and researching the load capacity of joists/lumber the things I have found are; -the joists do more than support the above floor, they contribute to the integrity of the building -it is recommended that you check your local code as it relates to the build you have -get professional advice on the your plans -tributary load functions are the hidden job of joist systems, in other words, they are doing more than they look to be doing -the OC spacing and joist size determine the load carrying ability -a pool table weighs over 1000lbs -dead weight loads, such as a pool table or drywall need to be calculated against the structure to determine if the structure needs beefing or not These are by no means all inclusive. Others may have different insights and thoughts. However, the main theme here is, do the research. Unbalancing the structural load of the building, although not apparent at first, could show up at a later date. This could be in the form of weakening, shifting or something else quite apparent. Your 2x8s seem suspect by design. Please use a trusted professional for advice. Do what they recommend. Then build what you want. Get more than one opinion. And be safe. xSpace has much experience with construction. He has recommended the same things I have said above, in less words. Although the research was good for me. Thanks for sending me in that direction. Also, there are insurance implications (at least in NJ) should you alter a structure without obtaining the proper permitting. Get some help rossome, it will save you much heartache in the long run. W
