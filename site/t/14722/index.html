Soundman202027 August 2010#2
Hi Thomas, Welcome! The dimensions of your room are reasonable: your room ratio works out to 1 : 1.57 : 1.83, which is not bad, and you have a reasonable volume, at about 41 m3. But the first thing you need to do is to rotate your layout by 90°. Right now, you have the speakers firing across the room (on the short axis), but you really need to get them firing down the room (long axis). It looks like the best way of doing that is to have the desk facing towards the window, with the speakers on each side of the desk. With your current design, your head is on the worst possible place in the room: Right in the middle! You need to try to get your head positioned at about 38% of the distance between front and back walls (that's just a guideline, though), then arrange the speakers so that they form an equilateral triangle with your head. The basic layout of your treatment in the first plan looks OK, so all you need to do is to adapt it to the new orientation of the room. You also need to add treatment at the first reflection points on the side walls. Make those changes, post the updated SkethUp, and we'll take another look! - Stuart -

Soundman20207 September 2010#4
1. You recommend to rotate the room about 90° clockwise. But if i do that, it won't be possible to build the back absorbers at the two corners, because there 's the door to enter the room. isn't that a problem?
Well, you still have place for the one on the right rear corner, and you could probably build a "gobo on wheels" type trap, that you simply wheel in front of the door when you need to do critical listening. That would be one option. But a room has twelve corners, so if you can't get bass trapping in the two best ones, then you can go for the other ten as well.
2. In the new plan, i push the whole workingdesk to the wall, so that my head is positioned at about 38,29% of the distance between front and back walls. Would that be enugh?
That should be fine. The 38% figure is only a guideline, a rough starting point. You don't need to hit it exactly: as long as you are in the general ballpark, you'll be fine.
3. In the actual plan, it is only possible to place an absorber at the right wall, because of the window at the left wall. But i've planned to hang a heavy weighted curtain in front of the window. Would that be enugh absorption?
A really heavy curtain, pleated, would probably work fine. Or you might be able to build something on wheels here too, or even make a "plug" that fits into the window frame and can be taken out if you want to natural light in the room, or put back for critical listening.
Don't get me wrong, i'm not to lazy to change the plan, but i'm really worried about the back wall. But if it is more important, that i rotate the listening position then having some absorber in the back corners, then i will change the whole plan!
Well, it's not a "life or death" kind of thing, but overall I think there are more advantages to rotating the room than there are to leaving it as is. Acoustically, it makes more sense to have the speakers firing down the long axis, and you have an issue of symmetry with the current layout: your doorway looks to be right at the first reflection point on the left wall, and the window is recessed on the right. Once again, those won't make a huge difference, but to my way of thinking, making a whole lot of little changes can actually add up to some big improvements! - Stuart -

Soundman202011 November 2014#19
I took a look at the REW data, but I'm not exactly sure what I'm looking at! There are 20 different measurements in that file, with cryptic names that don't mean much. I assume that the refer to locations in the room where the measurement was taken, but but "bottom front edge L" doesn't tell me much about that.... Also, the levels are still too low: there seems to be a lot of noise in the measurements, so I'm not even sure if they are 100% valid. By increasing the level to around 80 dB, you will be greatly improving the signal-to-noise ratio, and removing doubt about the validity of the readings. There's no calibration file in there, so I assume that you didn't calibrate REW either. So what you should do is to first calibrate REW, both for the actual sound level in the room (using a hand held sound level meter), and also for your sound card, then take a new set of measurements with the mic located at the mix position, exactly 1.2 meters above the floor, on the room center line, and pointing upwards at 45°. Do one test with just the left speaker at a level of 80 dB, then do another test using just the right speaker, also at 80 dB, then one more test with both speakers on, and without changing any levels at all. Post that new MDAT file, and I'll take another look at it. - Stuart -
