jwl6 February 2007#2
Superchunks work really well. If you have the budget for 703, go for it, though rockwool or cotton might be less expensive and give similar performance. In a room that shape, I'd focus on the corner directly behind the mix position, I think that one might be the most important because a lot of the sound will accumulate back there. That said, do as many corners (including wall/ceiling) as you can manage. I'd do normal superchunks on all corners, and put a big absorber on the rear corner, both to act as a bass trap and also to absorb rear reflections. Also because of the shape of the room, you'll need to work extra hard at taking care of side reflections.

jwl6 February 2007#4
not sure what you mean by plywood panel, but I probably wouldn't do it. If you mean a membrane absorber, I think there are better solutions (namely superchunks and slot resonators), especially for smaller rooms. If it were my room, I'd do superchunks in the corners, with a big chunk on the back corner to absorb rear wall reflections, and put slot resonators tuned to some of the problematic frequencies of your room on the side walls.

also keep in mind that the back-side walls will reflect so you need to put some absorption at those reflection points to keep things balanced.
knightfly11 February 2007#7
Definitely absorb a WIDE area across that rear corner - except for low bass, any sound that enters a 90 degree corner will be reflected right back out parallel to the incoming sound - so you'll get all sorts of cancellations at the mix position with your left speaker trying to cancel out your right one, and vice versa. I'd put at least a 6 foot wide, THICK absorber all the way across that corner and hope it's enough... Steve
