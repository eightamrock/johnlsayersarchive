Soundman202029 December 2008#2
Hi John, and welcome. Have you measured your room, with something like ETF? It's hard to know what to treat unless you know what to treat! But as a general rule of thumb, I'd start with abundant bass trapping in the corners. Search this forum for "bass trap" and you'll find plenty of examples of how to do that, most of which you can build yourself easily and cheaply. Then I'd measure the room, to see where the remaining problems are, and treat for that. Probably some diffusion, some absorption on the walls, slat resonators perhaps, maybe a cloud on the ceiling, and a few gobo's on wheels as the final touch, to deal with specific situations with specific instruments. But I'm sure the acoustic experts here can give you a lot better advise than that! That's just my opinion. - Stuart -

Soundman20205 January 2009#5
If I run sine waves from 40 to 300 and measure with my omni mic, i'm not sure if that would be valid because it could just be my speaker / mic response that is not flat.
It would give you an idea, at least, assuming that you have fairly decent mic and speaker: To be more accurate, you could compensate your curves for the frequency response curves for your mic and speaker, or even beg/borrow/buy a cheap measurement mic: Behringer makes one that isn't too expensive, and is reasonably accurate. But overall, I'd expect that the nulls and peaks in your room would far exceed the response deviations of your equipment: I'm assuming that you are using reasonably accurate studio monitors and a reasonably decent omni mic? If you can treat your room well enough to get to the point where you really notice the response issues of a good mic and speaker, then you are doing well indeed! In any event, the issues with your room will be in the time domain, more than in the frequency domain, and you can't blame your mic/speaker for that! Think "waterfall plots". - Stuart -
