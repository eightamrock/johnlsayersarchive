Improve door isolation

Started by Berre on 27 October 2004. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 2560.

Berre27 October 2004#1
Hi, I am currently finishing my small studio, based on a lot of principles adviced over here, in this fantastic forum. My floors are floating, my walls are very solid, I have some panel traps like Ethan's, etc. I already did some lsitening tests inside the room, and I am very happy with the sound. So far, so good. The only real problem I notice, is that my studio door seems to be a major source of sound leakage. Even though I have an airlock (and thus two doors) I find the isolation could still be improved... a lot. The only question is how I can do this without changing too much. I will post a small sketch of the layout, but what I already can tell is that the door in question is a 50mm solid wood door and that a lot of care was already taken to ensure that the sealing is done properly. But when you stand in the airlock, it seems as if the sound is still coming right through the door... even rather high frequencies are still quite audible! Now, some people already adviced me to stick something to the door, e.g. a thick layer of rockwool covered with fabric. But I have my doubts if this will work. What will it do? Will it dampen the sound coming through? Will it dampen the vibrations of the door itself? And what's the best way to do this? On the inside of the studio? In the airlock? As you can see, a lot of questions... So, all advice will be greatly appreciated. Thanks. Regards, Berre

Sen27 October 2004#3
Hi Berre, you say that you're not happy with isolation when standing INSIDE the airlock!Well how is it outside the SECOND door?Two doors and an air lock are supposed to "work together" to provide good isolation between the space (your studio) inside the first door (which you labeled PROBLEMATIC) and the space on the outside of the second door (which in your case i'm assuming is the outside of the building?) I think the rockwool or other absorbent would help a bit to absorb the vibrations INSIDE THE AIRLOCK...so yeah put some on both doors facing the airlock area.I guess you can put some on the other side of the STUDIO SIDE DOOR if you need more high freq. absorption :) So don't judge your isolation inside the airlock , but outside of that outter door, unless you have something important happening in the airlock area... :) And make sure both of your doors are well sealed...remember: 2 leaves of mass and an air space! cheers
