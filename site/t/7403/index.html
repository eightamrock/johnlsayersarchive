sharward1 November 2006#2
The problem with such a "stopper" middle leaf is that it would divide the air pocket between the leaves into two smaller halves, and in fact the total of both halves would be less than before. Don't underestimate the value of the air between your leaves. It may not seem like it's worth much (air is free, right? Well, except for the square footage you lose...), but I can assure you it's worth a lot. A reduction in the air gap causes a worsening of the resonance in the the opposing leaf... And that resonance may actually amplify the noise that was stopped by the first leaf. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward1 November 2006#4
packrat wrote:
I think you have wasted too much space in between the walls. If you have 2x6" +3" space + 2x4" you have 13 inches of air space between the walls. Somewhere I read more than 12" and you pass the point on diminishing return.
I'm the one who often writes about the diminishing return after 12", and I have to respectfully disagree with the notion that he's wasting too much space with 3" between frames. First of all, lumber dimensions are nominal, so 2x4s are 3/½" and 2x6s are 5½". Thus, the gap is actually 12", not 13“. And in this case, he's got plenty of square footage to throw at his very lofty (no pun intended! ;-)) isolation goals. I never said there's no point to air gaps wider than 12". I have said, basically, that the last inches account for less isolation improvement than the first inches. I wouldn't object to his even adding a couple of inches if he wanted to. --Keith :mrgreen:
