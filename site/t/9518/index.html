rod gervais8 October 2007#9
Actually - the celotex forms a part of the 2nd (of 3) leaves - adding drywall to the bottom of it would only add mass to that 2nd leaf. I don't think I would proceed in that manner, right now the celotex itself isn't doing much to either help or hurt you - and I don't believe that the single layer of 10mm (3/8"?) plywood is doing much either........ BUT - if it were me - I would take the money for the 2 layers you were going to put there - and add one them to the ceiling below all of this....... and then consider using a layer of green glue between the inner mostr (room side) layers........... BUT - I caution you - I still believe that the walls - and flanking around them to the upstairs is going to be your biggest weakness......... Rod

generally - if the heater is directly under the tiles it would be covered by the thinset or cement, if its under wood floors its usually left with air space and (as shown) has an insulator underneath to ensure it goes up... maybe another good reason to follow Rod's suggestion to ignore this middle leaf and add the additional drywall to the lower ceiling - it may cause the upper heating system to operate differently (such as soaking up heat into the drywall instead of being reflected back...)
