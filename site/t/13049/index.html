Doro, In your part of the world a basement drain is more of a requirement then an afterthought. The drain was installed for a reason that still exists. Your area of the world requires interior heating on a day-to-day basis that far out numbers somewhere in a warmer climate. With heat comes moisture. Add to that an exterior that is mostly frozen until some thaw takes place on a sunny day, now you have moisture on the exterior as well. Even if your walls are currently concrete, concrete can leak and absorb moisture. And if it is block, well, block and brick can hold moisture like a sponge! To answer your question on using wood products is difficult. But I can say with the utmost certainty that any type of pressed wood or composite type will break down if placed on this floor. I appreciate the french drain you are having installed and would ask that you make it known that you would like to have the exterior of your walls resealed against water if you have not already asked this. Any and all wood products that come in contact with this floor will always have the opportunity to come in contact with moisture, it is the nature of your situation. It may not be much and it may not be often, but the opportunity exists, is what I am meaning.
