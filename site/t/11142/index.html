Location: Western Washington, US Overview: My goal is to design and build a studio on the second floor of an as yet not built garage structure, the only limitations being that one of the external dimensions can be no more than 25 feet in length, and the overall building construction will be "standard" wood frame (no brick or steel frame). Since the first floor will be a garage, the stairs to access the studio will be external. Currently I am in the research and design stage of the project. The studio will be recording live drums and electric instruments, so it will be fairly loud, but the structure will sit on a large property away from neighbors. I took some time to make some mock-ups in 3D Home Architect. One consideration when making these was that the owner of the property intends to do all of the inside construction himself (with my help, of course). He hasn't specified any budget, but I doubt that when he made this once-in-a-lifetime offer he was thinking of room-within-room construction and two doors for every door. But on the other hand, he was very adamant about making it a "real" studio. So my thought is to do standard construction (2x4 studs, 2x6 crossbeams, one layer of Sheetrock on each side, stuffed with OC 703, vaulted ceiling, hardwood flooring, and a minimum of angled walls) and to correct for room modes by adding broadband traps and diffusers. I was thinking of taking advantage of a vaulted ceiling design to use some hanging resonators covered by a faux ceiling cloud of OC 703. Given all of this, I would really appreciate any feedback you could give me on these initial designs. Is it folly to not use the room separation designs? I'm not particularly worried about isolation from outside, but I would like to minimize the chances of the drums bleeding to much into the vocal booth track (a little bleed never hurt anybody). Also, I'm interested in how you know if you need a Slot Resonator in a room, and where you put it. Do you build one and then tune it to the room, or is it a more specific tool?

xSpace wrote:

Most often on this forum the term is used in reference to how many layers of Sheetrock are applied to the surface of a wall, but professionally a single leaf is a wall structure. Two leaf construction would be one wall made of two structures separated by an air gap. At least that's my understanding. Please correct me if I am wrong. In the text you quoted I was using the more colloquial definition to imply how many layers of Sheetrock may be used on the wall surface in contrast to my original intention of only using one layer per surface.

I think what led folks to a particular frame of mind was:
"Apparently two or three leaf construction with separated and floated walls isn't at all beyond what the owner is willing to do"

As a rule your isolation is only as good as the weakest link...often this is the ceiling. If you design a room or rooms with a double framed wall on one side and a single framed wall on the other, the isolation is reduced.
