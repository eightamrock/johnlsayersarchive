Ponch wrote:
Follow-up question: Can you convert (kg/m^3) to (kg/m^2) by taking the cube root and squaring it?
:) You just multiply the kg/m3 with the thickness expressed in m Hence: a panel of 12.5 mm = kg/m3 x 0.0125 a wall of 20 cm = kg/m3 x 0.2 Search for: "density different materials metric" which results e.g. in: http://www.simetric.co.uk/si_materials.htm But you still have to be careful: Gypsum board depending on type and brand can vary from 680 to 1075 kg/m3 (maybe wider). A brick wall can vary a lot in density. Wood can differ a lot between types. (there are also sub-links in the above link) And so on ........ Eric PS: and if you find a standard: refer clearly in your calculation document to this and possible additional other sources. .

yup. basic math :) m3 is 1mx1mx1m for example: Gypsum has a density of about 800kg/m3, just like MDF a 12,5mm (12,5 x 10-3 m) gypsum board than has a density of 800/1000*12=10kg/m2 a 15mm MDF board has a density of 800/1000*15=12kg/m2 :)
