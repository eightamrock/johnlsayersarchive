Soundman202029 May 2009#2
To be honest, I think I prefer your first design. The only thing I'd change on that is to take the two panels on the side walls that are just in front of the first reflection points, and move one to be a cloud above the mix position, and the other to the rear wall, to have more absorption back there. And you do need to do something about those windows: they are right behind your speakers, and look like they are first reflection points to me! Even if they aren't, you'll still have all kinds of comb filtering ant other weird reflections coming off those. Maybe build removable absorptive plugs for them, that you can insert when doing critical listening and remove at other times. Or gobos on wheels, to wheel in front of them for critical listening. I doubt that your mixes will translate well if you don't do something about those windows. - Stuart -

Soundman202030 May 2009#5
There's an easy way to find out where your first reflection points are in a room: While you sit at your mix position, get someone else to hold up a mirror against the walls, ceiling, floor and furniture, and move it around all over the place. Any time you, at the mix position, can see a speaker reflected in the mirror, then there is a first reflection point where the mirror is. At that point, you want absorption. It is surprising where you might find first reflection points, too: Don't forget to move that mirror around the ceiling and floor. A commonly forgotten location is the desk or console surface. Since you can't really put 2 inches of rock wool on top of your desk, you might need to change the design of the desk, or move the speakers slightly in order to deal with that. Or if it is on the console, then you could consider tilting the console a bit, to change the reflection angle.
anyway, I think this should be a lot better. The center wall is made from two standard 4x8 sheets of drywall (with stuff inside, not sure what yet) and the sides are two sheets cut directly in half. ( I did this for pricing reasons) what do you think?
The drywall is fine (although two sheets might be overkill: one would probably be enough) but you need absorption on it. The point of treating your first reflection points is to stop sound "reflecting" off those points directly from the speakers to you ears, so you need to put at least a couple of inches thickness of fiberglass or mineral wool insulation at those points. So you could just make those gobos as frames with drywall, plywood, MDF or something like that on one side, and at least 2" of insulation on the other side, covered with acoustic cloth. You don't really need the center section of that wall, so if you need to save money, you could get by with just making the two angled gobos, and positioning them where the mirror tells you that you need them.
