Soundman20209 October 2009#5
OK, with that additional info, I just don't see any way you are going to drop the extreme SPL inside that room (probably getting close to 120 dB), down to a level where neighbors 20 feet way are not going to hear it at 4 AM (probably below 40 dB), working the way you outlined. You really don't have much choice except to build a full "room-in-a-room" system, with plenty of mass on the leaves and a large air gap. Unless you can persuade the neighbors to move away, and leave their house empty! Seriously, the cupboards will have to go, for several reasons: The three-leaf issue that you already identified, plus you are going to need to get access to the inside of the walls, to beef them up before you build your second leaf. And your garage door idea is not going to fly either: You need to seal both leaves absolutely airtight in order to get good isolation. even a small hole, crack or leak in one leaf will make a huge difference. What I've seen from many similar threads here, is that most people just seal up the garage door totally. They leave it in place for aesthetic reasons, or local code, but beef it up and seal it, then build an inner leaf, which is also sealed. Regarding the "control room in a functional laundry" idea, I'm not convinced that would be a good idea at all: you don't want high humidity in a control room, and you do want symmetry and very carefully contrived acoustics. You also want a reasonable size room, and most laundries are postage stamps. Unless your friend's laundry is unusually large and can be completely gutted, it doesn't sound like a good proposition. I'd suggest that you search the forum for threads by folks who have built )or are building) both a control room and a live room/rehearsal room in a two-car garage. There are quite a few of them! Read through those threads and I'm sure you'll find lots of ideas that you can use in designing a studio for your friend. The other issue is the budget: 5k might go a reasonably long way to doing just a simple windowless single-door rehearsal space inside a double-garage, but I don't see it going very far at all towards doing that plus a control room as well. There are also other issues like electrical and HVAC that you need to consider, and they don't come cheap. Cram half a dozen musicians into a small highly insulated extra-sealed airtight space with their instruments, amps, lights, and other gear, and the temperature will go up pretty fast, with the air getting really stale just as fast. That room will get really uncomfortable after a while. You need to cool the air, pump in fresh air, pump out stale air, and do it all without losing your isolation. I guess I'm not giving you much good news here, am I? - Stuart -
seb9 October 2009#6
This isn't exactly intended for comercial use
Unfortunately, that fact alone doesn't make it produce any less noise. There are 3 things at play. How loud the music is, how sensitive the neighbours may be, and what degree of sound reduction there is between the two. I don't like to bet on how sensitive neighbours are - or, in 5 years those neighbours move out and someone who doesn't appreciate rock music at 3am moves in. I wouldn't want to curtail your friend's creativity but 3am may be out - even 10pm may be out depending on ... The level of sound reduction. There is no way of getting around a room-within-a-room for some serious sound reduction. The new frame is expensive, as are the internal wall linings, although you could always do an extra layer of lining every 8 months for 2 years to allay the costs (3 layers total, provided that the exterior cladding on the garage has got a bit of mass in it).
