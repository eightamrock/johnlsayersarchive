rod gervais11 December 2009#12
Hey Sun, Nice to see you finally ready to start. Seeing as you have a bunch of rigid materials - here's a relatively inexpensive floor i have been having good success with rgar won't cost you an arm and 2 legs - and does not introduce the drum efect that a framed deck will. put down 2" of 703 (or any rigid material in that same 3 pcf range) and then 2 layers of 1/2" pluwood over that. Make sure to overlap the joints in the plywood and screw together with 2 or 2 1/4" screws (drywall screws will be fine). do this after building and sheet rocking your walls, Make sure that the plywood does not touch the drywall on the walls - keep it 3/8" clear all aropund and caulk the edge when finished. This will soften your deck and warm it up a touch. build yor walls with 2x6 at 24" centers instead of 2x4 at 16" cntrs and the studs will be exactly the same total board feet of lumber - just a few feet more for the plates - but a decent increase in Low Frequency isolation. The budget sounds real low to me- even with what you already have - don't forget backer rod and caulk - that is a pretty hefty part of this equation when calculating cost. Rod

:) What? Got up my nose? "6. I was planning on a single wall with 2 sheets of drywall on outer face and 1 on inner face. Do I need to budget for 'green glue' or are there other value glues I can use?" This is a three leaf assembly when you consider that your coupled wall assembly will be inside the exterior walls. Actually, taking your exterior walls into account you will have MASS[exterior siding] -AIR[insulation or air space] -MASS[interior sheetrock of EXISTING wall assembly] -AIR[distance from interior existing wall sheetrock to your proposed new wall] -MASS[your 2 sheets of rock on your NEW wall] -AIR[insulation or air inside your NEW wall] -MASS[your 1 sheet of rock on the interior of the wall you are wanting to build] So what is that all totaled up...M-A-M-A-M-A-M Almost looks like a word doesn't it:) You should spend a few months reading...it's your greatest advantage over the disadvantages you have from knowing nothing about this field. "What I need now is a plan that is easy to build, meets the requirements and is easy to take down without reducing the re-sale value." :) The love is here my friend but you may not know what you are asking. I don't recall the dimensions of this room but if I were you I would try to fit a footprint inside of that existing structure that doesn't have any one measurement being equal to or divisible by any other measurement, example gratis: 8 foot by 8 foot OR 16 foot...or 6 feet by 12 feet OR 18 feet, etc. You can do the floor decoupling as Rod suggested if the money and material is available. It isn't a requirement, but you have options if you are so inclined. Frame up the walls and install your sheetrock one ONE side of the frame[interior side]. But what about the ceiling? If you are going to install a ceiling then you have a different process in front of you. After you frame up the walls, install 1/2 inch OSB sheathing to the interior of this frame. Install your ceiling joists and hang the rock on that. Then install the sheetrock on the wall framing. The OSB will keep the framing erect and keep it[the structure] from falling on your head when it racks from poor construction methods. See...no one wants you to get hurt OR anyone some years down the road that might read this thread and think that a temp environment can be built with no effort. Much in the same way that you as a professional musician cannot tell your student how to play at a stellar level without instruction, schooling and repetition. It is easier to build a permanent structure then it is to build a temporary one...and I speak from experience not what I read or some one told me :)
