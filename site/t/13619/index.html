Overview: 2400 sq. ft. unit on the 3rd floor of an old shoe factory in Milwaukee, Wisconsin... known as the Fortress Building. 5 or 6 total floors. Other wings of the building are current homes to manufactures with heavy duty equipment. This whole unit is already in our possession. It is the combination of myself recording bands and some friends doing video production. They will occupy most of the building while I will be building in an area of about 540 sq. ft. My goals are to build a large enough tracking room for drums, a bass player and two guitar players. The room only has to host the drummer and players with their instruments. The amps will be isolated outside the room. The tracking room is the focal point of this post but eventually I will be treating a control room. This is very much a DIY build, perhaps more than many of you may think. I plan to create my own a/c/ & heat duct system using a powerful window heat/cooling unit. I also plan on running some sort of pvc type piping system to house an extension cord and a breakout box..................... Soundman202021 September 2009#5
I would like to keep as much drum sound out of the lower floor as possible but now I realize it may not be possible. A floating floor will do nothing.
Actually, a correctly built floating floor WILL do what you want. That's not the issue. The issues are: whether or not you have the skills to do it properly, whether or not you have the budget to do it correctly, and whether or not the building structure would be able to carry the load. It seems like the answers to those questions are unknown, so it makes no sense to consider a floating floor, even though it would in fact accomplish what you want. Another thing I'm curious about: What its the purpose of that short diagonal wall on your diagram, across the top right, between your inner leaf and outer leaf? It seems to be creating a third leaf in that area. thus probably reducing your low frequency isolation. - Stuart -
