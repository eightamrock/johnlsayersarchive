len-morgan21 September 2006#2
Can you provide a link to the design you're talking about? It sounds like an "inside out" wall to me but I'd have to look to be sure. If it's a "normal" wall (i.e., drywall facing the inside of the room), OC 703 is going to be a wast of money. The main purpose (as I understand it) of the insulation behind the drywall is to dampen it's resonance (like touching your finger to a cymbal). If, one the other hand, the insulation is covered with cloth of some sort and facing the inside of the room, you'd want the 703 (or mineral wool or something else dense) for it's acoustic properties. This "inside out" type of construction is mainly used when you don't have enough floor space to add acoustic treatments ON the drywall after the wall is built in the "normal" way. I hope that clears things up a little. len

len-morgan21 September 2006#4
It appears to me that there is NO drywall on the wall at all. As long as the wall the frame is up against won't transmit sound (or allow noise in), this would work great. He has stuffed the cavities with mineral wool batts, a very economical and very effective solution. The only drawback I could see to this construction would be having exposed fabric all over the walls and cieling. Very tempting to pencils and small fingers. Any equipment or mic stands that fell over might just puncture all of your careful fabric stretching. You should note that if you build this way, you won't NEED to build any panel absorbers inside the room because it will be essentially completely dead. You might need a little in the corners to tame the LF but that should be about it. len

It sounds to me like 8 pcf mineral wool but that would be just a guess. It would be best if you confirmed it with the supplier. Try SPI in the references. I was told by Knightfly 8 pcf would be a little too dense so I got 4 pcf. You should be able to get mineral wool a little cheaper than that. len-morgan22 September 2006#11
rasdestroyer wrote:
f i were to build cloth panels that fit onto the wall frame to cover the insulation , would i want the rockwool in between the studs to be flush against that cloth/frame, or would it not really matter?
Only for how good it looks. If the insulation sticks out too far, you're going to see it "poking" through the insulation (like a baddly stuffed pillow). You would have to pay close attention to how you attach the cloth frames though so that they wouldn't rattle against the studs. Also, if you chose this construction technique, you couldn't use pink fluffy insulation - it's not nearly dense enough to work. The mineral wool (or similar product) would be best. As far as your slat resonators go, considering this construction method, yes you'd want stand alone absorbers. Not a bad thing really. I just want to emphasize that your are no where near the point of taking hammer to nail yet. You're going to want to have a design drawn up (use SketchUp from Google), and then have everybody here kick it around before you start unless you want to risk doing a lot of work and spending a lot of money that you'll have to lose later. Believe me, the more time you spend planning and redesigning, and "building in your head", the faster and better your end result will be. I know the smell of sawdust is a strong pull but resist the urge until you are ready. len
