xSpace5 February 2011#2
John uses a lot of built in absorption in his inside out walls and brings it back to life with the slats. I like the idea of using a corner broad band frequency absorber like he uses. In your room(control room) that still has far too many open hard areas, I would opt for something like his work but without the hard surfaces, thinking of the super chunk bass trap here...at first. Given that I didn't get room dimensions to work with for your control room, I wouldn't want to speculate further. I will say this, if you spend the maximum amount of energy on your control room it will be time well spent. The reason I suggest this is that whatever you are hearing out of the tracking room...it's being colored by what ever distortions the control room is having. A reflection free zone is required in such a small room as what it seems you are in for monitoring...you got it close, but the front wall is bare and surely must be getting involved in the mix early on. And if the back wall isn't damped in some way in respect to a reflection free zone...its just as guilty. Hops that helps,

I agree with Brien, too little absorption and given the height and width are close in size means you have a lot of reinforcement going on in the control room. If you are staying with stands for the monitors, I'd still build slat resonators and an angled cloud for the front of the room to get as much angle and mode interference as you can. Line the ceiling and walls with spaced absorption and use hangers or super chunks in a set of back wall absorption. If you can make the ceiling analysts with hard backs and hang down a bit to act as wave guides.
