Soundman20206 June 2009#2
Hey Steve, and welcome! Ouch! That's not a pretty room you are stuck with! Apart from the floor, I'd say your single biggest issue in there is going to symmetry. Your stereo imaging is going to be shot to pieces, I don't think you'll be able to get a decent sound stage in that room as it is, and your mixes will most likely not translate very well at all. I'm wondering if you could extend that ugly angled ceiling across the rest of the room (drywall on framing), so at least the front of the room would be reasonably symmetrical? You'd lose the window: how important to you is natural light or the view? You'd probably gain a bit of isolation from the outside, and you won't create a third leaf since you say that wall is brick. Yeah, it would make the room a bit smaller, but it would make it symmetrical at the front, which is important. Regarding isolation: you say that you probably can't add too much extra weight to the room, but you CAN decouple it better! This would involve taking of the existing drywall from the inside of the room on all the walls that are stud walls, putting resilient channel across the existing studs, then putting the drywall back again. If you can put a bit of extra weight up there, then use the drywall that you remove to "beef up" the drywall on the other side of the wall, between the studs, and make your new layer of drywall (on the resilient channel) 5/8. For the "Chimney" wall, I think I'd frame in those little "nooks" on either side of the chimney and put drywall over that, for both isolation and symmetry.
...told me to put down two layers of high density sound insulation(rockwool)
Actually, rockwool is not really "sound insulation". In the sense that it does not stop sound. (If you don't believe me, put a piece of rockwool in front of your speaker playing at a normal level, and see if you can still hear the music with the rockwool on front.... It does not stop sound.) It absorbs sound very well, sure, but wont stop it. What stops sound is mass, not insulation. And high density rockwool won't help you any extra either. Once you get past roughly 48 kg/m3, there is no additional benefit to higher density: It does nothing useful acoustically, except eat money out of your wallet. 48 kg/m3 is the recommended density for acoustic insulation. in walls, ceilings and floor. However, that said, I'm not sure how it would work when squashed up under a couple of layers of "soundboard" on your floor.... hard to say. Depends on many factors... Of your three proposed solutions, #2 does not add mass, so it is the least useful. The other two do add mass, and they also attempt to decouple. That may or may not be successful, but unless you can also isolate the walls from the floor, any benefit you get might not be that great. Of the 3 options, I'm inclined to go with #3, but once again, whether or not it will work effectively depends on many factors. Sorry to be so vague about your floor! Maybe others can give you better ideas on what to do there. But no matter what you end up doing with the floor, unless you also fix your serious symmetry issue, it will be really, really hard to mix well in that room, and to produce mixes that translate well. - Stuart -
