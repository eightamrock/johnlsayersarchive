Spyrow12 September 2007#4
You are going to spend some money, so you have to think everything very carefully. I'm not so experienced as others here in designing studios but I would build something where you can record a battery, despite the money increase. A battery is 100dB loud, if you own or can borrow a decibel meter you can imagine an aproximation. As John states you can get an STC of 33 with a normal sliding glass doors (doing them airtight). A pair will have at least 36 (33+33) and if you build them non-paralel with diferent sizes, width, density, etc, you can get... nobody knows, maybe ~40-42. The STC is "true" for 500Hz and above, but the sliding glass door is worse in the low end than a STC 33 door or window for example. If you are recorded a battery sometime in a bad studio (I did) you know it's possible. It's not the perfect situation but it's possible. I'm designing now a professional studio and it will have sliding glass doors in the battery room entrance :) By the way, if you don't own a decibel meter you can use some software (wavelab has a meter inside) to get used to dB changes (8-10dB aprox double loud) and think about how many isolation you can allow.
