Ethan Winer7 February 2007#3
decode wrote:
i wouldn´t mind to cover a part of them with acoustic-modules, but as i want to keep some daylight, how should i do it ?
Can you make some stands? Or maybe hang them on long wires from the ceiling so you can push them out of the way to get more light? The photo below shows a commercial acoustic panel mounted on a stand. > if i place 2 bass traps on in each corner behind the console would it be enough absorption < No, sorry, not enough for good results. All rooms have 12 corners - 4 where the walls meet each other, 4 more where the walls meet the ceiling, and another 4 where the walls meet the floor. In your case with a drop ceiling, the real corners are above the tiles. This is good, because you can treat those corners and they'll be hidden so you don't have to spend time making it look good. :) --Ethan

decode wrote:
would it be a big no-go if the one absorber on the left has a greater distance to the window than the right absorber has to the wall ? i think that could lead to a unequal frequency balance, more low frequencies on the right ?
Low frequencies propogate all over the place, so a better rule of thumb is, "As much bass trapping as possible wherever the heck you can fit it." :) --Ethan
