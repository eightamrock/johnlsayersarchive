AVare18 August 2004#2
How do you attach the second lair? I'm assuming screws would transfer sound right? Am i supposed to glue it?
First one is straightforward, longer screws. The goal is to try and create a single mass with the two layers. Steve will correct this if I am wrong, but as I understand it the jury is out on whether to glue or not to glue. The more common belief is not to glue. The USG Gypsum Wall Manual is full of this sort of information, including screw types, screwing schedules (spacing) and much other useful data, tips and tricks. There is a link to it at the top of the forum. I strongly recommend you d/l it and study it. Good Luck!

Bob, I hope that was all meant in fun - without at least a "smiley", sometimes people take things like that more as mocking - Still, if yer gonna put words in me mouth, I prefer chocolate or pizza 8) Ptown, the answer to your question depends partially on the framing you will use - if using a single frame with no resilient mounts, then screws through both layers on each side of the wall WILL cause noticeable isolation loss. If either side of a single frame has resilient mounts, the damage from screw flanking is almost totally negated by insulation fill within the wall. If doing staggered studs on common plates, you'll get maybe a dB or two less isolation using screws for both layers, that's just a gut feel though - With full double frames, no problem whatever. Use half as many screws for all but the final layer on each side of the wall, then full schedule (check the USG manual as Andre mentioned) for the top layer. The possibility of glue softening the wallboard and causing Laminating screws to pull through caused Rod and me to decide that wasn't a good way to go - timing is too critical on how long you let the glue set before laminating, plus it's not clear just how strong the shear is with the stripe glue/laminating screw methods of attachment. Not worth the risk in our opinion... Steve
