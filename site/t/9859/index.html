Eric_Desart7 December 2007#4
Certainly don't trow that away. 1) As condition looks perfect (does look hardly used) it certainly looks fine and stored responsibly, hence it should work OK. 2) I'm not sure, but I doubt that B&K made ever anything from class 2, hence that should be a class 1 as per IEC 651 (documentation can tell that). As such more accurate than most people will have here. But it's a very stylized and simple type. 3) If ever needed that microphone capsule + that calibrator can always be used on/with other good modern SLMs. (Is the code of that mic noted in the booklet, I assume it's possibly still an current type?) Only both of these 2 cost easily $ 1000 to $ 1250. That calibrator is a costly (still actual) thing and works with any standard 1/2" and 1/1" measurement mic. 4) That whole thing as is, becomes a collectors item in time (becomes like having a well-know old timer). Handle that with very much care as your father did. Only that casing shows the typical B&K quality (:) and related prices). I've looked back in my old documentation and it doesn't appear anymore in catalogues from 1994. Hence it must be significant older (must check old archives = too much work) :) If it was mine (:) I wished it was) I should put it in a glass cabinet as a decorative show item (outside light to prevent UV discoloration on casing), and search for the exact history. For me that thing is worth much more that way (except maybe for parts as e.g. the calibrator) than for it's nowadays very limited functionality. I rather should buy a radioshack for standard use in studios, and put that one away for safe keeping (In that 'close to new' condition it's acoustic history, a pity you should damage or scratch it, or warn that casing out). 5) :mrgreen: Back then in the old days, that brand was so dominant in the pro-world, that in the industry, using it, made you an expert, even when you had no idea what a dB was. Just having/using it was enough. You just humbly mumbled silently that you used B&K equipment and you were taken very seriously. Hence your topic title as also Andre confirmed: Is this a good Db meter? (it's dB) They only made "top of the line" equipment, but also sold it at "top of the line" prices. At around 1991/92 they decreased lots/most prices suddenly with around 25%, since they got too much trouble/competition with new stuff (other pro-brands) that established in that market.
