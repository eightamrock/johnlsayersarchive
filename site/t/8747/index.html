Are floating walls necessary on separate slabs?

Started by s2dorat on 7 May 2007. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 8747.

s2dorat7 May 2007#1
Situation: Med-large facility, rural - no noise issues, music recording Control room will be around 400 sq' and around 5000 cu'. I have a couple of .skp shots posted at: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8743 I'm building from the ground up and pouring separate slabs for each room. I am also using double wall construction for each room, so I should have truly unconnected spaces. With that in mind, is it still necessary to isolate the walls from the slab (rubber footings, foam rods, etc...)? Would there be a benefit to NOT isolating and having a more rigid structure? Also, I am planning to use a sand-filled raised floor in the control room, but not a "floating" floor. In other words, the floor will be raised, but will not bear the weight of the room. The weight of the walls will be directly on the slab. Comments? This is an incredible forum and I thank you all for your help!
sharward8 May 2007#2
Yeah, I would think you'd be fine with that -- separate slabs and separate frames, no attempt to actually float anything. The exception would be if you had impact noise close by, such as rumbling 18-wheeler trucks or trains. --Keith :mrgreen:
