Goodmorning forum! We are in the process of designing an echo chamber for our studio Montrose Recording in Richmond, VA. So far we have decided that a covered, unused space in the barn near the studio would be the best place to put the chamber as it 1) already has a concrete slab poured, 2) is covered from the elements, and 3) is in a quiet environment (other than the tin barn roof!). The space we're looking to construct on is 19' by 11'6 with a ceiling that slopes from 8' to 14' (see attached drawings for clarification). Realistically, the chamber will have to be slightly smaller than this to accomodate for the wall construction. Currently we are leaning towards an outer shell with staggered wall construction and an inner shell with a standard interior one as per Scott Putnam's REP article (http://www.prosoundweb.com/article/prin ... ho_chamber). My biggest confusion and what I'm primarily hoping to get some clarity on here is how to apply the Sabin ratio (2:3:5 by my understanding) to the real world dimensions of a chamber style construction with no parallel walls, or floor and ceiling? Is it done on an average? I have tried this formula several times with no conclusive luck. Any help or insight would be especially appreciated. And please let me know if any clarity is needed to be given. Our budget is a loose $2,000. Adrian Olsen Note on "chamber scan1", the braces are non essential and will be able to be removed..

Soundman202019 February 2012#9
to the real world dimensions of a chamber style construction with no parallel walls, or floor and ceiling?
Just adding my US$ 0.01 (don't think it rises to the level of 0.02!). I'm not convinced that non-parallel would be the best way to go. Sure, it might spread the modes around a bit more, perhaps evening out the spread, but they'll still be there, ans still be problematic, coloring the "signature" of your chamber. Especially the tangentials and obliques. So predicting the resonant behavior becomes a job for FEM/FEA, and can no longer be accomplished with the simple tools and simple equations: Wouldn't it be better to keep it more or less rectangular, and adjust the dimensions for a good ratio? Yeah, Everest does say you can average to a certain extent for low angles, but Eric Dessart (I thin kit was Eric, but I might be wrong) has pointed out that nobody really knows much about that, or how large/small the angle has to be before averaging is no longer valid and FEM/FEA becomes the only realistic option. I've also heard of using movable barriers in chambers, to change the "sound", but can't find any info on that. Have you considered going down that path? Maybe some kind of massive, reflective gobo-style "wall on wheels" that could be moved around, to change the character? Maybe also install movable transducers, or multiple transducers on a patch bay.... Just some wild, off-the-wall ideas for you. - Stuart -
