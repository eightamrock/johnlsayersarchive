Soundman20206 July 2010#5
Hi Peter, Please also re-size your drawings to conform to forum rules. They are too big right now. Also, it would help if you were to explain what it is you want to do in there, what your goals are, what your budget is, etc. Folks here will gladly help you navigate that "minefield" that you speak of, but first it would help if we know where abouts you are right now in the minefield, and where you want to get to! :) It looks a bit overwhelming when you start out, but once you start getting your head around the basic principles, it all starts to make sense, and the piece start falling into place. For instance: is this going to be a commercial space, or just a hobby space? What kind of music are you going to record in there? (There's a big difference between recording a single harmonica, and recording the entire philharmonic!) How loud are you (in terms of decibels)? How loud are people around you? How quiet does their noise have to be inside your rooms (again, in terms of decibels)? How quiet does your noise have to be in their space? What other noises sources are around you (airports, roads, railway lines, people, machinery, etc.) All of that factors in to the amount of isolation that you are going to need around your room to control noise going in and out. Then, it matters what kind of work you do: If you plan to track, mix and master for major Top-Twenty groups, then that's a bit different than just making CD's for your garage band or your friends. Etc. The more info you can give people here, the more they can help you to design what you need in order to get there. - Stuart -
