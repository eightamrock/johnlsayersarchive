vinnie2k wrote:
In terms of flooring, I read that wooden floors are usually good, is that true? If you are in a basement (it reads you are) often this is enough if the basement is mostly underground, damped by the Earth. That is a matter of "isolation" and how much you require weather or not you would want to consider a room in a room type design for higher isolation purposes. Since this is a not yet built room, you really have to get the room built before anything can be suggested, since the actual configuration of the room for any isolation needs and treatments of a finished room are dictated by the actual room. Wood floors are nice looking and lots of folks use them. In a basement you would have to have a weather tight room, no anticipated leaking or water infiltration or the flooring will eventually, in short order, de-laminate from the floor since it would be adhered directly to the concrete. Wood flooring has no magical properties. It looks nice but sonically it is about as bright acoustically as a slick concrete floor or commercial vinyl. But, concrete damped by the earth is a very good isolator of sound. And a thick floor 100-150 mm) is better than a thin floor. Stained concrete looks very nice and several here have done this with much success

I'm wondering if the 38% rule is cast in stone
It's not written in stone, no. It's just a guideline, but it's a good guideline.
I could move my speakers (and then my head position) closer to the back wall a bit more?
I would not do that. The room is not big enough that you could get the speakers far enough away from the wall to avoid comb filtering and other problems. I would keep the speakers where they are, and build the wall where it is supposed to be, in order to get symmetry for the front of the room. Also, if you move your speakers and mix position away from the front wall, you will have your head in the center of the room, which is a a bad position, and the new side wall would have to be longer, not shorter.... - Stuart -
BriHar20 December 2011#20
I concur with Stuart. The first order of business is to get that partition up, then you will have the necessary symmetry to proceed. The next step will then be to measure your room sonically using a mic and software such as REW. This will then help determine where the optimum position of LP and your monitors will be. It will also help in determining the amount and positioning of treatments you will need. Until this step, don't worry about positioning, the 38% is just a typical design parameter and analytically the best starting point.
