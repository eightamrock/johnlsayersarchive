Hi, First: congratulations! I was in your situation about a year ago and am still going.... :roll: Second:your plans... There's no point in framing the eastern wall if you're not framing any other of the outer walls because it won't give you any isolation framing just one wall. sharward2 December 2005#9
jal wrote:
. . . ur saying every room has to have fresh air ventilation in it? i mean some rooms in my house dont have windows or ventilation. . . .
Not all rooms in a house are habitable rooms though. By definition, habitable space is space in a structure for living, sleeping, eating or cooking. Bathrooms, toilet compartments, closets, halls, storage or utility space, and similar areas are not considered habitable space. Here's a document published by the City of Boise about natural light and ventilation requirements, according to the model codes they use in that municipality. It does a good job of explaining in simple English what ventilation is all about. I would caution you, though, against using this document as gospel, because in all likelihood your municipality uses different model codes, so the requirements may be different -- possibly more strict. Your best bet would be to pay your local building department a visit for assistance with this. They're the ones to enforce code, so they're the ones who are in the best position to advise you on the particulars. Show them pictures of what you have so far, show them your plans, tell them that you need to build rooms without windows, and ask them what you'll have to do in order to meet code. (Frankly, I'm surprised you were granted your permit without having to present plans demonstrating all of these details! :shock: )

Jal is referring to a private message conversation we had on this subject... The HRV (short for "heat recovery ventilator" is not a substitute for air conditioning. In my personal case, I plan to use both an HRV for ventilation purposes and an air conditioning system (specifically, a mini split) for comfort and dehumidification purposes (although a standalone dehumidifier may also be in order). In short, air conditoner for comfort, and HRV for window substitution. Hope that helps! --Keith :mrgreen:
