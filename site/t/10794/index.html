Hi there, my room is 6,63 m long, 4,54 m wide and 2,25 high, what i know, is not really perfect but I have to handle this. An issue of this room is, that it is in basement and I have to leave some space (around 20-30 cm) in one corner because of moisture so that the air can transport it away from the corner. Unfortunately in the rear corners are the door and an other window, so that I can not treat here anything. Cieling is painted concrete, floor is glazed tiled, walls are paperhanged. Windows, door and radiators can be watched in the pictures. I have built some basstraps with 45kg/m^3 glass wool, each pack around 1 m long and 40x40 cm. You can not see them in the pictures, but they are installed in the corner near the windows. I used them in my old room and they worked fantastic. In addition I have built some angeled walls to break the parallels in the listening zone. Propably I will do so on the rear wall, too. At the ceiling I have put 4" glass wool above the listening zone to avoid reflections. As you can see in the frequency-plots the simple bass treatment with the traps are doing something well, but I still have some heavy resonances. I would be happy if someone can give me some hints about my room. I can not built new walls, because it is rented!! Main Question: The most important thing is to flaten the frequency response and get rid of the nulls and peaks. I plan to build panel-absorbers for this. I have experience with that, but always build them directly on the wall. Now I would like to build them free standing at (!) the wall like a closet. Is 16mm MDF thick enough for the corpus? Best Regards, Studiotools
