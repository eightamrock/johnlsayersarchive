sharward1 August 2007#29
Try this first. I had a couple of forms that were difficult to remove, and this is a variation of what worked for me. Drill four 3/8" holes all in a row into a piece of 2x4 -- two pairs, each pair two inches apart, with about six inches between the pairs. Drill four 3/8" holes into the form board close to an end, using the 2x4 piece as a template. Insert four 1/2" x 6" (or so) long lag bolts and washers into the 2x4 piece, then through to the form board, allowing a generous gap between the 2x4 piece and the form board. Place a couple pieces of scrap 2x4 or a scrap piece of 4x4 on the old concrete in the doorway as a fulcrum. Insert a long steel prybar in the gap between the form board and the scrap piece in the area between the two pairs of bolts. Rest the prybar on the fulcrum, then push down on the bar, which will (hopefully) raise the form board without yanking the lag bolts out of their holes. The reason I suggest using four bolts instead of two is that the more bolts you have, the less pressure each bolt will be carrying, thus reducing the likelihood of pullout. If it fails, try again with six or even eight bolts. The kerosene idea is a good one, as long as one is careful. Hopefully that fuel won't cause too high of a flame. Maybe experiment with a small area first...? :roll: --Keith :mrgreen:
