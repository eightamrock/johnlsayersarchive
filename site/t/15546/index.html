First, please do what John said. But a couple of comments are in order:
they prepare the whole sound insulation design
I think you mean "isolation" not "insulation". It might seem like a minor point, but precision in using the right term is actually very important, and can save you a lot of heartache later! Insulation is pink fluffy fiberglass or mineral wool. Isolation means to stop sound getting in and out, and is also incorrectly called "soundproofing".
... based on 3 concrete floating floors standing on elastic pads(Silomer).
:shock: While that will certainly work (if it is done correctly), it sounds like way overkill for what you are trying to do, and I sure hope you have a LOT of money to do it! Take a look at these to get an idea of what that involves: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 http://www.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/obj/irc/doc/p ... /ir802.pdf
For the walls they recommended us 2 gypsumboards of the same size(12,5mm)
Bad idea: 12.5 mm too thin, too flexible, resonant frequency is too high, mass is too low. Use only 16mm. But I don't get this: they want you to float concrete floors, but then only use 12.5mm drywall???? Huh? :shock: Makes no sense. Major big-time expense on the floors, but then flimsy low-isolation partitions for the walls???
I must tell you that we already have finished the 3 floors and they look great but.....
so that must have set you back many tens of thousands of dollars already! Wow! Pity you didn't come here first: you probably could have saved a LOT of money. Maybe you could post some photos of how those floor were floated?
For example:shouldn't the 2 gypsumboards to be of different size,one 16mm and one 12,5mm?
They should BOTH be 16mm. 12.5 is too thin, for the reasons mentioned above.
Are 3 gypsumboards more effective than 2
Yes!
I read in a topic here that 2 are more effective for the dreadful low frequencies while 3 are suitable for the high freq.
Actually, it's the other way around: 2 is probably enough for highs, but 3 is better if you have low frequency problems as well.
