Soundman202022 May 2016#2
Hi there "javamad", and Welcome! :)
I am attaching a photo of one unit. It is 71 square meters and completely new build.
That would be a fantastic space for a studio!
My hair-brained idea is to start small and build a mix area using as little materials as possible
I would start out by first checking the sound levels inside. "light industry park" to me implies noise: At the very least, cars and trucks arriving/leaving, plus weather noises, such as rain, wind, hail, thunder, etc., plus people talking outside, radios, phones, aircraft flying over... all of that can trash your mixing sessions, firstly due to distraction, and secondly due to masking the subtle sounds that you are trying to hear in your mixes. In other words, I would suggest that you think about isolation.
One idea is to build a control room first. Then as I get some more funds I will build out the live room.
That makes sense, as it would allow you to both isolate and treat your room at the same time.
The second idea is to just drop my mixing gear into the unit with minimal installation.
Perhaps not such a good idea... That room is over 70m2. ITU specs for critical listening rooms suggest a size of not less than 20m2 and not more than 60m2. They also suggest that a certain range for the overall decay time for the room, based around a reference volume of 100 m3. Your volume is over 400 m3. Getting the correct reverb time in such a large space is going to require some interesting acoustic treatment! In other words, that room is probably too large to use as a control room.
Will I need to build any sound isolation into my partitioning of the control room to get low ambient noise?
Absolutely, totally, completely, and definitely! The control room will need to isolate your room from the outside world, and also from the future live room. Both rooms must be designed together now to achieve that, even if you only build one of them.
2. If (for cost reasons) I was to start by just placing my mix set up in the center of the unit, with no partitions at all, would the walls be far enough away to have a no reflection zone
No. The volume is just too great, that is a highly reverberant space anyway (all surfaces are hard, solid, smooth and reflective), and even though you might not have high levels of first reflections, the overall reverberant field will be overpowering, with very long decay times. Treating that room as a control room would be complicated, and expensive. It would be possible, yes, but the cost and complexity are not justifiable.
Would some standalone broadband absorbers around me work?
Nope! :) Sorry. Your first option is a much better solution: Design and build a proper control room with suitable isolation and treatment, and design it along with the live room at the same time, even if the live room won't be built for now. - Stuart -
