knightfly5 June 2004#3
Just a couple - For that kind of isolation, be sure to keep at least 8" air/insulation gap between outer leaf (the osb and siding) and inner leaf - if you float your inner walls on the individual floors, watch having enough support around the perimeter of each floor to support the extra wall weight - look for EPDM instead of neoprene, longevity is 2-3 times as good. Insulation - be careful here - heat insulation isn't always sound insulation, stay away from ANY foam based stuff. Best density is around 2 to 2.5 PCF for in-wall insulation for lower frequencies - higher density lessens LF performance, lower density lessens HF performance. YOu want a snug fill if possible, or at least have the batts contacting the inside of the wallboard in each stud bay - this damps the panel, which improves TL. I'm hoping one of those "windows" into the CR is really a door, otherwise entrance/exit could get tricky without a chainsaw... Other than that, looks like you're off to a great start - not seeing any "triple leaf" problems other than the lounge, pretty minor... Steve

dymaxian7 June 2004#7
I know this has been discussed before, but I need to ask again...Should I sheetrock the shell's walls and ceiling?
Well, that depends. AVare is right about a triple leaf system being a thing to avoid. BUT Your roof deck, for example, will PROBABLY NOT COUNT AS A LEAF. If your roof is going to be flat, and made of precast concrete or something, then ignore what I'm saying. But if it's going to be wood trusses, with plywood sheathing over the top of it, and roofing paper and shingles atop that, then it will most likely be VENTED and will leak sound if you use it as an acoustic 'leaf'. The soffits of these roofs are vented, and the insulation is placed at the bottom of the trusses so that the attic space is not heated. The reason for this is very good (deals with water leakage in the winter) but it's also a long explanation so I won't bore you with it if you're not interested. My point is that at least on the roof, you will not want to use the roof deck as a 'leaf' and will therefore most likely want to drywall the ceiling. Talk to your builder (if you're not doing this yourself) about this and see what he thinks. He (or you) may have a totally different idea on how to build the roof that will render my argument invalid. If you'll be building up the outer shell walls very solidly (MDF and a layer or 2 of exterior drywall) then drywalling the inside of the shell walls will not be necessary, but in this case you should watch the builders like a HAWK to make sure they don't punch any holes in it. BTW if you're looking for that kind of isolation, I'm suprised your outer shell won't be concrete block or something along those lines... do you still have time to change gears for that type of construction? Just thinking out loud...
