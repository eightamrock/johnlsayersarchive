One word answer for all that. POPPYCOCK! I am a licensed builder and I see it all the time. Building codes are generally a good thing.....they generally help save the consumer from unscupulous contractors etc...but there is as much fat and red tape in them as anything. IN MY OPINION Sharward, you take them to the enth degree. This is one of those cases. I have worked 16 hour sessions with musicians stuck in a 10 x 8 booth with no ventilation, and aside from the occasional "man it's hot in here", I have never seen single one drop dead, pass out, complain they can't breath, etc.. Am I saying fresh air is not a consideration? NOT AT ALL. But I am saying in the thousands of things you need to consider in building a studio, it is not even on the first 5 pages on priorities. Then I got the pro in to do the second layer on the iso wall and to caulk the gaps and re do the ceiling. Question What should he do about the original gap ?? When you say it isn't tooled properley , i presume you are advocating the "wet finger, deeper on the edges, thin in the middle " tooling ?
Nymo, I don't know what to do about a gapo that big except for to cut back the drywall and fill it back in with the proper gap. The reason we say a caulk joint of 1/4 to 3/8" max is bacause with 5/8" drywall that is the largest gap you can get backer rod to stay in and still have room for caulk - when I look at the joint in the picture I can't even imagine how there is backer rod behind it....... When I say tooling" I mean that the caulk has to be firmly compressed into the material it is sealing to. It should feather-edge down to literally nothing at it's outer edges...... I can see that at th ceiling - but I donot see that paper thin leading edge at the bottom of the joint. Sincerely, Rod
