kendale23 October 2005#36
Found this on Google search: Flanking: The transmission of sound around the perimeter or through holes within partitions (or barriers) that reduces the otherwise obtainable sound transmission loss of a partition. Examples of flanking paths within buildings are ceiling plena above partitions; ductwork, piping, and electrical conduit penetrations through partitions; back-to-back electrical boxes within partitions, window mullions, etc. Hope this helps.

guitardad722 October 2006#49
I was always under the impression that you'd had to caulk all the layers. So what I understand, you "mud" all the layers except the last one, which you caulk, right?
Perimeter of all layers is caulk (well stuff with backer rod then caulk). That would include all corners: wall to wall, wall to ceiling and wall to floor. All seams of drywall in a single layer of is specifically: Mud (drywall joint compound), drywall tape and more mud (all applied at same time)... then sand to make flat for next drywall layer OR mud & sand 3 times if it's the finish layer. Only exception is finish corners for appearance sake can be stuffed with backer rod, caulked (raked back a bit for), mud, tape and more mud so your left with a good looking corner to paint. See Rod's comments on this in the following... In reference section the 3 links to caulking threads: REFERENCE AREA - Useful threads/links Note Sharward has decided to caulk a few of his drywall seams instead of mud... the recommendation from all I've read is mud, however I could see an argument that caulk in a seam may fill the seam better and is more dense which might make is better for isolation over all. :roll: Also in regards to:
Marc, I know about the orientation of the speakers, but the confusing thing is that I've read the opposite (on other forums though) too. The more you read and learn, the more questions you have. I considered the diamond shape, but takes away ALOT of space.
I know if John or Steve were to kick in here they would say face the short wall. Yes larger CR's can face the long wall but the recommendation in a room your size (and mine) is to face the short wall. Square footage lost to splaying with a diamond shape would be less then that lost to splaying side walls entirely one way. The wide part of the room would be in the middle and not the back making it feel bigger. Splay is 6 degrees per wall, 12 degrees off making them parallel or 1 foot in 10. Let's say the long walls are 10 feet long for an easy number to work with and see attached pic. I AM NOT saying this is the best thing for acoustics in CR alone with regards to reflections, but it would definitely break the parallel walls up and max out square footage, especially for a single room studio. I've seen a few of johns designs namely "paddy2" and "studio in a container" where the room shape is similar to the "diamond" but the splay comes from a slot resonator.
