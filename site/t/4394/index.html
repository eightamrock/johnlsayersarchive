sharward3 October 2005#7
I'm not sure that your floor is actually floated. :? It doesn't look like it in that image. Are you 100% sure you want and need to float your floor(s)? Please explain. It is a fact that many floating floors are constructed to make isolation worse than if they were not floated. That's not to say that all floating floors are bad -- sometimes they are good, and sometimes they are necessary. But when you don't have people or equipment in the space below the studio that you need to isolate from, floating is often unnecessary, increases costs dramatically, and sometimes makes matters worse than if the floor were not floated. --Keith :mrgreen:

knightfly17 October 2005#20
OK, outer walls up and mostly rendered; looking pretty good so far. I'm hoping your inner concrete block are solid, not hollow? When raising this inner wall, be extra careful NOT to spill any mortar between inner and outer wall - you want complete isolation there. Any connections between the two should only be isolators, if needed for sway bracing. These can be either lag bolted (with lead shields) into the masonry or mortared in as you go, as in when building the inner wall. Are you planning to use any insulation between inner and outer walls? If so, what kind? Do you know how to fix the batts in place on masonry walls? I'll try to get to your questions soon enough, but don't always have the option... Steve

If you're doing an inner stud frame, insulation's easy; look for fiberglass strapping, typically used to tie large cardboard boxes for shipping and to strap loads of materials down to shipping pallets - you can fasten this to the back side of studs every foot or so horizontally, then stuff the cavities with insulation and put up the wallboard... knightfly20 October 2005#25
Ceilings/roofs are a bitch for sound isolation; make sure that whatever you do will provide a mass-air-mass system similar to your wall construction if possible; you want a continuous outer mass, a continuous air space, and a continuous inner mass surrounding your space. Anything less is a compromise and may disappoint. If you can post some details on the new roof plan, please do... Steve
