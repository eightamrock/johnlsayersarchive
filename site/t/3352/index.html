ceiling treatment without losing too much height

Schroeder7 March 2005#1
Hi everybody! For 2 months I`m already planning our new basement studio and I`ve learned a lot in this amazing forum!! Thanks alot, guys! Our control room (12,9 x 13 x 6.8ft) will be constructed on the basis of an RFZ design. (Multiple) Splayed walls, absorption on the back wall, no early reflections, etc. The room is very small and almost square (arrgggh), but we are doing our best to fight normal modes problems. We will use tuned Helmholtz-, panel- and slot-absorbers to deal with that. My question: While the horizontal design is almost perfect, we have problems concerning the design of the ceiling-treatment. The standard advice for treating such low ceilings is to make it as absorbtive as possible, mostly with 2"-4" 703. But we would lose too much height when applying so much 703 (my partner is quite tall ;-) . An idea was to use a thin layer of 703 (1/2"-1") in combination with a well calculated area of slot-absorbers with mininum depth (should be as effective towards "lower" frequencies as a thicker layer of 703, shouldn`t it?) to deal with the mids (low to high). I think this should be fine, because the main problem (concerning reflections between parallels) between ceiling and floor are the mids, because the high frequencing usually don`t reach the ceiling with so much intensity, `cause they are radiating from the speaker almost horizontally and only the lower frequencies, such as low mids, radiate more directly towards the ceiling. Am I right? Please help me with my basement ceiling! Everythings perfect until now, so I don`t want to lose so much sound quality because of poor or wrong ceiling treatment. Please help, thank you very much! Best wishes, Schroeder :P
Schroeder9 March 2005#2
I`ve forgotten a detail in my description: The floor is covered with laminate. Please help! :-] Thank you!
knightfly10 March 2005#3
Schroeder, welcome to the board; I think that, under your circumstances you are on the right track - here's about as good as I can do for sizes, this would lower your ceiling a total of 2", or 50mm, and should be run so that the slats go sideways (left to right as you face your speakers) - you should do a complete (but not STUFFED) fill of insulation - if you can spare the height, 2" insulation and 2" furring strips would be better, then you could widen the slots a bit (easler construction) HTH... Steve

knightfly11 March 2005#7
OK, first you can convert meters to feet by multiplying meters by 3.28 - that puts your dimensions in feet at 12.8 feet, 12.7 feet, 6.79 feet (using average height) - this puts lower axial modes at 44.1 44.5 83.2 88.3 89.0 132.4 133.5 166.4 176.6 178.0 220.7 222.4 249.6 264.8 266.9 309.0 However, when you factor in tangential and oblique modes the only area where your room has modal balance problems would be a LACK of modes around the 40-60 hZ region - for that, normally just broadband corner trapping (including wall-ceiling corners) makes more sense... Steve

My idea was to deal with the modes individually because they are likely very strong (doubled in intensity because of two similar dimensions, and even tripled in the "80s"Hz). 2 Helmholtz absorbers (with filling, moderate q) to deal with the 44Hz in two corners, another 2 Helmholtz absorbers (with filling, moderate q), and 2 panel absorber on the side wall near the ceiling for the "80s" Hz (also not too high q). Everything above the "80s" I wanted to treat with some broadband absorption (backwall) and the slat absorbers on the ceiling/sidewalls (splayed). By building tuned absorbers for the modes which are "doubled" and two more for those who are "tripled", I thought I could get a quite linear response in the control room.
