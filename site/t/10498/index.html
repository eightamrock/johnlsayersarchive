Hey there. Long time lurker, first time poster... Great forum and I'm really glad there's a place on the internet where all this acoustic knowledge can be pooled and available to people like me to learn... Anyway, here's my issue: The long term plan is, of course, to be able to dedicate a room to my studio, but from September I'm moving back in with my parents for a year or so as rent is getting a little ridiculous in the UK housing market at the moment, and there's no way me and my partner can afford to get our foot on the property ladder at the moment. That means I've got a bedroom... and not an enormous one at that. The room has sloping corners at the ceilings, and is a very shallow L shape (as you can see from the sketchup models). The double bed in there is fairly essential, as the midi controller doesn't make too comfortable a pillow. So what would people advise to try my best to treat the room? I'm thinking a cloud, the side absorbers from John's 'DIY wall units' (both included in the models). On the back wall I think I'll have to make do with shelving filled with books (and I need that storage anyway), but bass traps are the problem... I can do the corners behind the monitors but the only available corner at the back of the room is the top corner on the near (window) wall... otherwise there's a door and a bed in the way... Help! As for dimensions, the room is essentially 450x240x270cm (L x W x H) with the extra corner where the door is as a 175x35x270cm box added on. As far as I can work out, the figure in the model is at the 35% mark and the monitors are positioned as well as I could manage. Many thanks for help in advance, Guis.

Okay, then your current design will work. Not much alternatives I reckon. You've gotta work with acoustics to get things "right". Focus on absorption. There more than 4 corners in your room so make use of that. Here's a handy link to handy ppl making handy absorpers: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5930 Replace the shelfs above your bed for some thick absorption panels (10cm / 4") and create a (near) RFZ and you'll be on your way to be a mix-god 8)
