Ethan Winer23 April 2007#3
cchord59 wrote:
so I have a few Ideas, but I would love to hear yours.
Repeat after me: More bass traps. More bass traps. More bass traps. :) Seriously, this is your only viable option. But trap placement matters too, especially if you have a build up at a specific place and frequency. One great way to find the best place for more traps is to play bass-heavy pink noise through your speakers, then walk around and climb up on a ladder etc listening for where the bass is loudest. That's where more traps should go. (Or use an SPL meter or a microphone connected to a recorder and VU meter.) It so happens I have exactly the noise source you need here: http://www.realtraps.com/lf-noise.htm Also experiment with speaker placement and listening at different spots in the room. These are free to try, and can often make a big difference. --Ethan
lovecow23 April 2007#4
cchord59 wrote:
So the build up is about 5 feet from the front wall right where I am always sitting, and its becoming very annoying.
Sounds like you're sitting at a boundary interference peak from the front wall. (Which could be exacerbated by the presence of several modes at this frequency.) Moving your speakers and listening position back from the front wall might help, but could also move the problem to another (equally annoying) frequency. Regardless, it's probably the best thing to try initially since it doesn't cost you anything. If that doesn't make it better, you might try beefing up the front wall absorption - either pull the 2" material away from the wall 2", or - better - make the front wall treatment about 6"+ deep instead. (I.e., add two more 2" layers over the top of what you have.) You might also try this over a wider area than what's shown in the "front wall" pic, making sure the treatment area overlaps the rear of each loudspeaker by a foot or two in each direction. Make sense? :)
