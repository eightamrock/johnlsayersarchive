Aaronw28 July 2004#5
The 3 I mentioned above, I've used. Basically I used what they had in stock at the time. When I first bought some, I bought the Boss, but was paying $5 a tube for it. When I went over to another supplier where I buy my Roxul, they carried the Grabber & Titebond. I paid only $3.25 a tube when I bought a case of 12 tubes. Some of these also carry a fire rating, so depending on local codes (if your building a commercial room), may depend on what you have to use. They're for the most part, pretty much the same thing. The Boss seemed to dry up and shrink a little bit, so I had to go back around and fill in some spots. I didn't have that much of a problem w/ the Grabber or Titebond, but I've used only a couple tubes of the Titebond so far. It's all easy cleanup though. No oily silicone stuff. Aaron

knightfly29 July 2004#10
Z, with the exception of the Boss stuff, it sounds like any of the acoustic sealants would work fine and not cause problems later - that's supposed to be one of the criteria, since there is quite a bit of construction that can NOT be accessed once completed. There do seem to be some differences in usable temperature ranges that may narrow down choices a bit - for example, the OSI stuff seems to have a wider temp range than the USG - here's the link to their spec sheet http://www.osiliterature.com/IMAGE/PDF/sO11175.PDF Also, if you read through the great bunch of links you've assembled you'll note that some products say they are for UN-exposed use, others say for either unexposed or exposed. I'm not positive, but I think this may be relative to whether or not the product forms a "skin" - also note in some of the literature that it makes a HUGE difference whether you use a single or double bead; I saw one instance where they spec a 10 dB difference between the two. This just backs up all our statements about caulking heavy. From all this, my first choice would be the OSI, partly because of the wider temperature range - followed by any of the others, except the Boss - if even ONE person has problems with it, it's off MY list... Aaronw29 July 2004#14
Aaron as far as the mineral or rock wool... Would you need any protective clothing to install this stuff?
Check out this sticky on insulation: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1742 Yes, I would recommend wearing long pants, long sleeves, and either a dust mask or respirator if you have one and gloves & goggles. Almost forgot...a little trick when getting ready to put up insulation...cover any exposed skin w/ Baby Powder. I know this sounds strange, but it helps keep the fibers from sticking into your skin. It's not too bad if you're installing in the wall. In fact, I was wearing shorts when I did the walls. BUT, when I did the insulation over my head in the joists, wear the long clothes and eyeprotection and mask. That stuff starts falling, and gets in the air and can be pretty nasty.
Also is there any other recommended fiberglass besides 703.
There are a couple of others. I believe Knaupf (I think I spelled it right) has a rigid fiberglass w/ similar ratings, and there's another, but I'm having brain fart and can't remember the name. :D Aaron
