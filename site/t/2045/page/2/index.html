knightfly29 July 2005#43
Within walls, there doesn't seem to be more than a dB or two difference between rockwool, mineral wool, rigid fiberglass or the "fluffy stuff", with a few cautions - this assumes that the cavities are mostly full, or (in the case of the softer, "fluffy stuff" insulation) slightly overfilled - letting the insulation touch the wallboard surface tends to help damp the panels, which improves wall performance. IF you use OVERLY dense rigid board insulation, you run the risk of problems - one, a compression fill will "short out" the wall and lower performance. Two, if you DON'T fill the cavities, the rigid board can act like a third leaf (bad) especially if it's denser than about 3 PCF (48 kG/m^3) - this shows up as slightly better TL in mids/highs, but a few dB WORSE TL at low frequencies. NONE of this applies to the FOAM board insulations you see at Home Depot; these are all CLOSED CELL and are NOT usable in sound construction. If you have any of this stuff already in place, it either needs to be glued to one of your mass leaves while still leaving an air gap between leaves, or it needs to be REMOVED. Preferably removed. Facing on standard fiberglass batts - for EXTERIOR walls, you need a vapor barrier - in climates where cooling is more prevalent (like Florida) this barrier needs to be nearest the OUTSIDE wall surface, so the paper would go out - in cooler climates where HEATING is more prevalent, the barrier goes to the INSIDE surface. For INTERIOR walls, no vapor barrier is needed - so batts should be UN-faced for these walls. If you MUST use faced batts, put the paper against one wallboard surface or the other - but do NOT put TWO batts in a wall that BOTH have paper, and do NOT put paper ANYWHERE but against the wallboard - either will create somewhat of a THIRD LEAF, which may improve wall performance at MID range but WORSEN it at low frequency. If using any of the rigid board insulations (rockwool, mineral wool, rigid fiberglass) and all you can get is the FRK types (membrane glued to one side) this is best removed for IN-wall use, except for the earlier mentioned EXTERIOR walls... Steve
