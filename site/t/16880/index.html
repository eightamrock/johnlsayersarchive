beau26 August 2011#2
Hi, Unfortunately, I am not one of the people that can be of most help to you with your design. However, I think you should consider showing the following in your sketchup file. 1. where all of your windows are 2. where the street is 3. what sides the closest neighbors are 4. measurements for all of the rooms, including ceiling height 5. what are your existing walls made of now? drywall? concrete? brick? etc. Then, I know sometimes it helps if you go outside and measure the noise on the street, and if you can, find out what your local noise laws are, post them here. This way it will let the people here have a better idea how to help you. For instance, if your drum and amp room is on the street side, that room will require significantly more isolation than if your sound lock was on the street side. The more info you give in the beginning, the much more the nice people on here can help you. Then, after all of that, please upload your sketchup file as an attachment. It was a real pain in the rear to wait 60 seconds to download the file, enter the code, only to find out i typed the wrong code, and then have to wait another 60 seconds to enter a different code. By uploading it as an attachment to the forum, you will make things much easier and quicker for people trying to help you. Looking forward to seeing what happens here, it looks like you have a lot of potential. Beau

Soundman202028 August 2011#6
Don't really know why it was so huge in the first place but oh well.
SketchUp is quirky like that. When you delete an object, it doesn't actually remove it from the file until you purge it :shock: so files can get pretty big as you try things out and then deleted them. 3D text also takes up a LOT of space. Regarding the second design, I reckon I'd switch around the control room (CR) and live room (LR) in your design: put the CR where the LR is, and vice versa. The CR would be oriented with the door to the sound lock at the rear, and the outside window to the front. The window (door? :) ) to the LR would be on the right. That would accomplish lots of things together: Good symmetry for the CR, which is absolutely critical for a good CR, but most likely not wanted at all in the LR. It would give you space to soffit mount your main monitors. It would give you space for bass trapping in the rear corners of the CR (which you cannot do at all with the current layout). And it would leave the bay window area available as a small machine room / storage closet / mini-iso booth. It would give you a nice non-symmetric shape for the LR. Etc. Another comment: You have lots of doors and windows in there! That's great, but what about your budget? Doing doors and windows right for high degrees of isolation is not cheap: thick glass, heavy solid-core doors, multiple seals, etc. If you have the budget, then fine! If money is tight, then you might want to re-think your door/window plan. One final idea: if you do it right, your sound lock could do double duty as an extra iso booth... :) - Stuart -
