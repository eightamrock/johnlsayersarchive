dahnielson16 June 2007#1
Hi everybody, here's the planned floor plan for my home/bedroom studio. I'm living in a rented single room apartment that leave me no choice in terms of what room to use, since neither the kitchen nor the bathroom is ideal for making music. Still, I feel this is somewhat the room from hell, acoustically speaking... It's almost square (4396x4480mm), the only thing it almost got going for it is the ceiling height (2490mm) not making it a cube. The room lack symmetry and is hard to layout without erecting false walls and such. Another constraint is the fact I'm supposed to use the room as an office and sleeping area in addition to a music studio. Furniture: On the floor plan you will see my current furniture and its placement in the "rear" living area (from the studio point of view). I figured that if I put all my bookshelf (almost reaching the ceiling) against the back wall it would at least help to absorb low frequencies due to its mass (in addition to being out of the way). Recording: We primarily record "over the wire": samplers, synths, electric guitar and bas via amp+cabinet emulators. Only vocals and acoustic guitar will be recorded live in the room. So the treatment is primarily for monitoring. But I'm planning on building a temporary "booth" using my regular panels while recording vocals and possibly building some dedicated free standing panels for that purpose later on. Equipment: Most of my equipment will be rack mounted in a desk I'm currently building (10U of easy access to modules and processors). The monitors (ESI nEar05) will be placed on rigid stands I intend to build (whose top will be made out of a slab of gardening stone). Next to the desk is a Roland HP-piano and a JX-10. Doors: The room has two doorways, the one to the right leads to my balcony, while the other (currently sans door) leads to a small hall. Walls: The front wall and the upper half of the left wall are single gypsum board on studs. The back, right and lower left wall are brick and mortar (me thinks, it's hard and at least the house facade is brick and mortar). I've got a big window with drapes on the right wall. All the walls are covered with wallpaper. Floor: Vinyl carpet on concrete (I think?) Ceiling: Painted concrete (I think?) Treatment: Planned acoustic panels are colored green on the floor plan, solid green being hung vertically against the wall and hatched green being hung angled from the ceiling. The big problem is treatment of the doorway that end up almost in the middle of the early reflections zone. I'm planning on building 2x4' panel absorbers using 3" of Roxul (RW40/RHF80/AFB) or similar isolation hung 3" from the wall. Not showed in the plan, but I'm thinking of mounting triangular pieces of isolation to all corners of the ceiling. Questions: * Vertical placement of monitors: According to my measurements my ears are 1300mm above the floor when sitting in the listening position, is that too equal in distance from floor and ceiling? Mounting the monitors straight would make the stands 1130mm high and the woofer center ~1225mm above the floor. Or should I make the stands slightly higher and tilt the monitors downwards? * Doorway: How to treat the doorway? To keep the symmetry at least half the doorway should be treated just like the right side wall. One idea is to build a simple frame to mount the panel on so it can be hung on the door hinges still in place (I've still got the old door stored in the cellar that I can plunder remaining hinges from). Or can I just hang a heavy drape to cover the doorway and hope that it will absorb similarly (due to the hall acting as cavity behind it) as the panels on the right wall? * I guess my planned treatment will take care of the reflection free zone? * What else can I do? Especially in terms of my spatial constraints? Other topics related to this studio: * Roxul: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8955 * Panels: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8959
