Soundman20203 March 2009#2
Which of the two doors are you concerned about? The door in the inner leaf of the wall, or the door in the outer leaf of the wall? Just like the two leaves of the wall, the two doors should not touch each other, and neither should the two jambs, if you want maximum isolation. I'm not sure why you think your two jambs need to touch each other. If you build them sturdy enough, then each will work just fine on its own, without needing support from the other. - Stuart -

Folks, this is what I say in the book -
I’ll let you know right now that this is one place I don’t worry about maintaining the separation of wall assemblies with the frames, even when using totally separated assemblies. When it comes to carrying a door that might weight well over 300 pounds (if you build them like I do), or even standard solid core doors, you do not want your door frame attached to a stud that can move over the years. As I noted above, tests have proven that a through jamb does not effectively lessen the total isolation value of a wall assembly to any great degree. So don’t worry about any miniscule amount of isolation you may lose. Just build the frame straight through the cavity.
Especially if the wall framing is either 2 x 4 studs - or light guage metal framing. I do intent though - to provide an alternate details for totally isolated frames (minimum 2x6 walls) in the 2nd edition of the book. Sincrely, Rod

Mike, Reality is that you need to decouple - and you also need to construct buildings that meet certain structural requirements. Personally I would always opt for a minimum 2x6 wall - but see people all over the place using 2x4's. When you take a sound door that weighs over 300 lbs and hang it off a 2x4 wal frame that has sheathing on only one side of the wall face - I do not have "happy happy joy joy" thoughts running through my head when I try to picture how long those studs are going to stay in place before something starts to move. Structurally I always try to err on the side of caution........ I view it the same was I do with the 3 and 4 leaf conditions created at air locks, etc...... sometimes you just live with what you have to live with. BUT - it is because of the fact that there are certain things we have to live with (that begin to compromise isolation) that we must take such care with everything else to make certain they are as close to perfect as we can get - and beyond that - don't sweat the small stuff... BTW - that through jamb detail is exactly as constructed at Power Station - and believe me - we have no issues there with lack of isolation.
