Ro16 July 2008#35
The pucks only work if you know exactly what you're doing. How much dead load you got (currently I'd say it's too low, but I dunno the puck specs so it's kinda guessing) And what's your expected live load. These things are vital to calculation when opting for a spring-like floor. A spring works most efficient when at least 5-10% compressed. too much load and the spring will be be overcompressed, too few load will make the spring dead, there's no compression at all. Both will undo the expected function.

Lou17 July 2008#39
FPR - Hi Dude! I'm no expert on this my friend, but I have to concur with Brien on this. Please don't misunderstand me, we all have to work with what we have, and you certainly have a really interesting project on your hands! :wink: You appear to have gone for 45 degree geometry front and rear. This will make placing your surrounds so they hit the 'sweet spot' tricky since you'll be generating two spots at least a meter apart (at a guess). I'm sure you're aware that 30 degree is better, and that your surrounds should be within the 110 - to 135 degree range. You seem to have a lot of room there and, I feel that if you first map out your maximum internal area (which I'm sure you've already done), then scale your control room perimeter down to achieve the prefered geometry, you might even be able to create an access route around the back of the room so that people wont be constantly walking through the rear of the mix position. This could also provide a natural walkway connecting your two stairwells. :wink: Just an opinion, nothing more, hope it helps, and wishing you all the best. Regards, Lou. 8) (Any chance of some dimensions of your last layout shown as a plan view? Maybe even your current .skp?) :wink:
