dymaxian7 October 2004#2
Welcome to the boards! Eeek! 90 degree corner behind the mix position! If those walls are reflective, you're going to be getting in trouble. Not un-fixable, tho- my first move would be to cut off that corner with a 45degree studwall, built wider than your speakers are (if your speakers are 6' apart, build the wall 8' long or so). If you build this wall with 24" clear between the studs, instead of 24" on center (or 16") then you can put a 2' wide piece of rigid fiberglass in each stud-space without cutting them, then just cover it with fabric. And if you're planning to do this, you can mount bass hangers in the space behind this wall. This idea will take up a bunch of floor space, but with that shape of room you've gotta put up a lot of absorption back there. My advice would be to do everything you can to absorb sound hitting that corner, because it'll come straight back at you if you don't. It's a big enough control room (guessing there, without dimensions) that having the mix position face the "short" wall instead of the "long" wall won't be too horrible. Of course, there are a million ideas for designs out there, and with as much space as you seem to have, fitting all this stuff in there shouldn't be too hard. Hope this helps!

Sean, as you can see i've been playing with your plans again. If the french door you are refering to is the door on the 30 deg wall, it not a good idea. What you want is uniform symetry to the room especially at the mix position. This symetry is not just obtained by wall angles, wall surface density/reflectivity etc... come into play. By putting a glass door at that location its reflection will distroy the balance you are tryin to achieve (see note on plan I numbered the other issues on the plan so you could refer to to area were talking about easily). 2. It seems to me that you have plenty of access to and from the control room. Again IMO I would lose the double sliders, and put in sealed double 1/4" glass windows of a size to your liking, not too big. I'm sure you know what I mean, studio windows each sheet angled. Theres all kinds of plans for these on this site, if I'm not clear. The thicker angled glass will be better for isolation and will produce fewer resonant aborations. 3. I can't remember if your post said you were going to seal off these windows, It's a good idea if you are. How much space do you plan on leaving between your new wall and the existing? space is good. 4. This needs to be fixed you still have that troublesome 90deg corner. I drew in a slot resonator like you have on the other side of the back wall at the same angle. 5.In the middle is just built up cloth covered rigid insultation made pretty, it doesn't nave to be angled like in the drawing just make it look cool and use whatever material you decide (703, roxul, etc..) to make that middle section acousticly dead as possible. Some space beween the actual wall and the layers of treatment 1" sounds good to me. The space will resulting in a reduction of energy as waves pass through each layer of treatment. Example a 500hz wave passes through layer 1 lossing substantial energy as it does (hopefully only a small portion this wave is reflected back into the room). The same wave now with less energy hits layer two with its 1" gap between. This wave also passes through with much less energy again, this wave also reflects off layer two, only two hit the backside of the first layer, this time the reflected energy attempting to reflect through the first layer is so greatly reduced that this reflections acoustic affect are negligible. I'm sorry If i went on too long but I get excited about this stuff, and this site is a blessing from heaven. Later, David

Godskid10 October 2004#8
1. the french door idea is a bad one if it is to be orientated on your 30deg wall to your air lock regardless of how you seal the door or the thickness of glass. The point is 30deg side L/R should have aprox the same density and reflectivity- you want both side to behave the same way. the glass is highly reflective and will distroy this symetry, even with heavy curtains covering it. 2. It's a good idea to angle the windows regardless of the angle of your wall wall, but not necessary. It looks cool, but more importantly the angled glass (tracking rm) will deflect the possibly high spl levels hitting it, reducing the vibration of the glass. the less the vibration of that glass, the less transfered to your CR room glass, the angle deflects from the outside and directs from the inside, directing most waves passing through at an angle other than parallel between the glass. The supplimental angle caused by you wall angles serves to further enhance this effect. I haven't modeled what I'm talking about but I think it's sound advice. The only reason this would be unadvisable is if your window angles are such that they create unwanted reflections in your room. 3. Curtains between the sliders is fine, looking at your room geometry, you might even get a real nice live sound leaving them opened or partially opened. 4. 1/4 and 3/8 is fine. i'm not an expert on glass I personally went thrift shopping and found a couple of glasstop dinning rm tables 1/2" and had the glass cut $45.00 for the tables $30.00 for the cutting- presto. 5. The rear wall, look at the SAE drawing earlier in the thread. The slot resonators shown in the corners can be fabric covered with out the wood if you have problems with that wall being too live. I don't think it would matter if your center treatment overlaps the slot resonators, make it look good and make it dead. I've got to go. Another thing to think about... John's plans solve this problem, but the last one you posted doesn't, so I thought I'd mention it while you're still tinkering with the layout. Unless you just take John's plans and run, which is ok too... Most of the acoustic treatment for your control room will be on the back wall. Your last plan shows a door on the side wall, but pushed all the way up against the back wall. Leave yourself some room there to put up absorbers- even 6 or 8" will make a difference, but it wouldn't be out of line for you to have a foot or two of bass hangers behind a false wall back there. Plan your treatment right into the design, and it'll be a lot easier later on. See yaz!
