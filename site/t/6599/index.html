knightfly8 July 2006#6
It may be; part of that depends on what type of concrete walls your basement has, how much of those walls is above the ground outside, and how your upper floor framing is done; the more info you can post about what is already there, the more we can figure out about expense and methods and materials. For example, if your concrete walls are made of hollow blocks mortared together, this already makes a 2-leaf wall so about all you should do is add more mass directly to one or both surfaces. If you can get access to the tops of these blocks, it's possible you could add sand to all the cavities; this would turn the wall into a SINGLE leaf of mass with EXCELLENT damping; then you could build a new frame inside (not touching the outer wall), insulate that, and get some decent (drum level) isolation. If your ceiling joists (the frame you might see from below if it's not covered up) are heavy enough, you can add more mass to that in the form of gypsum wallboard (also known as plaster board, gyp-rock, sheet-rock, etc) and get better isolation to the upstairs; this is important because without it your sound will continue out the upper walls and STILL get to the neighbors. The distance to your neighbors is a good thing; sound decreases proportional to the square of the distance. You should review the "don't even think about..." thread for ideas of what kinds of details we need, research as many as possible, and if possible post a rough sketch of what is already there; if I understand correctly, you have about 10,000 kroner to spend; in order for us to know how far that will go, you'd need to check pricing on 15mm plasterboard, 2x4 framing lumber (35 x 90mm), and any other materials you see mentioned here for isolation construction; but given the distance to neighbors and your concrete basement, it's possible at this point... Steve
