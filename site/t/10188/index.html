UnderTheGroove12 February 2008#1
Hi everyone, I've learned a lot here and am hoping to get some specific information. My wife and I are buying our first house and I will be using the second bedroom as my studio. The room measures 12'6" x 11' and I am trying to figure out the best positioning for acoustic treatment. We are also having some electrical work done to the house, so I will be able to create a circuit just for this room. We are also having central air conditioning installed. Background Info The house is located in southern California and was built using post and beam construction in 1940. We are hopefully taking possession the last week of February. All work will take place after that. I do not need to have a soundproof room, but I would like to be conscious of how any decisions I make will increase or decrease isolation. I will primarily be composing and mixing music in the room as well as recording electric guitars, acoustic guitars, and vocals. I'm usually working alone, but do have clients and musicians in the studio sometimes. I currently have 6 traps (4'x2'x4") and I am planning to buy more once I get in the studio. Air Conditioning There will be one vent installed in this room. I have asked the contractor to not share this duct work with any other room to help avoid noise passing from room to room. I have also asked that he take into account the extra heat that will be generated by the equipment in the room. Is there anything else I should be aware of? Electrical The house is going to have a new panel installed for the air conditioning and other reasons. I asked the electrician to create a separate circuit just for the studio room. He is also going to run new wire to this room and will be adding 2 new outlets. All outlets will be 3 prong. I'm not sure how to calculate the amount of power I will need, so I just told him to figure it would be the same as a high end home theater setup. I know nothing about electricity and wiring, so I'm not sure how to add up the requirements of my gear. Any tips? Is there anything else I should think about for electrical wiring? Acoustics I will be using my 6 current broadband traps. I would like to hang six 2" thick traps from the ceiling to create a very large cloud. (I will also be using this to hide some rope lighting to create indirect light for ambiance) I'm not sure what would be the best choice for the positioning of the traps in the corners. Because of the small size of the room, I'd like to keep as much of the treatment at the wall/ceiling corners as possible. With monitors that are ported to the front, will I need some traps on the wall directly behind them?

gullfo27 February 2008#13
generally - try to get the electronics on a single phase, star grounded system. the lights, motors, etc should all go onto another phase. electricians may balk due to "load imbalance" but your studio electronics are probably not going to be a huge load so finding one of your phases with the least amount of induction and other noise will be best. if not, consider isolation transformers etc... maybe post questions in the wiring forum.
