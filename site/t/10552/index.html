rod gervais9 May 2008#9
Some concerns - 1st - your isolation is as good as it's weakest link - in this case it's the walls with only 1 layer of drywall. That will be your effective isolation - the extra drywall on the party wall to the control room and main room is not going to help you. The reason is because all of the walls with one layer will eventually intersect and pass that control room wall - and at that point you will have just the single layer as your isolation. So add the 2nd layer to the remaining walls - or don't bother with the rest. 2 - the ceiling has the same issue - you have walls up - BUT - unless your ceiling runs all the way to the concrete wall - and you caulk it at that perimiter - you will short circuit through the edge and into the room above - and that will effectively determine your true isolation - the same goes (in that case) - with the use of green glue on the ceiling - even if you add the 2nd sheet to your walls - if you don't add green glue - they will be the deciding factor. 3. I wish you had posted this while you were determining your construction methods - because you threw away a lot of LF isolation going the route you did - and the LF transmission is the hardest to deal with. You're using light guage (25ga) steel studs - which are the same as 2x4 wood studs with resilient channel on them - great for frequencies in the upper to middle range - but lousy (in comparison with wood by itself) with low frequency transmissions - which are the true enemy. Sorry to have to be the bearer and all that......... Rod

Lou16 May 2008#16
Hi Fresh_Oil - Not sure where in the world you are? (Neither is anyone else at the moment! :wink: ) In reference to your enclosure question: From personal experience and U.K. Building Regs at least, your recessed light housings need to be FIREPROOF and not come into contact with insulation. Moreover, they need to have a small vent hole which will not only allow your electrical cable access to the bulb, but also provide a passage for heat generated above the bulb to dissipate naturally. In short, IMHO, this needs to be done correctly or you have a potential fire hazard on your hands! Hope this helps dude - Great space btw and a real tidy worker too! :D Good luck with the rest of the project! :wink: Regards, Lou. 8)
