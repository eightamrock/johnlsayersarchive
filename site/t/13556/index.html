Soundman202031 August 2009#2
If you could put some real-world numbers to your build, then it would be a lot easier to answer your questions! "Will this be enough isolation?" Enough for WHAT? Enough for a full rock band with a sleeping baby next door? Not a chance. Enough for a single piccolo played lightly, and no neighbors within 50 m? Sure! If you build a single-leaf wall, you are basically limited to "mass-law" with regard to how much isolation you can get. Mass law says that each time you double the mass of the entire wall, you get an extra 6 dB of isolation. You need an extra 10 dB (roughly) so that it sounds about half as loud (subjectively). So you need, very roughly four times the mass to get it half as loud. So let's say you have two layers of 5/8 drywall as your single leaf, and and after you listen to that, you figure that you need to get the level down so more, to about half what you are hearing: You need to ad ANOTHER SIX layers of 5/8 drywall to do that, theoretically. Of course, all this assumes that the wall is the only way for sound to get through. If the sound is actually getting around your wall ("flanking") by another path, such as through the ceiling, floor, side wall, or out one window and back in another, then NO amount of extra drywall will help. That's the reason folks here recommend full two.leaf MSM walls. There are no easy cheap ways to isolate a room. The easiest and cheapest is a full room-in-a-room two leaf MSM wall on double studs. Anything less that that won't do the job. There are no magical materials that defy the laws of physics (despite what some unscrupulous manufacturers might claim on their web sites). You cannot persuade the laws of physics to somehow not be applicable to your studio. I wish you could, but it just ain't possible. So most likely what you propose will not accomplish what you want. If you need to get sufficient isolation so that a loud rock band does not bother the neighbors, a single leaf wall just won't cut it. (Unless maybe you make it out of very thick concrete, or lead sheeting... :) ) - Stuart -
