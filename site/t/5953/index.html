knightfly8 April 2006#3
What you see on that page is pretty much to scale; note the 8" dimension for depth (not critical, deeper is better) - each half of the plan shown is a 2 foot wide bay, same as rigid insulation typically comes. The total width as shown is 4 feet, the 7 foot height is just so you can actually move it into place in a room with 8 foot ceilings. If you have a way to get it in place, you can make them however tall will fit. Another option is to make them half height, minus maybe a half inch each; then you can stack two units - they'll be lighter and easier to handle. Don't use masonite, in fact a heavier back will keep the trap from trying to emulate a resonant panel absorber, which it is NOT. I'd tend to go for a back of 3/4 MDF instead. If you have any scraps of insulation left over, putting them inside the traps behind the main pieces of fiberglass is a good idea... Steve

knightfly8 April 2006#5
You're over-thinking this; I know, because it's where I was about 10 years ago... For a rear trap, all that's necessary is for the absorbent to be away from the rear wall by up to 3 or 4 FEET, if you can afford the space. Whether you build several smaller boxes with mineral wool/rigid fiberglass across the fronts, or one LARGE one, makes no difference acoustically - all that matters is the thickness of the absorbent (more is better, 4" is about minimum) the density of the absorbent (3 PCF is ideal, up to (maybe) 8 PCF but that's pushing it, and I'd face that dense a material with softer stuff), and the distance from the front of the absorbent to the solid wall behind. IF you intend to move stuff later, I'd be more tempted to build backless frames (gobo's) and just stuff 'em with rockwool and cover with cloth; then you can play with depth from the wall and somewhat tune your space. Until you get into building tuned absorbers such as Helmholtz/slat/slot resonators and panel traps, hermetic sealing isn't necessary at all. If you still wanna build boxes like the SAE plan, each box can be 4x4 feet (slightly shorter if your ceiling is exactly 8 feet) so you can stack two - if you build four of these, you'll cover a space 8 feet tall by 8 feet wide. Each "bay" inside the boxes should be 2' x 4', so it will fit normal insulation batts. And finally - if your space is as isolated as you need it, you might consider just framing out the rear of the room and building a full-width, full-height rear trap - it's easier than building several boxes, and won't tend to rattle if you get too loud... Steve
