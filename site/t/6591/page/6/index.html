I'd need to see this drawn out to comment authoritatively... And even then use "finger quotes" around the word "authoritatively." ;-) Anyway, you are wise to question the appropriateness of this advice from an isolation perspective. And he is wise to question the appropriateness of the 20 foot spans on the I joists if they are deflecting quite a bit.
studio911 wrote:
Other things/options we talked about were, doing the LVL beam up against the I-Joists, but putting somethng between the two (like sill seal or rubber or something) to decouple the two, but still add support to the floor above.
No. You'll almost surely overload the makeshift elastomer, which would defeat its purpose at best. Even if you didn't, it either wouldn't be securely fastened or the flanking would occur at the fastening points.
studio911 wrote:
Again, the I-Joists are doing what they are designed to do, and acting like its supposed to, but he said if I could tie in 1 LVL with the joist system, it would stiffen it all up greatly on the second floor. :?
If there is a concern about structural integrity (and I would imagine this is not the case, since they've been engineered to code and he's not willing to fail you), I actually think a floppier floor/ceiling might be better for sound isolation. I remember reading a comment from Steve (knightfly) a long time ago that the best wall assembly isolation-wise would be one without any studs at all... :lol: ...but obviously that would not be appropriate from a structural integrity perspective. At the risk of pretending to be an expert :P, I'd say your existing plan is probably best left alone. Insert all the obligatory "your mileage may vary, void where prohibited, consult with your friendly neighborhood structural and acoustical engineers" disclaimers here. ;-)
