sharward25 April 2007#105
Hey there, Can you take some more pics of the rebar grid? Or did you cover the rebar grid with plastic? (It's sometimes hard to tell the order in which the pictures were taken.) If you covered the rebar in plastic, that's no good! The plastic is supposed to go under the rebar grid. You'll need some "dobies" (short for "adobes" -- they're little half-brick sized concrete blocks with wire attached to them) so that you can lift the rebar grid up a couple of inches. You need to allow the concrete to get underneath the rebar or it's not going to do you any good. :roll:
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/files/victor3_small_185.jpg
Oops! My guy forgot to put the plastic down before putting the rebar grid together! So he had to do some fancy footwork in order to get the plastic underneath it.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/files/rebardone1_small_317.jpg
You also need a one or two rebars inside the perimeter haunches, also supported on dobies...
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/files/rebardone3_small_168.jpg
Notice in the above photo that the two upper dobies are two of many supporting the pair of rebars inside the haunch (below the grid), and the lower dobie is one of many supporting the grid. You'll also want to place your J-bolts in advance... Use a small piece of wood (stakes are good for this) with a hole drilled in it to hold the J-bolt in the position you want it to be after the pour. Also, do your rebar extensions overlap by at least 18"? I think that's what code requires. My guy did them with at least 24" overlapping just to be on the safe side. --Keith :mrgreen:

rod gervais8 May 2007#113
Hey guys - this is not a structural slab - and thus the steel is for shrinkage control - and it needs to be in the TOP 1/3 of the slab - preferably the top of the bar within 1 1/2" of the top of the slab. The shelf looked just about right - the cement bricks should be on their side - or close to it - I don't usually use those - I generally purchase rebar chairs the right height. http://www.jankeproducts.com/rebar_chairs.htm you only put bar in the bottom 1/3 of the slab when it's structural - and then you have to put another mat 1/3 from the top as well. Slabs don't become structural until somewhere around 8" thick - (depending on the actual load) and this slab is a long ways from that. If the slab has not been poured yet - when they lift the bar - make them set the brick under the bar to keep it the right height..... and then just watch to make sure they do - becuase it will do you no good what so ever at the bottom of the slab (or even in the middle). Rod

There is rebar manufactured using FRP (fiberglass reinforced products) and it has a specific gravity less than that of concete (and thus will float) - but that is not what I see in your photos.
