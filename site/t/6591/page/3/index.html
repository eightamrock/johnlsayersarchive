That’s a yes, insulate both. All guides & ”how to’s” for building double 2x4 frame iso walls state insulate both frames. Each wall gets more dampening as a result.
I think I am getting real close though to how it is going to be now… E - Are these 2 walls in the tracking room splayed and angled enough so there are no parallel walls to worry about? I guess after its built, I will also be using absorbers and traps in the corners and walls as needed.
I just skimmed through your thread and could not find these things mentioned before. 1. Symmetry in CR is very important. 2. In CR always try to face the short wall, not the long the wall… especially in smaller rooms. 3. Side walls in CR should be off min by 12 degrees or splayed 6 degrees each. Room should get bigger toward the back. 4. In TR, Johns guide line is to throw parallel walls off by 24 degrees. How he comes up with complete designs seems magic sometimes. 5. Maybe a butchered version of Johns Small Studio In 3D http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5457 would be a guide for your design shape.

Solid Metal Entry Door… hard to find one that is really solid, cost effective and not already two leafs. Note I did buy a metal exterior door, need it for code, it's going to make my end double door assembly kind of triple leaf BUT I plan to make the other door a SUPER DOOR. Oh and yes every time you add a door you comprise sound iso some, how much depends on how much money you spend on the door and how well you install it. Some pro, studio doors cost $4,000 plus.

My advice on SketchUp is spending some time with it just goofing to get the hang of it. There are a few little glitches to work around (at least with my version). And stay in 2D as long as you can, resisting the urge to see your project in 3D. To check if you’ve gone into 3rd axis rotate a bit and then hit TOP again. Hope this all helps you out. Keep posting. Marc

What kind of insulation will you be using? I'm known around here for being a big fan of Thermafiber Sound Attenuation Fire Blankets (SAFB) - Nominal Density (2.5 pcf), or the competitive Canadian product, Roxul Acoustical Fire Batt (AFB) - ≥2" (2.5 pcf). They perform a bit better for isolation than regular fiberglass, plus they're quite superior in their fire resistance. I got my hands on 3" x 24" x 48" Thermafiber SAFB locally for 55¢ per square foot, plus a flat $25 for delivery. The home improvement stores don't carry it -- you have to go to an insulation supplier and it might be a special order. --Keith :mrgreen:
