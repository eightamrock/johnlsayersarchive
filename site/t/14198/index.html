But seeing you asked: Yes, by all means your main speakers should be soffit mounted. As everyone here knows (even folks like me, who are not paid experts), soffit-mounting your mains forces them to radiate into half space, with the soffits acting as infinite baffles. That greatly increases the bass response of the speakers (by 6dB), tightens up the bass in general, improves stereo imaging, eliminates comb filtering off the front wall, and has several other benefits too. You definitely should soffit mount them, if the speakers themselves can be mounted like that. And provided that you do it correctly, of course, with the speakers at the correct height, and the correct geometry for the speaker angles and the mix position, at the correct position in the room, with correct splaying of the walls and ceiling, with correct treatment of the first reflection points, correct bass trapping, correct room acoustics, and correct adjustment of the speaker response. But since you already hired an expert, I guess he already told you all about this, and what all those "correct" things are...

Soundman202019 March 2010#9
... endanger the cooling efficiency of the electronic.
I hate to say this, but that comment kind of shows that they don't know much about soffit mounting! Part of a good soffit mount IS the cooling ducts, which should be carefully designed to provide the cooling that the speaker needs. A soffit mount without a cooling duct would be a really poor, foolish soffit. The fact that they didn't mention it suggest that they don't know much about good soffit mounts. It is also hard to see how a soffit could "damage" the mid-range response. It migh improve it, but I'm not sure how it would "damage" it. Yes, a soffit sure will "affect" the low range (which kind of is the entire point of soffit mounting), which is why you need a method of rolling off the bass response of the speaker to compensate for the boost that the soffit will give you, but once again, this isn't "damaging" the response: it is improving it. Yes, I'd agree that a badly designed, lousily constructed soffit certainly can make a good speaker sound bad. But a well designed soffit should only improve the sound, if you ask me. The only time you might not want to soffit mount speakers is if they have rear or side ports. (Or drivers that fire sound out the side or back). As long as it is designed to only produce sound out the front, then soffit mounting should work great, provided that the soffit is designed and built properly. But anyway, if you can't soffit mount the speakers you have (or don't want to), then my suggestion would be to get speakers that CAN be soffit mounted, since the benefits to soffit mounting out-weigh the negatives, in my opinion at least. (Others might not agree) - Stuart -

Since it looks like your speakers are designed to mounted horizontally, you'll need to have enough room available to do that, as they will take up more space (side to side) than for vertical mounting.
