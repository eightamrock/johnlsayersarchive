I'm going to talk out loud for a moment. Why do some rooms get built to a near completion stage and THEN questions are asked? Most of what I have gathered suggests that leakage is a concern. If your windows or doors leak you have to fix that. Caulk is sold by the case per room! If you have space or cracks or potential leaks, you have to correct these. You mentioned an approx. 6 inch thick concrete slab on approx. 3 inches of rigid insulation? Where the heck did that come from? And what is below the rigid insulation? I 'm a bit thick headed and might not get "it" all at first glance, so if I ask for a real picture or 12 it's only because it is difficult to visualize. And just to show that I am really paying attention:
2)i can see the attraction/versatility of john's back-to-front wall. what sort of insulation is used on the rear face, and how is it attached?
Inside out wall is what it is referred to as. The insulation is attached into the stud cavities that face >into< the room. The elegance of these walls are that they allow the builder to apply more mass and then to have the qualities of absorption directly on the room side when complete. In a room where space is at a premium the use of inside out wall construction will benefit the user in this area as well. After covering the insulation with a flame retardant breathable material typically a slat type finish is applied. At least that is what I got out of it :)
