gullfo15 February 2011#2
the ceiling height 68 (1/2 = 34hz) and the length (38hz) are probably combining to produce the low end problem. you probably need a lot more back of the room absorption and even in the front. you might consider a panel trap on the front and back wall for the low end and hang an absorber in front of those. not sure what you have for a cloud but consider building one angled and using a hard backing. also, with the larger monitor, presumably you are still using the same levels? its only the extended response that is causing this?

gullfo16 February 2011#6
the membrane panel absorber would typically use 1/8" (2.5mm) or 1/4" (5mm) panels (plywood is generally a good choice) because you want th panel to vibrate, then the sealed cavity and insulation inside dampen the panel vibration. on the cloud, make a 3/4" (19mm) panel then line the face of it with cloth covered porous absorption - 4" (100mm) thick rigid insulation. then hang on a angle (say 15°, more if possible if possible) over the mix desk.

gullfo13 March 2011#18
the clouds need to be bigger and thicker and hang them down from the ceiling on angles (say 15 degrees) so you maximize their absorption. if necessary, use plywood or MDF on the back of the clouds. skip membrane panels until you know the other absorption tactics aren't solving everything. you want broadband control to flatten the room response so you're not just selectively trying to treat problems you have not identified yet...
