Hi Cyeazel - You engineer said you could use up to 38 psf if you accept a 40 psf live load. I would expect that 40 psf is just fine... that's the standard live load for a residential floor. So if you'd be comfortable putting your studio stuff on a second floor of a house, then the 40 psf is fine. I looked at Keith's (sharward) isolated floor. My quick estimate is that it is about 60 psf dead load or a tad more. This is a long shot, but... if you can find a soils report for your property and your engineer didn't know about it... he may be able to get a design load from the soils report and give you better allowable loading. Sometimes the building department has a copy. Creatch

Yeah, that long a stretch won't work for drywall. You need to get your engineer guy back and have him evaluate those trusses - tell him you want to hang 2 layers of 5/8 drywall and at least a foot of unfaced 1 PSF fiberglass insulation on them, suspended INSIDE your similar inner walls on the kip/rim system on your floor (see your other thread) and see if he thinks it's OK... Steve (if so, then 2 layers of 5/8 DW with green glue between AND the insulation as your inner leaf, similar choices for walls (inner AND outer for the room(s) where there's no concrete backing up the inner wall) Keep in mind though, that a double framed wall with 2 layers of 5/8 DW each side (assuming 8 feet tall) will exert approx. 80 PSF on your floor (80 PLF as well) so in order for this to work, your walls upstairs would need to coincide with a support wall DOWN stairs or your 28 PSF limit would be destroyed...
