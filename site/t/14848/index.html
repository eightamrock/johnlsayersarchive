jhbrandt25 September 2010#2
Nice beginnings... First, I recommend that you build a 'one room' studio in your 'live room'. Use the room that you were going to make into the control room a booth. Tear out the closet in the main room - too small. The minimum recommend volume for decent modal response is 1500 cubic feet. The kitchen is way too small but would make a good booth. What isolation will you be needing? How close are neighbors? The building seems to have a crawl space underneath. If you need isolation, you will have to do a lot of work. The STL of this type of construction is probably not better than 30db, so considerable 'beefing up' will be in order and you will need to deal with the floor. Cheers, John

jhbrandt26 September 2010#18
You can use AutoCAD and export to sketchup. It's 'sketchy' but works. I still haven't gotten the hang of sketchup... too busy. :P Here's a 'second' on buying Rod's book, it should answer a lot of your questions about construction and raise some more. ;) Concrete block (8 x 8 x 16) around the perimeter of the building, sealed well, cores grouted, and painted will give you around STC50-55. If the floor sound like a drum, the best way to solve this problem is to brace the flooring with addition posting underneath. This is done by placing concrete pads in a pattern beneath the joists and stacking cement block up to the joist. Use large shims to get the right height & make it snug. Do this in a 3 or 4 foot pattern beneath the main room and booth area. The earth is a very good damping material. Once you get your layout, position the operator in the control room/main room and draw site lines to the booth. If you can adjust windows and doors for best ergonomics, do it. Since you will be doing a major remodel you might as well get what you want. I would recommend facing the end closest to the booth and rather than the corner and possibly having only a small window to the booth. This is for symmetry reasons. You can angle the window towards the rear of the CR to point any early reflections toward an absorptive back wall. Cheers, John
