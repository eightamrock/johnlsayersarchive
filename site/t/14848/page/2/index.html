gullfo30 September 2010#33
agreed. like a lot of non-purpose built structures, this one represents a number of challenges and trade-offs and the results could be sub-optimal. that said, if you go with a small isolated control room, will that be worse than a large open room? maybe, probably. if you sell all your recordings via MP3 and mix using headphones only, then room acoustics for mix down is a non-issue. if you record using headphones and mix using free standing monitors, then an open space might just work. if we attack the problems of a small space correctly it may prove completely usable especially if isolation is a more important requirement. trade-offs. then again, maybe there is another building in the area which would give you better rooms but then you may have to expend a lot more money on materials because of the size etc and paying rent and the possibility you do all the work and then your lease is suddenly terminated or otherwise no longer can afford it. maybe starting small and sub-optimal is "good enough", maybe getting the rental income is more important. dunno. maybe we haven't played with all the possible room configurations yet...

Before you give up, take a look here for a second or two: http://johnlsayers.com/Studio/Mainpage/MP-Mark.htm If John can do that in roughly 990 ft^3, then I don't see why you can't do something pretty damn good in 2400! (Or even in 1000) :) Seriously, you'll see a lot of comments here from the experts along the lines of "Sometimes you just have to live with what you've got". That seems to be kind of the second slogan for studio builders. Sure, your space is not ideal, but it doesn't mean that it is impossible. It only means that you'll have some limitations, and you'll have to work a bit harder to squeeze out every last inch and every last decibel. Another example: Have you ever noticed how good music can sound in your car? That certainly is a bit less than 1000 ft^3 :!: :shock: I know of quite a few engineers who use their cars as the ultimate listening environment, to check on their final mixes... So if you can build a highly successful studio in a container, and have a great listening "room" inside a car, I reckon that your space is NOT a lost case at all: It's just a matter of making the absolute best of what you have available, and using every trick in the book to do it. Take a close look at Spark One: Notice that the 990 ft^3 includes BOTH the control room AND the booth... And those dimensions are BEFORE installing the isolation and treatment... :) You actually have a lot more space than that! Will yours be a fantastic world-class studio? Probably not. But it can still be damn good, if you ask me. All is not lost! - Stuart -
