kendale8 January 2007#11
Aloha, Here are a few bits of information I’ve collected along the way that you may find interesting: • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position • Monitor angles 60 degrees • Monitor height – woofer at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

kendale10 January 2007#16
Aloha,
could you explain Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall
As I understand it, 38% of 15' 7" would be roughly about 5' 9", so that would a good starting point to position your ears/seat/mix position from your front wall. This will place you in a postion that will help to minimize any peaks, nulls and standing waves occuring in your room from adversely distorting your perception of your monitors. One should note that positioning alone will not free you from room anomolies, hence the need for acoustic treatments to help further treat your room response. Set your monitor height (center of woofer) at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) If monitors are more than 3’ in height off the floor, they must be angled down. There is so much more involved than I could ever hope to know and understand, much less communicate. Perhaps there are others more learned that will offer more insight.
