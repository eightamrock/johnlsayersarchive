Anyways, I'm not a moderator here, but seeing as they are all very busy at the moment, I will try my best to answer some of the basic stuff. I am currently deep into my own design/build and have learned a lot from some of the more experienced members here. They are seemingly infinite in their knowledge which only goes to show how much can be learned in regards to acoustics and studio design. It really is endless! I hope this pushes you to spend as much time as humanly possible in the design phase. You'll be glad you did. In regard to your design: 1.) Since this space will be functioning as a control room, it definitely needs to be symmetrical. Currently it is not with that left wall splayed like that. This doesn't mean that splayed walls are a problem, but they have to have purpose... such as creating a "Reflection Free Zone" (RFZ) or to reduce flutter echo (which your splayed wall might help with flutter, but since the opposite wall is not equally splayed, you are creating problems already for imaging in your control room...drastic problems, maybe not, but symmetry up to the mix position at the very least, is vital). 2.) The mix position does not seem very realistic. Most likely it should not be that close to the front wall considering it would create very short first reflection times off the front wall (which creates phasing problems and other artifacts that would be better explained by someone more experienced). You want to keep reflection times as long as possible...ideally 15-20ms or more. This may not be possible in your space, I'm not sure, but pulling back the mix position would definitely be better than right by the wall. 3.) Have you thought about soffit mounting your monitors? The advantages (if done properly) are huge compared to free standing monitors. Just a suggestion... 4.) Why are you angling the front wall like that (the "v" shape)? If that is what the "acoustic designer" said to do, then I'm really not so confident he has proper acoustical reasoning behind his proposed plan. Those angles would serve no purpose other than to add difficulty in construction as far as I can tell. :? Well that's a start anyways...I'm getting tired. I hope that helped some. I'm sure you will get some great advice from some very experienced members around the forum. It just takes a little time. They all do this for free and unfortunately, they do have real jobs that take up a lot of their time and energy. One thing I will say for sure though...the sooner you can attach and upload a sketchup file (existing space as well as proposed plans) and maybe even some pictures of the space, the quicker you will get more detailed replies. Looking forward to watching the progress! cheers Trevor

Okay...I withhold my statement in number "2" regarding the monitor placement/mix position being too close to the front wall (until further clarification by an expert). :oops: I read where Stuart (an expert by all means) gave this advice to someone with a small room and free standing speakers...
The room is not big enough to allow you get the speakers far enough away form the front wall to avoid the worst artifacts, so you'd need to put them up tight against the front wall, and compensate with EQ adjustments.
Most likely, if you went with free standing monitors, they would be close to the front wall (to help couple the LF drivers and encourage low end response). However, I do think you would want to spread them out enough to bring that mix position back a bit and spread out the stereo image...unless you really want as much space behind you for the purpose of "live room" space...since this is a multi-purpose room. Soundman202017 September 2012#10
I went over the link that John left me and would like to do something of the sort. My only worry is that using 1x3's may not give me the support i need to spand the width of the entire room, and if i use 2x4's i loose more roof height....
Maybe I'm missing something, but I don't see John recommending 1x3s for your ceiling joists in that thread, and there is no way at all that 1x3s will support the ceiling load that you plan to put up there. Neither will 2x4's: not even close. To span 19'6" with a load of two layers of drywall, you are going to need 2x10's at least, and probably more, depending on what type of wood you are using and other factors too. And that's assuming 12" OC spacing: if you do 16" OC then you'd need even larger joists. Nineteen feet is a long distance to span...
i made a quick sketch of the basic idea (i have 8' - 4" to the existing ceiling, which will give me a 7'-10" roof height once all is said and done... please advise, can? no can?
I figure more like 7 feet, assuming you build inside-out. 8' 4" less a 4" air gap (minimum) leaves you at 8' on the dot. Two layers of 5/8" drywall is 1-1/4", leaving you at 7' 10-3/4". 2x10 joists means you lose another 9-1/4", bringing the final height down to about 7' 1-1/2". If you build conventionally then you could raise that a little, but then you'd still lose ceiling height from the acoustic treatment on the ceiling.
how bad is that hurting me regarding the "three leaf" effect?
Depends on how much isolation you need, and at what frequencies. You might want to consider add a layer of drywall on top of that existing stuff, before building your inner-leaf. (If a 3-leaf system is inevitable, then it makes most sense to put plenty of mass on the middle leaf.)
tried to keep the mix position a little closer to the front (which might be an issue?)
How far back in the room are you, in terms of percentage? The theoretical ideal position is at 38%, but that's really just a guideline. Anywhere between maybe 30% and 45% should be reasonable. Just stay away from 25% and 50%, as those are baaaaad places to be. - Stuart -
