Soundman202015 September 2012#9
Another question? How does the room treatment change with wanting 5.1 surround? does the back wall design change?
Oh yes! Everything changes! 5.1 is another animal entirely, since you have some speakers facing the "wrong" way, plus that sub down there somewhere, thumping away merrily. Different ball-park. A lot tougher to get a good reflection free zone, and a lot, LOT tougher to keep enough absorption in the right places while also keeping enough reflection in the right places, and also doing soffits.... 5.1 drives you crazy... :? :P :?: :yahoo:
Ok here's an updated layout.
That's a very long, thin room for 5.1. Correct speaker layout for a 5.1 room is based on a circle, with your head at the center and the speakers arranged around the circumference. Circles don't fit well into long narrow rooms. You should probably be looking at a room with a ratio more like Sepmeyer 1.
Is the floor raised for any acoustical reason?
It's normally raised more for practical reasons: so the client can look over your shoulder, and also feel like a king, way up high there on his mountain-top sofa... :) But acoustics also plays a part: the raised floor has the potential to become a resonant box, so it needs to be designed and built with that in mind. - Stuart -

Soundman202016 September 2012#12
Should i go back to more of the square room starting point if im going to be doing some 5.1 work?
The issue with square is that you were way TOO square with tie first one: just a 1/4" off! :shock: That would mean that all your modes related to length and all your modes related to width would line up perfectly, exactly on top of each other, making for a really lousy room. So you need to be a bit off square. Then with your second layout, you went the other way, to a very long thin room, which presents another set of problems. Try for something in the middle: rectangular, but neither very square nor very elongated. There's a large set of known good ratios that you could look at, and find one that makes sense for your space. - Stuart -

The Recording Academy also has a very detailed document on setting up multi-channel rooms, called "Recommendations For Surround Sound Production", that I think you can download form their web site: I looked for a link, but couldn't find one.
