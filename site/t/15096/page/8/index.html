Soundman202016 August 2011#185
planning for flush mounted mains,
There seems to be a problem with the way you have the front of your room depicted: from the drawings, it looks like you are not using soffits at all to mount your speakers, and instead the seem to be mounted directly into the inner leaf! Not only that, but they are not angled: They do not face the mix position, but rather face directly towards the back wall, head-on, at 90°! That would put your sweet spot somewhere way outside the back wall, way beyond the building... I also don't understand the areas marked "space for CR treatment": that too seems to be outside the inner leaf. How can you treat the acoustics inside a room by putting the treatment outside of the room? Maybe you could explain that, and also provide more detail (in 3D) on your SketchUp model, since it is hard to figure out what the various lines are supposed to be. Regarding your other questions:
-What is a wise size of conduit to use to leave myself room for snakes and cables room to room through the slab?
Basically, make them all as big as possible (within reason!), allowing for future expansion, and use separate runs for electrical, audio, and other things (telephone, video, etc.). Keep the audio runs as far away from the others as you can.
As for speakers on the console, if my set up is as follows in the pics, planning for flush mounted mains, but also i want to be sure that speakers on the console are far enough from the wall to function well. Is 4'8" good?
Maybe, maybe not. The best plan is to find the right place for the speakers once the room is built. Your mix position and speaker geometry are a little strange: it looks like you have your mix position marked at the apex of the triangle, which is at 1/3 the room depth. The general recommendation is that the listening position should be at 38% of the room depth, which of course means that the apex of the triangle will be a bit further back than that. Also, even though you commonly see it in photos of all kinds of studios, putting speakers directly on top of the console is not a good idea. Much better to put them on stands just beyond the console.
-Would one be better to plan for floor AC plugs directly Under the console or out from the back a bit?
Personally, I prefer to just have two or three plugs at the front, somewhere, feeding power strips attached under the desk and inside the racks for distribution. But that's more of a personal preference thing: I like to have as little clutter as possible on the floor, and try to hide all the distribution under/inside the furniture. - Stuart -

Soundman202023 August 2011#194
Several questions: Why are your speakers no longer in the center of the soffits, where they have to be in order to work properly? Why have you removed the rear corners of the room, which are essential for good bass trapping? Why is the mix position roughly in the worst possible spot, at the geometric center of the room?
I know, nothing too serious, and kinda messy, but, Well, perhaps i am wrong, but given high's travel in strait lines, that would be the path they would follow from the speaker given the angles of the walls. Not something to consider?
I think you need to study the concept of ray-tracing. Yes, highs do travel roughly in straight lines, but not as one single line emanating from the center of the speaker! :shock: There is a wave front that follows a roughly cone-shaped path from the speaker, spreading out with distance. Pack in a few million individual straight lines within the cone, follow each one of them for a few bounces around the room, and you'll have a rough idea of where the actual wave front is going by seeing where each of those those few million "strait lines" goes. It's just a tad more complicated than a single line can represent. You might be able to simplify it to a dozen or so lines, but certainly not to a single line.
On another note, any comments on using a floor box for xlrs in the recording room, poured into the slab? Better in the walls?
Better in the walls. If you stick it in the floor, the connectors will gather dust, get stepped on, spilled on, take up valuable floor space, etc. - Stuart -
BriHar23 August 2011#195
Regarding your attempt at ray-tracing, besides the excellent 'splanation from Stuart, trying to do anything like this by hand with partially curved and very inexact lines is absolutely futile. I suggest you simply try and locate your prime reflection points - but at least use a ruler and perhaps a protractor. Use the system similar to that outlined by Ethan Winer http://realtraps.com/art_room-setup.htm by copying a mirror image of your room to either side of the actual room diagram, then drawing a line from the actual listening position to the centre of the reflected images of your monitors - where it crosses the actual room boundary is your primary reflection point for that wall. You can do the same for the ceiling and even the floor using the side-view of course (including the desk if you've got it scaled in the drawing). Using this method can also help you work out the required angles for walls, hardback-clouds etc. for RFZ as well as treament.
