Soundman202023 March 2011#142
I was thinking a false ceiling with the correct angles to deflect the highs and mids at around 15' and bass trapping in the corners and ceiling(above the 15'), as from what i understand lows are omni directional. But perhaps i misunderstand the directionality of the lows?
Lows are not directional, sure, in the sense that they wrap around objects that are much smaller than themselves, but the wave fronts still travel and expand in the same way as all sound waves. So most of them will hit the absorption at an angle somewhat less than 90°, and thus will take a longer path through the absorption, which is good but also partially invalidates the "1/4 rule of thumb". That rule is only true for waves normal to the surface, and for absorption mounted directly on the surface. About your ceiling angle: Yes, it can be angled to help direct reflections away from your mix position, but you can't really do that with a peak down the center-line. The normal way to do that is to start with the ceiling lower down at the front of the room, and angle it up at about 12° or more, so it is higher at the rear of the room. - Stuart -
