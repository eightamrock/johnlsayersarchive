gullfo17 December 2010#27
it looks like you will be on your way to meeting your STC 65 goal. are you planning on beefing up the ceiling the same as the walls? the ceiling is going to be large and sound leaving that way could end of traversing further than the wall leakage. on the plus side, you could have a significant air gap between the outer and inner ceilings and that will not only help with the HVAC ducting etc but also the isolation. are you intending to support the inner isolation ceiling on the inner isolation walls? if so, i'd consider wood framing or use heavy steel for the inner isolation wall to ensure it can support that load.

gullfo31 December 2010#36
use the light insulation in the gap between the inner isolation and outer walls and ceiling. use the heavier insulation inside for treatments. the lighter insulation in the air gap has been shown to do better @ LF than you would expect versus simply calculating it as if it was working within the room as an absorber. using the lighter insulation inside behind rigid insulation is an option where you can benefit from it.

seaweed wrote:

That in mind, given i will need to build the detached walls on the inside of the main rec room, and i have 3 windows to deal with there, what about 2 double pane windows? the plexiglass is very light and won't do much for isolation. you're generally better off with 2 single panes - they can be laminated - of 3/8" and 1/2" respectively. thus preserving double leaf and avoid triple and quad leaf. in general (also) matching the mass of the glass to the mass of the walls will reasonably ensure you don't create a weaker point with your windows.

seaweed wrote:
Diffusion ...snip... Ceiling will sit @ roughly a 16ft average.
assuming the ceiling is doing it's isolation thing and is effectively a live ceiling (acoustically), i'd consider trapping the peak ala super chunk and some slats (widely space). then a couple of options - scatter absorption to make the ceiling diffusive and absorber the mid-high bits somewhat; hangers below the super chunk and hanging polys - create broad band absorption and some space coupling effects and the polys are diffusive; same w/ hangers and hard panels (as in another thread); or using numeric diffusers suspended on different levels to create pseudo QRD.
