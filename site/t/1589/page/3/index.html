What would be the best way for fastening the walls to the floor? Usually with concrete slabs you have the anchor bolts put in when its wet, what should I do with my wooden floor/foundation?
knightfly18 May 2004#52
You would just use 16d nails, run a bead of construction adhesive before tipping up the wall frame, and then toenail the bottom wall plate into the perimeter joists through the double layer of OSB flooring - space the nails about 12" on center, all around the perimeter. With a flat floor surface, you'll be able to construct each wall's frame laid flat on the floor so you can nail up through the bottom plate and into each stud without having to toenail the studs down from the top and into the bottom plate - then, once the frame is complete, you tip it up into place and nail flush with the outer edge of the floor. Ok, thanks we got two walls done, finishing the shorter walls today, after this, it'll be the ceiling. Anything special on ceilings?
knightfly20 May 2004#54
Yeah, you'll need to build a 2x6 version of your floor frame, but without doubling the perimeter joists - the outer edges of the frame should line up flush with the outer edges of your wall caps, the two end joists (the joints at the corners of the frame) should be glued with construction adhesive and screwed together with 3-1/2" screws so those joints are airtight - this is because these perimeter 2x6's will be your outer isolation mass (you should add the same two layers of outside siding on these as on your stud walls) - Then, once the joist frame is in place, put your two layers of OSB (no coincident seams, caulk with acoustic caulk at every seam) on TOP of this ceiling frame - what you want here is a solid, airtight barrier. YOu will need a moisture barrier in this leaf as well - one way would be to use 30 pound roofing felt between the two layers of OSB - you can put one layer of felt (comes in 3 foot wide roll) lengthwise, NOT overlapping seams but just butting them together firmly - do a second layer running at 90 degrees to the first, so you have virtually no cracks through both layers, then put the second layer of OSB over that. Caulk everything as you go. Any construction you won't be able to access later should be painted well before you continue. The roof itself should be also 2x6 joists, it can be 24" centers if you want - note that for a 4/12 pitch, your shed roof will be nearly 4 feet higher on one side - I'm out of time for now, will continue this tomorrow nite if possible... Steve

knightfly30 May 2004#68
Reason your room is wobbly is there's nothing keeping the frame from "racking" - normally (I probably forgot to mention this, my omission) you put your first layer of sheathing (OSB in this case) on the outside of the frames BEFORE you do the roof/ceiling frames - you should do that before you go any further (don't go on the "roof" any more til this is done) - You'll need to fasten the sheathing along one vertical edge of each wall first, then get help if necessary to pull the frame either way to make the bottom of that piece of sheathing line up with the bottom plate, and nail that - Use 8d galvanized nails for the first layer, 10d for the second, and space the nails every 8" along each framing member including studs. Center of every third stud should fall at the edge of a piece of OSB. Caulk as you go. Looks like you're doing pretty good so far - you'll be amazed at the change in your structure once you get a layer of sheathing up... Steve

knightfly1 June 2004#75
Neither plywood or OSB are "dirt cheap" any more - 1/2" plywood CDX grade here in the northwest has gone from around $11 a sheet a year ago, to $22 a sheet currently - OSB isn't a lot cheaper. For any sound isolation where you're not exposed to weather or moisture, sheet rock at around $5.50 is better isolation at 1/4 the price. Right, you mount small channels on the surface of the walls and run your wires through them - the outlet boxes then mount on that channel and stick out from the wall, instead of into it. Looks less elegant, but better for sound isolation since you're not punching holes in your airtight construction. Do you have an electrician to help you with wiring? This isn't an area where you "sorta" do it right... Steve
