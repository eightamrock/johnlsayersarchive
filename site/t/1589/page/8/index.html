knightfly12 March 2005#177
I would replace the slanted absorbers and flat one across the top of your drawing with just flat 2" rockwool panels (cloth covered but NOT backed) and spaced so that the rockwool has about 2" air gap behind it (a 4" wide frame will do this) - this will give you more usable space, and the slanted stuff on the opposite side will kill any flutter between that wall pair. Same with the slats on either side of the door; not necessary, may or may NOT need any absorption there so try without it first; you can always add more absorption if the room's too live, but space is at a premium here. If you're not doing any critical mixes in there, but mainly just practice, I wouldn't worry much about symmetry; you'll never hear it with a couple of drum kits and a metal band playing in there. I would also build the slats behind your drum kits with the lower of the two REVERSED in angle; in other words, peaked in the center with the shallow parts of the traps at the outer edges... Steve

knightfly17 March 2005#181
If you mean 12" along each wall, that will give about 18" width of absorbent; it will do SOMETHING, but not enough, or at low enough frequencies - a 24" along the wall measurement gives about 34" wide absorber, with about 17" depth at maximum; this is reported to work pretty well in almost any room. If you have 24" wide batts, you could put them across the corners and see how it works; that would be about 17" along each wall from the corner, and would stick out 12" into the room (bisecting the corner angle that is) Absorption frequency; check out this http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3378 For inch/feet use, change 344 to 1130, that's the speed of sound in feet per second. Answers will be ALSO in feet. Your 6" total depth would get you down to about an octave below 1/4 wavelength, or around 220 hZ; deeper by a little for non-perpendicular incidence of sound. Your frame idea, deeper behind the drums, will work fine; generally, people recommend that you use at least the same depth of air behind an absorbent as the thickness of the absorbent. HTH... Steve

knightfly3 April 2005#189
Right; the entire attic is a "cold room" - the AC unit cools the attic, the fan in the RETURN duct pulls warm air out of the room, the decrease in pressure inside the room causes the cool air at the BOTTOM of the attic to drop into the room through the lower duct, and the AC unit re-cools the air in the attic as necessary. Actually, the upper, warm air duct and fan, should be as near the TOP of the attic as you can get it, at least where it dumps into the attic air space... Steve

knightfly17 June 2005#197
Acoustic caulk is definitely NOT a sham; first, every type I've seen comes in the large contractor size tubes, which are 3 TIMES as much as the "home-owner" kind (29 oz. vs. 10 oz) - with a bit of looking, these large tubes of acoustic caulk cost just a little more than the SMALL tubes of regular caulk. Next, about the only "regular" caulk that won't shrink and crack (leaving you with leaks in your construction that will weaken low frequency isolation) is GE's "Silicone II" stuff; a 10 oz tube of that costs AS MUCH as a 29 oz tube of OSI, or USG, or any of the other acoustic caulks. If you plan on your construction being together (and functioning as a studio) for more than about 6 months, I would NOT bother with the regular stuff, or you'll wonder why after 6 months to a year your neighbors are complaining about the noise, when they didn't notice it before... Steve
