Aaronw5 August 2004#129
So, this RC I have has predrilled holes in it with the fat flange part that is raised. We attach the RC to the wood studs through the predrilled holes and the drywall to the flange where there are no holes correct?
As far as the predrilled holes, Don't worry about them. You'll find that half of the time they don't line up w/ a stud anyway. Screws...you don't need self drilling w/ RC. Just use regular dart (pointy) type screws. You'll be fine. Yes, the drywall attaches to the flange section of the RC. I don't have any ceiling pix yet, but here's the RC pic I have of one of my walls... It'll basically be the same thing, only the spacing will be 16" instead of 24". As far as screws...1st layer use either 1 1/8 or 1 1/4...2nd layer use 1 5/8" or 2".

Before you start, please re-read the "complete section" and Aarons' floating floor stickie's to make sure you've not forgotten anything - also, I hope you have at least one helper and a couple of sturdy stools to stand on - if you don't want to spend the money to rent a drywall lift, you can make a pair of "tee's" out of extra 2x4's - make them so they are about 2" longer overall than the distance from floor to ceiling, and wedge them against the ceiling while fastening. You're right, the RC is kinda wimpy, especially if you try to fasten a sheet of drywall up against it in only a couple of places and hope it will support the weight - however, once all the fasteners are in it's fine. knightfly1 September 2004#146
Hey, glad you're still alive :) I covered this in the drawing near the bottom of page 3 - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... c&start=30 Notice the 2x6 that's part of the corner framing detail - this is so the RC has a place to mount to, without the danger of the two walls' channels bumping into each other. Neither RC gets closer than 1/2" to the intersecting wall frame, you want them to NOT touch each other or the intersecting wall. This is also a good way to do NON-RC walls, either way you get a large enough mounting surface that just isn't there when sticking to 2x4 framing... Steve
