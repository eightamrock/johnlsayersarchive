knightfly28 July 2004#110
DC, Aaron is currently doing (or recently done) his studio, so he knows firsthand about what you can and can't squeeze into stud cavities - as to making deeper cavities with different resilient mounts, the RSIC-1 clips and hat channel increase air gap by about 1/2" over RC, but if your insulation is TOO stiff you might get too much coupling through the wall where the hat channel tries to compress the insulation. I don't think I'd try this. For lower frequency isolation, staying just under 3 PCF is your best bet - If you can get the 4" SAFB, I would just do that - then, buy just one roll of 3-1/2" fluffy fiberglass, cut strips of it about 3" wide, and place them in the centers of each stud cavity before you add the wallboard over it. That would be the simplest (and likely the best) way to handle the insulation for your wall frames. For your floated floor, either rockwool or the 4" SAFB's are fine but you still need those strips to make sure the floor doesn't resonate... Steve

knightfly29 July 2004#112
Assemble the box with the interconnecting conduit or flat raceway so you have accurate dimensions, then hold the assembly against the wall with the covers removed, mark at least two holes for each box and one every two feet or so for raceway - drill these to fit plastic drywall anchors, making sure you have all studs and RC located with marks on masking tape so you can MISS them with the screws - then, just before you mount the boxes/wiremold with the correct screws for your anchors, spread a bead of caulk around each hole and force some into the anchor hole - "butter" the backs of the outlet boxes with caulk, insert anchors, tap flush with a hammer, and install the screws. Caulk around the finished box/wiremold assembly, wipe the excess around the boxes and wiremold and let cure. I like to put surface mount electrical around 2 feet off the floor, this gives a good resting place for bottom of acoustic treatments and keeps hum far enough off the floor not to interfere with any mic/guitar cords laying on the floor. What little damage this does to your wall isolation will be almost entirely canceled by your in-wall insulation... Steve

knightfly31 July 2004#114
1. Is RC-1 the type of RC I want to buy? Yes, but be careful - USG stopped making this a while back, so you need to ask the manufacturer (whoever they are) about spacing when hanging a double layer of 5/8 drywall. Generally you can use closer spacing (such as 12" instead of 16" if your ceiling joists are on 24" centers, or 16" instead of 24" if your ceiling joists are on 16" centers. 2. I don't remeber if we covered this yet, but RC-1 is strong enough to hold a double layer of drywall on the ceiling? See #1. 3. For double layer of drywall, the second piece will also screw into the RC correct? (just clarifying) Correct - 1/2 the screws for first layer, then a full schedule for the top layer. LOnger screws for the top layer (actually the bottom layer phsically, when doing the ceiling - top layer means the last layer you put on.) It wouldn't hurt to re-read the "complete section", and search the USG manual for "fastener schedule". 4. Is there a negative difference in using 4x12 pieces of drywall rather than 4x8? Only if you have a bad back - otherwise, 12' sheets are better - less joints to mud and tape, less chance of sound leaks... Steve
