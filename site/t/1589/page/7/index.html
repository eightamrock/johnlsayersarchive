Ok, so using the side wall and corner absorbers will help with sound exiting the room right? No, sorry - acoustic treatment does effectively NADA for sound escaping the room; only your mass-air-mass barrier all around the space has any effect on that. [b] What you have is an isosceles triangle that is also a Right triangle; so, if you draw a line from the peak of the 90 degree angle to the center of the long side, you now have two isosceles triangles; this means that, if the side along the wall is 24", then the depth from the corner out into the room will be (Pythagoras) the square root of 2, or 1.414 feet, or just under 17" - And maybe I should of thought about this, but we already put molding into the floor, so the absorber won't be flush with the wall, is this a problem or just more space to be taken up? So I should be tuning all the absorbers to around 300hz?
knightfly11 March 2005#175
Yes; this isn't as drastic as it sounds; remember, these are NOT constant depth traps, so taking the AVERAGE depth and choosing values in the Helmholtz calculator that SAYS 300 hZ, will NOT give you a drastic PEAK at that frequency - only a center value that's around there. The main advantage of these over just absorbent placed across the corners, is that you keep more highs in the room; if this turns out to be a bad idea and you need LESS highs, you can always hang some thick blankets or some insulation over one or two of the traps. Your room size has no major mode problems, so wideband absorption will take care of most acoustic treatment needs... Steve
