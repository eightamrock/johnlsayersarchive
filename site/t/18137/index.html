I have had many thoughts about the ordinary version of a recording studio, and came to the decision that space and air, to feel comfortable in the room meant just as much as the requirement for vocal boot's and isolated small chambers that would be filled with rubbish or other storage. space is essential. which is also the basis for being able to make a proper acoustic control. I decided to isolate the building with straw bales. with plasterboard to the outside and clay plaster on the inside. The building is located far from neighbors as a real external noise problem does not arise, and straw structure allows the low frequency pass out of the building, with the expectation that I can achieve a nice frequency response in the low range. I use cutted straw,gravel and paper wool for arming and reinforcement of the clay. Well, you should still follow the general guidelines for soffits and for speaker / mix position geometry. Using bales of hay or any other materials does not change the basic layout. So you should still set up the geometry according to ITU-R BS.775-1, EBU 3276, or AESTD1001. Hi Lasse, and welcome to the forum! :)
One of my concerns is to me that i avoid the the pressure build-up in the corners and will have free passage to the back where the equipment will stay.
Well, you cannot avoid that. Modes are a consequence of the dimensions of the room, ONLY, and there is no way to get rid of them. Building a partial wall does not get rid of the corners: it just moves them to a different place. You can only control modes by choosing a suitable ratio for your room, and by damping them with suitable treatment in the corners, or in extreme cases by using resonant devices. Building your soffit wall only part way across the room does NOT eliminate the corners: the corners are still there, just in another place, and you have now also added back the problem that soffits are supposed to prevent: edge diffraction around the sides of the speaker baffle. In this case, your "partial wall" is the speaker baffle, and since it is not sealed to the side walls, low frequency waves will wrap around the ends of that wall, get in behind it, and cause some of the the same problems that the soffit was trying to prevent. You can calculate the frequency at which this problem will start occurring by using the normal baffle-step response equations. - Stuart -
