xSpace1 February 2009#3
If you are in any way concerned with your sound bothering the neighbors...you need mass on the interior of this unit. Mass is what stops sound, not insulation. And you need a lot of it, mass that is. Also, your overhead joist layout is more likely 24 inches, just from looking at the layout. If you are going to do anything with this overhead area, adding additional joists to develop at least a 16 inch layout is important to support the added weight.
Soundman20202 February 2009#4
Hi Drew,
should the wall be at an angle to the back wall, or should it be parallel? does that mess with stereo imagery of drums or stuff like that?
That would depend on the shape of the inner leaf of your control room. You can probably design it so that the door can be angled.
should the control room be a specific ratio of dimensions for width and depth to height?
Absolutely, most definitely, YES! Search this forum for "room ratio" to get an idea of how to figure out the best ratios for your room. This is important!
i'm going to build bass traps and diffusers and stuff like that
Well building bass traps is a good idea, while diffusers probably not such a good idea for a small room like yours will be. And you can't really decide too much on what treatment you are going to need until you actually have a design!
again my thoughts is to build a wall just inside the garage door with a man door and an A/C unit.
You want to put an A/C unit inside the air gap between your two leaves? I'm not sure how that would work. Not very well, would be my guess.
along the back wall, should i fill that with 702, or regular insulation, should i drywall that or is it better to have uneven surfaces? should i fill it with 702 and then do the spaced panels? would that work like a resonator?
All of that would depend totally on the way you design the two rooms. It is impossible to even guess at how you should build it, without first seeing the design of what you plan to build, in terms of layout, dimensions, construction, etc.
what about the ceiling? ideas? it's studs spaced 16" i think. 702? in the gaps? regular insulation? should i put a drywall or something over the studs with insulation or leave it exposed? probably insulation and then put a fabric or something right? should i do the hard plastic over the insulation and then fabric?
Once again, until you have your design in place, and have posted details about the usual questions regarding sound levels, what your expectations and budget are, etc., it's impossible to say what you need to do, except in very, very broad terms. Like xSpace said, if you want to stop sound getting out and bothering the neighbors, then you need mass, and lots of it. Ditto, if you want to stop sound getting IN, such as the sound of traffic, machinery, noisy neighbors, etc., then you also need mass. Sound goes both ways through walls. If you want to block sound going either way (inside to outside, or outside to inside) then you do it the same way: with mass. Insulation does not stop sound. Mass does, and more specifically, mass when used properly as part of a "Mass-Air-Mass" system. Insulation is for treating the room once it has been properly isolated. Mass is for isolating it. (You do need insulation as part of the isolation, but that is NOT part of the treatment. Two different things). I'd suggest that you start looking over this forum at some of the design and construction projects that are about to be done / are being done / have already been done, in spaces about the size of yours. That will give you a god idea about how to proceed with your design, and then once the design is finalized, then you can start building. Don't make the mistake that you'll see here repeated several times: people starting to build before they have a proper design in place! It NEVER works. Design first, then build. - Stuart -
