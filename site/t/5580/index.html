The third option is acceptable except for one area - the corner on the south wall. Again it would probably produce unpredictable asymetrical reflections towards the mix area. You've shown it with absorbant - that's good - but it will have different properties than the wall on the other side and I think that would be undesirable. If it were me, I'd construct the new 2x4 walls with a 2x6 plate and staggered studs, insulated on both sides, 2 layers of 5/8" drywall on the exterior of the room and 2 layers on RC on the inside. The inside walls I'd simply make a frame with insulation and covered with fabric or slats. This will hold your insulation farther from the wall (boundary) and give you attenuation for longer wavelengths. Adding the additional layer of drywall (inside out wall) would probably provide more isolation but less attenuation of interior acoustics in the same amount of space, since to get the same spacing of the insulation from the wall boundary you'd have to make the interior of the room smaller. On the other hand a single layer of 1/2" drywall would serve as a resonate panel absorber of a sorts, but only of the frequencies related to its properties. Does that make sense? :?:

knightfly10 February 2006#12
STC should be thrown out the window when designing rooms for drums, or any music related use for that matter. STC is an outmoded (but stubborn), single-number rating that was intended for VOICE isolation, and as such it's weighted around 500 hZ - it's very easy to build an STC 75 wall that will STILL pass drums (except the snare and cymbals :=), then build a DIFFERENT wall that's only STC 55 but won't pass drums AT ALL - it's all in the individual TL ratings at different frequencies. Generally, for music it's better to design for great LF TL, because if you do that the rest of the spectrum will take care of itself. If you look at the infamous "3-leaf" problem, you can actually get BETTER midrange isolation using 3 leaves; but the LOW end will suffer because LF isolation needs mass-air-mass, and the more of each the better. It's been proven that even the kraft paper on insulation can act as a third leaf when placed anywhere but against the wallboard, and I'm not sure that this isn't ALSO what causes differing densities of insulation to improve bass isolation at the cost of midrange, and vice versa (heavier insulation = worse bass iso, lighter insulation = better bass iso) - I kind of think that this could be caused by the heavier insulation acting more as an extra leaf in the wall, which would exhibit exactly those tendencies... Steve
