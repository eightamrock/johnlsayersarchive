xSpace7 September 2010#6
If you and your family have any interest in cutting your budget down to a manageable size you have a few options to consider. Garages have been converted with success but there are modifications that are quite necessary in order to reduce your sound levels. To isolate sound you need mass and mass typically comes in the form or hard dense surfaces. You may be able to cut the rest room out but that implies these guys are going to be in your house whenever the "call" comes in if you know what I mean ;) On the down side, it is rare to find a garage taller than 8 feet so you lose head room from the start...usually. Heating and air is not optional as many here have tried to overcome and will be able to support this statement. Ideally you would locate a separate building with a level concrete slab and built either out of concrete or poured cinder block walls, with minimum penetrations in these walls, e.g. windows and multiple doors. Although at least two means of egress may be a requirement to satisfy code, more and bigger doors can present some issues, but still might be manageable. Taller walls height is preferred, 12 feet is better than most, but a nine foot wall height is almost perfect for a rehearsal room. Since the mass-air-mass design will require interior walls and the addition of a separate ceiling this will still allow you a ceiling height of approx. 8 feet when all is said and done. HVAC is critical and the placement thereof. Get Rod Grevais' book "Build it like the pros". The chapters on HVAC alone are worth the cost to get you over the humps that you didn't even know existed. There are always trade-offs so don't get to caught up in these issues if you can. If you are fortunate enough to locate a building already standing that has the potential required, your budget could effectively be cut in half or even more. Last but certainly not least, read this, you and your designer: John Sayers "The Recording Manual" It is the "good book" around here! Good luck,
