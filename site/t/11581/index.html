kendale14 January 2009#2
Aloha Wayne,
the brown coloured wall = is this a slot resonator wall?
Yes, however, depending on how "live" you need/want your drum sound to be it could be either. One other function it performs is eliminating/redirecting reflections between parallel surfaces.
and are the grey walls simply absorbtively treated
Again, depending on how "live" you need/want your drum sound to be it could be either.
I assume the panes facing the front of the drumkit are sliding glass doors?
Correct, that is what the design indicates. :D
Any other details to help get this design built would be much appreciated including ceiling treatments etc.
John's design in this instance indicates carpet. Some designs may call for a more hard/reflective flooring (hardwood, parquet, concrete). Again, that may depend on how "live" you need/want your drum sound to be, as well as your budget. If a design calls for a hard floor, it is generally a good idea to have some absorption (cloud) over the area to eliminate floor/ceiling reflections, providing your ceiling height allows for it. Keep in mind the dimensions of a given space and the amount of "liveness" needed/wanted for the source that is being recorded. The acoustics you want for a live drum sound is totally different than you want for a vocal, for example.
By the way: what alterations to dimensions / treatments if any would be indicated (modally speaking) if I can afford an internal ceiling height of 9 or 10 feet or even higher?
That depends on what height you ultimately decide upon. Once you determine what height you will be working with, the other dimensions can be calculated. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
