z6061118 August 2004#5
The more absorber surface is exposed to the air, the more sabins of absorbtion you will get. Thus you get more absorbtion without a frame. You get more absorbtion still by having it out from the wall, both because it's effective at lower frequencies as well, but also because if you can touch the back side, then the back side starts absorbing the around the side reflections/defractions too. I believe the change is larger than the change in surface area (defraction etc). But to keep things simple, if you have a 2'x4'x4" panel, geometrically the sides can contribute an additional 50% of sabins if they are covered in speaker cloth. If you have a 4" airplane frame, that's got 3.5" holes in it with half inch spacing, that's 30% more aborbtion. As for if this is worth the labor, that's up to you. I'd ask if adding frames is worth the labor. If you put the side of the frames against a wall, then air can't get to that side, so there's no change if there's holes there or not. If you put the frames against each other, then again the air can't get to the side, so there's no change if there's holes there or not.
