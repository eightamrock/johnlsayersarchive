sharward7 December 2006#2
Hi Matt, Great post with great pictures. 8) Neat location. :) The thing that stands out to me is the fact that it's not on the ground floor. :roll: That may or may not be a major issue -- that would depend on how much isolation you'll need. You wrote that you need something "that will allow [you] to record music, with a booth big enough for a duo or trio of acoustic players and primarily focused on basic post production work (Voice over for TV/Radio, Multimedia etc)." I don't see "acoustic drums" mentioned specifically, so hopefully that's not on your list of things you hope to do. If so, then you need major isolation. I'm also not clear what you mean by this: ". . . situated on the top floor of a 3 story building built on a solid cast concrete slab . . ." Does that mean the third story floor is a solid cast concrete slab? Or are you saying the building itself was built slab-on-grade? (Or both?) Before you get too far into engineering how you'd build your inner leaf walls, and possibly floating your rooms, you need to know how much weight the floor will support. You'll definitely want to study "Cyeazel's project studio...... planning and preparing" thread as an example of a studio design that is going on a non- ground floor, complete with structural engineering reports that advise how much weight his existing floor can safely hold. Even if you won't be very noisy, you may need to keep other noise out. What are the surroundings? Who or what is going to be occupying the space below you? A print shop? A childrens' day care center? A sign shop? A machine room? What noise sources are in the neighborhood? It being a retrofitted warehouse, and probably in a commercial or industrial district, are there any train tracks in the area? An airport? A freeway with rumbling 18-wheeler trucks? Downtown streets with honking taxi cabs? It may not seem that noisy when you're just walking around, so having a handheld sound level meter can give a clue what you're dealing with in terms of ambient noise. Compare that with your home neighborhood to see what deficit you're facing. Another thing to think about: is there any chance that the roof would be made accessible to people not specifically working on HVAC equipment up there? Is there any risk of guests or other tenants wandering around up there for smoke breaks, taking in the view, or causing mischief? Imagine building a studio that's practically bulletproof as far as isolation goes, only to have a rude awakening of having footfall noise from the roof rendering the space useless and building management refusing to secure the roof area... :roll: Moving out of the bedroom is an exciting prospect... Just make sure that the environmental noise issues in the vicinity of the place you plan to build will accommodate your needs and you take into account any contingencies. I imagine the terms of your commercial lease will be rather strict and long, so make sure you can live with it on all levels before you commit. More comments may follow, depending on your response. --Keith :mrgreen:
