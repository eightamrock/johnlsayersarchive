Soundman202016 February 2009#5
Hi Koen, That's a bit better, but it seems you still didn't read the "Don't post until you read this" thread completely! One key item tells me that.... :) Anyway, a couple of comments:
i thought of using the following scheme [brick|air|brick|air|metal stud with insulation|3 layer of gypsum] for a total of 0.5 m thickness.
That would make a three-leaf wall! Very bad idea. Try searching for "two leaf" and "three leaf" on this forum, and you will find out why. This forum is a HUGE library of information on the correct way to design and build studios. The information you need to avoid making bad mistakes (like the one you just mentioned) is all over this place. Just pick an interesting thread, and start reading. Follow the links to other threads, other web sites, etc. You really will find all of the information that you need, but it will take time. And you'll probably have to "un-learn" most of what you have heard about studio construction in other places. A lot of it is just plain wrong, as you'll discover here. But your basic wall construction concept of "brick - air - brick - air - gypsum" is a three-leaf wall, which means that you will have LESS isolation at low frequencies. Your kick drum, bass guitar, toms and other low frequency instruments will pass right through those walls. And so will low frequency noise on the outside, coming in, such as cars, trucks, buses, motorbikes, etc.
So soundproofing the outside walls is very important.
You cannot soundproof only your outside walls! The outer and inner leaves of a wall, plus the air gap and insulation in between, all act together as a single system. If you change any part of it, then you change the acoustic behavior of the entire structure. You have to think about and design the entire wall SYSTEM together, not as individual parts. It is not that you have two leaves of wall, one of which stops some sound, and the other stops more sound. It doesn't work like that. The two leaves work together as a "Mass-Spring-Mass" system.
threated with e.g. auralex acoustic material and some selfmade quadratic residu diffusers.
Diffusers in such a small control room are probably not going to do anything useful for you, and might make your stereo imaging worse. Diffusers need a lot of space between your head and them, in order for there to not be any lobing. I don't recall off hand, but I think the recommendation is for at least six meters of space from the back of your head to the surface of the diffuser. I do't think you have enough room to do that. And while purchased acoustic treatment will certainly work, you can get the same or better effect from making the treatment yourself. For example, building bass traps yourself is easy and cheap. Or you could buy them from Ethan, ready built. Check out his web site, which has another gold-mine of useful acoustic information and tips.
Do i benefit when ramping the walls, ceiling? Or leave better rectangular?
Start with a rectangle, and choose the dimensions of that rectangle from one of the set of good room ratios (search the forum for "room ratio" if you don't know what that means): The ratio refers to the inside dimensions of your finished inner-leaf walls. Once you have selected the ratio that you are going to work with, then you should angle your side walls away from each other, at least six degree for each side wall if you plan to have your speaker triangle intersecting at 60 degrees, or 11 degrees if you plan to have them intersecting at 90 degrees. This is called "splaying" your walls. Your ceiling can be angled too, at least 12 degrees, sloping upwards away from your speaker position. Especially if that would help to get a higher ceiling in some places. The average dimensions of your room-with-splayed-walls should still be the same as the room ratio that you chose. The above is for your control room. Your live room should have non-parallel walls, and can have diffusers if you need them. Regarding your layouts: Personally, I think #3 works best, but it all depends on what you need, and personal taste, too. Second choice would be #1. Forget #2 and #4: they are not symmetrical, and trying to mix in there would be impossible. - Stuart -
