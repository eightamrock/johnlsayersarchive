knightfly22 February 2004#2
Part of this would depend on whether your outer ceiling is already a mass-air-mass barrier, so that you have isolation under control. If that is the case, then your inner sloped ceiling can be whatever you decide. Making traps part of the sound isolation will weaken the sound isolation at the working frequencies of the traps ( it's gotta go SOMEWHERE) Putting 703 at an angle does NOT change the effect of parallel walls except at higher frequencies, so you would still have some of the problems of standing waves. What you CAN do, is build a SOLID sloped ceiling but leave a couple feet open at the front of the room (where the bass pressure will be highest) and fill the entire ceiling cavity with spun fiberglas, then close off that two foot wide strip (across the entire front of the ceiling) with thick 703 (at least two inches) - don't restrict the spun glass from contact with the inner ceiling wallboard, you want it damped. Once this is finished, you want to check with a mirror against the ceiling to be sure there isn't a spot where you can see either speaker in the mirror from the mix positions - hanging a 703 "cloud" over the mix area will fix that too. Same check with walls, absorb any spot where you can see a speaker reflected, then stop and listen. Any more treatment only if it's too live, or uneven reverb response (ringing at specific frequencies, etc) Hope that helped... Steve

Good to hear you're underway! How deep are the ceiling joists you're planning to use? Is there any way you can drywall or deck the top of them instead of the bottom? I forget how much headroom you say you have up there, but you'd need enough to get plywood sheets up there (unless you cut them apart for handling, which aside from needing more caulk may not be a bad idea). If the ceiling is going to slope up by 2', you may not have much trouble getting sheets up on the low end, and just sliding them up to the top. If you can do it this way, you would have the sealed room, but the joist space inside with which to hang bass trapping. And the whole thing would be sloped, both the decking above and the absorption below. It'd be a bigger pain to build, but if you can do it, it'd accomplish what you're trying to do pretty cleanly.
