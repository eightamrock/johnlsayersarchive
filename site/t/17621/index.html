Soundman202011 June 2012#2
First layer -12.5mm gypsum, on top of that 9mm genesis acoustic board and then green glue and a second layer of 9mm genesis acoustic board.
I don't think I'd go with such thin materials. That "genesis board" is a bit more dense than drywall, but not by a lot. Around 20%, it seems. So look at prices: if that stuff is more than 20% more expensive than plain old fire-rated drywall, then forget it. Sound waves don't care how much you pay for your mass: they can't read price tags, and they aren't elitist anyway! :) They just react to the mass, so get the cheapest mass that will do the job. Also, the claims on that PDF document are a little suspect, since they aren't back up by any independent research lab test data. Maybe they do have real test data, but I didn't see it there, so that's something to think about. Also, they talk about STC rating, which is a pretty useless measurement for studios as it days nothing at all about low frequencies.
1x gypsum - 1x genesis board - green glue - 1 x genesis board. I should get an STC of at least 70
I doubt that you'd get anywhere near STC-70! What did you base that on? According to IR-761 there is no construction at all that will get you SC-70. The closest is STC-69, which was accomplished with two layers of 16mm type X drywall, separated by a 205 mm cavity filled with two layers of 90mm fiberglass insulation, using 2x4 studs 24" OC. Next closest was STC-67, which was the same as above but with the studs at 16" OC. Building an STC-70 wall is no easy task. In any event, even if you did manage to build an STC-70 wall, that would be overkill since the floor, doors, windows, ceiling, HVAC and electrical system would not get you the same level. The room is a system, and the total isolation is only as good as the weakest link.
and a tl of about 30 down as low as 80hz?
Perhaps, but that depends on other factors. - Stuart -

Soundman202011 June 2012#5
When you say they are about 20% denser than normal drywall board do you mean the 15mm fireboard? So a 9mm genesis board is 20% denser than a 15mm drywall board? But then what about the thickness difference? Cost wise a 9mm genesis board is about 20% more expensive than a 15mm gypsum.
Not really. I was referring to the true density, not the surface density. The true density of good fire-rated drywall is about 750 kg/m3 (depending on manufacturer), while the advertised density of this Genesis stuff is around 900 kg/m3. That is regardless of thickness. Sure, a thicker panel will be heavier, but the density will still be the same.
A magnesium oxide board has an STC rating of 54.
Once again, that depends entirely on how the wall is built! Paper can have an STC rating of 54 if you build the wall suitably, while even concrete won't get to STC-30 if you build it wrong. I always get concerned when I see manufacturers claiming that their product alone has an STC rating of such-and-such, or that it reduces sound by 80%, since those are both entirely meaningless and useless tidbits of information. Unless they state how the wall was built, and how it was tested, then the claims mean nothing at all. That's why it is so important to only use products that have been tested in independent acoustic laboratories, with the full results being published and easily available. The lab will tell you exactly how the wall was built, exactly how it was tested, and exactly what the results were, with no vested interest. If the manufacturer doesn't provide such data, then the product is suspect.
I'm thinking of using these with green glue for my walls and ceilings then. Anyone have experience with them or any other thoughts?
My concern would be with how thin they are. At just 9mm I would expect them to be very flexible and also to have a high resonant frequency, neither of which is good, regardless of density. At higher frequencies, mass law rules, but at lower frequencies it is rigidity that governs isolation, so thin flexible panels don't do well at low frequencies, even if they are very dense. Limp mass works well at low frequencies, but the panels you are talking about are not limp mass. - Stuart -
