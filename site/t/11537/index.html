Aloha, Here is a collection of different quotes, links and information pulled from this forum. Hopefully, you will find them useful. But before we go there, a couple of questions if I may: - Is your vocal booth the only use in this space or are their others uses (storage/recording/control room?) - if there is no back door on the wall you are building your vocal on, is it possible to build your vocal booth with the longest dimension along the back wall, thus freeing up more room up front for whatever use needed (storage/recording, etc.) - where is your control room located in relation to your vocal booth? And so, without further ado - here are those excerpts I mentioned: Steve (Knightfly) mentioned that "4' x 5' x 7' inside dim's is about the best modal compromise for a small booth." Modally, with a 6'4" ceiling you'd do well with wall dimensions (inside) of 5 and 7 feet. That combination shows no modal problems at all. This means that vocals in such a room shouldn't be "colored" by the room. You would want to keep the upper half, including the ceiling, absorbed with such as rigid fiberglas insulation boards, covered with a light plastic film (they shed) and fire proofed cloth (they're ugly) - you'd likely get better vocal sounds with the singer seated rather than standing, since otherwise they will be nearly at the ceiling. Being that close to a boundary (especially if it isn't absorbed) will cause comb filtering due to early reflections. Mode-wise, a 5 x 6 x 7 (inside) booth looks very good. It will also fit into an 8 foot ceiling area if necessary, and is big enough for 3 (very) friendly vocalists or 1-2 with a guitar, so that's what I'm going to start with. Once I finish (don't stop breathing while you wait, I have 'way too many projects still open) you could change dimensions to suit, just do NOT make any two dimensions the same, or more than one dimension in even # of feet.
viewtopic.php?p=443#443 A vocal booth need to be quite dead with no apparent room sound if you want a tight vocal sound. When I say dead I mean right across the frequency spectrum which requires various absorbers aimed at different frequency ranges. Ro14 January 2009#12
Room dimensions (and modes) are taken from the hard surfaces, so walls etc. Basstraps and a like are not walls. There you go. A one room setup might work perfect but you've gotta make sure there's no external noise from either outside or inside (computer, equipment etc) Acoustics are not gonna help you isolate sounds, its just to make the room sound 'better'. Is there another door, except for the main door, in that room? If not, a booth might be possible. It's gonna be tight, but it'll do for just one person. (unless ye wanna get cozy :) ) 20x10x10 is not much but it sure can work for just recording vox. If there's no backdoor I'd just add a 5'x10'x9' (extra ceiling) section in the back for vocals with a door and glass. That is, if you wanna spend some money onnit (whaz the budget on this project?) Add acoustics on the inside AND the outside which will be your control room. But let's first focus on a floorplan.
