gullfo18 November 2007#4
it you make 12 pairs back-to-back and connect those into panels of 4 each angled about 15 degrees and put them across the corners, you can eliminate a bunch of the echo and get some absorption as well without having to deaden the space. then get a few rolls of regular insulation, cover those in cloth and tuck them into each corner. up top, put the last two tiles hanging down a bit and angled to break up the ceiling. if things are still ringing, invest in some rigid fiberglass and cloth and tuck that up into the corners along the ceiling-wall. avoid egg crates, cheap foam, etc. not only is it not effective (although the treatments decribed above won't be winning awards either...), foam and paper can easily become a deadly fire hazard.

Eric_Desart24 November 2007#7
Just a tought based on belly work. If you make thicker panels of the ceiling tiles, I should not double them up. That specific painting type works a bit like Ecophon panels. I do have experience with this kind of material. It will reflects highs somewhat, causes some membrane effect improving lows, but which is positive when used on the room side (as meant). In fact these tiles can be very good to get a flat absorption curve in a suspended ceiling or wall cladding (improved by putting additional absorption behind them). However when a second layer comes in-between or on the backside, the whole thing will start behaving too much as a membrane damper. In itself a membrane damper can be a very good thing, but also involves a risk in this case because it will give a rather unpredictable result. Therefore I should combine these tiles with standard fiberglass to make them thicker.

Eric_Desart25 November 2007#16
No Kirk, It will not "just reflect these highs back". But therefore it's useful that you maybe email them asking for absorption curves which they certainly have. In fact it's useful to know in how far they act as a membrane or not. Often it can be an advantage having this painted side to the source in order to prevent too much mid and high frequent absorption. See how standard rooms are designed here with slat/slot resonators. The basic idea is just to prevent too much mid and high absorption. But it's of course more fun, knowing these curves. If you want to glue foam (I'm not advising this, just responding to your repeated request), and Auralex feels too expensive for you then search for other companies. But you can use flat foam also. While not exclusive, if you find polyester foam with a density of around 30 kg/m3 then you likely have good foam in your hands. If you have yellow pages and search for acoustics, lots of these industrial acoustics companies have such foams as well. Also note that this profiling of that foam is at the expense of low frequent absorption, hence flat foam is possibly even better, but it all depends. I rather leave these practical things to the experts here.

Eric_Desart27 November 2007#23
Kirk, Can you give the type name of that board and the measurement description? What you're looking at are boards in a suspended ceiling of 400 mm cavity, with framed edges. These look like boards with extreme low entrance empedance, meaning that the impression you got from the reflections in the highs are not really conformed by these measurements, and will have relative little effect. This also means that they will show relative little membrane effect. Hence you can compare that with Bob's absorption page for E-400 mount. Can you still remove this tape without damaging these boards to much? You must make a choice here, otherwise you must frame them. Another possibility, but only if you treat these things with care is trying these long stiching needles where women stich sweaters, or whatever this is called, with. I don't know enough English to find the right words here. But if you stick from these 1 foot needles in the edge of these boards, and the density is high enough, you could use that thicker head of that needle to hang them on. But even then it should have been better that you spray glued these back sides together. Hence you brought yourself a bit in trouble not thinking about that in advance. It should have been easy to spray glue some perforated thin stuff in-between these 2 boards, sticking out a bit on the top side.
