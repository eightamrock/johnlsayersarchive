Phazinhead wrote:
After doing some measure in the room (the first pictures below), I decided to change monitor's support by cement block filled with sand, it will be less sensitive to resonances at low frequencies than the ones I built.
very good idea!
The new idea is on the picture below. It is a structure made of 2 cupboards on each side of the room, linked by 2 wood beams, so as to hang the corner bass trap and the clouds. In each cupboard there would have lots of vinyls discs and books, it would act as a kind of diffuser and also to damp a bit mid and high frequencies, moreover it is heavy and would make the cupboard less sensitive to resonate. In the lower part of the left cupboard there would have a soundproof case for the computer, and in the another one a case for storing a synthesizer and some music stuff.
Small rooms don't need diffusors. And no, books and disc don't make a *real* diffusor, more like a deflector.
Do you think it is a good idea to place this structure so close to the monitors ? No early reflexions problem ?
Yes, I think it's gonna be a problem. Check your first reflections. add wool on the cabinets if you want. Or move it to the back of the room. (or remove it as a whole)
I'm also thinking to put wood slats on the front of some bass traps so as to make them broadband absorber between 100Hz and 200Hz, and to save some highs.
Just use thick wool to extend the lower absorption. 100Hz isn't THAT low. aim for lower. Sure, slats will reflect highs and therefor work as a lowendfilter. Very good. And it looks cool too. Make the wood and gaps variating in width to make it more "broadband", else you'll end up with a "tuned" (spec. frq) device. Unless you're aiming for that. But prolly that's not the case.
On what bass trap would it be better to place those slats ? (I thought to the 2 wall/wall front corner bass traps)
On every BASS trap. Unless you want universal broadband trapping (all freq.) I'd remove as much as possible from your room and start with acoustically dressing up your room. Get a good sounding room. Than add cabins as you will. Use basstraps in any corner. Add broadband behind monitors adn on first reflection points (including your ceiling). Add a basstrap, if possible, at the opposite wall of the monitors (the backwall). standard approach, but a good one.

Ro6 July 2009#12
That looks more like it, it's a good start. Now, about the broadband absorbers. you don't want them slatted. Just put plain cloth in front so it will absorb over the whole (well not very low) frequency band. The basstraps, however, do need a low-end filter like slats, hardwood, plastic whatever. have you checked this sticky? viewtopic.php?f=3&t=10304 Basstraps need to be thick, the more volume in wool, the better low end trapping. broadband can do with 5cm (2"). Slanting them won't matter. Having those spaced from the wall a bit will.
