vonrock23 August 2008#5
I think that in your situation, you would be better off purchasing an electronic drum kit for practice. As a percussionist, I cringe at just the mention of them, as nothing beats an acoustic instrument (no pun intended). However, you will not be able to sound proof (isolate) a room in a house for very cheap. At least not cheaper than what a half decent electronic drum kit sells for. Further more, you plan to be moving soon so any money (and it will be a lot, just for the proper tools) you sink into this is thrown away. Dense insulation and Mass Loaded Vinyl is NOT going to soundproof this room any where near enough to contain a drum kit playing at 'full orchestral strength'. I certainly don't want to poo on your parade. I do want you playing your drums, and not wasting your time and money.

John Hile2 September 2008#7
I think vonrock nailed it. You can use that $500 for the kit and then when you get your own place where you can set up a better studio situation, you can use that electric kit as triggers for hip sounds (Just an idea).8) There are a couple of different ways to add decoupling to your single stud wall that will not take up as much space as a double stud (room within room). Though in your Pics, it looks like you have no room at all. Aside from adding another layer of mass to the existing surfaces there is nothing that I know of that you can "put up" on the wall that will attain any sound isolation. Whatever you put up on the wall (like foam panels) will help control the noise within the room, but they are not designed to stop sound from passing through them.
