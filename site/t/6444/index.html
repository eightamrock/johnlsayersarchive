Luftweg14 June 2006#2
I think it's very advisable to use conduits for the various electrical and electronic systems... This of course would mean SEPARATE conduits for 110/220 volt power, low-voltage power, audio (line levels, mikes, etc... perhaps even in separate conduits), and communications (phone, cable, etc) ... I know that the power would HAVE to be in its own conduit, and can't be shared with other systems (per CODE). It would probably be a very good idea to make sure the conduits do not touch or come even close to each other, to avoid introducing noise from the electrical and magnetic fields. If the conduits are large enough, and are constructed as much as possible from single bent (hickey-ed) conduit tubing (i.e., the insides are a smooth as possible to avoid snags), it would be fairly easy for new wires to be fished through later, if and when you need to add them. But conduits can leak sound too, so they need to be routed with isolation in mind, blocking sound that might go through the tube (air), and conducted by the tube (metal or PVC)... PVC is purportedly better than galv steel for sound, but I've heard that there is no hard evidence to back up that idea... There are other code requirements that you need to conform when working with the electrical system, including types and gauges of wires, sizes and type of conduits, distance from floor, etc... you need to research the specific code for your own locality... knightfly14 June 2006#4
Use soft-connected joints in walls (rubber sleeves, or canvas) to minimise shorting out the wall, and at least two 90 degree bends; then, after all wires are in, stuff both ends with big handfuls of insulation and caulk around the wires. Occasionally these conduits can act like big "organ pipes" and cause unwanted resonances, besides transferring sound between rooms... Steve
