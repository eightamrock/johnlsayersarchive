knightfly18 December 2004#102
Jeff, I doubt you'll have ANY problems due to IMPACT noise for reasons you already stated - however, I would NOT recommend ANY layers on a floor that are only 3/8" thick, other than a possible MLV or similar layer which is NOT the final course - for any floated floor, high mass is needed in order to keep resonance low, and also 3/8 ply won't span any normal joist spacing, so shouldn't be used as a layer either. Mainly though, the mass is the main thing - for this reason, I would stay with 5/8 or thicker materials (the 1/2" gypsum inner layer is OK though) Steve

knightfly29 December 2004#105
Does the back wall already have house wrap (like Tyvek) on the outside? Where you're putting the R19, it should be UN-faced. The entire wall system should have only one vapor and one air barrier; the tyvek outer for air barrier, which will allow things to dry, and the inner VAPOR barrier (like the 6 mil poly) which should be right underneath your inner gypsum, between it and the studs. If you're using double frames like that, I see no reason to hassle with RC - it will probably HURT your performance a bit, but definitely won't help. RC is for ceilings where you can't use separate framing members, and walls with single frames that don't allow any decoupling. If you can spare the floor space, I would put the inner roxul in a separate frame at least a foot inside the inner gypsum, and fill between it and the gypsum with unfaced pink batts; you'll get a more even bass response at the rear of the room, and just generally a smoother sounding room... Steve

knightfly29 March 2005#117
When the door is down, how tight are the gaps between individual, hinged panels of the door? Also, gap size around the perimeter of the door? What I'm thinking is, use stick-on weather strip between each panel of the door, lowering the door a panel at a time and inserting the weatherstrip into the cracks while they're open at the curve of the door track; then, once the door's shut, fasten from the inside with some long screws into the side frames, and across the top of the door if possible, then caulk in place. Remove the parts of the door track that protrude further into the room, so you don't have to work around them for the ceiling (unless it won't be in the way) - then, as you panel the inner frame, fill the gap between the door and the first wall panel ENTIRELY with blown cellulose insulation - this will damp the door panel, provide your heat AND sound insulation, and also damp the inside wall panel. Cellulose has been tested and found to meet or exceed sound performance of just about anything out there. Because the outer door is relatively light, 3 layers of 5/8 wallboard on the inner frame will be necessary. The extra depth of air gap/insulation will help negate the lighter outer leaf of the barrier. Total hermetic seals EVERYWHERE of course... Steve

knightfly10 May 2005#121
As humid as the whole east coast is, I would tend to stay away from steel framing and possibly even gypsum in some cases - I recently read a report from one of the Canadian building sites (IIRC) about water retention in different construction; the level of water retention allowed before damage occurred, using a 2000 sf house, was 5 gallons total for gypsum/steel framing, 50 gallons for wood frame/wood paneling, and 500 gallons for all masonry construction. Steel studs transmit heat better, so get colder in winter which causes more condensation; this collects in insulation, and causes the studs to corrode. Check out these pages for more - http://www.maconline.org/designalert.PDF http://www.buildingscience.com/resource ... efault.htm HTH... Steve
