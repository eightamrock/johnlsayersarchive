The cement board is higher mass, so better - everything else will help lower resonance, except the concept of laying the joists flat. You want a larger air cavity for lower resonance. You might get away with 2x4's on edge with a concrete slab, but with the lighter floor the extra 2 inches will help lower resonance. knightfly24 September 2004#54
I just "faked out" the Insul calculator (modified ceiling calculations) and ran two different scenarios - one, using 2x6's (defaults to standard house insulation, no choice) sitting on 6" concrete, resilient mounts, two layers 3/4" OSB - the other, using 2x4's, adding a layer of 5/8" gypsum between the two layers of OSB - M-A-M resonance is 2 hZ lower (35 hZ) using the 2x6's without the center gypsum layer. If you do BOTH the 2x6's and the 5/8" center gypsum layer, M-A-M drops to 30 hZ - I don't have weight specs on the cement board, I assumed it to be nearly twice the mass of gypsum and recalculated for 2x6, 2 3/4" OSB, and a center layer of 5/8" cement board - drops to around 27 hZ. With ANY of these, you will definitely want to overstuff the insulation a fair amount to damp the panels - this won't completely kill the boominess but I've yet to hear complaints from any of our members who've used enough insulation... Steve

knightfly4 December 2004#74
You want your floor above the pucks to be essentially a "monolith" so regardless of using screws or nails, use construction adhesive at every joint to eliminate buzzes and rattles. IF you use screws, I'd use long "deck" screws - no pre-drilling necessary because they're slim, and they're about the only thing I'm aware of that's long enough to fasten 2x lumber together. Screws will take longer than nails, assuming power fasteners (either screw gun or pneumatic nailer) - by hand (in both cases) screws will REALLY take longer :cry: Because of the construction adhesive, either way should be fine - just remember that for nails, it's better to be standing on the material when you nail it (tighter construction), fasten while the construction adhesive is still wet, and nails should be "toenailed" (every other one slanted in opposite direction, along the joist) for more holding power. If using a nailer, finish each nail with a hammer to ensure they're "sunk" below surface, and also to tighten up the joint between flooring and frame - nailers are so fast, they don't "pull" the material up against the frame the same as hand hammering will. Multiple floor layers should be at 90 degrees layer to layer, and cracks caulked before the next layer. Don't caulk til just prior to adding the next layer, so the next layer will flatten the caulk bead and pull down tighter. Remember we're building a "monolith" here, NOT an air-separated multi-leaf barrier. Hope that helps... Steve
