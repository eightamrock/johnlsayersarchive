AndrewMc28 July 2004#2
floating the floors is a question of expense, you certainly will get better isolation by floating the floors, mainly between rooms inside the building, but of course it is more work and expense. If I had to choose one room to float it would be probably the tracking room, but you could always just float the drums within the room. I can tell you though that even without floating floors - if you build the walls correctly there isn't any chance that your neighbors will hear anything 100 feet away - they wouldn't hear anything even if they were 20 feet away stood on their porch.

knightfly28 July 2004#6
Strangely enough, absorption in rooms does almost NOTHING for isolation; in some cases, the end result is MORE sound leakage, because we tend to "turn it up" more when it sounds good :cry: As to float/no float, I agree with Andrew on this - your neighbors probably won't care one way or the other unless you really get carried away - ideally, you'll be usually mixing at around 80-85 dB, which normal house walls and your 100 foot distance would render pretty inoffensive. However, when recording drums you may hit as much as 110 dB, which WILL carry; especially the lower freqs from kick drums and even low toms. For this reason, and assuming you don't have a problem with outside noise getting in, it makes the most sense to float the room where the drums will be. Besides improving inside to outside isolation, this will also come a lot closer to letting you set mics by listening to your speakers instead of trying to do it with headphones. Keep in mind, too, that if you only float one room you'll have a "step" to deal with somewhere - and floating both the CR and the tracking area will definitely let you get away from using headphones (other than for the talent, of course) Hope that helped... Steve

AndrewMc28 July 2004#8
I'm a bit lost on what you plan to do with the walls. When you say you are going to stagger the studs - which studs? I'm assuming the external wall is already in place. Are you going to stagger some studs out from this wall? Is the wall sill 2x6 or 2x4? Unless you have a mass-air space-mass then you are going to be totally disssapointed with the sound isolation. Your drums played loud will eventually result in the cops being called by one of your neighbors. Unless the walls are good then floating the floor is a moot point. Floating the floor will help stop low end transmitting through the structure, but it won't do much to stop the sound reaching your neigbours. You can always float the drums, but I'd put the main focus not on floating the floors but on the integrity of the walls.

AndrewMc28 July 2004#10
You can fill the gap with the fluffly insulation you can buy at home depot. Remember to stop sound you need mass-air-mass. You can get a decent mass on the inside with internal drywall - but the weak spot is the mass of the external wall facing. I doubt you'll end up getting the 48db reduction you expect. I think also you will find the step between rooms a royal buzz kill. If it was me - I would skip floating any floors, put the drums on a floated heavy riser and instead of doing the staggered studs I would add a 2nd internal wall while also trying to increase the mass of the outer wall by putting drywall between the studs. You'll end up with a well isolated building that you will enjoy being in and can play music in late at night. I don't think you are going to get that with the current wall design.
