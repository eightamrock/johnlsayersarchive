sharward17 November 2006#2
You are wise to forego floating. Your observations are absolutely correct that floating rooms/floors are usually built and designed incorrectly and are extremely overrated in their supposed importance, especially when built over slab-on-grade construction.
Sideshow wrote:
I was planning to lay a plastic vapour barrier above concrete as well just in any case. I also plan on using laminate and the rubber matting that is recommended to use with it - directly on concrete.
Most (if not all) laminate flooring underlayments are "two-in-one" vapor barrier as well as a spongy shock absorber. Some are "three-in-one" and include antifungal properties. Thus, there's no need to lay separate vapor barrier and underlayment. It may even be detrimental in some way.
Sideshow wrote:
[b]1. As we bolt our frames to the concrete floor - if we find minor inconsistencies in the concrete level can we simply fill these with silicone?? (I was thinking of pasting the underneath of each frame "plate" with acoustic rated silicone so any anomolies auto fill - might be painful/messy on vapour barrier)
Actually, if you think about it, you've got gaping holes between the studs. ;-) What's critical isn't so much a perfect seal between soleplate and slab, but rather, a perfect resilient seal where the edge of the drywall meets the slab. To illustrate this, suppose your soleplates were a foot off the concrete. (Absurd? Yes... But go with me here.) Who cares? Just make sure the drywall is sealed where it meets the floor. The sound doesn't care how tight the seal is between soleplate and slab.
Sideshow wrote:
2. Does anybody have experiences they can share with laminate and underlay directly on concrete in a studio? (has it fared well in respect to transmission loss between CR and ISO rooms?)
I would think that your choice of flooring won't have any impact whatsoever on transmission loss between adjacent spaces. I plan to just etch stain my concrete slab. It's cheaper than laminate, is more durable, and it looks awesome, almost like marble! 8) --Keith :mrgreen:

I also want to say that I have a very nice laminate throughout half of our 2,000 square foot home, and it's wonderful, so you can't go wrong with that either. Although, I will say this -- the quality of underlayment you use is very important! The house had a lesser quality laminate/underlayment combo when we moved in. It was OK, but it wasn't very nice to walk on and it had a bit of a "clappy" sound to it underfoot. A plumbing disaster shortly thereafter ruined it and we had to have it all replaced. The difference in quality and walking feel is extraordinary. I was so excited when I walked on it the first time -- I said, "WHOAH, this is what this type of flooring SHOULD feel like." --Keith :mrgreen:
