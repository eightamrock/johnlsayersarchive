Steve ------------------------------------------------------------- *Original Post* Hello All, My name is Steve, and I have been reading all I can at this site and others for a month or so. Based on what I have learned, I have come up with some ideas for a home studio setup in my basement. Here are my details: What I do and want I am a recovering attorney who recently decided to get a real job, and, to my amazement, has had some success selling songs and recording/producing others' music. I live in Rye, NY and currently rent a pre-constructed live/control space in Williamsburg, Brooklyn. I hope to build a small space where I can record my own songs at home in as high a quality environment as possible, in addition to my main commercial space. I generally track everything myself for my own songs. I use V-drums rather than an acoustic kit, and I DI my bass, but do use a relatively loud guitar amp, acoustic guitar and sing, etc. I have the V-drums in a separate area, where I track onto a notebook and then transfer to my main workstation, so these are not a factor in the plans, below. Location/Environment I live in a fairly densely populated suburb of NYC. My nearest neighbors are 30ft away or so. I am planning on building my workspace in my basement, which is made of brick and is underground, except for the side that faces the nearest neighbor (of course), where there is a small Andersen casement window. I plan to work from here at least 2 days a week, and whenever the mood strikes me off-hours. I have two small and rather noisy kids who are home during the late AM/early afternoon with their nanny. Part of the basement, separated by a standard interior stud & sheetrock wall from the space I will be using, is a laundry room. My building skills My father was (he's retired) a contractor who restored antique homes. I learned from him while I was young and have quite a bit of experience in most aspects of home building, but no experience when it comes to acoustic construction, other than as described below. In addition, my father has most generously offered his skills in the construction of the space. What I have so far (Please see first image, below) Before I discovered this amazing forum, I built what I thought could be a control room and small amp closet/vocal booth in the space I have available. The control room space is a rectangle that is 7' high x 9.8' wide x 13.3' long. The purpose of these dimensions were to fit into the space I have and to try and get a Louden 1 ratio, or close to it. I now know that the philosophy of some forum members eschews room modes, but the room is already built, and I hope the dimensions are OK. The Amp Closet/Vocal Booth is a trapezoidal space built to no particular size (height is the same 7', dictated by ceiling height), but aimed at maximizing the space available (please see first image, below). Both rooms are floated on their own independent platforms framed of 2x4s on neoprene pads, with foam board insulation between the joists (I live in a flood area, and though the basement is waterproofed, I decided to use a non-absorptive form of insulation just in case). The deck consists of two layers of tongue and groove wafer board with 1/8" mass loaded vinyl in between, with laminate flooring. The walls of both rooms are 2x4 double-leaf with 3.5" insulation (Ultra Touch) between the joists, two layers of 5/8" gypsum on inside leaf mounted on iso-clips/HAT channel with Green Glue between sheets and acoustic caulk around perimeter/in any gaps, plus a layer of 1.2" homosote and 1/2" gypsum on the outer leaf, which is also GG'd & caulked. The ceiling is a flat, 7' 2x4 frame ceiling right now, but has not been insulated sheetrocked, etc., so there is some wiggle room there. Luckily, there was an HVAC duct and electrical taps in the space I am using, so I already have an HVAC duct and wires into the room through a conduit with as little damage to the leaves as possible (any holes insulated/puttied and caulked). My audio wiring is also in an unattached conduit between the rooms with as little damage to the leaves as possible. This was done by a licensed pro with me there, so code is met. What I would like to build (Please see the second image, below, and the linked sketchup file) I am hoping to build interior false walls in the control room to create soffit mounted speakers, slot resonator walls and a hanger bass trap in back. Despite the fact that this design would leave me with less overall space than applying foam panels, I would prefer this setup for a couple of reasons. One, it looks and "feels" nice, and that is very important for me creatively. Two, I think using the design ideas I have picked up on this forum and John's tutorials would create a better sounding performance space for acoustic guitar and other instruments that I would track in the control room, as opposed to a foam dampened room, which I have tried and not particularly liked. Third, and most importantly, in the end, based on what I think I know from reading on this site, and elsewhere, I believe this design would sound good and perform the function of a critical listening room fairly well. However, I defer to the MUCH more experienced opinions of forum members in this regard, and am very willing to alter my plans based on sound advice. I honestly am not sure what to do with the amp closet. My initial thoughts were to use a lot of foam and not worry too much, as I close mic amps anyway. However, I have been playing around more with using distant condenser mikes and close-up Royer ribbons, which seem to pick of the room more, so any suggestionsfor this very small space would be great. Budget I would like to keep it under US$10K, not including what I have already done. Anyway, here are the images and a link to a sketchup file (http://idisk.mac.com/s.kilroy//Public/SWStudio.skp). Please NB - I have never used Sketchup, and am really quite bad at it. Please assume that my carpentry will not be as sloppy as the plan would indicate. I greatly appreciate your input, including door and ceiling suggestions. Thanks. Steve K.

Soundman20205 October 2010#4
Hi Steve, and welcome! Great first post, too! It's nice when a newcomer reads the rules and follows them! :) One comment in your description caught my attention:
Both rooms are floated on their own independent platforms framed of 2x4s on neoprene pads, with foam board insulation between the joists (I live in a flood area, and though the basement is waterproofed, I decided to use a non-absorptive form of insulation just in case). The deck consists of two layers of tongue and groove wafer board with 1/8" mass loaded vinyl in between, with laminate flooring.
To me, that sounds like you have potential problems there. My bet is that those floors are not actually floating at all, and are most likely acting like large resonant cavities, that could cause you problems. They are also eating up precious inches of room height. Is there any chance they could come out? Are the walls resting on top of those floors, or are the standalone structures? I know you did that before you came here, but you might want to read through this thread to get an idea of what floating a floor is all about: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 From what you say, you don't seem to have anywhere near enough mass on them, and didn't do the math to ensure that they are in fact floating. And you probably don't need them anyway, from an acoustic point of view.
The walls of both rooms are 2x4 double-leaf with 3.5" insulation (Ultra Touch) between the joists, two layers of 5/8" gypsum on inside leaf mounted on iso-clips/HAT channel
That sounds like it might be a bit of overkill! If you have separate stud frams for each leaf (which seems to be what you are saying), then you don't also need clips and hat channel. The purpose of clips and hat channel is to isolate the leaf, but if the leaf is on its own stud frame then it is already isolated: isolating it again won't accomplish much. But from what you said, I understand that these walls are already built, so there's no need to undo that! I'm just commenting, FWIW...
The ceiling is a flat, 7' 2x4 frame ceiling right now, but has not been insulated sheetrocked, etc., so there is some wiggle room there.
Is that ceiling resting ONLY on the inner leaf walls? No mechanical connections to the outer leaf at all? Not even a single nail bridging the gap? If so, then you are in good shape. If not, then now is the time to fix that. Have you considered building the ceiling inside-out? It will gain you a few more precious inches of height. Your intention of doing speaker soffits is an excellent idea. It will improve your room greatly. - Stuart -

What do people think of adding another layer of drywall (sheetrock) to the outside, thus creating an inside-out wall to save space? I think that this would be easy to do, and wouldn't it alleviate the triple-leaf effect?
It would still be three-leaf, but if you put enough mass on it you can probably compensate. However, you'll need to provide accurate details of how the original outer wall is built (what each layer is, how thick it is, etc.), and an accurate measurement of the gap between that wall and the "homasote" wall that you have right now, in order to figure out your MSM resonance (theoretically) and your isolation (theoretically). How much isolation do you need, in terms of decibels? How loud are you? That window is also a concern: Can you seal that up? - Stuart -
