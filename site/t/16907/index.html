Paul88114 September 2011#11
Then here's a top tip #1 - before you insert the tube into your gun, examine the rear metal sliding seal cap inside the tube and make sure it isn't damaged in any way i.e. dented. If it is, as best you can push the edges of the metal cap back into contact with the inside of the tube. That way, it will ensure no caulk pushes out. Top tip #2 - Wear silicon gloves and DO NOT use your fingers to smooth or touch the calk after you have applied it. Use a putty knife instead if you need to shape your joint, remove any excess etc. After using a tube (two at the most) , you will need to wash your gloves, knife and applicator gun in water. Be warned - this stuff gets everywhere and looks a damn mess when applied. I found that I used a pair of gloves for every four tubes. When dried it creates a great silicon-like joint that is very robust and quite hard - but flexible. I used 900ml tubes.

Soundman202014 September 2011#13
Very useful tips! I'll add another one: If you are going to do a lot of caulking ( :!: ) then get a pneumatic caulking gun and a small compressor. It saves a lot of time, and a lot of mess. It uses air pressure to force the caulk out of the tube, and you can control the flow rate very easily and very accurately by adjusting the pressure. Also, with good caulking guns, as soon as you release the trigger, the flow stops dead, with little to no "ooze" afterwards. That's my $0.02. - Stuart -
