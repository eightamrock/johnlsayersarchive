guitardad722 May 2006#3
Hey leftweg, I've been thinking about this question, did some searching & asking... since you bumped it... The only thing I found (and sorry I'm in a rush, don't have time to refind and post link) is that you can have a sample of the wood tested by a lab to determine species. Other wise you should presume (or atleast I would)it's #2 grade or better... most #2 grade wood seems to have similiar load weights so you may be able to do some guestimates while you wait. Other option is ask your neighbors or seek down some carpenters, architects, contractors or other "pros" who have worked on renovations to houses in your area and ask them. That's how I found out what species my trusses are made of. Other thing's to mention and I'm not a pro, I've not seen (yet) anything other than a 2x4 rated as #3.... every other larger peice of lumber seems to be #2 or better. Most floors/cielings seem to have a 10PSF rating min, with some rooms 20PSF.... but that is current code and may not apply to older houses. Hope this helps a bit.... but remember I'm not a pro and the end recomandation especially for your ceiling/floor joists (the safest one atleast) is to hire a structural engineer and he will be able to tell you exactly what dead/live load you can have with your floors. Marc
