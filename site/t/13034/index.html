Soundman202013 May 2009#2
The first thing that comes to mind is that your speakers are firing across the short axis of the room, instead of down the long axis. Second thing: the room is not symmetrical. Third thing: your doors are in the wrong place. To fix these two, you'd need to get rid of that white thing (bed? sofa?), then move your desk backwards and rotate it 90° to the left (anticlockwise), and seal up the alcove space (where the desk is now) somehow, perhaps with some framing and drywall. That would make it more workable acoustically, as the speakers would be firing down the long axis and the room would be more symmetrical. But it would still leave the issue of the doors being right where you need to place your bass trapping. That's the least serious of the three, and there are probably work-arounds for that too. You need symmetry in order to get a good stereo image. You cannot mix properly without that. You need the speakers firing down the long axis in order to get the reflections off the back wall as far away in time and space as possible. You need serious bass trapping in the rear corners (and as many other corners as possible) because bass trapping is much, much more effective in corners. I also don't see any provision for HVAC in there, and no windows either. That's another issue you'd have to deal with. My 0.02 - Stuart -
