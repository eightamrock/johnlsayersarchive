I've thought about adding a layer of green glue/sheet rock to the beam in the hope that this might help dampen it. Anyone have any ideas on this? I don't want to make the post any larger than it is. Cram20 March 2008#2
To address your beam - more detail could be provided for the span needed from wall to wall or wall to column to beam. In my basement room, I boxed in a column support and used it as support for headers that spanned to the walls. The beam is never touched by any framing or drywall. It just stands on the floor around the pole and then the opposing walls. Walls If you are concerned with the walls being installed on the laminate, here's an option: Snap the lines for the walls where the baseplates would run. THen take a strong utility knife and cut along that line. Take out the laminate down to the slab. Then lay your baseplate down for the frame and nail/screw down to the foundation floor. With multiple layers of sheetrock you may be putting up - you'll have wiggle room for how sloppy the line you cut and dug down to the floor. Meaning that it would extend off the plate and cover your edge. Just a thought.

Vapor - One thing I learned from Rod on another studio build here was that vapor barrier concerns are at the point of where the heat combats with the cold on opposing sides. So if a room is hotter than the basement wall (as it will be) then the barrier goes against the studs between the wood and the sheetrock. that way moisture to the sheetrock is prevented when condensation occurs.
