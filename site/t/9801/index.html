brianrcdd25 November 2007#1
Greetings everyone, from a previous lurker! I am about to embark on a studio build adventure. To that end, I started researching all that I could and quickly found this site to be a definitive reference for most aspects of such a project. I have been spending a couple of months absorbing all the information I can (that I did not already know), and quickly decided to buy Rod's book to add to the knowledge pile. Quick facts about myself: I currently have a two-room studio in the lower half of our split level home. This was a great opportunity to find out first-hand how NOT to do it, and why. About $5,000 in education - not a bad deal, really, compared to current tuitions. Of course, this was not a serious build, and was definitely before the bulk of my research. I am a network engineer-turned-regional manager for a major corporation. I also have some project manager experience in my past. All of these things will come in handy in this project. I am NOT a builder in any sense of the word. My wife and I actually did do a hands-on construction of a house a while back. While the house turned out fine (with professional help along the way), it reinforced some words I live by - find out what you are good at, and do it. Find out what you are not good at, and don't do it anymore if you can possibly help it. I spent many a year in my teens and twenties as a musician, out on the road and in the studio. I left the business planning to come back and build a small studio to create in and enjoy in my retirement. At a recent point in time, I asked myself why I was waiting for that! So, here are some facts I know you like to have posted: 1. New construction, ground-up. This will be an addition to a new (existing) home we are buying. 2. Area is residential/commercial mix. Have not yet checked into zoning yet, but the house across the street is also a major collectibles/antiques storefront operation. On my to-do list. 3. Neighbors are not close. The property is actually about 3 lots, on a corner. Not a busy street at all (no cars for hours on end). The nearest home is 150+ feet away. 4. The type of music recorded is really quite varied, but I tend to gravitate toward the quieter end of the scale - acoustic, orchestrated via virtual instruments, vocal group, Christian, etc. 5. Based on 3 and 4 I would say I am interested in good performance all-around, but I put inter-room isolation ahead of concerns outside of the structure. 6. I have found I prefer things a little on the dead side rather than the live side, adding acoustical properties electronically. 7. My current situation suffers greatly from cancellation on the low end. I have a sweet spot of about 1 cubic foot in which to stick my head. I have vowed that will not happen again. 8. I plan to complete the structure without any huge preconceived notions about room treatment, but with the knowledge a certain amount will be needed. It seems to make sense to do the best possible room build, but build with options that will allow the necessary treatment after construction is complete. Then test, treat, repeat as necessary. 9. Not much space is needed in the control room for racks of gear. I'm pretty happy with the current equipment setup - Protools LE, 003R audio IO, Control 24 desk, ALP-5 monitors, Samson subwoofer (long story on why I needed that). I have a rack mount, water-cooled PC that is near dead quiet. I don't use a lot of outboard hardware, preferring to mix in the box. I find that modern plugins can do the job if you put the right touch on them, and refuse to invest thousands in outboard hardware just because some people expect to see it. Yes, I will buy some good pres and some better A/D, but the building comes first. 10. Because I mix in the box, I like to have a lot of screen real estate. I currently use 4 monitors, two above and one to each side of the C24. This has been a bit of a design problem, because of the the potential to block both the window and the monitors. But it should be less of an issue with a larger room. 11. Again, I am not a builder, so I intend to contract the work out. We are currently speaking with several to find the right one. I understand fully the caveats Rod points out in his book regarding this arrangement. In my professional life, I work with them all the time. Left unwatched, they can devastate your project in short order. Trust, but verify. 12. Current budget for the construction falls into the $50k to $60k range. Somebody tell me now if this is unrealistic, but I should be able to make that work. The intent is to construct within current income/budget (which is actually not too limiting), so as not to immediately be dependent on client acquisition to recover costs. Initially, this will be for my own enjoyment, and not-for-hire. my long-range plan is to build up reputation and client base thru gratis (and near gratis) work over several years if necessary, to create a viable business that can provide income on down the road. Using this methodology represents a fairly low-risk approach. Sketchup/Design Notes 13. I chose to start with a design concept that has been presented in this forum many times. I have seen many variations on the theme, but the basic idea seemed like a good place to start. Lots of interesting angles that are pleasing to the eye and quite functional from an acoustical perspective. 14, As shown in the drawing, the space will be 24 by 30 foot, on concrete slab. The attached house is not on slab, thus the step up at the door leading to the house. 15. The sketchup file uses many layers so you can turn parts on or turn off for visibility. 16. The walls are intended to be 2 layers of 5/8" sheetrock. Walls to be properly insulated, but not shown. 17. HVAC is not shown. I understand this is a huge part of the design, but I just haven't gotten there yet. Assume it will be a new system, not connected to the house at all. I am (re)reading Rod's chapter on this feverishly as we speak. 17. The framing (or any other) layer is not intended to reflect actual construction techniques. (told you I wasn't a builder :lol: ) But I do want to be able to use it as a tool to explain basic concepts to the contractors who get that deer-in-the-headlights look when it comes to acoustical architecture. So, that's the current state of affairs. I would like to hear any comments, and will field any questions. I anticipate this will take a while, but my goal is to finish in a year. So with that in mind, the design needs to start coming into focus pretty soon. I consider the folks who frequent this forum to be among the best and brightest (and the most helpful I have ever seen), which is why I am laying it out here for all to see.

gullfo26 November 2007#4
i think the design looks really good and to Steve's point you just need to be sure the reflections off the doors are trapped so you don't get a significant volume of reflections from behind or within the booths. if you decide to add 5.1 to the mix, you will also want to consider that maybe single glass doors would reduce the target from the back speakers and still provide enough visibility into each of the side booths as well as increasing the isolation values (although not necessarily making it less expensive since good 50 STC doors can be several thousands of dollars each...) put plenty of bass trapping in the CR and the drum room. as far as commercial - think ADA compliance in your design - do the stairs meet the criteria? sliding doors need flush mounting with the floor and narrow guide tracks (Rod has some good references in the construction forum threads like the MeowMix thread).

You notice which item is first on the list. :wink: The phases will probably equate to year 1, year 3, and year 5, if my financial situation remains as it is.
