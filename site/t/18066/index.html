Hi "nemo", and welcome to the forum! :)
Here a sketchup test with many possibilities
"G" is your best option, but there are still some problems with it. The reasons for selecting this one are mainly because of room symmetry (which is very important), and also to have the speakers aiming down the longest axis of the room, instead of across the short axis, as some of your options show.. However, all of your options show the speakers resting on the desk, which is a big mistake. The speakers should be placed in stands behind the desk, and the stands need to be very massive (heavy). You also show the speakers angled inwards at about 40°, which places your mix position (the place where your head will be when mixing) at about 25% of the room depth. That's a really bad position, and also the angle is too high. It would be better to have your mix position at about 38% of the room depth (about 2m from the front wall) and angle the speakers more like 30°, then move them a bit further apart until they are both facing exactly at your ears. That should get your layout correct. Then you can start planning the acoustic treatment for that room. - Stuart -

Soundman202012 January 2013#4
So I made a new version in Sketchup:
That looks much better. There is only one problem that I see with that: the point where the axes of the two speakers intercept. It is your EARS that should be at the 38% location, not the intersection. Since your ears are on the outside of your head, you need to move the speakers about 20cm further apart so that the intersection is behind your head. Also, don't go crazy about that "38%" thing: it is not a rule, just a theoretical good starting point. Once you have the system set up, then move around until you find the best position. It probably won't be at 38%. Many engineers find that it is better to be a bit closer to the front wall. Others prefer to be a bit further back.
Did I must choose the emplacement of all furnitures and gear (amps) before thinking of the acoustics treatements?
Not necessary. Just the speakers, chair and desk. With those in the corret place, you can then position the basic treatment correctly, and when that is in place then you can think about where to put any other equipment that you might need.
How must I process? Early Reflection first? Bass trap?
Both! :) Put large bass traps in both front corners and both rear corners of the room, as much as possible: The best type is the "sperchunk" design. Search this forum for that word, and you'll find out how to make those. Very simple, and very effective. Then put two large panels of absorption at your first reflection points on the side walls, another two between the speakers and the front wall, one on the ceiling directly above the desk at the first reflection point up there, and finally cover the entire back wall with absorption, at least 10cm thick (15cn is better). That's the basic idea for treating a room like yours. - Stuart -

Soundman20201 May 2013#10
First, i changed my decision about the placement of the desk in the room, due to conveniant issues.
Unfortunately, that's a REALLY bad idea. Your room is no longer symmetrical like that, so your mixes will not translate well. It is critical to have good symmetry around the mix position: the left half of the room must be a mirror-image of the right half.
I added a cabinet and a little sofa
The cabinet is OK, but NOT in that position! For the same reason as above, symmetry. Also, that's a very large flat surface on the sie of that cabinet: it will reflect sound back to your ears, and distort the stereo image. If you must have that cabinet, then put it in the center of the back wall.
I begin thinking of the skyline diffusor. I need to learn some things about their placement
The only correct placement for a skyline diffuser in a small room like that, is "not inside the room"! :) That room is way too small to be able to use numeric-sequence diffusers. You need at least 3 meters between the diffuser and the ears of anyone doing critical listening. - Stuart -
