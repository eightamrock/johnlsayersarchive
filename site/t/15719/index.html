Hey Mike, Welcome! :) There are a few general "rules of thumb" about laying out your studio and positioning your speakers and head correctly, and there's a rough procedure from getting you from where you are now, to your final design. First, your room ratio: That's the ratio of height to length to width, normally expressed as something like "1 : 1.14 : 1.28". Your ratio right now is 1 : 1.52 : 1.68, which isn't bad but could be improved. So first question: Do you need to isolate your room ("soundproof" it)? If so, how much isolation do you need? The reason I ask is because the additional inner-leaf walls that you would need to isolate your room can be designed to give you a better ratio, and a better layout. But that costs money, and you didn't mention your budget (although you did say that you are prepared to do whatever is necessary). So first order of business would be to decide if you are going to isolate your room and then treat it, or just treat it as-is. So let us know if you are willing to build more walls inside that room, and if you have the budget to do so. Also, tell us about how that room is built right now, and what is around it. For example, is the floor concrete? Wood? Do you have noisy neighbors close by, or does your noise bother them? What is above you? What are the walls and ceiling made of? How loud are you? Etc. And the biggie: What is your final goal? In other words, why are you building this? What do you want to achieve? In other words, give us as much info as you can so that we can better understand where you are and where you need to go. - Stuart -

Soundman20209 March 2011#6
I would love to isolate and then treat it but I just learned that anything I do will have to be able to be taken out if and when I leave here (ugh).
Ouch! Not nice. That creates a real problem for isolating, since anything you do to isolate means "damaging" the existing room to a certain extent. One option you have is to take off the existing drywall from the walls and ceiling, beef up the other side from inside (between the studs/joists), put resilient channel (RC) on the studs/joists, fill the cavities with rockwool, and put two layers of 5/8" drywall back on top. That would leave the room looking pretty much the same as now, except about 2 inches smaller in all dimensions (due to the thickness of the RC plus the extra drywall), and it would improve your isolation quite a bit. Not optimal, but probably worthwhile. And you would not have to do anything when you leave. You would still need to build plugs for your windows and replace your doors with solid cores, plus add good seals to them, but it would work quite well. Another option is to take off the existing drywall on walls and ceiling, then build a proper "room in a room", but that would mean major re-working when you leave: You'd have to take out your "room-in-a-room", and put up more drywall on the original studs, to leave the room back the way it is now. It's a lot more effort and expense, but will get you much better isolation, and a much better room too, as you'd be able to move walls around and angle them as necessary. The big issue in both cases is the floor: wooden floor probably means that you can't add much more weight to it safely, so your options are very limited. You'll need a structural engineer to take a look at that floor and tell you how much extra mass it can support, before you start. - Stuart -

gullfo9 March 2011#8
put the mix desk centered window on the 13'6" wall (left) even though this goes against the "shoot down the long ways" guidance. treat your walls and ceiling on first reflection points with 4" absorbers. super-chunks in the corners (or using a 4" thick rigid insulation spanning the corner - 34" wide and pink insulation behind it). on the wall behind your mix position, build out one of John's 12" deep absorber cabinets. on the rest of the room - scattered 4" thick absorber on the ceiling and walls to bring in the room "reverberation" to the desired level (probably around 500ms for all frequencies). put your monitors on stands close to the wall. a heavy drape over the window will help dampen that a bit and allow some light when needed.

Soundman202010 March 2011#10
why not shoot down the long ways?
That would be the normal and recommended way of doing it, yes, but in your case you really don't have that option: the door, window and closet all mess up your options there. If you are not going to isolate, I'd go with Glenn's suggestion and just treat it as-is. The only thing I'd add to what Glenn said is replace the carpet with laminate flooring. Much better acoustically. So your desk would be facing the window, centered on that wall, with your speakers on each side of the window (or on the center line of the room if the window isn't centered), up close to the wall on very massive decoupled stands, about 1.7 m apart (67"), with absorbers behind them, up against the wall. Your chair will be about 1.4 m from the front wall, and the speakers will be angled roughly 30° so that they are aimed just a little behind your head. The idea is that the axes of the speakers intersect about 7" behind your head. The stands should be sized so that the acoustic axis of the speaker sits about 1.2m above the floor (47"). That gets your basic geometry right. The numbers are not carved in stone, so you can adjust them a bit to get the best sound, but they do give you an excellent starting point. The rest of the basic treatment is exactly as Glenn said. Then when you are done with all that, run an analysis of the room with REW, and post the results here so we can see what else might need doing. The above would be the best approach if you aren't going to isolate or build walls, and I don't see how you can build any walls in there, unless you go the whole hog and do the "room in a room" approach. - Stuart -
