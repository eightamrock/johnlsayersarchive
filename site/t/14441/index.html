Hello Studio Geniuses, I live in Pompano Beach, Florida. US This is my second post. Been all over this site trying to find any other alternative to Green Glue. I am working on junkyard standards. I have a very small isolation booth project in my bedroom which will be for mixing. The dimensions are depressingly small. I am ready to put on my second layer of drywall but I am out of money. Green glue is not an option for me. on this page viewtopic.php?t=598 it says 5. Fastening a second layer of wallboard to a first layer, when the first is mounted on WOOD studs, should only be done using 4" wide stripes of slightly thinned joint compound over the stud area, then fastening with Type G laminatings screws approximately 2-3" away from the studs and parallel to them, on approximately 12" centers. This allows the two layers to act as separate panels, which maintains each panel's coincidence dip separate from the other. It also keeps any fasteners from penetrating BOTH layers of wallboard AND entering the WOOD studs. NOT doing this will lose about 9-10 dB of isolation due to flanking through the fasteners and into the studs. 5A - replaces 5, above - Fastening a second layer of wallboard to a first layer, when the first layer is mounted on WOOD studs: First, if building a SINGLE frame with wallboard on both sides, it's STRONGLY recommended to use Resilient Channel on ONE of the sides of the frame – otherwise, you lose several dB of isolation at mid and higher frequencies (above 100 hZ typically) – follow the rules for mounting wallboard to RC on this side – no screws in line with studs so you don't accidentally "short out" the RC. Fastening a second layer when there is NOT RC under, the most practical way is to mount the first layer using half as many screws as normal, same with any following layers EXCEPT the outer layer – this one should have full fastening schedule used, and with long enough screws to penetrate all layers and solidly fasten to the studs. Then is taken out in 5a. So is the 4" wide strips of joint compound a solution for me or was this not effective and that is why it was removed? Also could I use non hardening caulk? If so, which of the two would be best. Thank you very much in advance. Paul

Soundman20208 June 2010#22
Then drywall layer 1. Then more foam tape (instead of green glue) then 2nd layer of drywall.
There's your problem. As André said, the MSM resonant frequency of that construction will be very high, easily up in the voice range, so the wall will NOT isolate well at all. Most likely even ordinary speech will be clearly audible through the wall. The "more foam tape instead of green glue" is your mistake. If I were you, I'd take off that second layer of drywall, take off the tape, then put the drywall back again, directly on top of the first layer of drywall, with nothing in between (no glue, caulk, foam, carpet, whatever... nothing). That will improve the isolation hugely. If you don't do that, the wall will be pretty lousy at isolating. One more question: What thickness drywall are you using? 1/2" or 5/8"? Or something else? - Stuart -
