gullfo22 July 2007#2
for aesthetics and performance, cloth covered 703 (or similar) insulation will be hard be beat. name brand acoustic foams are designed to deal with certain acoustic treatments and also are (typically) fireproof - whereas a number of other types of foam may have less performance and might not be fireproof... one tactic would be to cover the corners of the room with 703 type absorbers, place a number of absorbers on the walls - staggered from each other on opposite walls - and some 703 absorbers on the ceiling as "clouds". a pair each over the bass and piano, three over the drums. for the drums, i'd also create a a pair of low "gobos" to try to isolate the kick and toms a bit. so in your room you're probably looking at around 16 panels for the corners and walls and another 7 for the ceiling. 2 gobos. this should keep the space fairly alive while trying to sort out the room modes. if the prices for this are high for you, there are numerous do-it-yourself plans for building the absorbers. i'd start there and then do some measurements to see what frequencies are still out of line and you may find you need some slat resonators and/or panel traps to get them under control. lastly if you have the option, you might be able to add a couple of ceiling diffusers to any open spaces on the ceiling rather than adding more absorption.
