Hi there Jimi! Welcome to the forum! :) Sorry you ended up waiting do long for a response: I've been real busy the last few weeks on a project, and unable to check the forum, and it looks like nobody else is helping out with answers either, right now! Anyway:
I really love the look of Helmholtz slotz resonators though I haven't managed to get my head around the calculator yet!
You can probably just use John's generic dimensions for your slot walls. They should work for most rooms, to get you some general broadband treatment.
I was thinking of putting soundbloc on the existing walls. They're all solid walls i think made out of breeze block then plastered
Do you have photos of those walls? It always helps to actually see what you are dealing with...
1: would it work?
The basic plan looks OK, but the rear soffits probably don't need to be as big as that. In fact, some people just use stands for their rear surround speakers. In your case, if you are going to have people sitting at the back, those soffits would block the view for them. Also, with people sitting behind the rear surrounds, they won't be able to hear the 5.1 effect. You might need to have a duplicate set of speakers right at the back, that can be turned off for critical work in the CR, or turned on when there are people listening in the rear section.
3. I like the idea of having a raised section behind the listening area so students can see whats going on a bit easier, i would there be any negative effects to this or a better way of doing this?
That's fine. Many studios have a raised section at the back.- As long as you build it right (No resonant cavity underneath!), you should be fine.
4. Is there an optimum frequency i should be tuning the resonators for?
Not really: the slot wall is more of a broadband device. You can tune individual slots if you want, but that's usually not necessary, unless you have something really problematic that needs special attention.
5. I've seen a few post about using board band absorption above speakers though this has a mixed consensus , should i be looking at doing this?
Which speakers? A 5.1 room is rather complex to design, since you have reflective surfaces all around, and speakers facing them. It's different from just a 2.0 or 2.1 design. - Stuart -
