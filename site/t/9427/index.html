Spyrow18 September 2007#4
Hi mike, If you doesn't have to deal with insulation take a look at this wonderfull Ethan Winer articule on room acoustics: http://www.ethanwiner.com/acoustics.html Then, don't worry too much about furniture, it's not that important. The most important things are bass traps and the reflection free zone. The metal door doesn't matter, just don't put a mirror in front of it :P There is another important point, the room dimensions. If you can move take a look at the ratios of the other rooms in your house (there is a list of recommended ratios in the link, in the ModeCalc). If you can't, don't worry and fill all the corners with bass traps. You can even build helmholtz resonators but that's more difficult. Try to find rigid fiberglass in Israel and get a good DIY tutorial. There are some in the studiotips.com forum, maybe here too, I've not searched. Don't worry about the ventilation, just turn off the A/C when mixing or recording something acoustic. And if you are handy enough build the desk!! do not pay $2000 for one :? Anyway I would advise you to book an studio for 1 hour when you mix is ready to take a look at your low end. There is imposible for a bedroom to reproduce the low frequencies like a club will do. Almost all you should know is in the link but feel free to ask. Cheers!

Well, it's a difficult room for its dimensions. The only thing you can do is filling it with bass traps. If you build a bass traps with fiberglass covered by fabric they are going to be broadband absorbers too. If you put a lot of them you can suck off the live of the room and make it too much dead, so as gullfo said a carpet would be even worse. But it depends on how much absorbers you build.
Mike A wrote:
a few questions: 1. Is the speaker placement ok? i left about 30cm between the back of the speakers and the wall. i'll also be putting them at an angle (ok?).
There is no simple solution. When a speaker is a node it excites that mode and when is in an antinode it doesn't, but the room is full of superimposed nodes and antinodes. There is a rule for speaker placement which says that you have to put the speakers at 38% of the largest dimension which is maybe a place with a good relation between nodes and antinodes.
Mike A wrote:
2. Is the sweet spot going to be limited only to the chair area, or will people sitting at the back sofa hear it properly?
If you don't treat your room it will sound very differently from one place to another. As you treat it it will sound more similar everywhere.

If you are going to build a helmholtz resonator better take someone to measure your room first. Maybe you could build a resonator of 57Hz based on the modes calculation but the real problem is 43 Hz, the sound is a caotic thing difficult to predict. If you can afford a software like ETF, and good flat onmidirectional mic (behringer does one very cheap) you can measure your room and be sure of a lot of things: speaker placement, speaker equalization for the mixer position, bass trap placement and quantity, carpet or no carpet, building resonators... etc. Or.. you can just fill it with bass traps and go for the 38% placement. Nothing bad either :)

Then add more in the walls (not directly in the wall, with an air gap) to create the refection free zone to avoid the comb filtering of the first reflections. http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=536 In this link you will find a comparison test with links to two more threads. Trip-21 September 2007#11
Actually the "38% rule" is for the listening position, rather than speaker placement. Anyhow, in such a small room, all rules can go down the drain (RT, modal placement) ;) Your only rule is "do what the pros do and hope it'll work" :). This room will probably ring hard - the walls are massive and it's basicly a square. Broadband absorbtion will be the way... Yes fiberglass basstraps will work as broadband absorbers too. Insulate all 4 corners (there's a prob with 2 corners, door and window though) with some nice superchunks, put a massive ceiling "cloud" absorbtion over your head/monitors, the front wall (behind monitors) should also be massively treated in order to let the back wall left hardly treated (window), and put some nice absorbers on the side walls (including the door). That'll be a nice start. I can't think of basstraps placement due to the limitations - maybe someone else can turn on the light bulb? ;/
