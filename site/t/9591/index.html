Forum members - I've been a member of this forum for a couple years now. Sometimes posting and other times just reading. I bought & read Rod's book a year or so ago and it has taught me quite a bit (as well as this forum) many thanks to Rod and all you guys! Now that my kids are no longer babies, I have time and *some* money. So working with what money I do have, I wanted to run my current build by you to get all of your opinions, thoughts, or any mistakes you may see me making? I DID get all my permimts beforehand. Please understand that in most cases, I know "what" and "how" to build (according to pro-studio techniques), but that damned "budget-thing" always gets in the way! - Both my Live & Control rooms are 10' deep x 11' wide with a 7'-6" ceiling. - The floors are just carpet over the concrete. - All walls (against the concrete basement walls) are 3" away from the concrete with plastic vapor barrier. - All studs are metal w/Track. - Insulation will be stuffed in between the studs (John's Manville R11). - The walls (that divide the control & live rooms) is a double-wall, with a 3.5" air gap in between each. - Each walls has it's own sliding glass door. - One side of the double-wall will get double-layer 1/2" rock and the other side of the double-wall will get double-layer 5/8" rock. - All my walls are framed out from the concrete floor right to the ceiling joists. - I installed "double-leg-channel" (hat-track) right to the engineered I-beams. I'm going to stuff R11 insulation between the joists. And then install one layer of 5/8" rock to the ceiling channel. (budget restricts me from doing double-layer) - I'm going to build 10 panels using Jonh's Manville 2'x4'x2" acoustic panels (the same as O.C. 703) for the walls and the ceiling/wall corners. I'm also going to do the super-chunk bass traps (floor to ceiling) in each corner. I will post pictures of what's done, and as I progress. So, any mistakes, thoughts, suggestions?
tmix16 October 2007#2
Brad, Man I feel your pain with the budget thing! My only thought is you probably wont get a huge amount of isolation at the ceiling level (and some possible flanking between rooms) because of having a low ceiling and not being able to have separate totally detached ceilings. Having said that, you may not personally have issue with that, it may work fine for you or at least be able to live with. I would try to hang the ceiling sheetrock where it did not bridge from one room to another if possible stop it at the inside of each room. Making compromises is part of the equation some times, as long as it does not involve safety compromises.

SharpNine19 October 2007#10
Brad, Hello! Maybe you knew already but if your concerned with ceiling isolation you shouldn't use the hat track. Over on Lou's construction thread they've been talking about the very same thing! I guess attaching the drywall to the hat track is not really all that much better than screwing it directly to the joists. If there is any benefit at all it is only marginal. If you need all the iso you can get you might look at the possibility of taking it down and putting up actual resilient channel which is different than regular hat track/channel. I have budget constraints on my project as well so hopefully that wouldn't hurt too much! Chris

Everything else looks good to me... As far as layers of sheet rock, it is all about mass baby!!! In Rod's book (page 35) states that "...every doubling of mass increases isolation by an additional 6 dB." That is a lot!!! He does go on to say that this "mass law" applies to non-rigid assemblies, but can be used as an approximation for wall use as well, A general rule of thumb is that 6 dB is twice as loud.
