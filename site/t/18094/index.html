Soundman202024 January 2013#2
Hi David, and welcome to the forum! :) Firstly, about your new studio:
We will either be building a garage this summer or next summer and I have the opportunity to build a 2nd story on it that will give me a 24' x 24' or a 24' x 36' space
Go for the garage, definitely! There's a reason why you seldom see studios on upper floors: It is damn hard to isolate them, and expensive! So unless you have very deep pockets, save your self a lot of trouble (and money) and build on the ground. Also, square rooms are lousy acoustically. Very lousy. I assume you'll be dividing up that 24 x 24 into a couple of smaller rooms, such as CR, LR and maybe a small iso booth, but just in case you were thinking of doing one big room, leaving it as 24 x 24 would be a problem.
I'm not concerned with Soundproofing.
Really? So in Michigan you never get rain, wind, thunder, airplanes flying overhead, trucks, cars, or trains passing by? No police, ambulance or fire sirens? You don't have other people walking around the building, opening and closing doors, flushing toilets, talking, listening to radio / TV? No phones ringing? No vacuum cleaners? No dogs barking in the yard? No neighbors mowing the lawn? ... etc... Unless you manage to schedule all of those (and about a million others) so that they don't interfere with your recording, you might want to re-think the "no soundproofing needed" position....
-The room is 12' x 12' x 8'
:shock: Ooops. There's part of your problem. It is square, but not only that, there's a mathematical relationship between both the length and width as compared to the height. There are serious modal consequences to such rooms. In your case, you have major modes at 47 Hz, 85 Hz, 94 Hz, 105 Hz, 117, 126 ... etc. All of your modes line up, all the way up the spectrum. And looking at your REW waterfalls, you can clearly see some of those, very obviously.
Except exterior wall with windows insulated with closed cell foam.
That is great thermally, but useless acoustically. That wall is probably not isolating very well at all, especially in low frequencies.
doors are both foam filled doors
Once again, not much use for isolation. Solid core is the answer.
-Four 2' x 2' skyline diffusors (pine) on rear wall
They are not doing anything useful for you in such a small room, and in fact could be part of the problem. They are also taking up the exact space where you need lots of deep absorption: the rear wall.
Is there a possible layout change such us moving the computer to the corner that might improve the symmetry enough to have a positive affect?
I would rotate your layout 90° or 180°, so that you are facing a flat wall with no "bumps" near your head. Symmetry is critical in a control room, and you don't have it right now. you can see that on your REW graphs as well: the response in the mids and highs is quite different, with major peaks and dips at different points. In other words, face either the "laundry room" wall at the bottom of your diagram, or the 'two 35" windows' on the left. That will improve your symmetry greatly. I would also suggest getting your speaker off the desk and on to massive, heavy stands just behind the desk. Speakers on desks is never a good idea, for several reasons, mostly related to vibrations and early reflections. Then set up your listening geometry correctly, following the usual guidelines.
-monitors placed on Auralex monitor pads.
Keep those, but put them on top of the new massive speaker stands, to help a bit in decoupling. That shelf right above your speakers is also not doing you an favours....
Reference of 75dB SPL
You should really use 85 dB for testing. That's the "standard" calibration level (if there is such a thing). At 75 dB you might not be triggering all the modes, and in any case it isn't loud enough to allow for good decay detection in REW, on the impulse response.
Right Speaker = Green, Left = Red
... and they are both showing a whopping mode at about 140 Hz! Not surprisingly, that's the frequency where all three dimensions of your room coincide: at 141.3 Hz, you have your 0,0,2 axial mode, and your 0,3,0 axial mode, and your 3,0,0 axial mode, all lined up perfectly. The 47, 85 and 96 Hz modes also seem to be well represented, although it's hard to be sure which is which, and to see what else is going on. Could you post the actual mdat file from REW, so I can download it and take a closer look?
My plan is to basically add more of the 4” bass traps.
Bass traps will help a bit, but the room is fundamentally flawed. Bass traps cannot remove the modes: they can only help to tame them a bit. I would not expect to see a major difference in the very low end modes, but you might get lucky in knocking some dB and ms off that big one at 141 Hz.
Right now I'm thinking of removing the Diffusors and putting three 2' x 4' traps on the back wall.
Yes! Definitely get rid of the skylines. They are part of the problem, not part of the solution. But I would take it much further than you are planning: Do the entire center section of that rear wall with 6" of 703, spaced about 6" away from the wall. In other words, two 4' x 8' panels of 4" 703 side by side, and another pair of 4' x 8' panels of 2" 703 behind them. You could help things even more by leaning those panels forwards, so they are closer to the wall at the bottom and further away up near the ceiling. Then build two "superchunk" bass traps for the two vertical corners. Cover the front faces with 6 mil plastic. You are going to need major bass trapping in there, but you also don't want to kill the highs.
I also thought I could add 1 – 3 more traps on the ceiling
Yes, and angle those two, including the one you already have up there. It is too small, but every bit helps. Angle them lower at the front of the room, and higher at the back. And if you can, then make the new ones hard-backed, to help deal with the vertical modes.
A final idea for places to put them is on the backs of the doors.
That too. And also some of the horizontal corners (wall/ceiling). All room modes terminate in corners, so corners is where you get the most "bang for your buck".
My budget is to stay under $500.
Spend it all on 703, and even forget cloth and framing right now. You can make them pretty later, as finds permit, but you need every last sabin of absorption you can get right now, so go buy a truck load of 703.
Are there other options that I should consider for room treatment or anything I should do to get more out of my bass traps?
Corners. Put as much as you can in corners. Make them big, floor to ceiling, and as wide as you can afford.
Any other suggestions for getting the most out of this with what I have to work with would be great.
Without moving the walls, and on such a tight budget, there isn't much more you can do. Just throw all the absorption you can at it, and cover the corner bass traps with plastic to keep the room a bit live. The other thing you could do is to optimize the speaker/head geometry, once you get your desk turned around. That might help some. But please do post the actual REW data file, for further analysis. - Stuart -

Great, but they are tilted the wrong way! The should be tilted lower at the front, higher at the back. You could probably also put the front one lower down, the middle one a bit lower and leave the rear one at the same height it is at present.
Also here's the dat files from previous measurements.
Those are from BEFORE you made these changes, right?
When I did the measurement I tried getting the SPL reference higher than 75dB but the peaks of some of the frequencies were apparently clipping in REW around 94db or so. 75dB was the highest I could go without clipping.
Did you calibrate REW before you did that? Clipping is a function of your mic, interface and sound card, not REW itself. If REW is showing clipping at only 75 dB, then it is likely that either you did not calibrate REW correctly, or there is an issue somewhere in your signal chain. 75 dB is only the level of loud speech, and that should not be causing clipping. Also, for some reason the analysis only ran up to 10 kHz, so I can't tell you anything about what is happening in the high end, but there clearly are issues in the low end, as well as several problems with early reflections. There seem to be room mode issues at around 49 Hz, 89 Hz, 129 Hz, and 160 Hz., and there are reflection issues which I suspect are coming off the desk. It will be interesting to see how that changes after you have done all the modifications you mentioned, and measured the room again. Only this time, please make sure that REW measures all the way up to 20 kHz! - Stuart -
