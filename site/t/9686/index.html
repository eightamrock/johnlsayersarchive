nice drawing. you might make the angled side walls shorter so you can increase the angle on them. the angled walls only need to extend past the primary listening position. same for the ceiling. that said, can you use steel (or wood) frame and drywall? what about plywood? if you have those options, you could build both treatment and isolation relatively easily. if not, consider make the concrete rooms rectangular with proper modes and using the mineral wool and drywall to create the treatments. you may also consider flipping the CR to face the "vocal booth" which could also be large enough for drums...

gullfo1 November 2007#4
if you start your angled walls (from the front) at the same position they are, but terminate them at the mid-point of the room, the angle will be steeper. you could also turn the CR sideways and get a decent sized room for drums or vox etc. if the walls are concrete, and you build an inner set of walls with a decent air gap between them, plus insulation and well sealed, as well as multiple layers of drywall, you can achieve pretty decent isolation.

the isolation is going to be tough for a live drum kit. you'll need multiple layers of drywall, proper sealing, a drum riser to reduce structure borne noise, etc... lots to consider. gullfo30 November 2007#14
drywall tends to crumble and would likely not have a good edge. wood is generally nicer to work with. that said, in theory you could build the resonator/absorption into the concrete. there are a number of places like churches and thing where Helmholtz resonators are built into the blocks and walls. from a practical standpoint, wood will be easier to work with, have a nice wood tone/finish, and you could adjust later if you need to. i think you'd want the slats running down each side like how you have the walls already, but make them inside out using the framing discussed on the SAE site.
