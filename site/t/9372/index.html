xSpace3 October 2007#11
I'd ditch the styrofoam in favor of insulation. Styrofoam does keep things cool but there are more useful products that you can incorporate into your project. I would consider the 4X8X 1/2 OR 3/4inch styrofoam panels "underneath" the OSB as this method can develop a clean flat appearance to your exterior siding when it is installed. It doesn't have much of an R rating, like a point zero 5 or something like that. Couldn't you build two walls decoupled one from the other, and maintain a flat surface on the exterior and the interior while increasing your wall depth? I spend it like it's your money!;)

Ok, so I haven;t posted on this since October. I have a bit of an update and some questions. I am going to give the update and follow with questions/comments on subsequent posts. I built a 32x24 pole barn with a concrete floor, ceiling height from concrete to bottom of roof truss is 10'8". I extended 100amp service into the barn from my house and upgraded my house service to 200amp (from 100amp). This is where I was as of February. Late February we actually started doing the "studio" portion of the build. There is an apprx. 17'x13' control room, 17' x 22 tracking room and a small iso room (plus a utility room that could double as a iso/amp room. These rooms are currently framed in using the double-wall method. We have also gone to great lengths to eliminate any flanking connections. We even cut the 2X10 inside headers that hold up the trusses at the dividing walls. We also installed a furnace above ceiling in the truss work. We are currently finishing this and the cabling and we will start drywall and such. I plan on going into more detail on several things later and also will be posting pictures. But for right now I have one specific HVAC question. One issue we have been dealing with is the air returns. Putting them in the divider double walls seemed an absolute no-no so that left the exterior walls. Even with that, I had real concerns about sound traveling through them between rooms. We had gone to some trouble to properly insulate and isolate supply runs but hadn;t though much about the air return. I have one idea I would like to run by ya. I propose to put the air return inside of a super chunk bass trap. The return would be in the back corner of the trap (say an iscoles triangle with a 10"base). The entire return cavity will be lined with duct liner (see CertainTeed ToughGuard 2 or OC equal). I would place a rigid board (OSB, Drywall) to form the triangle's base and seal the cavity which would also be lined with duct liner. Outside of the board I would start layering with OC 703 until I have apprx. 12" of depth or so and them treat the outside of the trap as your normal super chunk trap. So if you were looking down at the trap it would look like the all seeing eye pyramid on a dollar bill. The tip (part with the eyeball)being air return cavity with OSB sectioning it it off from the trapezoidal base made of 703. Now, I would then cut a 6" or 8" square hole (depending on register size) horizontally through the 703 and OSB into the cavity. This whole would also be lined with duct liner as to not disperse any fibers. The air return register would secured (some how, still working on that part) to the outside of the trap. Now of this description made sense to anyone it would be a miralce but if it did, any thoughts? It seems that the journey through the bass trap and up the cavity lined with duct liner (which is pretty much the same material as 703 just real thin and with a liner facing on one side (check accoustical data for the ToughGuard 2 its pretty impressive for what it is.) Then to our tin above ceiling connections (interior lined with duct liner) and then through flexible insulated duct to the furnace (and then making an identical reverse trip back to another room) should pretty much kick the db crap out of any sound transmission from room to room.

gullfo21 March 2008#15
couple of thoughts - the wrap and insulation - could be a moisture problem because the wrap normally would be outside to prevent moisture from getting into the insulation space where you're going to have a mixture of temperature and humidity... why not run the HVAC through the ceiling? put access doors outside so you can service the air handler, use large oversized flex duct, and build silencers (mufflers) overhead and maintain equiv isolation mass on areas where the ducts penetrate the ceiling. this should keep the rooms isolated and be easier to install and maintain. keep the bass traps in the corners doing what they're supposed to do...
