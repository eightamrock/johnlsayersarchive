cyeazel13 September 2005#2
Nick, I'm not sure that there would be any real noticeable difference between 3 layers and 2 layers seeing as you don't have separate framing for any of the walls. You may reduce low end transmission somewhat b/w the rooms with a 3rd layer but because your framing is all staggered (not separate), I'm not sure that the gain in performance would be worth the trouble. I'd also hold off on making any choices until you get some more opinions. I hope this helps a little bit. C. Yeazel

cyeazel14 September 2005#5
Well, since your framing is staggered (not separate or decoupled), you are kind of limited as to the amount of isolation you can achieve between rooms. I think the best thing to do would be as much more mass (layers) as you can afford to all the walls to help get better isolation. If you want to isolate drums from the other room, then more mass is probably the best way to go in your situation. Keep the number of layers throughout the room consistent too.

knightfly15 September 2005#7
Figuring 1/2" gypsum (you didn't specify) and 2x6 plate/cap, 2x4 stud staggered stud, a "perfect construction) would yield about STC 55 with 28 dB TL @ 50 hZ; adding a third layer on BOTH sides would raise this by 3 dB at all frequencies and lower the msm from 54 hZ to 44 hZ. That's in a PERFECT world; however, it's likely your non-isolated construction's FLANKING will make a third layer almost un-noticeable... Steve
