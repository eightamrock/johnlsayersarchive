Soundman202024 February 2011#6
What about this solution would that work?
I wouldn't do that. Your speakers are firing across the short dimension of the room, instead of down the long dimension, and even worse the front half of the room is not symmetrical! Regarding your comment on floating you floor, you might want to take a look at these before you do that: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 http://www.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/obj/irc/doc/p ... /ir802.pdf Also, regarding the question of isolation: you can't guess. You need to take real measurements of how much you already have and how much extra you need, using a real sound level meter, and providing actual figures, in terms of decibels. Based on that you can choose the right method to get that amount of extra isolation. Any other method is just pointless. You can't guess at acoustics, and hope that a certain plan might work. Sound waves don't react too well to "hopes" and "expectations"! :) They only react to mass, rigidity and gas flow resistance (among other things). It's not hard to do: all you need is a sound level meter and a good loud source of full-spectrum sound. (A complaining neighbor also helps! ) - Stuart -

Soundman202025 February 2011#8
So the window like this would work, a good solution, thanks a lot.
Yup! That's a good basic layout for you to star planning with. You can actually go pretty far with planning over the next four months (and you'll probably need all of that time to do so!). Probably the only thing that will change once you do the actual measurements is the mass (thickness) of your leaves, and the depth of the gap between them, which might also imply some changes to the final room dimensions. But you sure can do most of the design before you get to the point of deciding on the amount of isolation. Don't forget the electrical and HVAC part of things: Those seem to be the rather important bits that many people who come here forget at the beginning. - Stuart -
