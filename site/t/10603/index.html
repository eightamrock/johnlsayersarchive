Hi, I've recently found I have a reason to completely dig up all of my property to replace a trashed septic system - so while I'm at it I'm going to build a detached garage/second floor bonus room. Based on local codes, I can get approval for 22'x26' two car garage. I want the building to fit in and echo the house architecture, which implies kind of a timberframe ceiling/roof. (See pictures). Studio use - personal song writing and small rock band recording projects. Also, I play in a fun band and will want to use the space for practice/jamming. Generally, I don't record whole bands at once. I'm typically a one track at a time guy and it is usually me playing. However, it would be nice to be able to do the whole band at once now and again. I've looked at a lot of the floor plans and it seems like often people chop spaces up into little rabbit warrens with a bunch of isolation rooms. I'm wondering about an all in one big room approach with heavy use of wheeled gobos so I can reconfigure the room flexibly. I mean, really, the only things I ever mic are drums, accoustic guitar, and vocals. I use DIs for guitar and bass (with amplitube software amp models) and virtual instruments for everything else. Tracking I imagine would be done with headphones for all. Environment - I live in a rural area - it is very quiet here but I have neighbors - focus would be on keeping sound in rather than out. I expect to do heavy sound insulation of the main room - massive walls and ceiling and raised floor on rubber pucks to decouple from garage below. And the questions: I know this seems to run counter to the conventional wisdom - so I'm floating it here - am I nuts? Can a large single room setup be successful? What do I need to watch for? I could see doing a single control room primarily for mixing (box in the room in one corner) and leaving the rest open if I get a major gain there. Since I'm usually tracking myself I'd be inclined to make it fairly large as I'm pretty likely to be sitting at the desk with an acoustic guitar trying to track a part and I'm always banging the thing on stuff if the room is too tight. Instead of that, I'd be inclined to surround the desk area with movable panels and hang clouds for mix time. I'm hoping near field monitoring in a larger room implies less need for hard core treatment but I'm new here. Pic 1 - floor layout - stairs moved outside. Tried adding room from other thread. Might work. Most of the time, I'm self-tracking or I'm recording the band practicing (and I'm playing) so I think I just want one big old empty room. Pic 2 - Architect also suggested more common peaked roof so I drew both kinds of beams - hence the long ridge beam - I'm still leaning to hipped roof as I like the symmetry it affords in the corners but I could be convinced either way. Pic 3 - Ceiling in my house from the real estate listing - we want to echo this in the design of the new building but on a smaller scale.
