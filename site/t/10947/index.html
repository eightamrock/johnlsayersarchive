I decided to build the braces along the lines described here and then apply them as seen in attached image 1. Image 2 - 5 shows the result after cutting, milling and drilling each sway. Image 6 shows a rough estimate of what the final brace will look like. The final version will have a continuous piece of neoprene, from one profile and on to the other. Taking into account the ability to mount the actual sway to the frame and board. Even though I am happy with the result I still wonder if I may have overlooked something critical. If so, please comment. Also, I have not been able to find any good mounting tutorials. If anyone knows where I can find some more reading material on the actual mounting process that would be great. rod gervais17 July 2008#2
A couple of things 1st - I would space the studs 24" oc for the maximum LF Tl values.... Next - the tail of your clips have to be longer to allow for connection into the framing members and the clips that you need at the concrete wall - right now it's physically impossible for you to make attachents. Next - you are going to have to be very careful with your neoprene - you have to preload the rubber to the correct compression to get to whatever your designed center frequency is....... In order to do that yuo will have to devise a way to compress these together while measuring the load - and you will have to determine in advance what that load has to be - while taking into account what amount of the load you have is related to compression and what is related to friction of the member. No easy task. It seems to me that (for the cost of all of this) it would have been just as easy to purchase these things ready made. Rod
