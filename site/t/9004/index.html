I wouldn't bother making the entry door any larger than the largest point of entry to the basement itself. Dan Fitzpatrick originally planned for a single 36" door into his studio (a partial garage conversion), but then he realized that there was no point in having such a large door because the other two remaining doors leading into his garage (since he sacrificed his main garage door) were smaller than that... :roll: --Keith :mrgreen:

gullfo25 June 2007#7
first suggestion - you should get a RS sound level meter and have someone do a solid work out on the drum kit while you measure sound inside and out to find out how much isolation you need to meet with ordinances, neighbors, and people living in the house... if you have a bass amp handy, have the drummer and bassist play. those will be the hardest sounds to block. second - as shardward suggested, a smaller door will help, and isolation on the window (keeping in mind the permit process may require you to replace that window with an egress window since people will now be habitating the space - and they'll need air to breath - etc...) the $500 budget will be very tight as the sound meter and Rod's home studio construction book will cost you around $100 :-) and permits for construction will likely run another $100-200...

sharward27 June 2007#11
I think the best $100 one can spend in starting a project of virtually any budget is the sound meter and Rod's book. I suppose one could save the book money and spend a few months here, as I did (because Rod's book hadn't been published yet), to learn the fundamentals. The problem is, as usual, often people come into this forum and ask more questions than they attempt to answer on their own by searching and learning from the many threads full of valuable information here. One could argue that the cost of the education in time and money is less critical when budgets are small, but I disagree, because if the budget is small, then often, there's little margin of error in terms of being able to throw more money at the problem when it isn't solved. There will always be those who have small budgets, and there will always be those who don't follow through with the learning prescribed, and there will always be those who suffer both disadvantages... And I suppose there will always be those who think that preaching conservatism on these subjects is unreasonable and elitist. Oh, and it appears that Jake already has Rod's book, and it wasn't the "first thing" we told him. :roll: Whatever... --Keith :mrgreen: P.S.: There's more information about this project in this thread, which was moved to the Acoustics forum. mickdoo22 - i recommend Rod's book primarily for the reason's Sharward noted and because it enables someone a better grasp of all the things to consider when defining what they can do with their budget. not many people want to overspend on a project, and certainly less if you have a limited budget. you could skip the $100 and put that into drywall and studs, and maybe you'll have constructed yourself a studio. otoh, if your expectations were a bit high, then you might consider doing the research and testing need to map out a plan for your studio.
