jbassino5 September 2009#3
Concrete walls are your outer leaf. Now you have to build an inner leaf , in this case, wood studs with layers of drywall ON ONE SIDE ONLY, decoupled, not touching, completely independent from any wall, or existing ceiling. That will make your 2 leaf MSM system. About the pillars, yes, if you follow the shape you can save space, now you have to be creative to use that space for something or make it look good at the end.

SquarePants7 September 2009#6
I like "inside out walls": existing concrete>air>drywall>timber frame with insulation but otherwise: existing concrete>air>timber frame with insulation>drywall is fine - just treat your room with "traps" later as needed. How is the existing ceiling made? What is above? (another room?) You might need to add mass to ("beef up") the existing ceiling - such as directly attaching some drywall, if possible. Then build your studio ceiling on top of your inner walls - not touching the existing structure. Make the existing structure absolutely airtight with an acoustic caulk/sealant. And the same for the inner structure that you will build. Include both ventilation (fans/ducts to provide air to breathe) and air conditioning (heating/cooling) at the design stage. Hope that helps a bit :) , Geoff

Soundman20207 September 2009#12
I like "inside out walls": existing concrete>air>drywall>timber frame with insulation but otherwise: existing concrete>air>timber frame with insulation>drywall is fine -
But even in the inside-out case you should still have insulation in the "air" gap between the leaves, if you want maximum isolation. Not doing so will cost you around 5 to 10 dB of isolation, theoretically. It serves three purposes, two of which are: damps flutter echo and other resonance in the cavity, lowers the resonant frequency of the entire MSM system. The insulation must cover the entire surface of a leaf, and the thicker the better. - Stuart -

Soundman20208 September 2009#14
How big is your air gap? How much isolation do you need? Fill the entire gap if you want to! Just don't jam the stuff in so tight that it starts bridging mechanically (flanking). As long as it lies in the gap loosely, more is better. There's a brief research paper on that somewhere here, I recall. From memory: half-filling a 6-inch gap gives you something like 6 dB isolation gain, while completely filling it gives you a 10 dB gain. (I think those figures are roughly correct, but look for the paper and check them to make sure.) In any case, more insulation in the gap is better, as long as it does not bridge, and is the right density. - Stuart -

jbassino8 September 2009#16
It means that you put so much insulation compressed that its acting like a mass and therefore, providing solid contact between leaves. You can attach insulation directly on the concrete walls if you want, using nails, wires, or whatever, but dont spend extra money building a frame just to hold insulation agains the concrete wall If you only put insulation on the inner leaf frame, fully filled, and have a good air space you should be fine. If not, attach some directly on the concrete if you want to be relaxed about it :)
