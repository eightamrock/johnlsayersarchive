I think if you have it, use it. It can help you develop a better structure even if it may be more rigid. And the ability to screw into the OSB rather then hunting down studs is beneficial from an install point of reference.
Ro30 July 2009#3
Just realize that the density of OSB is lower (about 500kg/m3) than plasterboard (about 750 kg/m3) which means lower mass and finally results in less isolation. A combination of both would work.

xSpace31 July 2009#11
A lot of good input on OSB and drywall. If I can add a little more of my experience. If you want to use OSB then the most beneficial place is as the base/first layer. As mentioned above, it will allow you a place to screw into that doesn't require such an accurate positioning as locating a stud. The reason why I would not sandwich it between drywall is that due to the rigidity of the OSB this interior placement may create a very rigid barrier and this is something we are trying >not< to develop. Screws that penetrate into OSB create as rigid a connection as into a stud so placing this material inbetween the drywall would not be, for me, the best placement for the OSB. As a finish material, that is your personal preference, but it is not a desired locating, again, due to the rigidity of the panel and the screw penetration making it more so.. As a side note, OSB here in the States @ Xit 4, Alabama is cheaper than or the same price as 1/2 inch sheetrock. edit: One of the MAIN advantages is to develop a build that can stand up on it's own with no need for sophisticated acoustic sway bracing or the like.

All I can say is that according to Rod, the structural rigidity of placing the wood sheet good material directly to the stud face was what he was specifically after, in my build... and I really do trust Rod. If you think about the rigidity of wood sheet goods and gypsum, attaching the wood makes for a much stiffer mass, as opposed to the gypsum. I would think that the increased standoff distance of sandwiching the OSB would decrease structural rigidity a significant amount. Thereby, making a slightly less rigid assembly. Granted we're talking minute points of reference here, but this is a game of millimeters, where every bit of stuff, like LF TL is important. I can tell you this much from a practical standpoint... The rooms at my place are currently damned stout, and I am finding that they are extremely effective at isolating sound...

If every mm counts, and sound isolation is key, you'd be best served my using the densest materials, which isn't OSB. If you have the space it works great, but if I only had 1" of space, I'd use two sheets of 1/2" drywall. More mass.
