knightfly9 October 2005#28
Sorry for the confusion of terms; by "double framed wall" in this case, I meant your existing concrete and a second leaf, held up by a separate frame - in other words, a 2-leaf wall. Which side of that leaf the frame goes on will not change the TL of that wall, unless the position of the second mass (all layers of paneling mounted to the new frame) changes relative to the concrete. By this I mean - same air gap, same TL. If you intend a 500mm air space, there is only one MINOR reason for building the new wall "inside out" - that is a slight savings in lumber cost by not needing lumber on BOTH sides of the gypsum panels. The downside of "inside out" is trying to build the entire wall and THEN tipping it into place without a CRANE or 12 strong men to help - it also makes mounting of IN-wall insulation (necessary for damping and several dB of TL) very difficult. Keith brings up another valid question about your concrete walls - hollow block already act as a 2-leaf wall, so it would help if you know whether your concrete is solid or hollow block, or?? Steve

knightfly14 October 2005#35
Three layers of plasterboard can be supported by a 2x4 WALL frame, but usually NOT for ceiling (unless it's a VERY small room) - however, these would need to be placed on closer centers - 400mm centers instead of 600mm centers - this will actually HURT isolation of the wall by a couple of dB. Better to use 2x6 wall framing (all of it is same size) and space the studs on 24" (600mm) spacing - this lets the wall "flop" just a bit more, which improves isolation a little. BTW, our lumber sizes aren't "real" - years ago, a 2x4 was really 2" x 4", and came from the saw mill rough surfaced, right out of the saw. Then they started smoothing the wood (planing it) which made the actual dimensions smaller - this eventually evolved to further reduction in size (but not cost :? ) to the present day, where a "2x4" is really 1.5" x 3.5", a "2x6" is really 1.5" x 5.5", etc - this is probably a major source of confusion when people in Europe try to match inches to millimeters. In either case, if you intend to support a ceiling frame on your inner walls you need to double the top plates - see the diagram below for a visual... Steve
