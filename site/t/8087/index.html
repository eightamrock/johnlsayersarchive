len-morgan2 February 2007#4
First things, first - You HAVE to reduce the size of your pictures to less than 700 pixels wide (see the very first post in all of the forums). Most of the "regulars" here will just move on to the next thread if they have to scroll the screen to read the text. I can make a few comments. One you don't want the bare flexible duct. You need the insulated type (if you have to go flex). Most of the experts here recommend using duct board instead. It's a much better insulator and isn't as subject to getting punctured. I'm not sure how close your drawing is to scale but from the looks of it, the blue and green endpoints (in the garage) are way too close to each other. The out is going to be sucked right back in. You need about 3 ft (1 M) as a minimum. You will probably also need some kind of isolator where they go through the new wall (like a little piece of flex (insulated) between the two leaves to prevent mechanical vibration. The last piece of advice I can give is to get Rod's book and commit it to memory. Hope that helps. Fix the pix and you'll get a lot more. len

rod gervais3 February 2007#12
Personally - I do not recommend the use of flex duct of any type as it can kink and slow flow very easily - PLUS - becaus of the manner in which it's constructed it' prone to noise. Duct board is similar to the rigid boards you use for sound attenuation - BUT - it has a polymer lining (black faced) so that the fibers don't break free under air movement. Before we can answer the rest of your questions you need to provide some info - such as - how much air do you need to provide (good rule of thumb is 15 cfm per person). a fan blowing into the room is not the best installation - a fan pulling out of the room ( as I show in the book ) is best - that way the fan noise itself is being pulled away from you - not pushed into your room. If you use duct board - you can touch the structure without worrying about flanking taking place - however you cannot with galvanized or hard plastic ducts - for that you really want to use a canvas accordian type isolator for the passage through the wall. I don't see enough info on your fan choices to really be able to advise if they're any good or not. BUT - they should be rated for continuous duty operation to be of any use to you. Rod
