msikio13 November 2007#44
Gabriel, Thanks for the postings. That's some amount of work ! Is there a subjective better option from a musician's point of view ? Beside that, it looks like your sub is a bit too loud. I would go for damped heavy wooden panels, suspended from the ceiling all around the room. The walls are first covered with industrial style high density 2" mineral wool faced with a glass tissue. Then the wood panels are suspended with hooks and chains or ropes from the ceiling. They should not touch the floor or the ceiling. It would be a good idea to vary the thickness of the panels to get a broader absorption. You might have to experiment a bit with the placement of panels. The hard wood surfaces will maintain a live sound in the room. The panels will vibrate to absorb bass frequencies. That should work for the type of sound you are looking for. If the room is still too boomy in the 250 Hz region you might have to swap some of the panels for more expensive perforated ones.

I figured out this design based on your requirements, my own experience, theory and tips from various people and acoustician... but I never built such a design yet. There is no way to be sure such a design will work as there is no equation that I know of associated with it. It relates to know principles : MSM and so on but it doesn't follow the usual recipes. As you feel experimental here's my "gut feeling" to reply to your questions : The panels should be 1/2 inch plywood with 0.72 lb/ft2 limp mass completely glued to it with contact adhesive. The overall weight of the panel should be about 2.3 lb/ft2. Yes, I would go for full size 4 x 8 panels. You might experiment with one panel, play a slow sweep in the room and record the vibration with a (cheap) contact mike. I think it should work... but as I said... no guarantee... I would stick to 3.5pcf 2' glass tissue faced mineral wool. Much easier to work with. In case you feel the room is too live you can easily remove some of the panels and your don't have to face non covered mineral wool. Still be careful, the glass tissue is quite fragile. You don't necessarily have to buy the limp mass from acoustic suppliers, some roofing or floor damping material can sometimes do the trick for less money.

lovecow wrote:
Gab, ...some preliminary suggestions. This is stuff you can try without having to place anything permanently - just try it and listen for what kind of difference it makes: 1. For the (wall/wall) corners of the room, I would suggest stacking four LENRD in each. Then place 4" of (8 lb) mineral fiber boards over the LENRDs, spanning the corner. This technique - 4" panels over LENRDs - has been shown to be extremely effective for controlling bass. I helped Mitch Gallagher do it in his - significantly smaller - recording booth and it helped considerably. (He discussed this in his book. Measurements are provided.) 2. For the rest of the walls, I would suggest using the 2" Studiofoam on the walls. If you can, place the panels over a 2" air gap. Coverage should not exceed about 30% for each wall, should be more or less centered on the wall, but panels should be spaced and staggered to minimize parallel flat surfaces. 3. Finally, use the diffusers you have on the ceiling. I usually suggest placing diffusive areas over instruments - in your case, over the piano and drums. Minimum area for these diffusive areas should be 4'x4' over each instrument, but more cannot hurt. If you can, start with the stacking LENRDs and mineral fiber panels over the wall/wall corners (#1 above), then evaluate. If that is providing ample bass control in the room (it won't be perfect, but it should be much better), then you can move on to the Studiofoam and diffusers. I hope this helps. Also, please let me know how you would feel about posting this publicly. I generally prefer not to offer advice privately through contacts made on public forums. I would feel more comfortable if this advice were...made public. Feel free to copy the above to a post in the appropriate thread if you like. Otherwise, I would like to do so at some point.
