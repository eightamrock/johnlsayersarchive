have you tried 4" thick 3pcf or 6pcf rigid fiberglass across the corner? a properly sealed poly floor to ceiling? something to trap a lot of air and acts on the velocity and/or pressure on the corners. the lenrds seem too small to do the job. a superchunk absorber is a stack of 703 (or equiv) insulation in the corner with at least a 24" face. keep in mind that modes permeate the room - treating just one corner isn't typically going to show all the response shift that 2 corners will. and 2 won't be the same as 4... low frequencies are big wavelengths and they tend to interact across the entire room.

gullfo20 November 2007#78
the poly is not as efficient as the heavy rigid fiberglass for a broad band absorption, but it does adds diffusion and trapping in one unit - at the cost of extra space. and it does introduce the possibility of reflections if the mics are close (but walls will be problematic as well). if you feel more comfortable that the corner absorption with the fiberglass will reduce the need to additional panels in getting the reverberation under control, then go with that approach. if keeping the room more alive is important and you plan on adjusting the space using smaller absorption panels, then the polys would do it (assuming the space consumption is ok). a live diffusive room will not sound small but a small room will sound small without mode control and balanced reverberation. in the case of your room - get the bass trapping up. then adjust the reverberation until you feel its balanced. there is a limit to what measuring a single corner will give you.

Gabriel, thanks for the pictures, measurements, and listening reports. Interesting. Are the wall made of one single sheet of plasterboard ? If think my design would improve also the ringing walls problem, but of course the heavy guys approach would be to affix a second layer of plasterboard with Green Glue or mass loaded vinyl in between. If you want to stick with material you already have you should try corner absorption between the walls and the ceiling. As an experiment you can try between the walls and the floor. This should improve even more the TR in the low-mid/bass register. If you're happy with it, you then have to find a way to set the panels between the ceiling and the walls. Then, to counterbalance this added absorption in the room, so you should probably keep diffusion on the walls. Concerning the cymbal problem you should probably go for a BIG diffuser above the drums.

Eric_Desart27 November 2007#98
B.D.R. You asked me to have a look here. But you're doing right with all your testing. And whatever it remains difficult to draw general conclusions, which isn't that important since you're testing what's important in your specific circumstances. When you speak about 1/4 round isolation (duct type isolation), then I do have measured them in a lab as well, and they also show this peaky around 100 Hz behavior (depending on size). I even wonder if not a bit more. Hence using 1/4 circles will not gain you a lot. Be careful to make panel traps or slat slot resonators in corners (which such open diffusers are) without testing them. You don't know exactly how they will behave. If that rounded 1/4 circle is a closed panel, it will show an entirely different behavior than a traditional flat panel trap. In groups like this one just combines principles in the assumption all these principles will support one another, but in fact people don't know. I as well see it as possible that you degrade principles. This is not just an addition of possible good properties, but a new system with unknown or hard to quantify properties. :twisted: The more Glenn masters sketchup (and Glenn is very good at it), the more unanswered questions are suggested as possible improvements. Foam sculpting magic becomes sketchup magic. For me, we do know how corner absorption works based on measurements (hence empirical). Converting them to slat resonators, in the assumption that it will reflect more highs is certainly correct, but what happens with the corner absorber in itself, I doubt anyone has any objective data to compare that. That standard Lenrds do less in the lows is the logical consequence of being so small. What they do one can check on Ethan's site. And if good measurements are still not on Auralex's site is their fault, and for me enough not to consider them any further. It's almost a poor joke that to know what Lenrds do we have to check a competitors site. In fact for their size and cost they are really good, but it's commercial hype that they still wants to live from the old magic rather than giving objective data. But still it's not that simple that you can take 1 room with a very specific pattern in function of modal behavior, as a general conclusion of which is best or worse. For what is best is what solves the specific frequency related limitations of YOUR room. Which does not means that your measurement series aren't very valuable. To compare absorbers in themselves one has to use more neutral rooms and for now, with the uncertain behavior of corner absorption, even official measurement methods have their question marks.
