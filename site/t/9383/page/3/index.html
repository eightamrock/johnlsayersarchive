This is great information, Gab. I'd like to provide a quick technical perspective on this. For the sake of brevity, I'm going to focus on two of your modal problems, at 47 and 55-58 Hz. This type of analysis can be repeated on your own with any of the other modes that are resonating ("ringing"). The mode calculator I used was Bob Golds'. Looking at your waterfall graphs, I was able to eyeball the following modal decay times (given as "traditional" T60's): Untreated Room (pinkish waterfall) 47 Hz = ~1.6 s 55-58 Hz = ~1.6 s LENRDs (red waterfall) 47 Hz = ~1.5 s 55-58 Hz = ~1.5 s This is not unexpected since LENRDs alone do not address problems below ~60-80 Hz very well, relatively speaking. LENRDs + 8 lb Mineral Fiber (greenish waterfall) 47 Hz = ~1.1 s 55-58 Hz = ~0.8 s This is a significant improvement and the audible result should be that of having much "tighter" bass, particularly for something like the kick drum. What's your take on how it sounds, Gab? Things getting better? Like/don't like? Please share! :D

lovecow16 November 2007#57
Gab, A few thoughts on that: 1. I did not address your "linear T60" inquiry above. To that end, I do not subscribe to this school of thought. A small rise in low frequency T60 relative to the high end is, IMO, better than "flat" in terms of time response, especially taking into consideration your desire for a "bigger sounding" room. (Others here may have different opinions, and you should absolutely hear them out. :) ) So, since the high frequency T60's are initially lower (according to your graphs) and the low frequency treatments will naturally bring down to the high frequency T60's as well (also evident from your graphs), you should be headed in the right direction. 2. You should note that the type of diffusers you are using will affect the low frequencies. They are probably more band-limited, but they will have some effect. 3. If you would like additional control at the low frequencies (after adding diffusion), you could start trapping the wall/ceiling and/or wall/floor corners similar to the wall/wall corners. However, I would hold off on that (but leave room for it!) until you know you want it, mainly because it will be more difficult to do, at least for the wall/ceiling corners. Some would say (I no longer do) that you cannot have "too much" when it comes to "bass traps." This might be true in your room, but considering the type of sound you are looking for, I think the diffusion as a next step will get you pointed in that direction better. 4. Bass may not be the only problem with the piano sound. Piano notes are rich in harmonics. Therefore, cleaning up the mids/highs with diffusion might provide a marked improvement, even for lower notes. Also, I wouldn't rule out some wall absorption - there's a chance some might be necessary. Finally, keep in mind that diffusion as the next step is my suggestion based on your desire to have a "big" - and, presumably, "lively" - room. The next step might be removing or covering some more of the ceiling tiles. My preference would be to cover them with more diffusers. Ultimately, you could wind up with 50% diffusion on the ceiling, somewhere around 20-30% on the walls along with a few absorbers, and - maybe - a few more traps. HTH. Best regards,

I think you might be ready to just "tweak" the room. From the above comment, I would guess that you probably need to swap out some wall diffusers for some absorption. How much is up to you and your ears. (I think we're beyond the point of measurements helping us evaluate this space. It's really coming down to you and your preferences.) I would suggest you use 2" panels with 2" airspace behind, or solid 4" materials, or some combination thereof. Which ones and exactly how much is up to you. Foam tends to shave off more of the very high frequencies (> 6-8 kHz), making the room relatively "darker." Fabric-wrapped fiber will reflect relatively more energy up high, which is probably what you want. This will be a step by step process. Remove a diffuser or two; replace with absorption; evaluate. Repeat until happy. And remember that "perfection" is not going to be attainable. Your desire is for a large-room sound. Your reality is a small room. Compromises are going to be inevitable. :wink: Happy treating,

two things to look for: one - room modes which are the large dips or peaks in the response below 300hz. second - the high frequency reverberation times and/or echoes. the room modes are handled by the low frequency traps, and the mid and high frequencies are handled by the diffusion and slats. the slats would be tuned if there were problem frequencies that needed adjustment, otherwise you would target them to be more broad and balance the room. in the ideal world, the measurement line would be flat or slightly tilted to favor the ears ability to hear certain frequencies (bass usually needs to be a bit higher than high frequencies so most people opt for longer RT60 on bass frequencies than on high ones...) in your case, you have some modes as Jeff points out, and a couple of mid points that may be taken care of with broad treatments. otherwise the high end is reasonably flat so you just want to adjust for reverberation which requires some careful use of absorption to get it to the right level without over doing it. this is where the waterfall plots can help (although you ear is the better guide - which is one reason why Jeff asks you about how the room sounds...) from a process standpoint, I'd say get the bass modes flattened a bit, then work on the mid-high RT60 with some absorber panels. you've been doing a good job with the measurements but you want to work those corners, treat all the corners of the room.
