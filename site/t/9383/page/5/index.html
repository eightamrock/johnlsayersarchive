B.D.R. In order to judge communication, between whoever, I should want to see the exact communication from both sides, in order to see everything in the right complete context. In general absorption is no insulation, but can be part of insulation if used in a cavity between a paneling system as e.g. a wall. But in fact they confirm that in that text by relativating the effect on TL by adding absorption. That sentence about a slight decrease in sound leaving the room is a bit page filling not understood by laymen, and partly compensated by increased amplifying or harder playing (hence this is theoretical stuff in the margin). Eric_Desart28 November 2007#108
Yes you could use True Panels inside your walls to increase isolation. But it should be completely senseless. The best absorption to put inside a wall is low density fiberglass or rockwool or whatever cheap comparable product at a fraction of the price and acoustically possibly/likely better. This does NOT mean that True Panels aren't good or worth their price (that's subjective), but that you have to use materials how and where they are meant to be used. Putting True Panels in a wall equals trowing money away with a possible lower TL effect. Acoustics is physics/techniques, not the magic of materials. About that conversation with Auralex.
