So... there is a concrete step in the floor...I will build two risers, one for the right leg and pedals of the piano , and one for the drum set , please see the atachment. 1) Do you have any suggestion for the risers/step situation..?
How high is that step? It would seem to be somewhat of a safety hazard to have to negotiate. Is it possible to pour an additional concrete slab throughout to even out the floor? I wouldn't recommend a floating or suspended floor due to a tendency to resonate at certain frequencies unless done right which is expensive!
2) Where I should set up the speakers, computer rack and sub woofer?
Ideally, in another room. :wink: This will help give you isolation you need to make critical decisions on mic selection and placement. If that is not an option and you are not mixing, where ever there's room and will not interfere with the musicians. If you are mixing, here are a few bits of information that I've collected along the way - • It is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension because you don't want rear reflections to interfere • Speakers angled 30 deg. in from a flat wall result in speaker baffles being perpendicular to the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position • Monitor angles 60 degrees • Monitor height – tweeters aimed at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls 6-12 degrees
3) I will need gobos or similar constructions between the instruments?
That depends on how much bleed you want in your recordings, but typically having a few gobos between the drums and the rest of instruments is common practice.
and what kind of acoustic treatment you suggest for the walls/ceiling around the piano and drum and bass corners?
I would start with 2' x 4' x 4" thick panels of 2.5 pcf mineral wool in all four corners - floor to ceiling. Next, try to break up the parallel wall surfaces with angled slat broadband absorbers. Finally, I would add several cloud absorbers (3"-4" thick mineral wool @ 2.5 pcf) over the drums & bass. Depending on how close you are miking the piano, you may be okay there. If there's too much bleed in the piano mics, perhaps a couple of gobos or the moving blankets will help. In a room this size, I would not use diffusion, especially around areas that are close to the mics due to comb filtering. I think you will find in this forum the majority of acoustic treatments calling for 3"-4" of fiberglass/rock wool/mineral wool @ 2.5-3 pcf instead of the typical foam products available. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

gullfo27 October 2007#23
you want to look at the low frequency decay and waterfall, to determine what room modes you have. in general you'll want broadband bass trapping in the room but you'll want to find other frequencies which may need additional control. the goal being a neutral or balanced room that minimizes the impact of what you hear from either the instruments, or from your monitors. the other bit to check is your RT60 reverberation to get that to a level you're comfortable with. for testing purposes, the subwoofer and speakers in the corner is fine as it will excite the entire room. the mics for recording are one location to measure as well as each location someone will be sitting or standing to verify you have people in locations with minimal peaks or lows in the response.
