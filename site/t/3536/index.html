Hello everyone, I’m thinking about building a room-within-a-room-construction and wondering if the width of the cavity between the in- and outside wall matters. My idea was to leave a space of approximately 40 centimeters (15-16 inches) between both walls, which won’t be filled with insulation. Would this make a significant difference as far as soundproofing is concerned or am I just wasting space? Thanks a lot in advance! Kind regards, 74Loveplane
drfrankencopter6 April 2005#2
This is probably a question for the construction forum but....all the papers I've read suggest that low frequency attenuation is to a large extent dependant on the seperation between the two walls of a double wall construction. So, no it's not wasted space. That said, there is likely a point of dimineshing returns. When you say 40 cm between the walls, is this the distance between the two inner most edges of the drywall? I would recommend putting insulation in between. Technically this will lower the attenuation somewhat as it couples the walls slightly, but it will be more forgiving to imperfections in wall assembly and holes (such as those for outlets and tie lines). It will also help damp out any cavity resonances. Check out the BBC and NRC papers on wall construction for more details. Cheers, Kris
