Gash, you actualy get slightly better LF TL values with fluffy insulation - BUT - if you have rockwool you do NOT want it packed touching both surfaces- it's too dense as it is and touching both surfaces will create a bridge between the 2 - and thus a flanking path. Rod

gullfo1 July 2008#7
one of the bigger problems with hollow floating floors is the potential for creating a booming sounding floor which doesn't provide the desired isolation either... the floor sitting on sleepers w/ some isolation pad under them, may be good for situations where you need air flow under the floor but in practice you're better off fixing a moisture problem before building. having isolation pads directing supporting a massive floor and having appropriate dampening (but without strong coupling as Rod points out) is the more ideal solution for floating a floor. you can go about it a number of ways - the better option is a thick concrete floor with isolation springs properly engineered to support the entire room (and expected loads). another option might be neoprene or KIP pads with soft/semi-rigid insulation for dampening supporting multiple layers of MDF, gypcrete, wonderboard, drywall, and flooring - again engineered to support the weight of the room (and expected other loads) so its resilient but not unstable. Rod's book goes into a lot of details on floating floor pros/cons including methods for increasing mass of wooden floors using sand etc.
