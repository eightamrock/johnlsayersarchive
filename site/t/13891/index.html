xaMdaM23 December 2009#2
trodden, Most important first... Floating floor You should really go back a re-read that sticky... I think you're evidently taking away just the exact opposite info of what you should have, other than it's IS going to be EXTREMELY expensive to build a floating floor over a crawlspace/basement... to the tune of a structural engineer, ripping out the floor, putting in much larger joists (double to triple 2x12 joists) and even then, you'll likely need support posts under those massive joists, if you're talking true room within a room design for a construction project of any size. Now, about mud/tape on the first layer... Technically, you should mud/tape. However, I didn't. I caulked the 1st layer of gypsum with SilenSeal caulk. I think you would be OK with just caulk, as I was, but only if your seams are butted real tight. Then too, I also used 3/4" OSB as my first layer against the studs, and caulked that layer with OSI's SC-175 acoustic caulk. As far as the joints go, I would put the first layer of gypsum (wide side) vertical and your 2nd layer (wide side) horizontal. (It's actually cheaper to mud/tape as opposed to caulking.) Depending on your stud size and the air gap between the 2 walls, you should save you money for screws, caulk and other misc hardware, and just use pink fluffy in your stud bays. Max

Yes, the fluffy (thermal) pink, yellow or white stuff. Recycled cotton, cellulose, and recycled polyester also work well, as does mineral wool. See the NRC data in their IR 693 report http://www.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/obj/irc/doc/p ... /ir693.pdf Perhaps I'm mis-understanding. What I'm referring to are insulated sealed cavities in walls, floors and ceilings. Exposed insulation on the wall or ceiling surface is for in-room reverb control, and you see 703 commonly used.

The reason is that with an inside out wall, by design, the insulation being on the inside of the room is now part of your acoustical environment. In that respect it is better to use the 703 or comparable or even the rockwool. Whatever is respected as a product for acoustical treatment is what you should consider.
