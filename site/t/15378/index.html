gullfo15 December 2010#8
ah, the joys of new construction! looks like everything is neatly tucked away in the joists and the ducting us nicely organized so soffiting those off should be reasonable. i would say definitely get some drywall up under the upper floor and then use the isolation clips and hat channel to create the isolation ceiling. does the HVAC have a fresh air exchange? is there any considerations for the rest of the house in terms of say the studio needing to be cooled but the rest of the house heated? if you can use the house HVAC, soffit off the ducts and build the baffle boxes as extensions of the soffit to silence the supply / return vents. otherwise you might consider a separate split unit heat pump and a separate air exchange system.

gullfo18 December 2010#14
if you're using the clips, you use hat channel/furring strips, not RC... the RC would only be used without the clips. also, either RC will be less isolation than the clips. for ceilings, you need to use RC-2 and on the walls RC-1. the real problem is RC is that it's really easy to "short out" if you use the wrong screws and drill through into the frame or collapse the RC by laying out wrong.

gullfo23 December 2010#17
gregkraus wrote:
1. Do I install all my clips and channel & install my drywall layers on my ceiling across the whole basement ceiling first and then build my walls up to that? This would give me complete isolation from the upstairs. 2. If so, How do decouple and attach my walls from my ceiling and the floor above?
build out the decoupled walls then add the ceiling inside that and stagger the drywall layers (wall and ceiling) with bead and caulk. here's the idea on the walls - neoprene, etc under the bottom plate and over the top plate. isolation anchors to hold in place. see this link as an example and product: http://www.kineticsnoise.com/arch/wallmat.aspx if you just do the entire ceiling first, then you're going to be coupling between rooms via the ceiling layers and your air space is also separated. one of the advantages of not closing that space between rooms and ceiling is the air can flow all around the isolated space which reduces the compression of air and in turn improves your TL.

gullfo28 December 2010#19
the isolation of the inside-out wall can be quite good but it depends on a number of factors such as the outside wall construction etc. for a framed and sheathed exterior wall, it's probably lower than a normal wall because the air gap is more critical due to the (relatively low) mass of the exterior sheathing. for an exterior wall of block or concrete wall, then it should perform as well. use the rigid insulation on the inside. use pink (light) insulation in the air space - do not compress it or you'll introduce coupling and have a lower TL.
