Need to know the guage of the existing steel framing. If it's 22 AWG or thinner, probably not. If it's structural framing (20 AWG and heavier), I would say yes. But don't quote me on that. From what I've learned here and remember, you want one wall rigid, and the other a little more flexible (or floating), so each wall absorbs different frequencies. Then again, there are other things to consider...Flanking. And tying into the existing structure, etc. Depends on how high this wall goes, what kind of ceiling treatment, and the other walls and their construction. Are we just beefing up the one wall, or building a room w/in a room? If sound can get around the second wall, it's not going to do much.

sharward27 October 2005#10
fisher of men wrote:
What if ........... I build my new wall 3or 4" away from the existing wall do you think that will help.? Or is 1 or 2" enough?
That's one of the worst things about three leaf walls -- the middle leaf essentially cuts the air into two separate "airs" so you don't get the benefit of the total possible air gap. 1 or 2" is most likely not enough, especially when low end (bassy stuff) needs to be isolated. 3 to 4" is marginally better. Getting up to 8" is much better. 12" is great. More than 12" is greater but, from my second-hand understanding, there is a diminishing return after 12" -- it does get better, but the benefits are not proportional to the extra air.
I don’t think the existing walls studs are strong enough; they probably used the cheapest stuff they could.
Please understand the reason we're asking about the gauge. We're not asking if it's strong enough to handle another layer. (That is a good question and shouldn't be left unanswered.) The reason we ask is that thinner gauge steel studs are actually better at isolation than heavy gauge ones. The relative flimsiness of the stud help reduce the amount of structure-borne noise transfer to the opposite leaf. Typically builders will use the cheapest studs they can get away with. If they're just partition walls that aren't bearing any weight, then they're probably the thinner gauge variety. However, one shouldn't make any assumptions. Just to confirm, you are saying that you are certain that you do not have the option of adding an extra layer of drywall with Green Glue sandwiched in between (and also attached with screws, by the way)... :?: --Keith :mrgreen:

knightfly28 October 2005#13
If you can afford it, I'd add an extra layer of drywall to your side of the existing wall FIRST, sealing it like you've done so far; then, move in about 12" and build your new frame, place strapping or lath across the inner edges of the studs and fill with insulation, and put two (minimum) more layers of 5/8" drywall on the inside of the new frame. The added mass of a second layer on the old wall, plus the wider air gap and at least two more layers on the inside of the NEW wall, would get you somewhere around STC 67, with TL @ 50 hZ around 35-36 dB; the 3rd leaf effect caused by the other side of the existing partition will lower the bottom end isolation - this is hard to predict, but probably somewhere around 3-4 dB worse around 100 hZ. This means that if you were to play acoustic drums in that room, people on the other side of the wall would start to hear anything above approximately 85 dB SPL on YOUR side of the wall... Steve
