evan24 April 2006#1
I've been searching around on the web trying to find a decent place to get some good acoustics advice, and I came across this forum. Here's hoping that despite this being my first post, someone will be kind enough to help me. :) We've recently redesigned the back room and have moved the stereo / home theatre out there. It's a small room (haven't got the exact measurements on hand, but it's roughly 8 feet (w) x 15 feet (l) x 14 feet (h). It's pretty tight - the couch is sitting about three feet away from the wall in the rear with the surrounds on stands pointing towards the roof in the back. The TV and amp are sitting on an entertainment unit that's roughly the same width as the room. It's floorboards with a carpet covering about 2/3 of the room. The front speakers are floor-standing and are placed half-way out from the entertainment unit so I could get enough room to toe them in. After being toed in, they're standing about three feet away from the rear wall and the rear corner is less than a centimetre from the skirting. Nothing can really move - everything "fits", and without resorting to buying new furniture, I can't really move the speakers anywhere. In a nutshell, here's the problem - I've used RoomEQ with a radioshack SPL meter to measure the frequency response of the room. I've (apparently) taken into account the distortion from my soundcard and the inherent distortion of the SPL meter. I realise it's not the ideal measurement tool, but given that I'm not going for studio quality (only as near as I can get), it's probably "good enough". The room response (not surprisingly, from what I've since read) shows horrendous bass response. I've got a major null point at around 80hz - from memory, it's something around -20db to -25db. It also peaks just before and after - the response is roughly 10db higher than it should be. I've got the curves if anyone wants to see them, I just don't have them on this computer. The upper end is overly bright, but that's likely due to the french doors we had installed (lots of glass). That I can deal with - we're having insulated curtains installed soon, and I'm pretty comfortable with trying to dampen the room. Otherwise, the response is mostly pretty flat - it's definitely not studio quality, but it's pretty good. I can work with it, anyway. So, my major priority is trying to solve the bass problem. I don't mind building something - I'm pretty comfortable with woodwork, and I'm not scared about getting into anything harder. Here's my major constraints: * I need a solution that's reasonably cheap - I can spend up to a few hundred AUD total, but the cheaper the better. * It needs to be "spouse friendly" - I need to able to either hide it or turn it into a room feature. Panels look like they may work, but I don't know the dimensions of what I'd need or where I'd need to place it to know whether I could fit them or not. * I'd rather not mount it into any fixed structure (roof or wall) - while I could, I'd strongly prefer not to. I could compromise on the wall if necessary. Any ideas? If anyone can point me to the basics for a reasonably frequency targeted bass trap (80hz +- about 20hz) with the general principles behind the design, it'd be a big help. Full instructions or better ideas would be even better. Any and all suggestions are appreciated. :) Thanks in advance ... Edit: One other thing - corner mounting would be extremely difficult. Because everything's so tight, something that creates a triangle between the left / right walls and the rear wall would eat up too much space. We'd need to move the couch out to the point where's it's blocking the french doors.

Welcome to RSD too!
I believe (and I could be wrong) that a corner absorber would need greater depth than that, hence my interest in rear wall absorbers
Corner absorbers use the reflection from the adjoining walls of a corner (a celing is another wall in this message) to effectively double the effective amount of absorption at low frequencies. A wall or corner absorber uses the same depth for low end absorption. Good luck! Andre

Each speaker is 20cm (7.8 inches) from the side wall, 195cm (76.7 inches) from each other, 90cm (35.4 inches) from the front wall, and 320cm (126 inches) from the back wall. Sorry for not replying sooner - yesterday was a public holiday in Australia, and I decided to use it to build some broadband absorbers (based on one of the threads here). I used two strips per absorber of leftover wool roofing insulation (rated R3.5 for heat insulation) packed into a 35 cm deep plywood case I built. I built them out of ply so they'd be easy to hang and move. I'll take some photos when I get a chance and post them. They've already made a massive difference - eyeballing it, I've had a roughly 6db drop across the board from 200hz to 7000hz. That puts it pretty flat (+- somewhere roughly between 3db and 5db). The high frequencies are better, but are still too loud relative - that glass sure is a pain. Now I've got a personal reason to hurry up and get curtains ... 'Course, the best measure is that my wife's eyes bugged out when she heard the difference. I took them away and she immediately said she didn't like the sound of it. So, despite having these two massive padded boxes hanging on the wall, she doesn't actually want them out of the room anymore! She no longer thinks I'm mad. Well, not totally, anyway. :) The bass still exhibits the same problems - I've got a big dip at around 30hz, a peak at around 50hz, and a big dip at around 90hz.
