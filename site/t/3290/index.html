knightfly25 February 2005#6
IF the backing is facing OUTWARD, it will lessen the absorption of higher frequencies; this, and a slight tendency of the backing to act similar to a panel-type bass trap, could be construed as "trapping more bass" - However, I would think this effect would be VERY slight; normal panel traps operate by the front panel moving in response to sound pressure, then internally this pressure change is converted into air movement; finally, the air movement is converted to heat when it passes through (and back through) the absorbent. The absorbent (important point here) is NOT to be in contact with the front panel (unlike being actually attached to it) or else the panel becomes too damped, and little if any conversion takes place. Losses (trapping) in a panel trap occur first with the power required to flex the front panel, then by traveling both directions through the absorbent inside, and (if there's any sound energy left) by bouncing off the front panel and back into the absorbent again. Energy in the absorbent itself is converted to heat (by the resistance of the closely spaced fibers in the absorbent to the passage of air). Bottom line - Not so much... :?
