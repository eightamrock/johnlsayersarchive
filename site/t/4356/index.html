sharward20 September 2005#8
It very well might suffice. The main benefit that floated or isolated floors gives you is elimination or reduction of structure-borne noise (flanking). For what it's worth, I debated whether or not to float my floor for ages. Ultimately I received the best permission slip I could get from a local acoustical engineer who said that since the noise I'll be generating is airborne, and since I don't have anyone living underneath me, floating a floor in my case would most likely be overkill. There is also a tendency for people to float an insufficient floor, making matters worse than if they hadn't floated at all! :shock: So, while I'm not against floating floors, I think they're overrated and often insufficiently planned/calculated. My personal decision to replace my existing slab with a new, isolated one is mostly for structural reasons -- my existing concrete floor inside my garage is thinner and weaker than a typical household slab. Figuring that my inner leaf walls will weigh several tons, I didn't want to risk breaking the slab and ending up with a ruined project. In your case, being a standalone structure, my gut tells me you can probably get away with a single slab. With a liberally wide gap between your rooms, your iso should be awesome. You could design a gap so wide that you could walk inside of it and maybe even make it a storage closet. That way you're not really losing the square footage. 8) --Keith :mrgreen:

You've expressed sentiments to that many of us can relate, that's for sure! Think of your plan like something you're trying to sand smooth. You start with something rough and you use course sandpaper at first, then finer grained sandpaper, on down the line until you're using the smoothest paper you can find. In the end you have a smooth, highly polished plan. Right now you're just trying to figure out what plank of wood to start with! :lol: Sometimes I get a little apprehensive about recommending a particular plank, because what works for me may not work for someone else -- and given the fact that my construction work is just now getting underway, it's theoretically possible that what works for me may not actually work for me! :shock: :lol: Hang in there...
