Soundman202014 November 2010#4
Sad to say, but the dimensions are terrible. The room has an almost-square profile (2.21 x 2.25), fails two out of the three key tests cited by BBC engineers for minimum acceptability, and is classified by Bob Golds calculator as being a "A worst case scenario calculated by RPG". I'd really, really, really try to adjust either the width or the height as much as possible to get away from that ratio. If the outer shell is already built, then your only options are to make the room narrower or lower the ceiling. - Stuart -

gullfo17 November 2010#12
the speaker should be wrapped all around - top, bottom and sides and fit snug (rigidly) but not tight (so you avoid the vibrations going into the frame) otherwise use a separate stand internally. true enough but you don't want the speaker to necessarily contact the baffle plate - you make the speaker sit rigidly, you make the baffle sit rigidly, but you join them "softly" so the seal is there but the vibration transfer is none or minimal. if option one represents an opening though the filler to allow air flow, then yes.

gullfo28 November 2010#20
for baffle - use 25mm MDF, for slats, 25mm-40mm depending on specs. use hardwood, pine, or MDF. for "panels" use 1/2" (12mm) plywood. for the back panel of the soffits (lower baffle extension really) use 16mm plywood.for cloud framing 1-by (19mm) lumber. the rigid insulation - 100mm @ 48kg/m3 or thereabouts. for isolation wall air space, normal light (pink) insulation works (12-16kg/m3). non-hardening caulk. caulk is your friend :) for cloth, if you can breath through it, it should be good. for exposed cloth - fire retardant is a must. painting the framing to match the cloth color helps hide them. stick with the formula on the slat/slot ratios. wider slots tend to impact higher frequencies and might not be want you need.
