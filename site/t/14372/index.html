Soundman202011 May 2010#4
There is isolation from the outside world. All the walls are brick and I'm building a second wall for the outside facing wall (on the bottom of the design picture).
To be honest, you might as well save your money by not building that wall too. Sound from outside will simply flank around it, through the other walls, floor, ceiling, windows, doors, other rooms, etc. That wall is not going to make much of a difference at all, despite what you might think. Studio isolation is an "all-or-nothing" proposition. You have to do all of the walls, and the floor, and the ceiling, and the doors, and the windows. You cannot successfully isolate a room by just putting up a single leaf on one wall, any more than you can build an aquarium by putting only one sheet of glass in the frame! Fortunately, you already have brick walls, so they will give you some isolation from the outside world, but you only have a single leaf all around you. So your isolation will be something like STC-50, plus or minus a few points. Not bad, but it could be much better, and that's assuming that all of the doors and windows in the building are as good as the walls, which is very unlikely. Most probably, your isolation is going to be limited by the doors and windows in the building, and by the ceiling. Perhaps STC-thirty-something is what you'll get, or maybe forty-something if you are really lucky. It also seems rather pointless to use that double-glazed window. Firstly, it won't gain you very much in terms of isolation between your rooms, since the walls, doors and ceiling most likely aren't up to the same level, and in any case the window itself might end up resonating, and won't offer you any low-frequency isolation anyway, since it is made from ordinary thin window glass with a tiny air gap, not made from thick laminated glass over a very large air gap, which is what you really need. - Stuart -
