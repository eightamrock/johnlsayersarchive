That's a really low ceiling! :shock: Not much room for isolation up there, so hopefully you don't need any. But there's plenty of floor area there: enough to make a live room plus an iso booth, or a live room plus a small control room. If you want to, of course!

You might want to rethink your budget, big time! You will blow at least twice your entire budget just on that set of acoustic grade sliding glass doors.

Excellent! Then your final floor is finished!
but does cause a bit of a problem with dampness/moisture. Nothing a dehumidifier wont take care of.
:shock: :!: Oh ooo! Major red flag there: If you have damp problems right now, then you MUST solve those before you can build anything in there. And no, a dehumidifier will NOT take care of it. It will just make you think that you took care of it but the real problem will still be there, and still b causing damage as it grows and spreads. Damp problems in basements can only be solved from the OUTSIDE, by correcting the issue with the seal and drainage on the outside of the basement walls. Now is the time to call in a contractor to do this properly. Brien can fill you in on the details of how to go about it, but this needs to be fixed before you build anything in that basement. Damp is an indication that something is wrong, and needs to be fixed.
i live about 100 feet from a busy street, and about 100 yards from a highway where school buses and trucks pose a bit of a challenge.
OK, so you do need isolation. You are going to lose some ceiling height here, so I hope you are not too tall! Realistically, you are going to lose about 5" of headroom if you hope to get decent isolation.
with the ceiling height being 7ft 3 inches it will probably never be an optimal room for recording drums,
Exactly. And that will be down to under 7 feet by the time you are done isolating. There won't be any place to put the overhead mics. Or at least, there wont be any place where you can put them and expect to get really good drums sounds out of them. The ceiling artifacts will probably be too ugly. Close mic'ing should be fine, but it will be a challenge to get usable cymbal and ambient sounds.
but i got to thinking that a great sounding room for recording guitar or vocals might not be out of the question.
You should be OK with those.
Do i have the space to allow for a flattering (or at least closer to flat) room sound?
Well..... yes and no. You can treat any space that is reasonably larger than a closet to make it pretty much "dead" acoustically, but that won't necessarily make it "flat". And "dead" sounds pretty lousy, anyway: it is uncomfortable, and unpleasant. In fact, truly flat response is an elusive chimera, even in professionally designed, high end studios! And not desirable for a live room, anyway. There are two types of "flat": there is "flat" frequency response, and there is flat time-domain response, and they are two different issues. So the question is: can your room be treated to sound good for recording vocals and acoustic guitar? The answer is: yes, it can. I would suggest that you start out by defining how much isolation you need, in terms of decibels. To do that, you need to beg, borrow or buy a sound level meter. They aren't expensive: around US$ 50 will get you a half-decent used one on e-Bay, and a new one goes for around US$75 /but do avoid the cheap US$ Chinese junk that is flooding the market right now! Most of that is worthless.... Once you have determined how much isolation you need, then you can plan how to get that isolation (plenty of data right here on the forum to help you with that!), and once you have that in place, you will know what size your final room will be, so then you can start working on the design for the acoustic treatment that you will need. That's the normal process for designing a studio. - Stuart -
