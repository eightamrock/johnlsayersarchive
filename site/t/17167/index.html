Isolation doesn't depend just on wall thickness: it depends on a number of other factors, including what you are trying to isolate, and the materials available to you. You can build a fantastic isolation wall in less than 10 cm, but it will cost you a fortune as it needs some exotic materials. Or you could have a one meter thick wall that offers almost no isolation, built with other materials. In any event, 400 mm (that's nearly 16 inches!) sounds rather thick for just 40 dB TL! With that much space to build a wall, you should be get at least 70 dB, if built half decently, and even more if built really well.
We have the space on the left of the stairs of the which is 9m wide, 6m deep and 3.2m high. As there are rafters in the ceiling we can go up to 3.2m without touching them. You will also see from the blueprint that there are windows at the front and back of the building. It would be great to incorporate some of them into the studio rather than just blocking them off.
Are those windows operable, or fixed? Meaning; can you open them, or are they solidly glued in place? A couple of other comments on your layout: Machine Room! I don't see it. This is a video post facility, right? So where is your machine room going to be? Video equipment is noisy and needs cooling, especially if you have things like VTRs in there, or routing switchers, disk arrays, format converters, signal management, etc. Even basic sync generations and DA's need to live somewhere! And your editing systems should be in the machine room as well, if you want them to last long and run well. HVAC: I don't see that either. It can take up a lot of space for video post, especially if you also need a quite system with good isolation between rooms. I've been in some facilities where they never considered HVAC until it was too late, and it is MOST unpleasant to work under those conditions! And I've been in others that I helped design, where the HVAC was practically inaudible, and isolation so good that you sometimes wondered if you were alone in the building, and all the equipment was turned off! There's about a million other things that need to be considered in designing a video post facility, but let's start with the above, to get you on the right path. Anyway, please check the posting rules, and fill in that "missing detail". Also, please provide more details on what you plan to do in those rooms, and on the construction of the building itself. Sounds like a fun project! Right up my street, too! :) - Stuart -

There's several ways of laying out your rooms, but it would be good to get more details on your design goals, and the full purpose of each room. For example, you show "Mix studio A" and also "Edit Studio", plus two off-line suites and "Audio Edit Suit 1". What do you actually mean by these terms? Different people use different terminology. The two off-line suites I get: no need to explain those. But what happens in "Audio Edit Suit 1", and how is that different from what happens in "Mix studio A" and in "Edit Studio"? In sound studios, we normally speak of the "control room", where the actual console and speakers are, and all the magic takes place, which is generally the heart of the facility. It's where the actual tracking (recording) mixing and maybe also mastering are done, and needs to be as acoustically perfect as you can make it, with absolutely neutral frequency response and carefully controlled RT60 times that meet the normal recommendations for each frequency band. Then you have normally have a "live room" where acoustic instruments are played and recorded (drums, bass, pianos, keyboards, guitars, etc.), and one or more "isolation booths" or "vocal booths", for recording individual voices or instruments. I'm guessing that your "Edit Studio" is what we would call a "control room", and your "Mix studio A" is a "live room"? Is that correct, or did I confuse myself there?
What would be the ideal space for the main mix room in the area even if this means we have to lose some of the video suites to the other side of the floor?
If we are talking "control room", then the ITU and EBU recommendations for critical listening rooms, are a total room volume of greater than 1500 ft3 (roughly 43 m3). There are also recommended ratios of room dimensions (height vs. width vs. length), so based on your available room height you can look at possible footprints that would give you at least 43 m3 in total room volume. If we are talking about "Mix Studio A", you are allowing 5.5m length and the available height is 3m, so your room needs to be at least 2.6m wide to meet that criteria. However, that volume is the FINAL volume of the room, after installing all the isolation walls, so you'd need to add a bit to that, to get good volume. Also, if you splay your walls and/or ceiling for a good RFZ (or any other reason) then that reduces final room volume, so you'd need to consider that as well. If we are talking about "Edit Studio", then you are spot on 42 m3, but once again you need to allow for the isolation walls, treatment, etc.
Yes it is a tight space but it is imperative we have 2 separate sound proofed rooms for the bulk of music projects. Also, the main room would ideally have a sofa at the back of the room so clients can attend mix session and listen to final mixes.
All of that can be done. You have the space, and there's no reason why you can't have two great rooms, both with sofas if you want. But once again, it would be useful to know what the exact purpose of each room is! - Stuart -
