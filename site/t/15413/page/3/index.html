JohnH16 March 2011#61
A little more progress. Just about all of the framing is done. We've decide to put strapping up on the ceiling to stiffen up the "floating inner structure", but that will have to wait until I do the electrical wiring and put up the insulation. I ran the circular saw through the OSB subfloor, between the inner and outer walls. Then did some impact tests on the floor using a 16 oz. carpenters hammer and my hand as a sensor. (uncalibrated; but one of my fingers might still be certified from years of vibration analysis work in industry :blah: http://www.nature.com/news/2009/090129/ ... 09.68.html) The difference is sustantial. Before cutting, you could clearly feel the the impulses transfer laterally throught the OSB (flanking) After cutting, it was hard to detect at all. The Green Glue joist tape does seem to do some dampening as they claimed. It's a subjective test but still valid. The inner walls are isolated on 1/2" thick soft rubber (see previous post for weight caluculations and psi). The foam "nerf toy" washers compressed and stayed flat, so I removed them and cut out squares of rubber (same as used under the plates) and used those under the large fender washers. The lag bolts were tightened enough to compress the washer pads about 75%. This is so when the weight of the sheet rock is added, and the wall plate rubber cushion compresses approx 25% (hopefully thereabouts) the large washers under the lag bolts will not be rattling around inside the wall when I play a bass note. Did another subjective test: Hit the outer wall framing with the bottom of my fist while feeling the inner wall framing..............the outer wall studs vibrated visibly at a low but audible frequency.........but again I felt nothing on the inner wall studs. This is good. The inner structure is sucessfully isolated from the outer structure, (except for the soft rubber it sits on, and the isolating bolts, made out of neoprene fuel line, fender washers and more rubber pads which seem to be working rather well, so far). So if you HAVE to put your music room on an elevated structure AND you CAN build an isolated frame for the subfloor, (joists not attached to existing framing anywhere), using joist tape and cutting your subfloor does help. Of course an isolated concrete slab is the way to go, but not always possibly for amateur studio's. So using all the isolation tricks you can, will still improve the situation with a wood frame structure to a noticable degree, especially when compared to normal costruction methods. With the framing done, I've picked up my ventilation and electrical supplies and plan on starting wiring tommorow. Then we can go back to stuffing more insulation in the new walls and ceiling, then on to more layers of sheetrock (gypsum wall board). Our electric code does allow us to just poke electrical wires through a small hole in the wall into a surface mounted electrical box (with a strain relief clamp) (like wiremold brand by LeGrand, I found at Home Despot) The hole can then be filled with acoustic sealant caulking. After reading all the other posts, this seems to be the fastest and easiest way to go. No worries about cutting large holes in the walls, putty pads around outlet boxes, special framing boxes etc. The surface mount outlets were $5.99 each which included the outlet (receptacle) and the wall (cover) plate. 8) PS: I did angle the front side walls at 12 degrees. Here are two photos. More to come. (Got to take the clients bowling, it's Wednesday) Cross bracing is temporary until sheet rock is installed.
