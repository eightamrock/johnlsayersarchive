There's something with your second (this) link. I've tested (somewhere begin 90ties) these things of your last link (Rockwool duct isolation) in a couple of diameters (huge ones with thick wall thickness) in 1/4 pieces as corner absorbers. I know them well since I worked a lot in these industrial applications. Very roughly you can say that they give comparable effect with flat boards stradling the corner. They also show this typical peak behavior. I haven't tested them in full circels. If you use them I think I should slightly prefer fibreglass versions over rockwool versions, not for acoustic reasons but because fibreglass is a little bit less brittle than rockwool, making it a bit more fun to work with (less easily damaged). But both are good to use This is all too extensive, but in general, while looking cool, it is comparable with the traditional methods of boards stradling the corners. And this duct isolation in large sizes is relative much more expensive than flat boards. Hence I haven't tested all possible combinations, as such I draw conclusions in analogy. I should only use the principle (1/4, 1/2, 1/1 pieces) as part of some cool design (is subjective of course), not because it should give some special enhanced absorption.
