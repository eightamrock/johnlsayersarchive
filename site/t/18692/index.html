That should not be too hard to do. It is a reasonable goal.
The Hollow space around between the inner actual room and the structural wall is ventilated with a perforated grid door. ... The inner room is concrete Hollow blocks for walls, concrete floor slab and the ceiling is concrete-pot-concrete.
The reason for the lack of isolation is very likely due to the "perforated grid door". Please post a photo of that. With the structure you already have, you should be getting very good isolation, so the cause of poor isolation is almost certainly because the two leaves are not sealed properly, and there are holes or cracks where air can get through. The photos show that the mortar work on those concrete blocks is very poorly done, so there are very probably multiple air paths through that wall. So before you build anything, I would suggest that you first seal all of the walls and the ceiling. I would plaster over that side of the wall, then seal the plaster with masonry sealant once it is fully cured. You also need to replace that "grid door" with a solid door that has good seals all around, and check carefully all around the room for any other cracks, holes or passages for air, such as through the electrical system, for example. Then check your levels again, to see how much it has improved. - Stuart -
