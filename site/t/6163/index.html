But in the mean time I wanted to get your opinions on plastering the walls and ceiling because I've not seens it mentioned on this site. Thanks Len, I guess if I do it now, I need to make sure there's a small gap at the bottom before it meets the floor. One other thing regarding plastering - my builers are telling me that we shouldn't put the caulk/mastic between the joints of the final layer of plasterboard (they say they need the small gap to help apply the plaster to the plasterboard). I think I need to seal all the gaps before plastering even though the plaster will seal the gaps itself. What do you think?
len-morgan5 May 2006#4
Are you talking about filling ALL the joints with caulk? That is generally NOT necessary. You want to fill wall-to-wall, wall-to-cieling, and wall-to-floor joints but not the joints between the sheets of drywall on walls/ceilings. In other words, where you have a corner of any sort, you want to calk. Anywhere else, standard joint compound is fine. len
Temas5 May 2006#5
But in some places they have not managed to get a tight fit in between the boards and there is often a small gap. I have been filling these gaps with an acoustic sealant (which is probably quite similar to the joint compound). For the seal between the floor and walls, I have been using a silicon based sealant. Thanks again.
knightfly10 May 2006#6
Joint compound and sealant are NOT similar; joint compound dries hard as a rock and has about the same density as gypsum wallboard, while sealants typically have around half the density and a good sealant NEVER dries entirely, staying flexible enough to not allow any cracks to leak air. If you'll be plastering, the plaster will fill the cracks between sheets - with a multi-layer wall, you should have no joints in the first layer coincident with joints in the second layer, so even if there becomes a crack in the plaster where two boards meet it won't go all the way through. I'd listen to your plasterer but don't let them plaster up to adjoining walls/floors, etc; there, you DO want acoustic sealant... Steve
