guitardad7212 December 2006#4
n8nod9it Maybe you should start a single construction thread so that we can follow your thinking path of question and use pics you've already posted to help us, help you. Guessing from your other thread and questions... I do not think you plan to rip/demo part or all of your garage slab out and create an isolated slab. You're existing garage slab would have been poured with no iso in mind, the perimeter of that slab directly touches every thing around it, making it not isolated. An isolated slab will not touch anything around it, there is a gap between it and other slabs, footers and/or walls. Typically inner walls will sit on a new iso slab, outer walls sit on (most likely) existing slab and footers. BUT if I'm guessing wrong and you do plan to create an iso slab (like Sharward) then we will all enjoy pics of you doing this :D Floating the walls (with inner ceiling resting on inner walls) is just as hard to figure out as floating a floor (almost). That is getting the right compression on pads which will not over do it (not having enough pads/wall to heavy, squashing pads so they are useless) or not having enough compression on pads (to many pads/wall to light and again effect is useless). A careful calculation of weight of construction is needed to figure it out and I think margin for error is about 10 to 20% (need to verify that in Shardwards thread or a post by Knightfly somewhere). U-boats: The manufacturer will not release tech specs on the product... this was in a thread recently, advice is do not buy from a company who has either not tested or will not release tech specs. SO if you're still planning on 2 layers 5/8" drywall or equivalent on each inner and outer wall, then NO floating the walls is not needed or will be to hard to figure out for a marginal gain in iso. Just use sill seal available from HD or Lowes in between slab and PT bottom plates of walls to elimate gaps from imperfections in slab and bottom plate. 1/4" gap around perimeter of all drywall "complete walls" is adequate and necessary to stuff backer rod and acoustic caulk into and obtain the best iso result from wall assembly. If you are like me, an amatuier drywall cutter, then you may want a few different sizes of backer rod to account for bad or ragged edges drywall cuts hear and there. DanF's "ode to backer rod" somewhere in his thread is a good read. BTW, floating floor is not a good idea either... unless you float concrete which is expensive. Floating a wooden deck will make the deck amplify like a drum head instead of increase isolation UNLESS you build very correctly, very well measured, planned out and use something like (memory failing) 4 to 5 layers of drywall on walls. Hope that helps. Marc
