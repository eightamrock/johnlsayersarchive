Once again, sorry for straying into the studio domain, but somehow it seems that the sound insulation expertise is hanging here... I'm building a home theater in my basement and try as good as I can to make it well sound insulated. This means room-in-room, gypsum+ GG + gypsum + GG + gypsum on ceiling/walls, double sound insulated doors etc. etc. Now, I'm considering in-wall surround speakers and what I'm trying to figure out is how bad this is for the sound insulation, punching through the walls like this. Before yelling, please look at the sketch below (seen from above). My plan is to add the same amount of layers behind/between the studs as the rest of the wall. The surround speakers, as you may know, will be high passed at more or less 80Hz. So the worst bass (problematic frequencies sound insulation wise) will come from other speakers. I don't expect anyone to actually have tested this, but theoretically - how bad is this solution? :oops:

Cram4 March 2008#4
in your image, we're looking from above at a speaker enclosure. In the back of the enclosure you have layers of wall board. On the sides of the enclosure, the drawing just shows framing - as if we're looking at the header of your framework around the enclosure. Wouldn't the same layers of wall board have to be on all 5 sides of the box? Also think about what you're going to do with placing the speaker within this hole - are you going to try to buffer the contact with the speaker and the speaker enclosure? Some people have gone as far as making an enclosure that goes all the way from the speaker location to the floor. They basically have tubes of cement (mass) sitting on pucks/pads that the speakers would rest upon. Then creating a false or true front to house the concrete tube. good luck. those are a couple things I noticed while poking around this site for the last year.

That peice is in the wall. I'd also consider what xSpace said with regard to the Perminent nature of a speaker housing. Your question about the studs not being mass - Only wall board or something as dense as wallboard (mdf, concrete) is going to qualify as MASS. The framing of a structure is resonant or more resonant. Hell wood's born to vibrate. IF you were going to go the route of your drawing starts with, I'd have the entire inner housing fitted with one or more layers of wallboard and the wire would have to be caulked in nice and snug to stop any air gaps. I hope all this is a help to you. There's another fine example of one of those platforms, but it's on an older build thread and I cannot find it just yet.
