the ceiling is on joists supported by the inner isolation walls. the ceiling is (from inside to outside) 2x 16mm, joists 1 layer of 100mm rigid insulation then fill with pink insulation, air gap, overhead concrete. storage room wall (from inside out) 2x 16mm, stud, 4" rigid insulation, 1" gap, stud (existing), 4" rigid insulation, 2x 16mm for the floor, if you have Rod's 2nd edition book on home recording studio construction, he has put forth a nice option: put down 2" of 48kg/m3 rigid insulation then on top, 2x 12mm plywood, then your flooring (say 12mm engineered wood). this all sits within the inner isolation walls and is separated from them by about 12mm. if you think you need the walls isolated as well, he has an example using Mason Industry ND isolators on a sleeper to isolate the walls from the floor. this combination should give you substantial isolation but will take more work.

There are are no more "extra few dB" in there! If you are decoupled then you are decoupled: decoupling again serves no purpose. That would be about as useful as using an umbrella indoors: the roof already stopped the rain, so you don't need the umbrella too! Same thing: if your walls are correctly decoupled, then RC won't buy you any more isolation. - Stuart -

Soundman202019 October 2010#55
I'm assuming if we put the second layer on the inside between the studs that would create a third leaf?
No it would not create a third leaf, and that is exactly what Glenn is referring to. This is what is called "beefing up". It means that you carefully cut strips of drywall to fit IN BETWEEN the studs, hard up against the drywall on the other side of the studs. Meaning that there is no air gap between the new strips of drywall and the existing drywall. Tye are in direct contact. Do not glue them! You can use Greenglue if you want, but not glue. Hold them in place with cleats, and seal around the edges with caulk. You can do two layers like that if you want. - Stuart -

Soundman202019 October 2010#60
Also could I beef up the concrete wall to ensure the isolation is sufficient or would that not make much difference?
Well, you could, but you'd need an awful lot of drywall to make any difference! IIRC concrete is about 3 times more dense than drywall, so assuming that your wall is about 6" thick, then adding 18" of drywall to that would theoretically give you an extra 3dB of isolation. That would be something like 30 sheets of drywall.... :shock: In other words: beefing up concrete is almost pointless. Not needed, won't make any difference at all. However, where you CAN make a difference is to put more mass on your inner leaf. More layers there will definitely improve things: Ideally, the masses on each leaf should be the same, but you already have so much in the concrete that you don't need to go to incredible extremes. Probably 3 layers of 5/8 with GG, plus a large air gap (say 10") will get you pretty decent isolation. And fill that cavity with as much insulation as code allows and you can afford. There are studies showing that fully filling the air gap with insulation can gain you anywhere up to 16 dB of isolation. I'd also think of putting drums and amps on risers, and perhaps even using Rod's new suggestion for floors: Glenn outlined it earlier, a few posts up. That should give you the extra bit that you need to keep the impact noise out of the building structure. - Stuart -
