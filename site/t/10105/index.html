Recycled Bowling Lanes as Floor in Live Space...?

Started by joewally on 27 January 2008. 5 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 10105.

joewally27 January 2008#1
I have an unusual flooring question: I am tearing down the woodworking shed in my back yard and am building a bigger 24'x24' garage for my studio. I recently drove by a bowling alley that went out of business. There were contractors there cutting the actual bowling lanes out in 10-12 foot sections and throwing them out. They told me I could have all I wanted, so now I have enough to floor at least the live space. It's essentially a bunch of 1x2" pieces of hardwood glued together, supported by 2 inch angle irons underneath every 2 feet (very, VERY heavy). The garage will be on a concrete slab and although I don't need it, a bowling alley floor would be unique! In your honest opinions, was getting these a waste of my time? It took a LOT of work getting them to my house and I'd like to use them if at all possible.
xSpace30 January 2008#2
I would think the hardwood would be a nice addition, just because it has an interesting history. The metal angle with the hardwood, on top of a concrete slab...remember what those bowling allies sound like? Someone can correct if need be, but this is reads like a mass/air/mass configuration. I do not know how that affects you but I am thinking your noise floor level literally raises. I'd look into removing the metal angles and keeping the wood. That stuff is expensive to purchase so I would keep it, even if I only used it in the house bathroom ;)
