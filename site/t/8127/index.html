gullfo8 February 2007#6
well your options in such a small room will be limited by your desire keep the offset window and the closet. you could at the very least put some trapping in the front corners (partially block the window), add some heavy drapes over the window, put some absorption on the side walls and hang on the closet and entry doors. you can also add some to the wall-ceiling "corners" as well. a ceiling cloud and carpet will also assist. Kendale's diagram was right on the money but obviously you have constraints so you should take the suggestions and apply them where you can. more research on this site will also answer most of your questions regarding materials and options. getting an acoustically correct environment by yourself requires study and thought. trying to do it in sub-optimal spaces is particularly difficult.

jwl8 February 2007#7
I'd go with gullfo's plan, except I'd do even more. I'd also put superchunks along the wall/ceiling corner above the window, along the floor/wall corner below the window, and along the side wall/ceiling corners to well behind the listening position, maybe all the way. I'd also probably put John's angled slot resonators along the side walls (below the top superchunks) aiming the sound toward the back of the room. The rear short wall next to the little door hallway would then need a higher degree of absorption since the side wall reflections will be aimed there.
