Schroeder21 May 2005#35
Hi! First to my question: I asked you about the ceiling not because of acoustical reasons. I just wondered how the psychological impression of a low black ceiling could be. You know - people always make ceilings as light as possible to make them look higher. But John`s advice was to make especially very low ceilings black so they should look higher. Therefore I wanted to ask you about your experience with a black ceiling in a low basement? We will only apply little absorption to the ceiling because they are very low (2.10m). I`m very contented with all the rest of our design. We will probably be planning another 2 weeks until the building phase will finally start! :-) To give you an impression I`ll attach a pic... (we`re not finished with all details in autocad...) To your room: You seemingly have not enough bass absorption. What about using all "unused" space for absorbers? You wouldn`t lose too much then... First try some different microphone positions when measuring. Some frequencies could be canceled when your exactly in an null point. My proposal: - Soffit mount your speakers. Apply basstraps in the soffits (below and above the monitors). Do you have monitors with adjustable bass-rolloff?? Soffit mounting will increase bass level around 6dB, don`t forget about that! - You didn`t fill the space above the ceiling plates with rockwool, do you? Fill it. Will improve absorption, too. - Make your backwall "one level". Simply apply rockwool around your diffusers, cover it with cloth. Then your diffusers are "flush mounted", looks better, will reduce early reflections from behind and probably will add little (higher) bass absorption. regards, Schroeder

First of all I would really try to do some additional measuring with different mic positions, so you can be sure that these both frequencies are REALLY a problem in mixing position (Make sure you won`t sit right in the centre, in the middle between front and back wall). Check your modes. With your dimensions (3,80m, 3,10m, 2,21m) I get modes which don`t really match with your problem frequencies. Right dimensions? Nevertheless, check your modes and find out between which walls these modes are resonanting. Apply the absorber there. Use a helmholtz resonator which is tuned to your 84 Hz. In general I think any bass absorption will smoothen out the curve (think of the null - you can`t raise the curve after all....) . When you add tuned absorbers additionally, it should be perfect. High frequencies: Don`t know whether I`m right or not, but it seems that you`ve got lots of high frequency damping although your absorbers are covered with perforated metal plates? Ceiling absorbs too much highs? But neverthelesss: The more bass absorption you are going to apply, the smoother the whole response will be. Just take care of your highs by covering (for instance on the back wall) the absorbers with thin plates/foil. Another issue: There are always less highs because they are absorbed by air and all the surfaces in a much higher degree then the other frequencies...
Make your backwall "one level". Simply apply rockwool around your diffusers, cover it with cloth. Then your diffusers are "flush mounted", looks better, will reduce early reflections from behind and probably will add little (higher) bass absorption. Schroeder31 May 2005#39
Yeah! Looks much better now! :-] For absorption of bass the surface doesn`t matter. It goes straight through... You`ll have more absorption of the high frequencies when you cover the rockwool with cloth and more highs left and absorption of the mids when you apply the perforated metal / slats, as you probably know. With slats you can tune the absorber to your special problem frequencies. You can`t change the holes in the metal pieces, can you? Just do some measuring after you`ve filled the rockwool in - without any surface. Let your measurings be your guide on that decision - cloth, metal, tuned slats? Fill the space completely with rockwool. That should work fine and doesn`t cost much. Bye, Schroeder PS. Last week of making plans in autocad...
