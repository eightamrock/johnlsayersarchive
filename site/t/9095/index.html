sharward11 July 2007#4
Is there some reason this discussion is happening in a new thread instead of a continuation of the original thread you started on this topic back in February? :roll: Part of the problem with having multiple threads is that it's difficult to keep track of the information given and the advice/answers/feedback given in response. From the original thread, it seemed you were agreeable to doing separate frames, which will be far superior performance. It wouldn't surprise me if staggered stud performed better at some frequencies and worse at others due to the widening of the gap (good) and the flanking path of the plates (bad)... gulfo's point about using RC on the staggered stud walls would likely lessen the flanking, but it pushes the first layer of drywall into the room another 1/2" or so. That makes your framing a total of 5 3/4" thick, whereas double frames would be (at minimum) 7 1/4" thick, a difference of only 1 1/2" -- which leads me to the obvious question of why double frames aren't on the table for discussion right now. :?: Whenever someone brings up staggered studs here, I for one always try to talk people into doing separate frames, primarily because so many folks coming to the forum don't even know they're better and are simply moving forward with the assumption that "staggered studs are the best for isolation." I know this because I was one of those people! :lol: Really, I think your best bet is to start over at the beginning. How about starting a new thread that contains all the information we asked for in the "BEFORE YOU POST, YOU MUST READ AND FOLLOW THESE INSTRUCTIONS" announcement thread. You seem to be intent on just asking seeming simple "A or B" type questions, but the best advice will follow a full-fledged introduction with lots of detail, particularly in the areas including (but not limited to) existing construction, square footage, layout, obstacles, environmental surroundings, how much isolation you need, and of course, budget. In the absence of that, I think I'll have to resort to answering all future questions with a generic and slightly smart-assed "It depends." ;-) --Keith :mrgreen:

jamminji wrote:
Anyway I am more then happy to put rc on but if it is not needed then I would rather put the money into gear.
Jam, reality is that you get a slight benifit form RC channel on a staggered wall - but not what you would get by adding another layer of drywall - The biggest gain with RC on this wall is for frequencies above 315 Hz - which is where your problems lay....... Here is test data on 2 walls taken from the IRC -IR761 study. TestID TL-93-251 2x4 staggered w/ f.g. insul dbl 5/8 ea side STC 57 50 Hz 21.0 63 Hz 21.8 80 Hz 25.3 100 Hz 32.0 125 Hz 36.4 160 Hz 38.7 200 Hz 44.1 250 Hz 51.7 315 Hz 55.0 400 Hz 56.8 500 Hz 56.9 630 Hz 57.3 800 Hz 59.3 1000 Hz 61.0 1250 Hz 62.6 1600 Hz 60.3 2000 Hz 53.1 2500 Hz 53.7 3150 Hz 60.2 4000 Hz 65.5 5000 Hz 69.5 6300 Hz 75.0 TestID TL-93-216 2x4 staggered w/ f.g. insul RC-1 1 side, dbl 5/8 ea side STC 62 50 Hz 20.0 63 Hz 21.9 80 Hz 28.9 100 Hz 35.8 125 Hz 37.9 160 Hz 42.6 200 Hz 47.8 250 Hz 54.1 315 Hz 57.4 400 Hz 61.1 500 Hz 65.2 630 Hz 67.5 800 Hz 70.9 1000 Hz 72.0 1250 Hz 74.4 1600 Hz 73.8 2000 Hz 66.6 2500 Hz 65.2 3150 Hz 71.7 4000 Hz 75.3 5000 Hz 78.7 6300 Hz 82.1 I guess the bottom line is for you to look at the potential gains and determine if that little improvement in the low frequencies still makes using it worth while. Sincerely, Rod
