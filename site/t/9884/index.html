myfipie14 December 2007#2
AlexHoward, If you are new at all of this stuff and your room is small then I would stick with broadband. Not that tuned traps are super cool, but I read so many times that guys have: 1)Tested the room wrong and built a tune trap wrong 2)Use a mode calculator, built a tuned trap only find out that the calculation was not correct. 3)Just guessed and built it completely wrong. There is no reason why you can not start with OC products or mineral wool, build or buy some bass traps and put them into the corner. If the room is still not the way you want it then start to look into tuned trapping. But I bet you (if the room is small) you can get 90% of the way there with just the broad band approach The other option (which if you want only tuned trapping) is to hire a person to do it for you. If they mess up then it is on there head and not "out of your pocket". That is where guys like John come in. Glenn

gullfo15 December 2007#8
AlexHoward wrote:
However here is my plan, as I understand right angle corners need traps, as they will create standing waves… correct? do you always need bass traps at to the rear of the control room?
the angled wall in the back of the CR is a bass trap. also, you'll build bass traps overhead, the front corners, and the entire back wall should be trapping. same for the live room, vox booth, etc. corners don't create standing waves. corners are where all room modes terminate eventually so trapping there make that location very effective.
