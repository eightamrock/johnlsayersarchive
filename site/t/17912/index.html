Hi "nick303", and welcome! :) Sorry about the delay: lots going on here, and I'm playing "catch-up".
I am looking at the feasibility of converting what I like to call the "loft" area above my garage into a studio space.
That is basically the same as a second-floor studio, which introduces some important factors: it is very hard to isolate a second floor studio, since the amount of mass you need to do so successfully is generally much larger than the structural limits of the rest of the building. So your options are basically: 1) try to move the studio to another room, on the ground floor. 2) realize that you won't be able to isolate it, and just live with the consequences.
It does get loud in the studio sometimes. If I'm working on a track destined for a dance floor somewhere I like to get the monitors / sub moving and get a sense of what the experience would be like in a club. Not club volumes by any means, but at least loud enough to get a feel for the track at louder volumes.
It sounds like you do need isolation, so moving to the ground floor would be your real option here.
I will pull up the subfloor, but obviously the framing will remain as-is.
Why do you want to pull up the sub-floor? Also, what is the rating for the existing floor joists? How much extra load can they handle? That is a major question here: you probably need to call in a structural engineer to analyze what you have, and tell you what load you can put on there safely.
basically the lowest part of the ceiling is 6' and the highest part is 7' and this is what I am uncertain about. Can I build the space with proper acoustic treatment and still have a reasonable ceiling height (how much will the 7' height be reduced to)? Is the space itself suitable given its shape / layout?
That depends on several things, but first and foremost it depends on how much isolation you need. If you decide to go with no isolation at all then you can use the space up there between the trusses for isolation, and not lose any ceiling height. But if you need isolation then you are going to lose several inches, maybe around 4 to 10 inches, depending on your isolation needs and the construction method you choose.
The big question is do I have enough ceiling height to make this work?
In order to answer that question, you need to answer another one first: "How much isolation do you need?" In terms of decibels. That's the starting point. You need to dust off your sound level meter, and take some measurements to determine that. Based on the number you come up with, you can look through the documentation to find all of the construction methods that will give you that much isolation, then choose the one that best meets your needs and your budget. - Stuart -
