Double Leaf ceiling?

Started by Death By Darwin on 30 April 2010. 3 replies. In the Library under Walls, floors and ceilings.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 14332.

Death By Darwin30 April 2010#1
Just a quick question, would one normally consider the sheathing of a roof and shingles and whatnot as an outer-leaf and the ceilling hanging under the roof trusses as the inner-leaf of a two leaf system? or is the "attic" space generally large enough to be considered like a separate room itself? I am just wondering if I have a existing ceiling and under it build a "room within a room" with it's own suspended ceiling, would the actual roof sheathing not constitute a third leaf? Regards, -jER
John Sayers30 April 2010#2
shingles or sheets of iron wouldn't be considered a leaf as they aren't airtight so don't cause a spring in a MSM setup.
Soundman20201 May 2010#3
I am just wondering if I have a existing ceiling and under it build a "room within a room" with it's own suspended ceiling, would the actual roof sheathing not constitute a third leaf?
Even if the roof deck does act like a third leaf, the air gap in the attic space is so large that the resonant frequency of the system would most likely still be plenty low enough that it wouldn't be an issue. - Stuart -
