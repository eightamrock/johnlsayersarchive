Soundman202029 August 2009#5
I see Javier's point. The ceiling angle over the mix position is only 2 degrees. Ideally, it should be around 12 degrees. I don't think 2 degrees is going to be very useful. The rear section of the ceiling (behind the mix position) is angled at about 7 degrees, which is much better but still not ideal. Continuing that same rear ceiling line would bring the front of the ceiling down to the top of the window. That would make it pretty low, but perhaps still usable. And 7° is better than 2°. Another option would be to do what Javier suggested: Leave the ceiling flat there, and just used angled treatment. Third idea: Bring the front of the ceiling down half way to the top of the window, angle the ceiling up until it nearly reaches that central beam, joist, duct or whatever it is, then flatten it out from there back (or even angle it up again once yo get past the beam), You can get an angle of 10° like that. Maybe more, if you fiddle with the angles and construction methods. - Stuart -
