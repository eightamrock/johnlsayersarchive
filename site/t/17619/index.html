Our only acoustic challenges for the rooms are for them to be good enough to get good vocals that can be sent of to mixing. I feel that I wasn't clear enough in the description of my concern. So just trying to clarify things. Firstly Room Dimensions: Room 1: 6000mm x 3500mm Room 2: 3000mm x 3500mm Room 3: 2500mm x 4500mm (decided to make that room longer by coming into the storage space) ASSUMED PROBLEM: So as originally planned from walls C to E would have been... Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock -> Stud 90mm x 45mm (with insulation) -> Gyprock -> Airspace (250mm, Due to existing pillars) -> Gyprock -> Stud (with insulation) -> Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock. My concern is that this in theory is a triple leaf wall. From all the reading I have done here and also in 'Google' research, I believe that this is a problem. I understand that when there is a large airspace that this problem is not too big of a deal. In this scenario the airspace that I can have between the two walls is 250mm, though this is larger than in most cases I've read about, it feels like its not enough and should not be avoided as it will overall become a triple leaf wall. For the other walls A,D,H, & G Plan was to just go Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock -> Stud 90mm(with insulation) -> Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock. All framing being 90mm x 45mm hence having an airspace of 90mm, though it being a double leafed wall. My Solution for the ASSUMED PROBLEM: Remove the middle layers of Gyprock between walls C and E. So now it would be: Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock -> Stud (with insulation) -> Airspace (250mm, Due to existing pillars, which now simple gives more airspace) -> Stud (with insulation) -> Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock. This gives an airspace of about 430mm (actually about 480mm, because framing sits a little inside of the pillars) between those walls. It should solves my third leaf issue, and gives me a large airspace which hopefully is more effective than the previous setup. For walls A,D,H & G, build a second set of framing on the outside of the pillars maintaining an 430mm airspace all the way around. Then as planned double layering all Gyprock with Green glue sandwiched in between... So now becoming Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock -> Stud (with insulation) -> Airspace (250 mm) -> Stud (with insulation) -> Gyprock -> Green Glue -> Gyprock From here on there is a minimum 2000mm distance to the next walls (which are concrete/Brick walls) then another 1200mm corridor space followed a concrete/brick wall and neighboring businesses (Wall A side has a quite ally way street and then the next building). So my questions: 1) Did I have a problem to begin with? 2) If so, does my solution solve the issue, if not, what would be a better solution? 3) Do these designs give us enough isolation between each room? For example to be recording vocals (just pop, nothing fancy :P ) in two rooms and in the other tracking some acoustic guitar, while someone is cooking in the kitchen and another is on the phone in the lounge area. Not expecting for it to be dead quite, but getting everything down to a murmur would be great. 4) If everything is done to my solution, how should the ceiling area be tackled, between the pillars (Aprox 480mm airspace)? Treat it as if it were a room, with two layers of gyprock with green glue and cover the 'roof' side with 19mm particle board? The ceiling joists are 140mm with insulation, so hoping that there is enough airspace for it to be not a concern. A note for anyone conserned about mass, we can always add more mass to the interior and exterior walls with a layer of green glue and gyprock in the future if we are having problems. Right now its just getting it right (airspace, wall leafs etc) to get by. We're not on the tightest of budgets but should be kept in mind we have $20 000 for all 3 rooms to be done. We have so far spent about $5000 on all gyprock, framing pine, insulation, 48 tubes of green glue, 12 tubes of green glue sealant and electrical for Room 1. We are ordering materials and building in 2 phases (Phase 1: room 1, followed by Phase 2: Room 2&3) as we don't have the floor space for all the materials along with the equipment and furniture that we already have there. Hope this clears things up now, and gives you guys a better understanding of what we are doing. Thanks in advance
