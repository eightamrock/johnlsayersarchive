I couldn't seem to find blue prints for and iso room, so I whipped up some drawings in Illustrator for an 8' x 8' x 7' tall iso room. The details for the door section, ceiling or floor are not fleshed out yet, but ... this is what I've come up with so far. What I'm using this iso room for: Maximum-ish sound containment for quiet drums in a second floor apartment (with no one above or below and easy going side neighbors) and also ultra quiet inside for vocals and acoustic guitar. My concern is building each wall 8' in two 4' x7' sections so I can get them into my apartment and connect them inside (with the drywall and plywood already screwed on). 1. I'd love some feedback on how I might connect the sections to each other once I get them propped up on the decoupled floor (yet to be drawn, but probably plywood on a 2"x2" frame. 2. I'd love some feedback on the layering order and thickness of drywall, plywood and fiber. I left out that I'll probably have some standard R13 fiberglass insulation filling the insides of the walls too, or Bonded Logic. 3. The Owens Corning 705 comes in six packs of 2'x4' panels... would it be worth the extra expense to cover the full 7' of the inside walls (for maximum sound containment). 4. Should I use the same layering and design for the ceiling or could I get away with a lighter ceiling design?

Also, in consideration for the knock down design,(where your illustration reads "big locking clamps or something?") you could install 2 studs on one wall section and keep one stud on the other at that joint. This would allow you to install your sheating materials projected past the single stud to create an 1-1/2" pocket. This pocket would allow the double studded section to be inserted into this pocket and be fastened together. The double stud section does two things. One it creates a backer for the sheathing applied to it up to that first stud. You then leave your sheathing materials 1-1/2" short on the double studded wall. Hope that makes sense,
