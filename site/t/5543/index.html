knightfly1 February 2006#2
For the floor, I will lay 2 x 4's on their sides and will have them sitting on 1/2" Neoprene cut into strips. I am then going to nail 3/4" MDF on the 2 x 4's and that will essentially be the new floor. This can be a recipe for disaster - first, check out the first page of the "floating floor in basement..." thread in the REFERENCE section for more on loading/compression of neoprene - also, you do NOT want an un-damped wood floor; at the very least, you'd want R-ll fiberglass insulation between your flat-laid 2x4's so the plywood won't RING. The walls will be framed with 2 x 4's and I will be using 5/8" fire code drywall which will then have a layer of 1/2" mold resistant drywall glued over that. I am going to leave 6" of airspace around the perimeter from the existing walls. Also, the two walls are essentially very thin plywood glued (I think to the cement walls). 6" air space, provided there's insulation, should be enough; but don't GLUE the panels unless it's with Green Glue, and even then this isn't really GLUE so much as a Constrained Layer Damping material. You'll still need to use fasteners on a normal spacing. When you use REAL glue to attach two panels together, you create a new, lower, but SINGLE coincidence frequency in the wall which actually REDUCES the effectiveness. If there's thin ply glued to the concrete, this may be forming a vapor barrier. Putting anything such as kraft facing on insulation, or oil base paint, or foil, in ANOTHER part of that same wall will TRAP MOISTURE and cause mold and rot. The ceiling construction will consist of 2 x 4's again layed on their sides, attached to the wall framing with screws and a layer of either 5/8" drywall or 1/2" mold resistant drywall... 2x4's, even ON EDGE, are only rated for about a 10 foot span with one layer of drywall. Laid flat, they're not even LEGAL or SAFE. And, unless you have some sort of resilient mount, they won't reduce ANY sound from getting upstairs. I essentially am just trying to isolate the room from the rest of the house as much as possible, so I can create music in to the wee hours of night without disturbing others in the house. This is the best plan I could come up with through the research I have done. I understand, and starting with only 7 feet of headroom is a bitch; still, what you're proposing isn't going to work. Have you taken any sound level readings or done any experiments to see just how loud the sound nas to be in that room before it's objectionable to others? That would be the first place to start. Don't give up hope just yet - there's usually more than one way to "skin a cat"... Steve

If you float your floor, no matter HOW narrow the air space you will want insulation. You can compress R-11 enough in a floor so that it'll fit alongside flat-laid 2x4's, it won't short out your neoprene - with the neoprene, your air gap should be around 2" so you're compressing the fluffy insulation by about 45% or so, which isn't enough to make it firm. It will just help damp that 3/4 ply "drumhead", which should have a resonance around 90 hZ which you WILL want damped.
