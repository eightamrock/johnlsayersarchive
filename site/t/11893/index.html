Hola Javier, y ¡bienvenido! ¡Que bueno ver otro Santiaguino aquí!!! Yo estoy en Vitacura, construyendo un pequeño estudio en mi casa. ¿En qué parte de Stgo. estás tu? Pero creo que sería mejor continuar en Inglés, por respeto a los demás miembros del foro. :) A couple of comments regarding your post:
Ceilings are 2.9 meters high.
Great! More height is good, acoustically!
Flooring is wood on all the rooms.
That might not be so great... Do you mean that it is solid wood flooring glued down to concrete, like the typical "parquet flooring" that is common in many older houses in Santiago? Or do you mean that it is an American style wooden sub-floor, supported by wooden joists down below, with tongue-and-groove flooring (or something similar) on top? If you have "parquet on concrete", then that's fantastic! You can leave it like that if it is in good condition, or just take it all out and use the bare concrete for your floor, or maybe lay laminate flooring on it (called "piso flotante" here, not to be confused with with a "floating floor").
Also, all you're seeing is on the first floor, and nobody is using the second floor, and probably , nobody will.
Great! But you will still need to build an inner-leaf ceiling up there. If you don't, then sound will flank through the ceiling above, and you will not have good isolation. What is the ceiling made of? Is it concrete "losa", or is it wood laid on joists?
I don't have exact dimentions for the 3 rooms you see below and I dont think it matters for the kind of questions I have,
Actually, it does matter: It is important that your control room (CR) is built according to one of the good room ratios, which is the relationship between the height, length and width dimensions of your room. The exact dimensions aren't important for your rough plan of how to proceed in general, but they will be very important when you get to actually designing your inner leaf walls for your control room and live room.
Indeed, thats gonna save me from opening a big hole to put a window, however I'm not quite sure how to properly isolate them.
The easiest method is to fill in the space by building a "leaf" that matches the mass of the existing wall, and install one side of your window in that. Then build a simple 2x4 stud frame a few cm away from that, which will become part of your inner leaf in the control room, with a couple of layers of 15mm "volcanita" on only ONE side of those studs. Fill the cavity between these two "leaves" with rockwool (lana mineral). If you need better isolation, you should use Green Glue between the two layers of drywall, but you can't buy it here in Chile: You have to import it. I need to import some soon, so if you want maybe we could get together and import for both of us in one shipment, to share the costs a bit? It isn't cheap, though!
I'd like to keep the window on the control room, since natural light is always welcomed.
That should be possible. You will need to replace the existing glass with thick laminate glass, then put another pane of thick laminate glass in the inner-leaf wall of your control room. There is a place in Los Cobres de Vitacura that sells the right kind of glass, but it is not cheap!
1 - Whats the best way to close the opening (I want to put a double window there) btw Control Room and Live Room?., should I create a framing in both sides and leave the existing wall like a sandwich?
No. That would create a three-leaf system. Like I said above, the best thing is to build a "wall" that closes up the gap in the wall, and install one window in there. That wall should be built to match the mass of the existing wall. Brick would probably be best, or many layers of drywall on a stud frame. Then you will build the inner-leaf for your CR (control room) as an entirely separate stud frame wall, about 20cm away from all of the existing walls and a new ceiling a few cm below the existing ceiling.
2.- Lets say I don't want to create a room_within_a_room in the live room (for budget reasons). Will making a new stud-drywall wall on the left side of my control room , isolate it ?
Not really! If you only isolate one side of a room, then sound will still be able to get out through the other FIVE sides. If you need good isolation, then you need to isolate all six sides of the room. Tell us about your neighbors. How close are they to your house? What do the municipal regulations say about sound in your area? And what do you plan to record in there? If you need to record drums in your live room, then you definitely need to isolate all of the rooms, all around on all sides, or you might get a nice friendly visit from the pacos.... :)
3...in order to crank guitar amplifiers without bothering other people in the house. What could be the best aproach to do so?
Use the same basic idea: build a stud frame within the existing walls, with a couple of layers of drywall on one side. Build a new inner-leaf ceiling across the top of those walls, also with a couple of layers of drywall. Fill the gap between the walls with rockwool, and make sure everything is sealed air-tight.
4.- I also want to put a window between the Control Room and the Vocal Both, any advices?
Depends on that wall! Is it a load-bearing wall? Brick? Concrete? The basic idea is to cut a hole in that wall, and install one pane of thick glass in there. The other pane goes in the inner-leaf wall of your CR. If that is a load-bearing wall that is supporting part of the second floor, then you will need the advice of a structural engineer to tell you how to proceed. He will probably say that you need to install a concrete lintel across the top of the window frame, extending a long way into the walls on each side. If that's the case, then you probably need to hire someone to do that for you. Don't take chances!
However, I visited a friend's studio that has a similar layout than mine, and he didnt create new walls at all. just the existing concrete walls isolated the rooms pretty good, but I wanna make sure that I'm doing the right thing.
Concrete is a pretty good isolator, but you can still improve things a lot by building the MSM walls that you see all over this forum, and that I outlined above. Good luck with your build!
