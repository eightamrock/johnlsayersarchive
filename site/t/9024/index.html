Yay! We bought a house that we take possession of at the end of July. The studio will occupy part (most) of the garage, which is separate from the house. (Southern Ontario, Canada) Here are the specs: it is roughly 12.5' by 24' and is of concrete block construction with brick exterior. The roof is currently plywood and shingles, with full rafters/joists at the top. Yes, I'll need to give consideration to the ceiling, as I also want to use the area up top as an attic for storage. The garage has its own breaker, located on the 6.4' wall on the right side of the tracking room. I have attached a pic below. There is a side door, and a window on the one side. Here is a picture of a proposed layout below. The front area is going to be storage, as the property does not have a garden shed, and my wife would rather not have a shed.... you know.... with the big garage and all. :lol: I would like to have a drop-down staircase, or a ladder or something to get things into the attic part above the studio for storage. Our canoe, maybe? If someone is able to get into the big wide front garage door (or even the side door for that matter), they will have another locked door to contend with before actually entering the studio. Another interior wall between the studio and the big garage door means one more layer of wall and airspace that sound would need to travel through. With the tracking room where it is, there is then TWO walls and significant air spaces between the tracking area, and the biggest structural sonic leak - the large garage door. The rest, divided into the mix area and the tracking area has the slanted wall for two reasons. 1. Sound reflects off it unevenly, minimizing nodal build-ups, etc. and 2. The window, being an escape route for sound, is a better feature in the mix area than the tracking area. The two tables in the mix area feature two computer monitors, and two Yorkville monitors. The other thing on top of the desk is going to be my keyboard. (Korg) The thing sticking out from underneath the desk is a portable (on casters) low-rise home-made rack thingy that rolls and is stored underneath the desk. My rack gear and mixer sit not quite horizontally (slight angle upwards for ergonomics) side-by-side, so I can roll it out to put it where I want it to go (depending on cable lengths, etc.), and so I can roll it around and get around back so I can patch-repatch things as needed. The squares along the wall represent either doors or windows. It should be pretty obvious which is which. :wink: I'm thinking a pair of thermal sliding patio doors to enter the tracking area to allow for some visibility, easy access, etc., as well as sound control. My goal is to have a decent usable space that will not be sound-proof, but sound efficient, in that I will be able to run a session in it (perhaps even with a full band), without making our neighbours mental. Some noise is okay, but I need it to be pretty subdued. The dimensions are approximate, and will not yet take into account the thickness of studding/insulation/drywall, etc., so maybe subtract 6 inches all the way around for those. Am I on the right track so far? Any foreseeable problems? Any better suggestions? Thanks! (yes, surely more questions and discussion to follow ) Chris

gullfo29 June 2007#18
you could put in the double windows (replacing the single window), one on each side of the wall. the mix desk though should sit in the smaller end of the CR. the angled wall move the sound past your listening position to help create a reflection free zone. if you can use double wall construction. not staggered studs. 2 walls back to back (assuming 1 layer of 5/8" drywall on each side - not in between) would be 8 1/4" + you should put in at least 1/2" or more between the framing so call it 9". this gives you about 7 1/2" of air space (insulation counts if its normal wall insulation). if steel framing works for you(meaning cost/effort) it could improve isolation a bit more over wood, but wood works well and most people are familiar with it. another key design aspect - heating and cooling. fresh air. since the garage will have space above, i'd put in the hvac unit there and set up isolated ducts to you don't get sound conducting/traveling through the ducts between rooms... do a search on hvac and then if you need help check in the construction forum to discuss ideas.
