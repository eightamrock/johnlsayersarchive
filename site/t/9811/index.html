Skibble wrote:
I honestly believe that it would allow the material a little bit of jiggle room.
Very bad idea that is.......... first and foremost - you do not want dsrywall installed with a little bit of jiggle room......... What happens is that you develope nail pops (where the head of the nail pops throught the finish above) and broken tape seams all over the place. Drywall has to be installed properly - with the nails (or screws) firmly set - and then a small dimple in the paper - just enough to solidly lock it in - but not enough to create a tear in the paper face of the sheet. The same with subsequent sheets - Understand that all of the components that make up these assemblies have different expansive coefficiencies depending on themperatures - humidity, etc........ if a buling isn't built tighly to begin with - it can only get worse - and it will do that pretty fast. No - the other thng - the question of the value of the idea in general - I hope no hope that it would even come any where close to the value of even simple RC-1 - never mind anything more involved than that...... I would actually be suprised to see any value at all (when you compare tested true methods to these ideas) if you take into consideration the costs involved........ BTW - for what it's worth - this isn't really a new idea - been thought of - discussed - and shot in the head around here - although there are still some manufacturers putting mineral water into little bottles - selling it as snake oil and finding enough of the sheep willing to buy it to make it worth their bother to keep making it.......... Not because it works - but rather just because they can........... Sincerely Rod
