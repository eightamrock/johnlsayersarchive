Hey there, I want to build a small project studio in a bungalow. But before planning the design of the studio I've got a very basic question: Which room should I use as the controlroom? There are two rooms: 1: 3,65m x 2,95m x 2,40m (LxWxH) ( 12" x 9,7" x 7,8" feet ) 2: 5,50m x 4,50m x 2,40m (LxWxH) ( 18" x 14,8" x 7.8" feet ) In the sketch, i labeled the small one "controlroom" and the other "liveroom" but I have some doubts.. There are a few things you probably should know before answering: 1. Most neighbours are hundreds of meters away... but one: There is a house on the property which is currently rented. The couple will move out in about one year. "Their" (actually the house is my family's) house is about 6 meters away from the bungalow, so isolation would be pretty damn important. The small room, is at the far side of the house, so isolating it will be easier - plus the big room has one big and two small windows. 2. I read it is easier to optimate the acoustics in a room used for recording, rather than treating a mixingroom.. But is the small room big enough for a drummer? I want to record drums, acoustic and electric guitars, bass, vocals, etc. Will drums in a room that small sound bad? Will isolating the small room make it too small once and for all? 3. I also live in the bungalow. I planned to put my bed and a locker etc. in the recordingroom (the big one), but a bed and a locker in the controlroom is not a good idea, I suppose. If I choose to use the big room as the controlroom, would you divide the room with a drywall construction? 4. Budget is around 20.000€ maximum. Also, I could add (bigger) rooms later on (not in the near future, though). I cannot measure how loud I am right now, because we strip the house out of contents.. I will do it after it's done, though. Hope that's enough information to help me with this question - i'll include more information when it comes to the more detailled design questions. Thanks in advance! Max

jhbrandt15 August 2010#4
My vote goes to the larger room for a control room. First the blue room is too small being only 25 cubic meters & falling short of my recommended minimum of 42 cubic meters. Secondly, the control room is used these days more and more as an over-dub room and for recording in-the-box sounds from processors and keyboards. You will need room for these. Besides, the Red room has a nice modal distribution down to 30Hz and plenty of room with 59 cubic meters of volume. The blue room, IMO, would make a good booth. Treat it with maximum absorption & heavy trapping - especially on the ceiling and it will make a good vocal, amp, and acoustic guitar booth. Cheers, John
jhbrandt28 September 2010#5
Max, You can get a decent drum sound in the booth, if you can fit the drums... As long as you treat it as I described above. Note: the drummer will most likely play harder in a dead room - which might be good for Metal... I have seen some pretty small drum booths in my time, and they do work. Cheers, John
