In detail the following is my convoluted thinking which leads me to think starting OC measurements at 1’ 11 1/4” for outside corners OR 2’ 2 ¾” for inside corners (depending on if measurement is taken from inside or outside corner of frame itself) is NOT a good idea. Benefit of this would be a few less cuts of full pieces of drywall, less waste. With the standard OC measurement you would waste a few pieces to make up 5/8” or 7/8” overlaps/BUMPS. This same method of thinking has me wondering about all other wall coverings (sheathing, etc....) and 1st stud measurement of OC from 1 of the 2 walls at a corner. I realize disputing standard OC measurements method may sound absurd, but it’s my own time I waste. Also fully well realize 2nd and 3rd layers of drywall for our purposes (sound iso) do not apply hear (we’re going to skip stud bays), I’m only pointing out in drawings a variance in 1st layer. Marc, You asked a question that I answered in accordance with industry standards. No one in the world trys to layout walls (especially multiple layer walls) by trying to figure out lap conditions. If the first lapping sheet has to be trimmed by 3/4" - then trim it- you'll find out that (seeing as you are going to have to cut all subsequent layers to fit anyways) that the effort you are putting into this is much more than the work of trimming a sheet of drywall. That plus - if you trim by a bay the waste is always eaten up somewhere else. And a 1/4" trim is meaningless - it's a quick zip with a utility knife. As a matter of course - when I construct buildings - the interior studs layout in concert with the exterior walls - the exterior walls are laid out for sheathing fit - from left to right (facing the front of the building) and front to back. Thus all inside drywall has to be cut for the first sheet anyway. I follow this throughout the building with even my non-bearing wall assemblies. This way I can walk into any building I built at any point in time in my life - and tell you exactly where each and every framing member is. No - it isn't industry standard to layout walls the way you're thinking of doing it - which is why you would never see those dimensions in any of my sketches. Sincerely, Rod
