I don't know if this has been mentioned else where, I've not seen, may as well bring it.... a "simple" thing with corner detail of multi layers of drywall I discovered which could throw someone off if not expecting or planned for it: When installing 5/8" drywall in layers to inside and outside corners, as you add more layers the far edge of drywall (which will be a seam) gets pushed off center to the stud it's meant to line up to, eventually the far edge of drywall (if not cut) will get so far off center of stud, only one of the 2 peices of drywall at seam will be able to screw straight into stud. The off center seam peice of drywall not on 2x4 needs screws to go at a hard angle, not getting good ancorage in edge of drywall OR what ????? :oops: Note an outside corner would be harder to "correct" then inside corner... inside corner drywall could be cut short. Example: Inside corner, with 3 layers of 5/8" drywall: - 2x4 frame built 24" OC, - PLUS the "on center" studs are slightly pushed from true 24" OC by 1/4" on one wall leaving room for backer rod and caulk - AND other wall "on center" studs are pushed 7/8" out from OC to leave room for thickness of other walls drywall 5/8" and 1/4" of backer rod and caulk. - Install drywall (step 1 & 2) and all seams will line up center on 1 1/2" wide stud, each peice of drywall will have 3/4" over lap on to stud (1 1/2" divide by 2 = 3/4") - Remember drywall is exactly 4' x 8', 4' x 10' or 4' by 12'. - 2nd layer of drywall... let's say 1st layer was installed horizontal, 2nd layer vertically and 3rd will be horizontal for the good finish... - Step 3 your drywall seams will not line up on stud center... it'll be 7/8" from the center of stud, got 5/8" drywall plus 1/4" for backer rod and caulk... stud is only 1 1/2" wide, 3/4" would be center but drywall seam is landing at 7/8" past this...... the next peice of drywall's edge will not meet the stud at all unless the peice which starts in corner is cut 7/8" short. is this what we are suppose to do? - Step 4,5 & 6 to 3 layers of drywall share problem - For inside corners drywall can be cut short to allow seams to land on center of studs however.... OUTSIDE corners I can't figure out for a wall longer than 12' -With outside corners, drywall would have to be lengthened to allow seams to meet at center of stud. With each layer of drywall we go 7/8" from O.C. in the direction of the corner... can't make 4' peice of drywall wider? - IF wall is less then 12 foot long... no problem 12x4 could be used. The last layer of 3 horizontally installed will meet all studs and could hold 2nd layer of drywall in place - but past that length 12' how do we get the seams to line up? that is without installing more 2x4's in wall frame to make the fasteing area of stud wider (this would be similiar for techniques in repairing drywall holes which I've learned all about :roll: ) Maybe this would not be a common problem as you could start measure of OC from an existing wall BUT, what if there is no existing wall working towards an outside corner? rod gervais12 October 2006#3
Marc, seeing as your best installation is where joints never fall on top of one another - this shouldn't ever be an issue. In the best installation - your first sheets run vertically - so with a full sheet layout you would have joints at 4' - 8' - 12' etc. Then install the 2nd layer horizontally - using a full sheet - and starting that sheet on the center of the 2nd stud from the wall - cut to fit into the corner - this breaks your joints at the ends of the drywall sheets by one full stud bay. For the 3rd layer - step in another stud bay ( to 4' if framing 2' oc - 32" if framing 16" oc) and (again) cut back to fit into the corner. That's the way it's done. Sincerely, Rod

(now if I could only perfect cutting a nice straight line in a piece of drywall :oops: ) The only thought/concern I have left (and will address even if it's only for my self) is where exactly to start measuring "On Center" from. Working with an inside corner we should start O.C. measurement from the inside corner of the two frames, not the end of sole plate of each frame. One sole plate will give the correct measurement, the other will be 3 1/2"s off because it extends past the other sole plate to make the outside corner. I remember reading code somewhere that max distance of studs is 28" and this must be the reason. Maybe not necessary but if we want to be PERFECT, we would bump the OC measurement of studs on frame that gets drywall 1st by 1/4" allowing room for backer rod and caulk at perimeter, then seams should line up exactly middle of studs. On the other frame (to corner) we would bump the OC measurement by 7/8" for 5/8" drywall already on other wall and 1/4" for backer rod and caulk. Bottom line would be to carefully plan out exactly where each drywall piece is going and plan stud accordingly so that we can follow drywall/Sheetrock/gypsum board installation instructions and drive screws no closer than 3/8" from edge into a stud . That is without driving in at an angle to hit stud or installing more studs to give a wider stud area to hit... and not miss. If studs are not planned out correctly and drywall is already up on other side frame(s) then it would sure be destructive to install an additional 2x4 next to a stud in frame for 2nd and 3rd layers.
