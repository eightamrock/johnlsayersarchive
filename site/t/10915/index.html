clunk14 July 2008#4
Hi Arjan the 2 x 4 is a nominal lumber size and the actual size can be as low as 1 1/2 x 3 1/2, see this link from the reference section for further info on sizes http://www.arnoldlumber.com/size.html I was looking at your floor plan and it raises a number of questions, the first is in relation to what I assume are the heights of the rooms, in the control room you show 3 different heights which seem just wrong to me ( I am no expert but have you tried drawing your plan in 3D?). The second is in relation to the ceilings and the spans in your rooms, in particular the live room which has one span of more than 5 metres at one point, I am pretty sure that 2 x 4 timbers would be pretty useless at such spans, in my own build we are using 2 x 6 timbers for spans greater than 3.2 m and 2 x 7's for spans greater than 3.6m, these are based on acceptable loads for these spans according to tables in the local building code here in N Ireland. Also the longest lengths of timbers available here are 4.8m in length which would not be sufficent to span the widest parts of your live room. Have you thought how you will deal with these spans? As I said earlier I am no expert and I really hope someone with greater expertise can help you find a solution to these potential difficulties. All the best with your build, Clunk

rod gervais15 July 2008#6
you should also talk about wall framing while you are at it. Here in the States, although 2' oc using 2x6 is your best LF wall system - and although you can use 2x4 2' oc with non-bearing walls - you cannot use 2x4 at 2' oc for bearing walls. You also have the option of using isolating hangers to carry some of the load from the structure above if you want to use 2x4's for ceiling joist. And - if designed correctly - they could then be 2' oc. Just be very careful if you take that route - if the hangers are not done right they become transmitters instead of isolators. Rod
