Soundman20204 October 2009#10
have you followed the thread by Soundman2020? He was having to really compromise on room ratios but [if I remember correctly] he just ended up getting as much space as possible out of a tight area.
Exactly. It got to the point where trying to squeeze out a few more millimeters of room dimensions to get even closer to a good ratio became a "battle of diminishing returns". In other words, the changes I could get by modifying a dimension here and there really didn't make much difference acoustically, and made the room itself strange in one sense or another, such as ceiling too low, or room too narrow. I checked many, many possible alternative dimensions, and found that the numbers really didn't change that much, or certainly not enough to make it worthwhile, so I went with a ratio that is a compromise: it gets fairly close to Sepmeyer #2, and still gives me reasonable dimensions. It's not optimal, but it will work, and there are no huge red flags with the ratio I came up with (just minor bright orange ones! :) ). I'm also splaying my walls and ceiling, which helps for several reasons. The only way I'd be able to get even closer to a good ratio would be to drop the ceiling way down low, or widen the room so much that I'd have no space left for a usable LR (Or knock a hole into my neighbor's garage!!) . The advice from the experts was to not get all tied up in ratios anyway, since they are just approximations to get you away from really bad dimensions, and close to good ones. Even the best mathematical models and predictions are only rough estimates at best, and reality could be entirely different from what the numbers say anyway. You'll have to measure the acoustics od the room once it is finished to find out how it REALLY works, then treat accordingly. So, as long as you are not building a cube or square profile room, have a reasonable height ceiling, and are fairly close to a known good ratio, you should be fine. Height is your best friend, acoustically: Low ceilings are not a good idea. Especially with drums, where you will want to put mics overhead: those mics will pick up reflections (and therefore comb filtering) from the ceiling if it is close. Make the ceiling as high as you can, if you possibly can. If not, then just live with what you have, and be aware of the issues that will arise as a result. - Stuart -

Soundman20202 October 2010#13
Great to see that you are finally moving forward! :) And congratulations on a well thought-out plan!
1. How advantageous would it be to put pink fluffy insulation between the 2 leaves? Considering I will have rockwool/rigid fiberglass lining the inside leaf.
Very advantageous. It damps resonance in the cavity, improves isolation, reduces the MSM resonant frequency, etc. Good idea to put it in!.
3. Do you think my proposed construction for the ceiling is a good idea for decoupling? See attached sketchup file.
Have you considered using Rod's technique of interleaving your new joists in between the existing joists? That way you could gain a few inches of room height. Your final inner-leaf surface would end up just an inch or two below the existing joists. You would probably still loose a bit below that for treatment, at least in some parts of the room, but it might be worth looking at. - Stuart -
