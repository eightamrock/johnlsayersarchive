Hi Omen, and welcome! A few random comments:
My goal is to build a control room and a ‘live’ room big enough to track a drum kit. ... As the room is now the internal dimensions are 5865mm (L) x 4150mm (W) x 2730mm (H).
That's gonna be a pretty tight fit! It should be do-able, but realize that both rooms will be small, and good acoustics likes "large" not "small". You will also be loud. A full drum kit can easily produce 115 dB SPL, and a lot of that energy is in the low end of the spectrum, which is the hardest part to deal with. How quiet do you have to be? You might have a great relationship with the rest of the folks in the house right now, but that could change really fast if you don't control the levels. Try to put a real number to how quiet you need to be in order to maintain good relationships with everyone. That number will dictate the kind of construction you need. If everyone around you is just ecstatically in love with drums and says that 95 dB in their bedroom at 3AM is fine, then you are done already! But if they say that 45 dB is still too loud, then you have some serious work ahead of you. Beg, borrow or rent a sound level meter, and get some real numbers as your starting point.
Because the garage is attached to the house I’m thinking that I’m going to have to build a “room within a room”.
If you want to isolate a drum kit, then yup! You don't have too many other options.
The garage when we bought the house had no ceiling. Just the floor boards above. This was my first problem. No carpet above the garage just polished floor boards. And with kids playing up stairs on the raw floor!! So my first job was to fill the 240mm floor joist with Sound Screen insulation and put up some 16mm Fire Check Gyprock
Did you decouple that drywall from the joists? If not, then it won't achieve much isolation. Plus, you now have a two-leaf system up there already, so what you built there will have to come down if you plan to build a room-in-a-room. You cannot have a multi-leaf system if you hop to isolate drums (unless you plan to put seriously huge amounts of mas on those leaves).
I have sheeted the wall in front of the garage door with 16mm Fire Check, filled with Sound Screen insulation and then sheeted the internal side again with 16mm Fire Check.
That sounds like a three-leaf wall already, and you haven't even built your inner leaf yet! :shock: Just to confirm: What you have from outside to inside is the original garage door, a small air gap, then a single sheet of drywall, another small air gap (the space between the studs), and yet another sheet of drywall. Is that correct? If so, that's a three-leaf wall, which is bad enough for low frequencies already, but it will turn into a four leaf wall (which is even worse!) once you build your inner room. It looks like you'll need to re-think that if you hope to isolate drums from the outside world. The drywall will have to come off that frame.
The opposite wall to the garage has the door to get into the garage. This wall was also insulated and sheeted with the Fire Check. I need to do some more framing and sheet that as well,
Once again, did you decouple the two sides of that wall? If not, then it will never isolate drums. Not even close. If you have not yet put drywall on the garage side of that frame, then DON'T! Leave it like it is, with just the stud framing for now. You'll see why later....
The floor is concrete.
Excellent! Leave it like that. Nothing better. Or maybe lay laminate flooring directly on top of it, if you don't like the look of bare concrete.
And carpet everywhere else. I don’t know yet. I might just go carpet everywhere.
Bad idea. Chances are carpeting your room will leave it sounding dull, cavey, honky, lifeless, etc. Unless you use carpet that has published acoustical properties, and those properties happen to match exactly what your room needs, then it will most likely make matters worse, not better. The general recommendation is: hard floor, soft ceiling. Leave your floor as concrete, and either do an inside-out ceiling or use clouds.
I’m still undecided if I’m really going to need a “room within a room”.
To isolate drums, you really don't have much choice! There are only two basic ways to stop sound from getting from "A" to "B": 1) put a total vacuum between "A" and "B", or 2) put an really huge amount of mass between "A" and "B". Then there is a third method, which is sort of related to the second one, and is called a fully-decoupled two.leaf MSM sturcture. The problem is you already seem to have too many leaves in some parts of the construction, without even doing the inner leaf yet! You already have more than two leaves in some places: adding the inner-leaf will make that three or even (gasp!) four leaves. Unfortunately, you have already built stuff that will have to come down again if you want to actually isolate things. For example: the wall over the garage door, and the ceiling, will both have to come down and be re-built properly if you want to achieve isolation. Drums are the hardest of all instruments to isolate, and the way you are going so far is not going to do the trick. As you have already discovered! As you say: "It’s still very loud up stairs above the garage." Yes it is, and it will get even louder in the low end if you add another leaf. Simple fact of physics.
I’m going to definitely need another ceiling.
Nope! Bad idea! Adding another ceiling will REDUCE your isolation for low frequencies, NOT increase it! You need to re-build the ceiling you already did, but you need to do it properly this time. If you try to add yet another ceiling, that will make three leaves, which will actually decrease your isolation to upstairs: Yes, you read that correctly: Adding another ceiling to what you already have will make matters WORSE, not better. Not intuitive, but true. You would be building a three-leaf ceiling, which will drive up the resonant frequency of the entire MSMSM system, putting the resonance dip squarely in the lower part of the spectrum, which is exactly where drums put out a large amount of energy.
If i did make a “room within a room” would i have to frame the perimeter of the whole room to hang the ceiling like I have in my Sketchup plan?
Well, sort of! But not like that. Your rooms are coupled, you have several three-leaf and four-leaf walls on that plan, the air gaps are too small, drywall seems to be in the wrong place at some points, and your plan is only 2D with no indication of what is what, so it's hard to figure out what is going on in there.
Or could I hang the ceiling off the framing of the angled sound baffles and speaker mounts?
I'm not sure what you mean by "angled sound baffles" and "speaker mounts". Are you talking about the speaker soffits? If so, then the answer is no: your inner leaf is the entire structure surrounding each room. After you decouple the two rooms, you will actually have two "inner rooms". They must be independent, decoupled, no mechanical connections at all between them, fully sealed airtight, and with only two-leaves, never three, and certainly not ever four! Ans speaker soffits are not part of the leaf structure: they are inside the room, and they don't form part of the inner leaf. So, what you'd need to do is to take off the drywall from the ceiling, take out the insulation, use the drywall to beef up the floor above you (between the joists), taking care to seal all of that hermetically, then put the insulation back. Then take off the drywall from the garage side of that wall yo built around the roller-door, take out the insulation, use the drywall to beef up the other side of that wall (between the studs), seal airtight, then put the insulation back in again. Do the same on the left hand wall (marked "front door"). Seal the brick walls. And ditto for the wall at the bottom, marked "house bedroom"). At this point you will have one sealed brick wall, three stud frames filled with insulation, and a ceiling filled with insulation. And each of those will be just one leaf, which is what you want. Now you can build your two inner rooms. Each will be a stud frame with a couple of layers of drywall on only ONE side of the studs. Each room will have its own independent ceiling, which rests only on the walls for that room, and will also be a set of joists with one or more layers of drywall on just ONE side. The inner leaf must also be sealed airtight. That will do the trick! However, I didn't see any HVAC in your plan. How are you going to deal with that? - Stuart -

Acoustic isolation in a room is sort of like an aquarium: It has to be the same all around in order to work. You can't make an aquarium with glass on the front, wood on the back, cardboard on the bottom, sponge on one side, and paper on the other. It will only be as good as the weakest, leakiest side. That's where all the water is going to leak out. So too with your room: whichever wall is weakest (has the least mass / sealing / ar gap / insulation) is going to define the isolation for the entire room. It is not really useful to double up on just one wall of the room, since the sound will simply leak around that, through the other walls. In other words, you need to do all four walls and the ceiling to the exact same level, and the then windows and doors also need to be done to the same level.
Yeah this is correct. Should i have made this wall part of my inner room wall? Because it probably not too late to do that. I need to pull the gyprock off anyway.
Tough call! It all depends on how much of a three-leaf the original garage door is creating. What is it made of, how heavy is it, and how well sealed is it? If it is just light weight slats that roll up, then it probably isn't much of a problem. Opinions differ on this kind of garage door issue, but I think I'd pretend that the 3-leaf effect of the original door is not going to be too bad, and pretend that what you already did is going to be your outer leaf. So once again I'd take off the drywall on the garage-side of that frame and use it to beef up the other side, sealing carefully, and maybe even add a third layer of drywall on that one, to get the mass up a bit more. then build your inner leaf separately, and leave as large a gap as you can afford between those two leaves. However, I'm not 100% certain that this is the best plan for that wall. Maybe one of the experts here can give yo a better idea of how to do that one. - Stuart -

Reflections aren't what you are going to hear, but rather the flanking that will keep that thing ringing.
