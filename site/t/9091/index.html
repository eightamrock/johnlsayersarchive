rod gervais10 July 2007#2
Johan, we live with the cards we're dealt...... the answer is "yes" this is a 3 leaf system.......... but also "so what" = because you can't do anything to change it. Adding all the weeps in the world won't change that (and by the way - it would bbe a bad idea to have them other than low on the wall where they are now) So beef up the existing wall with a much mass as you can - and then add your inside wall to complete everything. Rod

sharward15 July 2007#8
That's similar to what I did: 1 layer of 15/32" (call it ½") OSB glued and cleated with 1½" deep wood pieces, 3 layers of ½" drywall screwed into the OSB, then one layer of 5/8" drywall with Green Glue on its back over the top of everything, with the screws of this final layer driven diagonally into the cleats and studs. I used many cases of acoustic sealant in this process, but I did not use backing rod :oops: because I wasn't aware of its benefits at the time... :roll: ...I'm not too worried about three-point caulk failures because there's so much "overkill" going on... --Keith :mrgreen:

You've got these massive studs going right through whereas with inner leaf, you've got a total barrier of dry wall where the green glue (supposedly) has a lot more area at it's disposal and can damp the entire leaf. :?: :?: :?: I've almost decided to leave the green glue out of the outer leaf and put my money on the inner leaf. It's the load I'm worried about... STC-wise I feel I'd be ok with an RC-2 decoupled construction. Guess I'll have to consult a building official on this. That's it for now!
