John Sayers13 June 2007#4
To change scale - select the component you want to scale and you'll find a blue or yellow outline frame. Now select the scale tool. (Tools/scale) A yellow/blue frame will now show the scale options around the component as green squares. Select the one in the corner but in the middle, it will highlight as being connected to the opposite middle corner. As you move the mouse you will see in the lower right data input box the scale percentage readout like .87,.98 - meaning a change of .87% on the red axis v a change of .98% on the green axis. Make the component fit into your space and type in the rescale as equal in both directions - i.e. .87,.87 Then hit enter. The component will then rescale to 87% uniformly and all the angles and proportions will remain equal. In that .skp model you can select all the front components, i.e the speaker units, the side slots and the units between the speakers and group them together. Then you can rescale the complete unit to fit into your space. All the vertical dimensions will retain the same proportions but the lateral dimensions will rescale proportionately to your room. Don't alter the vertical dimensions. If they don't fit extend each part but don't alter the speaker height etc. Cheers john

Aloha, A few areas (among the many) you may want to consider carefully - Before you begin framing, confirm and reconfirm with a structural engineer that the floors can and will support the studio build, and then how is constructed (so you'll know what you need to do in order to improve it's performance) - Measure and determine how much does the floor transmit sound and how will you address that issue - There have been a several posts regarding the difference between house frame construction and studio construction (and they are very different.) Do a search of the construction "stickies" and threads to find out as much as you can before they begin framing. Here's a good place to start - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4442 http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2125
Is soffit mounting going to be worth it? I think it would look impressive, but that's another detail in the mix to get figured out.
I think that more importantly than the "WOW factor" is the improvement in your soundstage and imaging. Some accurate measurements of your room - length, width, height, doors and windows with their placement and measurements would help immensely in fleshing out a Sketchup file for your architect. For a complete working set of plans, may I suggest: http://www.johnlsayers.com/Pages/Promo.htm Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros (Paperback) by Rod Gervais http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5670 Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

with HVAC you need a supply and a return. since each room is (in theory) sealed, you need a (at least) supply and return in each room to move the air and ideally keep the noise down while isolating each room. if you're building a room within a room, then you could run a variety of ducts over the ceiling to accomplish this task. you might want to post the question in the construction forum where Rod and Knightfly are likely to spot it and weigh in on the best options.
