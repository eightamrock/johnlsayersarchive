Purusha9 June 2010#2
I have a question regarding the superchunks and broadband absorbers. Can I cover them with very thin layer of PVC folio before putting the cloth over? I am concerned to have the rock-wool micro fibres airborne in the studio if I cover them with cloth only. I believe also they use some chemicals in the process of making the rock-wool which could vaporize in time.
Purusha13 June 2010#3
BUMP on my question....
xSpace13 June 2010#4
If PVC folio is the same thing or equal to .35 mil plastic, yes you can cover them up. It has been suggested and even mentioned that you can gain back some of the audible highs in a room just by doing this very thing, which makes sense.
Purusha14 June 2010#5
It's a very very thin and light plastic folio, for covering the furniture while painting. I attached a photo if it helps.
xSpace15 June 2010#6
Thing is, the fabric will stop any air-borne particles. But you can install the plastic/folio if it makes you feel better/safer. Good luck.
Purusha15 June 2010#7
OK, Thanks. I will use it. I prefer not to breath the formaldehyde from the rock-wool ...

Great work purusha, lotsa screws there in those ceiling panels! wow.. One question: I noticed you use wood for frames and have put wool in there. how did you do that? Just making the panels a bit bigger so they get "stucked" in the frame? Of have any construction behind the wool? I'm asking because I'm in the process of filling my woodframe with wool and am not sure if just cramming them in will do... love to hear from your experience.
Purusha13 October 2010#13
The best way is to make the space -0,5cm less high and wide to fit in the panels tight. Check my photos, I used also a wire to make sure they can't fall out behind the panels.
