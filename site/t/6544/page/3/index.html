SonicClang27 October 2006#57
Yeah the ceiling took FOREVER! We started it in late 2001 and we didn't finish it until early 2006. So it took well over 5 years just for that portion of the build. But, as you'll see in upcoming episodes, work didn't continue on the same pace it has been. We went from working almost every night, to working one day a week for the last three years. But yeah, the ceiling tiles are constructed of two layers of 1/2" OSB and one layer of 1/2" drywall, and it's all bolted up to those 2X4's. And as you saw in part 7, we used weather stripping to seal each piece. Being able to remove parts of the ceiling was a major thing for me. When I was still living with my parents, we built a few bedrooms in their basement and we drywalled right over a bunch of utilities. The next spring we went to turn on the outside faucet and it burst INSIDE the basement ceiling. Water was absolutely everywhere. The next room they built they used drop ceiling... but that wouldn't work for me if I wanted my basement somewhat soundproof, so I designed this system. It's insane, but it fricken works. :) Eh... I'm not too extremely worried these days about including a bunch of disclaimers. I don't think anyone would find my videos on youtube randomly and think "Oh cool, I'm going to build a studio like this guy and follow what he's doing exactly". Most people are finding the videos off this site, and the HR site, so they're already doing research on the subject.

SonicClang6 November 2006#62
Very good topic of discussion! How to stay motivated... The overall thing that has kept me moving is the fact that I want a recording studio in my house :) but of course when I come home from a hard day at work and I'm tired and I'm years away from finishing, that's not going to get me downstairs to work. So the big question is, what keeps you motivated on a day to day basis? In my case I think the fact that my friend Pete has been so closely involved has definitely helped. And once our band broke up, we went from practicing on Tuesday nights, to working in the studio Tuesday night. We enjoy hanging out together and we usually have a good time building and talking, it hasn't necessarily always felt like WORK. With the two of us working on this thing, we weren't going to let ourselves cut corners and build something that wouldn't be of the highest standards. If we did do that, we'd know it and we'd have a hard time looking at each other. Plus, when you've spent years working on a project, the last thing you want to do is get lazy near the end just to finish. "It's taken us this long to get this far, what's another year going to hurt?" In 2006 (as you'll see in the next episode) I got REAL sick of working on a studio. I just wanted to HAVE a studio. So this year there was a big push to finish and the most amount of work got done in 2006 than in any year since we started. Then in about July a local newspaper reporter saw a couple of my videos of the construction process on youtube and e-mailed me asking if he could come over and do a story on me. At that point I was still pretty far away from being able to say it was done. The walls hadn't been painted yet and all the ceiling wasn't even done. So I had a couple big "work all weekend until we're so tired we can't work anymore" sessions with my brother in law John. And then the weekend before the reporter came my wife helped me get everything painted and finished up. We ended up having to completely repaint the entire live room because we hated the initial color. And I was still hanging a door for the control room literally 20 minutes before the guy showed up. At the time, it was an extremely stressful couple of weeks, and I hated the fact that I had to work SO hard! But now the studio is about 99% done because of that big push. So I guess the best thing anyone can do to get motivated is to get interviewed by a newspaper. :) I promise you'll work your ass off to get it done.
