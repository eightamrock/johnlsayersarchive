len-morgan1 July 2006#7
What you might consider (since recording is secondary to rehersal), is making your "mixing station" on wheels. If you think about it, when you are recording, you don't have to listen to the sound you are recording (except maybe on headphones). Once you've recorded the song, roll the desk into a good position for listening/mixing and mix there. Besides, if you've got the drum kit playing live, you're not going to hear anything from the speakers while you're recording anyway! If you can hear the recorded drums, you're probably also going to be feeding the speaker output back into the drum mics. Playing/recording and mixing are two different activities that should take place at different times and under different conditions (unless you are mixing a live concert for an audience of course). If your space (and budget) is limited, spend a little for some wheels (and bungee cords to tie everything down with!) and then do one thing at a time. I don't think you're going to be able to go from mic to CD in one shot so why try? len
