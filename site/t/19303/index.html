What type of wood floor? Is it laminate or some such that is laid directly on the original concrete slab? Or is it raised above that, leaving a cavity between the floor and the slab?
I'd also love to have enough isolation that any noise wouldn't be an irritation throughout the rest of the house.
Decibels. You need to measure them. So you can come up with a number that answers the question "How much isolation do you need?". In other words, you need to take two basic measurements: #1: How loud your band is in a typical session, and #2: how quiet you need to be to "not be an irritation throughout the rest of the house", and also to meet your local noise regulations. You then subtract the "quiet" number from the "loud" number, and the answer is how much isolation you need, in decibels. Based on that, you can then select the type of construction and materials that will get you that amount of isolation.

Soundman202028 February 2014#4
I currently have a laminate wood floor laid directly onto the concrete slab. I'm perfectly happy to tear this out and replace it with a better material if necessary.
Don't do that! Laminate on concrete is perfect! The problematic floor is the other type: with an air cavity underneath, since that can potentially by a resonant system that messes with your room acoustics. But what you have is excellent. No need to change that.
I don't expect the band to be playing beyond 100dB
What type of band? Most rock bands get up to 115 or more. The drums alone can hit 115, played reasonably hard.
a reduction of 50 dB seems reasonable. Even so, 60 dB would be ideal if it was doable without over-complicating the design necessities.
50 to 60 is a good goal for a home studio. 50 is doable with reasonable care and a good budget. 60 is quite a bit harder, but still attainable if your budget is high.
There is currently no central HVAC supplied to the room-just a ductless mini split AC unit that we plan to replace. (This could be replaced with a similar unit and moved to a better location, or we could rethink the AC entirely.)
Mini-splits are fine, and quite common in home studios. They do a good job of cooling and controlling humidity, if they are sized right for the room, occupancy and general heat and humidity load. However, they don't do ventilation, which is the other part of HVAC, so you still need ducts, fans and silencer boxes for that, in addition to your mini-split.
The studio will primarily be used to individually record instruments from a typical band arrangement (elec/aco guitars, drum sets, vox, etc.), but I'd still prefer to have the tracking room large enough to accommodate a small band rehearsal (perhaps 4-5 musicians). If it's possible, I'd really love a dedicated control room as well. (Perhaps a modest control room and one live room?)
You can probably do that: you seem to have enough space, if you design carefully.
As for the budget: The person funding the project has just told me "We'll do whatever needs to be done." Still, I think he'd have some sticker shock if we're moving much beyond $10,000.
The best way to estimate the true cost of building a home studio is to call around some local contractors in your area, and ask for their flat rate per square foot for finishing an unfinished basement as a TV room or home theater. They will give you a rate that includes materials and labor for doing that at a basic level, so add 20% or so to their number, multiply by the number of square feet you have for your studio, and that will give a good idea of the total cost, realistically.
1. Is it feasible to have a control room and live room in this space, or would it be more beneficial to use one big room?
It is feasible, yes, with a space that size. But your budget probably doesn't allow that. You might find you are limited to doing just one room for now, and doing the other one later as more funds become available. that leaves you with the dilemma: which one to do now, and which one to leave for later?
2. Would anyone be willing to offer suggestions on a possible layout within the space available?
For a space with that shape and size, I think I'd probably first take a look at doing a "corner control room" in one of the corners on the left wall (19'4" wall, marked as brick), and making all the rest into a large live room. If a corner control room won't fit well, then I'd go with a rectangular control room up that same wall. There are many examples of both types of studio here on the forum, so take a look around and find something similar to your situation, then see if you can adapt it. - Stuart -
