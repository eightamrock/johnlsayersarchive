Soundman202017 June 2009#8
In the picture it has brick as but only my outer wall is brick. Assuming in the outer wall in the design was a stud with 2 layers of 16mm plasterboard, how would this wall construction compare to a standard double stud wall? A standard stud wall would have a larger cavity (inner wall has 2 x 16mm on the inner side as opposed to the outer in the design above), so can I assume it will provide better isotion?
Well, yeah, a larger air gap will increase isolation, but there is an awful lot more mass in a brick wall than there is in 2 layers of drywall, so that makes a major difference. Brick is nearly three times the density of drywall (depending on the type), so a 10cm thick brick wall has roughly the same mass as about 18 layers of drywall! Like Lilith said: a good brick wall is preferable to a good drywall wall any day, provided that it is well sealed. So if you can choose whether to make your outer leaf from bricks or from a stud wall, then most definitely go with the brick. Your inner leaf can be drywall on studs if you want. And with a brick outer leaf, that will be a really good wall. Lots of STC there!
Does the thread get into the acoustics of the room at some point? This is still an area that confuses me, specifically as to how many absorbers I need, angles of the wall etc.
I think he mentions acoustics in there somewhere, but I don't recall the details. Probably your best bet is to look through some of the rooms John has designed and built, and the rooms that others have built here, then choose one that takes your fancy and adapt it to fit your space. The general rules of thumb are to keep your floor hard (reflective) and ceiling soft (absorbent), especially if you ever plan to record your drums, since you don't want the overheads to be close to a reflective surface, such as a hard ceiling a few inches away. Make sure that your walls are not parallel, to help reduce flutter echo. You'll need some absorption in the room to deal with resonance and reverb times, but don't overdo it. A totally dead room is not pleasant. I'm sure other folks will have additional ideas to offer on that front, but a really good place to learn about acoustic treatment is Ethan's web site, at www.realtraps.com . And right here on the forum, of course! - Stuart -
