Soundman202013 April 2009#4
Id obviously be using the usual method of two panes with variable thicknesses at a slight angle
You don't need the "slight angle" for acoustic purposes. Acoustic myth. It wont help. Angling might help for visual purposes (reducing reflection of room lights, equipment and people in the glass), but it does nothing for isolation of acoustics, and in fact will REDUCE your isolation. Not sure about thickness, but 3" sure does sound like a lot! What are your overall target numbers for isolation? That will help dictate the thickness that you actually need. It makes no sense to have a fantastic STC-60 window if you have an STC-30 door, wall or ceiling somewhere as your weak link. That would just be a waste of money. Most people here go for thinner glass. Search the forum for keywords like "window" and "laminate glass". You should find many threads showing what others have done, and how successful they were. Do you have Rod's book? Check the chapter on windows. 3/8 and 1/2 are two numbers that come to mind. - Stuart -

mika13 April 2009#8
After we know your wall design, i have to agree with Ro. You need one piece of glass per leaf which matches the same mass like the leaf itself, to get the same amount of isolation. After we know that your brick has a mass of 2000kg/m³ and floatglas has 2500kg/m³ you will end up with a glassthickes about 80% of the brick thickness. You have 2 leafs, so you need 2 pieces of this thick glass :wink: Do you realy need this amount of isolation? cheers Mika
