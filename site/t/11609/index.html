rod gervais21 January 2009#3
OK - first the isolation qustion - no the slabs cannot pin together as this will still couple them. Beyond that point - you really need to get together with a local engineer who understands the conditions with your soils. You're correct that as lay dries out it will setle - and quite considerably - BUT - you also can have the possibility of the reverse - it's called expansive soils - and in seasons with heavy rains you could have the entire slab lift. I do not know anything about the soils in your area - and so I am not qualified to give you advice relating to it - and seriously recommend a local engineer. I did run across a situation in a studio I designed in Canada where we ended up having to build the exterior foundation on piers - and then installed piers - with interior grade beams to create a structural slab for the inner structural frame. There was no choice in this as the soils were not capable of handling the load without severe settlement problems - not unlike your situation. The idea you have for multiple layers of slab still will not address the issue of settlement from what I can gather about your ground conditions from your post. And it sounds like much more money than needs to be spent to acheive a sound structual condition. I wish you luck...... Rod
