Soundman202016 February 2009#2
It's the distance between the inside surface of the outer leaf, and the outside surface of the inner leaf that counts, not so much the distance between frames (although obviously, the two are related!) With air gaps, bigger is better. Less than 4" between the leaves is not really recommended, 8" is great, even more if you can spare the space. Somewhere I have a document with graphs of isolation vs. air gap, but I can't seem to find it right now! - Stuart -

Soundman202016 February 2009#4
Probably Ro or Andre or another real expert can comment on that with authority, but to my way of thinking, it isn't an issue. It just means that some walls will have better isolation than others. And of course, you can splay your walls, too (angle them) so that the gap varies: That is actually a good thing, I think, since it would reduce flutter inside the wall itself. If you do splay them, then the smallest gap is the weakest point, and it only gets better from there. So keep your smallest gap to at least 4", and it should be fine.

And why does your partner think that "sound board" would make any difference in any case? On what principle of physics does he base his hunch that sound board works better over a larger air gap than drywall? Besides, your partner seems to have it backwards: an MSM wall doesn't work well when the gap is too SMALL, and works BETTER when the gap is large. Your 1 inch minimum gap is going to be your week point, not your 12 inch gap! If you can, you should increase that 1 inch gap to at lest 3 or 4 inches. the improvement you will get is well worth it. You might want to get him to look at the graph below, which comes from a detailed paper published by the BBC acoustic research department, several years ago. Basically, if you want more isolation, then use a larger air gap. More is better, always. And that statement is based on real world physics and laboratory tests, not on "paranoid hunches"! :)
... and I keep coming up with the advice that "the more air space, the better".
Yup! That's because you keep coming up with advice based on science, not "feelings". :)
I do plan on putting some insulation inside the walls for dampening.
Once again, "YUP"! You certainly do want to do that! It should gain you around 10 dB of isolation. I'm attaching a second graph from a real paper about real acoustics, that shows what a difference insulation makes on total isolation. Quote from yet another research paper: "Adding a sound absorptive material inside the cavity of a single stud partition (wood with resilient furrings or steel) or a staggered wood stud partition increases the STC rating by 5 to 9 points depending on the type of sound absorptive material used. For double stud partitions, an increase of 10 to 13 points was obtained depending on the amount of glass fiber insulation added to the cavity." - Stuart -
