Mark Blasco20 July 2006#8
I have been working in a similar sized space for the last 4 years, and while I understand your desire to have 2 rooms, you will be much better off (in my opinion) with just leaving it as one room. You will be much more comfortable being in a larger space. While it certainly is possible to fit a drum kit in such a small space (if you divide the room), it is not comfortable at all, and that is before you put in the acoustic treatment. With the space you will lose because of the treatment, I don't think you are going to have enough space for a drum kit to fit. If you design your room as one control room as big as will fit, you will still have room to fit a drum kit in the back half of the room. This will sound much better, as you will have more than twice the space, which is a good thing for acoustics. Just get yourself a good set of isolation headphones and you should be good to go. I know that isn't what you want to hear, and you may go with the 2 room idea anyway, but please try to imagine working in such a cramped space for hours at a time. Also keep in mind the acoustic problems this is going to cause for you, and how much space the acoustic treatment is going to take up.

Mark Blasco23 July 2006#10
Recording a drumset in a one room studio takes a little practice, but if it is the same set every time, you just have to get all of your settings once, write them down, and then you are good to go every time. It does take a little more time with a new kit, because you have to record a sample and play it back to check your sounds, but the fact of the matter is that you are going to get a much better sound with a bigger room, so the tradeoff is worth it. Here is something to help you get a feel for the room size. Measure out how big you would want to divide it up if you were doing 2 rooms. Hang a sheet or blanket from the ceiling where the wall divide would be. Now, try to setup your kit, setup your mics, and get from one space to the other without touching the blanket (that is where your wall would be). Working in a 1 room studio is not as hard as you may believe. I have been doing it for years at my house, and have gotten some pretty good results. I highly suggest getting a pair of isolation headphones so you can hear what the mic is picking up. If you have the money, splurge and get a pair of in ear monitors - they work great for this (and are great for listening to your ipod as well) :D

Mark Blasco23 July 2006#12
For isolation headphones, I would NOT reccomend the drummer ones with the built in metronome - I have a set, and while the isolation is good, the sound quality is not. They are good for drummers to hear what they are playing to without their headphones cranked up, but the sound quality is not great. If you can expand your budget, one solution is the westone um2. They are musicians monitors, but universal fit. They will give you the best isolation you can get because they seal off your ear canal just like earplugs. They are more than you said you wanted to spend, but you can also use them with in-ear monitors, or you can use them with an MP3 player as regular headphones. I am sure someone else will have a suggestion for isolation headphones. If no one here has any opinions you may try the forums at pro sound web or harmony central.
