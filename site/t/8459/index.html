I thought that the room was fairly symetrical as the monitors are almost centered (i can move one of the lcds to put the speakers dead centre) and the side absorbers and celing absorbers are symetrical and identical.
For the most part, the side & ceiling absorbers are fine as far as placement and symmetry goes. You may find that multiple flat ceiling absorbers will work fine and be a lot easier to build & install. Am I correct in my understanding that the layout prescribed by Glenn (bed centered on rear wall/wrap around work station) in your other post will not be used? It was a pretty good suggestion. So, the areas in question are: - what appears to be a desk of some kind on your left, and the bed on the right. While they may have a similarly sized footprint, their reflective/absorptive properties are quite different. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. - the off centered positioning of the rear wall unit. Could the wall unit be centered on the rear wall, and ceiling corner trap run the full length of the wall? This positioning may also allow for corner traps in the rear of the room in addition to the proposed ceiling corner trap. - Perhaps some type of window treatment to stop the reflections off the window into the mix position.
Do you mean that the smaller superchunk (right hand side - window) will be an issue? I was hoping that the bed which is also on that side would act as a bass trap to some degree.
While the bed may have some degree of absorption qualities, it's a bit difficult to predict what kind/how much/to what extent it will have, so I would not count on it as an integral part of your design.
Finally are my monitors going to be accurate sitting on the desk or should they really be on sturdy (sand filled) speaker stands?
• Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. They would be better on stands, angled in 30 degrees, and the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips.
Would it be wise to run a thread in the "acoustics" forum to discuss the type of traps and thickness etc. or keep it here?
Chances are that it's already been posted; its just a matter of finding it in the wealth of info available. Perhaps a search would prove fruitful. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
