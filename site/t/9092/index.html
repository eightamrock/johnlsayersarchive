Eric_Desart14 July 2007#3
Nikodemos, I superficially checked that other thread. It's a bit unclear to me what these double walls you talk about exactly look like. Hence, just general: double glass blocks will not behave as a 4 leaf wall. These blocks act roughly as a single leaf. The same goes for hollow bricks and hollow concrete blocks. Hence you can use them. Their TL can roughly be estimated in function of their mass. Hence solid blocks are better. Whether you need them or not, needs more exact details from your side. But since I still don't exactly understand that wall build-up ....... I can't very well imagine that you integrate glass blocks in a gypsum board leaf. But you also can combine glass blocks and a window pane as a second leaf. Note that glass blocks more behave as bricks than as a flexible panel as drywall (relates to isolating above or below coincidence). If you explain your combination better it easier to get the feel.

Eric_Desart14 July 2007#5
Nikodemus. Your drawing looks perfect. What I should do is using the solid glass blocks to compensate for the loss in mass versus the concrete wall. Glass ca 2500 kg/m3. Concrete depends on mix, but say very roughly 2200 kg/m3. You compensate that somewhat already by your larger cavity, but not enough. Hence get the most of it by using solid glass blocks. Your laminated glass inside fits the mass of the drywall, which is OK too. It should have no sense here to combine heavy glass blocks in these drywall leafs. I couldn't make a better design. Now you have to find something to close that gap between inner and outer glass visibly with something that does not connect inner and outer wall. You of course can use non-transparent lamited glass on the inside as well, but still it's better that no dust can nest itself between these panes. And use something acoustic transparent as e.g. fabric. It's good that there is an acoustic shortcut between the cavity behind the drywall and the cavity between the glass. As such the cavity behind the drywall will act as edge absorption for the panes. Always note that hygrothermal conditions can cause trouble for moist, but that's for a lot of cases (also drywall) where is sinned against basic principles of a damp screen at the hot side.
