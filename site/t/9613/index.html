Started by Kyle on 18 October 2007. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 9613.

Kyle18 October 2007#1
If anyone had the opportunity to attend the AES show in NYC this year, you may have seen the SSL display which included the Mix Shed. If you are unaware of the mix shed(see: http://www.solid-state-logic.com/shed/ ), it is modeled from Peter Gabriel's writing studio at Real World Studio in Bath, UK. I was wondering about the concept of designing a pre-defined space that is acoustically optimized for mixing and overdubs. Really, taking the SSL concept and creating a blueprint of sorts for users. The mix shed structure used by SSL is from Summerwood ( http://www.summerwood.com/ ) which is available in differing sizes. Any thoughts on a group effort to choose a design (size) and gear list and draft a mixspace within those confines? Perhaps this could be a fundraiser to benefit this great site; chip in a bit of money for a completed sketchup file with all the bells and whistles such as soffit monitors that are matched to the rooms acoustics, acoustically neutral rack positions, etc... Money might help offset site hosting costs. Any thoughts?

That first sentiment seems an antithesis to the nature of this website and forum: people helping others at all levels of understanding. As to the budgets, permits, etc... that is the reason a size could be chosen that best represents the average area available to most site visitors. The acoustic properties of a 16'x20' space are largely determined by interior treatments and reflections from equipment and associated racks. Sound containment, on the other hand, is a function of building materials and Mass-Air-Mass construction. I would imagine a group task to design and outfit a common space would be quite educational for site visitors. Yes, budgets are varied. I have a $7,500 USD per month gear and expense allocation, that doesn't make the exercise any less relevant for me than it is for a newbie. My ATC SCM150s are as much in need of soffits as another's Mackies. The concepts are universal, that's why so few on this site answer the same questions repeatedly. Finally, how many times have we seen a question asked, a design offered, and zero follow thru? At least a yearly design effort could raise donations for the site and give more plans for people to work with. I feel the standard gear list would be a dual Mac G5 running pro-tools HD, most users on this site use PC's and would be substituting recomended gear for what they can afford. Neumann for berhinger Mac for PC green glue for bubble gum :) That's the whole point. A DAW, controller, and related peripherals would pretty much have the same issues of reflections and ergonomics until you get to the likes of a 8-12' SSL. Whether its a PT HD expanded system or a native Logic DAW, the gear is less relevant than the room (except speakers) when dealing with acoustics; which as you may recall, is the point of this site. If you don't have the knowledge or ability to find the info your looking for, than you shouldn't be doing it the first place. 9 out of 10 people i meet in this industry are all talk, with no knowledge or experience to back it up. anyway the fun is in the chase While that certainly is reason for some to hire professional expertise, this site is devoted to helping others understand what are sometimes rather abstract properties of physics. Most people seem to do well in grasping these concepts with visual aids. A completed plan with Sketchup file is great for visually bringing together concepts. The few on this site proficient with Sketchup offer so much for free to others, it is truly mind-numbing. At least a group effort with access to the file in a members area with passcode controlled by a small donation would benefit everyone. Serious members and newbies alike can visit a plan and ask questions and the donations help sustain a fantastic site and become an asset to draw more informed questions. On the whole, much like the SAE drawings, just scaled to real restrictions and constraints. If you have every envisioned actually using the garage plan and done so with real gear in those rooms, you may become somewhat claustrophobic.
