Soundman202022 April 2013#2
I have often read here you advise people to show a 40db range on the vertical scale of a waterfall graph. Many say it needs to be minimum 60db above the noise floor to have any real meaning. Your thoughts?
Exactly! :) That's two entirely different things you are talking about. One is the way you VIEW the data, and 40 dB of vertical scale below the average seems to be a good compromise for most cases. You can generally clearly see modal issues by viewing the top 40 dB of the decay. The other thing is how the measurement was taken: There, indeed, you would hope to have 60 dB of headroom above the noise floor, in an ideal situation. Inside a well isolated studio that should be possible, but you'd need to be producing levels up around 90 or 100 dB to do that. Fortunately, REW doesn't actually need to see a decay of 60 dB to come up with accurate results, but ideally it would be good to give it as much as possible. So yes, 40 dB of view below the test level, and 60 dB of headroom are good rules of thumb. - Stuart -

Soundman202022 April 2013#4
If you run the test at 85 dB, then I'd set the top to 100 dB, since there might well be modes that go up that high. Then set the BOTTOM to about 40 dB for a typical room. You can always change it a afterwards if you can't see thins clearly. This isn't a "cast in stone" rule that you have to follow religiously always! It's just a good place to start from when you are viewing your waterfall, and then re-adjust things as necessary, to better see the response. Or post your MDAT file here, ... - Stuart -
