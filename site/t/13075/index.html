Soundman20207 June 2009#14
- Does it matter if the left Wall/ceiling panel is 40% shorter than it symetrical at right ?
You should try to keep the room as symmetrical as you can at least as far back as the mix position. Behind that isn't so important.
- Does it matter if I place on the rear wall only acoustic foam and no bass trap ? Or do I need to bass trap a lot even in the rear ?
It's a small room, so you should try to get as much bass trapping as you can into it.
- Does it matter if there is no cloud panel ?
Yes! The ceiling is a first reflection point, and should have absorption on it to prevent reflections muddying the sound, creating comb filtering, etc. - Stuart -

John Sayers7 June 2009#16
I feel I must comment on this room layout. This room is going to sound dead and dull because all the highs have been removed - you've left practically no high end reflections in the room. Also the term Bass Trap is missleading because placed flat on a wall the absorption tapers off the lower the frequency. Take a look at this room http://johnlsayers.com/Pages/Hello_Testing.htm It's a standard bedroom yet Paul Goodwin produced award winning commercials in that room, the hallway outside was lined floor to ceiling with international awards - all the dialogue was recorded in the room - there was no booth. As you can see there is reflective windows and and a reflective front and ceiling - the rear wall and rear ceiling is the main absorption area. Its a matter of balance.
