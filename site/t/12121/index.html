Soundman202013 April 2009#5
If you cannot remove the drywall on an existing wall, then you have no choice but to build a three-leaf system, which means that your low frequency isolation will not be good. In that case, what you need to do is to compensate as best you can for the lost isolation, and you can only do that with more mass, more air, more insulation, and copious amounts of Green Glue. In other words, you will need to increase your air gap as much as you possibly can, so that your new wall is as far away as possible from the existing wall. I seem to recall a figure of at least eight inches air gap from somewhere but I don't recall where. Even more would be better. Then you need much more mass on that new wall: I'd go for four or more layers of drywall, and at least some of them with GG in between, at what they refer to as "100% coverage". The drywall will double your mass, which theoretically gains you 6 dB of isolation, and the GG will get you some more at the low end, plus the extra air gap helps a lot too. And of course, you would need to fill the entire cavity with rockwool to maximize isolation. Doing those four things would go a long way towards compensating for the three-leaf system. Rather expensive, though. Much cheaper to take off the existing drywall from your side of the wall, use it to beef up the other side from within, between the studs, then add RC, or better still RSIC clips + hat channel, two layers of 5/8 drywall with GG. That would theoretically get you about the same level of isolation overall, and even better in the low end, for less money. But as they say here: "you have to live with what you've got", so the method I outline above would be the best alternative. - Stuart -
