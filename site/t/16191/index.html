m-ga6 June 2011#1
I'm considering building a studio, or rehearsal room, at the bottom of the garden. I've attached a photo of the site I've got in mind. This is between 50-100m from the nearest houses (on one side; on the other it's about a mile of woodland). There was previously a barn on the site, but this is long gone. The plot is approximately square (and could easily be adjusted as trapezoid, or non-parallel walls, etc), extending beyond the kid's Wendy house at the left hand side. I'm going to be using it mainly for rehearsing drums and electric guitar, and a priority is preventing sound leakage. I'd like to record as well (ie. the acoustics should actually be good!) but it can be just one room inside, for now. The major drawback is that I'm on a very tight budget. I'll be doing as much as possible with found or reclaimed materials. The obvious route seems to be to use the existing back wall, and build up the sides, getting as much solid mass around the studio as possible. I'm considering digging down a bit, so you walk down steps into the studio, then using the removed earth to create a living roof. This would leave me with solid earth on three of the sides. I'd most likely build up the walls with breeze blocks, and whatever other mass I could find, and add a couple of layers of double glazing at the front (I'm thinking of second hand PVC windows) and two sets of PVC doors for entrance/exit. I'd need a ventilation system with baffles as well. I quite like the idea of the studio being almost invisible from the outside, as if it's built into the hillside. Hopefully this will be enough to prevent sound leakage, which would leave just the acoustic treatment to deal with. The next step is drawing up some plans in Sketchup, which I've yet to learn, but I thought I'd put this up now in case anyone has comments, there's anything I've overlooked, and so on. Thanks for listening!

"This then gives me something close to solid earth on all four sides." I appreciate your situation but digging down 3 feet doesn't change anything. In order for this to be the most effective, with your line of thinking, you have to dig down as far as it takes to put the walls totally below grade. Again, you are in a unique situation but do not let it fool you in that you can skimp here and hold out there and you will still get whatever isolation specifications you are trying to achieve. When all is said and done, the roof will be the weakest link, and this is again the most common area in the breakdown of isolation. But I do look forward to your continued progress, I think you have a very good chance to hit whatever mark it is you are attempting to achieve.
