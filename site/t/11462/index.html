xSpace4 December 2008#10
Consider where the rafter supports are after you moved them, versus where they originally were. There is a wall beneath and somewhere close to where the original position is. There is no wall beneath the newly afixed area...my guess. Don't think that snow is the only reason, it is not. The roof itself is heavy. Do you have a tile roof? It just got heavier. In unison, the supports help deflect wind loads as well, while maintaining the structure support. Each time you take one out, two things happen. The roof gets weaker in that area and the roof load is transferred to your exterior walls. Now, where once you had a support system per rafter you now have rafters on either side of the unsupported rafter trying to hold each other >plus< the unsupported rafter up. There are ways to do this, if in fact, it can be done in your environment. But pulling out structural support with no idea what it is you are doing other than your neighbor did it and got away with no problems, is not the recipe for a good design. And since I'm on it, whatever pressure points you develop in this build with your attic walls most certainly require support from the wall directly beneath it. If it is not there...all the above apply to that as well. And this process continues all the way down to the base foundation level. Doesn't matter if you want to hear this or not, you need to hear it. You do not know what you are asking of this structure and at what point a fatal collaspe will happen. There will be little warning. I do not even know how a load bearing wall under the roof ridge applies?
