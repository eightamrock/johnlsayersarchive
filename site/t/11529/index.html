Soundman20204 January 2009#5
Hi Rob, and welcome! Glad to see that you read the stickies and did the diligence! It's a nice change!!! Getting to your room: You don't say if you are looking for isolation, or for acoustic treatment. That is two totally different and independent things. Acoustic treatment does not stop sound. Acoustic isolation does not treat the room. In fact, good isolation usually makes the room acoustics worse, not better! You say:
Tracking can be at various volumes and mixing is usually about 85-90dbs.
But you also say:
The floor contruction is spaced vertical 2x8's with a particle board deck. At this time, it is scheduled to be covered in berber carpet.
Doing the math, that will give you maybe STC 20 to 25 or so (you don't say how thick the deck is). Meaning you'll have levels around 70 dB getting through to the room underneath. (And even more while tracking!). That is loud. Are you sure that is what you want?

Aloha and welcome to the forum Rob,
My main goal is creating a fairly accurate area to mix in.
For starters, here are a few bits of info gathered from the forum to consider: Setting up the Mix Position • It is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension because you don't want rear reflections to interfere • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Angled side slot absorbers/absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls angled out 6-12 degrees from front of room to rear • bass traps in corners (rear wall corners, rear wall/ceiling, front wall corners - if no soffits) • cloud (absorption panel/s) over mix position • Mix position roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall to start with • avoid sitting in peaks/nulls (both front to rear AND floor to ceiling) • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Monitor height – tweeters aimed at ear level (avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right & mix position • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. If you will be using soffits for your monitors: • Soffits angled 30 degrees each (60 degrees total) in from a flat wall result in speaker baffles being perpendicular to the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right & mix position • The baffle extension (the face of the soffit) should be as large as you can make it, up to about 45" in each direction from the woofer in a system with a LF cutoff of around 45 Hz - There's a formula based on LF cutoff that ensures full 2 pi response for the woofer if the baffle is at least the size obtained in the formula's solution I'll try to post some plans/illustrations when I locate them. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
