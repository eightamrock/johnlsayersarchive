Hi, Firstly and foremost, THANK YOU John for this website and for what you do. I've been lurking these forums for about two years trying to absorb as much as I can to formulate a plan to build a place to call my own. (I'm still learning and still pretty dense when it comes to things like modes and nodes.) Background None. I am not construction or electrically or acoustically inclined. (I do have an HVAC friend) Goal My goal is to be able to have a room that will accommodate tracking, mixing, and jamming (3-5 people) while also using the room for my daytime office job. I would like to get enough sound isolation to be able to jam/record and also have my room be acoustically optimized to the fullest posible extent (given my limitations). How Loud Most of the time I won’t go above 90db. Fairly quiet street save for the occasional souped-up Honda muffler going by every so often. I am indirectly above a flight path with the PDX airport 10 miles away. The house 2 two story with attic, garage is bottom floor. Not as much worried about sound getting in as the sound that would get out and disturb the neighbors as their garage is 10-12 ft from the outside of west my garage wall. Budget $2000 initially, but we all know how that goes. I have a tight budget right now. I already have the heat pump. Plan Floor space is limited so I am going to do John’s Inside-out walls with 2 layers of sheetrock and green glue in between. Wood frame will be on neoprene on concrete. Layout is the way it is because I need to have the water heater/furnace path clear and also cannot block the electric panel. Run HVAC from the Heat pump and tie in to the house’s air return, use silencers, etc. (more on that later) Questions Will the closet/booth in the design significantly affect the isolation to the outside. Is there going to be a third-leaf thing going on? I don’t want to go to the effort of totally decoupling it so if it’s going to be a Hugh factor, then it can go.

xSpace3 March 2010#2
The closet, as a coupling factor, will depend on what kind of noise it will encounter from the exterior for me to consider if it should go or not. I would make it go away or break it loose completely. Then there is your room geometry. You want the room to open up to your back, to be able to let the lower frequencies develop and then make attempt to trap them. As it is now, yours is backwards :) No big thing really, swap it end for end and then take a look and see what kind of issues you run into.
