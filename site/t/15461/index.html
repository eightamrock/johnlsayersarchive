xSpace5 January 2011#5
Thanks Jet, the clarification was to understand the mixed language "dual staggered stud". Like I said, it is either dual (two) or staggered. You are talking about a double framed assembly wall with offset studs, if it ever comes up in conversation :) Here is what you have to take into consideration, Since you are using 2X6 anyway, you should modify the interior room side layout to a 24 inch(609mm) center. It is always better to take the entire room into the design process...to develop a proper mass/spring/mass assembly. This would have begged the question of should the ceiling be modified towards that end, but we move forward. You are losing a lot of head room with this procedure you are using. Your exterior perimeter wall frame should go directly to the ceiling, this assumes that you are not in a basement and not under an unsupported truss span. Your interior wall will have to be reduced in height to allow for the installation of this reduced height ceiling. BTW, you did not include even the first measurement of this room, only help for an issue that you most likely shouldn't be doing any way.

gullfo7 January 2011#11
on the walls and ceiling which exist and will pair up with the new room, remove the drywall and (on the walls) beef up the exterior or house walls with the drywall you remove. on the ceiling, put in 2x layer on the upper part of the joists and then plan on using isolation clips underneath for the 2x drywall + GG inner isolation ceiling. using pink insulation in the ceiling. on the walls - build you isolation frames up to the ceiling joists but decouple using neoprene strips (top and bottom) and decoupled anchors (rubber washers and tubes to reduce contact). the existing wall and the new wall get pink insulation. the inside of your room, 2x drywall + GG. stagger the drywall layers between ceiling and wall.

xSpace8 January 2011#12
An alternative: If you can put some time into making certain your doors/windows and that garage door are sealed up well enough you can build one framed wall with rock on the inside of it. As it stands...you can do what Glenn suggests but that garage door is going to defeat your work. You would need to get about 12 inches of space between the existing coupled wall(your garage wall) and the frame of the new wall. Again...any windows, doors or paths that lead out of the structure are going to be your weak points and cannot be over emphasized as to the importance. The ceiling...that is a different thing all together. This is what I would suggest or let me say this, this is the way I would approach it. I would identify the point on the ceiling that is directly outside of the new framing, establish my layout from the floor up to that area on the ceiling and cut only that much of the ceiling out, My new framing would not even touch the existing structure. Joists would have to be aligned so as not to interfere with the existing overhead trusses, most likely run parallel with the existing joists. My new framing would have OSB installed to the face to add rigidity, joists would be installed on top of that. You will have essentially a room in a room, less the cost, plus the isolation. There are many details that I have not mentioned, fire-stop being one, and electrical, hvac being the others...
