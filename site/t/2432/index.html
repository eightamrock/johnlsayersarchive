knightfly9 October 2004#2
No problem; just your basic low budget, can't change anything, you've got to be kidding, generic black magic application scenario... :roll: First, you can spend your entire $1000 on a set of platinum plated, litz wired headphones and you will NEVER know from the phones what your recording will sound like when played on speakers in a real room - that's just a fact of acoustics, the only exception to it is if you could afford an anechoic chamber to put full-range speakers in; then, you might hear the same thing as in your phones, but it still wouldn't vaguely resemble normal sound in a real room. Second, you didn't mention if your sound bothering others is ALSO a problem - not that it matters, because without serious construction you can't change that anyway. Sound isolation is a 2-way street, and requires heavy masses separated by damped air spaces, period. What you COULD do is build something like this for your windows, since they will undoubtedly be more "leaky" than your walls - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=272 So about all this leaves (and it's within your budget) is to build some of John's wall units and improve the acoustics of your space - you can find them here, along with some basic ideas of where each type should be placed - http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm Adding a window between rooms will lessen the isolation you already have, unless you can afford a second wall and two heavy pieces of glass (several hundred$$ just for the glass) As to instrument and mic placement, this is nearly an infinite subject. There are entire books written on this subject. Some of the variables are things like what part of the instrument emanates the most/best sound, modal peaks/nulls of the room, speaker/boundary interference (micing amps) - for a good start on this, check out Amazon for Professional Microphone Techniques by David Mills-Huber, Philip Williams This covers most instruments, including sax, grand and upright piano, hand percussion, ad infinitum... I'm not trying to be mean here, but when you tie my hands and ask me to box Mohammed Ali, I get defensive... :? knightfly9 October 2004#4
That room will have flutter echo problems if you don't break up those parallel walls - I'd build a few of the angled slot resonators shown in the link to wall units, only I would build each one as a single "tooth" in the sawtooth pattern - in other words, build each section as a 4'wide varying depth slat absorber, going from 4" depth one side to at least 10" wide at the other side- you could alternate these with absorber sections (plain rigid fiberglass, cloth covered) along 1 wall of each pair of parallel walls - this will tighen up the room, kill flutter but not deaden it as much as just absorption would. For the ceiling, you'll need to do a "cloud" of 2" rigid fiberglass or rockwool, at a minimum covering the ceiling area over the piano; probably more, but listen before adding the rest. It's better to space this away from the ceiling by at least 2", more if you can. Floor should be mostly un-carpeted, with a few throw rugs you can move around. IF you can, find out exactly how the 6" thick wall is built between CR and live room, and if it's OK to build a second door to enclose that alcove at the lower right of the plan... Steve
