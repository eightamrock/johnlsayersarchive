Hello everyone thank you for reading this! I live in Guildford, south of London, UK. I am planning to build a studio for recording and rehearsal/writing. The majority of time using the studio would be for mixing/producing electronic and live music (80-90db), practising acoustic instruments and singing. There would also be the occasional use for recording live drums (up to around 120db). One room is already part of the house on the ground floor and I plan to build an extension on the back of the house. The extension also has to house a small shower/toilet facility in one corner. I’m planning to put this furthest from the existing kitchen if I ever decide to sell the house and someone wants to knock it through. Due to blocking in the existing rooms I need to incorporate some level of light for the kitchen and to give the studio a pleasant feel. I was thinking of skylights and one or two windows (depending if I have a half frosted glass door). I was planning on having a normal sloped roof to give extra sound space inside and would look better on the property than a flat roof. Any advice on how best to box in/leave open this roof space would be appreciated. The existing room I can use is adjoining the neighbour’s house so sound isolation is imperative, although the extension would only be attached to my house. I have a builder friend who is going to come and look at the project and give me feedback, he also has experience in sound proofing but any studio specific tips would be very useful. My main request is for design ideas as I don’t know which room is best used for control room and which for live. General layout ideas/advice would be greatly appreciated. It would be good to have a door from the extension into the garden (maybe triple glazed) for light and access rather than carrying gear through the house it could come through the side gate. My sketch shows the best plan I’ve made so far! Any ideas on best placement of windows and door would be useful. The two rooms are currently linked by a double glazed patio style double door. Is this adequate for sound exclusion to monitor drums? I like laminate wood flooring and would like to keep some energy in at least one of the rooms for writing and practising but also understand the need to minimise reflections for mixing and drum recording. I’m keen to make the room sound as good as possible and can see many different designs on here for bass traps etc...obviously these all take up space so how many and where do I need them! My budget for construction and sound proofing is approx £20,000 not including bathroom. With this in mind any comments would be greatly appreciated, thank you.

In general, the live room should be bigger than the control room, and the AES/EBU/ITU specs all call for a minimum floor area of 20 m2. However, it is still possible to build studios with much less floor space, and live rooms that are smaller than the control room. Done that way, it isn't optimal but can still be very usable.

The two rooms are currently linked by a double glazed patio style double door. Is this adequate for sound exclusion to monitor drums?
Nope. :) Drums are the loudest of all instruments, and the hardest to isolate. It takes a lot more than glass doors to do that, unfortunately. Sound levels on a drum kit can exceed 115 dB, easily. Most people consider about 45 dB to be "quiet". So you'd need about 70 dB of isolation to make "drums" into "quiet". An ordinary house wall gives you about 30 dB, give or take, so you need about ten thousand times more isolation that that: Yes, 10,000. Decibels are on a logarithmic scale... Most home studio builders aim for about 50 dB of isolation, and are very happy if they can get to 60. But 70 is a different ball park. It can be done, but it ain't easy, and ain't cheap!
I like laminate wood flooring and would like to keep some energy in at least one of the rooms for writing and practising
Excellent choice! Laminate is a great surface, acoustically.
but also understand the need to minimise reflections for mixing and drum recording.
No problem. The general rule is that if you have a hard reflective surface in one place in the room, then its a good plan to but a soft absorptive surface on the opposite side of the room. So if your floor is reflective, then make the ceiling absorptive. Normally 4" to 6" of something like OC-703 will do the trick. That's also good for the overheads mics on a drum kit, to minimize reflections coming back down from the ceiling, so "hard floor, soft ceiling" is a great plan for a drum room.
I’m keen to make the room sound as good as possible and can see many different designs on here for bass traps etc...obviously these all take up space so how many and where do I need them!
Bass traps are most effective in room corners, because all room modes terminate in corners. So if you put your traps in all the room corners, then you are guaranteed to be treating all possible modes. There are 12 corners in a room... :) It is normally not possible to trap all of them, but trap as many as you can. And make them as big as you can. The "superchunk" design is very simple, and very effective. 36" across the front diagonal face is ideal for superchunks.
