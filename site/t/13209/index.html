Hi guys, I'm so glad I was introduced to this forum before I start building my studio. I have just a few questions. I started off basically getting very excited about John Mayers "small studio" sketchup plan and then I started talking to the people that own the building and also looking at my budget and had to re-think. Here's the story... LOCATION I’m based in Auckland New Zealand STAGE I’m at the planning stage of designing then building my studio. CURRENT ROOM & PLANNED USE FOR STUDIO I’m about to move into a room that will be part of a community radio station and needs to be used for recording and mixing mainly electronic dance/hip hop/funk style music. Also to be used for recording voice-overs, adverts and doing production for the radio station. I’ll have the occasional drummer and percussionist coming through too. The building we are in is zoned for business and the studio has various offices and shops around it. No homes nearby. I don’t need to be loud most of the time but i will ocassionally need to blast the music. I reckon on average I’ll be doing lots of nearfield type editing of audio but also mixing at around 65-70db The room is partly on the ground floor on piles and partly over a car parking space. There are no rooms above it, just ceiling then roof, but the roof is corrugated aluminum (noisy when it rains!) I want a recording room thats big enough to fit up to three singers/musicians at a time or to put a small table in to record two people doing an interview. I want the control room to be comfortable for three people and have enough room for a pair of turntables, keyboards a sofa and the usual studio gear of course. I'm hoping to have some storage space in the recording area. LIMITATIONS The landlord won’t allow me to change the wool carpet or add extra walls apart from the wall between the recording room and the kitchen/office replace the ceiling and the dividing wall & sliding door between control room and recording room. Other main issue is the place is rented (I have a 2 year lease but will hopefully be able to stay there longer) and the landlord is OK with it being a studio but wants the room to be back to its original state when/if I eventually move out (she’s happy with the partition and extra walls. For both these reasons I’m hoping to sound proof the recording room as much as I can but its less critical in the control room. But I do want the control room to sound and look good for mixing etc. BUDGET My main issues are I’m on a tight budget of around NZ $3500 - $4000. On the plus side, our radio station has around 40 DJ’s, some of whom are able to help out for free. I can get the following for free: >lights and lighting design >architect (he can use sketchup, but I thought I'd talk to you guys before I talk to him about making a design >electrician (but not parts) >Builder (only available for around 4 hours per weekend) >Some wood (a guys who works nearby has loads of precut beams etc. but may nt be right size) >Some sound proofing foam >I’ll have free help with carrying, fetching, helping etc. >I can also get signal connectors cheaper than retail price I think realistically I'll have to pay a builder to do the big stuff and hopefully i can make acoustic panels & bass traps myself. I checked out John Sayers’ “Small_Studio” Sketchup file. and I’d like to base my design on that but make it work within my constraints. I A description of the room at the moment structure is wood frame on piles . FLOOR Underfloor beams size = Joist 250 x 50 mm @ 450mm centres Span between bearers is 3500mm Floor is 20 mm particle board, covered with underlay and wool carpet (I’m not allowed to remove or change/ damage the carpet) What I want the the control room section to be is on piles on what seems like earth & soil and under the recording area is a basement/carpark. walls Length= 7.45m Width=3.9m Height=2.53m thickness = 6.35cm (2.5inches) No insulation in walls walls covered with wood panels stud = ? I’ve also attached a rough floor plan of how I’d like to divide the room. My current plan please note, when you see my sketch the window corresponds to the bottom of the John Mayer sketchup of "small_studio" i.e. its the other way round! Far side of room is all single glazed window from floor to ceiling. I’m hoping to install a sliding glass door in one half of the total window so that I can have easy access from directly outside. I’ll need to build a wall (wall 5) and also divide the room into two (wall 4) I want to keep the natural light so don’t want to turn any of the window into a wall or block too much of it. MY QUESTIONS 1. Will John Sayers “smalll_studio” design still work in terms of a good sound even if I dont build the extra walls in the control room? 1b. If not, how do I go about making the control room sound good and have some degree of sound insulation (maybe panels hanging form the ceiling beams? 2. I want to block off door A and convert it to a wall. how do I do that? 3. Is it worth the expense and effort of doing a two leaf wall 5? when wall 2 will be untouched? 4. It gets damp in NZ. Do I need to worry about mould, ventilation, mildew etc.? Should I treat the wood or buy treated wood for such conditions? 7. where do I put air con units/ ducts? Are they essential in a studio that will be hired out on a semi regular basis? 8. SOFIT mounting. My monitors (Dynaudio BM5a’s) are rear ported. I’m told by two people that doing so will make the bass sound exeggerated. And Can I get away without sofit mounting to save money? I already have a pair of good speaker stands 9. Can I make removable bass traps and acoustic panels? 10. Whats the best way of soundproofing (as much as poss) the sound of rain on the roof from both rooms? 11. Would the low power transmitter of the radio station hinder any recordings? The transmitter would be around 30-40 metres away HUGE THANKS FOR ANY HELP RECEIVED! Jason

xSpace25 June 2009#2
"10. Whats the best way of soundproofing (as much as poss) the sound of rain on the roof from both rooms?"s You have to find a way to get under the tin and damp the metal, sound proofing is secondary in this respect. If you can get to the underneath side of the metal you can place dense insulation type panels directly against the metal to stop the pinging. Homosote would work if you have that in your area.

John Sayers17 July 2009#15
Jason - as you are building a room within a room green glue isn't really necessary, plus it's very expensive. Tell your carpenter to lay the first layer of plaster vertically on the frame and seal it. Then lay the second layer horizontally and seal it. Check out Eliot's build here: http://johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?f=11&t=6968
