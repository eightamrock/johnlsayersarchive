knightfly18 March 2006#19
You have read the REFERENCE section, right? Especially the part about mass-air-mass between any two areas you want to isolate from each other? The whole purpose of double frames is so you can put ONE leaf (any number of layers)on ONE side of ONE frame, and the SECOND leaf on the OUTER side of the SECOND frame - this gives you the widest air/insulation gap between masses, which gives you the best bang for buck in isolation... Steve (Not tryin' to get in yer face here, just making sure you don't do something you'll not like...)

Sandersd20 March 2006#24
Are you actually going to cut up sheets of drywall into 4'x2' pieces and place them in the ceiling grid and caulk the edges? :shock: If you are - you're completely nuts :? Why? Because it will not matter how well constructed the walls and floor are - they could be ten foot thick - the sound will simply flank the walls thru the ceiling. All the time and money you've spent will be wasted. You will not be able to isolate each room because the sound from the adjoining room will travel thru the ceiling and into the next room. If your walls extended to the roof and were sealed to the bottom of the roof, then you could do what you are intending and it would not affect room to room isolation. The ceiling should be 2 layers of drywall attached to the ceiling framing. The sheets should be full size sheets - 4'x8' or 4'x12'. the first layer should have the seams taped and the second layer should be applied perpendicular to the first and have joints finished. The end result is a ceiling boundry composed of one continuous - from wall to wall - diaphram 1 1/4" thick (2 layers of 5/8" drywall) with no cracks, joints, seams, etc. Only then can you expect adequate isolation from room to room. I hope I haven't misunderstood your intentions. If so I apologize. :oops: Good luck :D
