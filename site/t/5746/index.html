knightfly9 March 2006#5
Resonant panels are TUNED treatments and as such need to be placed in very specific locations within a room to work best; they also can make things WORSE if not correctly chosen for the job. This is why everyone, INCLUDING Ethan, now recommends broadband absorbers FIRST - it's much easier to get a smoother sounding room with less "rocket science" that way... Steve

knightfly10 March 2006#8
Ed, you're right about the time and money thing; John, I, and Aaron are all spread pretty thin these days; I understand your frustrations, but at the same time before I can make recommendations I need you to respond to Dan's question in your other thread http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=38808 about the strength of your place, which floor you're on, etc - I have to say that I really admire what you're doing, by the way... Making portable treatments is fine, Kendale has done so in his thread and they work fine. Just make the backs as solid as you can (I'd use at least 19mm backs since the formulas assume RIGID backs for both slat absorbers AND resonant panel traps. Positioning of slat absorbers isn't quite as critical as for panel traps, since they seem to act both as a passive absorber and as a resonator to some extent. Panel traps, in order to do much at all, need to be at the high PRESSURE point for their specific tuned frequency - this can end up being a place where you can't allow them to be. There are two main ways to determine WHERE these need to gp; one is to walk around the walls with a microphone hooked to a RTA, and place the panel where it's design frequency peaks. For all but first harmonic modes (lowest frequency ones for each dimension), there will be more than one choice for this. Another way is to download the Harmon mode calculator from their site http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5801 And only consider wall locations where the frequency you're trying to control has a PEAK in the display - if the Long wall of a room gives a mode frequency of 90 hZ, for example, and it's a SECOND harmonic, (12'6" length will do this), then there are three places where a 90 hZ resonant trap will work; at either end of the long wall, or at its Center. IF you use the calculator and set length to 12'6", you'll see what I mean - it's very visual, and the curves represent sound PRESSURE so where they are highest is where you'd put a panel trap. For absorbent traps, you do NOT want a solid back; just a perimeter frame and some corner braces with the rockwool inside the frame, cover with cloth and make some feet so it will stand up, and you're DONE. If you make these at least 4" thick (100mm) you can place them across corners, along walls, or several FEET away from a rear wall (in the CR, for example) and they will work. No airtight seals, just put them up and listen to the difference. Before I would build even ONE panel trap, I'd build at least EIGHT of these; 4 for the vertical corners, one overhead for a "cloud", two for sidewall reflections, and one for the rear of the room - with free-standing speakers, another one or two between the speakers and the wall you'd be looking at while mixing. There is a pictorial on another member's design of a "cloud" in the "stickies" section here - and a concept drawing of a free-standing version here http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download.php?id=2884 I drew this using pipe for a stand; the connections are what we call "floor flanges" - or you can just extend the sides downward and fasten boards at right angles to them for feet. This would be for a portable side absorber, for corners you'd do it more like these http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4581 Only it's better if they are wider (like 1 meter) for corner absorbers. Again, NO BACK other than necessary corner bracing. In all these units, stay as close to 3 PCF (48 kG/m^3) as you can find, and NO RIGID FOAM insulation; it's USELESS for acoustics, period. Hope this helps some; I still need your answers on the other thread linked at the top before we can help much... Steve
