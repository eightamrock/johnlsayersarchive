eightamrock15 September 2008#38
James - I face some similar issues. Here is an example of what I am putting together. Keep in mind that this is a basement studio. with 2 walls of each room being separated by 3" from the concrete blocks. I have 2 isolation rooms that I am building right now, the first is a 10' x 12' drum room the second is a 5' x 12; guitar tunnel / vocal booth. I have 2 walls (2x4 construction) separated at 1" with 2 layers of drywall on either side (1 layer of 5/8's and 1 layer of 1/2") of the walls assembled with GG between each layer. I have no drywall on the inside of each leaf (wall). I am using high density insulation between the walls, but after cruising this forum and getting advice on my own posts, I have decided that drywall is the way to go. So I stopped there. The interior walls of each room are decoupled from the ceiling using 1/4" fan fold cut into strips. This is a pretty high density insulation material that is rigid to keep the walls in place, but has a little to no sound transmission. I am doing nothing but commercial carpeting on the floor since cement is the best isolator and I am building directly on my slab. The ceilings will have RSIC-V Clips and Hat Channel for decoupling with 2 layers of drywall like the walls 5/8" and 1/2" and GG assembly. I accepted early on in the project that it would never be sound-proof, and based on my small budget this was the most effective way to go. I am putting this in the basement of a townhouse.. so my concern is that my neighbors are not severely inconvenienced. The best advice I got on the forum as well was, "Use More Drywall" and "build a room in a room"... Which is what I am trying to do. Hope this helps you some...

Because of the windstorm that rocked the midwest (remnants of Ike), we haven't had power for the last four days. I was supposed to start framing in my new fourth wall on Sunday. That didn't happen. After various input here and re-reading several sources outside of here, I decided to spend the extra money on a second layer of drywall for the entire room, and a double layer on the outside of the two walls which I have access to the other side (top wall on the drawing, and the new wall, right side on the drawing). I've also decided to augment my ceiling plans with Ethan's idea of using the rafters under my subfloor above as a bass trap. I'm going to install 703 or 705 rigid fibreglass as follows:
Ethan Winer wrote:
For a typical unfinished basement ceiling you can take advantage of the gap between the support beams and the floor above by placing rigid fiberglass between the beams. Short nails or screws can support the fiberglass, making it easy to slide each piece of fiberglass into place. Then cover the fiberglass with fabric as shown below in Figure 3b. You can optionally pack the entire cavity with fluffy fiberglass one foot thick and you'll probably get similar results. Treating a basement ceiling. Figure 3b: 705 between support beams, covered with fabric.
Since I already have a drop ceiling in place (I just have to augment/adjust it for the new wall) below the rafters, I won't have to cover it with fabric. I was considering doing Ethan's suggestion of soft fibreglass above the drop ceiling in addition to the above rafter treatment, but I don't have the space to do so between them.
Ethan Winer wrote:
Treating a "dropped" grid ceiling is even easier: Simply lay fluffy fiberglass batts on top of the grid, above the ceiling tiles. The thicker the fiberglass, the better. One foot thick R38 is perfect for this if you have the space. If you don't want to bother covering the entire ceiling that way, at least put fiberglass batts around the perimeter to treat the important wall-ceiling corners. And since the fiberglass is not exposed to the room and doesn't show, you don't need to cover it with fabric.
As I posted near the beginning, the tiles in the drop ceiling are acoustic, and they'll also be augmented with existing wedge foam on the underside, which I already have. This should at least soften the mids and highs on the ceiling, and hopefully the rafter traps will work as ceiling bass traps. I'd consider building and hanging absorbers and diffusers, but there's just not enough room. The drop ceiling, which is only 2.5 inches below the rafters, is already at less than 7 feet. Anything hanging below that will make the room difficult for tall people - it's already a bit claustrophobic for those taller than me (5' 10.5").
