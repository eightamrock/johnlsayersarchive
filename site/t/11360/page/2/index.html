Ok now let's start with the panel construction. I designed the frames first in sketchup wich is great and so acurate. After the designs where finished I ordered all the parts and have them pre cut in order to assemble them. All the height sizes are 1.22 and the widths are 61cm x 5cm (Hi Absorber), 80cm x 10cm (Mid Absorber) and 80cm x 15cm (back wall broadband and cloud absorber) Also there are Corner Bass Traps 61cm x 28cm deep. All will have clothing + 1" thick rigid fiberglass layer, then 3 1/2" glasswool, and finally a layer of expandible polystyrene (the material used for packing) wich is porous too but retains the small glass particles to scape from the back of the panel and float in the air. My theory is that the deepness of the cavity and the different thickness and density of the materials inside the frame will make an excellent panel absorber for all the frequencies. Of course I will need to build slats for low mids, and maybe some hangers for the super lows. My plan is to reduce the reverb in the rooms and later test and treat individual anomalies like standing waves or specific room modes with a dedicated panel absorber tuned to the annoying frequency. I used 18mm thick melaminic MDF for all the panels.

Ro24 March 2009#33
it all looks, really great. Good craftsmanship! Great photos. Keep'm coming. couple o' comments: 2x 1" thick wool for a basstrap is waaay to thin. You need atleast 4" (10cm). The pink insulation on the back of the rigid panels, how thick is that? And what's with the hardfoam backing? (EPS/polystyrene) It has no acoustic value to start with. It would be wiser to put that on FRONT since it's a basstrap and you wanna keep it for low frq only (the polystyrene will reflect mid/high frq and therefor acts as a low pass filter) For a basstrap to work it needs volume in wool. thicker is better.

Soundman202012 August 2011#48
Looking at those photos, it seems you are not using full range speakers for the test, so everything below about 100 Hz. on those graphs is most likely not valid. Anyway, it looks like you don't have enough bass trapping in that room yet, and what you do have is not in the optimum locations. You get the best effect by putting the bass traps in the tri-corners (a corner where three surfaces meet, such as wall-wall-ceiling, or wall-wall-floor). Theoretically you get an 18dB boost from tri-corners, and only 12dB from normal corners, so simply lifting your traps up to the ceiling or sliding them down to the floor might improve things. But you still need more of them, and they should be deeper, too. There also seems to be something wrong with the way you have REW set up, since the waterfall plots never get down much below 30 dB, and you have peaks beyond 300 ms. even at 250 Hz, which seems unusual: Do you have a lot of ambient noise in there? Anyway, I would add more bass traps in better locations, and measure again.
