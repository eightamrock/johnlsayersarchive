Regarding floating floors, I strongly recommend that your read this thread: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=8173
and how many slat resonators, low bass traps, and HI freq absorbers to put and where.
The basic idea is to have as much broadband bass trapping as you can get, and the best place for that is corners, especially the front and rear vertical corners. For a small room, you need lots of bass trapping. Your first priority with absorption should be your first reflection points on the side walls and the ceiling, above the mix position. It seems that the best advice for the rest of the treatment, especially from people like Rod, is to wait until you have the basic room built, then take measurements to determine where your problems are, and design treatments for those specific problems. Sure, you can predict nodes and performance with calculations, but that is never going to be as good as actually measuring the room once it is built. My $0.02 for today! - Stuart -

Soundman20207 November 2008#9
Should an electronic drum Kit hooked via USB to EZdrummer or BFD be a better option for having great drum sounds?
That's the 64,000 dollar question, isn't it? I don't think anyone else can answer it except you. It all depends on what you are trying to do with the your recordings. Personally, I much prefer a full acoustic drum kit to an electronic drum kit, simply because even a half-decent drummer can get very different subtleties out of an acoustic kit, simply by the way he tunes the heads, the way he hits the drums and cymbals: position, angle, velocity, stroke, stick, style, etc, etc. You just can't do the same thing with an electronic drum kit. And as good as sampled or synthesized drums are today, in my opinion they are still no match for a good set of acoustic drums played by a master drummer. It just does not sound the same, and there is nothing you can do with electronic drums to get the same feeling and character into you drum tracks. That's my opinion: yours may be different. (Your mileage may vary. No warranties either express or implied. Void where prohibited by law...) On the other hand, if you don't need subtly in your recordings, if all you want is an absolute pounding constant repetitive beat that never changes much no matter how you hit it, then sure, electronic drums will do that for you just fine. They also have stacks of different pre-programed sounds that you can switch beteen at the touch of a button, whereas an acoustic kit only has the sound that you set it up for, it takes hours to change that, and even then can never give you the broad range that an electronic drum kit can. Another advantage of electronic drums is that its fairly easy to separate out the individual pieces onto separate tracks for more control in mixing, if you want to do that. even a very well close-miced acoustic drum recording will have lots of bleed between mics. It's unavoidable. But cleaning that up (or enhancing it!) is part of the fun of mixing drums, in my opinion. So it's really a matter of personal preference, the style of music you plan to track, and what you are tying to accomplish. However, a really good electronic drum kit is not going to be much different in price than a decent set of drum mics and a basic pre-amp. I wouldn't worry about a dozen mic stands, either: most drum mics these days don't need stands: Just clip them right onto the drum rim, at least for the toms and snare. You only need stands for the overheads, kick and hi-hat. I'm sure everyone has his own opinion on electronic vs. acoustic drums, but what more can I say? The only person that can REALLY tell you what is best for you, is you! That said, if you plan to mix and listen at high levels, you'll still need good isolation in your room. If you are pumping out 100 dB from your speakers during mixing, then you aren't far short of the level of the acoustic kit anyway, so you might as well do the best you possibly can with isolation, regardless of whether you decide on acoustic drums or electronic drums. That's my $0.02 on the subject, FWIW! - Stuart -
