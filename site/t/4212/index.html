sharward15 August 2005#2
larlev wrote:
I have read numerous post regarding 2x6 staggered stud construction and fitting a double door into the opening. Couldn't you use a 2x8 for your walls and a 2x6 for your ceiling, you would have ample room for a double door, without having to rip down 2x4's to make it work.
I think if you can afford the square footage of 2x8s as sole and top plates (actual size 7 1/2"), it would make more sense to do double wall construction with 2x4s (actual size 3 1/2") with a little space between the frames. Keep in mind that staggered stud construction is a compromise -- not nearly as good as double frame, but much better than single frame. Once you're at a certain "footprint," you run out of reasons not to do actual double frame.

larlev wrote:
OK I have read and read...... I plan on using double wall construction....
Good.
2x6 staggered stud ceiling...
Do you mean staggered joist ceiling -- the technique of allowing the tops of your inner leaf joists to tuck between the outer leaf joists? :?
heavy carpet under base plates
Huh? :shock: Where did you get that idea? Is this your attempt to decouple the walls from the floor? :?
whatever insulation is best....
Why not fill the cavities with Thermafiber Sound Attenuation Fire Blankets (SAFB) - Nominal Density (2.5 pcf) or Roxul Acoustical Fire Batt (AFB) - ≥2" (2.5 pcf)? This stuff will probably satisfy your local building department's requirements for a firebreak in the void between the walls without having to permanently couple them together with lumber. That's what I plan to do on my project. 8)
2 layers 5/8" gypsum on both sides and 2 on ceiling.
2 on each leaf of your ceiling? Or 2 on a single leaf ceiling? If a single leaf ceiling, your ceiling will be your weak point, will not compete with your walls, and that would be very bad.
I can only build the inside dimensions to about 8'x10' with a ceiling height of 6'8" to 7' if I am lucky. I no this will be small but that's it.
I'm pretty sure that will not meet code. Most doors are 6'8" -- imagine a ceiling no higher than a standard door. Not good. Not good at all. That doesn't even count your acoustic treatments. Not good.
I really don't want to remove any material existing on walls and ceiling.
That could be a huge problem. You may end up having a 3-leaf wall, which will be crap for isolation on the low end. I have just reread all (at this point 5) of your posts on this forum, and none of them actually cover the level of detail that is called for in the "Before You Post" announcement. It really would be best for you to read that announcement again (several times if need be -- sometimes that's what it takes) and then post back with answers to all of the questions raised there. Otherwise, we're giving you feedback on very incomplete data and the results could be (at best) disappointing or (at worst) downright dangerous. :shock: (cont'd. next post)
