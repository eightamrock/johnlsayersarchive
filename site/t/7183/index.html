knightfly5 October 2006#22
That's the right sketch, although the one with the modified moulding would actually need a wider "heel" on the moulding for stability. The general idea for both double doors and double windows is to vent the space between the leaves INTO the inner, insulated wall area for a bit of added absorption, thereby slightly improving performance of the door or window. Ceiling - if it's already built, what do you propose to do about it? Here's the thing - optimum use of materials and space for walls is, as stated several times just in this thread by several people, mass-air-mass. So if you were to use just ONE of your air gaps in the ceiling as a bass trap, it probably wouldn't change isolation much - ideally your ceiling should be filled with fluffy, non-backed fiberglass and then several vents (think of a heat register for example) placed around the perimeter of the inner ceiling - this, because the highest SPL of modes will be at corners whether it's wall/wall corners or wall/ceiling corners or whatever. Placing vents at these positions so that the bass can enter the cavity and be absorbed is a good way to utilize ceiling cavities, assuming you already have a 2-leaf barrier OUTSIDE of this. Please understand that even degreed acousticians are VERY hesitant to "guess" what the results of multi-leaf walls will be - the BBC, in true British fashion, (and in my biased Yank opinion :=) simply built more walls until the problem went away. I hope this helped, I just got off 12-hour night shift so probably missed (or screwed up) a few things... Steve
