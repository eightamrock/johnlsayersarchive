Soundman202012 October 2011#4
I suppose my best course would be to completely remove the current drywall.
Yup! That will help to avoid the dreaded 3-leaf problem, and will also give you access to the interior of the walls, so you can beef them up from inside.
Not sure how much space I am going to lose as you can see I don't have much to begin with.
That depends on how much isolation you need! You say you are peaking at around 100 dB right now, so you need to determine how quite that needs to be after the isolation is complete. You can figure that empirically with a sound level meter (testing at various levels to see when the wife / neighbors call "acceptable") or legally: Call your local municipality and ask for a copy of the noise ordinance. The difference between the "must be this quite" number and the "I am this loud" number is the amount of isolation you need. That will dictate the type of construction you need, and hence the thickness of the wall that you need. Simple! :)
As you see there are windows in the rooms. ... I would rather keep this windows than block them in.
Ouch! :) You can keep them if you want, but you might need to replace the existing glass and frames with something more substantial, if you need high levels of isolation.
Floors are currently carpeted but will go with wood or wood laminate.
OK, but what is UNDERNEATH the carpet? That's what matters. Concrete slab on grade = good. Plywood / OSB subfloor on joists = not so good...
Construction: Cinder block with stucco on the outside.
Good start! That should be giving you decent isolation for your outer leaf.
The space is small but not sure what the best utilization of the space is. If someone can help me kick start this thing then we can go from there. Thanks so much for any help.
I think I'd start by taking out that dividing wall, where you said "I am willing to remove this if it makes the space easier to configure". Yes it will. However, you don't say if you need both a control room and a live room or iso booth, or if you just want a control room! For just a control room, that's a good-sized space! Adding an iso booth will reduce it slightly, but trying to add a full live room will make things too small (based on the dimensions that you said were wrong and should be ignored... :) ) So if I were you, I would be figuring out the basic plan first: (1 room only, or CR + iso, or CR + LR ), then think about the orientation that makes the most sense, given the positions of the existing doors and windows, and the isolation requirements (taken from the above sound level measurements). Based on that I'd do a rough outline of the inner leaf (leaves) for the room(s), figure a good ratio for the CR, figure the optimum geometry for the CR (speaker location and angles - console location - mix position - RFZ - etc.). Then with that layout in place, you can start figuring the basic treatment, following the usual rules of thumb for small rooms. That would be my approach, FWIW!
seems to be a big area. You will succeed
It isn't all that big, and he will only succeed if he plans it carefully, designs it well, and builds it properly. - Stuart -

Soundman202018 October 2011#9
Question, how much space should I leave between the existing walls and the ones for the studio?
That depends on several things, Ken, including how much isolation you need, what type of music you'll be isolating, and how much mass you intend to put in each of the two leaves. A basic rule of thumb is to never have less than 4" (10cm) of air space between the inner leaf and outer leaf, preferably 8", or even more if you have very high isolation needs, or need to isolate very low frequencies.
