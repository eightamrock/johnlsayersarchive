giles1174 July 2004#4
Here are some links with basic ideas of construction. A desk is really not that hard, you have to have the frame that holds it up and the flat surface you choose to use. A hardwood is the best way to go instead of a plywood. How deep you wanna go is another factor. Most of the desks you mentioned (except for the argosy) cnsist of 3 flat surfaces joined together with some sort of framing. a Lower flat surface, the main flat surface and the monitor shelf (flat surface) Not hard at all. Easily built for under $100 Bucks. The next Question is how pretty do you want it. that is when the price goes up. These links are not specific, but give you some construction concepts. http://popularmechanics.com/home_improv ... fice_desk/ http://www.mercurynews.com/mld/mercuryn ... 854.htm?1c As for me I built a desk to house my Mackie 32x8 and a 24E extender. I kept it simple. I only wanted to impress myself, so I made it up of a series of 2x4's 3/4" Particle Board and 2x6's. Made an armrest the height of the front of the console and covered it in neoprene and vinyl fabric. Painted the desk black and voila I had a mix desk with a monitor stand above the console. Bryan Giles

so would it generally be a bad idea to put the speakers on the desk? i know that early reflections should be considered, as well as the right angle and height positioning, but if i attached a similarly built stand to the desk, is there anything im forgetting that would cause problems? thanks a million for all the help, ev
knightfly5 July 2004#8
Placing them on the desk the main concerns are early reflections (placing them behind the desk, the angle of incidence off the desk is usually shallower and reflections miss the ears and go for the back wall instead; also, decoupling. This can be helped with something like foam mouse pads, or the Auralex Mo-pad thing. Place a large flat mirror on your desk (or over your console, if it's on the desk) - if you can see your speakers in it, move them away from your head until you can't see them in the mirror. If you can't do that, some people just use a piece of Auralex foam mounted on a piece of plywood and mount it temporarily over the console, at an angle where you can still see the controls but the sound can't reflect off the console. Compromises are everywhere; mainly it helps to know what your choices are, so you can choose the lesser evil... Steve

If you might consider starting with a simple, super strong, welded steel bench custom built to your size requirements, you could take a look at these guys: http://www.benchpro.com/ The most basic bench (4 legs and a laminate top) is a great starting point for a custom studio desk. I had one build to 48"w x 30"d x34"h and I'll probably never buy a replacement - it will certainly outlast me (welded steel). The price was a pleasant surprise: about $225 for the size I wanted plus about $60 for Fedex Freight delivery. I had checked out probably every studio furniture brand and quite a few hi tech "creative office" furniture companies and was always disappointed by how non-sturdy the desks were. Although I am operating without a console right now, I might have one of those little Studer 269 mixers in my future and those things weight about 70lbs. 90% of the studio or "creative office" desks looked like they would fail if you looked at them funny while supporting that kind of weight. The Benchpro table could easily support the console and whatever truck delivers it to my studio.
