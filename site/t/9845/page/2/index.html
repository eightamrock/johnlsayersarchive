The first layer of drywall goes up on the iso room ceiling. The yellow wall behind the drywall installer is the west iso room wall. The door leads to the backyard. It’s currently comprised of one layer of regular drywall over fiberglass insulation. When you hit it, it resonates like a drum head. I was originally going to install one layer of regular drywall over RC-1 here. I changed my plan because I was so impressed with this cellulose insulation – fist-pounding drywall over the cellulose yields only the dullest of thuds with almost no ring at all. So I decided to pull off the drywall on this wall, give it the cellulose treatment, and then cover it back up with one layer of QuietRock 525. No RC channel. This morning I had a teleconference with my drywall subcontractor, his QuietRock seller, and a rep from QuietRock. The QuietRock rep is adamantly opposed to installing QuietRock 525 over anything other than studs. Two reasons for this (1) it’s not worth it given 525’s already allegedly awesome capabilities, and (2) craftsmanship issues resulting from less-than-optimal attention paid to details when securing the drywall to the channel, the idea being that it could do more harm than good – “short-circuiting” the effectiveness of the 525, according to the QuietRock rep. That said, and turning our attention to the south wall (the furnace and water heater are right behind it), I was advised by the QuietRock rep to just put one layer of 525 over this wall, which spans the full length of the studio (both rooms). I was prepared to face this wall with RC then apply both QuietRock and regular drywall (the other side of the wall has one layer of regular drywall). Well, he talked me out of the RC, but I’m still going to have one layer of 525 and one layer of regular drywall, with a case of Green Glue between the sheets in the area right in front of the furnace. I’m doing this because I already have the materials – might as well just use ‘em since they’re already sitting there. In addition, the electrician positioned the boxes on this wall to accommodate two layers of drywall, and these boxes are already sitting in a heavy nest of cellulose insulation. Taking them out of there and repositioning them would be a mess and it would displace the cellulose that’s already there, compressed into those important spaces where having good insulation is really important.
External image, not preserved — original: http://aycu26.webshots.com/image/36785/2006039789343829721_rs.jpg
Incidentally, holes in the electrical boxes will be plugged with this putty stuff after all the wires are secured. This material is actually designed to cover the ENTIRE *outer* surfaces of all the boxes, encasing every box in a solid wall of putty. But again, we put the cart before the horse and installed the insulation before doing the boxes. I don’t really care. I can stuff as much of this stuff as I want to right in the front of the boxes. Same difference to me. Learning what little I have about building a studio before actually getting into it for real, I’ve been condition by Gervais to worship mass. The QuietRock guy telling me to forget about having two layers of drywall wherever I can have it, and especially over the noisiest area in the studio goes against my intuition, whatever it’s worth. Then there’s the matter of my case of Green Glue – which you can only use by doing double layers. Add to all this, the sound-absorbing quality of the cellulose insulation. I’m convinced that this insulation is the #1 best sound absorber in my plan. It’s so good that I feel like I don’t even need to fret over any of this other stuff. I could actually start recording in there right now if that stuff wasn’t dripping all over the place and making a mess. So whatever I do will be cool. Maybe a bit of unnecessary spending, but happy endings. If I had more area to cover I’d be way, way more concerned about budget and these kinds of decisions regarding channel, drywall types, Green Glue, all that. My studio is small so the there’s a definite ceiling (no pun) on how much money I can waste.
