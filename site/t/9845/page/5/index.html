Corner absorbers. I had originally planned on wrapping triangular frames (3) and sticking them in various corners of the iso room - equilateral triangles, three feet on a side. When it came time to do it, I was kind of stumped as to how to mount a pre-wrapped corner piece like this. So I decided to put up the frames first and then stuff 'em as opposed to trying to mount wrapped items. I sanded down the cheap lumber (cheap because we had originally planned on wrapping 'em), stained and polyurethane 'em, and decided that they looked OK enough to hang.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/MountedFrame.jpg
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/Turnbuckle.jpg
I hung the frames using three turnbuckles per frame. The drywall anchors got some brown Gorilla Glue. Here you can see drywall anchors, turnbuckles, s-hooks, and screw eyes. This was super easy to do. You don't even really need to measure much, just make sure that you space the anchors far enough apart so that you can get some pull on the turnbuckles. Stay as close to the sides as possible and try to get it so that most of the pull comes sideways/parallel to the wall. Don't use the ceiling as an anchor point! Just tweak away at the three turnbuckles until you have a nice tight fit. These frames are very secure and they aren't going anywhere.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/FoamStuffedFrame.jpg
I stuffed the backs of the spaces with some Auralex and other bits of foam. Here's a shot of one cavity about half full. I bought a bunch of Auralex thinking that I was going to glue it to the walls, but then I changed up the plan. Exposed foam kind of goes against the grain of my design concept for the look of the studio, which evolved to 'arboretum without plants'. :D So, this was a great way to get rid of the foam. I may actually get a plant, but it'll have to be cactus or something. :wink:
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/FeltCoveredTriangle.jpg
For these, Debra glued felt to the back of the 703 triangles and then gift-wrapped them in the lovely green fabric that she got for $1.99/yd. I wish we could find more of this stuff. I guess that's one of the drawbacks of using fabrics that you get on sale like this - it can be hard to get more when you need it. We ran out after these three triangles. We looking for more but it doesn't look like it's gonna happen.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/FinishedFrameNice.jpg
When you tap on the front of the panel, it resonates at a frequency that sounds like it's somewhere between a low tom and a floor tom. Maybe these'll help dampen out some specific problem frequencies (like that buildup you always need to scoop out at 250k) in that part of the spectrum in addition to the general broadband absorption. I was actually kind of surprised at the lowness of the pitch. Midnight and all's well,

The sound here is astounding; big and round with no hollowness at all; the exact opposite of what you'd expect when barking up into a hole like this.
External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/TFusorwith8lbInsulation.jpg
T'Fusors have a 1" ridge for laying in a piece of material. I used some 8 lb. stuff and put the scraps in the bowls. Incidentally, this row of T'Fusors is under the entrance foyer at the front of the house. Maybe I reduced footfall noise in this area a bit by stuffing the T'Fusors. Can't hurt! I attached the T'Fusors with Tubetak adhesive and staples. Advice: if you plan on using Tubetak, do yourself a huge favor right away and get yourself a Black & Decker electric caulking gun. Tubetak is good stuff, but it's EXCRUCIATINGLY hard to squeeze out of the tube. Make it easy on yourself I say. It's totally worth the $40 or so you'll pay for it.

Vertustudio23 June 2008#123
Hi, My primary reason for putting in those corner traps was to change the shape of the tri-corner space. The 703 triangles resonate at about the same frequency as a lower-pitched tom-tom, and my uneducated opinion is that this is a pretty good frequency to have some absorption on, considering that I'm always carving that range out of my tracks with EQ anyway. The foam behind: I'll be honest; I put it there to get rid of it. I've been asked about sealing the corners around the insides of the triangles. I didn't do it, but I could have easily, because the lumber is already very tight against the wall and there's only the tiniest of spaces there if there are any at all. I'm sure it's not a big deal that I didn't. It's been a while since I posted here, and those triangles are still super tight on the wall, and very easy to look at. I would recommend this turnbuckle method that I used to put these up. Works like a charm, and easy as pie to throw up and secure. Fun too; there's something very satisfying about tightening a set of turnbuckles. :wink:
