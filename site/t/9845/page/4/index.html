The fabric wrapped duct foam inserted between windows and door jambs is an upholstery fabric by Mill Creek Fashions. It was an expensive designer fabric but Debra found it as a large quantity remnant 50% off, so it may be a discontinued print. It's a heavy material similar to cotton duct. External image, not preserved — original: http://i281.photobucket.com/albums/kk206/mikescheibinger/CRDark.jpg
After we installed the pass-through panels, I began slowly and deliberately polishing stuff up and moving it into the space. A lot of old gear never made it back in—liberating myself from that junk was sublime. Here’s what the control room looks like at the moment. This is basically an 80+ channel lightpipe/analog setup that’s perfect for music composition and tracking pristine hi-fi mono or stereo passes (through one high quality stereo recording channel). To the left of the 02R console is another digital mixer (01V, covered at the moment), and to the right, an additional DAW system (MOTU 828 / laptop in for repairs) that functions as a virtual instrument farm that I address via MIDI. An assortment of guitar gear can be seen on the far left; I’ll be running speaker cabs in the iso room and playing LOTS of guitar at the board (down with amp simulators!). The new rug is a cosmic beauty that Debra picked up at a T.J. Maxx for $300, proving that it pays to shop around. The only new gear in this picture: the KRK VXT8 monitors. I’ve always liked KRK speakers. They’re especially nice for people like me who spend lots of time composing and tweaking, because they have a pleasantly elastic sound that I find to be ‘motivating’ or ‘stimulating’. I can stay interested pretty much endlessly working through these. The VXT8s LOVE being cranked! Before I got ‘em, I thought I was going to need to get a pair of Primacoustic Recoil Stabilizers to put them on, and I still might do that (Anybody have any comments on these?). However, the VXTs, with their dual-wall cabinets and the thick pads on their bottoms, actually vibrate my console LESS than my little Finnish Pro-X monitors (very nice) sitting there next to the KRKs. That was surprising. I like how the KRKs interact with my 32 sq. ft. ceiling cloud (made of 703)—while the KRKs have that characteristic pleasant, kind of ‘rubbery’ zing to ‘em, the cloud just DRINKS UP an enormous amount of their sound, which helps to stiffen it up and dry it out. So the KRKs and the cloud work well together to provide a vibrant and exciting sound that’s still clinical enough to engineer with. Other things I like about the KRKs are the muting system (shuts down the power stage when you walk away); being able to power up and down w/o popping, and oh yea, those little logo lights on the front. :D Really cool. I can’t think of a better value in the price range ($1,200/pair). I couldn’t imagine using a larger monitor in this space. An engineer buddy of mine stopped by for an extended music listening session. He owns a pair of the elite KRK Exposé E8Bs. He said that the VXT8s sound similar, except that the Exposé E8B’s soundstage is ‘about five times as deep’, which makes sense considering that the Exposé E8Bs are about five times the price of the VXT8s! As for the VXT8’s imaging, the jury is still out because I don’t have my sidewall treatments in, and I only have a few panels of raw 703 propped up behind me right now. Even in spite of that, I like what I hear, even at low levels. Old jazz recordings are especially interesting to listen to at low levels in the mix position—I’m hearing new things in the soundstage all the time on classic Miles and Coltrane stuff. After the room treatments are up I’m sure it’ll sound even better. So I’m 90% set up in the space and getting busy already. I’ve been jamming out on some heavy dub music (reggae sounds INCREDIBLE on the KRKs—I’ve never heard Family Man sound punchier) and kicking those KRKs deep into the night while Debra sleeps peacefully—sometimes she can hear muffled thuds, like a very distant tribal thunder, heh. Joy! There are a few room-related (not speaker-related) problems: there’s at least one frequency at which the bass pretty much disappears. Backing away from the mix position takes you under the ductwork soffit, where the bass blooms – not good, especially with the big KRKs in play. I’ll get the whole story after I put all my wall panels up. I *knew* that soffit was going to be a sonic drag. The KRK’s big sound really highlights the issues. There’s probably no correcting the soffit issue, which would actually be a client’s listening position if there were a sofa there (there’s not). I’ll be tweaking on that though and we’ll see what happens. Any advice is appreciated.
