For an instrument to sound good you must hear a balance of its high and low frequencies. If your room is too dead in the high frequencies the instrument sounds lifeless and tubby, whereas if it's too live in the high frequencies the instrument sounds distant and has no presence. To balance a room you must have a true balance of the high frequencies and the low frequencies with the right amount of deadness to liveness.

knightfly12 January 2007#13
I think part of the confusion is the term, "sound board" - this has, up until recently, been used to describe a product called Celotex or similar products, which are MUCH lighter than drywall. The product you're describing sounds like one of the newer "super drywall" products spawned from the increasing requirements for better sound isolation between apartments, etc... You're correct that you can change the acoustics/modal responses in your room by using these "porta-walls" - anything that provides a hard surface will have a corresponding mode set within the room. It's also handy that, depending on what you're after, you could place either the hard OR soft side toward your space and affect the acoustics in yet another way besides effectively changing the room size. As long as you realize (and it sounds like you do) that these won't give enough isolation to amount to much, I think you have a good plan to optimize your space acoustically... Steve
