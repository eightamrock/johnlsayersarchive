I've read Rod's book twice now, so I feel ready to start a new post about my build. I'm doing a garage conversion studio in the house I just bought in Los Angeles (Van Nuys, CA) My budget is around $3,000. The existing room is 19x17. I plan to duct in AC from the house. The unit is about 4 years old and is oversized enough to allow for the extra Sq. footage in the new studio. I intend to do a room in a room construction. Aside from the ceiling which I'm thinking I would like to vault using one layer of OSB and a layer of 5/8" drywall over the OSB. The roof is 3 years old and is on it's second layer (composite shingle). Any thoughts or ideas on the ceiling would be greatly appreciated. I've already begun doing a beef up with 2 layers of 5/8" drywall in the existing stud bays. It's an attached garage, the firewall is very thick lathen plaster. Approx. 1 inch thick. I realize it would be ideal to remove it and do a beef up but I'm going to take my chances with it. I plan to frame in a new wall in front of the garage door with 2 layers of 5/8 drywall on the outside before beginning my room in the room build. I would like to end up with a vocal/gtr booth in one corner also. My biggest concern at this point is the ceiling and what is the best route to take with it. I've attached a few photos. Thanks in advance, Heath

jimmyjams22 August 2011#10
Here's some updated photos. I've completed just about all of my beef up and insulation. All that's left is the small portion over the garage door. I ended up using furring strips around the outside perimeter of my garage door and then caulking and painting it to match. On the inside I framed a wall out of 2x2's inside the garage door cavity with the door mounted on it. The wall was fitted with 1 layer of 5/8 gypsum before I lifted it into place. Once I had it nailed in I caulked the inside and then stuffed in one more layer of gypsum in the 2x2 stud bays followed by insulation. I just finished the framing on the walls this weekend. I angled the two longer walls 6% to avoid standing waves in the room. I also ended up going 24" on center since they are non bearing walls. It's awesome to finally see the room take shape!

Soundman202021 September 2011#22
Quiet glue and genie clips accomplish two different things. Genie clips decouple the drywall from the studs. Quiet glue adds constrained layer damping between sheets of drywall. Two different concepts. - Stuart -
