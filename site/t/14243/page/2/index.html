Soundman20208 September 2010#37
However, I since I am 6'5" tall, in order to get the speakers to ear height I have the design currently with the center of the woofers at about 4' 4". Is the 3' 11" rule simply because not many people are as tall as me?
Yup! :) The idea is to have the acoustic axis of the speaker at the same height as your ear, ("you" being the guy sitting at the mix position). If that happens to be higher or lower than 1.2m, then adjust as needed. Either raise/lower the speakers to get them to the right height, or tilt them forward/backward a little, for the same effect. By the way it is NOT the #center of the woofer" that you want at ear height, but rather the acoustic axis of the entire speaker cabinet, which is generally much closer to the tweeter than it is to the woofer. Some manufacturers do publish the location of the acoustic axis of their speakers, others don't. If you can't find that info for your speakers, then use a point either on or close to the tweeter, and you won't be far wrong... So if you have your woofers at your ear height right now, then you'll probably need to drop your speakers a few inches to get the acoustic axis at ear height. Regarding the vent issue: active speakers in soffits will practically always needed cooling vents, while passive speakers possibly wont, unless you run them at very high power levels all the time. - Stuart

The answer is: "yes!" :) As the saying goes, there are many ways of skinning a cat. There are many approaches to designing a studio, and most of them work. A rectangular room with parallel walls, properly designed and treated, can be a really great room. And it can also be really lousy if not designed/built/treated right. On the other side of the coin, a room with splayed walls and ceiling can also be a really great room, if done right. Or it can be really bad if done wrong. So the answer is: "six of one, or half a dozen of the other". Both approaches work, and there are pros and cons either way. Design trade-offs for one reason or another. Splayed walls do take up more space, absolutely, but then so do large treatment panels. Also, splaying walls makes it much harder to figure out room modes, whereas for a rectangular room is is dead easy. But splayed walls are necessary for a true RFZ. Soffit mounting your speakers almost begs to have your side walls splayed too. And splaying just looks cooler! But it is harder to do and might be more expensive. Etc. For every reason that you can give me for splaying a wall, I can come up with another one for not splaying it. And vice versa. To me, it seems to be more an issue of personal preference: do you like the look of splayed walls, or hate it? Do rectangles mess with your sense of aesthetics, or do you love them? That's pretty much it, as far as I can see, since either approach can be made to work just fine. - Stuart -
