Hi there NBarnes, and welcome to the forum!
and put some free carpet I got down in the room
Oops! :shock: Then you are just in time to get it out again, before you advance so far that you can't! Carpet is really lousy for studios: it does the exact opposite of what you need in a studio: it provides a lot of high frequency absorption (you don't need any), a fair amount of mid frequency absorption (you only need a little), and it provides no low frequency absorption at all (you need lots). There's a reason why you never see large areas of carpet on professional studio floors.... :) So get rid of that carpet, and do your floor as something hard, solid, rigid and reflective, such as concrete, ceramic tiles, or laminate flooring. Those are the best surfaces.
Here's a diagram of the empty space, it's awkwardly shaped in an unfinished basement and it's tough to figure where to fit everything.
You don't have a lot of options there at all! To be honest, I would close off the "vocal booth" space, then use the area marked "drums" for the desk. That's about the only place that makes any sense at all.
It's also not symmetrical at the mixing position as the left wall is MUCH closer than the right wall.
That's a major, big-time problem. Symmetry is absolutely critical. If you don't have symmetry, then you don't have stereo image, sound stage, clarity, etc., and your mixes will not translate.
I'm thinking of making some sort of a makeshift wall
You keep talking about "makeshift" and "movable" and "on rollers", and suchlike: is there any reason why you cannot build a proper studio down there? - Stuart -
