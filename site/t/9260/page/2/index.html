johnp4 April 2008#40
Hi Joey It maybe be different for you as yours is a flat roof and mine is a pitched, but when i had the building inspector come round a month or so ago, i had a lot of condensation building on the membrane under the tiles. He noticed that my roof had no ventilation and when i described to him what I was doing wrt room within a room, he said that he wanted me to put ventilation to the outer roof. What I have done is every 300mm put in a soffit vent which in fact almost within 24 hours cleared up the condensation problem. You might also want to consider something like this, and also get some treatment on the mold once you've sorted out ventilation

I think you already have your proof. It isn't specifically movement of air, the external climate and the internal climate can create condensation. The warm air moving towards the cold air. You can kill the mold by spraying bleach onto it. Please wear goggles and a face mask and long sleeves when spraying as this can be very hazardous to your health. Another thing I would look into if this was my building would be to seal the outside. Regular block is very porous and can act like a sponge. It can suck in water and hold it in the blocks. When you consider that rain water spills over the edge of your roof and then just waterfalls onto your existing blockwalls, this is an area that requires attention. hth,

TomVan5 April 2008#44
Hey Joey, Actually code in the Sates is to ventilate. Mechanically or by way of vents alone. Reason being the slightest bit of moisture can cause dry rot and or mold if it has the chance to build up. What I ended up doing was to build two Silencers and installing a fan in one of them. Installed at opposite ends of the building. Put grates over them on the outside so no bugs or birds could call it home. That way the area in question would get fresh air moving through it. And in the summer it helps vacate the air beween your walls and ceiling which will get very warm and makes it hard to cool the working space. Hope that helped Peace Tom

Hi Joey With regards to Isolation, the answer for my build is putting in vents would make little to no difference. My construction has standard uPVC facia and soffits, which would be pretty useless for isolation anway. What i'm in the process of doing is beefing up that outer layer, which is the standard pitched roof joists, by boarding out the loft area with 18mm flooring board above which i'm hanging RW3 traps. This will then be beefed up with two layers of 12.5mm plasterboard from the inside (as per Rods book) pior to building the inner room which will have a completley independant ceiling (ie joists resting on the inner frame wall) I think its effectivley similar to what you are doing. The difference is that the building regulations inspector mandated that i provided ventilation to the outer roof...period. cheers
