Aloha, In Rod Gervais’ book “Build it Like the Pros,” http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5670 he points out both L.W.Sepmeyer’s & M.M. Louden’s formulas most widely used in professional studios worldwide. He also notes that the orders listed are (top to bottom) the first to third best ratios in both cases. L.W. Sepmeyer Height Width Length 1.00 1.14 1.39 7’ x 7’10” x 9’7” 1.00 1.28 1.54 1.00 1.60 2.33 M.M. Louden Height Width Length 1.00 1.40 1.90 1.00 1.30 1.90 1.00 1.50 2.10 On small vocal booths Steve (Knightfly) mentioned that "4' x 5' x 7' inside dim's is about the best modal compromise for a small booth." Modally, with a 6'4" ceiling you'd do well with wall dimensions (inside) of 5 and 7 feet. That combination shows no modal problems at all. This means that vocals in such a room shouldn't be "colored" by the room. You would want to keep the upper half, including the ceiling, absorbed with such as rigid fiberglas insulation boards, covered with a light plastic film (they shed) and fire proofed cloth (they're ugly) - you'd likely get better vocal sounds with the singer seated rather than standing, since otherwise they will be nearly at the ceiling. Being that close to a boundary (especially if it isn't absorbed) will cause comb filtering due to early reflections. That said, it'll be tight fit, but overall, it's doable. Aloha, I was reminded of the tonal coloration of small booth designs, and thought I'd offer this bit of info collected along the way -
My understanding of small vocal booth/voice-over treatments tend to lean toward the "dead" side. During the insulating phase of my front soffits, I noticed how nice and quiet it got with each 4' x 2' x 3" section of rock wool that was installed. Bryan Giles cautioned however, against building a square booth saying; "Be careful, a dead square booth will have low end issues (you'll hear the issues on your mic.)
When construction your vocal booth, you may want to consider putting up the cloth covered insulation and do a few mic tests to see how the booth sounds before installing the slats, and then add them in increments while testing to see at what point you have a nice balance of liveness/deadness or have covered the wall/s being treated (Note that John's design calls for only two of the walls to be treated with slats.) Be sure to listen for any comb filtering that may occur when positioning your mic and adjust as necessary. You may also want to consider a cloud absorber to treat the ceiling/floor reflections. Aloha 8)
