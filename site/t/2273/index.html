Slat resonators are very easy to build. You have your main iso wall. (the square/rectangular room) you determine the angle from the wall that the resonator should follow. (like the angle in your design.) Build a wall frame along that angle. Seal that area of space behind the wall frame. I used drywall. Between the studs put in insulation (rockwool) cover with some cloth. Whatever color you choose :) I used green. The attach the slats to your false wall on top of the fabric. That simple Hope I kept it clear for yah :) There are pics in the studios under construction section to show the build process. :) Here is one. knightfly8 September 2004#11
LRG, welcome to the site - looks like you are getting there quickly, it's always nice when people do a bit of looking around before asking the same 10 questions; On the slot resonators - everything you do will affect the outcome, but some things are more important than others - I've corrected a wrong formula in the helmholtz calculator that's on the SAE site John sold a few years ago, the correct version (including one John added recently for imperial measurements) can be found here - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1363 This requires Excel on your computer. Note there are entries for slot width, slat width, slat thickness, and distance from the wall - you can see just by changing any one of these what the change in center frequency will be. One important thing is the cloth behind the slats - this needs to be immediately behind the slats (as in, touching or nearly so) or it shifts the results (not sure how just yet, I just got Cox & D'Antonio's new absorber book last week and haven't had more than a few minutes to dig into it, and it's deep enough to drown in :? ) - The main reason for slanting the fronts of these is to maintain the RFZ in a control room, or (for a live room) getting rid of parallel walls to help control flutter echo. At frequencies below maybe 200 hZ, the wavelength of the sound is long enough that a varying depth absorber will mostly just AVERAGE out, because in a smaller room you're already in "pressure" mode instead of "wave" mode, so the entire resonator will see the same phase for all practical purposes - for that reason, the "depth from wall" part of the calculator should get the average depth of the absorber. So, if your particular absorber goes from 4" to 20" deep, you should enter 12" for depth. Higher frequencies will tend to see this absorber at a more "local" area, so the effect of varying depth will (IMO) broaden out (almost always a GOOD thing) Hope that helped... Steve
