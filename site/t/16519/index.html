Hi Daniel, and Welcome! Looks like you have a reasonable basic design.
the rooms are next to a day care centre in the same building,
:shock: OK, so good isolation is a key design goal, for sure!

Good! excellent base. But what about the walls, and the ceiling/roof?
I am convinced about the room within a room designs and plan to build with floating floors that rest on neoprene.
Sorry, but you don't have the budget to do that correctly. Floating a floor successfully will eat up your entire budget, plus more. The good news is that you most likely do not need to float your floor anyway. I'd suggest that you read these carefully, to understand what is involved in floating a floor: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 http://www.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/obj/irc/doc/p ... /ir802.pdf Take a careful look at both of those before you decide of you actually do need to float your floor. To be honest, the ceiling and walls are far more likely to be the weak points in your studio than the floor.
About soffit mounting the near field monitors, I'm willing to do it but I have to be able to change them later on since I'm planning to get some Genelecs in near future.
There is a method (invented by Barefoot, I think) for doing that. If you search the forum, you should be able to find it. Basically you leave a square section in the middle of the soffit that is larger than the biggest speaker that you might ever use, and you build specific square plates that fit into that and also fit around your speaker, each time you change. I don't recall all the details, but it's here on the forum somewhere!
I did find viewtopic.php?f=12&t=718&start=0 about flush mounting for variable size.
Yup! That's the one!!!! Not hard to do.
Also one of the reasons is the door in between the two main rooms. I don't know if it's a good idea to soffit mount with a door in the middle?
No problem. Take a look at some of John's designs for very small spaces, such as the container-studio. You can do that, if you design carefully. Make your soffits as big as possible, and the door as small as possible, to maximize soffit space.
I am not sure about room dimensions and ratios. I would need some help there.
Use one of the many on-line calculators, plug in your existing dimensions, and see here you are. Adjust your dimensions until you get close to one of the known good ratios, and that's it. You don't need to worry excessively about ratios. As long as you are not close to a really bad ratio, and you have a fairly even modal spread, that's all you need. Not necessary to worry about hitting a fantastic ratio perfectly: just stay away from bad ones, approximate a good one, and you'll be fine. - Stuart -

Going back to your first post, you said:
most of my mixing is done between 85 - 90 db with the occasional peak of 95 db. I intend to have a drum booth in the live room, so for the live room I think the insulation will have to be more then that of the control room.
Drums easily go to 115 dB, and more if you have a heavy-handed drummer. Getting that down to a level acceptable for sleeping infants is a tall order. You are talking upwards of 65 dB. You don't have the budget for a floated floor, but you do for the next best thing: drum riser. At the very least, your drums and amps should be on isolated risers, to prevent impact noise and vibration getting into the concrete floor. This is on the GROUND floor, correct? In other words, underneath that concrete slab is just plain old mother earth? I'm looking at your updated design, and you seem to have three-leaf of four-leaf structures in the area of the drum booth: You need to fix that, if you want maximum isolation! 3-and 4-leaf will rob you of isolation in the low end, which is where you need it most.
I'm just concerned about good sound isolation between the two rooms
OK; so there is already a brick wall between those two, correct? And that cannot be removed? How thick is that wall? It will be a partial 3-leaf system in that area, but you can work around it. Just need extra mass and larger spacing. Have you decided on what NC curve you want for your control room? Tell us more about that roof.... Concrete? Metal? How thick? What is above?

Tell us more about that air vent... Purpose? Size? Photos?
If RC isn't required then how about using the staggered stud wall method?
RC and staggered stud accomplish the same thing: decoupling. You need maximum isolation, so you need two leaves on separate frames. And ONLY two leaves. Not 3, not 4, not 1. Only 2, at every point around your studio.
Also is it required to float the walls on Neoprene or should I put the base of the frames directly on the concrete floor?
For what purpose? If you want to use neoprene as an airtight seal, then fine. But if you are trying to float your walls, that's a different story entirely.... Read here: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 In other words, that is hard to get right, and pointless if you get it wrong. Beside, there may be issues with your local building code: where I live (Chile) internal stud-frame walls must be solidly attached to the floor with bolts, nails or suchlike, thus negating any possibility of decoupling (the reason here is earthquakes. Big ones! You don't want the wall jumping up and down on the floor, or sliding across it due to the movement... There might also be fire code issues. Check with your local municipality to find out what you can and cannot do.
Please keep in mind that I am trying to reach an as high as possible amount of sound proofing because of the activities in the building on the same floor.
The floor is not your biggest problem: The ceiling is. You need to figure some way of isolating your section of the building from the day-care section, such as maybe a vertical brick firewall that goes all the way up to the roof and seals against it. If you cannot do that, then you are probably stuck with a 3-leaf solution, implying more mass and larger air gaps to compensate for the lost isolation in the low end.
I'd like to be able to record live drums, which is why I want a drum booth so that it provides additional sound proofing and separation as well.
it won't work with the way you have it now: you have the drum booth built as a separate structure inside the live room, which is built as a separate structure inside the outer leaf. Thus, 3 leaves at least (maybe more, depending on where you were planning to put drywall). To clarify: All three rooms are inside the outer leaf, individually. Each of the three rooms is surrounded by its own inner leaf, and no room is inside any other room. Imagine three matchboxes sitting inside a shoe box. Three separate rooms, not touching each other, and sharing the same outer leaf.
The second picture shows how the frame is attached to the brick wall. So that's easy to remove. It's just some small wooden slats behind the wall.
You might as well get all of that out, I reckon: it is no use to you the way it is at present, and it would be useful to be able to see the bricks, and how they attach to the ceiling. - Stuart -

Although the Rockwool would add mass, it isn't enough to make a big difference, and that isn't really the purpose of insulation anyway: It is meant primarily to damp resonance, second to absorb, but not to add mass. It won't help at all to isolate, but will help in other ways. Think of it like this: Is a sponge useful to mop up spilled water every now and then? Yup! Can you put a sponge over the end the tap, to stop the water coming out. Nope! Doesn't work. Water pours through, same as always. It's the same with insulation and sound: good for treatment ("mopping up" spilled sound where you don't want it). Useless for isolation ("in front of the tap" trying to stop sound getting through). So I'd still use absorption in that roof space, and it will help with some things, but it won't isolate.
