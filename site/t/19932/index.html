Yup. Like RJ said: that wont work, and would in fact make the situation WORSE, not better. Correctly floating a floor is an awful lot harder that most people think! And seldom necessary anyway. Here's why: viewtopic.php?f=2&t=8173 You might want to suggest that your Facebook buddy should read through that thread as well, to understand why his idea doesn't stand a chance of working. To float a floor successfully, you need two things: a lot of mass, and a lot of math. (OK, make that three things: you also need the correct type of resilient mounting, the right amount of it, in the right thickness, cut to the right sizes, placed at the right locations, etc., etc., all of which is what the math will tell you). And the amount of mass you need is measured in hundreds of kilograms per square meter, ten times more that what you can get from a couple of sheets of plywood. Several inches of concrete is about right... - Stuart -

Soundman202029 May 2015#15
My questions now are in regards to the 38% listening position. Due to the amount of gear that I NEED (that's what I tell the significant other at least), my initial idea was to position myself as close to front wall as possible - with some room between the wall and the desk. I have the soffits at 45 in this design. - I've read that this is not optimal, but can this work? What would I need to consider?
The 38% listening position "rule" isn't a rule at all. It's just a good starting point, but it isn't written in stone. You can change it a bit if you need to, as long as you stay away from the "bad" points at 25% and 50%. Also, 45° is OK, but it's about as far as you want to go. "Standard" is 30°, and you can go a bit less than that too, if you have to for a very long, thin room. So anywhere in that range, say 25° to 45° is OK, but try to stay away from the extremes if you can.
Optionally, I could split the room in 2,
I don't think the room is big enough for that.
Also, would splayed walls be a benefit in this design or better of sticking with rectangles?
Keep it basically rectangular, then add splayed sections needed to get the RFZ right.
- Is it possible to use a splayed wall say for 30-40 percent of the length?
Yup! You only need to splay the front part of the side walls, and only far enough back to ensure that the RFZ is properly established. So you need to go maybe as far back as the mix position, maximum. - Stuart -

xSpace11 September 2015#17
Problems/Concerns: - The existing structure studs are 2x4, so I have 3.5" to fit drywall into. I initially wanted to do 2 layers of 5/8 between existing studs, but I don't know if that would leave enough room for insulation. 3.5 - 1.25 (2 layers) = 2.25" - Is this enough for insulation?
Assuming you have distance between the bottom plates of the framing, yes you will have enough. As long as the insulation isn't compressed in so much that it creates a bridge or short circuits the isolation you will be OK.
- The air gap between the yellow wall and brown was is 3" on the width sides and 2" in the front (west side). The 3" was done on the sides because the yellow wall needs to be in this position and gave me the correct ratio for the width. The 2" front wall is just because (no real reason). Should I shrink the front to 1" gap? (more on this in next point).
No sir.Move your west wall starting position to match the rest at 3", and allow the East wall to move into the open cavity to maintain your wanted ratios. Essentially you just move the entire foot print of the framing (looking at the above picture) left to right.
- I'm not sure how to deal with the length. As you can see the back wall of the room is the inner leaf and the white wall is acting as outer leaf, but there is a gap of a little over 3.5' there. I feel like I should try and utilize this space, but the width is very small, so I'd love suggestions.
Either develop a machine or storage room or a deep bass trap area. With small rooms the modes are the acoustics as they say, so low frequency is going to be an issue anyway. Either option will help. If you do create an actual room you should install a solid core door with proper seals to ensure good isolation and no rattles. Also you have to consider that if you choose to make this an active room you may well need to provide air in/out if nothing more than to extract humidity from the small room, ESpecially if it becomes a machine room of some sort that will generate heat.
