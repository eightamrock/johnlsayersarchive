Soundman20202 August 2011#4
Hi Chris, and welcome.
1) sealing the cracks between the doors and the door frames with rubber or putty,
Sealing cracks will help, but it probably wont be enough. You need mass, and those doors don't have it.
2) building broadband absorption panels that cover the entire area of both the inside and the outside of the doorways.
contrary to popular belief (and intuition), absorption does not stop sound. Think of it this way: can you stop the water coming out of a tap by putting a sponge over the tap? There's your answer: the sponge soaks up water really well, but cannot stop water getting through. Same with sound: you cannot stop sound by putting an acoustic "sponge" in the way. You need mass. Acoustic absorption is a necessary part of treatment and isolation, but it cannot isolate all on its own, in any reasonable thickness. The normal way of dealing with a problem like yours is to build a "plug". Basically, a large piece of wood, or maybe a wooden frame with drywall on it, rubber seals around the edge, and absorption attached to the back (mineral wool, pink fiberglass, 703, etc). You build it carefully so that it is a tight fit into the frame around those doors, and so that the rubber seals tightly. That will do the trick. HOWEVER! That may not be the entire problem. You mention. "but the sound they make (footsteps...". Footsteps. That is impact noise, not just airborne noise, so it might be flanking through the building structure itself. If so, then the door isn't the problem: the entire building is the problem. I'd suggest that you should try to determine if the footstep sounds really are coming only through the doorway, or if they might be coming through the floor/wall/ceiling structure itself. I'd start with that, before spending too much money on building the plug. - Stuart -

The footsteps are on the hardwood floor most likely, not in an overhead area. Any door that you can shut off, that does not have to be used, then you should at minimum stuff loose insulation into the cracks around the perimeter of the door(s) as a start. Doors that glide on a track or slide require modifications. It could be as simple as a rubber seal nailed into the door edge to reduce the flow of sound through the crack. The bottom of the door, being elevated might take something as simple as a dense material pushed up to the bottom of the door to slow those areas. Doors are difficult and need attention that cannot be administered directly if you cannot modify the door so you have to go with simple things that produce mass as Stuart suggested but do not alter the interior environment and can also be easily removed. Weather stripping on the door edges at the jamb area might help as well.

Soundman20203 August 2011#10
So I am thinking that I will get the best results if I combine the sealing of the cracks around the doors with adding mass with the gobos.
You seem to be missing the point of isolation: Gobos are not meant to isolate a room, and indeed cannot isolate it. Gobos are treatment. Putting a couple of gobos in the room will do nothing at all to keep the footsteps and other noise out of the room. All that gobos will accomplish is to maybe improve the acoustics of the room itself. Yes, sealing the gaps around door will help, and yes you need mass, but unless you put the mass into the doorway so that it seals tightly all around, then you will not get the isolation. That's why I mentioned the concept of plugs.
also expanding foam seems like it might work for the same purpose.
If you are talking about the typical closed-cell polyurethane foam that comes in a spray can, then that isn't very useful acoustically, and sticks like crazy! Even better than caulk. You also seem to be missing the point of sealing: If you put masking tape on the door then aply some type of sealant on top of the tape, trying to stop it from touching the door, then you are defeating the purpose of the exercise! To "seal the door" means that you seal the door! Not the tape. The sealant has to contact the door and the jamb, all the way around, to be effective. If you absolutely cannot stick any sealant to the door, then just buy several meters of rubber seal, the kind the use around car doors, and stuff that up all around the edges of the door, on both sides, then put tape on TOP of that, to keep it in place. The tape does not seal: the rubber does that. The tape is just there to keep the rubber in place. You'll probably need about 30 meters of rubber strip to do that. Get the very flexible soft spongy kind, just like you find around the edge of your car door. Don't get a hard rubber.
I'd build them to these specifications here,
Once again, that's a gobo, not a plug. It won't help to isolate, since it is treatment. What you need is a plug, not a gobo. And yes, you do need to seal it in place with the same kind of rubber strip that you use on the door. It must be an airtight seal, all around the door frame. So you will have two seals: once around the edge of the door itself, and one around the plug.
2 to cover the inside and 2 to cover the outside.
Bad idea! that would make a three-leaf system, which would REDUCE your isolation for low frequencies. The footsteps and drums would be louder like that, not quieter. Although the voices would be gone, for sure! [quote]It seems like facing the reflective side towards the unwanted noise source would be most effective, quote]It would make no difference at all. If you went with gobos, instead of a plug, you could face it either way, and the result would be exactly the same: no isolation. - Stuart -
