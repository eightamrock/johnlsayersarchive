Hi James, long time no see. That really is a good question and I really wish I knew a good answer. The air flow is a given, it has to be there. But as I am seeing this, the air that is needed for the actual sheathing of the roof basically remains unobstructed, yes? I think this is important in so many respects as it pertains to material longevity, thermal control and the like. As this progresses you will have a room in a room type design, with a sealed inner air envelope yes? So that is also good, as we both are aware. Whatever you do in this cavity, if this were me, I would not allow the insulation to restrict any of the air flow area from the porch(is it the porch?) to the rafter to rafter space, and I would also keep an open area from the sheathing downward of at least the height of the venting baffles, more if I could get it. Reasoning is the baffles are more to prevent soffit or overhang from being stopped up with insulation while allowing the owner to make use of the maximum depth of insulation needed to maintain an energy efficient home. They have never been considered a replacement for open air access. Outside of that, you may require someone that can tell you with more authority than I currently possess, me being just some guy on a forum in the construction trade :)
