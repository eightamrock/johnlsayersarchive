jwl12 March 2008#2
Hey there Jon, that's a wicked cool space. Congrats. Are you looking to keep it one big room, or are you looking to build out multiple rooms? You mention building out the walls with M-A-M, but how will you achieve airtightness and decoupling for the ceiling? I think my inclination -- assuming you will be staying here a while and want to build it out right -- would be to "beef up" the ceiling/floor planks with additional mass (drywall/greenglue) as Rod describes on pp 67-68. If your Sketchup drawing is accurate, you'd probably have to build a frame in place to hold the drywall there... perhaps you could use a variation of John's inside-out wall design, and use 2 layers of sheetrock with GG in between, then some 2x4 framing below that, installed so that they are attached to the beams? the sheetrock would be flush against the ceiling/floor beams. Then you could stuff insulation inside the framing. Then once you get the walls built, you could do another ceiling attached to the inner walls. This would get you true M-A-M.... All the standard disclaimers apply, ie, make sure the beams can hold the additional weight, etc etc etc. If you aren't going to do room-within-a-room construction, then I'd consider doing something with resilient channel or RSIC-1 clips. I'm sure others will have construction ideas here, too....

gullfo14 March 2008#6
i don't know, you might need more massive beams in there :shock: i think that those beams should be OK from a mass standpoint but from a airborne-to-structure borne transfer? hard to say. one option would be to build a partial soffit along the edges of the beams (use RSIC or other rubber isolation mounts to hold the soffit frame) and finish the wall off to those and cap on the face of the beam. then if you find the beams are conducting too much sound, you'll have the option of adding a face over them without having to tear out the other ceiling bits to redo things...
