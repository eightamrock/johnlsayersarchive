I doubt that anyone here will tell you that it is OK to ignore code. And most likely you can be forced to tear down or re-build if your construction does not meet code. Building codes exist for several reasons: safety is one of them. If you build something that does not meet code and then someone gets injured or property gets damaged as a result, you would most likely be held liable for everything, perhaps even criminal liability (for example, if some dies as a result of what you built). Code should never be ignored, broken, or even bent. - Stuart -

Soundman20201 May 2010#4
The simple answer is no, code departments can not make a person tear down a structure or building.
Actually, they most certainly can! Sorry to disagree, and maybe in Alabama inspectors don't have any authority to enforce regulations, but where I live, a building inspector certainly can close and condemn a building if it infringes code dangerously, for any of several reasons. OK, he can't do that if you painted it the wrong color, but he sure can if there is a structural issue that could lead to collapse. So your blanket statement is totally wrong. It might apply where you live, but does not necessarily apply to the rest of the world. Form first-hand experience I can tell you that Municipal inspectors even have the authority to shut down an event in a public auditorium, theater, etc., and to seal the building, for infractions as simple as unsafe rigging, insufficient aisle widths, blocked emergency exists, fire hazards, etc. How do I know this? Because I've been in meetings for organizing such events where the municipal inspector has read the relevant regulations to the organizers, to make sure that they are aware of the facts. If you rig a speaker, scenery, projector, screen, lighting truss etc. in such a way that it does not meet the regulations, inspectors can shut down the entire building and prevent it from being re-opened until the offending item is taken down and re-rigged correctly, and yes, they can order you to tear down unsafe structures, even a temporary one for an event. If that item were to fall and injure someone, or start a fire, or damage property, then the organizers would be responsible for the consequences, even criminally. It has happened. If something in the building is dangerous and does not meet code, then the entire building can, indeed, be condemned, or at least be closed down until the problematic structure is torn down or fixed. So yes, inspectors actually do have that authority in some parts of the world, and your claim to the contrary is just plain wrong.
Do inspectors exist? Who are they?
If you are concerned, then call your local municipality, and ask! Let them know you think there might be a safety hazard in a building where you work, and ask them what should be done about it. Ask them where you can get a copy of the relevant regulations, and what they can do about it. Even ask them to send an inspector. If you think there is a fire hazard, then call your local fire department and ask them. In some places, the fire department can shut down a building if it is a major fire hazard. One major point: Since you rent space in tat building, you might even find that you would be just as liable as the owners of the building in the event of an accident! It would be a good idea to call around and find out as much as you can. - Stuart -
