knightfly7 September 2005#6
According to a report by Stanley Roller of USG, their findings show that optimum PCF for in-wall insulation for ALL frequencies is 2.5 PCF: this is why pretty much ALL the "SAFB" products (Sound Attenuating Fire Batt) are produced in this density. However, the way it works is that lighter PCF helps low end, heavier helps high end. Total difference, as long as you actually HAVE insulation in the wall, is only about 2 dB from what I've found so far; but with today's subwoofer culture, and for live drums, etc, it makes more sense to go LIGHTER in weight for improved LOW end isolation. In actual practice, standard household fiberglass (NOT FOAM BOARD) insulation performs as well as anything (in walls that is) and is lots cheaper than ANY of the higher density material. Overstuffing the cavities can be done, but NOT TOO MUCH or you will REDUCE isolation by increased coupling of the two sides - I would NOT put 6" batts in a standard 2x4 wall, if I said that anywhere I would have assumed that at the very LEAST there was RC involved, which increases the air space by about 1/2" - RSIC clips and channel increase the air gap on a 2x4 stud wall by more than that, and so slightly compressed 6" standard batts work fine for that. The purpose of "overstuffing" is to increase the damping factor on the wall panels - if you have LOTS of money, there is now a much better way to go. http://www.audioalloy.com/welcome.html One of their tech directors posts a LOT on studiotips.com, join and browse for more info. Also check their other pages at the above link for examples of damping on various materials. You still need insulation inside tho - GG isn't a replacement for that, just an excellent addition if you're really looking for MAXIMUM isolation... Steve (Adding GG between layers will add approx. $1 per square foot of wall)
