Corridor as Airspace in M-A-M

Started by JoeMilner on 28 August 2009. 10 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 13547.

JoeMilner28 August 2009#1
Hi all, It's been quite a long time since my original project in 2003, a film sound mixing stage in a former theater that I own here in Los Angeles. I got a lot of help from many Sayers forum members, especially Knightfly, John & Rod, and now the time has come to tear it down and build a bigger one! In working on some preliminary drawings I've come up against a problem. The mixing stage will be built in the main room of the theater, which is 100' long and 45' wide (I know, pretty big). The stage itself will be a little over 50' long, and positioned roughly in the middle, front-to-back. Because of this (and other reasons), there needs to be corridors on either side of the stage to get from the front of the building to the rear w/o travelling through the stage itself. So here's my question; I'm already losing 5 feet on either side of the stage for the corridors. So that's 10' of interior stage width lost right off the bat. To build a typical M-A-M double wall system with 2 or 3 layers of d-wall on each outer face ends up with a wall assembly that's around 16' thick, and that's without any interior acoustic treatment. What I'm getting at is that I'm eating up the interior width of the stage like crazy. The existing exterior walls of the theater are 3 rows of brick, common for construction here in 1930. If I were to use the exterior wall as a MASS leaf, the corridor as the (giant) airspace, and then the "inner half" of the double wall system as the inner MASS leaf, how would that perform? I imagine I'd need to add some sort of skin (maybe fabric & pegboard or slats?) to the "corridor side" of the inner wall to protect the wall & insulation but not actually add enough mass to create a 3-leaf system.....Basically I'd end up with a M-A-M system that's about 6' 8" from one side to the other. Or, do I just have to suck it up and build the M-A-M assembly normally, but unfortunately 5' in from the exterior wall? Sorry for the long post out of left field, and I promise to post pictures this time! In the meantime, let me see if I can get a pic up of the existing stage. Best, Joe

I've wondered about this for a while myself. Logic says that a corridor is great "A" to have in your "M-A-M" design. I've always thought that the ideal studio floor plan would incorporate service areas like corridors & bathrooms to contribute to aesthetics utility, and isolation in a holistic way. I'd like to see an "official" answer from the people who design studios for a living and write books about it too.
