Soundman20202 February 2015#2
Hi. Please read the forum rules for posting (click here). You seem to be missing a couple of things! :) But to answer your basic question:
is 800 sq ft. a good amount of space to build a control room and live room, ?
Yes, if designed well. In fact, you could conceivably get a vocal booth in there too, if you wanted to. ITU, EBU, AES and other specs call for a control room to have at least 250 ft2, 300 ft2, 350 ft2, or so in floor area, or more, depending on various factors. So assuming 300 for your CR, that leaves 500 for your LR. You can do a great LR in 500 ft2, assuming it has a decently high ceiling. Taking off 50 to 100 ft2 or so for a vocal booth, and you would still have enough space for a good LR. However, that assumes typical usage: you didn't say a whole lot about what else the studio might be used for, so the above might be applicable to you. If you want to track full symphony orchestras, then you are out of luck with such a small LR. If you wanted to do complete soundtracks for major movies, then ditto for the CR. But for recording and mixing a typical band with 5 to 10 members, then yes, that's a good sized space. - Stuart -

Soundman20205 February 2015#6
I hear what you are saying, but trying to do a high quality 1,600 ft facility in 30 days isn't realistic. I wish I could tell you something different, but that's just reality. The design process for a high quality studio the size you are talking about, would take at least that long (probably longer), even with a dedicated team of designers, and even assuming you could find a top quality designer who is able to start first thing tomorrow morning. There are so many factors that have to be considered in a studio design, and they they are all inter-related. Even a simple decision such as changing the model of console or main speakers to be used in the control room has a long series of ripple-on effects that flow down to hit multiple other things. Such as the size and position of the desk, location of the mix position, room geometry and therefore room acoustics, size and placement of windows, structural issues, HVAC, electrical, doors, decoration, and those in turn ripple on further down the line to affect the other rooms too. All of that takes time. Unless you know exactly what you want, in perfect detail, and are absolutely certain that nothing will change before the construction crew arrives, then there's little hope of even getting a place like yours designed in 30 days, and certainly not built. Building permits can take that long alone. You might or might not pass inspection at each stage. There might be delays in supplying materials that you need (suitable laminated acoustic glass for high isolation windows isn't usually available off the shelf, for example). Etc. You might well be able to get someone to hack together something basic in a couple of weeks, and perhaps build it in a couple of months, but from what you say, that's not what you want. You seem to be implying that your studio is a high quality operation, well financed, well known, your services are in demand, and therefore presumably you are looking to build a world-class studio. World-class studios are not designed and built in 30 days. Galaxy cost 13 million dollars and took nearly four years to design and build, for example. Good studios aren't built in a month. Even cheap low-quality back-street studios aren't designed and built in a month. If you really do want your design done fast, then I'd suggest that you contact John Sayers himself by PM immediately, and tell him about the great urgency of the project, to see if he might be able to do something for you. To get that kind of turn around, you'll probably have to fly him from Australia to North Carolina immediately and keep him there for as long as it takes, which won't be cheap, but you'd be guaranteed of the best possible design in the shortest possible time. John's studios really are world-class (which you already know, since you are here on the forum! :) ). But overall, I'd suggest that you might want to rethink your plan and put it on a more realistic basis. I'm not saying you can't have something jury-rigged within the empty shell inside 30 days, but it won't be a world-class studio, or even a high quality studio. Perhaps one option would be to throw together any old thing in one corner of the place, while you take the time needed to get the real studio designed and built properly. That way, you could at least be making some money off the temporary setup while the process goes on, (but by doing that you run the risk of damaging your reputation by having the low quality place initially). Anyway, that's a thought. - Stuart -
