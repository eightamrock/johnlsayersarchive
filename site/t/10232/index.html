awilki01 wrote:
Andre always suggests going with cheaper materials and filling up the entire space whereas Ethan tends to always mention the air gap.
Both approaches are good. Filling the space with insulation and using twice as much material absorbs bass a little better, but only a little. So if cost is not the issue for you, go ahead and fill the space. But not if that means you need to have fewer bass traps. In that case use half as much material with an air gap the same size, to have twice as many traps total. Is this clearer? --Ethan

Cojonesonasteek31 December 2008#17
We went with 6" of 703 and 6" of air gap behind the fiberglass with a grand total of 72 s.f. of absorber surface area at the back of the control room. The traps are mounted flush with the ceiling and in one case flush with the back corner (the studio's door is in the other corner so the other panel is offset). Clients have said the bass response of the room is much tighter than before the installation of the new traps. We still have some modal issues in the center of the room (where the producer's desk is) and will address those with tuned absorbers (the room is 24x16x11 so we have a good deal of volume to work with).
