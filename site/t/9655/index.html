Going above the garage?

Started by DrumEatDrum on 24 October 2007. 18 replies.

DrumEatDrum24 October 2007#1
Hello all! Wow, what a fantastic site. I wish I had found this years ago when I made my last studio (that only worked so well….) I am in the early stages of designing a new room. My story is my wife and I had a kid, and decided we should look to living in a better area. With a stroke of good luck, we stumbled upon a deal in a great area (Woodland Hills area of Los Angeles), on a cul-de-sac, with great near by schools. And while I was sad I would have to leave my beloved studio behind, I was looking forward to building a better one in my new garage. The garage itself is attached to the house via a hallway, but there are no shared walls with the house. I got thinking, it would be really nice to actually have a garage though, and I noted I had a front/side yard (since I’m on a cul-de-sac, the property is rounded in front, so what I think of the side of the house is the front, depending on where you are standing). So I determined rather than convert the garage, I would build off of it, and use this yard area that I don’t use (we have a big back yard). However, after meeting with numerous people and some research, it was determined this idea would not work. There is a small, but steep slope between this yard and neighbors house (drops about 5 feet over a 6 foot distance) and due to the roundness of the Cul-de-sac, keeping the proper distance from the property line is an issue. So getting a permit would be difficult, if not impossible, and I would have to spend $15,000 to $20,000 to have a special foundation, special footings, retaining walls, and soil work and maybe I could end up with a 14x10 room, which would be much smaller than the 17x20 room I left behind. :cry: A new proposal came up, and that was build a 2nd story above the garage. Permits for this should be easy (my other neighbors house is 2 stories), and oddly enough, construction costs would be $20,000 cheaper, and the end results would be a 20x20 room :D , much more to my liking. The room height I am unsure of right now, but there is a slight pitch to the roof now that will be matched in any new construction. The issue is can I effectively soundproof a 2nd story room? :?: The room’s purpose: Practice drums (and I have to say I play very loud) Low budget recordings on my Digi 002 Occasional full band practice I mainly like to practice in the evenings, after my son goes to bed. I am not thinking about any control room or iso booths at this time. Main concerns: Issue 1: Floors: I don’t have to worry so much about sound escaping through the floor and into the garage, but I do have to worry about sound escaping the garage itself. My main concern is even if I have the best walls, the floor could potentially undo any time and money spent of the walls From reading this forum, floating the floors is out. I will float a drum riser (I read some good threads about that on here). I can insulate under the floor and make have a finished ceiling in the garage portion. Is installing RC in the garage ceiling (i.e. under the studio) worth it? Issue 2: My original plan was have a contractor build the frame, exterior walls, basic floor and roof, and then sound proof later on my own. This would at least give me a temporary space to set up an electronic drum set, and not require as much money upfront. However, this creates problems into itself. Getting the city to sign off on the electrical won’t happen until it’s done, and I don’t want to have install some wiring, just to redo it later (since I would like to use surface mount wiring if possible). And the same really goes for any aspect of the room. So now I’m thinking I need to design the finished product now, and do it all at once, or else I could be throwing money down the drain on a room I’ll never get to be sound proof enough. Not to mention, there will be stairs, and lugging drywall up stairs myself doesn’t sound like fun. Issue 3: Windows. There is some talk the city could require them, and I admit the addition to the house would look more uniform (from the outside) if there were some windows. And to be honest, when I’m not actually physically playing the drums, it would be nice to open up the room, air it out, and have natural light if I’m just tinkering with something that isn’t making sound. But this just seems like a recipe for disaster for when I am on my drums. The windows would be double panned, and I could built baffles to cover them when, and seal those, but would just the thought of a window negate any benefits of doing a two-leaf wall discussed here: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4310 I’m starting to question if this is even going to work at all, and I’m feeling I am on the verge of sinking a lot of money into something useless. On the other hand, I can be in on building this thing from scratch. And take steps now to avoid problems later on. Other info: It gets well over 100 during the summer, so I will install an AC system. Rain is rare. The edge of the neighbor’s house is approx 20 feet away, albeit down hill. I've looked at the various threads on studios being built, and I didn't see any desigs that are on a 2nd floor like this, unless there were in an industrial concreate building. Budget: The construction is alreay an arm and a leg, but I am going to budget $10,000 to the sound proofing process on top of the cost of building the frame, exterior walls, and roof. So the main questions: Is this ever going to work doing this as a 2nd floor? Is there anything I should have done in the initial framing and building of the 2nd story that will save me headaches later on? Will I end up having to sound proof two rooms (the studio and the garage under it) in order to prevent my neighbors from calling the Police? . Any insight is greatly appreciated. I did give a contractor a small deposit to start working on plans for the framing, but it's not too late to stop him.
jwl25 October 2007#2
I think it will be a challenge to get good soundproofing on the second floor, while maintaining structural integrity. You will definitely want to consult a structural engineer on this. Soundproofing requires mass (among other things), and the issue will be, can the second floor safely hold that mass? Do you have Rod's book yet? Also, keep reading around this site. I think it can be done, but you will definitely have to take care of the structural end of things.

above the garage has several things going for it, one of which is the garage can act to trap bass from the room above. however if you have sound levels high enough, they will leak out of the garage. so you may have to put up something under the floor to prevent the sound from escaping or fix the entire garage. this is where testing your existing construction is important so you have an idea about how much sound is getting out or in, and where its getting out or in. with drums though - check Sharward's thread on this as it can get expensive very quickly with fines and all so improving the iisolation seems like its important. since you have electronic drums, maybe thats the better option for practice or rehearsals.

Thank you!!!!! :D I did notice that no one ever answered any questions about floors. :( I'm more and more annoyed with Rod's book that he says there are solutions that he has used in hotesl, but doesn't explain them. It's an amazing huge over sight by the editor. And the more I read, the more I think the editor didn't understand the concepts, as I've found a few places that just beg for more explanations. As this point, my concept is using: Aurlex Platfoam for the drum riser (perhaps even two layers) QuiteWood or Aurlex Sheetbock, or similar product on the floor, Insulate the rafters between the floor and garage ceiling, and then hang Quiterock on the ceiling of the garage. This of course adds up to be very expensive, and I'm still not sure how much sound will still escape through the floor joists out the side of the building, but I've yet to read a better idea so far.

I think the answer from Rod's book may be to have enough structural support to have a sand filled floor or a concrete floor. Most floors can't support that weight. I know mine can't. It would also help to structurally separate the ceiling below, so it is not attached to the floor. I may build my room with a mixture of double walls and drywall on RC. Then I may leave the floor alone and invite a drummer over and do some testing. If I need more isolation at that point, I will use Green Glue (including on the floor) and add more drywall. If that's still not enough, I can rip out the drywall ceiling below my studio floor and decouple it from the room above.
