I would use that space for recording and try to get another place for mixing.. those kind of spaces usually have very good acoustics for recording (one thing you have to do is to change those tiles in the floor :D I personally think that this room is too live and you are kind of going to destroy its beauty and its acoustics. 
would I be crazy to try to work in a real live room like this?
I would say yes and no. You may try to place a few absorbers although I feel you will need quite a bit in order to get the reverberation time into adequate values for critical listening in a room like that. I understand your idea of mixing at low volumes in order not to excite the reverberation field BUT in cases like your room the critical distance is usually quite short. So making the walls near your speakers absorptive is advantageous. The bonus of having such room if it was accurately treated is the size and shape is beneficial for room modes. Altough for prediction matter I usually prefer to have a room with rectangular shapes
