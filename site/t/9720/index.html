rod gervais7 November 2007#2
Rune, I would not place the drywall directly against the concrete - but would rather bring it to the HT side of the wall - keep the framing at least one inch free of the masonry. The above approach is a good one for the floor above - but don't leave a space between the drywall and the decking - install it tightly against the existing. Rod

Rune. I knew exactly whatI was looking at - and still say that you do not want to place drywall adjacent to the concrete as illustraed - add it to the inside if you need more isolation. If you can't do it in your ceiling - then doing it in your wall probably wouldn't make sense - the ceiling / floor then becomes your weak point - and that is the value of isolation - your weakest points shall prevail. Add resilient channel to the bottom of the floor joist to isolate the drywall from the floor above. Sincerely, Rod
