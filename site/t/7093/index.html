mpedrummer19 September 2006#2
I believe that your rock-wall (plaster over the rock, or drywall?) would certainly be an excellent first leaf. Leave a gap and frame a new wall in front of it. For the hollow wall with the door, I think your best bet is to remove the existing drywall on the inside of the room, then frame a new wall in front of that. Insulate both leaves, but leave an air gap between the insulation. From my understanding, the insulation is more to dampen resonances in the drywall than to actually insulate, especially from a sound point of view. To that effect, the rockwool/703 has been established as "best" and the fluffy pink stuff has been described as "a big pain to deal with". The pink stuff will compress more easily, which reduces its effectiveness as a damper, and the paper backing will need to be removed. Apparently, the paper itself can act as a third leaf, and weaken the STC of the wall somewhat. Oh, and I want to make a disclaimer - my advice above comes from what I've learned reading this forum, not actual construction experience as many others bring to the table. You may want to wait till someone confirms or refutes my advice before acting on it. MPEDrummer

You may be able to use the "chiller room" method for cooling, but I don't think you can use that method for fresh air supply. All of the code language I have seen on mechanical fresh air ventilation (required when you have no operable windows!) is that the air must come directly from the outdoors without another room acting as a "middle man." :roll: Here is an example of one city's paraphrased interpretation of mechanical ventilation requirements for habitable space, based on their adoption of 2003 International Residential Code. Your municipality may vary. Void where prohibited. Past performance not indicative of future results. One at this price. No warranties expressed or implied. ;-) (As an example, my city's model codes require about 50% more fresh air capacity than Boise's does.) Consider an energy recovery ventilator (ERC), such as the Fantech SE704N, for your fresh air requirements. You can equip it with a wall control. --Keith :mrgreen:

Shar, boy did someone really screw up when they read that one and interpereted it.. That is not what it says at all. The reference that states: "Natural Ventilation shall be through windows, doors or louvers or other approved openings to the outdoor air" only applies to a direct connection to the outdoor air when the method of obtaining air is taken directly through the room wall AND the fresh air supply is a passive system - not a mechanical system (which is why a minimum net free opening (as a percentage of the room area) is required. Exception 1 clearly allows for other methods of obtaining fresh air within a room that does not have to do with a direct connection to the outside from within that room space. It is commom practice to provide plenums within rooms and above ceilings for the purpose of moving fresh air throughout buildings. They specifically allow for mechanical systems that don't meet the general requirements noted above - one of which is a direct connection from that space to the outside world. The "Adjoining Rooms" section only has to do with providing air (and light) from an adjoining room without the use of mechanical means - at which point they proscribe a minimum net free opening and minimum room size for connection to the adjoining room. Sorry my friend - I would have challenged them on that one and won - they really did screw it up. Using an adjacent room as a fresh air plenum is legit as long as you can maintain the minimum requirements set for that fresh air........ The only room you could not pull fresh air from would be a bathroom - or any other space where the air is considered contaminated and an exhaust system is specifically required for the air in the space.
