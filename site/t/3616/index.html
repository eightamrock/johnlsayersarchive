knightfly22 April 2005#2
Hi Rob, welcome; here's the basic concept, explained fairly basically - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download.php?id=3651 Your brick walls may be able to be rendered both sides for a higher mass outer leaf - then you would need at least 300mm or more insulated air gap and an inner mass leaf, such as double MDF or triple 15mm gypsum wallboard on a frame - for heavy percussion 24 hours a day, a HEAVY floated floor is mandatory - something like 150mm concrete floated on 50mm elastomer blocks, or on "jack up" spring units. Your inner walls should sit on top of this inner, floated floor, and a new ceiling frame (with also triple layer of 15mm gypsum) supported on those inner, floated walls. Ventilation will need to be included, there are threads here regarding that as well. This is a reachable goal, but won't be cheap - if you HIRE it done, you will still need to learn as much here as possible or your contractor will very likely mess it up BADLY. The DIY approach takes longer, but will cost quite a bit less. If you can post either pictures or a sketch of your space, it would probably help; also, more info on your surrounding neighborhood. Re-reading the "don't even think about" announcement will give you an idea of each point needing to be covered. Also, don't worry about your English; it sure beats the crap outa my Dutch or Frisian... Steve

knightfly22 April 2005#6
Not sure of exact values, but even placed in the center of your large room the "3-leaf" effect wouldn't be entirely gone; best way to see if this works would be (since you already HAVE this box) set it up in the center of your larger space, close all the doors and windows, put someone inside beating the crap out of your loudest toys, and measure/listen outside with the outer doors closed and open; if your lowest frequency stuff is quieter outside with the outer door SHUT, you win: Be aware that this may NOT get you better iso at lower frequencies, so this is a TEST; it may or may not work. Frankly, I don't know which way will be quieter (although the extra leaf WILL help at mids and highs) ... Steve

knightfly10 May 2005#16
This one, definitely - preferable to construct a very heavy one mass wall on a floating slab... then have an airspace to the outside wall and maybe put some extra mass to those outside walls Even a single thickness of outer siding on a garage, when followed by an insulated, WIDE air gap and a heavier inner mass, is far superior to ANY triple or quadruple leaf construction for low frequency isolation - and since this is the most difficult to isolate, the LF should be the deciding factor in construction... Steve
