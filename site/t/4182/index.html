len-morgan9 August 2005#1
I'm going to be building a new studio, I've got a building for it, but I'd like some advice as to the layout. Goals: 1) A GREAT control room 2) A good tracking room that ideally could fit a small band. A drum booth in the tracking room would be nice, but it could also be separate. 3) At least one, preferably 2 vocal booths. 4) A reception/office area with enough room for a filing cabinet or two, a computer desk, regular desk, etc. When I'm not recording here, I'll be "working." I write software for a living and my "office" doesn't need to handle visitors or clients ( I work out of my house now) but it could be set up as a lounge area/office combination (i.e., dual use). As I said, I have just purchased a building to put this in. I've attached an SU file showing the basic layout. I haven't been able to figure out all the bells and whistles of SketchUp yet so it's very basic. By way of explanation, the windows along the front of the building need to stay (for building resale purposes) but they don't have to be "functional" as long as I have a studio there. There are no neighbors any where near by so noise OUT of the studio won't be a problem. The streets (it's on a corner) are fairly quiet. The basic construction is cement filled cinder block, then a couple of inches of foam insulation, then a red brick facade on the outside. This goes all the way around the building except for the glass front. The front is 5 49" x 58" windows. My available ceiling height is 8' 5" or so. The current ceiling is drop down accoustic tile w/ flourecent (sp?) light fixtures (which I know have to go) with 3 1/2" of insulation laid on top of them. The roof is supported on wooden trusses and is gravel-tar-tar paper-tounge and groove pine. The big "problem" I have with this space right now it's the pilars that run almost down the center. There's about 15' on the door side and the balance on the other side. I have been assured that if it's necessary, I can add additional beams and remove the pilars without the whole thing falling down. Of course, it would be great if I could just leave them up but I also know that that would cut into possible design options (I'd really rather not have to straddle a pole while I'm mixing!!). I think that covers the details I can't show in the drawing. I've got a couple of thousand to spend if needed but anything I don't have to spend on walls and sound proofing I can spend on equipment. I can always get more equipment later so I'd rather make sure I have a well designed/constructed studio rather than a new mic or preamp. Thanks in advance for any layout suggestions. Len Morgan
