I have now had a decent carpet underlay and carpet put down to help with footfall, and the last thing to do is try to isolate the unit holding my monitors from the floor as much as possible. The aim will be to reduce the amount of sound that will be heard in the living room below. I'm also hoping it will help reduce the sound reaching our neighbour. The party wall with the neighbours is actually a couple of rooms away. But it would seem that the way the houses were built, flanking is a bit of an issue. When the neighbours move their chairs in their tiled kitchen, the sound is really loud in any part of our house - including the music room...... Here's a basic diagram of the desk:

They will be mounted on MoPads....most probably in a position where they will be slanted pointing downward. I intend to follow advice from another thread and add more weight to the top of the monitors to make the MoPads compress more and be more effective, although I'm not certain if this is so important if I'm slanting them - seeing as there will be less foam to compress. I've marked in red the areas where the unit meets the floor and where it meets the long horizontal piece of MDF which holds the equipment. Soundman20207 August 2010#4
I guess the biggest question here is: how loud are you? At what level are you playing those monitors, normally? The next question would be details on the construction of the house itself. I'm suspecting that the issue probably isn't related much to your desk, or the way it sits on the floor (although it might be), but rather to the way the house is built, and to all the flanking that is already going on. It doesn't matter how well you isolate your monitors from the desk, and desk from the floor, if it turns out that the real issue is simply airborne sound making the room walls/floor/ceiling vibrate, and the structure of the building is transmitting that to the rest of the house. To get a rough idea, how about if you stack up several thick folded bath towels under each monitor, getting you several inches of isolation, and see if that makes any difference at all in the level that you hear in the rest of the house. If your towels are soft and fluffy enough then they should do a reasonable job of decoupling the speakers from the desk. If that makes a noticeable difference, then it is worth exploring the decoupling idea more, but if you can't really hear any difference "downstairs" with and without the towels (playing the same song at the same level, of course), they decoupling isn't your problem. - Stuart -
