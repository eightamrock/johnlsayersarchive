First Concerns/Questions: As I mentioned, I have had some time to think about my plans, and realized I could rotate the control room area. There are positive and negative aspects to either orientation. I have stated below the positive and negative aspects as I see them. I am hoping to get some advice from those more experienced as to which is the better approach. Thank you in advance for you assistance. Facing North and South Positive - Yields an air lock - Provides a storage area for mics, cords etc.. - Uses the exact ratio of the diagrams from John Sayers sample set. Thus insuring good room ratio. Negative - The room is not very deep (13ft 8in), limiting space for customers or other equipment - Front window into the large (phase II) recording room can only be 4ft 8in wide. Facing East and West Positive - Provides a deeper control room (15ft 10in) - The window facing the large (phase II) recording room is 6ft wide Negative - The room is 1 foot thinner than the “facing north” option - The room is not the exact same ratio as the John Sayer sample. It is stretched out due to the additional two feet of depth. - No air lock, but can have double doors to the large (phase II) studio What is the best orientation? It seems to me that using the exact same ratio as John’s proven samples would yield a good sounding room, but the extra depth of the “east-west” option provides more room for customers, and maybe space for tracking in the control room. I welcome all of your comments. Any encouragement is also appreciated, as I am very nervous about starting this project. I don’t want mess it up. Thank you. Joe

gullfo5 December 2007#16
in this case, i'd plan on building it flat, box in the ducts with soffits, and use clouds to treat the ceiling. it will be fine, many good studios use that tactic, and in some cases the clouds are big enough that it seems like a ceiling.... use RC-2 or iso clips and HC on the ceiling to isolate it as well as (if structure can support it - check with a structural engineer) beef up the underside of the above floor with drywall layers to further increase isolation through mass.
