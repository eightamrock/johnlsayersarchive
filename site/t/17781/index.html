Hi Brian, and welcome to the forum! :) Sorry about the delay in respond: lots of stuff going on at present! So just a few brief comments on your thread:
and a 1meter ferro concrete cube (with small iron door)
What is that thing?? Do you need access to it once the studio is finished, or can it be covered over?
and the speakers will be placed into the ”setback”, which has different length side walls.
That's not a good idea at all. Symmetry is very important for an accurate stereo image, good sound-stage, and identical response on left and right speakers. With that layout, you'd also have first reflection issues, diffraction on wall edges, no space for treatment, etc. I think your best option is to rotate the room layout 90° to the right, so you are facing the "cube", and then work out some way of fixing up that front wall so it is basically a single flat surface. Maybe a simple wood frame with some drywall on it. That still won't be ideal for symmetry, but will be better than the "setback" alcove idea.
Would be useful to extend the left shorter side wall to will be to equal length (1,48m) of the right side wall? What do you think?
Yes, but with the new layout rotate 90°: Extend the left wall back a bit there, to improve symmetry in the front half of the room.
So what do you think, the 2,6m should work well or little lower/higher?
I don't have time to work out the best height right now, but if you rotate the room as I outlined above then I think it will be close enough to rectangular that you can use any good room mode calculator to figure out roughly what would work. It won't be accurate, of course, but will get you to a reasonable solution.
Other important question is the acoustic Treatment.
I would go with the "standard" treatment: diagonal bass traps across the front corners, treatment on first reflection points and also between the speakers and the front wall, a cloud overhead, thick absorption across the entire rear wall, spaced away from the wall, etc. That would be my basic plan. - Stuart -
