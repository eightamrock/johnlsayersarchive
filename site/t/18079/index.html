Soundman202024 January 2013#10
In the day i have some problems, for example the wall that divide me from neighbours, i don't know how to make a good wall that give me a good isolation in this side with the existing wall!
For low frequencies, unfortunately isolating just one wall very well does not work: The sound will get out through the other walls, doors, windows, HVAC system, electrical system, etc., and once it is out, it will spread in all directions, including to the other side of the "good" wall. So isolating a studio means that you have to isolate all sides to the same level, not just one or some.
What do you think about this sketchup? In the north side there are 3 layers of drywall 1.6 cm and 25 cm of air gup! (I don't think that this can give me a lot of TL)
Actually, that CAN give you very good isolation... of there is a layer of something massive and solid on the other side of the air gap, and nothing "bridging" across the air gap. So if you have a brick or concrete wall on the other side of the air gap, then that's great! But you need to have the same around all sides of the room, if you want good isolation in low frequencies.
I don't know exactly how much TL i need but i think that 60db is good for me and this is not easy because i need also a good isolation of the low frequencies
60dB TL is very good! If you can get that much, then you will have excellent isolation.
Yes, this is like Mass Loaded Vinyl and here they call it ecological lead but no lead at all! I just thought that this "lead" might be good for isolation because i saw a lot of people (or auralex 101) that use this material with good results. Why???
Perhaps because of good marketing? :) Seriously, the issue is NOT that MLV doesn't work: it DOES work. The problem is that it is very expensive, and the sound waves really don't care about price. It does work, and does have its place in acoustics, but only in situations where the cost can be justified, and there are no alternatives. I have used MLV myself, to isolate a noisy drain pipe above a home theater, since it was the only thing with enough mass that I could bend around the pipe into the shape I needed, and then seal air-tight, in order to isolate the pipe. There was no other way that I could have done that, so the high cost was justified. But just using MLV as a layer of extra mass inside a wall makes no sense, due to the price. You get much more benefit, at much ,ower cost, from an extra layer of drywall. Compare the numbers: A single layer of 16mm drywall has nearly three times as much mass as a layer of 5kg/m2 MLV, but the drywall costs much less. So unless you have lots of money that you want to throw away ( :!: :D ) or really have a situation where there is no alternative, then you don't need MLV.
Maybe because it save space! I have heard that help a lot for the vibrations at low frequencies.
It can, yes... when used as a proper limp membrane! But that's not the way you propose to use it in your wall... :)
What do you think about 6 layers of 1.6 drywall???
Waaaayy too much! I mean, if you want to do that, then you sure can, and the results will be good, but that is "overkill". Like trying to swat flies with a shotgun! What you really need for good isolation, is a "room in a room". That simply means that you have your existing room, with the walls and ceiling around it, and you build a new room inside it, a few cm away from the walls and ceiling. So you build a simple wood frame that does not touch the existing walls or ceiling, and you put a layer of OSB on once side of that frame, then one or two layers of 16mm drywall on top of the OSB. That's all. If you do that right, and seal it all air-tight, then you can indeed get very good isolation.
What do you think about this sketchup?
Yes, that's the idea! What you are proposing in your new SketchUp model is basically correct, and all you need to do is to use 16mm OSB or plywood as the first layer on the studs, forget about the MLV, then one or two layers of 16mm drywall on top of that. And if you really want a good improvement in isolation, then spend the extra money on Green Glue (instead of MLV), and put a layer of that between the layers of drywall. - Stuart -

Soundman202019 June 2013#23
The problem is not the volume of air inside the soffits: that doesn't make much difference at all, since the interior of the soffits are acoustically not really part of the room. The problem is symmetry. Like Johnny said, room symmetry is very, very important. If your room is not symmetrical, then your ears will be hearing different things on the left and right: different equalization, different timing, different phasing, different reflections. And you will subconsciously try to "fix" that in your mix, so it sounds good in the room... but sounds bad everywhere else. As Johnnie said, you cannot fix the symmetry problem with slot walls: the depth behind the slots on the left and on the right is different, so they are tuned differently, and the equalization will be different. So that is not correcting the symmetry problem, and in fact it is making it even worse. This is an issue with the hard boundary of the room: the inner leaf walls. So what you show as "A" is the correct way to do it. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 July 2013#25
1) i would like to flush mount the adam a77x, they told me that i can flush mount these speakers (of course if well build) BUT in horizontal position because in vertical position i might have problems with stereo image. So, ok for the horizontal position! What do you think?
It makes sense. As far as I know, that's the best orientation for the A77X anyway. I don't think it would be good to mount it vertically, if is designed for horizontal. In fact, Adam makes an "A" and "B" version of these monitors, with the "B" being a mirror image of the "A". So you should buy one of each, with the "A" on the left and the "B" on the right, to ensure a proper sound stage and stereo image. However, that will make your soffits very wide. The A77X is 53 cm wide, and since you need a soffit at least three times as wide as your speaker (ideally), each soffit would have to be 1.6 m wide. That simply will not fit in your room. So you REALLY need such a large speaker for such a small room? I would suggest that an A7X or A8X would be more than enough. With an A7X, your soffits would only need to be about 60 cm wide, and about 80 for the A8X. I have done soffits for the A7X at 70 cm wide, and that seems to work fine.
2) Soffits: do i need to seal them? I mean the cavity behind the speakers (in red below)
You don't really need to seal the two bits at the top, between the soffits, but you do need to seal the ones on the sides, especially if you are going have slot walls on the side angled sections. - Stuart -
