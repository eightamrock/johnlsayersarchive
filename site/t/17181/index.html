Soundman20205 November 2011#2
Hi "silverbeetle", and welcome! You don't seem to have very much treatment in that room at all! That seems to be the basic problem. It is mostly bare walls, doors and windows.
I assume I'll need to make acoustic panels to catch the first reflection points and superchunk bass traps in the corners.
Exactly! That would certainly be the first thing to do. I'd also go for absorption in front of the window (behind the desk and speakers), and on the door.
With this room however I think the superchunks will only be possible in the front corners due to the cupboards spanning the back wall.
What about the other ten corners? :) There are 12 corners in a room: people tend to only think of the 4 obvious vertical ones, but there are 8 others...
Please note that due to being a rental property I won’t be able to treat the ceiling and the treatment will need to be freestanding.
If you absolutely cannot get anything up there, not even supported on stands at the sides of the room, then your first reflections from up there are going to be a major problem. You can put a very thick rug on the floor to help with the flutter echo in the vertical plane, but that won't help the very large issue of first reflections form the ceiling. Why can't you put a couple of screws into the ceiling, to hang a piece of 703? If you leave, all you need to do is take out the screws, fill the holes and re-touch with a dab of paint. Worst case, you paint the entire ceiling. No big deal. Couple of hours with a roller, and you are done.
Can anyone give me some guidance regarding how many panels I would need, their placement ...
For your first reflection points, make large panels of 4" 703 (or similar) that you can just lean against the walls, worst case, or build gobo-type free-standing units that you can space a couple of inches away from the walls, for maximum effect. One panel on each side wall, two on the front wall (behind the speakers) and maybe a third in the middle, in front of the window. Plus the cloud, if you can figure a way of getting it up there.
and is there any suggestions for bass trapping in the back corners considering the layout described above?
There seems to be plenty of room ABOVE the closet: put a large superchunk horizontally in that wall-ceiling corner, across the entire width of the room.
As far as materials for the acoustic panels I’m thinking of using Acoustisorb 3 (100mm thick):
Compared to 703, that isn't very good: It's not bad, but 703 would be better for bass trapping, if you can get it. That "Acoustisorb" stuff seems to have peak absorption in the mids and a steep roll-off into the mow-mids, where 703 has better absorption. You can use that if you can't get 703, for sure, but the performance won't be the same. Perhaps you could make your devices thicker, to compensate.
For the bass traps, does 600mm x 600mm on the equal sides of the triangle sound like an adequate size?
Yes, that's fine. Bigger if you can, but that's already a good size. You room actually has pretty good dimensions: the ratio is very close to Sepmeyer 1. The graphs are very useful, and seem to reveal some modal issues. You can see clearly how that changes when you close the doors: the 50 Hz problem moves up to 60 Hz, since the room is now shorter! In fact, you can see that many of the modal issues slide up the scale with the doors closed, indicating that they are related to the longitudinal axes of the room. With the doors closed, you also have new stuff going on in the 100-150 Hz region, too: Hence, the recommendation for absorption on the front window, and above the closet. I'd start with your basic plan of absorption in the first reflection points, super chunk on the rear wall above the door, and in the front corners too, if you can, plus absorption in front of the window, then measure again with REW to see what changed. Make sure that you have your measurement mic and the speakers in teh exact same location for the second measurement. - Stuart -

I've been doing a bit of browsing of the forums to find out more about clouds and came across this comment from you in another post: Quote: Make it thick absorption, and hard-backed, then angle it at least 12° (maybe more, as needed), and spaced away from the ceiling. Most of that is applicable, but you might not need the hard backing: that was for a specific case of a vertical modal issue. If you don't have that, you don't need it.
Am I correct in saying it should be positioned over my head in the mix position?
It should be at the first reflection point on the ceiling. Get someone to hold a mirror up against the ceiling and move it around, while you are seated at the mix position: any place where you can see the speakers reflected in the mirror is a first reflection point and needs treatment. Normally, that will be right over the desk, not over your head, so the light might still survive! :)
It's probably a very long shot but maybe several 3kg 3M removable hooks maybe be able to pull this off
:shock: Ummmm.... :!: I wouldn't do that, no. If your cloud has a hard back, it will be HEAVY, and even without one it won't exactly be featherweight... To find out what kind of mountings you need, hold a few 1x6 planks and a large bag of mineral wool about 1.5 meters above your chair, and allow them to fall on your head: The amount of pain you feel is directly proportional to the quality and strength of mounting you will need! :) :!: :lol: :roll: :shot: (OK, just kidding about that! The point is, you need solid, safe mounts.)
As for insulation choice for my panels and bass traps I’m feeling very overwhelmed/confused by the data and information flying around.
Ordinary pink fluffy fiberglass is fine: look for stuff that weighs about 30 kg per cubic meter, more or less. Mineral wool is fine too: look for stuff that weighs about 50 kg per cubic meter, more or less.
