Blue, In the traditional explenations in groups you will notice that angling walls is NOT mainly related to avoiding standing waves, since you have standing waves in ANY room of whatever shape, no matter how assymetric. There is a sticky topic here somewhere about standing waves and related animations. The angling relates to the distribution of the sound in the room, reflection free zone applications, bot mostly the traditional minimum 6° to better 12° per side is related to avoiding flutter, not standing waves. This 6° to 12° is an empirical value. If you should see the math of flutter, recognized in real live by anyone id and when it occurs, you'll notice it's a really complicated business. A lot of this relates to geometric acoustics, while small rooms are mostly dominated by modal acoustics. Now flutter is also modal, but with very short pulses in function of room sizes, not allowing standing waves to establish. But there is more to it. One can have flutter between more than 2 walls. The traditional description of a repetitive echo between parallel opposing walls is a bit too simple. Hence you maybe start feeling that you need to 1) either get a feel and insight for these concepts needing to study them, or 2) that you just have to accept the experience and combined knowledge from people in groups, who combined build lots of studios already proving/confirming the value of the followed approach. You will not find the simple formula you're looking for. If it was it was integrated in simple calculators already. Take notice that ALL these calculators are stylized approaches based on lots of theoretical boundary conditions. If you want to know you have to study it, systematically, and you will notice, as with everything else, the more you learn the more additional questions arise. Really simulating a sound field in a room calls for very complicated simulation software, mostly based on a numerical approach, not on simple analytic formulas.
