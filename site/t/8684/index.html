lovecow24 April 2007#2
No one's going to be able to tell you for sure unless there's lab-measured data available for something very similar to what you've built. Even then, it might only be meaningful if the "before" and "after" airspace numbers are available to compare (assuming numbers on the exact absorber you've built are not readily available). In general, it would appear that a 1.5x to 2x increase in absorption in the 125 and 250 Hz bands when you add an airspace of equal depth to the thickness of the panel is not uncommon. Some data I've found on 1" mineral wool panels jives with this. I wouldn't expect drastically different results with a 2" material. This is very rough, but should help. Good luck!
drfrankencopter25 April 2007#3
It's diminishing returns...yes you get better performance by spacing the insulation from the wall, but you get even better performance from stuffing the full space with insulation. For example,:
    1" thick 703 spaced at 0" has an absorbtion coef of .18 at 250 Hz 1 inch thick 703 spaced at 1" has an absorbtion coefficient of 0.25 at 250 hz 2 inch thick 703 spaced at 0" has an absorbtion coefficient of 0.8 at 250 Hz
If you can afford the extra insulation, the absorbtion coefficients are way better for a full stuffing, considering that they take up the same amount of room volume. 4" of insulation will give good absorbtion to about 100Hz but falls off sharply after that. At 100Hz or so, you should be looking at bass traps, like resonators/panel traps. Cheers Kris
