SharpNine16 April 2008#10
The ability to allow ductwork to run directly up into and between joist spaces rather than having to pass underneath the joist is a HUGE benefit for us basement dwellers. Like you mentioned, it would eliminate the need for a big bulky chase soffit. Kinda odd why they ran them like that in the first place. Definitely looks a little shoddy. I know in a perfect world you wouldn’t want duct connections at that juncture, or maybe they did that so the ducts could attach to standard top take-off connections on the main trunk. If you put them were you've shown it looks like you’ll have to use a normal take-off in order to extend them above the trunk up into the joist space before connecting a 90 degree elbow (which you have a few!) as it appears the trunk is lower than where they are currently. I’m definitely no expert, but I doubt that the ductwork was so perfectly planned out and balanced when it was installed that your proposed modifications would make a huge difference in pressure. To me, the fact that your re-route would actually lose a couple bends and hard turns in the duct would actually benefit pressure, possibly negating any concerns on the new locations in regards to the other branch of the trunk. If it were me, I would absolutely do it.
