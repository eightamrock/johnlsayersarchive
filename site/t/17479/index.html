Nope! :) Looks like you did your homework, and your first post was great! :D OK, so to clarify, this is a multi-purpose room that has to work as both a live room sometimes, a control room other times, and living room the rest of the time. Is that about right? To answer your basic question: Yes, for something like that, your best bet is probably moveable panels, perhaps in the form of gobos on wheels that you can push around as needed for tracking / mixing, then push out into the garage or somewhere when the room is just a living room. Of course, this is not an ideal solution, and will not turn your living room into Abbey Road, but if that's what you can do, then that's what you can do. So probably you should think of building a bunch of large, thick gobos on wheels, as tall as you can make them. There are many designs for gobos on the forum, but I think for your case, I'd make the hard side very solid and massive, maybe thick plywood, MDF or OSB, and I'd make the soft side very thick absorption, with an air gap between that and the hard side, as deep as you can afford. You could cover the whole thing (both sides, if you want) with some type of cloth, to make it look good, then arrange them as needed for tracking, and re-arrange them for mixing. The arrangement for tracking is more of a "trail and error" approach, probably, but for mixing I'd use several such gobos to create a symmetrical space around the speakers and desk, with the soft sides of the gobos all facing the mix position, and positioned at least at your first reflection points, with a couple behind you, as far back as you can get them. Symmetry is the key, and first reflection treatment is the second key, get those two right, and you can build yourself a fairly workable space, I reckon. The room isn't very big, so you don't have a lot of choices, but probably for mixing I'd set up a couple of gobos in a "V" shape at the thin end of the room, using the 1.75m wall as the front of the room, then put a couple on the sides, one in the middle at the rear on the 3.66m wall, and two diagonally across the corners on that same rear wall, as sort of improvised bass traps.
The cupboard in the corner is actually quite deep and tall inside so im not sure if this could be useful at all -
That might be a problem! It will act as some sort of resonant device, without a doubt, but the question is, what will it do? :shock: Your idea of filling it with absorption is probably the best bet. You might even be able to get some small variation in the room acoustics by leaving the door open or closed, but that depends on how big the closet is. But worth a try!
The whole room is carpeted.
Any chance you could get rid of that? Carpet is not very useful, acoustically: it does the opposite of what you need! So it can actually harm the room sound, making it dull and "honky". So one option could be to take it out. Another might be to lay sheets of thick plywood on top whenever you need to use the room for critical work, then take them out again when you are done.
I am able to get Rockwool 'acoustic slabs'
That should be fine. Get the thick stuff (67mm) and use a double layer in some of your gobos (134 mm thick), and a single layer in some others, so you have some flexibility. Or just make them all double-thick! In a room that small, absorption is your friend. What you could do is to put plastic across the front (soft side) of some of the gobos, just behind the cloth, to make them more selective: that way they'll absorb bass just the same, but reflect the highs back into the room, so it doesn't become too dead. That would be a good basic approach on a tight budget, where isolation is no needed and nothing can be left in place. And like Aaron said: Photos! Post some photos of the room, so we can get a better idea of what you are dealing with. - Stuart -
