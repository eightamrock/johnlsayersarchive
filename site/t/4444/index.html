I'd say keep the window. Real light is nice sometimes, hang a curtain and a wooden blind over it, put a sofa in the middle below it. Have the main set-up facing the window and have strorage area's or booth's either side with smaller windows, again with curtains and blinds. You could even have a synth room, vocal room, drum room or small kitchen in the rest of the space, but i can't imagine it unless i see it from a photo. Normally on this forum people suggest the angled box type shape because of the standard control room ethic, but there's no reason not to have an elongated design as if you build new rooms with more walls you'll loose about 20% of the space. Unless someone want's to come up with some reasons, then you can always segment it with portable things like heavy curtains, thick foam, or specially made abosorbing blinds, then u have flexibility.

What you are saying is OK if your goal is to make a dead room, but usually the goal with a control room is to minimize reflections, especially at the mixing position, which is why the "angled box type shapes" are normally suggested - because they work. Mixing in a totally dead room is not a pleasant experience for most people (I happen to have one of those rooms, hence my desire to change it to something else). That being said, there is a lot of merit to an elongated design, especially with the length that Sarath has. Check out this article by Ethan Winer for a simple studio concept that is basically an elongated design. Size-wise, it is pretty close to Sarath's space and the design principles should be valid. http://www.realtraps.com/art_studio.htm Darryl.....

DDev4 October 2005#13
Basically what you are trying to do is to have the side wall splay be at 6 degrees from either side. So, in your room, if you go to the top/short wall and measure in roughly 18 inches from each side, then go down the long wall about 13.5 feet you'll have your 6 degrees. Of course this leaves about 6 feet at the back end that is outside the splay that will have to be dealt with, but they are not as critical as the front section where you will be while mixing. I would put some corner bass traps in the bottom corners and make the back wall absorbent with 4 inches of rockwool or rigid fiberglass for a starter. If you want to flush mount the speakers, then you will have to add some soffits at the top end, similar to what I had drawn in my original sketch. There are tons of soffit design ideas elsewhere on this site that can give you a better idea of how to actually construct them (I just sketched out a location). I hope this makes sense. Darryl.....

"Flush mounted" is usually referred to around here as Soffit Mounted speakers, which are mounted inside a wall or partition section such that the front surface of the speaker is flush with the face of the wall.
