Hello there, I've been looking through the site for many months now and finally decided to sign up. I read the book (Recording Studio Design and Construction) and have come away with one question on what to do w/ my new place... I have purchased a general store building from the late 1800's that was not well taken care of. After a few years of renovation, I have made the front part of the building with the original tin ceilings into a live / work space (this has a crawl space underneath it). In the back there are two different areas that I am building the studio in. They consists of two 20 X 20 foot rooms (approx 400 sq feet and both have 11.5 foot ceiling) that are separated by a brick wall. One of the areas rests upon the 20 X 20 basement area which contains my furnace, hot water heater, plumbing access, etc. The is reached via a trap door in the floor. With this being said, I have concerns with pouring concrete or using sand in this area for two reasons: 1. I have had two contractors (one I trust with my life) tell me that since the building is so old and although we have leveled the area out, added support, etc might be at risk for having that much weight put on it. I could do the floating floor, walls, ceiling, and proper amount of RC and drywall though, but adding more to that could be bad for such an old structure. All I keep reading is now to NOT do this, but what if you have no choice - if you can't do sand or concrete because of weight? Onto the far back area. This was a newer (maybe 60 years ago) addition that has a poured concrete floor - straight to the earth. This is clearly a much easier room to do any "proper" work in. My big question is - which area do I use for what (if not able to use sand or concrete)? Is the area with the concrete floor the control room, or made into a tracking room? With past clientele, I'm very split on the amount of tracking and mixing work I do, and don't want to sacrifice either. Any thoughts on what else can be done in the "basement" area? I've budgeted for insulation, neoprene, and double layer of flooring - but is there some other sort of floor material that could help the low frequency area of this space? I'm very worried that the between the hollow floor and basement beneath is, I may have some horrible low end to deal with. But what would be worse, dealing with this for tracking a drum it, or mixing... I would assume mixing, but... One last item to consider: the concrete portion has the door which leads to the load in and parking area. This makes me want to use it for tracking room with the ease of getting bands in / out. Thanks for the help and I've attached a very simple drawing of the space. Brian
