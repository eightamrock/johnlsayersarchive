The idea is to have only two mass with a sealed air space in between. If the building ceiling is already massive (wich i doubt) then you won't need to do what you describe, otherwise that may be an option. Don't forget this is all depending on the amount of isolation you need. Remember that small rooms are more difficult to deal with acoustically, so having great isolation at the expense of bad sounding room is something that needs to be tought about. :wink: 2X4 for your ceiling joist are a no-go. This all depends on the span you need, if you have really wide rooms then you may end up with 2X8 or even 2X10. Look for the span calculator in the reference section. Please don't assume that it will hold the weight.... :? I agree with johz don't float you room(s). eventough i have float mine :( Also since you have access to that height, i'd strongly go for a 12' ceiling for you rooms. 12' finished inner leaf ceiling that is.

Hi pete Here's the link for the span calculator : http://www.awc.org/calculators/span/cal ... cstyle.asp 15' span would definetely need 2X8 @ 16" OC.
Would this be for a better room size ratio?
Well, that depends on the width and depth of the whole room of course.
Better distribution of modes?
Defenitely. You may also slant the ceiling in the CR (if CR there is )from front to back. in a 12 degres angle. If i had that ceiling space this what i would do in a snap. If these rooms are for band rehersal only then 10' is OK IMHO. 4' feet as an air gap is a huge "spring" although i'm sure it'll be efficient, i just can't tell you what you'll be loosing by reducing it to 2', probaly some very low end stuff. But keep in mind that whatever you build it won't be totally soundproof in the whole spectrum anyway. Metal Monsters will probably be a problem Is the concrete floor in a basement, on a second floor ?, please give us more info as to where this will all be built. In any case,did you made sure if the floor can carry additional dead loads, and if it can, how much more. This is really important for safety reason. Don't assume that a concrete floor will handle anything. Same is true for the building ceiling make sure that you can add dead load to it, and if you can how much, get a figure. As for insulation, in you case i would go for rockwooll in the cavities slightly press against the board. same for the ceiling. Now as for thickness i am not sure for your situation, 3" for surE but may be 6". Knightfly or Sharward may know more about that. Complete fill the whole 4' air gap of the ceiling would be a waste of money IMHO.
