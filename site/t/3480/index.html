With what little you've volunteered so far, here's a start - whether it goes any further is up to you - I consulted last year on a situation similar to yours, except the studio is "semi-attached" - it's in Arizona, in a fairly new development. Stucco exteriors, fairly tight budget (owner wants to save enough for a $35,000 piano) I met with his contractor, and what we ended up with was putting THREE layers of stucco on the outside (stucco is close to the same density as gypsum wallboard) on 2x6 framing, eliminating the obligatory foam board insulation, adding two "insert" layers of gypsum between studs and against the stucco backer board, then a 3" gap between frames, inner 2x4 stud frames, and 3 layers of drywall on the INSIDE of the INNER frame - prior to inner wall construction, tack a poly sheet across outer studs and fill with blown cellulose insulation (instead of the EPS foam board) then REMOVE the poly so it doesn't form a third leaf. Erect inner frames, fill BETWEEN frames with UN-faced standard fiberglass batts, then between studs of inner frame with either rockwool or rigid fiberglass, 2 to 3 PCF - then 3 layers of 5/8 drywall, offset, taped, mudded, etc, caulked layer by layer around perimeter (hope you've read through the "complete section" sticky) IF you don't want to fill between the frames (cost) you can build the frames on the floor, screw light lath across the studs at 2' centers, then stand the frames up, insulate and rock; the lath will keep the insulation in place against the rock for damping. This may cost you 2-3 dB of isolation. Total wall thickness in this case will be nearly 15-1/2", from the outer stucco coat to the inner finished wall surface; with acoustic treatment, another 6-8" typically. So figure losing about 4 FEET of your total OUTSIDE dimensions for a quiet, good-sounding situation. With a proper floated CONCRETE floor and comparably built ceiling leaves, drums would have to hit 100 dB (at KICK drum frequencies, even louder at higher freq's) inside before they would be slightly audible at the outside wall. Height inside - as tall as you can get it in most cases; if you want a floated floor for maximum isolation, both between you and neighbors and between rooms, and you want to do it RIGHT, figure on losing a FOOT for the floor (above the basic slab floor) - ANOTHER foot or more between inner and outer ceiling leaves (your roof doesn't count here, it needs to be VENTED or it will cause problems, even in the desert) Also, ceiling height (to the innermost SOLID barrier) needs to be tied to OTHER room dimensions, so those should be chosen first. As to interior space, ALWAYS go for as much as you can swing, either limited by funds or available space or :cry: spousal approval - it will NEVER be enough.
