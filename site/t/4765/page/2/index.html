knightfly17 January 2006#37
Tom, what was the HEIGHT of your speakers AND mic for the measurements? If the mic was near center of the vertical distance, it could be picking up a first axial harmonic null at 71 hZ - or possibly the second axial harmonic of the length could also do it, if you're near 3 or 10 feet from either end with either your mic or speakers. Thing is, a full modal analysys of your dimensions doesn't show any lack of modal density in ANY of the bands; the tangentials and obliques fill in just fine, so there's no holes shown in a Bonello graph of the room itself. This doesn't mean, however, that PLACEMENT can't cause an axial (or tangential) problem. If you're getting desperate due to the impending gig and you have more absorbent laying around, I'd "shotgun" things for now - add even MORE to your corners, etc, cloud, and save some time to familiarize yourself with the new environment using favorite commercial mixes, etc... Steve

knightfly27 June 2006#44
First of all, my condolences on your breakup; I wouldn't recommend doing anything rash - there may be a possibility of reconciliation, and the less said/done the better mended. (OK, Dear Abby is closed for the night ) On the subject of your room, I would start by placing ONE of your speakers in a corner of the room, facing into the corner - play an 80 hZ test tone through ONLY that speaker, and walk the room with a SLM ("C" weighting, slow) - check high, low, and medium throughout the room and make note of where you get peaks and nulls in the sound level @ 80 hZ. I'd really like to know where this is coming from, since it doesn't really match any of your room dimensions - if it goes away when you place your speaker in the corner, it's because this isn't a room mode at all but a product of mic or speaker placement with respect to room boundaries. There is, however, some chance that this could be a Tangential mode @ 82.9 hZ (first one of length + height). If so, you would see peaks at a midpoint of your front and rear walls, again at midpoints of your ceilng and floor, and nulls halfway between any two of those points... Steve

knightfly5 July 2006#46
I'd put the speaker facing into the rear corner closest to the two adjacent walls - maybe even pull out the absorbent there if it's not too hard. You never mentioned what mic you used on the RTA - hopefully an omni? For this test, all SLM's are omni (good thing) - and you'd just play the frequency you're concerned with thru the single speaker (in that position it will excite the room fairly evenly) and then look for peaks/dips, and map them out. This will help figure out what mode (if it is a mode) that's causing the problem. I gotta say, your room dim's aren't bad at all and I'd be surprised if this isn't something else entirely. In fact, I'm not sure what frequency to suggest you tune slats to since those dimensions are really well balanced... Steve
