knightfly8 January 2006#19
Question 1: I have seen two different ways slots are made: A- The 703 is directly behind the slat and B- The 703 is on the wall behind the slats. I'm not sure which way to build them as of seeing these two ways this morning. I suppose if the 703 is directly behind the slats, it would have to be recessed somewhat to allow the slats to resonate. (A) is the correct way; although these are called slat or slot resonators, what's actually doing the "resonating" is the AIR column behind the slot, not the slat itself (although the slats CAN resonate, it's not a desired thing) - in fact, if the slats are ALLOWED to resonate instead of being somewhat damped, IMO it will usually allow the trap to contribute ringing artifacts to the room and cause more problems than it solves. Question 2: Knowing the problem modes of my room, should I tune the resonator to help out those frequencies. Should I even worry about them. I've attached a picture of my mode results. I know slot resonators only go down to a low mid octave and wouldn't be able to attack the very low problems. I could calculate using the .xls file, but I think it is only for a fixed depth, right? The .xls file is only set up for fixed depth - from what little I actually know about helmholtz, the varying depth of a corner slat resonator won't "widen" the response by as much as might be expected, because the trap will tend to "average" across the varying depth to some extent. Not sure just how MUCH of this occurs, but learned acousticians claim that in order to get the full benefit of a variable depth resonator you would need to construct some sort of "eggcrate" separator behind the slats so each becomes its own "mini-slot" with a finite depth of air column behind the slot. So far, in a corner-mounted slat resonator, what seems to work best is to use the AVERAGE depth behind the slats as a value for the spreadsheet - any "broadening of the trap response due to differing depth would tend to just make the trap seem less "peaky", and therefore less noticeable while still working to pull out the intended range of frequencies (but without losing all the highs). Question 3: I am considering using sanded plywood for the slats on the resonator because the wood would match my mix desk. I know what you're thinking, but no, Martha Stewart has not been advising me Are there considerations to be made on using plywood vs pine? The vertical studs will be 18" on center. Very little. If poured concrete "boards" match your decor, they will still work the same provided they have the same thickness, width, and spacing. Again, it's the AIR column and the measurements asked for in the spreadsheet that make the most difference. That formula, BTW, was (as near as I can tell) derived for use with 703 fiberglass from owens corning - at the time it was written, F. Alton Everest was only using that particular material. Changing the material by any appreciable amount would change its gas flow resistance, thereby changing the resonance of the trap and consequently the center frequency. Please don't ask me how MUCH, I'm still learning about this stuff myself (and will til they "pat me in the face with a shovel" - however, one of our members uses a different formula which (IIRC) requires this parameter to be entered - he claims to match calculated and actual center frequencies very closely, and has test results to back it up. Here's a thread where this is discussed - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... sc&start=0 HTH... Steve

Tom Zartler8 January 2006#22
Lookie here what I found
how many slats do i need to adjust to one frequency that it starts to work? As a rough guide (this isn't a very EXACT science) a typically designed helmholtz will absorb around 60-75% at resonance, sometimes slightly more - this is partly a function of the percent of depth of the absorbent inside, 50% fill tends to return around 70% (again, ROUGH estimate) - so, if you use 70% then each square foot of absorber face would result in approximately 0.7 Sabins of absorption AT RESONANCE. There is usually about a 2 octave range, again this is dependent largely on insulation fill. More insulation, wider range of absorption, but LOWER "Q" (less than 70% absorption) Random slats usually won't widen the band as much as expected, because the wavelength vs. absorber size causes the absorber to "average", more or less. Same with depth from wall; you can get a rough idea by using the AVERAGE depth, so if yours goes from 6" deep to 12" deep, use 9" -
What this means I have no clue, but I think I may be on to something :)
