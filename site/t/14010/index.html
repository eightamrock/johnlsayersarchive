We just closed up our "dead air space" and hung our heads in shame. Here's the problem. When we decided to make the basement into a separate live room and control room instead of a open space project studio, we build our left dividing walls up against the existing sheetrock with a 1/4 inch caulked gap. Well, of course the sound is now traveling to the back of the rock in the drum room between the rock and 2" rigid insulation and straight into the control room! When we sealed the windows and had a drummer play the only sound we could hear was leaking into the left wall. The isolation from the ceiling was better than expected and there was none through the right wall, due to a decoupled room container the oil burner. We cut the top sill 2x4 and footer on both sides of the wall and the vibration was knocked down considerably but one piece of 4 x 8 rock, the one we are carrying in the picture below, is bridging one room into the other. Very dumb! Anyway, If we cut the rock up to the electrical box where the natural seam is, remove the rigid insulation with rockwool and stop it with some mass, leaving a 1/4' gap before the dead air space, that should do it I hope. The dividing walls are 2 layers of 5/8 rock with green glue and the dead air space is staggered studs filled with rock wool and finished with sonex. The ceiling is floating on hat channel and iso clips. We were actually very pleased with isolation we got everywhere but the left wall. We not only failed at stopping the sound from the left wall but invited it in. I hope cutting the rock will work. Any suggestions? Thanks.

xSpace10 February 2010#13
The issue with your room is that you have established a two leaf( double wall) in the middle of a room that has hard connections. Basically the two leaf is being jumped over, under, thru and around by all these connections. You can cut up the sheetrock or what have you but this is going to have minimum impact. The path of least resistance has to be terminated and that requires decoupling...not running over. "There is no physical connection now between the two rooms." I am afraid there is. The connection is as much with the ceiling as it is with the walls. Even cutting the sheetrock that seems like a good idea isn't helping as much as you might think. The vibration is running to the outermost side of the framing/wall and back into the existing framing wall on the opposite side room.
