jwl14 February 2007#2
No, the shape of the room will not "kill reverb" per se. What it will do is greatly reduce/eliminate "flutter echo" which you definitely don't want to hear while playing or in your recordings. What affects reverb is the construction details of the room, and of course the size of the room. Think of it this way, in a perfectly sealed, perfectly reflective room (hypothetically speaking) sound will bounce around the room, slowly losing energy until it dies away. If the room is not airtight, then some sound will escape out of the room. If there is absorption in the room, then much of the sound will be absorbed as it bounces. Diffusion is another way to affect how the sound moves about the room. Wall shape won't really affect this much, it will just affect WHERE the sound goes when it bounces. In a well-designed room, you can use this to your advantage not only to kill flutter echo, but also to create a Reflection-Free Zone to get rid of first reflections, which when listening to stereo speakers tightens up the stereo image.

jwl15 February 2007#7
I agree that gobos on the back wall make sense, especially in a one-room design. When you are recording, you can move the gobos around strategically to maximize isolation in the one-room design. Then when it's time to mix/critically listen, move the gobos to the back wall again so they will absorb rear reflections. Another option is to combine your bookshelf approach with absorption; fill the back wall with absorption of whatever variety you like, and then put bookshelves in front of that. :-) Note that bookshelves aren't necessarily good diffusors, but they should break up reflections a bit.
