knightfly10 February 2006#2
James, from your pics I’m only seeing a single wall frame where the door is; is this your plan, or will you be adding a second frame and door to make the entire structure a m-a-m construct? Ceiling - what’s your intent there; resilient suspended from outer joists, or separate inner frame rested on inner walls? If the first one, is your roof strong enough? Usually a floated floor is done with the inner walls resting ON TOP of the floor, so the wall gets floated also; then, the inner ceiling gets supported on these floated walls, making the entire thing isolated from any hard contact with the outer mass leaf. Whether or not you float the walls though, you should use what’s called a Perimeter Isolation Board; typically made of Homosote/Celotex, because the softer material keeps transference to a minimum should the inner structure shift enough to cause contact with the outer shell. You could also use VERY dense rockwool, like 8 PCF or greater. Have you read the first couple pages of Aaron’s thread on floating floors? It’s in the REFERENCE section, and I explained how to calculate deflection of unknown materials there. Depending on how close other people are, and how much noise you intend to make, you may want to NOT float your floor at all - in fact, if you plan ahead you could wait til you see if it’s even necessary and if so, build a floated floor after all the rest is done - no need to waste time/money if it’s not needed… Steve

knightfly18 May 2006#7
I deleted your question in Acoustics since all the pix are here; you don't seem to have looked very far for info on acoustic treatment yet, I understand you're wanting results quickly but everyone gets tired of typing the same things over and over - I'll do a quick run-through, then you can read a few dozen threads so it makes more sense :? The triangle shaped pieces are "bass traps", also called corner traps - they go in the corners :wink: preferably floor to ceiling in all corners possible. They can also be adhered to wall-ceiling corners if you have enough. If you'll be recording your drums (or not) you'll want some of the flatter foam on the ceiling - some kind of lightweight metal frame hung over the kit can work here - here's one method http://www.recording.org/ftopict-26889.html Next step is treatment for flutter echo between parallel walls - place 2'x2' pieces of the foam on one wall, direcly opposite should be bare wall. Repeat randomly til you're happy with the sound. (This may never happen; that thin foam won't absorb nearly as low frequencies as thicker mineral wool will, so the sound will likely be a bit "boxy" - the "fix" for this is thicker, more absorbent material and deeper, wider corner traps. If you have any intention of keeping your drums from being heard outside the room, you WILL be adding a second frame and couple more layers of wallboard and a second door... Steve
