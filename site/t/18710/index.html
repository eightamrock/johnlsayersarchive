this is the REW spl graphic after spending days moving speakers and absorbers all around everywhere.
SPL alone only tells you a very, very small part of the entire acoustic picture, and not even the most important part. The rest is in how the room behaves over time, not just in the frequency domain alone. Also, there's right way and a million wrong ways to set up a room: Trial and error from an unknown start usually leads to one of the million "wrongs", seldom to the "right"! :)
What is causing those cancellations!?!?!
Room modes. Plan and simple. Or rather, they are mostly room modes, but there are also some other issues mixed up in there, related to reflections, SBIR, comb filtering, and other stuff. By setting up your room correctly, you can eliminate or greatly reduce most of those non-modal issues, and proper treatment will deal with the modal issues (as far as possible) as well as most of the "other stuff" issues. By the way, you have a mains hum problem in your system somewhere: Just from looking at your REW data, I figured out that you live in the USA... :) The hum is pegged at 120Hz, and is only about 40 dB down from the peaks, so I'm surprised you haven't noticed it. That needs fixing too. You also need to bring up the level of your REW tests, and/or calibrate REW properly: You need a sound level meter to do that, and you should conduct your tests at a level of about 82 dBC for each speaker by itself, which should give you around 86 dBC for all three together. (And yes, the sub does seem to be underpowered.) It also seems that you greatly changed the position of the mic and speakers between the "before treatment" test and the later tests, so they cannot be compared at all, unfortunately. Also, the "before" test only runs from 40 Hz to 4 kHz, so it isn't much use at all. That's a shame, as it would have been really good to have a solid, valid, "baseline" test of the untreated room against which to compare the results of the treatment. Is there any way you can remove the treatment from the room easily, and repeat that "baseline" test? But don't do that until you get the geometry situation correct: there's no point in that. The speakers and mix position must be fixed in their final locations before you can do the baseline test, because the location of the mic CANNOT change between tests: moving the mic invalidates comparisons.

and perhaps might have been better off remaining in. THAT !!! is the million dollar question here.
Probably not! That was a much smaller room, by the looks of your rough SketchUp. Small rooms almost never sound as good as large rooms, if done right. It would really help to know the dimensions of the rooms! Anyway, I would suggest that your plan should be to first resolve your symmetry issue: it is absolutely critical that the left and right halves of the room must be mirror-images of each other. When that is resolved, then you should get rid of all the "junk" in the room, and then carefully place ONLY the stuff that you absolutely need (meaning just the 2 main speakers, the sub, the desk, console, gear, and your chair) in the geometrically correct positions and orientations, then run the baseline REW test like that. THEN install the basic room treatment that that is always needed (bass traps, first reflections, cloud, etc.) and do another REW test, then based on the results of that test, see what still needs to be done. That's the usual way of approaching studio layout and treatment. Hope that helps! - Stuart -
