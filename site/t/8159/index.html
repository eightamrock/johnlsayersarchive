gullfo9 February 2007#4
what is the room constructed of? how does it currently sound? one thought is to "incrementally" treat the room. put in absorbers in the corners to improve your low-mid broadband asorption. put up some wall and ceiling based absorption to kill off flutter echos. put in some carpeting. keep working the room until it sounds good. keep in mind if the treatments can be adjusted then you can change the room - if you need it deader, turn the treatments around to face the "soft" side out, when you need more life, turn them face out to the "hard" side. knightfly12 February 2007#11
OK, didn't run those dim's into cad but if the two short walls are parallel, that (and the ceiling) is where your flutter is coming from - considering your wish to use foam panels, I would get the Auralex TruTraps (more even absorption, so less coloration) and position them NOT opposite each other on your short walls (stagger them, in other words) - I would build something similar to David French's design, here http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=5930 Depending on your budget, you may want to look at alternatives to foam that will kill flutter but keep the room brighter - one such is here http://www.rpginc.com/products/flutterfree/ I've no idea the cost of these any more, use the email at the bottom of the page to find cost and dealers near you. I guarantee it won't be cheap though. Using more conventional things like your choice of foam panels, you should get roughly half of the upper half of all your walls treated (very general rule), remembering NOT to place panels directly opposite each other for best flutter control. HTH... Steve

knightfly13 February 2007#13
I'd start with only the upper half of the walls, and if your foam isn't at LEAST 2" thick I would try to find a way to DOUBLE it - thin foam is about as bad as carpet for uneven absorption. For the ceiling, I would still do a thicker, heavier duty hanging cloud as I linked to. But yes, you have the right idea for placement - if there is foam on one of two parallel walls, there should be blank wall opposite - doing it this way keeps from covering the entire surface, yet still keeps it so no two bare wall sections are facing each other... Steve
jwl13 February 2007#14
Just another voice in the chorus. For a small booth, I would definitely make it as dead as I could. I can't see a room of that size imparting a nice sound to any recording you might make. I'd also pay some attention to bass trapping in that booth as well; you won't need much if it will only be used for vocals, but you'll get some nasty room modes in a booth that size otherwise. Your foam panels will have very little if any effect on the low end. Space wise, your best bet might be to hang a cloud that's at least 4" thick (of 703 or equiv) hanging at least 4" from the ceiling. 6" thick 6" down is better. Either design will provide some low-end absorption.
