Have you calculated the weight of those walls?? You are looking at quite massive loads that really only sit on a 3" width of wood that without anchors will stay in place only as long as you can guarantee you wont have a seismic event or other catastrophic event. Anchors are cheap insurance, and strong enough to give that peace of mind. You could go the nail gun or construction adhesive route, but if you choose the nail gun you still have a more expensive installation, and the construction adhesive about the same as anchors, but not nearly the same sheer strength. Will there be a problem holding up twol layers of dry wall using nails?? No. But Anaheim does require anchor bolts, and IMO you'd be foolish not to use them looking at the big picture.
sharward8 March 2007#5
Under normal and calm circumstances, nails would hold a soleplate to a slab. Even under heavy loads from above, they'll still hold -- some might argue they'd hold better under heavy loads as opposed to light loads. However, apprioriate length and width anchor bolts (and, ideally, if the slab is a fresh pour, J-bolts embedded into the concrete) have far superior lateral shear strength (i.e., sideways). This is important under not normal and calm circumstances, such as earthquakes, vehicle intrusions (i.e., a car crashes into a building), etc. They are also superior in their ability to hold the walls down and keep the building together under semsic and hurricane loads where there is a tendency for the building to be subject to significant upward jolts. There are countless buildings without anchor bolts or J-bolts that have become statistics in catastrophic faiulre. The Loma Prieta (San Francisco) qake of October 17, 1989 and the Northridge quake of January 17, 1994, have taught us a lot. Even our very own Kendale experienced quite a shaker back in October of last year. I'm betting he'd be one to recommend the use of anchor bolts in your case. --Keith :mrgreen:
