Hi Guys, This is my first post, I have been reading the forum over the last month since I decided I need a studio space which is outside of our home. I make bass-heavy electronic music (under the name Truth), as well as working on stuff for Film and Games. For the last couple of years I have just used a room in the house, treated very simply with some home-made panels on the walls. My wife is sick of me using a room in the house for the studio, and we have a second child who is 10 months old, and ready to go into his own room. I have decided to have a room built by a company who specializes in sleepouts/external offices. Two reasons for this. Local council rules state that I can build a structure with less than a 10m Sq internal footprint without having to get any consents. Also, cost is a factor, and this is the cheapest option, as I'm not so great at actually building stuff. When we eventually sell the house in 5-10 years, the studio will revert to a bedroom or home office. I was going to do a little more research before posting, but I just got word the company I am using is ready to start framing up this week (I gave them the go-ahead on Friday and anticipated a wait of a few weeks). I will start off by saying, I realize this is a small space, and I am used to bigger, but this is what I realistically can build with the space available, budget and council rules. My main question here (for now) relates to the ceiling angle, but feel free to chip in with anything else, or just to rip my ides to shreds! I'm not too sure about the fact the back wall is pretty much a wall of glass (Sliding door and opening window as per council rules), I'll obviously have super-thick curtains which I pull in front of this. I was also thinking I could mount a couple of the acoustic panels gobo-style and pull them across behind me when I am working. So the question is, will the angle of ceiling make much of a difference? At the moment, the standard design has the ceiling starting at 2.2m in front of where I'll sit, and raising to 2.4m at the back. I can customize this to spec if it makes any difference, which I am assuming it will. I was also thinking of making an angled panel suspended from the ceiling above where I sit, but I'm not too sure about what I'm doing there either. Inside dimensions: 3.4m x 2.5m Below are rough sketchups of approximately how I will set things up. All the traps will be removable when we eventually sell the house. Thanks so much in advance! with these two points in mind, you must be aware that you won't be getting very good isolation. Bass heavy music and 'out of the box' construction don't play well together. So as long as your neighbours dig what you do this may not be a problem. As said by Andre: Studio construction is 90% planning and 10% building. It's worth putting the brakes on getting the build done at this point if you require any kind if isolation for your room. stevev20 May 2015#4
in those specs they say the 1st layer is unstopped. you'll need them to stop both layers otherwise you'll have the possibility of an air gap in the joi of the first layer. They're also using a standard plasterboard. If possible and in your budget, gte them to use a 16mm fire rated plaster. Much better. called fyrchek in OZ but I'd think there's a comparatice product in NZ. For cavity damping you're going to want a fibreglass batt around 30kg/m3 density. That table doesn't mentio weight. You'll want to check that out before proceeding.
TruthTristan wrote:
I probably won't have any lighting installed in the ceiling, rather use lamps, to limit the number of perforations in the plasterboard.
Ideally you don't want any perforations in your plaster for power, except for the one mains inlet that is unavoidable. Surface mounting power/lighting is the way to go if possible. Inside out wall construction allows this.
TruthTristan wrote:
I'm also concerned about sound transmission out through the floor.
sounds like it will be an issue. what is the floor constructed from? Look, it sounds like you know that you aren't going to get great isolation from this type of construction. I know what it's like to want to get the build over the line, but with the money you're going to spend on this, you could achieve a better end result by putting the brakes on and doing a bit more homework....and probably not spend a lot more than you're probably about to. all the best, Steve
