sharward30 October 2006#2
Your drywallers installed 1/2" of drywall directly to the bottom of the joists, then installed RC, and then installed another layer of drywall on it? That's a three-leaf ceiling, friend, and that's not good at all. :( Maybe an extra leyer of drywall with 100% application of Green Glue on your ceiling, if you can afford the weight without overloading your joists (which is a big if -- do not make any assumptions that you don't have span table and/or engineering data to back up!) may provide you with some relief... :roll: ...Or, quite frankly, ripping down that entire two layers of drywall and starting over, with RSIC clips and two layers of 5/8" drywall with 100% the recommended coverage of Green Glue in between. Another option, since it seems the upstairs floor is an unfinished subfloor at this point, would be to seal all the seams and the perimeter upstairs with a good acoustic sealant, add a second layer of 3/4" T&G plywood upstairs, with 100% recommended coverage of Green Glue sandwiched in between -- again, only if the joists can support the extra dead load. In any event, the choice of floor covering upstairs is also going to make a difference... Laminate? Ceramic tile? Carpet? Engineered hardwood? :roll: I'm sorry we didn't catch you earlier before making this mistake... But looking at the other threads you started in the construction forum and the design forum, there honestly wasn't much opportunity. We never saw anything that even hinted to the design of a wimpy three-leaf ceiling system. The devil is in the details with this stuff, as I'm sure you've since learned. I realize the train has already left the station in your case, as your house is being built as I type this and your options may be extremely limited at this point. Nevertheless, I strongly suggest you buy Rod's book, study it very carefully, and accept the fact that in spite of this being new construction, you're most likely going to have to approach this from a retrofit perspective, quite possibly after the house is built and you're already living in it. Perhaps the situation isn't as dim as I've made it out to be, but lacking details to the contrary, I have to call it as I see it. :( --Keith :mrgreen:
