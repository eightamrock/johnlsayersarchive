31 Hz modal problem

Started by ejb on 29 January 2010. 16 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 14003.

ejb29 January 2010#1
Hello guys, I have built a control room in an odd shaped (octagon) room, 20.5 ft between parallel walls. These walls climb 10 ft vertically, after which they rise at a 10/12 pitch toward a point in the center. The center point is 18 ft above the floor. The walls are double framed, double layered drywall, but there is unfortunately about 100 sq ft of window surface. The floor is wood, over concrete floor. Using a calibrated mic and monitor pair, I have tracked pink noise in the room & found, out of 16 separate locations, a 31 Hz problem at every location. I believe it is obvious where it is coming from (1130 / 18ft = 62.8 Hz; 1/2 wavelength of ~31 Hz). The ceiling is the deepest corner in the room, converging at about 77 degrees. Because I believe the trouble is definitely forming in the ceiling, my plan of attack is to build a ceiling bass trap - probably a hemholtz type. I already have the formulas, etc. to design such a trap, but I've 2 questions I can't seem to answer: 1. How much surface area of this bass trap will I need to equalize the (average) modal peak of 8.5 dB at this frequency? 2. If I shift the design of the trap such that its physical dimensions are be deeper instead of wider (adjusting the perforation percentage to keep the tuning the same), will the increased depth allow me to get more absorbing power? [In other words, does a bass trap of higher volume have more absorbancy than a shallower bass trap of the same surface area, if both are tuned to the same frequency?] The room volume is 4900 cubic ft, if it helps.

Soundman202029 January 2010#3
I have built a control room in an odd shaped (octagon) room, 20.5 ft between parallel walls. These walls climb 10 ft vertically, after which they rise at a 10/12 pitch toward a point in the center. The center point is 18 ft above the floor.
This might seem like a strange question, but why on earth did you build it like that? It doesn't make much sense, acoustically, as far as I can see. Why octagonal? That gives you FOUR sets of parallel walls, all at the same spacing! Kind of like two square rooms, superimposed on each other!!! I would expect that you'd have huge modal problems at 27.6 Hz, 55.1Hz and all multiples thereof from the first order axials, not to mention the related tangentials and obliques. Not to mention serious flutter echos at the same frequency, etc. Plus, your ceiling is going to focus reflections and do other strange things. It's also hard to see where you'd put your bass traps in an octagonal room! Even if you solve your 31 Hz dip (which at 8.5 dB isn't all that bad too stat with), you'll still have all the other issues to deal with. Anyway, that's just my 0.02...

ejb1 February 2010#7
xSpace wrote:
Have you done anything to treat the walls, stop the reflections, flutter echo, etc?
No, sir. I was taught to treat acoustics in the following order: 1. Modal problems, first. Start w/ the lowest frequencies, then re-test to check effectiveness & see how the next frequency bands were affected. 2. Reverberation time. Treat specific problems first, then broadband absorption until reverberation level is up to par. 3. Keep some flat spaces in reserve for diffusers. This was the method I learned a few years ago. If things have changed, I'm open to learning the new techniques. But the opinion ingrained into me was that it was a mistake to treat other acoustic problems before attacking the modal problems first. I cannot post drawings due to computer equipment trouble.
