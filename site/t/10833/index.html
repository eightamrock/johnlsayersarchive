Hi zh.guitar and welcome to JLS RSD! You did nothing wrong. What it is that sometimes a thread does attract some people to post on, or they think other people can reply better, or the people who will reply have not been on the site or haven't had the time. First off, your budget is quite realistic if you are going to do much of the labour yourself. If not it is tight, but still realistic. To your questions in easy to answer order:
2. General layout
It looks fine. If you think of recording in the future with a mobile, have conduit/duct(s) in the floor, for running snakes etc. The bigger, the better. Several to keep different level signals seperate. A hospitality/kitchen counter? Or is that the frig in the drawing?
4. Any forseen problamatic areas that I am missing?
If this a commercial facility or possible, ADDA requirements for access. Windows? If you are building from scratch, I recommend making the outer wall from brick or concrete for better low frequency isolation.
3. Sound proof doors a. with the 2 doors, do I need to worry a lot about the inner door if I purchase a pre-fab door from Overly ( or equivalent ) with a STC rating of 55 or above for the exterior door.
High STC rated doors are very expensive, and still not very good with low frequencies. Two low 40s STC rated door assemblies with the hallway very absorbent will be more effective at low frequencies and cost significantly less.
1. HVAC design. Since this is from scratch I am going to hire a pro but thought that some of the ideas on this site might be better.
Since you are not worried about sound between studio/control room etc. this is relatively straightforward, for studio familiar designers. Keep the air flow velocity around 100 fpm at the vent, 200 fpm for five feet of the ducts by the vent, and 300 fpm for the rest. Use acoustically lined ducts, use a decoupler between the unit and the ducts and have at least one bend in the ducts before entering the rehearsal room and one before the vents. This will give world class performance. You can go as high as 300 cfm, 600 cfm and 1,000 cfm and the noise level will be around 30 dBa. For reference, the THX standard for cinemas IIRC is 30 dBa. If this seems daunting to you, go with a pro design it. Glenn Stanton, John Sayers, and Rod Gervais all do this professionally. Good luck and congratulations. You know that good studio building is 90% design and 10% construction. Andre

AVare12 July 2008#7
This post is directed at people reading thread because the subject matter fits in. I am certain that you have researched this already zh.guitar, or are about to, to confirm what I wrote about high STC rated doors. Door systems regardless of their high STC ratings, almost all have poor Low Frequency (LF) Transmission Loss (TL). The attached image is from the IAC (one of the top acoustic isolation door manufacturers) door brochure Door Bulletin SCD 000. It shows the TL of doors rated from STC 43 to 64. At higher frequencies, the greater the STC rating, the greater the TL. However, at 63 Hz, the highest rated door has only 1 dB greater isolation than the lowest rated door! With music the greatest difficulty is controlling LF TL. For effective LF TL multiple doors and sound locks are necessary.
