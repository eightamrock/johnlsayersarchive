The manufacturer didn't think it was a good idea... (KRK VXT6 - nearfield)
That's strange: it is not rear ported, and it does have the necessary bass roll off control for half-space, which is exactly what you need for soffit mounting. The manual also does not mention anything about not soffit mounting them... so I'm not sure why they would say that, when the speaker is ideal for soffit mounting.
but from about the top of the monitor and down to the floor it just going to be fabric covering the insulation. And from the top of my monitor and up, just cover the insulation with a solid panel.
I don't think that would be a good idea. You would have a partial soffit like that, since the surface above would certain act as an infinite baffle, while the surface below would not, and you would still have all the usual artifacts from speakers that are not soffited: SBIR, edge diffraction, power imbalance, etc. Also, the speaker does not have a variable bass roll-off control, which is what you would need for that situation. It only has an on-off switch for half-space, which obviously gives the 6dB reduction that is needed for full soffit mounting, but you will only need 3 or 4 dB, since you are only doing half a soffit, so you'd only need to remove some of the baffle step response correction, not all of it. I also suspect that your half-soffit would "steer" the sound, or affect it differently above and below, which is not what you want to do, really. So I would suggest that you either complete the soffit by extending the front panel down to the bottom of the speaker (which is what you show in your image anyway, but not what you explained), or forget the soffit completely, and just mount your speakers on stands. It's usually not a good idea to mix two different design concepts, unless you fully understand what you are doing, and can compensate for the side effects.
The boxes on the side of the listening position, is also a idea from this forum. With the 12 degrees and slots at the front. 2) Do i have do box it in, or could i just make a frame and cover it up?
It has to be a sealed box. Those are slot resonators, which is another form of the Helmholtz resonator, so there has to be a sealed cavity behind the slats.
In the back i'v planed to just make som boxes in the corner and fill them with insulation and have a cloth front. But would this have much effect? And how thick do they have to be?
I would suggest that you consider "Superchunk" bass traps for your rear corners. They are the best way of doing that in a small room.
