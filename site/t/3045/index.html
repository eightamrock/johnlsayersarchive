drfrankencopter2 April 2005#14
My first post here....first of all, great forum, I'm gonna like it here! Malcolm's paper on control room design was one ofthe first acoustic references that I read that just bade sense. You could tell he had tons of practical experience in the field. He was the guy who got me thinking in terms of 'acoustic feet' (i.e. the total path length between direct sound and reflected sound). I hope that someone publishes his paper on a site somewhere because I think a lot of people just getting their feet we in considering acoustics would be well served by reading it. Sure LEDE has fallen out of favour, but the principle behind is still very much en-vogue. The whole idea behind the overwhelming majority of modern control room designs is that early reflections in the range of 0 to 20ms (from the receipt of direct sound) must be reduced. How this goal is accomplished becomes a matter of tase. LEDE aims to kill the early reflections with absorbtion. RFZ aims to re-direct the early reflections so that they are beyond the 20ms time window before they reach the listening posiition. Controlled image design strikes a balance between the two approaches with the use of strategically placed absorbtion and controlled wall splaying (check out the BBC library for more info on that). Other control room designs are less common...like a Moulton room (which embraces early reflections), or a Hidley 20Hz room (broadband absorbtion galore, reflective front, absorbtive rear). Nevertheless, I'd say most control rooms are design using the basic principles on which LEDE was founded. Malcolm Chisolm had a way of making the black magic of control room design more accessable to the average enthusiast. Like okay you want few reflections in a 20ms window, well if there is 10 feet of room from your listening position to the rear wall, then you're a long way towards your goal. I think re-releasing Malcolms papers in HTML would be great for the community. Cheers, Kris
