When I started trying to figure it out, all I had after two days was a mess of lines and surfaces, like something out of a nightmare Picasso might have had on his worst day, while drunk! :) On the proposed layout: that can definitely be improved! There are many possibilities for that space, but you'll need to work on your goals and concepts a bit in order to figure things out better. For example, try to come up with a description of the way you see a typical day in the life of your studio: Do you have multiple clients doing different things at the same time in different rooms, or is it just you guys jamming for fun? Do you need to run two recording sessions at the same time, or one recording session plus one mix / mastering session? Do you need to be able to rehearse in one area while another group is tracking and a third client is mastering? Is the primary purpose of the facility rehearsal, or tracking, or mixing, or mastering? What types of groups / music / instruments will you be recording / rehearsing? Etc. Try to figure out your needs in that area, and that should help you focus on which rooms should be your priority, and which can go on the back burner for now. You will also need to look into local noise regulations, and conduct some tests with a sound level meter, to see how much isolation you need. Also, this is a going to be a commercial facility, so you need to consider things wheelchair accessibility, and other things that you wouldn't need to bother with for a home studio. Lots of things to think about, but the more you get done, the more it will lead you towards a good design. I'll take a look at your links tomorrow, but I gotta get some sleep now! :) - Stuart -
