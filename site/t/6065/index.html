The dilemma is that because of the topology of the building area the studio must be built on a post and pier foundation. I have been told by an architect that I can get a slab pored in a basin for a post and pier foundation but I am sure that the slab will be limited in thickness. The ground slopes away from the existing house so the posts will start at around ground level at one end and will end up about 4 or 5 feet off the ground at the other end. How thick is thick enough for this slab to not have major flanking to the outside? If I were to not go with a slab due to engineering considerations (not sure what that would be but...) is there a way to get enough mass with some sort of wood deck system?
Ok - first off - with a pier and post foundation you can always install a series of grade beams around the perimeter of the building and backfill within that to effectively raise the grade of the 1st floor. If the existing earth is structurally sound - then this fill will suport a slab - and you will (in essence) be building a slab on grade building - thus with a little care to pour isolated slab assemblies (as outlined in chapter 4 - see detail 4.1) you would be just fine. But - please do not misconstrue the section on elevated wood isolating decks (which are specifically speaking about decks constructed on top of existing deck assemblies). In new construction - as long as your engineers understand the mass required - and the center frequencies needed to effectively create isolation - a wood frame deck assembly in post construction - can absolutely give you the isolation you need - this because form the very beginning the structure will be designed to carry the mass required to create that isolation. Part of this will require that an acoustical engineer be brought on-board to verify the design will meet the isolation - and that should be early on in the process - but either way - with an elevated wood deck - an elevated concrete slab or a slab over fill with grade beams at the building's perimiter - you can acheive the isolation you require. Sincerely, Rod
