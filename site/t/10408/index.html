Been reading for a while now and picking up loads of ideas; what a great forum :) Anyway, first post here so before I go into detail of what I will be trying to achieve, just a simple question - can a control room be too small? Basically what I will be doing is building a new outbuilding in the confines of my garden, but will be restricted by fences, boundaries etc. The space I will have to work in will be approx 12' long x 10' wide (height will be about 8' internal dimension). I figure that with the correct double thickness wall construction as the standard STC63 detail, the walls themselves will be about 6" thick, so the internal available space will be about 11' long x 9' wide. The room is designed in my head and on scraps of paper, with countless revisions coming about from reading stuff on here. By the way I'm just outside of London, UK. I've read all the 'before you post' topics, just wanted some initial thoughts first; not expecting anyone to do my design for me :wink: Hopefully I'll get some positves out of this and commit my ideas to sketch-up (once mastered!) Thanks in advance.

yellowfever wrote:
Thanks for reply. I understand that a smaller room will be harder to treat, but the size I quoted is what I'm stuck with unfortunately. So with the right treatment, broadband, bass trapping etc, I should get a usable space?
Yes. Of course you can treat it. I just tried to answer your question that, yes a CR can be to small. But you are like me, stuck with whatever we got or can get our hands on (in my case not a size problem per se just an near 1:2:2 ratio :cry: ), so unless we can choose another room (in my case not, because I ironically live in a so called "studio apartment") we have to deal with what we got. So, yes, a lot of bass entrapment need to be performed. :wink: And since the room will not have as a flat response as a bigger one, there need to be some care to setting up monitors and listening spot. There's no real magic to the treatment: a bass trap at the rear end wall and SuperChunks in the corners for bass absorption, cloud in the ceiling and panels on the side wall between your listening spot and the monitors for RFZ. Or something like that. Your axial fundamental room modes (for 8x10x12') will be 71:57:47 Hz with the ratio 1.0:1.25:1.5.
