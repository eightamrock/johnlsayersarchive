StoneCutter, we have the same set-up. Ours is on 90 acres and the building is a 24x40. We split the building in half with a woodshop on one side and the recording/rehearsal on the other. We ended up with a 16x16x9 tracking room and a 16x7x7.5 control room. The entire 24x16 facility sits on 2" certifoam on the slab, two layers of 1/2" osb railroaded. The control room is elevated 24" and is supported off the control side wall of the control room and posted on neoprene pucks for isolation. The partition wall is doubled and decoupled with many layers of rock and osb. I like the elevated control room as it provides a better view of the tracking room as well as helping with iso. Due to our location we weren't as concerned with noise getting outside so much as noise getting inside. Right now we're struggling with door and window design. We think we have some good ideas and some great info from this site but nothing has materialized as of yet. Good luck and keep us posted. PS: R76 in the ceiling almost keeps the noise from the rain on that steel roof out :wink: . Little more work on that situation as well.

sharward29 December 2005#16
I'm assuming by "stringers" you mean "studs" -- the vertical pieces of wood. ("Stringers," or "noggins" as they say in the UK, are the small horizontal pieces that are sometimes fit between the studs to bridge them together.) Properly beefing up your outer walls (and ceiling) will help you achieve better isolation between inside and outside. If you need that, then you should do it. If not, then it may be a waste of money. If your primary concern is room-to-room isolation, then you should invest your material money on the interior walls. --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward29 December 2005#18
I stand corrected -- those aren't studs after all. Let's call 'em "stringers" for lack of a better term! ;-) Don't plan on the holding the drywall strips with only glue. You'd probably need to also run some 1x2s or 2x3s vertically over the whole thing, attached to the "stringers," in order to keep them from falling down. And don't glue the drywall on their faces because then you'll end up with a solid piece that will probably resonate at a worse frequency than three individual sheets will separately. You'll want to carefully read my thread, starting at the point where I began my outer leaf beef-up, which starts on page 24 and runs through page 32. You probably don't want to do exactly what I did, but there's still tons of great information about beef-up in those pages. --Keith :mrgreen:
