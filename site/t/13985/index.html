Hi Guys, I am new to this forum have read a whole lot. I record quite a bit at Kev's GURULAND. and that's how I know of John's designs. Everyone loves the rooms there. I have moved house and my personal; Mix and Overdub room is having a reincarnation. Here is the rough plan so far.
Image not preserved: 1_G_F.jpg
Existing building The roof slopes downwards from North to south, North High end is 3000mm South Low end is 2100mm (all the good outboard will go on the "high end" and the Behringer stuff will go on the "Low end") {joke hah ha :lol: } The room is a workshop area stuck on the side of the double brick garage. the door at the top leads into the garage. The door on the left goes to the back yard. there is a double sash window on the south wall (yes at the bottom) which will be half blocked off and sealed and I will take advantage of some natural light. The walls are single Brick and the roof is tile and the floor is Concrete. Prerequisites: A vocal booth positioned where someone could walk in and out of the control room without disturbing talent. Fen Shui - Don't want my back facing door as I am working in the control room. Stop traffic noise from semi-busy road 35 meters to the south. (occasional trucks using compression breaking) Stop traffic rumble from highway 350meters to west Wheel chair access (700mm wide) Construction so far: Bare rafters had Sarking (enviorosheild) stapled on and then 6mm MDF to seal it. 10mm plasterboard on all the brick walls stuck on with stud adhesive. All sealed airtight with flexible acrylic gap filler squeezed into good gaps same thickness as plasterboard. I plan to build a free sanding frame with a 15mm gap between the 1st layer of plasterboard already mounted to the brick walls. The frame will be clad with two layers of 10 mm plasterboard. I know the air gap is small but i am being like one of my customers wanting too much for too little. I suppose I could double that gap to 30mm. NOW in the rough image i have pointed areas where there is not much room for absorption. in the north side the reflection from the left speaker on the hard (brick/plaster) surface on the right will bounce directly to me and the reflection from the right speaker on the South hard surface will reflect to the couch... What can I do in these small spaces to redirect or trap/apsorb . the further east you look the more room I have for adsorbing rock wool hangers etc. So the main problem I feel is the areas of hard surfase just east (to the right) of my splayed surfaces adjacent to the speakers. I am sure someone will hit a bomb shell that I haven't noticed yet. Neibours to the north there are neighbors about 10 meters away but there is a two car garage as an"air gap?". to the east (right there is a footpath, side street then a house about 25 meters away diagonally adjacent (north east) and one in the opposite diagonal (south east) directly across the road is a double garage. I normally mix quiet and remind myself to turn down often. sometimes I get carried away during the daytime during traffic noise etc. but at night I am very disciplined with my volume. So 100% isolation is not really necessary. However if a small design change can help with minimal cost I will be happy to take advice. Plan Let's say I have a small budget, I already have heaps of rockwool and quite a few absorbers and the floor materials from the last studio (that I just moved out of) The Room within the room will sit on hi density foam to decouple from the floor. the floor will be also sitting on foam and will be 19mm (3/4") yellow tongue with the fake floorboards , the locking type 8mm thick. The floor will not touch the frame to aid in decoupling. The speakers will be flush-mount/ Soffited. The room will be mostly dead. The gray dotted lines will be the absorptive walls I would like some guide as to where to Bass trap and where to put reflective surface to liven up a bit. The ceiling 2100mm high will be total absorptive 120mm rockwool with material over. The inside of the room in room's roof will be lined with 120mm rock wool and possibly have hangers in the cavity (remember the roof is angled roughly 3000 at tall end (north,top) and 2100 low end. Please if you need more info please ask as many Q's as poss AND keep in mind I am in a real hurry to finish as I have customers banging at the door and I basically have no income until, at least, the control room is finished as I have some recordings to mix and a couple of albums to finish.... It's a weird feeling having work but nowhere to do it.
