Hi Johannes, and welcome! Sorry about the delay in replying to your thread: lots of stuff going on. Just a few comments on your post, in more or less the same order you mentioned them:
I had treated it with 10cm thick, heavy, rigid fiberglass
How "heavy"? Optimum density for fiberglass for general absorption is around 30 kg/m3, but there's a range of densities for different needs: lower density is better for low frequencies, higher density is more useful for absorbing high frequencies.
height 1,40m,
Are you talking about the acoustic center o the speakers? That's a bit too high, unless you are really tall or use a very high chair. Standard height is 1.2m.
with 1/12 octave smoothing
You shouldn't really use such high amounts of smoothing, as it tends to hide narrow-band resonance issues, making them invisible. 1/48 is more appropriate.
Side-to-side in the back: 4,25m Side-to-side in the front: 4m Front-to-back left 4,64 Front-to-back right 4,60m The height I measured is 2,59m
That's quite nice, actually. Decent size, reasonable ratio. Should work out well.
I'd say that I mix in between 85-90 db A-weighted most of the time.
It is always better to measure loud levels using "C" weighting, since that more closely models human perception. A weighting is more for quiet sounds.
I remember thinking that 83db C-weighted according to Bob Katz recommendation seemed very loud to me.
83 is the level used for calibrating your system, not necessarily for mixing. Your system should be calibrated and tested using 83 dB (both speakers, or 80 dB each), but you should mix at whatever level you find comfortable. If it is less than 80 dB, that's great.
I have left 10cm space to all the walls, to ensure ventilation of the old structure to prevent mold.
If you suspect that there is a humidity problem in the room, you really should get that fixed first, before you install any treatment.
I threw up a quick design. You can see it in the sketchup pictures. It's not at all detailed.
I noticed! :) I can't even figure out what the plan is there! Which parts are slot walls, which parts are absorbers, which parts are soffits.... Actually, it loos like there are no soffits, since the speakers seem to be mounted on stands. Any reason why you don't want to soffit-mount?
but I imagine it to be a construction/frame of roof battens (the ones they use in theatres) filled with rockwool. I have not decided what the cladding will be.
The cladding should be whatever is needed at each point, in order to create the most neutral environment possible.
I thought I could decide later between cloth and gypsum board with holes in it or a mix of the two.
You could do perf panel, but you would have to calculate the hole sizes and percent coverage according to the frequencies that need treating. And that would involve a lot of drilling! I would suggest doing slot walls: much easier to build, and very effective.
Will the corner in the right be a big problem as to different speaker responses of the two monitors?
It might be, but if you build sealed slot-wall modules with enough mass in them, then that should take the "kinked" corner out of consideration.
Is this big enough trapping to tackle low-end problems?
Perhaps, but I suspect not. You might need more. But it is best to do the basic treatment then measure the room response and see if you need more.
From your experience, will that be a space too small to work in?
The room itself is fine: The dimensions are very close to ITU / EBU specifications. But you do seem to be wasting a lot of space with those "modules". You could make the working area much bigger by making those things smaller: They don't need to be that big.
The possible speaker places are very limited, I think. In my old room I could try out lot's of positions to find one that worked for me. An inch or two always made a big difference. What could give me some of that flexibility?
I would start out with the standard placement and treatment, then see if that can be improved with small changes to positions. Best of all would be to soffit-mount your speakers, of course. - Stuart -

The other thing that bugs me a bit, and maybe it shines through in the first post, that building a fixed soffit in on space seems to me like a hit or miss scenario - and I'm afraid to miss, quite frankly
It's not that hard: the principles are simple, and there are several designs here on the forum that you can just copy, and maybe adapt to your room dimensions, if necessary. At the most basic, a soffit is just a big, thick, heavy plank with a hole cut in the middle for the speaker, and angled across the front wall of the room. That's it.
Actually I do have a question about that: Do I choose a spot for the speakers based on a 60 degree triangle and a listening position or would I try different places with speakers on stands to find a position with even bass and build the soffit there and adjust the listening position accordingly?
Just go with the 60° plan. The reason for "trial and error with speakers on stands" is to find the place where the artifacts are the least objectionable, but if you soffit-mount then most of those artifacts are gone! There's no SBIR, reflections, phasing, comb-filtering or any of the other nasties that you get from speakers on stands. So you don't need to fiddle around finding places that work. Just go with the "standard" geometry, and maybe adjust your chair position for the best location in the sweet spot, once the room is built.

Soundman202016 May 2013#9
I cannot make the soffit meet the ceiling, because it needs ventilation. I can put MDF on top of the frame though.
The normal way of ventilating the soffit is to create a sort of "chimney" up through the insulation, then out through a slot in the front panel, up high, near the ceiling. Some people use "chicken wire" mesh to create that "chimney". Others have used duct board, or even very large PVC pipes. So there's no problem in taking the soffit all the way up to the inner-leaf ceiling. As long as there is a goo free-air path past the back ofthe speakers, they will get enough cooling and ventilation.
Then I'm thinking of not making Slots left and right, because I'll have to get so much MDF already. I would leave the slots so it doesnt eat up all the highs and guides them around the RFZ but reat it like a midrange absorber. In the spaces left and right (between the odd window) I'll put hangers. Sound good or is the slot the better way?
I would go with slots. I'm not sure how you would put hangers in the side walls anyway: there's not much depth there! Just a few inches. You don't have to do the slats in MDF: Any type of wood will do. And you don't have to do them all the way up the entire wall: you could leave a section at the top and bottom that is just absorption, if you want. - Stuart -
