knightfly14 August 2004#4
Agree with Innovations 100% - most of the sleep I lose comes from trying to help people ADAPT rather than purpose-build their rooms. Also, please don't build ANYTHING square - there's only two room shapes worse for acoustics; a perfect cube, and a sphere. Another consideration - your carport is ATTACHED to the house - this is a recipe for flanking noise; any attachment points will transmit structure-borne noise directly to the shell of the house, which will be more than happy to regurgitate it into every room. One good thread to check out for free-standing rooms is Dietcookie's one (it's a "sticky" - called "from shed to studio" - you could scale this up to your needs but keep the same construction techniques; once you decide on a better shape than square, I can calculate spans and stuff for framing sizes... Steve

knightfly15 August 2004#6
If you're going to build a rectangular room, 22 x 19 x 8 works a lot better - more balanced modal response by far. I'd plan on at least a couple of slat resonator wall sections, placed across corners and tuned to some of the typical drum problem frequencies (like around 300 hZ for kick drum) Raising the ceiling to 9 feet doesn't hurt modal distribution, and gives more distance to the ceiling for overhead mics. For acoustically good rooms, you need to stop thinking like a carpenter - all those even multiples of 4 feet, parallel walls, etc, are great for easy construction, but SUCK for acoustics... Steve

knightfly16 August 2004#9
Those would be final, INSIDE dimensions between hard surfaces - so the outer shell would be larger. If using blocks, consider having them filled with sand and doing an inner, lighter wall of maybe one layer of 15mm drywall on metal studs placed about 8" inside the concrete, and filled between with insulation - this construction, if carried out as well for ceiling, etc, can get you about STC 80, with TL over 40 dB clear down to 50 hZ - if you can match this with the ceiling construction, you could thrash your drums to pieces and by the time the sound got inside the house, it should be totally inaudible. Granted, a ceiling that good would be tough to build - not to mention doors (a sound lock would be mandatory) windows (pricey for sure) - the good news is, with just single layer wallboard inside you'd need less bass trapping to get good sounding drum tracks in there, since the wallboard would act as a panel trap for lower frequencies. Hope that helped clarify a few things... Steve
