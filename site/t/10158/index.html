Rod, your book is amazing, I have found it very helpful with my build. I'm currently building a home with a basement studio and I have a quick question about the section in your book which covers adding mass to the existing floor above the room prior to insulation and adding a resilient channel ceiling with RSIC clips. It mentions in the book that the process would be to secure the drywall to the above floor with finishing nails, then calk and finish off with a 1X3 support when all the layers are up. It seems like it would be easier to just use drywall screws to secure it to the ceiling first as opposed to the finishing nails, is there a loss in isolation by doing this? There is a reason Rod recommends what he does. Yes it would be simpler to just use drywall screws. I don't recall the exact reasons off the top of my head, but IIRC you want each sheet of drywall to be independent in terms of mass, as opposed to acting as one giant piece of mass, which is what would happen if you screwed them all together to the floor above. Double-check the book, I think Rod explains his reasoning there. Of course I could be wrong, I'm going from my (notoriously unreliable) memory here.... :wink:
gullfo10 February 2008#5
one issue is the flex from the floor above. rigidly attaching the drywall (which has a different elasticity than the wood flooring and joists) could cause the screws to tear through the drywall. the cleats hold the drywall up without binding it (except via soft flexible caulk) so the floor can bend and even if the drywall is cracked due to flex its still has most of its integrity - side to side - and so the cleats should continue to work. if you have a lot of flex or nails cutting through the subfloor, using some celotex or blueboard on the underside of the floor before adding the drywall gives a bit more space for the floor to flex and should not create a significant 3rd leaf effect.
