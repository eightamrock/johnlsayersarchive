Okay so I read the thread about combining hangers with superchunks, but it wasnt quite what I meant and it didnt really have a definitive answer. However, quoted from SAE:
Acoustic Hangers. This is a system of fibre board panels that are wrapped with insulation and are hung freely using wire or rope. The large hangers 1.8m x 500mm work in the low frequency range whilst the panels 1.2m x 300mm effect the low mid frequencies. It is common to have up to a 1.2m space at the rear of the control room with the large hangers whilst the smaller hangers are effective if suspended in the ceiling cavity created by a false ceiling.

gullfo14 October 2007#8
you're actually using a dense fiberglass for the superchunk and using a lighter insulation on light density particle board, osb , or plywood for the hangers. generally the hangers need a proper cavity to sit in to be effective. superchunks can work in just a corner. another option is to use a polycylindrical diffuser across the corner lined with rigid fiberglass, and optionally perforated to add some additional absorption.

gullfo3 January 2008#10
generally, polys diffuse above 500hz, and they absorb (depending on the cavity space) below 300hz. (Everest MHoA pg 204 or thereabouts). sealing top and bottom is good to improve the panel absorption characteristics and adding a layer or two of insulation inside the cavity is also good.
