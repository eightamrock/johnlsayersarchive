BIG81 May 2008#8
Sure, first, let me say that room calculators are meant for rectangular rooms. yours is not (neither is mine) but the Height axial modes and the width (the longest dimension in the horizontal plane, in your case) will show. The length ones will be a little tricky, though, since you have two different lengths, one of which your thinking of using for monitoring>> needs complicated modelling which I don't know how to do. Assuming you had a rectangular existing room of sufficient volume, you could use a good mode calculators that would show you the axial, tangential and oblique modes like this one to: 1) modify one of the dimensions of your room, if space permits, that will get you to a preferred room ratio, for instance, the length. 2) do sine sweep tests in your untreated room, so that you can identify from the frequency response which of the peaks/dips can be attributable to room modes (results from mode calculator), and which may be due to other things (first reflections, resonance...), and then 3) decide if the effect of the modes on the response is acceptable for your needs (in which case you would only need to deal with first reflection and reverberation time), if not, 4) is broadband treatment suitable to deal with it (peaks & dips of a few dBs), or would you need narrow band tuned treatment. 5) if broadband can deal with it, it be good to know which dimension (L,W,H) is responsible for the modes you want to treat, so that you can emphasize treatment in the concerned corners (eg:if length, you could emphasize the broadband trapping on the horizontal wall to ceiling corners across the width, besides treating the 4 vertical wall to wall corners) ************ if you first treat/add treatment to your room, and later test it out, you may find out that some/most of the treatment was redundant and you may need to take some of it down to make way for tuned treatment. It may also be that your room was already good, to start with, that you didn't need most of the treatment you added. :lol: Best to have an idea of where you going before going there. Cheers, Jean

Jean, mode calculators are not a substitute for measuring a room! When the goal is to treat a room you measure it. Anything else is just guessing. I assume you missed that thread a few years ago at Recording.org where I showed some measured versus predicted mode frequencies disagreeing by 20 percent? Hence my correct advice that mode calculators are meant for designing new rooms. Even measuring isn't needed for rooms like the one in this thread.
