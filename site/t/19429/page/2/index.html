Soundman202019 May 2014#35
So, I used the calibration noise (white or whatever) and made sure that the level was about 80-81dB. Then I made a measurement (link below) but it still measures 130-ish dBs?
Did you actually tell REW that the level was 80 dB? I mean, you saw that you were getting 80 dB on your hand-held meter, and that's great, but then you also have to notify REW that the level is 80 dB: You do that with the "SPL meter" tool, click on "Calibrate", select "Use REW speaker cal signal", then REW will play the test signals and also opens a window called "SPL Reading calibration" where you type in the reading that you are seeing on your hand-held meter. Then click "Finished", and conform that both your hand-held meter and the REW meter are showing the same readings. If you did that, then it is not possible that REW shows 130 dB while your hand-held meter shows 80. It just can't happen. When you do the above procedure, make sure that both the REW "SPL Meter" tool and your hand-held meter are both set to "C" weighting and "Slow" response. After you have done that, then repeat the loop-back calibration procedure for your interface: there might be an issue there too. - Stuart -

Soundman202020 May 2014#44
I am in the middle of removing the plastic layer from the back and the side walls...
:shock: :!: Whoa! Hang on there! I'm not sure why you are doing that, since you are still a LONG way from completing the treatment of the room! Treatment is a complete system, not just a bunch of individual parts, and judging from the photos you only have about half of your treatment in place (perhaps less). The rest of the treatment will also be affecting the highs, so you will likely end up with a room that sounds too dead if you remove the plastic. What you are doing is sort of like taking the filling out of a cake you are making because you don't like the way it tastes at present, but you haven't even baked the cake yet and you still need to put in a bunch of ingredients.... :) Of course it tastes bad right now! You have to add ALL the ingredients and follow ALL the procedure, before you can taste it the way it is supposed to be... It's also like taking the drums out of a mix you are working on because you don't like the way it sounds right now, but you haven't recorded the bass or vocals yet, and you haven't done any EQ on the guitars or applied any dynamics either.... It is way too early to decide that the drums sound bad until you hear them in the complete context of the full mix. Same here: it is way too early to decide that you don't need the plastic when you have not yet heard how the room sounds as a whole with all the treatment completed and in place... --- I haven't had a chance to take a look at your latest REW data, but I hope I'll be able to do that in a few hours, and then suggest the best path for treatment. - Stuart -

Soundman202021 May 2014#46
OK, I had some time to look at your data.
...or am I missing something here?
Well, you have data smoothing set to 1/3 octave, so you are probably missing about 90% of the useful information in the REW data! Set it to "no smoothing", or "1/48" smoothing only.
calibration done (twice for it to actually be effective) and new measurements made:
Excellent! The levels are believable now, and that strange "dip and rise" in the mid range decay has now disappeared, so that's good. But I'm still concerned about how you are getting such flat response at 20 KHz, with no roll-off evident. That's hard to understand, especially when you have your bass traps and some other absorption in the room already, and it basically does not change at all when you add and remove plastic and carpet... There's something going on there that doesn't make a lot of sense. Typically in a control room, SPL response rolls off above about 8 kHz at about 3 to 6 dB per octave (and it should!) But yours does not. In fact, you have a rise of about 5 dB in your high end: no wonder the room sounds strange to your ears! On the other hand, you have a roll-off in your low end, also of about 5 dB. That will also add to the overly live sound of the room. Basically, the entire frequency response is badly skewed towards the high end, with a difference of about 10 dB. In other words, your high frequencies are about twice as loud as your low frequencies: That is probably why you have the sensation that there is way too much high end in the room. But I don't think the problem isn't the room, since I can't see how it could do that: it seems to me that there is something wrong with the way the speakers and / or signal chain is set up. What controls do you have on the back of your speaker, and how do you have them set? What else do you have in the signal chain between your DAW and the speaker? Is there any type of EQ in there?
The RT60 clearly shows the high-mids response time lengthening when the plastic is on
Yes, but that is very likely because the highs are over-boosted to start with! You first have to solve the problem of why your speakers are putting out ten time more energy in the highs than in the lows, before you can say anything definite about the room treatment.
Left wall upper half not covered with plastic
I see a LOT of insulation in there, but no studs: how is that wall built? What is behind the insulation, and what is behind that? How thick is the insulation? What type of insulation is it? (especially the density). I get the impression that something is absorbing a lot of energy around 500 Hz, and that is also skewing the room response, and that is in addition to the overall major skewing in the frequency response of your system. - Stuart -
