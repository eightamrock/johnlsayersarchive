knightfly28 November 2004#51
I would just use the packed rock wool covered in cloth in that case; that extra 20 foot path (round trip from your head back to your head) is borderline for diffusion in most recommendations. A "slatted" bass trap is actually a helmholtz resonator, unless it's not sealed at the slats, in which case it's an un-estimable thing - removing the slats turns it into a wide-band absorber. If you've done no homework at all, and therefore ended up with a GROSS modal imbalance, or if you just want the room brighter and still have need for more LF absorption, then slats in that particular location could be used with little bad effect - this because the reflection path would be (from your head) to the (approximately) 45 degree surface of the slats, then to the floor somewhere in the rear of the room - by the time these specular reflections reached your ears again, they would be called "reverb" - Back to the gross modal imbalance - if for some reason your room had equal, or even multiple, dimensions - you could end up with modes that are "stacked up" and therefore causing serious problems at those frequencies - in that case, you would be in need of frequency-specific absorption, and should calculate a helmholtz that would help tame those modes - I'm not sure how well it would work to use varying slat/slot width in a slat absorber when you want broader bandwidth action - John's done this in several rooms with apparent success, although I've not seen any measurements - however, with two of the Aussie equivalent of a Grammie awards, it's probably safe to say that John "don' NEED no steenking measurements"... From a more analytical approach, Barefoot presented some logical reasoning as to why the variable approach shouldn't work too well - briefly, it involves the size of LF waves (huge) vs. the surface of the absorber (not so huge) along with the need for individual septums between slatted areas to define cavity depth in a corner absorber (still not sure I buy that one, but no data available either way) As a general rule, however, it's hard to go wrong with broadband corner absorbers (no slats) - if you build them right (everything but the slats) and things are too dull-sounding, you could add slats later (calculated for worst MEASURED frequency imbalance) - That probably makes the most sense; I doubt there are more than a couple acousticians on the planet (certainly not ME by a long shot) who can accurately predict what any two "identical" rooms will sound like until they are complete - there are just too many variables involved. Even acoustic labs can't agree from lab to lab what you get with different constructions - some results lab-to-lab for so-called identical wall TL values (for example) have varied by 10 dB or so. My (somewhat simplistic) approach to all this is to take more Excedrin, build with the knowledge I have available, then tweak to satisfaction. I already spend too much time obsessing over what-ifs, and not enough time caressing my favorite musical instrument(s)... Steve

Arthur Rice13 October 2008#65
Hello fellow builders... Just thought I'd add a couple things I've learned after several years of great service from my build. My only regret is cooling the place... I doubled what I thought and the HVAC guy thought I'd need but I should have doubled it again. It's ok most of the time if I'm working alone or with one or two more people but if you add just a couple more it gets really hard to control the air flow (unless you frost the windows in the rest of the house). I ended up adding an extra unit just for the studio, which would have been alot easier if I'd done it from the beginning. So I thought I'd add this for those that are at that stage to ponder their next move. ADD MORE AIR!!!! Also, an obsevation about my soffit mounted speakers... it's the only way to go. They sounded great from the beginning, about 2 years ago I added a sub to my system and they even sounded better. My advice is to go that route if you have the space. That's it for me, just thought I'd share some real world things that have come up on my end. Other than that it was a great experiance and I ended up with a great studio that I love working in everyday. It's great to get paid for something you'd do for free!!!
