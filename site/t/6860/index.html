Great Forum :) I am in the process of getting a small studio built under my brick house. It is a very small space (the control room is 3 x 4 metres, with a 2 x 1.4 metre booth.) The ceiling height will only be 1.95 metres, once the ceiling is soundproofed. If I build a typical two leaf stud/double plasterboard system wall, I worry that I will lose a lot of my limited floor space. I also worry about white ants infiltrating such a system ( I am in Brisbane, Australia.) I also worry about security. I am worried about most things:) Isn't it easy to kick in such a wall though? I guess I would prefer a one-layer concrete wall. My question is this... What is my best option in terms of materials for the walls? I want good sound proofing, but also some absorbancy so I'm not left with an impossibly reverberant space. My choices seem to be Besser block, Besser block filled with sand or aerated concrete (like Hebel block). The Besser block has an advertised STC of 50. Concrete-filled Besser block is rated 55, while cavity Hebel panels are rated at 58. The Hebel looks promising, but will still require rendering or a gyprock layer on the inside and I worry the cost will build up. Maybe I should persist with the cheap, traditional stud, two leaf gyprock system wall with rockwool in between, that everyone is so keen on in this forum. Any ideas would be hugely appreciated:) I don't want to make a costly mistake here. Thank you. Ross Mac
diziet21 August 2006#2
This is one of the dilemmas I've faced when trying to design my garage stoodio :roll: http://forum.studiotips.com/viewtopic.php?t=2203 I've thought about a large number of different designs. :idea: Here in the UK I've been told that cost wise a masonry wall and stud wall are equivalent. There is an issue, if you have a wall cavity about ensuring you do not get condensation. the following document will be useful in deciding whether to go for the complete double skinned wall or to go with a concrete wall: http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/pubs/fulltext/ir586.pdf The NRC has quite a large number of documents that are worthwhile reading, including: http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/pubs/fulltext/ir761/ir761.pdf Also, don't rely too much on NRC numbers. They do not take into account low frequency performance (which may be critical depending on the type of noise you're making!!) :D In particular I noticed that masonry walls with plasterboard attached by RC were not nearly as effective at low frequencies as a double masonry wall. Hope this helps

diziet wrote:
. . . don't rely too much on NRC numbers. They do not take into account low frequency performance (which may be critical depending on the type of noise you're making!!)
I think you meant to say, "Don't rely too much on STC numbers." NRC stands for National Resource Council, a Canadian organization that includes the Institute for Research in Construction (IRC). We love those guys!! 8) 8) 8) STC stands for Sound Transmission Class. That is the extremely flawed single-number measurement that you want to take with big, fat grains of salt. The awesome IRC reports provide both STC ratings as well as critically important performance charts that span a spectrum much larger than that which is accounted for in STC ratings. Learn why STC is a joke in any kind of studio construction context. --Keith :mrgreen:

lobe3 January 2007#6
Well...it's a couple of months later and I'm impressed with the results at this stage. The rooms are now built in 110 mm core-filled Besser block. The ceiling is made of two layers of contrasting thickness Gyprock on isolating mounts with Soundstop insulation in the cavity between the upper floorboards. Parallelism has been avoided by building 6 degree angled walls. And there are Raven-brand sealed, non-parallel double doors in the entry and between the studio and booth. The control room is deathly silent. I will not need to build an inner room. I can just hear my wife if she is shouting outside. The isolation between control room and booth is not as good, probably because of the glass windows in the doors, but it is still pretty damned impressive and will be improved when I spend more time sealing the doors with Firesound acoustic caulk. I may add a plasterboard floating room at some stage, but only if I really need to. Most of the instruments I will record will be acoustic strings and vox. The ceiling isolation is great too. The only way you can hear music playing loudly downstairs is if you put your ear to the upstairs walls. As I was warned though, there is a problem with vibration-borne sound, because of the use of masonry and its excellent ability to conduct structural noise, so if my wife stomps about upstairs or puts on the dishwasher or ceiling fans, there are definite, annoying rumbles in the studio. Oh well...I was warned. She'll just have to contain her urge to stomp about and clean when I'm doing a recording session. The other downer is the ceiling height, which is generally 205mm, but is as low as 195 in the middle of the control room. I will be creating ceiling absorbers to tame the ceiling to floor parallelism, but I worry that I will have some major bass problems with such a low roof. Unfortunately, I was unable to build down to legal height because of drainage gradient issues. At this stage I can't hear bass problems In fact, the rooms sound overly bright, but I'll keep you posted. As to be expected from masonry walls, the rooms are very echoey, so my next step is to build bass traps for the corners, absorbers for the walls, throw down a few rugs and see if I can make the room sound dryer and flatter. I've had two glass brick windows installed in the very rear of the control room, to let in lots of natural light and hopefully let out some bass. The glassbricks are great. They are very soundproof, let in lots of light, but stop people seeing in easily. I've had track lighting installed too, the low voltage ones the forum suggested - and I have had formwork trays built into the slab for my wiring. I haven't yet tried out the lights with audio wiring in place. I hope there are no buzzes. My electrician ensured me he had isolated the room's power as much as he could. We shall see...I mean hear the results in the coming week. I will be getting the audio wired up as soon as I finish painting the rooms. When I finish the project I will post some pics. I might even try putting some mp3s to demonstrate the sound isolation. Thanks for this brilliant forum. It has been incredible!

lobe17 July 2007#12
Good questions. The Besser blocks are a good, secure outer layer and as long as you are building a stud/insulation room within a room inside this, then the besser block outer shell is a good option. Concrete does tend to transmit sound better than wood so keep this in mind too. As for materials, I used insulco semi rigid for the wall absorbers which I got from a place near the Logan Hospital (can't remember the name...something like Discount Insulation.) I used regular pink batts to go inside the plasterboard/stud ceiling. If I was doing the project again, I would put 50mm electrical conduit under the slab before it was poured, and have all my wires in there already. Snaking through wires was a real pain in the #$%%^. I would also have dug deeper to give myself more headroom. Hope this helps. Ross.
