QUESTION: How do I calculate the whole system with both leaves of the cavity wall? Is that a triple leaf?
Technically, your existing concrete block cavity wall is a coupled two-leafed system. Even though there's a cavity in there, I'd bet good money that the two walls are are not decoupled: Very likely there are metal ties across it, there are possible also blobs of mortar creating bridges, and in any event there flanking paths at the top, bottom and both ends. So basically it acts as a single leaf... mostly! (sort of). For your calculations, it's is probably fine to consider it as a single leaf. I doubt the air cavity is going to make a lot of difference. You could do a check as if it were a 3-leaf system, but my guess is that the difference will be minimal.
inner leaf of 6"x 2" wood frame filled with insulation (pink fiberglass or rockwool, not sure yet) with a double layer of plasterboard,
That implies you are building your inner-leaf "inside out". Correct? IN other words, you are building your inner-leaf wall with the plasterboard (drywall) facing the wall cavity, and the studs facing the room. Is that correct? - Stuart -
