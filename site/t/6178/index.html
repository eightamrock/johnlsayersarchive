Something to think about is that the floor is a "corner of a room" and corners are where bass build up occurrs - so moving bass traps off the floor isn't necessarily the best idea... Check out Ethan Winer's video to get some ideas about layout for panel traps: http://www.realtraps.com/videos.htm also these may help: http://www.realtraps.com/placing_mt.htm http://www.realtraps.com/install_mt.htm And unless you make the large room very dead, with absorption on all surfaces, you are going to have serious reverberation issues at your mix position. cheers, chris

guitardad729 May 2006#10
simply pull the wires out the wall and caulk them tight.
Not that simple, I don’t think that will meet code. From the wall to the box the wire should be in conduit pipe or "racetrack" or other covering. I’ve got no specific electric code advice to quote, but when I suggested running wire in conduit like this with a local inspector, this is where he said a big yes. Even though the wire is behind something… that “something” is not a real wall. Also caulking between conduit pipe and drywall will make me feel much safer then caulking standard household wire. Bottom line household electric wire 12 gauge or 14 gauge or other should never be exposed and that is code. AND note attaching boxes to frame work of absorber is not my idea, like many other things I can't remember where on this web site I read that, but it's here somewhere. 8) Also another page from Ethan, http://www.ethanwiner.com/acoustics.html#bass%20traps AND note on this page, better pic of Ethan’s room. All though the rear is not as wide as tmix's room, it’s still an “L” shaped room. http://www.ethanwiner.com/music.html Symmetry and RFZ at listening position in CR is required; the rear of the room can bend Symmetry a bit, though not recommended because of loss of symmetry in the rear of room (couch where client sits) IMO.
