Cram17 March 2008#7
pianojam wrote:
Humidity can be horrible to acoustic guitars and pianos. Maybe any guitar. bowed necks, rusty strings, mold on the walls... Of course I hail from Louisiana, where it's almost always above 80% humidity outside. So YMMV
Clarification - Humidity variations can be damaging to the woodof our instruments. I live in the Northeast of the US and keeping the instruments in our basement is actually preferrable because the relative humidity stays within 50-70%. Basments typically are bufferred from environmental temp and pressure changes unless they are half exposed to the ground (walk out basement). You're asking about holes for ventilation of a basement. You've indicated that the holes have no ventilation control - or that they don't have a forced air mechanism. Given those two interests, I'd recommend plugging them up when and if your buddy wants to have work done. Your location is Belgium and the seasonal change there would fluctuate. I think taking advantage of a basement bufferring those changes would be good. Less airflow to that area may be better. If you are really going to do a room within a room construction, I think those should definately be plugged as cold air could flow in which would increase condensation behind the wall board. The construction would be - ------------------------ framing & insulation - vapor barrier (plastic) wall board ------------------------ I believe you are the same person who asked about the requirements from the owner for not affixing anything to the deck above or even the floors & walls of the concrete. I think that issue supercedes this one.
