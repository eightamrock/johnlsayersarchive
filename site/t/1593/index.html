dymaxian28 April 2004#3
Greetings! Just from looking at the photo, I can see a couple things... First of all, placement of the desk. That's what's causing the left speaker to be louder- it's getting reinforcement from that corner. How to fix this; try to get your desk, and especially your listening position, in a spot where the left and right walls are symmetrical. If it's not, you're going to have trouble with your stereo imaging. I see what looks like a door/opening to the right on that photo- if that's the case, then moving the desk to the center of that wall really won't do too much to help you out. If there's another way to make the room symmetrical (or you can install a door there somehow) it'd help a LOT. Second of all, your walls are all reflective. This means that sound coming out of your speakers are hitting the wall and coming back at your ears, just milliseconds behind the direct sound. This causes comb filtering the likes of which will make it really REALLY tough to mix in this room. How to fix this; hang rigid fiberglass insulation on the walls at the spots where sound would reflect directly from the speakers to your ears. This will absorb those reflections and prevent them from reaching your ears. Finding these spots is easy- sit in your listening position and have a friend or significant other hold a mirror flat against the wall. Have them move it around until you can see the speakers- those are the places that need absorption. Third (not able to see this from the photo) do you have any low-frequency absorption in the room? I'm guessing you don't, since you don't have anything to absorp hi-freq early reflections as mentioned in #2, and absorbing bass is trickier. If you don't have any low-freq control, your room is going to project way, way more low end than your mixes actually have. To test this theory, try this- play just a single, held-out low bass guitar note thru your speakers, get up and walk around. I'd be willing to bet a box of 703 that you'll hear the volume change as you walk around. Some spots will be louder, some quieter. If there's a quiet spot where you're mixing, you could be adding as much as 10db of low end to your mix at any given frequency! If the low end of your mixes have been tough to get right, this is the reason right here. How to fix it; depends on how far you're willing to go. Ethan Winer has 2 suggestions I'll relay to you. One is to strap 1" or 2" rigid fiberglass insulation (as mentioned above) across all the corners you can in the room. Low freq sound builds up in the corners, so that's the best place to put the stuff to absorb it. The other thing he recommends is building wood panel traps to hang against your walls. Go to http://www.ethanwiner.com/acoustics.html and you'll get a good grasp on both these ideas. If you're at all handy with a hammer, you can do this stuff yourself. This is just what I see from the photo. But these three things alone would make a huge difference to the sound of your room. It looks pretty much untreated at the moment. Hope this helps... If you do some work on the rest of the room, having speakers close to the wall isn't necessarily a bad thing. It just means you'll have to compensate for it. If your speakers consistently lack low end, then this might actually help you out a little. When you get your room treated, and have the desk about where it'll be, play back a commerical CD that you're really familiar with, and tweak the EQ on the speakers until the lows sound natural to you.
