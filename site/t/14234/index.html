Death By Darwin wrote:
Maybe I should think about building the inner-leaf on the floor like I am planning on doing in the live room
Yes it's all about damping inside the mediums and maximizing impedance at joints. I neither have the practice to know nor the knowledge to calculate it, but I would recommend to build one wall on the floor if you have room to do so. On the other side, concerning room acoustics the smaller room may be of more problem than a little bit less insulation?!
I am still a little confused on how to analyze the results of the room mode graphs. ... I looked at some of the known good rooms in the program briefly but the differences were hard to observe, at least for a noob :wink:
As the number of modes is fix as long as you have your boundaries, you want the negative effects to be as small as possible. Our intent is to achieve a mode distribution that is as equal as possible. A number of modes close together will boost the reverberation time in this frequency range (less damping = longer lasting energy). Because of the same number of modes in every rectangular room such a accumulation will lead to other frequency ranges with wider gaps between the modes resulting in a much shorter reverberation time. What you want is similar reverberation in different frequency ranges...therefore...equal distribution. good luck :)

Soundman20207 July 2010#22
Greater angles, and more rays. One often-mentioned "trick" for finding first reflection points in an already-finished room is for you to sit at the mix position while you get a helper to hold a mirror up against the walls and move it around all over the place. Any place where you can see either of the speakers reflected in the mirror is a first reflection point. And that includes the ceiling, too. I think that's what Andre means with his comment: You can work backwards from the listening position towards the speaker, not just forwards from the speakers towards the listening position. Also, even though speakers do tend to focus the highs in tighter "cones" than the lows, the do still spit out highs at large angles. Yes, highs are more directional and you hear them better on-axis, but even at fairly large off-axis angles you still get some highs, just at a very much reduced level. And since you are soffit-mounting your speakers, lows won't be too much of a problem (provided that you compensate for the half-space boost, of course!) My $0.02. - Stuart -
