Hi there " alalal2015", and welcome! :)
I'm wondering whether it would be worth using resilient tape on the stud walls on its own. Ie without a resilient bar system as well (I think this would lower the ceiling/bring the walls out too much)
Resilient channel is only a bit more than an inch thick. It takes up very little space. In fact, if you want good isolation, then "space" is an important part of that. The equations that define how much isolation you get at low frequencies have only three variables in them: 1) the mass of the wall surfaces (surface density) on each side of the cavity, 2) the depth of the air gap inside the cavity, and 3) whether or not you put insulation in the air cavity. In all cases, increasing the numbers improves isolation. More air gap, more mass, and more insulation all increase the isolation. So trying to save space on wall cavity depth, is not a good idea if you need good isolation.
I'm wondering whether it would be worth using resilient tape on the stud walls on its own.
Practically no use, for one simple reason: you still have to nail through that, and each nail is a solid, hard, rigid flanking path that short-circuits the tape, bypassing it entirely, and transferring all vibrations from the drywall into the stud. Not very effective for decoupling. On the other hand, resilient channel really does decouple, when used correctly, since there are no solid, hard, rigid connections between the drywall and the stud.
Someone on another forum suggested it might help, but limited.
VERY limited. Not even measurable, probably.
The question is whether it is worth doing at all?
My opinion: No. A waste of time, and a waste of money.

Yes, for that application O would use the tape. That's what it is meant for. I would also add a second layer of subfloor, for extra mass, then put something like laminated flooring on top. You are not going to be doing anything that creates floor impact or high levels of floor vibrations (no drums or bass, for example), so you should be good with that. If you ever do need to do something liek that, then just build a simple "drum riser" on top. - Stuart -
