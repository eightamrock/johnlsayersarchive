knightfly22 September 2004#11
Yeah, given the circumstances I'd use all the air space and mass you can - if the garage door is down, even 3 feet away it will act somewhat as a third leaf and lessen your isolation, especially at lower frequencies. BTW, typical shell resonance for kick drums is around 300 hZ, but the thud from a loose-tuned kick drum head is lower than that. More like around 30 hZ I would think. Containing that will be interesting for sure - you'll need air gaps in the order of 12" or better, with at least 5 total layers of wallboard, to get decent low freq isolation. This would include a floated floor... Steve

knightfly23 September 2004#13
Right, like 3 layers out, 2 layers in - this would make each leaf have different coincidence and mass in order to smooth out the TL curve (no common resonant frequencies on both sides of the wall) For the door - if you have room for a slanted ceiling, you could accomplish both leaves on one frame and still leave access at the front of the door - you especially don't want to close in the spring at the top of the door; when that breaks (and it will, eventually) the door guy will need good access, especially to the adjuster collars (these are at the ends of the spring, big thick "washers" with holes every 90 degrees around them - the door guy has two rods that just fit these holes, and after installing a new spring he needs to be able to "wind up" the spring to achieve balance between spring tension and door weight, so you can actually open the door. Some doors are so heavy, that when this spring breaks you need pry bars to get the door to move. knightfly24 September 2004#15
If you can somehow swing a 3.5" floated concrete slab over 2x4's on 1/2" EPDM pucks, your mass-air-mass resonance should be down around 18 hZ, without including the weight of walls or ceiling - it's not likely you can get away with less than 2x6 ceiling joists, as 2x4's are only good for a span of 9'10", even using 12" centers. You want your floor resonance as low as you can get it, 10 hZ would be a good target resonance for drums if you can get it - the resonance needs to be a couple octaves below the lowest frequency isolated, or it doesn't work well. For maximum isolation, you might be better off "stepping" the ceiling in order to maintain the most headroom in the most part of the room, instead of sloping the ceiling. Generally, your two leaves of mass for ceiling will probably end up being the addition of an extra layer of wallboard to the existing ceiling, then new joists below that (just barely) with full insulation and 2-3 layers more wallboard. Your attic is likely vented, so can't be considered as the second leaf - the fairly large volume in the attic will lessen the effect of having a third leaf, but you would still need a total of 5 layers in two leaves for good isolation of drums. Same with the walls... Steve

knightfly10 October 2004#23
Not too well - it's much better for stereo imaging if your ceilng rises to the rear as you face the speakers; otherwise, you get reflections from the ceiling back to the mix position. These will be out of phase in differing degrees at different frequencies; in other words, it will mess up the sound field. Does it look like you could replace that metal access door with something more massive, like a 2x4 frame with two layers of drywall on them? Your lower, new ceiling would also need this - then, getting access would mean lifting the lower "trap door" up out of the way, repeating that with the upper one, and climbing up through the hole. The good news is, 2x4 trusses are generally strong enough so you wouldn't have to worry about adding a second layer of drywall to that existing ceiling - since the lower ceiling wouldn't be trussed, you would need to use 2x6's for the lower ceiling joists. For two layers of 5/8 wallboard, these would span 10'8" using 24" centers, 13'0" using 16" centers, and 15'0" using 12" centers. You could stretch those distances by about 3 feet each if you don't exceed 2 layers of drywall, so probably 16" centers would work for you, they max out at 17'8" for 5 pound dead load - that's the weight of the frame AND all attachments per square foot, 5/8" drywall weighs about 2.3 lbs/sf and framing would weigh about 1.5 lbs/sf, so that would be stretching it a little. 16 foot span should be OK though... Steve
