Hi "Magic". Welcome! Your basic plan seems reasonable, as far as can be seen from the basic sketches, but it would be better to see that as a more complete model in SketchUp, to be sure! Just a couple of comments: 1) Why such a huge sound lock? Lots of wasted space there. 2) It might be an idea to put the vocal booth outside the live room, so you can use them independently. More flexibility for a big facility like that. 3) You say:
A-Room use is for recording/mixing of hip hop vocals, and latin or rock bands.
... which implies live recordings of things like drums, percussion, brass, etc. in the live room, correct? So isolation is a major point here? What is your target figure for isolation? 4) You seem to have some flanking issues, and possible mult-leaf problems, but it's hard to figure out from that line drawing. If you could re-do that in SketchUp and post the SKP here, it would make it a lot easier to check. 5) " ... with cement blocks, with a clean concrete flooring, and 16 ft high concrete ceiling. " Great! You have a really good outer leaf there. Lucky guys! Are those cement blocks solid or hollow? If hollow, are they sand-filled? 6) But then you say this:
Control Room - 15 ft Wide x 18 ft Length x 10 ft High Live Room - 16 ft Wide x 12 ft Length x 10 ft High Vox / Iso Booth - 5 ft Wide x 7 ft Length x 10 ft High
... so if you have 16 feet of headroom, why are you limiting your rooms, particularly the live room, to only ten feet? That isn't logical. I'd rather go for more height in the live room, even if it means changing another dimension to avoid modal issues. 7) Budget:
We yet don't have a defined budget set for the project, but it will be something $10,000+.
That's just for the "A" control room by itself, right? Not including live room, booth and sound lock? Even then, it's only US$ 37 per square foot. Rather on the low side, I'd say. Dis you consider things like HVAC, electrical installation, permits, and inspections when you came up with that figure? Those are often over looked in estimating costs. (HVAC alone for that room could cost a third to half of that...) 8) Glass:
as well as we have the glass used for both the live and vox booth from our old place.
What type of glass? How thick? You need to know that in order to ensure that it matches your proposed surface density for the inner leaf walls and ceilings. If it doesn't match, then you won't be able to use it. 9) Doors: You didn't mention them. What are your plans there? It looks like you need at least seven doors, based on that layout, for the CR, LR both and sound lock. Figure at least US$ 500 to build each one yourself (probably more), or a couple of thousand each to buy them ready made. That's a large chunk of your budget gone, right there. 10) You say:
we had planned to use 2x6 wood studs for framing, using acoustical sealant under the wall panel panes, with a 6 inch air gap all around the outer leaf as well as between the rooms.
2x6 studs plus an extra 6" gap between studs and block wall gives you over 11" of air space, not 6". Or are you planning to build inside-out? For the amount of isolation that you seem to need, 11" is good, 6" not so good. 11) That leads to another couple of questions: how much isolation (transmission loss) are you shooting for with that plan? And what resonant frequency are you planning to tune the walls for? 12) "... Use of either R-19 or higher rating for the in wall insulation. ..." What matters for acoustics is not the R-rating, but the gas flow resistivity. Most manufacturers don't bother measuring or publishing that, but the density can give you a clue about it: for fiberglass, you should be using something in the range around 30 kg/m3 for that application. More like 50 kg/m3 if you go with mineral wool instead. 13) "... 2 layers of sheet rock/gypsum board ..." What type? How thick? Those are rather major points. 14) "... 2x6 studs for ceilings ..." If they are on the ceiling, then they are "joists" not "studs". But did you check the structural calculations on that? Can you span those distances with 2x6 joists supporting that load? What type of wood? I didn't check, but I have a feeling that 2x6 joists won't cut it for spanning 15 feet with a two or three layers of 5/8" fire-rated drywall, plus several inches of insulation, plus HVAC, plus electrical, plus.... 15) "... And maybe 2 layers of either marine board, or some type of high density plywood for flooring (leaving a small gap around room for filling with backer rod and acoustic sealant. ..." Why? You said you have a good concrete floor already. Why do you want to cover it with plywood? You could just stain it artistically, and have a great floor at very little extra cost. Or if it is in bad shape, then just lay laminate flooring over it. I don't understand the need for "marine board" or "high density plywood". 16) "... I've also attached a rough floor plan showing what we planned on for the angled acoustic walls, traps for the rooms...." There seem to be several issues there. What do you mean by "angled acoustic walls"? You already have your inner leaf, so those "angled acoustic walls" cannot be solid. What do you have in mind? Just cloth? If you add additional solid walls then you will create a three-leaf system, which reduces isolation in the low frequencies. 17) Your speaker soffits angles are way wrong: the acoustic axes seem to be aimed at about 90% of room depth, instead of the correct 38% position, which is where your mix position should be. I also don't see the bass traps you mentioned: There's nothing big enough in that rear section to be usable as a bass trap, and your door is in the location where the right rear bass trap should go. 18) " .. Still a little confused on which insulation thickness (2 inch rigid i guess) ...". Depends! :) You'll most likely be using several different types and thicknesses in different places. But not rigid. Semi-rigid 703 is about the best for acoustics. 19) "... angles, monitor soffit mounting (plan on mounting a PA system for playback only)..." :shock: :!: PA speakers in the "A" control room of a top studio? :shock: Ummmm.... Why would you do that? Why not invest in some decent studio monitors? A nice pair of Adam A8X's, for example, would most likely go well in that size room, and shouldn't break the bank. But PA speakers????? Sorry, but I just don't understand the logic of doing that. It would be kind of like buying a Porsche, then putting tractor tires on it.... :!: :D 20) "... and control room back wall panel design/construction such as bass traps/hangers, or if diffusion is needed. ..." The room is too small for most types of diffusion to be useful. Diffusers are more for large rooms. You'll probably be doing mostly absorption, hangers, slot walls, and suchlike. Maybe panel traps, but hopefully not.
Construction wont start for another 2 months or so, so I hope I use this time wisely to design and construct a great sounding room.
2 months is a little short to do a full design for a major studio like that, except maybe for an experienced studio designer. That sounds a little optimistic. There's a LOT that needs to be done! I'd suggest that you might want to re-think your budget and timing a bit, then use the "search" feature of the forum to do some research on the missing bits. For example, there's lots of info here on how to do the speaker geometry correctly, including soffits, angles, mix position, heights, distances, etc. There's also stacks of information on how to avoid three-leaf and four-leaf systems, how to build doors and windows, how to do HVAC, etc. I'd also suggest that you re-do the same basic outline as above in SketchUp, and post it here so we can get a better idea about those potential issues that seem to be there. But at least you found the right place! This forum is the best possible place you could be to learn how to do this right! - Stuart -

Soundman20204 January 2012#7
I didn't do a ray trace, but to me it doesn't look like you are even close to establishing a good RFZ there. Your speaker geometry still doesn't make a lot of sense either. Yes, I realize you are trying to cover the rear couch with a good stereo image, but the room isn't wide enough to do that the way you are trying to: the angles and distances are not right. The distance between the speakers is much shorter than the distance from speaker to sofa, and the angles are too shallow to give a good image back there. You can only really do that well with a wider room (which introduces issues of its own...) You also seem to have an engineer whose ears are located in his eye sockets! :) Seriously, the acoustic axis from the speakers should be aimed at the engineer's ears, not his eyes. The axes will intersect a few inches (maybe several inches) behind the engineers head, not in the middle of his sinuses! And I still think there's a lot of space to be saved be eliminating that unnecessary sound lock, and moving the the iso booth to the other corner... But that's just my opinion. Personally, I would splay the walls of the CR (and therefore also the LR) to help with your RFZ. But that's just my $0.02. Take it for whatever you think its worth! :) - Stuart -

Also how do you do the ray trace thing in sketchup.
Manually, painfully, with a lot of time, care and patience! :) (Beer and other beverages have been know to help as fuel for the occasion...) You start by creating a number of lines across a range of angles, in increments of, say, 5°, all centered on the point where the acoustic axis of the speaker meets the front panel. Then you extend each one until it touches a reflective surface, measure the angle of incidence to the surface normal, make another line at the same but opposite angle, reflecting around the other side of the normal, and see where it goes. Then repeat for all of the other lines. And you have to do it both vertically and horizontally, to check for reflections off the desk, console, ceiling, etc.

You said the distance/space behind the front wall spacing depends on what I am trying to do. I'm lost here, I thought there was a certain distance requirement for the front wall framing
Ahhh! OK, I thought you were talking about distances inside the room, as in speaker-to-speaker, speaker to mix position, etc. OK, once again there's no exact distance requirement for the wall framing. You see, a two-leaf MSM wall is actually a tuned system. Same principle as a pendulum swinging on a string, or a weight bobbing up and down on a spring, or the shock absorbers in your car. It is tuned to a specific frequency (set by the mass on the leaves and the size of the air gap between them, mainly). At that frequency it does not isolate at all. For all higher frequencies it isolates better and better. So obviously you need to tune it as low as possible, such that it isolates all of the frequencies that you are concerned about. You tune it by deciding how much mass to put on each leaf (= "how many layers of drywall"), and how big to leave the air gap. So there's no fixed size for the air gap, as it depends on YOUR specific situation. If you need to isolate deep roaring canon, earthquake sounds, rockets and things like that, then your needs are very different from the guy who needs to isolate acoustic guitar. Having said that, the general recommendation is that your air gap should be at least 4 inches. But since the framing itself will give you at least 3 1/2", you only really need about a half inch or inch between the outer leaf and the framing for the inner leaf, ASSUMING THAT YOU HAVE AVERAGE NEEDS. That's why defining your numbers is so important. Numbers in terms of "How much isolation do I need (decibels)?" and "What's the lowest frequency that I need to isolate?".
For the soffit angles, I was looking into the 30 degrees that you recommended for even monitoring in the sweet spot as well as in the rear of the room (client seating)
It's a good starting point! Putting the speakers further apart helps to get the aim point a bit further back in the room.
