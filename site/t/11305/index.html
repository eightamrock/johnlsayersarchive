After reading the circumstances of your environmental noise potential, and considering the size of this booth, my first suggestion is this. FORGET a room within a room. Your right about the fourth "mystery" wall. Sorry, I didn't put in the sketch. I'll tell you my reasoning for this design... I designed something that I would think to have excellent isolation as it's own structure without being in the garage. That way, with it actually in a garage, it could only improve it, even if marginally.
Well, from what I've read here, including a few after-the-fact "sob stories", I think that you not only might NOT improve it, even slightly, but rather that your third leaf might make things worse. I keep saying this: I'm no expert on acoustics, but I've learned from the experts here that, when it comes to acoustics, what seems logical and intuitive is probably actually wrong, and what doesn't make much sense at first glance is actually the best idea. The problem with a three-leaf design (from what I've read) is that you might well be setting up resonant spaces that somehow couple to other spaces, even though you can't see how that would happen at first glance. I recall reading a thread here about one person who had good isolation in his studio, until he decided to make it "better" by installing laminate floor over a foam underlay, right on top of his existing floor. His additional "isolation" actually made things far worse, since he now hears the clanking of machinery coming in from an adjacent office, which he never heard before his improvement. In short, even though a third leaf looks good in theory, in practice it might not be at all what you expected. Unless you are planning to put many, many layers of drywall, all joined by Green Glue and absolutely hermetically sealed, all around your other two leaves, I'm still not convinced that you will get the effect you are expecting. The extra "air - mass" layer around your inner two rooms is going to play acoustic tricks on you, I think. Once again, I'm just repeating what I've learned so far. Maybe the real acoustic boffins can chime in here and correct me, but from everything I've read here and in Rod's book, a third leaf is a bad idea, and your outside garage wall looks a lot like a third leaf to me.
Soundman202021 October 2008#20
I found what I was looking for, and it comes from a solid source, I think: A forum member who works for the manufacturers of Green Glue. In this case, he is commenting on something that isn't really related to his product: "The simplest and most relevant downside of a triple leaf system is that for a given amount of weight and space, you wind up with a higher frequency of MSM. And that means that whatever decoupling efforts you have will start to work for you at a HIGHER frequency, which is bad. The next downside is the potential for chaotic low-freq behavior (resonance behavior) as you introduce multiple systems. In some instances - such as the famous double stud system shown in quadruple, triple, and double leaves - this can be catastrophic. " Comes from here: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... c&start=15 So if I understand that right, your extra leaf is going to mean that your isolation will be less effective in the lower frequencies (where it matters most), and that the entire low-frequency behavior of your three-leaf system might be "chaotic" at best, and "catastrophic" at worst. I don't know about you, but when I see acoustic experts using words like "less effective" and "chaotic" and "catastrophic" to talk about something I'm planning to build, I tend to re-think my plans! :) - Stuart -
