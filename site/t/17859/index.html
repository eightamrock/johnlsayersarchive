Building a studio on the second floor is generally not a good idea. It is very hard to isolate the floor sufficiently, and there is also the issue of structural integrity. Building a studio implies a LOT of weight (mass), and that usually means reinforcing the ground floor. If at all possible, try to move your rehearsal space down to the ground floor.
Since we will play there with a full band (Acoustic drums, 2 guitars, bass player) I'd like to know how realistic I need to look at the "noise" outside. The neighbours are about 20-30 meter from the building, so thats rather close. How much will I be able to reduce the outside sound, so I could play untill 23:00 without my neighbours and their children going crazy?
The best way to answer that question is for you to get a sound level meter yourself, and actually measure it. In other words, measure how loud your band is when playing at maximum level, and also find out how quiet you need to be at your property line, both legally and also in terms of being a good neighbor. Call your local municipality and ask for a copy of the noise regulations: they should tell you what the legal requirement is. But to give you an idea: Just the drums by themselves can easily reach 115 dB, and more. Add in bass, electrical guitar and other things, and you could well be getting up to 118 dB or more. Most municipal regulations seem to set the night-time noise limit at around 40 dB, plus or minus a few. so you need roughly 70 to 80 dB of isolation, in total, between the interior of your room, and your property line. Fortunately distance is your friend here: those 20-30 meters will help a lot, since sound dissipates at around -6dB every time you double the distance. So theoretically, as long as you can get your level down to about 70 dB 1 meter outside your walls, that should be enough. Roughly. That still means you need around 50 dB of isolation. That's do-able, but not easy on the second floor. That is going to be very expensive to accomplish. So before looking into those other questions, I'd first strongly suggest that you re-think your plan, and either move the studio to the ground floor, or understand that you won't be able to play loudly in there, especially at night. Or if you HAVE to play loud at night, and you HAVE to have the studio on the second floor, then you will also have to spend a lot of money to do it. - Stuart -
