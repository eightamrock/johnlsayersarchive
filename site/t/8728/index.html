billshoup wrote:
On the ceiling, I plan to use RC-1, then two layers of 5/8” sheetrock. I will use 1” screws on the first layer (half coverage) and 1 5/8” screws on the second layer (full coverage).
You must do "full schedule" screwing on all layers. I realize there was advice posted on here years ago to the contrary -- using half the number of screws on all but the final layer. That wisdom has since been reversed in light of the fact that such a plan is not code compliant, and it's for good reason. Each layer of drywall is held primarily by the heads of the screws that were used to install each layer. Subsequent layers are held by the heads of the screws used to isntall subsequent layers. The theory behind using fewer screws on initial layers was prevention of fusing the drywall sheets together to resonate as one thicker sheet. However, as long as the sheets are not solidy glued together with construction adhesive, that won't be a problem. About two years ago, when I pulled permits for my project, I mentioned the "half schedule for first layers" plan to the plan checker, and he said (paraphrasing), "Not in my jurisdiction you won't! :shock: You'll use full schedule on all layers."
billshoup wrote:
For the new inside walls, I plan to use 2 X 4 24” OC framing (as space is limited) anchoring the framing to the concrete with no sheetrock on the side of the framing that faces the concrete. R-13 insulation, then RC-1, then two layers of 5/8” sheetrock, using the same drill as the ceiling as far as caulk and alternating sheetrock orientation, etc.
I didn't see (or missed) your overhead clearance from basement floor surface to bottom of joists (not the beam). The reason I ask is that you may not need to use RC at all. For sure if you have enough clearance to frame new inner leaf joists to attach to your new inner leaf wall frames, RC won't be necessary. On the other hand, if you're going to use RC on your joists, and then install two layers of drywall on them, then you'll probably want to use either RC or (even better) hat channel on RSIC clips. But if you do that, you're going to be adding about 10psf to your existing ceiling structure (counting your plan to add two layers of "beef-up strips"). If you haven't consulted a structural engineer yet, chances are good that you don't know either. ;-)
billshoup wrote:
For the wall that the door goes into, I plan on two layers of 5/8” sheetrock directly screwed to 2 X 4 24” OC framing on the outside, then R-13 insulation, RC-1 and two layers of sheetrock on the inside.
Same applies. By the way, be aware that low end isolation will suffer the most with your relatively small air gap. Double framing is superior. One reason it's superior is the wider air gap. Don't underestimate the power of the "air" in the mass-air-mass equation.

billshoup wrote:
6) Should I be concerned about anchoring the wall framing directly to the concrete, or is it important to use “sway braces” (page 79 of Rod’s book) for this to decouple them? It seems unnecessary to me (to use sway braces), since the ceiling RC will be directly attached to the floor joists, which seems would be more of a problem then anchoring the walls directly to the concrete. Or am I completely off track?
If you end up doing the full inner leaf joist framing attached to your inner leaf wall framing thing, you won't need sway braces at all. That's because the inner leaf it's all tied together and already isolated. When you use the existing ceiling joists to support both an upstairs floor and a downstairs ceiling, you run into an issue requiring decoupling. So, it depends on what you're doing.
billshoup wrote:
7) At this point I’m not sure what backer rod is, but I’m sure I will find out. I’m a little confused where all it should be used. Should it really be used most anywhere that I will be caulking? For example the seams between two pieces of sheetrock?
Only use backer rod when the surface changes direction -- wall to wall, wall to ceiling, wall to floor... Not panel to panel. Dan Fitzpatrick has written extensively here about the benefits of backer rod. So has Rod Gervais. Search. :)
billshoup wrote:
8 ) Taping and mudding - I assume that this should be done on the first layer of sheetrock so that the surface is as smooth as possible before adding the second layer? Or should this not be a concern? And I should assume it is normal practice to tape and mud the second (top) layer? (Boy that feels like a stupid question!)
I have a wall that I just did one layer of mud on the seams -- no tape, no subsequent mud layers. I personally don't think the miniscule air pocket, if any, in the tapered valleys between sheets is a problem. And if they crack, they won't leak air, 'cuz there will be another layer over it. That's just me though. I also have many layers, so many that it's often called "overkill." :twisted: So, I say, don't worry about it, but feel free to worry about it. ;-)

I'll also use a nice wood trim on all four sides of the box so they'll look decent.

Yes, because you don't want the area between the concrete wall and the new inner leaf wall to be a weak link to the upstairs above it.
