The first picture here is a picture of the control room from standing inside of the doorway looking toward the front wall where the speaker soffits will be. The flex hanging down is the HVAC feed for the room. The electrical hanging down is for lights to be put in a cloud above the mix position ala Kendale's studio. The three rooms on this end (and the bedroom above it) take feeds from the trunk inside the soffit at different locations and then snake wherever I could to try and get angles to increase isolation. That being said, I am putting automated dampers on each duct that seals the duct off via a switch in the control room. This will ONLY shut down the air coming into the room, but they are sound proof dampers made for hospitals (you will see the controller shortly).

My ceiling is going to be two layers of 5/8 drywall hung on top of RC-1 (spaced according to manufacturers specs). I know many people have beefed up the bottom of the floor above them by adding drywall between the joists. My ceiling is manufactured I-beams and carries TWO floors above it (though the majority of the weigh of the second floor rests on the outside walls and not directly on the floor). Basically what it consists of is a master bedroom suite and bath above it, with a 3/4 story loft above that (my office). ALL of the outside walls of the studio area (where the I-beams spam) are concrete and are foundation walls for the house. I'll post in here some pictures of some areas that make adding mass to the bottom of the floor difficult (very tight fits) and also a picture of the load info from the side of the I-beam. drfrankencopter26 March 2007#14
I wouldn't bother replacing the yellow fiberglass with rockwool....I don't think there would be a significant difference in performance there, provided you can stuff the the yellow insulation to a density that is comparable with rockwool. If you can stuff insulation between the sill and joist I think It'd be a good idea...though you might want/need a vaopur barier there. That will be a significant flanking path, so you'll want to do your best to beef that area up. Maybe you can get some blown in through holes cut in the joist. Cheers Kris
