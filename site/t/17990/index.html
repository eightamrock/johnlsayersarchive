Soundman20203 December 2012#3
but it's one large room so I think I'll get limited returns with whatever I do
Bigger rooms usually sound better, acoustically, so that might not be the case. However, the ceiling looks pretty low, so you might have an issue there.
The space overall sounds pretty dead in general cause of the insulation, but I want to use my existing 7 panels to maximize the potential of creating a workable mixing environment.
If it already sounds dead then you probably don't need more absorption. It looks like all the walls have deep insulation on them, so you might be over-treated. I would suggest that you download and run REW on your room, and see what it shows. It might turn out that you need less absorption (ie, cover some of that with reflective surfaces, or better placement of absorption. REW will help to determine that. - Stuart -

Soundman20203 December 2012#5
Could that be the result of too much bass absorption?
If it is in the low mids, then it is probably lack of enough bass trapping (small rooms need a LOT of bass trapping) and too much absorption in the mids and highs. You say that the insulation is covered with some sort of "thick vinyl", so that is probably reflecting back some of the highs, and it is mostly the mids that are being absorbed. REW will help to reveal if that is the case, but that's what I would expect, from what you say. - Stuart -
