I know that Auralex uses a propietary foam/rubber for their 'Platfoam' line. However, for the DIYer, will 1" neoprene work reasonably well? giles1175 August 2003#2
1: Neoprene with a Durometer of 60 or greater should not have a problem with a floated drum riser. Realize we float entire studio floors on 1/2" neoprene with a durometer of 60 to 80, so you should have no issues with a lightweight (in comparison) Drum riser Bryan Giles If you use open cell rubber, use a 1" thickness and you should be cool as well. I use open cell rubber for various applications (weight bearing) and compression has not been an issue either.
knightfly5 August 2003#3
Generally, for a compression layer you need at least 15%, and not more than maybe 60% compression. Too little, and it isn't a spring anymore. too much, and it isn't a spring anymore. If you're talking a total sandwich, I'd go with maybe a couple layers of that foam carpet pad that looks like somebody puked and they rolled it up. If you get too close to the resonant frequency of the absorber, you AMPLIFY instead of ISOLATE. This can really bite you in the butt. Whatever the original top of the riser is made of, is what I WOULD NOT use for the top layer - you want different materials/thicknesses for a different Coincidence Dip in order to avoid one particular resonance. Another thing to do would be put rigid foam (the cheaper, Home Depot kind) up against the underside (before putting on the top) and press 2x4's up against it from underneath, fastening them in place to hold some tension against the underside. This will damp the panel and decrease ringing tendencies. That's it for me, it's 3:19 am... Steve
