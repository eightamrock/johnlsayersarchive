gullfo4 January 2011#2
welcome! please provide your location in your profile. is the ceiling in the garage open to the roof or does the garage already have a drywall ceiling? depending on the answer, if its open, you might consider using isolation clips and hat channel on the joists to create a decoupled ceiling and create soffits around any support beams. if it's closed, i'd measure the existing isolation and see if you think that adding GG and some more drywall will meet your needs. if you're going with a "ratio" for the room rather than just using the entire space, then i'd build the isolation booth as a small room outside of the practice room to give you the best isolation and probably simplify the construction by not trying to nest isolation spaces.

gullfo4 January 2011#4
given the room height - i'd be inclined to remove the existing drywall and use it to beef up the floor above (i'm assuming you have a floor above it) or roof if that's an option. then build a new ceiling using isolation clips and hat channel. this will preserve a lot more space than trying to put in new ceiling joists. build your isolation walls to accommodate the lower ceiling so the drywall layers will interleave but remain decoupled. you can use isolation brackets to help support the wall or frame the wall floor to joist and use isolation clips and hat channel for the walls as well. use the isolation clips and hat channel on support beams to encase it. for support poles encase it in the space between walls or build a soffit around it.

gullfo5 January 2011#7
so if you already have the walls built for the new ceiling (that would be the 8'), then you could build new ceiling joists supported by the walls, leave the existing drywall and/or add some layers there, and then a new ceiling on the bottom the new joists to achieve the double leaf. if you're going lower than 8' or want to leverage the addition 1' or so, then i'd remove the existing drywall, put new 2x drywall on the upper part of the joists and then use isolation clips/hat channel and 2x drywall on the underside. this would be a reasonably performing partition and then i'd build my isolation walls to that inner ceiling level. any internal partition walls (which to me sound like static gobos...) could be ceiling height or less depending on what you're trying to achieve with them.
