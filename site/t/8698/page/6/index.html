Well, the Doors are in!!! Well, they are hung and shimmed, but are not trimmed out yet. That probably won't happen until I trim out the rest of the studio. These doors are probably not considered "ideal" studio doors, but I wanted to have doors with windows in them to keep open lines of visual communication between the rooms and also to prevent the feeling of claustrophobia. These doors are outside doors, solid core, with 2 panes of glass with some blinds sandwiched in between the 2 panes. I will use some heavy-duty rubber seals to make sure that they are air-tight, and be done with it. These doors were about US$325 each, pre-hung. I know there were probably better choices in this price range when it comes to sound isolation (like those without windows), but again, this is my home studio, built for my own needs, and the desire for the windows outweighed any other considerations. With the use of the airlock as I have done, I am sure that I will have enough isolation for my needs. Some pics: And he swore that he "didn't do windows"!!! doublehelix wrote:
<sigh> what's a poor girl to do??? :)
Back from page 8. Best remedy for a butt joint is a wide mud knife. The wider the better, like a 12 inch mud knife isn't too wide. With a clean damp knife and a pan of stirred up mud skim the mud on thin and as quickly, in one smooth motion, as possible. Look at the butt joint as a center line. Spread mud on the left of this line and then spread more mud on the right of this line. Using a 12 inch knife this should cover approx. a 24 inch wide area. The result is that the joint isn't such a dramatic change in the sheetrock elevation and it more resembles a flat surface. Good luck,
