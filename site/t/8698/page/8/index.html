Next up, I got back to work on my door frames. In Rod's book, he recommends using car trunk (or car "boot" for you Europeans!) rubber sealing gasket to seal the doors. He specifically recommends GM "Type K" seals. I was able to purchase some of the afore-mentioned (hard-to-find) seals from JC Whitney, a US-based internet car parts dealer. Not the greatest experience dealing with them, but at least I was able to get the seals. Let's leave it at that, we have discussed the perils of dealing with Jc Whitney in another thread already! To install these guys, I will first hang the doors, and shut them tightly. Then I will take the rubber seals, and stretch them along the length of the door, and make sure they are sealing tightly along the door edge, and then staple them to the door frame. Once they are in place, I will then install a set of wooden "stops" on top of the seals the hold them in place, and make things look tidy. In order to place the stops over the top of the rubber seals however, I have to use a router and cut out a rabbet grove along the edge of the wooden stops. This is kind of hard to explain, but fairly easy to show with pictures. First up is to set up the router. Since I do not own a router table, I had to borrow one from a friend. It is a pretty small table, which makes things a bit more difficult, but still workable. I want this groove to be cut 1/2" into the wood, and only 1/8" deep. This particular router table did not have a fence, so I had to use another piece of wood attached to the table with "C" clamps to act as my fence.

After that, I ended up having to go out of town on (real) business for a week, so things basically sat there idle for about 2 weeks. (Sucks!) The good news is that we are back working trying to push through to the end. This weekend, we worked pretty hard to get the 4 corner bass traps in the control room completed. These designs are based on the Studiotips Superchunk (R) design. For more info, check out the Studiotips website. Basically however, I cut triangle pieces of rigid fiberglass insulation, and stacked them in each corner. Due to my stupidity during the design phase of the control room, I ended up putting things too close to the corners so that they interfered with the placement of the traps. I am talking about things like wall plugs, audio patch bays, lighting, etc. I am such an idiot!!! Next time, I guarantee that there will nothing within 17 inches of each corner!!! In order to accommodate these screw ups, I had to be somewhat creative with the corner trap design. I borrowed heavily from another builder's designs, Brad Jacob. He had the same issues, and came up with a workable solution. Thanks for the tips Brad! You da man!!! Here are some pics that shows the progress so far... These first three pictures show my son cutting each 4' X 8' sheet of rigid fiberglass into 8 small triangles. The cutting spot was scored with a box cutter, and then the final cuts were made with an electric carving knife like one that you would use on your Christmas turkey! (Don't tell my wife, but she is getting a new carving knife for her birthday!!! ;) )
