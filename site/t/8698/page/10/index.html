My other "issue" so far is the placement of the NS10 speakers. I used to have them on the inside, but space constraints wouldn't allow for that to happen if I wanted to maintain proper symmetry with the main speakers. So I moved them to the outside, and raised them up pretty high on those crappy stands. They are far too wide to get a real stereo image, plus, they are behind the main speakers a bit, so the stereo image will also be effected. But, "it is what it is", at least for now. I do not want to put them on my desk, and I cannot move them forward without moving my desk back 6 inches, and I just don't have the room to spare. They are my "second" set of monitors anyway, and I use them for comparison, and for looking into the mid-range, so I am hoping that they will at least "work" where they are. I have not hooked them up yet, so I have no idea how they sound. I will have to come up with a more permanent solution later.

I was worried that it was going to be too dead, but instead, things sound very balanced. I am happy! I have had a couple of folks ask me how I hung these traps, so I took a couple of pictures showing the process: I used a "cleat" design that is often used with kitchen cabinets to support the weight, very similar to the method that Brad Jacob used with his traps. The first step (of which I do not have a photo) was to cut a 1X4 piece of wood in half lengthwise at a 45 degree angle with a table saw. You will be able to see what I mean from the pictures. After a bit of measuring, I hung one half of this "cleat" on the wall, making sure that I screwed it in tightly to the wall studs to support the weight.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.helixrecording.com/Pics/Construction/Dsc01721_SM.jpg
Here is an end-on shot of the cleat showing the 45 degree cut a bit better.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.helixrecording.com/Pics/Construction/Dsc01722_SM.jpg
Next, the matching half of the cleat was attached to the top of the bass trap. I wanted to make sure that there was at least 1.5" gap between the back of the trap and the wall, so I mounted the cleat on a wooden batten that I first attached to the back of the trap. I then had to match the thickness of the batten and the cleat on the bottom of the trap with some small pieces of scrap wood, and attached a few felt "feet" to keep from scratching the wall.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.helixrecording.com/Pics/Construction/Dsc01723_SM.jpg
Here is an end shot of the trap so that you can see the angle on the cleat a bit better.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.helixrecording.com/Pics/Construction/Dsc01724_SM.jpg
From there, it is just a matter of lifting the trap up onto the hanging cleat, matching up the angles. Pretty simple, really.
