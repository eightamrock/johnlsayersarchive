I am mostly concerned with with visual reflections. You hint at ways to deal with that and yet still keep parallel panes. Can you expand on this?
I was just speaking of sound reflections. Rod says in his book that if you use glass that is generally a bit heavier than your wall assembly, the negative isolation effects might not be that bad. You should be able to avoid the light reflections with the angled glass. I am wondering how it would work if both panes were angled the same direction. gullfo2 December 2007#121
my main concern with the doors - what if there is a fire? having the doors open so they're not in the way of an escape should be an important consideration. the 2 doors facing each other in the air lock - i would have them open into each room so they don't inadvertantly block each other. or have only the isobooth door open into the air lock since its behind the CR door and won't block it. this is also probably a bit late now as well (I don't think i'd ever seen the floor plan before) - if you had the air lock wall mirror the CR wall, you could have gotten some more space in the airlock plus an angled wall to cut some reflections side-to-side... otherwise.. its looking good! don't sweat small details that are cosmetic and will be covered! otoh, do sweat the small details that improve isolation and acoustics!
