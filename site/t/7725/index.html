guitardad7216 December 2006#2
Minimum you want 1" between wall frames/brick. This is the min recommended space I've seen anywheres between walls... so: Brick 1" Space 3.5" insulation in Stud Bay 2 layers 5/8" gypsum, AIR = 4.5" As you should be aware, adding more air will increase iso especially at low end. You could do: Brick 2" rockwool glued to brick 1" Space 3.5" insulation in Stud Bay 2 layers 5/8" gypsum. AIR = 7.5" If you've not already seen then check out: STC Chart link from: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html Even if not considering Green Glue, they have done a bunch of tests, web site loaded with info: http://www.greengluecompany.com/ AND this long document from IRC (Canada) http://irc.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/pubs/fulltext/ir761/ With all the above info (hopefully) you can then make a decision about sq ft of room verses iso of room. If it were me I would go with as little air space as possible, use green glue with 2 layers 5/8" gypsum minimum and strongly think about the rockwool glued to brick walls. You'll have a really small room anyways, acoustic wise you need to fight for every square inch. My room is bigger then yours and I am fighting hard for every square inch while trying to maintain my iso goal... Marc

knightfly26 December 2006#9
Forget expanding foam; you need to fill that void with masonry or the rest of the wall is not going to help you much at all. Maybe a fairly stiff mortar mix and careful use of trowels, take it slow enough so the "plug" doesn't slump too much and make it all bulgy. Mass is your friend here as anywhere. Triple leaf isn't the best for LF isolation, but with that much mass it won't kill you nearly as much as a lighter weight m-a-m-a-m partition would. Sometimes you just gotta do what you gotta do... Steve
