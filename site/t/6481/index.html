knightfly25 June 2006#4
IF you let me know which pix you can't see, I'll check into it - So that height requirement is the TOTAL height, not just the walls? Bummer... Roof pitch - usually depends on the type of roof; a "flat roof" material wouldn't require any pitch at all, because it's designed to be waterproof. A shingled roof typically requires a 4/12 pitch, meaning for every foot of sideways (run) distance it must rise 4" - this would put the height difference at 15" from wall to peak, if it's a center-peaked roof, and 30" rise if it's a shed roof. Your height limitations would likely call for a flat roof material such as GAF Tri-Ply, or a hot-mopped tar (messy and kind of out-dated) - under those conditions, a slight slope would work fine. The reason I recommended such a pitch for Dietcookie is that he needed room for air conditioning (Southern California) so I designed his space with an overhead "chiller room" - using a standard window AC unit mounted in the gable end of the roof, then a flat ceiling, super-insulating the attic part and gently pulling the warm air up out of the main room and replacing it with cold air from the attic. With your height restrictions, this wouldn't be feasible unless you're either VERY short or confined to a wheelchair :cry: If you MUST have AC (summers on the east coast are brutal IMO (humidity/temp both) you might be able to add a separate "dog house", super-insulate it, and feed the main shed through sound-proofed ducting - or, since you're primarily MIDI based maybe just a normal unit with a heavy PLUG/cover for cooler, nighttime mixing without disturbing neighbors - Floor; you do NOT want a single-leaf floor, unless your shed will be built right on the concrete; otherwise, most of your sound will get out/in with little to stop it. Shape - 10 x 12 is modally better (and not quite so claustrophobic) than 8 x 15, which is too close to a 2:1 ratio between length and width. Most couches are about 3 feet deep; a 2-seater would be about 4-1/2 feet wide. Using a cloth upholstered one will SLIGHTLY increase bass trapping. Key word here is "slightly". IF your shed will be built directly on a concrete slab, you should plan on pouring at least a 12" high "stem wall" so your wall structure isn't sitting on flat, un-drained concrete; otherwise you'll have rot/mildew problems. Not sure what your local city codes have to say about that; it might be considered too "permanent" to be just a "shed". For what you want, double sheathed outer 2x4 frame with resilient mounted double-layer gypsum on the inside (ceiling to match) should keep the complaints to a minimum; mixing at 85 dB SPL inside that (with good door seals, etc) may not even be audible outside if there is ANY ambient noise at all... Steve
