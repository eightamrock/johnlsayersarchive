xaMdaM2 September 2009#3
Yup, it's standard stuff... just layout your wall bottom plates like normal. Then make your top plates to match. Keeping in mind, the problem joints/corners that may have vertical angles. Again, you follow standard building practices of the top keeper plate crossing the top plate to lock in the joint; This pic is a lower corner in the iso booth... this is where two angles meet to for a corner to two planes. IIRC, the two angles are 12.5 and 17.5 degrees. Frankly, it's been so long ago that I did the framing, I really don't remember. :oops: The one thing I discovered when doing these *^&#@%!@*@^&%W@*^&!%!@&^ compound cuts is to have two compound miter saws... one for each angle! (You'll still screw up a few cuts, but it ends up far fewer if you get the cut schedule put down on paper.)

[EDIT] NOTE: That this photo is NOT a photo of the completed framing; There is also a corner stud that is NOT visible. Also, the 2nd keeper top plates are not in place at that point in time. The 2nd top plate pattern is thus, that the plate coming from the right hand side actually goes on, overlapping the plate coming down from the top/left. The 2nd top plate coming from the top/left butts up to the plate that is overlapping the joint.
