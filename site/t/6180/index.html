knightfly10 May 2006#7
You'll actually get a slight improvement in LF isolation if you only glue around the PERIMETER of your inserts, so they each maintain their OWN coincidence frequency; the cleats around edges will support the MDF and inner gypsum WITHOUT the panels being full-surface glued. When you glue two panels together (other than with Green Glue, which isn't a glue) you create a new, thicker, heavier SINGLE panel with ONE coincidence frequency that's LOWER than either separate panel would have - this puts the weaker isolation point of that panel at a lower frequency, which means it's harder to stop and requires more MASS to do so, which kinda defeats the purpose somewhat. HTH... Steve

knightfly10 May 2006#9
Yup; when attaching drywall to studs, you only use 1 screw every 8" at most and there are NO SCREWS in between the studs (for obvious reasons :wink: ) - so no more support is necessary. By only fastening your "beef" around the edges, you keep separate coincidence frequencies for each layer, which will help even more than usual since you're using differing thicknesses AND materials per layer... Steve

Keith, sorry I wasn't more watchful as you were doing that - but I'd thought I made it fairly plain about full gluing in this post http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=503 Dated in 2003, and updated 5/3/04, where I said, "USG, on page 118-119 of the USG construction manual, (once we deciphered it) does indeed say that it's OK to use strip lamination and laminating (Type G) screws for face layers on sound walls - they recommend only about 6 actual permanent fasteners per sheet of drywall, placed across the short end of the sheet - then, a stripe of joint compound next to each stud (placed immediately prior to installing laminating screws) and about 5-6 laminating screws per sheet (look at the manual, there is a a diagram next to the text on those pages)" However, I did go on to say that neither Rod nor myself recommended that method because we were't too confident in the ability of joint compound to maintain the second layer in place, so that likely could have been clearer - thing is, the stripe glue method in itself is acoustically correct, for the reasons I've stated here - Another link from the past, not specifically related to "beef" but still useful - http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... +glue#6529 And finally, IIRC weren't you concerned with the unevenness of your stucco or was that someone else? If it was you, I probably voiced a concern about the air gap created between uneven stucco and the first "beef" layer - I remember suggesting to someone about that time frame that mixing up some slightly sloppy mud and "buttering" the entire insert before placing it against the stucco would fill all the air voids and make for less "3 leaf" effect - I still think that's a better plan than NOT gluing that first layer of "beef", mainly because any appreciable air gap will worsen LF TL MORE than combining two layers into ONE would cause. Hope I didn't make things even MORE confusing by all that... Steve

The fire stops should be providing some type of support to structure, if they were at an angle it would be more. If it were me without thinking about fire stopping I’d leave them because maybe they are holding a warped stud(s) in place and that is a guess on my part :wink: but… If the wall is going to be above 8’ then your code should be… you need them. Since you got a permit, did your architect include any fire stopping in the plan? You might get in trouble for taking them out when it comes to inspection (unless you bury the wall out of site). It might be worth it to give inspector a call. But then again, I find alarming the amount of violations including fire stopping, I here about inspectors missing, at least here in Florida. They let (or builders some how hide) 12’ and 15’ walls pass without fire stopping :shock: :shock: But then comes the issues of the big old space in between the walls where fire would just travel up freely anyways :? Did you buy Rods book? Really I’d love to be able to provide links to everything in Rods’ book but… Hope Rod does no get mad at me for sharing this… but it’s a brilliant idea and hopefully a slight persuasion for any forum reader to buy the book :D Basically he suggests a method using a piece of drywall laid flat to connect and couple double walls, creating a fire stop. To decouple this drywall a bit, it sits on mineral wool, calk and baker rod… which all sits on the 2x4 plates with another piece of lumber holding it down from the top. Suggestion is to put it at the top of the wall so fire stopping is effective from double wall cavity to double ceiling cavity. Only question is losing a couple dB’s of isolation & and extra work of building the fire stop be worth having a safer structure… that would be while it’s burning… will the extra time you’ll have for the fire department to arrive be enough to save your house and gear? There is also the method on Sharwards thread, well his inspectors method (I think) where the insulation from each double wall touches. So after I’ve written and rethought all that, I’m still 50/50 whether you should take them out or not. :roll: :oops: :twisted: :twisted: :twisted: Marc
