Yet, since the PT here IS SYP, not incised, and not all that expensive either, and not restricted from indoor use (ACQ treated) as long as moisture is below 19 percent, I think it would be wise idea to use it in case all my 'fail-safe' redundant measures to prevent a basement flood are circumvented, AND if we get that hurricane that some are promising... it can't hurt... can it?
camistan wrote:
...I did a "moisture test" by placing some Saran wrap on my basement floor, and sealing around it with tape so there would be no air leaks. I watched for any condensation that would occur under the Saran wrap for 2 days but never saw any...
I think with that test what you are checking for is seepage, and not condensation, no? Condensation, I believe, comes when warmer, humid air comes in contact with cold concrete floors and walls... You could have no seepage and lots of condensation (humid air, cold floors/walls and not dehumidifier), or no condensation and lots of seepage (maybe usually be the case when dehumidifiers are used inside, and the ground below is wet and coming through), or both. Interestingly enough, from what I'm led to understand, a high humidity in the basement will actually slow any seepage to some extent (opposes the vapor pressure); so, a high level of dehumidification will actually 'pull' water through the concrete... This, I think though, does NOT account for much when there are substantial leaks through a floor, or if the hydrostatic pressure of the soil is high behind/beneath the concrete... But I myself wouldn't forgo the dehudification for the sake of slowing the vapor coming through the floor -- plus, there probably is a 'limit' in the increased rate of water vapor coming through the concrete (i.e., it can only allow so much through, based on the restrictions in the pororsity?). K

Keith: Actually, I think the SYP is somewhat stronger than SPF, Hem-Fir, or Doug-Fir... It is definitely harder too (although this doesn't matter for the framing; note that it is used in some finished flooring)...
