Lots happening. I'm trying to get the studio operational for a weeklong mixing session on Sept 11. Crunch time. Let's see, where did I leave off? We hired a drywall finisher to come and smooth the joints and screw heads. Money well spent. He knocked it out in no time, and did a great job, especially since I wanted to leave the walls with no texture... I'm just not a fan of the bumpy stuff. He finished that up yesterday (Wed) morning. Then that day we laid the subfloor strata, which consists of a layer of 1/2" styrofoam. The stuff I picked up at Lowes is pretty dense, it's not like the stuff you get a new piece of gear packed in. This stuff is actually spec'ed to be used as part of a sub-floor, so I don't think it will compress over time, but gives me a nice softish underlayment that should help to isolate the floor from the walls and whatnot. Then on top of that is a layer of 1/2" plywood, followed by a layer of 3/8" plywood turned the other way and staggered joints. The thinking was to avoid weak spots in the underlayment that the hardwood will be nailed to. Side note, check out the address on the Dow product... that's right, Midland, MI. That's the town where I grew up and my Dad worked for Dow. In fact, I think he might have spent some time in a plant that makes styrofoam. Also, we put a 2" border of the stryo around the bottom edge of the wall to give a little bumper for the hardwood and underlayment. It won't let the whole floor slide around, but it'll condense enough if the hardwood needs to swell a little if there's humidity. After we finished the floor setup, I primed the whole place. sharward6 September 2006#165
Mom & Dad wrote:
What is a cloud? :?
Hi Mom... ;-) A cloud is essentially an absorbant material (in this case, rockwool insulation with fabric underneath, surrounded in a light timber frame) that acts as a sound absorber overhead. You know how echoey a room is when it has a non-carpeted floor and no furniture? Well, if that same room had such a "cloud" overhead, it would echo a lot less. That's basicallly short-sleeve English for the acoustic benefits of a cloud. (And I'm by no means an acoustics expert!) --Keith :mrgreen:

guitardad7211 September 2006#170
That brings up another questions, however. What about the floor? Is hardwood sound sensitive enough? Why not use carpet?
Eh Mom & Dad. In addition to what Sharward said and link he pointed to: We don't want all of the rooms surfaces to be absorbing, a general guideline "arguably" is 50% absorbing and 50% reflective surfaces. We don't want sounds to go from the speaker, bounce off walls or ceiling and back to listening position. This messes up up the sound. By placing absorbers of the right size (to gobble up specific frequencies), in the right places we can make the room sound bigger. Comparison would be listening to speakers outside where the sound travels one way and nothing reflects back the listener. How ever, no matter what room you are in or outside, there is a floor. Our ears are accustom to the reflective sounds of a floor and I believe even part of our sense of balance comes from our ears telling us where the floor is and even how far from the floor we are. Since the floor is a given in any room, we seek 50/50 reflective/absorbing surface, carpet only absorbs high not low frequencies and the sound of wood in general good (not to mention ease of maintenance and cleanliness): the recommendation for floor is wood OR another reflective surface. Personally for my own room, even though prepping for it is going to more difficult then I 1st thought, I'm going with laminate or engineered hardwood floor. Marc :D
