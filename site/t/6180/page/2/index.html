Ok, so much progress has been made this week. Started in by breaking out some concrete and digging some holes where eventually we'll pour a couple of footings for the posts that hold the big beam. Demo'ed out the rest of the exterior, did a bit of the frame for the "window wall" (the wall that I'll look out of from the listening position) including the opening for the tracking room window. Today we put in the huge, heavy parrallam beam upon which we would fix the sagging roofline and hang the weight of the upper ceiling drywall. The lifesaver was the lifter thingy we rented to jack that beam up... probably 450 lbs. Once we had the beam up on the jack, it was a breeze to roll around into place then jack it up, and stick it in it's spot. Then with the beam in place, we forced the roofline back up to near where it should've been my wedging in increasingly taller pieces of lumber, until finally we could put in the 5 inch tall "wall" to support the roof on the beam. Amazing what can be accomplished with some wood and a sledgehammer. Then we started in on the ceiling joists. We were able to recycle some of the original joists we had to take out because they ran the wrong way. Save a few bucks there. I was quite pleased with myself... that's me up on the platform with the big nailer putting the joists in place. My arms (and everything else for that matter) are quite sore from the week's work (c'mon, I'm a musican for pete's sake), and we've got another day of framing tomorrow. We've got the plumber scheduled for Monday, so we need to frame the wall where the old garage door was so that we can get the raised floor in to set up for plumbing. Then if there's time, we'll continue on the exterior walls.

Stick10 June 2006#33
So, today we added a couple straps to the big beam to satisfy the architect, and put in the bathroom floor, which is raised to allow for the plumbing to exit and join the house's main system. Originally, the bathroom was raised to match the house floor height, but when we took out the door to the house and used the space for a shower, we still had to leave it raised for the plumbing. It'll be slightly clunky to walk up a step to go to the bathroom, but better than tromping through the house to relieve one's self. We also got the part of the exterior wall framed that has the bathroom window in it. In other news, I met with the guy that will likely install the HVAC. As I feared, it looks like it's going to be a split system with 3 wall units. I sure hope they're going to be quiet enough to live with. And yes, Keith, we'll also be doing the fresh air exchanger sort of like you're doing, though probably not to the sound isolation extreme. I researched the high-velocity systems and talked to a few people about them who pretty much said "no way" with their first breath. And after explaining that I really needed 3 or 4 zones in my 470 square feet, the guy said, "that's impossible". At which point we talked about the split setups. As long as the noise isn't out of hand, it should work fine. The units have remotes, so I can turn the tracking room's unit off and on from the control room. And of course the small holes are a sound proofing bonus. I was hoping to avoid the "look" of the the split unit, but I'm limited with a complete lack of attic space. In the control room it'll go in the back above the Murphy bed, where there's a nice hole anyway. I might put the tracking room one on the wall above the window to the control room so I don't have to see it when I look in there. And since usually the mic is facing away from the window, we'll get rejection of whatever little noise there is if we keep it running. Another bonus is that the outside unit will go far far away on the other side of the house, so the mechanical noise of the unit I thought was going to be on the roof will not be an issue. So, I've got two days to recover... I'm sore as heck. Swinging a hammer is hard work. Kudos to builders around the world, I have a greater appreciation for the skill and strength the job requires.

sharward10 June 2006#34
This is looking more awesome by the minute!! 8) 8) Stick, those mini splits are going to be expensive, but given your needs I think it's the way to go. I realize you're not very enthused about the way that the look -- but having seen a couple myself, I think they look rather cool! :P They'll be quite a conversation piece, too. I'm assuming you'll be having the mini splits professionally installed. I got three estimates last year before I got my permit -- high, higher, and insane. I'll be interested in what kind of pricing you get. I'm assuming that you will not be needing heat... Because as I understand it, the Mitsubishi setup that allows for one outdoor unit to be used by three indoor units is "cooling only." If you'll have "line of sight" from the control room to each of the units, you should be able to use the infrared remotes from the control room. In fact, you might be able to use the same remote for each one. Don't worry about the noise of the units. I recall that the lowest fan speed on them is 26 dbA. That's sleepytime quiet! And, worst, case, you run it on high when you're not recording or mixing, then periodically shut it off completely when you need absolute silence. Stick, I know you said you aren't going to go to the great lengths of making beefy silencers for your ventilation system... Just be aware that by not doing so, you may be shortchanging yourself, such that all your other efforts to isolate may be severely compromised. Enjoy your break -- you deserve it! 8) --Keith :mrgreen: P.S.: If you're ever coming down the hill and want to check out my place, PM me beforehand! :)

len-morgan20 June 2006#45
Stick, Tell me why you feel you need fabric between the walls? I think you mentioned that you only have 2" between them. What fibers are you worried about migrating? If you have an exposed area between two walls, why not just cover that area where people might be? As an example, if you look at my thread, the area between the big room and the vocal booth is double walled (but about 6" apart) as you pass through the double doors. I'm only going to cover around the door frames since people aren't going to be wandering between the walls (at least I hope not!). I would not put up just plain burlap without some sort of flame retardant. If you'd like to know why, go buy just a strip of burlap, hang it outside on a stick or something, and then take a match to the bottom corner. It's an eye opener! This is the reason carpets will be ok on floors but NOT hung on walls. They burn differently. And yes...others are watching. :-) len
