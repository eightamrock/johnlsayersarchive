Stick23 June 2006#55
Ok, end of another week coming tomorrow. Sven, the HVAC man, had a crane come and drop the unit on the roof. That was quite a sight. Fortunately, the roof didn't collapse under the weight, and he hot wired it up so we could do a test run before we close in the ceiling with 3 layers of drywall. Needless to say, nobody's getting up there once that's done. The unit runs pretty quiet, and though I couldn't really tell much from today's run, I get the sense that it's not going to be dead silent, but it certainly isn't going to interfere with work. I'm pleased with were we ended up with A/C. The real truth won't happen until we get it all closed in. Jim, the electrician came yesterday to do a little bit of the rough-in that'll be closed in once we do the first layer of sheetrock. He also took a look at what we're going to need in the studio, and what the HVAC unit was going to take. Unfortunately, my panel on the house is only 100 amps, so we'll be maxing it out when we pull the sub-panel to the studio. If the dips caused by the HVAC turning on are too much to deal with, we'll have to replace the panel, and likely have PG&E upgrade the line to the house. Hopefully it won't come to that. I nailed up the last of the joist hangers today, and finished digging one of the post support holes. Jeff is bringing by his roto-hammer for me to use on the other hole where the dirt is very hard and harder to reach. So, after that's ready, we call for our first inspection on Monday. Assuming that goes ok, we'll start slapping up some drywall next week. In the mean time, I'm trying to figure out some of the interior details, like flooring materials, fabric colors, bathroom fixtures and tile, etc. I'm hoping to find some wide plank pre-finished engineered wood flooring. Reclaimed wood would be even cooler. Oh, and I ordered the exterior door, and the bathroom door today. I still need to figure out how to handle the real acoustic doors for the interior. I really want those seals to keep up with my 3 layers of drywall. Any suggestions would be appreciated! I guess that's it for today... and now a few pics...

Stick27 June 2006#70
Monday at the Stickhouse! Inspection #1 Day. I was up and outside tidying up thinking that he might show up first thing in the morning. Nope. Jeff came shortly thereafter, so we touched up a few things for the inspection. Added a little rebar to the footing holes, stuck up the last 2x4 of the ceiling frame, etc. After there was nothing more we could do to prepare, we started in on the top plates for the bathroom walls. Then the delivery of 27 sheets of 4x12 5/8" drywall and 17 sheets of 1/2" green board (for more mass on the exterior layer) showed up. As we were finishing those, it was lunchtime. No inspector yet. Of course, that's about all I can think of... After lunch we finish up the top plates, and then add a couple of pieces to be ready to start the studs for the bathroom and Jeff gets a call from his wife, who works nearby and needs a signature for something. So, Jeff takes off for a few minutes. Of course, you know what's coming. The inspector shows up while he's gone. Fortunately, Jeff pulls up behind them, so I'm not alone for it. As it turned out, it wasn't that big a deal, and I probably would've been fine without Jeff there, but still, I was glad he got back in time. Pretty much the only thing the inspector said was to be sure our shower had a 36" radius. Nothing about the cement forms, nothing about the crazy HVAC, nothing about the huge beam's engineering. It was a little funny, because riding shotgun with Carl the inspector was Mitch the supervisor... so we figure either Carl was in trouble and Mitch was checking up on him, (so we might've had an easier time than Carl on his own) or Mitch really was just doing basic quality control. Anyway, no problems, nothing to mess with, we're good to go to close up the "lid" and frame the inner walls. We'll do another "rough-in" inspection when the inner walls are framed and the electrical is in place. So, tomorrow, Jeff's bringing a yard of cement in a buggy that we'll wheelbarrow into the two footings and a step on the back of the house that needed to be added. Then we'll put up the insulation into the joists above the outer ceiling, and start putting up our first layer of drywall. Couple things to double check... I'm assuming there's really no reason to tape the seams in the drywall of the first couple layers, and maybe only where the ceiling is going to be visible in the last layer... in the entryway and the bathroom. I figure we'll put up the sheets, I'll caulk it, we'll slap some mud up to fill the void from the bevels, and onto the next layer. Sound right? And we're going to use faced insulation, which is fine as long as the facing is against the drywall, correct? For one thing it'll make keeping it in it's spot easy, as we'll staple it up before we add the drywall. And Jeff said it wasn't a bad idea to have that extra measure of moisture protection above the drywall. Right now we've only ordered enough to do the first ceiling, so if there's a better way we should do the 2nd ceiling, let me know. I'm also realizing that I really need to get at least the snake cables that'll go between the walls ordered and shipped in the near future. Not sure when I'm going to do that. If I have time in the morning, I'll shoot some pictures of the holes and the start of the bathroom frame.
