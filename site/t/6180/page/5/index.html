Stick6 July 2006#101
Ok, back in the saddle today... we made some good progress. Finished up the 3rd layer of drywall on the upper ceiling, and got through most of the green board, which is the 2nd layer on the exterior. I also managed to finish up the beef-up wall including a couple putty pads... I couldn't do all of the them because the caulking wasn't dry around the last two. A word on putty pads. Leave that second piece of paper on as long as you can. That stuff is sticky and hard to get in place with your fingers. And then after you do pull off the 2nd paper, continue to use it to work the pad into where you need it. The paper sticks WAY less than your fingers. Tomorrow we get a new load of lumber. All the materials for the the inner wall frames and joists. Yee haw! Glad to get back to some framing and away from the caulk gun for a bit. Though, I guess I'm going to need it, as we're going to have to build the inside out walls of the Tracking Room first, and they'll have to be caulked and ready to go before we scoot them into place. Side note: I've decided to go with mostly Mogami AES cabling throughout. The exception is the long run through the house to the Piano Room (where the Yamaha C3 will reside). This will be rarely used, so I'm going to go with Redco's "house" analog cable, which is made to the same spec as Mogami's cable. Thoughts and opinions? And now, the pics:

Ok, finally back to work this week. First it was a conference down in LA, then 3 days at golf school in Monterey (OH YEAH!), and now back to the grind. Unfortunately, it's BLAZING hot here this week. My cheap thermometer on the house said 106° in the shade this afternoon. So, I'm a little behind on pics, so I'll try and get some tonight of where we are. The floor is leveled, the inner walls and ceiling are framed, the exterior windows are in, the electrician wired the place up, including the rough for the low voltage cans that will go in the clouds. Yeah, I had to come up with that pretty quick, but I think it's going look cool. See the plan below. We papered the outside today, and ordered the siding. We're waiting now on the HVAC guys to come and put in the cans for the vents into the inner ceiling, and then we'll be ready for our next "rough" inspection. After that, drywall! It's really cool to see the room shapes and the window frames and all. I think the rooms are going to turn out great. We're now trying to deal with a lot of the finish details. We've picked a great wood floor. We still need to figure out fabric and paint colors, as well as the entire scheme for the bathroom tile and sink and whatnot. On the clouds and lighting, I decided to go with low voltage halogen spots and an ambient light via some rope light that will go up in the clouds where you can't see it directly. I think the clouds will hang down a couple inches to make room for the light cans, and then we'll use 2 or 3 inch Roxul for the absorbtion. This should have the desired effect of eliminating the ceiling from the reflection equation. So, on the plan below, each different color of dots is a separate switch, so that I can control the amount of light without dimmers. And the rope light will all be on one switch. So, 6 switches in the Control Room and 3 in the Tracking room. Should look pretty cool. These are probably the lights I'll buy: http://www.ylighting.com/model1137.html. We'll just have to leave some space around them so the insulation doesn't come in contact with them. I guess that's where we are. The cement fiber siding might show up tomorrow, in which case I'll be staining it (110 12' lengths) so we can put it up in the near future. It'll be something between the Redwood and Mahogany color near the bottom of that page. Pics: The lighting plan... the white sections are the clouds that hang from the ceiling. They'll be a wood frame with Roxul in the middle covered in fabric. 3 layers: That's the inside out wall, but with the layers overlapping so the joint will be staggered. Before the inside out wall. After the first inside out wall. It was real fun sliding that thing over. We built it out about 18" from it's final spot and slid it over with a pry bar and some muscle. The inner wall frame start. The last pic is a shot I took on our last round at golf school... yeah, yeah, it's a rough life, but someone has to do it.
