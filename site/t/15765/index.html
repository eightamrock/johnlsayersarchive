gullfo26 March 2011#8
Since the silencer is really more part of either the exterior or interior isolation structure (depending on how your space is arranged) the backer rod etc isn't needed but you do want to caulk and seal, and you want to decouple the passthrough on the isolation boundary to avoid structure transfer as well as avoid any coupling to your ducts etc. On the insulation, if in doubt, cover with plastic or get the duct liner version.

Brian, I have not allowed the used of raw ductboard (or liner) on any of my projects since 1992..... It is polymer lined or it just never sees the light of day.... and for the very reasons you mention - I do not want to introduce fiberglass particles into the air-stream...... not because there is any certain health risk - just simply because it is not a good practice. But you can "over think" these things sometimes - and this is (IMHO) one of those times..... They do not make a little polymer touch up kit for these boards - and (so) every time you take a piece and fit it - you leave a raw, uncoated, edge....... So unless someone is building a box that is dimensioned for full sheets - there are going to be cut edges - no edge coating. This is just a physical reality - and nothing to get excited about...... The idea here is to minimize the probability that there will be a release of particles.... this is accomplished (with cut edges) by making sure that the edges are tight fitting when the construction is completed...... There is no reason to avoid using this product........ The first is to cut slightly oversized - you want to make sure there is just a touch of compression on all surfaces when you put the material inside the box,,,,,,, Next would be to make certain that you clear off any loose particles before that install....... And (for those who might still have concerns) you could always install a duct filter directly at the outlet into the room. If the airflow were to drop over time simply remove the supply register and replace the filter. This only after checking/changing the return air filter at the air handler unit. One note of CAUTION: The placement of a filter at the supply register (or multiple registers as the case might be) is NOT a substitute for a the filter at the air handler unit (which should be installed on the return air side of the system)...... failure to use a return air filter will result in your cooling/heating coils becoming clogged and will cost you a LOT of money in system repairs....... The additional filter is strictly meant to capture any possible release of fiber over time (over kill in my opinion - but I do have to mention it). If the return air is properly filtered - and the duct liner properly installed - in theory this filter should never require changing.
