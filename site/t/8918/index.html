Hey! First and foremost, HUGE thanks to all the people who contribute to cramming this forum with such useful info!! I found it just a week ago and have now comfortably secluded myself from all the non-studiorelated diversions, such as social presence, food and sleep. :? :) Well, how to begin? Quite simply, I grew tired of paying over-sized rental fees every month for my old rehearsal studio in Malmoe-city so I decided I'm going to be my own landlord and build my own place, saving money doing so. The object I have at my disposal is my garage, which is attached to my house in a "one-piece" sort of fashion with a second garage in between. The house is situated way off in the countryside, but even so I do have one neighbour about 3 meters away (his house being 7 meters off). Being outside in his garden, he would probably be annoyed by the sound level that my band (pretty loud rock) would produce. Being indoors, he would probably be able to hear the most of it, f.x. if he was trying to sleep! 8) What I want to do is to make me one single isolated rehearsal room of my 30 m2-sized garage. I will be recording (in my own humble kind of way), so that's also a purpose for the room, and I'll be mixing some stuff as well but I'm really not after the perfect setting for doing that. If mixing works out o.k. once the room has been isolated and acousticly treated, that will only be a bonus. I'm far, far away from being a decent sound engineer anyway. So far, I'm just having fun with it, which is what it's all about! The existing walls consist of (from the inside and outwards): single gypsum board + wooden studs with glasswool + gypsum + airspace + brick wall. I don't know too much about the roof yet, but it's basically isolated with two leaves like the walls. The weak points right now are the old windows in the back and the very leaky garage-door that opens vertically. First question: 1) I'm thinking of replacing the windows with new ones, but nothing expensive. Just normal 3-glass windows. Will that be enough considering the isolation of the inner room described below? Also, the garage-door will just be replaced by another, thicker pair of doors that open horizontally. This is because I want to keep the profile of a garage... and also I don't want to go into the whole masonry-thing. Will a thicker garage-door suffice? (I will put glaswool covered with fabric behind one of the doors and fix that door in the ground. Rubber strips isolating around it.) Ok. Now, here's what I think about the isolation. I have thought a lot about what type of floor-construction would be adequate. At first, I only saw one alternative and that's floating a whole damn room within the existing one. Then I started reading the threads on this forum, where the "floating floor"-model was questioned and I got some new ideas. You see, my garage has a concrete floor, possibly connected to the other garage, but probably not to the main house since it's based on a crawling space and not a solid foundation. And what's more I don't have much height in my room, so I'd really love to ditch the whole "building up my floor 15 centimeters"-concept. Question no 2: 2) Since the concrete floor gets really cold in the winter here in the north, and I don't wanna have to spend a fortune every month keeping the room reasonably warm, is it possible to lay a 2,2 cm fibreboard with a protective sheet underneath and use it as a base for the walls (and ceiling) of the new room? (Laminated floor and carpets covering the new floor-area.) How would it affect (a) isolation? (b) acoustics? I feel psychotic to even ask this, but... Erm... :P :oops: :P :oops: :P Or - putting it in another way - what is the lowest profile that still sounds good acousticly? Rightee! On the (imaginary) fibreboard I would then place floor-sills of wood and build up 7x4,5 cm studs at 90 cm centres. Gypsum boards on both sides of the studs. Glasswool in between. Four walls making a new room inside the garage, sound-class door (-30dB), windows covered or possibly accessible via a "pull-out-hatch" in the new wall. 3rd question: 3) I don't have much of a clue about how effective my construction will be. This is why I want to put one or maybe two gypsum boards on the outer side in order to have the choice of adding more boards on the inner wall after the room is finished. So! Let's say I've got only one board on the outer side and two on the inner side. If I add one or two more, how will the ratio 1:3 and 1:4 gypsum boards affect (a) isolation? (b) resonance from the wall (and thus the acoustics)? I think I'll stop here for now. I hope someone has the heart to help a complete moron put his grand plans into action... :roll: I'm a total new-beginner at this... Be kind...? Cheerios!! /JOHAN the Swede 8)

camistan12 June 2007#8
Johan, The 1st layer of gypsum after your outer wall is causing you to not have a M-A-M setup. Your outer wall is considered your mass, the 1st sheet of drywall after that is mass and the inner drywall is another mass. Seems like you need to omit the drywall after your outer wall. The way you have it set up it's M-A-M-A-M. Like Ro said, check out the stickies for great explainations on this. Also, if you research, there are LOADS of info on this topic on here.

andrebrito17 June 2007#15
First decide if you want to rehearse at night or not with your metal hardcore punk rock band :D This fact will define your sound insulation requirements. In Portugal for residential areas the noise limits at night are 45 dB(A) and during day 55 dB(A), maybe it's slightly different in Sweden. 10 dB of extra sound insulation does not appear to be much but it is ! Let's say your band is playing at 100 dB(A), your current garage attenuates 20 dB(A), distance attenuates 15 db(A) and your neighbor's wall attenuates another 20 db(A), you will end up in your neighbor's house at 45 db(A). Oh, that's fine... after all you don't need any sound insulation at all ! :-) Unfortunately this is not that simple since the type of noise you are producing has quite a lot of low frequency energy, meaning this will be a bit little more difficult to solve. (There are also a bunch of rules concerning environmental acoustics that are too long to explain here but 45 dB in a SLM can be translated into an higher value depending in the characteristics of sound etc etc) Also your windows and door are weak spots of your garage and must be replaced or improved, particularly if they are faced towards your neighbor's house. That means those windows have to be turned into a double windows system of different thickness (NOT triple glass windows!), the garage door has to be changed. I would probably say to do a room-in-a-room project although it depends really on how loud you are really playing etc etc The way I see it you have two choices: 1) solve the weak points and do a room-in-a-room project 2) solve the weak points and build a second wall inside the room way from the wall that faces into your neighbor's house and also in the ceiling. You can built a structure that you normally see in music studios construction or use something like this to place the gypsumboard (http://www.cdm.be/documents/pdfs/cdm-iso-t.pdf) Also keep in mind you will probable need some kind of HVAC solution to your problem... PS : if you want to float the floor there are several brands that sell materials for that purpose CDM, DANOSA, TEXSA, AURALEX etc PS 2 - it would be nice for you to get a cheap SLM (sound level meter), play your CDs loudly in your garage and check for the difference near your neighbor's wall. The cheap SLM won't show you real values (you need a calibrated SLM for that) but will give you an idea what is the real attenuation Andre
