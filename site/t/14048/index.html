I have been reading through various VERY useful threads on this site for many years now and am well aware of the MASS > space > MASS
Spring, not space. The "S" in MSM means "Spring". It comes form the principle of physics on which it is based: A mass suspended from another mass by a spring will have a certain resonant frequency, governed by the properties of the system.
Although I have not measured the exact distance from floor to the bottom of the wooden joists overhead, I can see in my mind's eye that there will NOT be enough space for me to install a MASS > space > MASS ceiling and then simply throw a new and obviously better, waterproof roof on the top.
Did you look at page 77 of Rod's book? If you do something like that, you don't have to lose more than an inch or two of height. In other words, your final inner-leaf celing can be just a couple of inches lower than the bottom of the existing joists. I started out with a very similar situation: The plastic garbage is now gone, replaced with a full 18mm plywood deck, and asphalt shingles. Works great!

Better to go stick with your existing concrete floor, which is just fine acoustically.
Regarding the "inner" ceiling (as just mentioned in paragraph above) - for the sake of retaining the fairly limited height, I would have to devise a fairly complicated system of new joists that would have to be tied into the framed wall structures, and that sit "inside" the existing joists so that they present a new surface for ceiling boards an inch or so below the existing joists
Well, it does not have to be complex at all! Very simple, in fact: your new joists will just sit on top of your new inner-leaf framing. Period. Nothing complicated about that!
OR I would have to basically fit the inner ceiling to RC suspended from the bottom of the EXISTING joists - which would have whatever external roof is chosen/suggested fixed to the top, so would be sacrificing decoupling I suppose?
Well, if you use or resilient channel, or alternatively clips plus hat channel, then you won't sacrifice much in terms of isolation. It won't be quite as good as a fully decoupled ceiling, but it would still work. But since you didn't say anything at all about sound levels, or even the purpose of your room, or even what you have in mind, roughly, it's hard to say if that would be enough or not.
I just wanted to get this online as soon as possible in case this external roof thing is a simple solution issue.
Simple solution: Rip off the plastic and dump it. replace it with plywood, roofing felt over that, asphalt shingles over that. Done! Then either hang your inner leaf below the joists with RC or with clips plus hat channel. Done! OR if you do need better isolation, the skip the RC / clips / hat channel, and just do a normal inner-leaf ceiling supported on the normal inner-leaf walls. Done! It doesn't have to be complicated. It just has to be done right. - Stuart -

"X" number of layers of "X" for my new roof, "X" number of layers of "X" for my wall to seal off the double garage doors,
It should be pretty much the same all around: For example, if you are going to use 3 + 3 layers of 5/8" drywall (or the equivalent thereof) on your roof / ceiling MSM, then it makes no sense to only put 2 + 1 layers of 1/2" drywall on the MSM system for the garage door wall. All the walls should also be 3 + 3 layers of 5/8" or equivalent in that case. If not, then your isolation will only be as good as your weakest link. So if your garage door turns out to be your weakest link, and you have no way of beefing that up, then it makes little sense to do multiple layers on the roof, since the sound will just leak out through the door anyway, no matter how good the roof is.

Actually, the garage door itself probably WILL still be a third leaf! It doesn't have to be sealed to act like a leaf. "OSB or Plywood? " Either. What you do not want to put on this roof is shingles. The manufacture will not cover anything less than 2 inch rise in a 12 inch run. And for good reason. Water will dam up under the tabs, especially if you get much snow and or freezing water and rot the roof in a few seasons. A roll roofing or torch down is suggested in these cases or another comparable sheet type covering.
