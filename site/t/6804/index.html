GapeOne wrote:
Q3: If I'm better off putting up all the walls in my design, what do I do about the space between the new walls I put up and the existing walls already there as there will be minimal space between them?
on the walls with the concrete wall opposite - you don't need the additional wall, just the air gap. in fact you could use 2x4 walls and keep the gap the same - the gap is really measured wall to wall not stud to stud. Rod's Gervais's book can really explain the construction details so I recommend getting a copy.
GapeOne wrote:
Q4: Due to the low, barely 7' ceiling, I don't plan on raising the floors nor adjusting the ceilings. Instead I simply plan on laying carpetting down over the existing concrete-then-linoleum floors. Is this the best route to go or what else can I do to treat the floors?
i'd say try small carpets to break up reflections and some absorption on the ceiling (opposite the carpets) so you can keep some life in the room but treat for flutter echos.
GapeOne wrote:
Q5: I don't care about retaining sound in my studio as noise is not an issue. That being said, I'm I better off insolation any walls I put up, or is simply leaving them un-insolated the way to go?
I'd insulate them regardless since it will help dampen the wall resonance.

if you're constructing an inner wall opposite the concrete or block one, the air gap is the distance from the sheetrock to the cement wall, so a 2x4 stud + 1" gap will be 4.5" and that should be fine along with insulation to dampen the sheetrock wall. make sure the insulation does not touch the block/cement wall since you want an air gap there or things can get moldy pretty quickly... i think you new design - facing the window is good - but you're still space constrained so its really a matter of deciding how you'll use the space you have. even without the angled walls, you could treat a smal rectagular room so its good enough. the carpets on the floor would cover hard surfaces alternating with the ceiling treatment. (alternating was the word my feeble brain was looking for the other day...) this way you keep some life in the room and reduce the flutter echos...

orangenumerik22 December 2006#23
Hi GapeOne As i read in your post, isolation (sounds going out) of the studio is not a concern right ? Here's the thing. You may want to build a studio from wich sounds escape and it's fine with me, BUT what you don't (usually) want is a studio with a poor separation between the Control room and the Booth\ studio. And to my knowledge there is just no shortcuts to get there, it's just one of those things you just can't do halfway and still have great results. If you are not decoupling your walls from the bulding walls and ceiling from the floor above you are loosing huge amout of separation between your booth and CR, due to mechanical transmission / flanking path. Maybe you're already aware of all that and my appologies if ti's just old news to you. Some suggestion for your existing basements walls If you are thigt on budget : Tear up the drywalls at CR and booth location and put RC channels, since Those two leaves share the same frame make sure that both leaves are not coupled, independent RC and a gap in the drywall. Hope this is clear enough. :? Better : Tear up the drywalls at CR and booth location and build another frame. any option you choose then Do a double frame construction for the rest with a minimum of two layer of 5/8 drywall on each walls (greenglue inbetween if you can ) and Rockwool in the walls cavities slightly pressed. Caulk every gaps and seams, corner and be generous.
