CptVagabond29 August 2008#2
The most important aspect of a control room is usually the listening position. For an optimum listening position, you should position your desk so that when you're sitting at it you're facing the 8 foot wall, with your ears at about 38% of the length of the room. Also it's generally a bad idea to put your monitors on your desk, as the desk causes unwanted reflections. If you can wing it, get some speaker stands that you can fill with sand, and position your monitors on those so that they make an equilateral triangle with your listening position. Doing all this may require that you lose the corner couch in favor of a love seat, or you could put it where the vocal booth is now and move the vocal booth somewhere else. I don't know, you'll have to play around with it a bit. Isolation is generally achieved with mass, so good isolation is pretty expensive to build. Check out some of the Studio Construction threads to see how those guys are building their walls. Most often I see people using 2-3 layers of drywall glued together with Green Glue, followed by a layer of insulation with good acoustic properties (OC 703 or Roxul or some form of mineral wool). I'm not sure how important it is to float your walls, but I imagine it would help minimize transference of vibrations to the floor and ceiling. Hope this helps!
