i guess it depends on how much isolation you need for your band. if you can level the floor with concrete and polish that, it may be inexpensive enough. the walls need treatment - bass traps and something to cut echos and reflections without making it dead. reduce the size of the window and still leave it large enough for light and egress. on the ceiling a few clouds. in the control room - bass trapping front and rear. side and overhead absorption on first reflection points.

actually, the existing window would be the same outside, you're only building a wall inside to hold a stronger window to reduce the amount of sound escaping. the blue "isolation" looks like foam acoustic treatment and not sound isolation. sound isolation and sound treatment are too different things. if you increase sound isolation, chance are you need to increase treatments, or use some tactics to reduce the amount of treatments.

gullfo10 October 2007#6
step one - isolate the space. step two - treat it. sometime you can get away with both at the same time but in this case, its probably simpler to build out the isolation first, do measurements using Room EQ Wizard, then add the treatments. in the case of the control room - you know you'll need lots of bass trapping, front, back, and probably top (to save space) and it should be broadband when possible. treatments in the live room should be broadband to avoid inconsistencies. for treatments - if you have access to rigid fiberglass/mineral wool then you can build some effective bass traps and absorbers fairly easily and should be in your budget. diffusion can be acheived in your live room by staggering the treatments on the wall and ceiling so you leave gaps between them, and on opposing walls. this stretches the "absorber budget" quite a bit. the styrofoam may be useful as a diffuser and if you have enough of it and its cheap, it can't hurt to try - with the caveat to be wary of fire hazards. there is a site which shows making a skyline diffuser from foam and it may be more efficient than random.
