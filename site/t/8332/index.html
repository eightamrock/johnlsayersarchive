Hey folks, So I'm looking for some design advice, and my studio's already half built :roll: . For some background on my project, check out this thread to see my design, and this thread for construction info (and build pictures!) My original floor plan was like so:
External image, not preserved — original: http://sanderspianostudio.com/samples/studio/floorplan5.jpg
Right now, this is what is built:
External image, not preserved — original: http://sanderspianostudio.com/samples/studio/floorPlanRevisedBefore.jpg
The interior is just bare framing - no doors, drywall or even insulation yet. The idea was that I'd finish out the bathroom, waiting room and piano room first so that those spaces would be usable, then build the "inner" framing for the remaining space (marked "future control room/booth") and finish it up later. Here's the thing, though - the "future control room/booth" space, as it is now (i.e. not split in two like the first floor plan above), is very cool - the ceiling is much higher there than in the piano room. We're thinking that the piano will sound better on that side because of the higher ceiling, and it would just be a more pleasant space to have as the "main" room/teaching studio. So if we follow that line of thinking, the room that's already framed up (marked "piano room" above) would be "my" room, i.e. the control room (we will have to sacrifice the booth, which I think I can live with). For good stereo imagery I understand the control room should be symmetrical, so I'm wondering if framing can be added to accomplish this, like so:
External image, not preserved — original: http://sanderspianostudio.com/samples/studio/floorPlanRevisedAfter.jpg
Which would give me a symmetrical room, albeit very wide and not very deep. Would this be a workable shape for a control room, assuming a reasonable amount of acoustic treatment? What do you guys think? Part of me feels pretty ridiculous changing plans mid-project like this, but at the same time I'm so thrilled with the building so far that somehow I'm not beating myself up over it--I just think it would be a shame to split up the space on the right. Sure, I should have designed it that way to begin with, but there's nothing I can do about it now, and as Rod says, "You gots to deal with what you gots to deal with". Looking forward to hearing what folks have to say. ---Devin
gullfo3 March 2007#2
I think your original plan was a good one. when you do the construction you should also consider putting the door to the bath on the side facing the waiting room. this way you could add an air lock in the resulting space or expand the control room (or shift it down to expand the drum booth). note: the top of the live room wall is a treatment wall not a third leaf.
