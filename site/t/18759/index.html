Soundman2020 wrote:
For interior walls - I'm wanting to do a live room, plus control room, as well as a small vocal booth (possibly in the corner of the live room?). What dimensions would be ideal for this?
That depends on what you want to do, and far how you want to go! For example, for optimum critical listening room, the specs from EBU, call for a floor area of not less than 40m2 for a reference room, and 30m2 for a high quality control room. That's about 430 square feet and 320 square feet, respectively. On the other hand ITU specs call for "20 - 60 m2" for a stereo room, and "30 - 70 m2" for a multi-channel room (5.1, 7.1, etc.). 20m2 is about 215 square feet. On the other-other hand, John has built highly successful studios inside a shipping container, and many people here on the forum have built great studios in small spaces. So, if you are aiming for a world-class studio that meets international specs, then you need a floor area of about 250 square feet or more for your control room, and about 5 times that for your live room, for a total of about 1500 ft2 or so. Or if you don't need to go to that level, then you can do a great studio in less than that. It seems you have about about 250 ft2 to work with, so I would try to split that to about 1/3 for the control room, and the other 2/3 for the live room. That would give you about 85 ft2 for the CR and 165 ft2 for the LR. However, I guess the dimensions you show are the absolute limits of your space, and you'll be losing some for that for the isolation walls and other things, so do take that into account. - Stuart -

However, all these ratios seem to assume you're building a rectangular room. If I were to go with the corner design you shared above, how do these translate?
That is, indeed, a problem! Mode calculations are very simple for rectangular rooms with 3 sets of parallel walls, but as soon as you add extra walls, or angle any of them, then the calculators are no longer accurate. For a slightly skewed rectangle, such as a typical RFZ design, you can use the average dimensions of the room to get a rough idea of how it will perform, but for a five or six or seven sided room with greatly angles walls, that's not going to work too well. On the other hand, there wont be any axial modes at all in such a room (except vertically), only tangentials and obliques, so that takes some problems off the table already. There are ways to predict the modal response of such a room, but they involve FEM/FEA software and an operator who knows how to set up the boundary conditions correctly, and knows how to interpret the results.... Having said all that, if the room is somewhat close to rectangular, then you could just plug in the average dimensions, ignore the length and width axials, and get a very rough idea of modal response... But I would choose a room size / shape that is similar to one of the ones that John or someone else has done successfully, and use that as the basis for yours. Just aim to make it as large as you can, and you should be fine.
