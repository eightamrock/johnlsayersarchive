lovecow31 December 2007#2
Zero, I don't have a definitive answer for you, but I can tell you a story. :) I worked on a live room of roughly the same size a number of years ago. The existing room already had ample low frequency control and absorption. In other words, the "RT" was completely acceptable. The walls were gypsum wallboard; the ceiling was acoustical tile, about 15'-18' high; and the floor was hardwood. There were some nice built-in bass traps for LF control; some wall panels and hangings; some area rugs; the room sounded OK. The complaint the owner and recordist had was that they had to really work to get a good recording. Even though the room sounded OK, they had a hard time finding the best placements for microphones when recording just about anything in the room, be it a solo guitarist or a group of singers or a full drum kit. They would eventually "get there," but for what sounded like a decent room, it just seemed to take a disproportionate amount of time to "get there." What I suggested to them was replacing about 25% of the ceiling tiles with diffusers; then taking the equivalent area of removed ceiling tiles and adding that much absorption to the upper half of the walls. After doing this, it was determined that about a dozen or so more diffusers were needed on the upper walls, mixed in with the absorbers. (All treatment sizes were 2'x2'.) The end result was a room that sounded more or less the same, but much more uniform. Both the owner and recordist found that they spent about 1/4 the time miking up the instruments, thus leaving much more time to capture great recordings. I was fortunate enough to hear performances in the room both "before" and "after." It was apparent that, even though the general quality of the room was unchanged, the "after" version possessed a character that was more conducive to music recording. So, while there's no quantitative answer as to the amount of diffusion you should be considering, I think it's safe to say you will need some diffusion for the room. IMO, start with the ceiling, evaluate, then add more on the upper walls if you feel you need it. (At least, that's one approach...) HTH.
