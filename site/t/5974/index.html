guitardad7210 April 2006#4
OK, A STC number is not really what your looking for although it will give you a ball park number, only kind of. STC was designed to measure the frequency of speach. Although it measures down to 125kH, the bottom is centered at 500Hz and the top at 5K, so it's almost useless for loud music and more specifically the bass drum and anything bass. What you are looking for is LF TL ( low frequencies transmission loss). In general the lower the frequency measured the more MASS AIR MASS construction required to stop the sound and higher (low) frequencies will be taken care of, but any consturction method can have flanking paths which can allow certian frequencies to pass right through... Most on this forum seem to use LF TL @ 50K = ??? (the amount of decibels desired of TL) Drums measure between 95 to 115 DB (in general) Heres a qoute from knightfly:
At lower frequencies, the threshhold of human hearing is around 55 dBa - so, if you're generating 110 dBa, you'll need about 50 dB MINIMUM TL at 50 hZ - this isn't easy but you can get close by using your removed wallboard as inserts between studs and against your outside sheathing - two layers of 5/8 against the sheathing between studs, CAULKED fully with NO AIR GAPS between outer sheathing or between layers, then fully insulated air space, then insulated inner stud frame, then 3 layers of 5/8 wallboard, will give about 45 dB TL at 50 hZ, which means that sounds around 100 dB will just start to be audible on the outside wall.
So, you have to carefully look at your current floor, whether or not you want to rip it up and try and create and isolated slab, your current floor should be creating a big sound leak acting like a drum head and flanking certian frequencies (unless I am miss understanding the type of insulation) AND PLEASE NOTE: floating a floor is not easy, your current budget won't permit it and it would have to be very precisely figured out. Most likely your current slab will do. Floating a floor incorrectly can be disasterious. You are going to want to take off everything on the current walls from the inside to the "old panel boarding" and put beef up layers of drywall inbetween the studs. Drywall is the material of choice. The siding may create a 3rd leaf depending if it's wood or plastic... well more so if there are any air gaps between panel board and siding. Please search for tripple leaf on this site. You should be constructing new 2x4 walls atleat 1" away from the current walls and if you add space "AIR" between walls you will be increasing TL. The ceiling you should consider Green Glue (please do a search on this forum for it)... you can use less drywall with Green Glue between two layers... 4 sheets drywall = 2 sheets of drywall with green glue in between approxamitely!!! This makes sence for the weight you'll be able to place on ceiling joists or truss. Ceiling load is something very important to figure out, don't want to kill any one. Of course ceiling will be MASS AIR MASS. Green glue is about $150 per case, one case will do about 6, 8x4 sheets of drywall... I think. Someone correct me if I'm wrong. Another good source to understand all this is: www.acoustics101.com BUT leave the manufactures suggestions of there own products out and the floating floor cr^p!!! Best advice I can give... buy Rods book: [url=http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1598630342/ref=pe_snp_342/102-4991612-6664139?n=283155]Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros (Paperback) by Rod Gervais [/url] For $30, it will save you money, time and confusion. knightfly17 April 2006#15
Your room, at its original dimensions, is not bad modally down to about 50 hZ; below that, it's a bit bass light. This is considering all three types of room modes, not just axial. For a one-room studio, you need to consider building some portable treatments to be used during mixdowns - this is because for mixing you want a deader sounding room - otherwise, you'll tend to think there's enough 'verb in your mix when it's really the room you're listening in. Check the "stickies" in the Acoustics forum for some ideas on treatments... Steve
