Folks, I'm gonna build a properly floating floor for my two rehearsal room ive built in the 2nd floor of my building and that I previously mentioned the flanking problem I had with them. I managed to find neoprene/rubber pads designed for floating systems. Their data sheets show me the ammount of weight they take in order to compress a certain %. So with some math, I know for a fact how much weight that pad must receive to compress the amount of mm i need. With some research, I found 10%-15% should be okay. So, My plan is to sum all the static weight (the layers of mass, the average people on it, equipment, drumkit, etc) and divide that by the amount of weight I want my pads to take, that will give me the number of pads I need to purchase. That calculation is for an equal amount of weight distributed across the room for each pad. Now, should I take into consideration where the drumkit will be put for example and distribute more weight underneath that section? Or placed equally should do fine? The main plan here is to put these pads, fill everything with 20mm rockwool and add 1 sheet of 20mm MDF on top. I cannot add more mass because my ceiling is very low already. This floating floor will be decoupled from the inner walls The goal is to gain 3db of sound isolation to the outside because we are over by 3db by the local regulation in order to get our permit to work as a rehearsal space. So you be aware of the problem, the sound coming from the 2nd floor (where the rooms are) are gettin into the first floor and and also the floor is vibrating the whole structure, so its cancellating the 2 leaf system so to speak.

Hey Javier, just dropped in here on the forum to see how your build is going. Too much other stuff going on for me right now to post here regularly, but I wanted to make a suggestion here: why don't you at least try Lilith's decoupling suggestion, partially? with a "quick and dirty" riser that will at least give you an idea. Easy and cheap to do, for a quick test: Place a thick layer of rock wool (5cm thick at least, more if you can) spread over a 4'x8' section of the floor, in that far corner where you normally have your drums set up. Place a full sheet of really thick plywood or MDF (19mm or more) over the rock wool, just resting on top of it, taking care that it does not touching the walls. Set up your drum kit / bass amp / whatever on top of that, bash away as hard as you can, and test for vibrations, sound levels, etc. My guess is that you'll see a fairly decent reduction in vibration. Will it be 3dB? Maybe. You might be lucky. I think we tend to underestimate just how much impact sound gets into floor from the drum kit itself, the bass amp, etc. Sure, airborne is a still a major issue, but impact is a biggie too and risers can help there. This might not get you as much as you need, but it's worth a try! For the cost of a bag of rockwool and a sheet of plywood, you'll at least get an idea of whether or not a proper well-built drum riser would work for you. If you want me to come over with my sound level meter when you are ready to test, just let me know. I could drop by next week, if you want. - Stuart -
