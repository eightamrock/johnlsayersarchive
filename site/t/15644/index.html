Soundman202025 February 2011#6
Hi Marco, About your questions:
I consider the second solution a better one more isolation on bass frequencies...and I think better stc values...
No, the STC value of the second idea would be much worse than the first idea. In fact, it would be about the same as the STC value for the existing brick wall, as it stands right now!
what do you think? is air space better?
Yes. It is the ONLY way to build a good isolating wall at low cost.
but the first person says that gluying something on the brick wall is not so useful,
The first person is right! Brick is MUCH more dense than drywall. Gluing rubber and drywall to it will not add any useful mass to the wall, so it will not help to isolate. You would need to add about sixteen layers of 15mm drywall just to increase the isolation by 6 db!!! so one layer is not useful.
is the layer of high density rubber not so useful if glued to the brick wall?
Nope! Not useful at all. It will make very little difference to the isolation that you already have.
1) the first person suggest a "light" version: 5cm airhole from the original brick wall 5 cm of rockwoll glued to a single or two ply drywall
Well, that's kind of the idea, but not really. First, you need to seal the brick: just paint it with something that closes up all the tiny little "pores" (air holes), so that the entire wall is airtight. You could also use plaster (stucco), which would be better. Then build a wooden frame at least 10cm away from that brick wall, and nail two layers of 15mm drywall to that frame, with both layers ON THE SAME SIDE of the frame (in other words, one layer, then another layer right next to it. Fill the entire gap between the brick and the drywall with mineral wool or fiberglass insulation. You have to do that on all four sides of the room, and also the ceiling. The new frame that you build must NOT touch the brick wall or the old ceiling anywhere, not even one nail. - Stuart -

Soundman20202 March 2011#8
but I'm also thinking about the inside-out wall construction that is often suggested in this forum and that I know gives really good results!!!
IT gives very good results when done properly! And it is exactly the same principle as I mentioned above: you have the existing brick wall, then an air gap that is filled with insulation, then one or two layers of 16mm drywall, then the stud frame. The only difference with "inside out" construction is that the stud frame faces the room, instead of the cavity, but the concept is exactly the same.
what advantages can I obtain with the inside out walls?
"Inside out" is very useful when you don't have much space, and it saves you money on wood, since the framing faces the room and can be used to attach the acoustic treatment, instead of building additional frames (as you would with "normal" construction).
my friend that used it for is studio tells me that it's a more difficoult way of contruction, and so I don't know if it's even more expensive!!!
Yes, it is a bit more difficult to do, because you have to build the walls lying down on the floor, with the drywall on them, then lift them into place, and they are very heavy! Cost should be a bit cheaper with inside-out, if you plan carefully. - Stuart -

gullfo2 March 2011#10
the lower plate is secured into the floor using decoupled anchors and the upper plate uses isolation brackets to stabilize the top. optionally, if this is not stabilized from an adjoining structure, then isolation structure needs to be self-supporting through bracing let-in to the framing or sheet material (plywood, OSB etc) to ensure that lateral (side-ti-side) shifts do not occur and the structure assemblies act to prevent inward or outward movement. typically this latter approach also uses decoupled anchors to ensure the bottom plate is stable.
