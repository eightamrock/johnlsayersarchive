sharward2 January 2007#6
It seems that footfall is your biggest problem that you're trying to solve, yet with only a 7 foot ceiling, you're very limited in your options. I think code calls for 5/8" drywall on ceilings... Although two layers of 1/2" might pass. You'll need to check with your local building department. But the greater concern is the 7 foot ceiling: most codes call for 7' 6" minimum for habitable space. Check with the building department on that before you go further. You're also adding a lot of weight to the joists. How sure are you that they'll handle the extra ~5 psf weight? Ventilation? Is the floor above air tight? Sometimes 3rd leaf can't be avoided. Sometimes it can if you rip the drywall off what would hae been the middle leaf. Pictures are worth 1,000 words. --Keith :mrgreen:

guitardad725 January 2007#11
I'm not going to answer that question, because if I'm wrong, people could die.
Got to back Sharward on that and verify he is not being rude. None of us on this forum are qualified to give an answer to that question, let alone try to guess an answer it without seeing it in person or at least a pic. At least get a qualified person like a structural engineer to look at it and give you the numbers to what you can safely add. You'll have both a dead load and a live load for the joists. Dead load = all the parts of structure, and Live load = all the furniture and people that could be above. That fact that "the furniture upstairs is light" has nothing to do with making a decision about what load you can add to the joists. This is a huge safety concern... imagine you just built with out factoring in any loads... 10 years down the road you or a new property owner throws a family party for 20 people and the floor gives out !!! OUCH. If you want to start and guess look for an online "span calculator". Figure in 40 psf for the live load above (OR look up your local code to find out required loads for room type above) AND add up weight of all existing structure and what you plan to add for dead load (requires a search for weight of all materials) THEN add in a safety margin (say 10% to 20% ??)... and you'll end up with a GUESS number that I would NOT trust until you have a PRO check it out. Please let us know how you make out. Marc
