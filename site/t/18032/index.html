Soundman202015 December 2012#4
Hi Joksu, and welcome to the forum! :) Marc is right: Low ceilings are not good for acoustics. However, there's also a saying by a well-respected studio designer: If that's all you have, then that's what it will have to be! As long as you are aware that it won't be a world-class studio, then it can be turned into something usable. After all, if your choice is a not-so-good place or no place at all, then I reckon "not so good" wins! Having said that, please do realize that this is only going to be a "reasonable" studio, not a "great" one. It can probably be treated so that it is usable, and you'll probably be able to turn out pretty good work there, so if that is all you want, then it can be done. I would say that you can, indeed, build a control room in there, and probably even an iso booth, but I don't think you'll be able to record drums in there, due to the low ceiling. Drums need high ceilings to sound good, so if you do plan to record drums, you would have to make that room as dead as possible, record with close mics on everything, then add ambiance electronically, but even then I suspect that the drums will sound "thin" and "muddy". That would probably be the best you could hope for.
But I can still make a mixroom from it?
I think so, yes.
How you would make it?
First, the room ratio is terrible! I would build false side walls such that the new boundary is 20 cm on each side, so the room becomes 3.35 m wide. I then would set up the mix position against the far end of the room, symmetrically, with the speakers spaced about 80 cm form the side walls, and therefore about 175 cm from each other, 120cm above the floor (acoustic axis), and angled inwards at 30°. I would then set your chair so that your ears are about 160 cm from the front wall. That means the rear wall should be about 440 cm from the front wall. You would then need to install the usual treatment: large superchunks in the vertical corners, thick absorption panels between the speakers and the front wall, as well as at the first reflection points (including the ceiling), thick absorption across the entire rear wall, spaced a bit away from it, and some absorption on the side walls, as needed to control the reverb time. The measure with REW, and see what else needs doing. That would be my approach. Maybe someone else can come up with a better plan. - Stuart -
