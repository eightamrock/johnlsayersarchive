Soundman20209 September 2014#105
Well, I'm all wet then. Or in light of the unnecessary expense, I should say I took a bath on that one!
Can you send a couple of photos of that cloud, and the room in general? That would help. The additional path length is about 44" (in other words, the reflected sound is taking a path about 44" longer than the direct sound) so one way you can do this is with the "string trick". So measure the distance from the acoustic center of your speaker right speaker (NOTE! "Acoustic center!"), to the tip of your measurement mic, add 44" to that measurement, and cut a piece of string that long (or make it a bit longer, so you have some extra to attach the string at each end). Attach one end to the acoustic center of your right speaker (masking tape works well), and attach the other end of the string to a mic stand set up exactly at the point where the tip of your measurement mic was. So there's now a "sag" in the middle of the string that is exactly 44" longer than the direct path. Now carefully move that sagging middle part of that string around the room, to see exactly where it touches "something": It will only touch one thing in the room, since there is only one reflection there. My bet is that it touches some point on the cloud. Mark that point, then take a photo so that I can see where the mark is. It's a fairly strong reflection, so my guess is that it is something hard, such as the wood frame of the cloud. There's a second reflection worth looking into as well: this one only affects the right speaker, and the path is 100" longer, so repeat the same procedure with another piece of string, 100" longer than the speaker-mic distance.
Also what do you suggest for the 2k-8k range? Just some more panels on the walls?
Yes, but it needs to be heavier and thinner than what you are doing so far. I'd suggest one inch thick panels of OC-705 would be good, pressed up tight against the wall (no air gap behind). 703 is too light for such high frequencies, so it has to be 705. 1" USG Thermafiber (4pcf) would also work.
I was concerned about a dip at around 525? Does this look like a problem?
It is most likely some type of SBIR artifact, or it could be something to do with reflections off the desk or console, or something else like that. It's not modal, and is likely positional, so I'd wait a bit on that one, to figure out what it is... it might even improve from the other treatment along the way. It has always been there, and only changes slightly with the treatment you have done so far, so I don't think it is cloud-related. But you might get lucky, and find that it goes away when you reposition the clouds! - Stuart -

Soundman202012 September 2014#109
Can you tell me what graph and reading specifically it is that is telling you about the reflection so that I will know what to look at when I do the next reading with something soft on the floor to see if it went away.
It's on the "Impulse" button, which shows the impulse response of the room. (You can also see it on the "Filtered IR" button: same thing). Turn on the "ETC" curve, turn off the others, then smooth it just a tad, at 0.1ms, and switch the graph mode to show %FS instead of dB FS. Zoom in as necessary so you can see just the first 50ms or so. You'll see somthing like this:
Image not preserved: amac-ir-etc.jpg
The first spike (at time=0ms) is the actual impulse itself. The second one (at time=3.18ms) is that reflection we are talking about. There's another smaller one 7.48 ms, which is your 100" reflection. You are probably right about that being the side wall, so that one will disappear when you put your first reflection point treatment on there. Those are the two "biggies". There's other stuff in there, but not worth looking at yet, until you get your desk in and some more treatment done.
I also seem to be having a hard time finding 705 in one inch thickness. Are two inch panels going to be ineffective for the 2-8k range or should I cut them in half?
Here's the specs for the 700 series of products, from Owens Corning:
Image not preserved: 703-specs.jpg
Take a look at the differences in the properties for 1" and 2" 705: The 1" stuff absorbs practically nothing at 125 Hz, and pretty low at 250 Hz, and absorbs really well above 1kHz, whereas the 2" stuff is significantly higher in the low end. You don't want more absorption in the low end: you need it up around 2k and up. Both the 1" and the 2" do well up there, but the 2" also takes too much out of the low end. If you can't get 1" 705, then you'd actually be better off with 1" 703, than with 2" 705. I wouldn't suggest trying to split a panel of 705 in half: that might not be so easy! :shock:
I'm hoping that the additional mid-high treatment will take care of the 100" reflection.
It does seem to be the side wall, so I'd expect that one to die once you get your absorption on the first reflection points. - Stuart -
Soundman202012 September 2014#110
One other thing. You said: "the only hard surface that the center is touching with equal distance between the tape..." It doesn't have to be the center of the tape! It could be at any point along the tape... All that the graph tells you is that there is a path that is 44" longer than the direct path, but the actual point that is causing the reflection could be close to the speaker, or close to the mic: Not necessarily the same distance from both. Yes, it is likely to be the same, but not guaranteed: only high frequency sound acts like light rays, bouncing off surfaces at the same angle it arrives. Lower frequencies act more like expanding spheres, and less like rays, so the reflection point could be some other place in that case. All you know for sure is the distance of the increased path length. So see if the tape will hit any other points in the room. I doubt it, but it's worth checking, to be certain. - Stuart -
