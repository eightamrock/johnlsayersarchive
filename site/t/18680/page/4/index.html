Soundman202029 May 2014#83
For the lights I bought two track lighting fixtures, for the ceiling lights I cut those boxes in but built 2x6 enclosures around them and acoustically sealed them
It's not just about sealing, although that is very important too: It is also about replacing the lost mass behind the electrical box. When you cut a hole through your drywall, you are removing something that has a surface density of roughly 24 kg/m2 (assuming 2 layers of 16mm drywall), so you need to build an enclosure behind the electrical box that has the same surface density, and THEN seal it. The thin plastic or metal of an electrical box has practically no mass to it, so you need to make up for that "missing mass" with the enclosure. You can do that with drywall, plywood, MDF, OSB, natural wood, putty pads, or whatever. It doesn't matter how you build up the mass again, as long as it is there. And sealed. - Stuart -

Should I shoot it with absolutely nothing in the room including treatment first and post that?
Absolutely! That will be your "baseline" test, which will show the room in it's most ugly, disgusting, terrible, worst possible acoustic state, but it also clearly reveals all of the bad stuff that needs fixing with the treatment. That first test in the completed-but-empty room is essential for helping you make decisions on how to treat the room. One very important point, though: When you do that very first test, make several very careful measurements of the exact location of the tip of your measurement mic, so you can always get it back to that precise location in the room for all subsequent tests. So set up your mic at the mix position, exactly where the middle of your head will be while mixing, at ear height, pointing straight ahead and angled upwards at about 45°, and carefully measure that location. Do one REW test run like that with just the left speaker running, another test with just the right speaker, and a third test with both speakers. You might want to also do a couple of additional tests, with the mic moved over a couple of inches left and a couple of inches right, until it is in the locations where your EARS will be when mixing, and pointing at the speaker on that side. That's not essential, but it's another "nice to have" set of data that you might want to refer to later. Your testing should be done with REW calibrated correctly by using hand-held sound level meter, so that REW shows the same reading as the hand-held (you can't use an iPhone app for this: it really does have to be a good quality meter). Set the level of each individual speaker to 80 dB, and run the REW tests for the individual tests at that level. Then when you run the combined test with both speakers on, DO NOT CHANGE THE LEVELS! the total level will rise all by itself to either 83 dB or 86 dB, depending on what test you are running (83 for non-coherent or uncorrelated sound, 86 for coherent correlated). Always put your mic back in the exact same location, and run the tests at the exact same levels, for all subsequent readings. If you don't put the mic in the same position for later tests, then you cannot compare the tests. Each one will still be a valid test, but if they are taken at different points in the room then you can't compare them, and cannot use them to decide on treatment, since they are measuring a different set of acoustic responses. This is VERY important! There can be big differences in the sound field just a small distance away. So take a set of readings as above each time you install treatment or furniture, so you have a full record of the results after each devices goes in. That way you can clearly see what is working, what isn't, and what still needs to be done. And of course, post the MDAT file from REW here on the forum at each stage too, so we can help you analyze it! - Stuart -

Built me a very HEAVY door that I hung myself... 125 lbs, 2 pieces of MDF laminated together with 2 pieces of 1/4" plywood, I used a 2x6 for the hinge side spine that I ripped a 2" wide by 3/4" deep notch out of. This way I could offset the MDF panels by 2"s and be able to screw one of the MDF panels directly into the 2x6 spine and then have the two MDF panel screwed to each other before being sandwiched by the 1/4 plywood on either side, this gives the door more stability than just having the 1/4 plywood holding everything together.
