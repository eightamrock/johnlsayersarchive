As my project winds down, I am getting to the stage where I really need to investigate the acoustic treatment in my live room. I have the control room pretty well under control now, although it was a long, tedious process. I thought I would start a thread over here in the acoustics forum to solicit some advice for my live room. Here are some Sketch-Up renderings showing the basic layout of the studio. The live room is the yellow room with the two rugs on the floor and the drum kit.

In the sketches, I have placed a bunch of corner traps, and an assorted collection of wall traps. What is not showing is that I also plan to have a large ceiling cloud over the drum position. The room will be used for tracking drums, and acoustic instruments such as acoustic guitars. Anything that is amplified will be close-miked in my iso room. Everything else will be going direct. Vocals will also be tracked in this room, but I have a bunch of portable gobos that I tend to use to surround the vocalist with to make a sort of makeshift vocal booth. The drums will be close-miked although the OH mics will be picking up some room ambiance obviously. I am hoping that the ceiling cloud over the drums will help keep some of the ceiling reflections to a minimum. I got some advise from someone that I trust a lot that my design has no diffusion, only absorption, and that I need to design some diffusion into my plan. Problems: 1) Money!!! (surprised???) As I draw near to the end of the project, I am running very short on funds. I have a boatload of rigid fiberglass, so that is not a problem. Also, the wood pieces used for frames and fabric covering is also already budgeted for. 2) I have no knowledge of diffusion, or how to decide on how many, what type, and placement information. 3) I am concerned that some of the designs and advise I get are targeted towards more professional studios. This is a home-based project studio with a limited budget. 4) My DIY skills are not up to the level of some of you guys! The thought of making one of those skyline diffusers are not something I look forward to making!!! Anyway, I will stop there for now. Please let me know if you have any other questions. I appreciate any advice that can be offered. Thanks!!!
gullfo13 June 2008#2
there's a few thoughts on this - one is that the absorption placed (small and scattered) results in a diffusive space. another is that numeric diffusers preserve more energy in the room. lastly, scattering devices like a polycylindrical (poly) will add "diffusion" and some trapping. you could build some nice polys fairly easily and inexpensively. Terry and Scott on studiotips have a number of good design ideas floating around, and there are some photos of rooms being treated with small polys - walls and ceiling. i recently posted a drawing on 2xX lumber "diffuser" you could place over the corner superchunk absorbers. maybe start simple - some well placed absorption. then add the hard types like poly and numeric diffusers.
