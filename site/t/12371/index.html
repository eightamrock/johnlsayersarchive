Aloha and welcome to the forum, Tom Okay guys, ease up a bit. Tom, generally speaking, most of the acoustic treatments prescribed in this forum are: - bass traps in front/rear corners (and any other corner - wall/ceiling intersections - if more absorption is needed) - absorptive panels front/center, rear/center & rear side walls & if possible/necessary over mix position - at least 6 degree angled slot absorbers per left & right of mix position (12 degrees total) More often than not, as you read through the forum threads, you'll find products like Owens Corning 703 semi rigid fiberglass, Roxul Rockwool, Owens Corning pink fluffy fiberglass insulation prescribed for insulating and absorption rather than foam based products. Brand names mentioned are what is available in the US, UK, Australia, and other areas of the world. Availability in your area may vary. Look for 2'-3" thick with ratings of 2.5 pcf. Perhaps by reading through the stickies at the top of each forum (Studio Design, Construction, Acoustics, etc) and some of the studio build threads, these may help shed some light on some of the processes involved. Here are a few I would recommend starting with - viewtopic.php?f=2&t=5670 viewtopic.php?f=2&t=2125 viewtopic.php?f=1&t=5457 Here are a few tips I've collected along the way. Perhaps they'll be useful. • It is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension because you don't want rear reflections to interfere • Soffits angled 30 deg. in from a flat wall result in speaker baffles being perpendicular to the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head • The baffle extension (the face of the soffit) should be as large as you can make it, up to about 45" in each direction from the woofer in a system with a LF cutoff of around 45 Hz - There's a formula based on LF cutoff that ensures full 2 pi response for the woofer if the baffle is at least the size obtained in the formula's solution - • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall to begin with, adjust position as needed • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position • Monitor height – tweeters aimed at ear level (avoid woofers at mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary) • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls 6-12 degrees The more you read (and take organized notes) the more informed you'll be to ask specific questions and make suitable decisions for your studio build. Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
