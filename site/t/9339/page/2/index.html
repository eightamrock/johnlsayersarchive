holickpro6 November 2007#28
Working 16 hours a day has made this build seem like it has taken years but in reality just a month or so. I have little things left to do, like some trim, a little paint, a mic cabinet I need to make, etc... but overall the studio is nearly completed. I have already had a few sessions and everything sounded great. The control room sounds tight and detailed, and the low end seems to be smooth, and the room is alive enough that I can tell I may have over-reverberated some of my old mixes :oops: . The live room sounds great also. As with the live room, it is also lively so things don't sound dead. In my old studio the live room just had bass traps and 2'x4' 2" mineral wool panels and everything sounded a little boomy and very dry, and this new room is the exact opposite. Music sounds tight in the low to mids, and the highs have just enough reverberation to make them sound a little bright and alive. I LOVE IT. I had a 3 piece swing jazz group in (2 guitars and violin) and they sounded amazing playing together. It was a joy to listen. BTW, for anyone who uses Yamaha HS80 Monitors , they sound like crap unless they are soffit mounted. I had a chance to hook them up for a listen before they were mounted and the difference is night and day. These pics are for my web-site (which is under construction), and I will have better, more detailed photos later on. THANKS FOR VIEWING! Any comments welcomed. Joe.

holickpro6 November 2007#33
After you get familar with all the functions, the DM3200 is a great piece of equipment. The initial setup is rather time consuming and somewhat confusing maybe even frustrating. The control layout takes a little getting used too, but now I know the thing like second nature and wouldn't trade it for anything. (well not anything for less than 100,000 anyways ;).) I use the firewire card and you have 24 ins/outs that sound great and are extremely reliable. The preamps sound amazing, I think they sound even smoother than the protools control D24 or whatever its called. The talkback sounds absolutely horrible, but you have the option of running a mic into one of the channels for talkback, and of course then it sounds great.I'm sure I'm forgetting alot of things you might like to know, so feel free to ask, and I would definately recommend a dm3200 or 4800, and when you go to set it up, I will give you my phone number if you need any info or assistance.
