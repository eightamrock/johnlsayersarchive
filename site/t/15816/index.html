if you could go to 8'h x 11'w x 17'd or 8'h x 13'w and 15'd it would be better than even multiples. that said, is that the exterior or interior dimensions? worst case, go with the 8x12x16 (more volume is better) and plan on treating the space more. the walls - you want both drywall layers on the RC. or better on isolation clips and hat channel. this way your mass is a single leaf then an air gap then exterior sheathing. much more isolation. the bathroom shouldn't be an issue.

gullfo9 April 2011#9
It's the latter - stud - RC - 2x drywall layers. Consider substituting the RC with isolation clips and hat channel. More isolation although it will be more money. If not, MAKE SURE they use RC1 on the walls and RC2 on the ceiling. It's VERY common for a builder to use furring strips/hat channel directly and that will NOT provide the isolation you seek. If you know someone who can double check their work as it progresses (I.e. On-site) it may help to endure they do it right. Like using the right lengthy screws etc to avoid coupling you drywall to the studs etc. Backer rod and caulking plus proper staggering for the drywall seams etc is something that needs to be checked to avoid leaks and resonant hot spots.

Soundman202014 April 2011#12
I've searched and I'm seeing some conflicting info.
It might not be conflicting, really: just different ways of doing things for different circumstances. The basic concept isn't too hard to follow: You have studs with something on the outside to keep the weather out, then you have RC attached to the other side of those studs, then you have one or two layers of 5/8 drywall attached to the RC (one or two, depending on how much isolation you need). That assumes that the "something that keeps the weather out" is reasonably massive. If not, then you might have to beef that up with some extra mass, but hopefully that won't be necessary since it seems that you don't need high levels of isolation. However, since you have figured out that the shed people might not be your best option, and that you are probably going to get someone with more experience to build it for you professionally, then you might want to also consider getting it designed professionally. You could e-mail John and get him to quote you for that. Or you could carry on doing what you are already doing: learning enough to design it yourself, and asking folks here for help each time you get stuck. But you do have a long way to go on that front yet. Which leads to another question: do you need it build fast, or can you live with what you have for several more months yet, while you learn this stuff and design it? If you have the time, then it would be much cheaper to spend that time learning and design it yourself. But if you need it fast, then hire someone to design it for you, and hire a contractor to build it. - Stuart -
