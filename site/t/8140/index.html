gullfo5 February 2007#8
speaking from experience, remove the wallboard and replace it. use extreme care to keep yourself protected and dispose of properly. or as suggested get a pro involved. you will never be rid of the mold (not moulding unless there is a process to grow crown moulding in which case sign me up :D ) because it has penetrated the board and will always be there waiting for a time when the conditions are right to start growing and spreading again. once you get the board removed, disinfect the area and make sure its dry before re-covering. ideally the best best is to remove the walls and rebuild so you can ensure circulation. there are also new "paperless" gypsum board with built in mold inhibitors. they're (in my area) about $4 more per pair of sheets for 1/2" and a bit more for 5/8" but at least you will be installing something more resistant. bottom line it has to be removed to be sure. check the underlying framing, insulation etc... because if its ON the walls, its probably IN the walls as well.

knightfly11 February 2007#19
If the normal relative humidity in your area gets higher than about 50%, I would consider looking for a dehumidifier to run in your space when not recording - mold generally can't survive under about 50%, and any wooden music instruments (piano, guitar, violin, etc) like 42% the best. I do this in my spaces, and both my instruments and my lungs thank me... Steve

knightfly11 February 2007#21
That would depend on whether you plan a complete air conditioning system built in with silenced duct work, etc - these will, if properly sized, run long enough to dehumidify your space. If not, the quietest free-standing unit I've used is the Sharp portable air conditioner/dehumidifier, model # CV-P09FX - may be a different model # for your power system. Here's a pic - shown being used as an air conditioner, which unfortunately requires that it's 5" diameter hose gets vented to the outside. As a dehumidifer, it does NOT need the exhaust hose to be run outside - instead, the exhaust will act as a small space heater, warming the room a bit. You'll also need a bucket to catch all the water that this unit removes from the air - in my case, a 4 gallon container fills up in about 36-48 hours. This is by FAR the quietest unit I've used, I have a Whirlpool unit that is about 12 dB louder than this Sharp unit and an in-wall unit that's at least 8 dB louder than the Sharp. Only downside for me is that the Sharp has no "humidistat" - you have to turn it off if the humidity gets lower than you want. Mine cost about $550 USD... Steve
