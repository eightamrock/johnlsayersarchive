knightfly17 January 2006#29
Big question, How to install the Rockwool? atacched to inner wall? glued? doesnt matters?, i search in forum but can get a good pic about how to place the rockwool, mineral wool. If you're using steel studs, rockwool batts will slide between the channel and stay put. If wood, you need to trim 1.5" off each batt so it will fit between studs on 24" centers - studs are 1.5" thick so cavity will only be 22.5" wide. IF you cut the rockwool (wood studs only) to 23", it will bow slightly and lock in place between studs, and ALSO it will press slightly against the gypsum boards - this is GOOD, it helps damp any vibrations the gypsum will get when stopping sound. mineral wool on floated floors is rested over the concrete only? Right. If your mineral wool sticks up maybe 5-6mm (1/4" or less) above your floor joists when all rubber, etc, is in place, then the floor will compress it slightly and it will keep the floor from ringing. This is what you want. If your mineral wool does NOT do that, you may need to add some strips of regular fiberglass insulation between joists, so THAT compresses slightly and presses against the underside of the floor - you do NOT want that floor to RING, this is important. when u guys said steel studs u mean the same studs that is sell for gypsum boards at home deppot? Yes. If you use steel instead of wood, there are different "weights" - 25 gauge is NON-load-bearing, which is fine for that type wall (ask your Dad) - the heavier, 20 gauge studs are for LOAD-bearing walls. If you will use steel studs in an INNER wall that has gypsum ONLY ON ONE SIDE, you should use the heavier, Load-bearing (structural) ones so they don't tend to TWIST later. Home Depot might have the heavier studs, or they might only have the lighter ones. Another caution about steel studs - if your area has high humidity and you plan to use steel framing, plan on running a de-humidifier in the rooms when you're not working in there - it only takes 1/10 as much moisture for a steel-framed structure to have moisture-related problems as it does with WOOD structures. There's a paper about this in the Reference Section, look for Building Sciences links. HTH... Steve

Some possible sources http://www.dehumidify.com/retail.html Some info http://www.dehumidify.com/FAQ.html Sorry I've missed some of your questions (busy doesn't describe my days :? ) Neoprene under floor joists - glue or free-standing, if you nail through the joist into the concrete then the neoprene is USELESS. Backer rod - should be a snug fit, mostly so it doesn't try to come out of the crack before you caulk. Caulk needs to contact both surfaces of the crack, but NOT the BACK of the crack. The backer rod is there so you do not have a 3-point caulk, otherwise when building shifts around your caulk joints will CRACK and you lose isolation. Caulk gun - this looks similar to mine, price is about what I paid caulk gun Insulation - for inside the walls, regular fluffy fiberglass insulation will work as well as the SAFB's, so if you've not already done it you might be able to save some money. Either will be fine though. You want the insulation to STAY in contact with the gypsum, one way or another. The 3" batts will be fine, 4" are more than twice the cost of 2" so if you want that thickness it would be cheaper to just buy twice as many 2" ones. Refrigerator door seals can work, but need steel to seal to as you said. Caulk - http://www.whitecapdirect.com/store/wor ... t-v2.0.jsp Best deal you'll find... Hope this helps, in future if you don't get responses in a few days please PM me... Steve
