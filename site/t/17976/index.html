Soundman202016 November 2012#2
Hi Tom. Welcome! :) There are several issues with that design. First is symmetry: the room must be symmetrical with regard to your head. In other words, the left side must be a mirror image of the right side. If not, then the EQ and time-domain response of the left and right sides is not the same, and your mixes will not translate well. Your layout shows the console offset to the left. Next, the speakers should aim down the longest axis of the room, not across the short axis, as you show. You want as much distance as possible between the speakers and the rear wall. Third, your head should be a long way form the rear wall. The ideal position is that your head is about 38% of the distance from the front wall to the back wall. In your current layout, your head would be way too close to the back wall. You could fix all of these by simply rotating the layout 90° to the left. :)
I wanted to make a QRD and some bass traps
You will definitely need bass traps, and lots of them. Small rooms need a LOT of bass trapping. But forget diffusers: that room is too small to be able to use QRD diffusers successfully. Once again, you need a lot of space between the diffuser and your head, and there is not enough space in that room. Numeric-based diffusers can only be used if the room is large enough. Most home studios are not.
I wanted to flush mount the speakers but as there's a door in the corner of the control room I didn't see any possibility to flush mount them.
Flush mounting is an excellent idea, and once again if you turn the layout 90° to the left, then you would probably be able to do that. - Stuart -
