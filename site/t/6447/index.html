HomeStu15 June 2006#1
Hello folks. Thanks for all this info. I'm in the process of ordering materials and starting construction next week, so it may be futile to post here, but I'm interested in any opinions of my plan. I am in the area of Santa Cruz, CA. My room is about 13x23 wth several unusual corners and angles at one end. 8' height. The walls are mostly exterior. It is on a concrete slab. I don't need a seperate control room. Neighbors are close, and I play and record everything from quiet acoustic to loud electric music, professionally, so I need good sound insulation inside and out. However I want to keep my construction costs around $5K and don't want to sacrifice much space, as the room isn't that big. This budget includes labor for my buddy the carpenter/studio owner who is great. Here's my plan: The existing walls are stucco exterior, standard framing with insulation, and 5/8" sheetrock interior. I am sealing off the big garage door to match the existing walls. To this shell I will attach 2x4s laid flat on the wall (1 3/4" cavity) with more fiberglass insulation between those sideways studs, then a layer of 5/8" rock/Green Glue/5/8" rock. The ceiling will not get any framing, just 2 extra layers of 5/8" rock with Green Glue between. And a bunch of fiberglass insulation stuffed above in the attic. On the floor, just the slab and carpet with the thickest carpet pad underneath. The other 2 doors, 1 exterior and one exterior, will be replaced with solid-core doors with green glue and 1 or 2 layers of rock attached and hung to seal tight with the best weather stripping I can find. Double doors aren't practical here. Whata y'all think of that? One more question: Is this Green Glue turning out to perform as well as it looks like it will? The website now says that you still need to screw in the sheetrock layers to the studs and that worries me a little. Do those backing strips help with the coupling issue? Interested in your opinions. Hope this is enough data to suffice for my questions. Thanks, Jim
