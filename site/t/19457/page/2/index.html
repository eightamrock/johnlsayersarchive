salbans wrote:

hmmm....another option, if you need better isolation between the rehearsal room half and the other half of the space is to partition off the rehearsal room half. That would effectively mean buliding a wall (to divide your space in to two spaces) which is sealed airtight against the existing brick walls, ceiling, and floor. You could then stick with 2-leaf construction for your rehearsal rooms. The outer brick shell would still act as a flanking path between the two halves, but it would probably get you some degree of TL between the two halves. Whether that is enough TL for what you have planned in the other half of the space is another question, which, i'm sure you've figured out, is what planning a studio build is all about. lots,and lots of questions :D

salbans wrote:
As it will be a single stud Im guessing hanging 2 solid core doors is going to be difficult
You need to have as much mass in your doors as you you do in your walls and ceilings. That way you don't build a weak point by having a flimsy door. Same goes for making it too heavy as well. It's no point having a solid lead door if you've got two sheets of plasterboard for the rest of your leaf. So having two solid core doors on one leaf won't be of any use to you unless you're building the walls/ceilings to the same degree.

salbans wrote:
One thing I probably missed in the thread, but how did you install the aircon and maintain the isolation. Im assuming it has the usual two pipes running to the external unit?
Aircon was installed as normal except: the penetrations between the internal wall and external wall are not directly in line. The penetrations are sealed back up with some very serious sealant/adhesive and are airtight once finished. You want as few penetrations in your walls as possible, but if you make sure they're sealed with the right kind of silicon/sealant then that's about as good as it'll get (short of running all your conduit etc in the slab, but that's a whole other story). The one I used is called Sikaflex 221 here in Australia. steve

Under slab cabling is really only feasible if you're building from scratch. I built from scratch and didn't do it and I beleieve i still have excellent isolation between rooms. I cut a channel about 20-30mm deep in the top of the slab between the rooms and laid my cables in it and concreted back over the the top. Cutting all the way through your slab to run the conduits underneath probably isn't advisable as you'd be cutting through reinforcing mesh and god knows what else might be piped under the slab. I think you'll be fine with wal penetrations as long as you pay a lot of attention to sealingeverything up again. lookin' forward to seeing some pics :D steve
