Adding to what Brien already said, you are also describing the construction of three-leaf walls, which is NOT a good idea. It isn't intuitive at all, but a three-leaf wall (such as you describe) will actually isolate WORSE than a two-leaf wall of the same mass and overall dimensions. You also mention that your current rehearsal space has a FOUR LEAF wall :shock: which is worse still than a 3 leaf! No wonder you have troubles with isolation! I'm betting that the base beat come thumping through those walls just fine right now...
the perimeter wall of the studio is made from bricks/blocks 100mm thick. In a rectangular shape. So, i could only realistically board the inside wall and then build from there on the inside.
That wall is fine by itself. It just needs sealing, to ensure that that the surface is airtight. Paint, plaster, stucco, etc. Any of those will do. Both brick and concrete block are porous, and need to be sealed. You do not need to "board" those walls: doing so would probably be counter productive. They are fine as they are, provided that they are sealed and that you build the inner leaf correctly.
IDEA 1 perimeter wall (blocks)/soundbloc plasterboardx1/air space/soundbloc plasterboardx1/Studwork(with insulation)/soundbloc plasterboardsx2
Bad idea! That is three-leaf right there, and depending on how you place that first layer, you meight even have four leaves there. Far better would be: perimeter wall (sealed) - 6 inch air gap filled with mineral wool or fiberglass insulation - 2x6 stud frame, 24" OC, with three layers of 5/8 drywall nailed to only ONE side o the studs, each layer staggered from the other two, and carefully caulked and sealed. That system uses the exact same amount of materials, and is the same total overall thickness, but will give you much better isolation, especially in the low frequencies. Of course, you do have to be consistent, and use the exact same construction technique around all of the walls and also the ceiling. You also do NOT need to waste money on Soundblock: Plain old drywall is the same mass and density, and works perfectly. Sound waves do not care how much you paid for the mass that stops them: they only see mass. The cost of the mass is irrelevant, and three layers of 5/8" (16mm) drywall is plenty of mass, especially when it is over a 9 3/4 inch air gap!
