"I an a Voice Actor and have a 4 x 4 double wall (enhanced) whisper room sound booth that has served me well over the years. I'm recently having having a problem with thumping noises (from the kids running and playing one floor below me). " OK, it[the booth] has worked well over the years as you say but is now failing to met your needs. Did the booth change in relation to position in the structure, like going from the first floor to the upper floor? Usually the upper floor is what gives folks the "foot fall" issue, but yours seems to be either a new addition to the structure [the children] or something else that has changed in the environment with the booth. I would have to say that anything you do to reduce this sound transference would need to be to the booth, the exterior of the booth, not the interior. Certainly you can reduce vibration on the interior side of an assembly but your first line of attack is to stop it before it gets into the structure. It is no different than if you were discussing a structure, stop the vibrations from the outside rather than attempt to do it from the inside, were the vibrations have already penetrated the environment and search only for the weakest link.

Soundman202031 October 2012#4
Hi "billysurf"- Welcome to the forum! :)
Carpet the downstairs?
That may well be the best solution. Impact noise is hard to deal with: once it gets into the building structure, there isn't much you can do to get it back out again. You could float your booth, but that would be prohibitively expensive, probably. It would be much easier, and probably much cheaper, to simply prevent the impact noise from getting into the structure on the first place, and a good way of doing that would, indeed, be to carpet the floor where the thumping is originating. - Stuart -

Glenn's drum riser is a specific thing for a specific acoustical issue. What the op is referring to is using owens cowning 703 rigid insulation and applying structural plywood to the topside, two layers layed perpendicular to each other to decouple the lower area from the structure. This is a good idea but should not be jumped on as an immediate answer since there are always mitigating circumstances that could render this useless. Like windows on the upper level that are producing air borne sound or overhead air traffic...ect.
