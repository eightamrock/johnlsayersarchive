Soundman202015 January 2013#2
Hi John, and welcome to the forum! :)
My “mixing” room is a really small basement: 3.91 meters length, 2.62 m. wide and only 2.04 m. height.
That's not a very good ratio. You can't reduce the height, for sure, and I guess you can't increase the length or width either, so probably your only options are to reduce the length and/or width. If you reduce the length of the room to 3.7m then you improve the modal response considerably, and get a very reasonable ratio of 1 : 1.28 : 1.81. So all you need to do, it to build a wall about 20cm from the current front or rear walls. It seems the front wall would be best, for your layout.
and the beam across the room.
:shock: :!: Ouch! that beam is a problem: IT is directly above your listening position. That will not be easy to treat...
The floor is covered with 5 cm cement mortar and a carpet.
OK; there's the first thing you can do to greatly improve the room acoustics: take out the carpet! Just leave plain cement floor, or if you don't like cement, then install laminate floor, vinyl, ceramic tiles, or something similar. Carpet is terrible, acoustically. Get rid of it.
Due to the very low height, “floating” the floor is forbidden.
Good, because you don't need to float that floor! There is absolutely no reason why you would need to do that.
My speakers are the Focal’s solo 6be with the Focal sub6. ... I also hesitate to soffit mount these speakers.
Why? Practically ANY speaker can benefit from soffit mounting, especially small ones where bass response is limited: And your speakers CAN be soffit mounted, since they are not rear ported and they do have the correct bass roll-off controls on the rear. They would sound much better soffit mounted, even if the soffits were not very big.
This is how I imagine the best solution to my mixing room:
The basic ideas sound good, except for the following:
-4 roundffusors in the rear wall, below the a/c:
The room is too small to be able to use numerically based diffusers: your head would be too close to them, and in the zone where lobing is a problem. I would just do the entire rear wall in absorption, except for two superchunks in the vertical corners. Except for that, you seem to have a pretty good plan! I would suggest that you should measure the room acoustic response as it is right now, BEFORE you do anything to it, then measure it again after you have put in the basic treatment (vertical corner superchunks, first reflection points, rear wall), to see what still needs to be done. Based on the results, you can see what issues have been fixed, and what issue still remain, so it will help you plan the next round of treatment accordingly. - Stuart -

Soundman20205 April 2013#11
OK here is the link to download the mdat file of the untreated basement
Great! OK, I'm looking at the data, and there seems to be an issue with the way the measurements were taken. Did you calibrate REW properly before doing that test? The levels seem very low. You need to do the tests at a level of about 80 dBC, and REW also needs to be calibrated for that level. Your test seems to have been done at around 60 dB. Even so, there's enough data to see that there are major RT-60 problems in there! You are going to need a lot of bass trapping to get that under control. The decay times for everything below about 400 Hz are well over one second! Some are hitting 1.5 seconds :shock: :!: You said that that test was for the untreated basement: do you have data from another test, after installing some treatment? But the biggest point here is that you need lots of bass trapping - Stuart -
