jwl22 March 2007#2
I'm not going to comment on the HVAC stuff, there are many others more qualified than I. Regarding acoustic treatment.... I'd scrap the Green Glue idea. That's mostly for isolation/sound dampening purposes. Your room won't sound any different inside if you use Green Glue, however OUTSIDE (if you install the Green Glue correctly) they won't hear as much. So yes. Learn to distinguish between isolation and acoustic treatment. Two very different animals, with 2 very different procedures to accomplish your goals. I'm going to assume isolation is not a problem for you, and that you are only concerned about acoustic treatment inside the room. The first place I would start is to read Ethan Winer's acoustics FAQ. Once you understand that, it will become clear on how to treat your room. In short, you have two goals: 1) even out the low-end response of your room with bass traps/broadband absorbers; and 2) tighten up the stereo imaging of your mix position by creating a Reflection-Free Zone at the sweet spot. Ethan's article explains this in just the right amount of detail for acoustics newbies. Complex enough to be somewhat challenging, yet brief enough to be undaunting. Your next decision, once you understand your basic goals in detail (which you will after reading that article), is to decide how to proceed. You can DIY, which is obviously labor/time-intensive but cheaper, or go with prefab applications (GIK, Realtraps, certain Auralex products, etc) which are easier but more expensive. OK, that's the basics, which pretty much apply to any small room studio. Think about the above, and follow up with additional questions. Keep reading and learning! There is a lot to learn, but if you are careful and ask good questions you can avoid expensive mistakes.
