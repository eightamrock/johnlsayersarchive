IF it's heavier/more dense then gypsum then yes, good choice. If it is soundboard lke n8nod9it said, then it's a bad choice... that is IF you had to buy it... but if it's FREE and you already own some then it will still add to isolation. Soundboard does not help in low end isolation ( referred to on forum as TL or Transmission Loss) as much as Gypsum but sound board will make a STC# improvement because STC does not really consider the low end, STC is meant for voice only.

Yeah that is Masonite... I still wouldn't get water on it for any reason, its a compression fiber product and any water on it would likely swell it, or stay trapped in it, which you wouldn't want layered between gypsum. I don't know how effective that will be in TL isolation. I suppose if you had enough for free, it would be ok to use but it is very thin. Also, that SAE article mentioned gluing the layers together which IMO from what i've read, cause the coincidence frequency of two seperate pieces of material to be merged into one new larger coincidence freq, instead of two seperate frequencies. Two would be better than one, two with GG inbetween would be best. I was going to use MDF in my walls because i could get it for cheap, but i decided not too...Much too messy to cut and heavy. But you'll have to decide whether the Masonite is appropriate after considering its acoustic properties and worth.

Bob,
however from the quote on the saecollege site above it says
The SAE site is a great source written by John back in 1998. John no longer has any control of the web site. It's a great intro into the world of studio building, however even John admits part of the info is out of date. Newer research, tests and discussions on this and other forums has proven a few of the suggested techniques wrong OR completely arguable. To get the full FREE benefit of the SAE one has to study it then come on this forum, search some more or post plan to get "fine tuned" to the best course of action for a build. PITA I say... I've been through many, many changes in my plan as a result... and so have a lot of others. The best single source for up to date info is Rod's book available in the stickies. That being said: 2nd and 3rd layers of gypsum should be screwed, not glued. This allows the layers some movement and improves iso. For a better explanation search for one of Knightfly's 100's of posts about the subject. Only exception to the glue thing is Green Glue www.greengluecompany.com WHICH is not really glue and you still have to use screws. Green Glue used at 100% coverage will make 2 layers of drywall almost act like 4 for iso. Using different thickness of drywall in multilayers, like 5/8" and 1/2" does improve iso in mid range BUT worsens in low end which is what we are after. Better off using all 5/8" drywall. There are some other things I planned on doing only to be shot down by older forum members: Don't think about floating a floor. Search on this forum for floating floor/wooden deck if you need to, almost all of the time it's not the right option. Inside out walls, yup they work, are a space saver but difficult to build and do not have the same STC or TL as a walls built normal. Side wall slot resonators, and slots in general are hard to build. It may be much more simple to just splay side walls and add some absorbers. ME I've not been able to make up my mind yet on using a slot wall or not. Looks like you want to face the long wall in your studio. In small rooms like yours (mine is a bit smaller) we'll get much better result facing the short wall. Larger rooms can face the long wall (and do). There's MORE stuff to be pointed out. The above are some very common things forum members plan on (like me) and then get corrected on. BTW figuring out what you are going to build for acoustics is MUCH harder then figuring how you plan to isolate.... and remember ALL acoustic contraptions in a room will make a third leaf and cause some iso loss (some cause very little, some can cause a lot like a slot wall) so we plan accordingly and beef up iso or take the hit. Marc

Your welcome. Yup out of date, Steve wrote that in 2003. Here's what Steve wrote on my thread when I was at a similar point of thinking as you:
Drywall - I've clarified the "different thickness" concept in a few places, but they're scattered and NOT in the reference section where you've been looking. I'll reiterate here, and if someone nags enough I'll amend the two main places in the reference section (why hasn't anyone invented 48-hour days (that don't wear you out any more but you get twice as much done ) yet??!? Pluses for differing thicknesses in successive layers in a wall - (NOT GLUED TO EACH OTHER) Differing coincidence frequencies, flattens out the Coincidence dip at around 2-3 kHz by a couple of dB. Minuses for differing thicknesses - confusion on job site, probable wrong thickness applied somewhere, causing tearout Loss of mass, causing worse TL on low end by a couple dB Bottom line is this - if you use all 5/8 you improve bottom end performance, at a 1-2 dB penalty around 2.5 kHz - but since bass is the hard part of isolation, it's better to build for low frequency and the rest will take care of itself. You also remove the chance of screw-ups, cut down on the open floor space you need to store two stacks of drywall instead of one, and improve on your chances for bulk discounts by ordering all the same stuff.

knightfly3 January 2007#16
Same stuff, Eric - I won't say it's no good, only that given a choice between a layer of soundboard (also sometimes called Celotex) and a layer of drywall vs. 2 layers of drywall, the one with more mass (2 drywall) wins. Period. Now, the diff between a layer of soundboard UNDER 2 layers of drywall vs. ONLY the 2 layers of drywall - you should see a bit more isolation, but then THREE layers of drywall would beat 'em all - and it's not that much more labor and costs typically LESS than the soundboard in most areas... Steve
