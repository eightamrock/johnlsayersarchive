A forum is about knowledge sharing and creating a community. I realize that the subject matter experts in this forum are too busy to answer the same questions over and over again. But continually telling new members to "read the stickies", even when an honest attempt is made to find the answers in existing posts is poor form. It also defeats the purpose of a forum. Soundman202018 January 2010#2
If you guys wanted a top down approach to knowledge sharing, why not just write a blog or a book.
Rod did that, several years ago. It didn't seem to help, though, as people still come in expecting to be spoon fed for free, while totally ignoring the huge wealth of info that is freely available here for everyone who can actually muster enough interest in building their studio correctly to bother to read the instructions on the very first page. Yeah, the attitude here sucks, but only for newbies who don't (or can't) read instructions too well. Personally, I wouldn't have it any other way! Yeah, it's tough for someone who comes here with good intentions and asks a question without realizing that folks here have already answered the exact same question a dozen times, in a dozen different ways, with the last time being just an hour or two back. But then again, if they would have bothered to READ the instructions and FOLLOW them, then they would have known that.... Or at least known enough to do what most well-mannered newbies do (who HAVE read the instructions), which is to say something like "I know it must be here somewhere, but could somebody please show me where to find xyz?". A question like that will get an answer. A question like "Hi I'm building a studio please show me how." will not. If you ask me, anyone who comes here and can't handle the "attitude" of the forum without falling apart, ranting, raving and swearing, probably doesn't have what it takes to build their own studio by themselves anyway. IMHO - Stuart -

I, as a "noobie", have no problem with the way that this forum is run. I am very new to acoustics and to any construction aspects in general. The amount that I have learned in the past year or so makes my head hurt, sometimes, but I've learned 95% of it from READING. I was hesitant to ever post anything here. When I finally did, I had put a ton of work into it. The "fear" forced me to really dot my T's, as they say. I posted a plan for our control room last April and no one ever replied. So, I just kept on reading everything that I could find and before long, I learned that the plan that I had wasn't going to work for various reasons. There isn't much out there that hasn't been done before. Yes, yes, every space is different and all that. Air is air, materials are materials and sound is sound. Learn all the theory that you can. Put it into practice and see how it applies.... or doesn't apply. People can give you all the advice they can stomach, but in the end, the work has to get done.. and once you actually START the work, that's when you really start learning. Respectfully, Seamus
