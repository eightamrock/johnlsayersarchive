Bassman wrote:
One common misconception about slats is that the various spacing gives you discrete frequencies of absorption (spearate resonators). This is only true if the cavity behind EACH slat is sealed. If the space behind multiple slats is not partitioned off, the effect of various slat spacing is moving the center frequency of the whole slat panel, not the individual slats. Take the average slat spacing for the whole panel to calculate where the panel is tuned. Of course, wool inside and behind the slats widens the absorption bandwidth of the entire panel as well. The really GOOD thing about the various spacing is a better diffusion of upper frequencies as they bounce off the slats. Bottom line is that unless you are partitioning the resonator, don't bother with a calc as it would be an estimate of center frequency at best. Just vary the spacing in some fashion, perhaps even a decorative one, and you should be good. -ashley

gullfo12 December 2010#2
you can go with one rigid type of insulation the resonators as they're not very deep. the slats will tend toward the overall center frequency. as a general rule the top and bottom slats should be the largest size used and should tend to have the smallers slats and wider slots towards the middle as this will affect the mid-HF more near your ears and more LF absorption near the top and bottom wall corners. the front corner traps i'd go with the 4" (100mm) rigid and then the pink stuff behind it. or better yet build hangers in lieu of the pink stuff - cellouse fiber board (Homasote) and light insulation wrapped around it and hung roughly floor to ceiling and spaced about 6" (150mm) apart. the closet idea should be OK remember the slat assembly will be heavy and needs good hinges. the vinyl linoleum floor should be fine. you want to decouple those speakers from the desk, maybe some of the speaker pads with the steel plates which angle them down slightly. i don't see any concerns with the lighting concepts.
