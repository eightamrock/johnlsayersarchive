Ewerton24 February 2013#1
Hello All, After years and years of reading many posts, a lot of books and studying many cases I've finally come to the point where I'm going to have my studio built. I'm having my new house built and I managed to convince my lovely girlfriend to allow me to include a studio underneath our garage. She keeps saying that it will be the place i will go whenever we have a fight. :D Here are some details of my project. Please forgive me if I forget something, but I will try to give the details without having a long post and will complete as I go: Objective: Home studio for practicing with my band and with friends, with the possibility of being used for some local bands for rehearsing. I also intend to put video cameras in the studio and stream some online lessons and live rehearsals. It will possibly used for some small professional projects. My idea would be having at least 2 rooms, to separate the drums and be able to record live rehearsals with the drummer in a different room. Current Idea is keeping the Band on Room #1 and drums on Room 2 Type of music played: I play from AC/DC and Deep purple to heavy rock like Iron Maiden, Megadeth and Iced Earth. Some heavier (and louder) bands will definitely be rehearsing Current Structure: No walls yet. Only the foundations of the outer walls are built. The plan is to have a ceiling height at 3.2 meters. The vocal booth has a lowered ceiling on the hatched area due to the entrance stairs. The dimensions of the big square is 9x7 meters The plot is in a hill so I had to "cut it", and the studio will be half underground. For this reason, the outer walls will be built with Structural Concrete Bricks filled with concrete in one hole and with sand on the other. The inner walls are not planned but my idea is to build them with 10mm Fired Clay Brick (not sure if this is the right translation, but I mean the solid bricks). Timing: I've started the construction, so I have to decide quickly on the layout and have it build. Budget: Not sure if this is meaningful because the prices in Brazil are different, but my idea is to control the expenses as my priority is the house. Neighborhood: The house is out in the country side but I still have neighbors on both sides. Also have 10 dogs, so the idea is to have good isolation from the outside Other Comments: I tried to fit John's corner studio on this space but the rooms ended up too small that I decided to create my own layout. I did not include a bathroom as there is an external one just outside the studio. The house position is on the east side of the studio, around 5 meters away. I really would like to have you guys to evaluate my preliminary design so I can go ahead and start detailing it. I still have a lot of questions ( specially on specific topics, like air conditioning), but I will take each step carefully. Thanks for reading this. I intend to make this my construction Journal. Peace, Ewerton

Soundman20201 March 2013#4
Hi Ewerton, and welcome to the forum! :) Sometimes it can take a while to get a reply to your thread here: There's a lot of activity lately!
My idea would be having at least 2 rooms, to separate the drums and be able to record live rehearsals with the drummer in a different room.
. Very wise. Trying to track drums in the same room as the control room has never worked well for me. I like to be able to hear how each mic sounds on the CR monitors, and you can't do that if you are tracking in the same room!
Some heavier (and louder) bands will definitely be rehearsing
:shock: :!: Louder and heavier than AC/DC, Deep Purple, Iron Maiden, Megadeath and Iced Earth??? WOW! :shock:
No walls yet. Only the foundations of the outer walls are built. The plan is to have a ceiling height at 3.2 meters.
Excellent. That's a good height for tracking drums. And with only the foundations so far, you have the perfect opportunity to do this right. Do you already have a concrete slab laid? If not, then there are things you could do to improve isolation, but if it is already in, then that's fine.
The plot is in a hill so I had to "cut it", and the studio will be half underground.
Even better! So you will have great isolation and great damping.
For this reason, the outer walls will be built with Structural Concrete Bricks filled with concrete in one hole and with sand on the other.
Is that to save money? There's not really that much benefit from the sand: Yes, it will damp resonance a bit, but you'd probably be better off just filling it all with concrete.
The inner walls are not planned but my idea is to build them with 10mm Fired Clay Brick (not sure if this is the right translation, but I mean the solid bricks).
You certainly are planning on a lot of isolation. Yes, a filled concrete block outer leaf and a brick inner leaf will get you good isolation, down to low frequencies. BUt there are some issue to be aware of during the construction itself, such as making sure to keep the cavity between them totally clean, no mortar drips, no rubble, nothing. That is important.
I really would like to have you guys to evaluate my preliminary design so I can go ahead and start detailing it
Both of your layouts look OK to me, but I think the second one makes more sense, as you can have windows with good sight lines from the control room to the other rooms.
I still have a lot of questions ( specially on specific topics, like air conditioning
In Sao Paolo, HVAC will certainly be important! You have four proper seasons there, with plenty of variation, so the HVAC system needs to be designed with that in mind.
I know I'm missing a lot of details and might look I'm lazy, but the fact is that I just want to make sure I'm on the right direction to focus my efforts on detailing.
It's fine. As a basic layout it makes sense. One issue you will have, since you only have one entrance, is that all the load in/load out will have to go through the control room. So all instruments, equipment, and people have to move through the back of the control room, and that includes musicians taking bathroom breaks, smoke breaks, going out to make phone calls, etc. There's going to be a lot of traffic through there... It might be a better idea to have the live room next to the door, so load-in/load-out and other traffic doesn't interfere with the control room so much. - Stuart -
