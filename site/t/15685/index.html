Soundman20203 March 2011#8
I was using the forum search to lookup details on the inside out technique but could not find any results on how inside out compares in STC to the usual setup.
It's the same, all other factors being equal. In other words, if the amount of mass in each leaf and the size of the air gap between the leaves is the same, then the STC will be the same. The only thing that changes with inside-out, is which side of the mass you have your studs on.
I have a feeling that there should be at least a thin rockwool slab between outside wall and the gypsum but would that improve anything?
Absolutely! You still have to put insulation in the air gap, no matter which way you build! That is part of the "spring". Fitz didn't show it in his drawings, but you still MUST have the insualtion in there (either rockwool or fiberglass).

Hi, got a new view on this subject by user space on the recording.org forum http://recording.org/showthread.php?p=365939 quoting space: ----------snip------------- Directly to the inside out wall, it is only as good as the counter-part typical double framed assembly. That means that the air space has to exist in the same form from one to the other or it is a different assembly, with different stc or TL ratings. An assembly that is framed the exact same way, with the same decoupled air space, with a hard boundary on the exterior but has the hard boundary on the interior of the inside framing will have an air space of approx. 8 inches. This difference of 8 inches versus 4 1/2 inches is where the low frequency suffers. In order to get the same transmission loss using an inside out assembly, the interior framing would have to be re-set to have a distance of 4 inches between frames to match the same type assembly that is typical room-in-a-room. --------snap------------- He gave me as well a headwash on the therm STC: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_transmission_class "The STC number is derived from sound attenuation values tested at sixteen standard frequencies from 125 Hz to 4000 Hz. These transmission-loss values are then plotted on a sound pressure level graph and the resulting curve is compared to a standard reference contour." #2 and #5 are both 3-leaf- The rest are 2-leaf, but with different levels of isolation. I', guessing that #5 would be the worst isolator, and either #1 or #4 would be the best. Your "other forum" guy seems to be saying the same as we have been trying to tell you, just in different words. There is no difference at all in the amount of isolation you get from a "normal" wall or an "inside-out" wall, if the masses and the air gap are the same. The equations do not even consider which side of the mass you have the studs on: it simply is not a factor. - Stuart -
