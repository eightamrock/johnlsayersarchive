knightfly13 May 2003#2
Can you post a drawing with as much detail as you can think of? Remember, it's the little details that make or break the design. For example, you mentioned nothing about where/how/what you used for caulking, whether the door is a single one or double with sound lock, what kind of threshold seal you're using, if it's a pre-hung door did you do the door first and completely fill the threshold with putty as well as double caulk around where the shims go - Also, that glass thickness seems awfully thin to me. For non-prehung doors and custom windows, I'd tend to go more along the lines of 9mm for the thinner, and 12mm or more for the thicker glass. Assuming you've done all the detail work, the glass and doors will be your weakest link. One way to hunt down leaks is to get a piece of soft tubing, about 20-25mm in diameter and 2-3 feet long - seal one end as tightly to your ear as possible, plug the other ear with cotton or industrial hearing protectors, play a boom-box in one room and roam the other room while moving the other end of the soft tubing along all cracks, seams, windows, doors, etc, to see where the leakage is the loudest. Always keep in mind, that if your construction wouldn't float without taking on water, it won't keep sound in or out either... Steve

knightfly16 May 2003#4
OK, a number of things come to mind - First, you appear to have (rightly) stayed with the concept of two mass leaves and only one air space (the inner insulation/cloth won't count as another air space since there's no paneling but only cloth) Second, 12.5 mm drywall, especially only one layer, is pretty marginal for a wall, even with 8" concrete as the other layer. Is the concrete a solid wall, filled blocks, or un-filled blocks, and is there paint or plaster or anything over the concrete? Third, even though you show the other two walls as the "leakage zone", I'm wondering if the door is adequate compared to the rest of the construction. I would have used higher density material there, plywood is kind of light. MDF or particleboard works better, and TOTAL hermetic seals are mandatory any way you can accomplish it. If the door does prove to be a problem, you could maybe add a layer of either sheet rock or MDF for more mass, depending on your hinges' strength - but usually, the seals are the biggest hurdle. I suspect that the thin windows are going to be even more of a problem, so I wouldn't rush to add mass to the outer wall layer (I hope it's accessible though, so you CAN) until you find out whether I'm right. There's no point in spending time/money on the walls until you know the windows aren't the main problem (weakest link...) Can you post another drawing showing the EXACT detail of what is physically connected to what in your window construction? For example, the inner glass should be bedded in rubber and have no connection whatever to the outer wall or glass, the outer glass ditto - there should be some type of absorbent surrounding the space between glasses, that can "leak" sound into the insulation cavity for absorption. Everything should be caulked with high quality, non-hardening caulk, etc... If you need a source for the hearing protectors I mentioned, you can get small quantities at some sporting goods stores (look in hunting accessories) - I buy mine in boxes of 200 pairs at a welding supply place for $32, since I do 'way too many noisy things to ever be without them. What we need next is the detail drawing of the windows, and for you to try my suggestions on tracking down the source of the leaks... Steve
