A while back you mentioned that you could hear some faint low frequency HVAC noise. Did you ever figure out where the noise was coming from? viewtopic.php?f=2&t=18654&start=90#p140339
It is either the sound of the air in the baffle box or potentially in the duct that is transferring into the baffle box and then to the registers. Either way it is faint due to the awesome information that went into the design and build. Information kindly supplied by Stuart and others across John's community forum. To hear the faint sound I have to climb a short ladder and put my ear right up against the register slots. Of course mics are another thing altogether and I would imagine that a solo instrument mix, featuring a single acoustic solo instrument, being tracked at ppp would require the HVAC to be turned off. I can live with that. But, if I were to do this again I guess I would look to increase the diameter of the HVAC duct, add inline silencers and redesign the baffle box cross sectional diameter to match.
Regarding the window in your live room. Did you use two separate frames to keep the inner and outer leaves decoupled, or is there one solid frame bridging the two leaves. This is a design element I'm researching for my own build at the moment. viewtopic.php?f=2&t=18654&start=90#p139902
Correct the window is decoupled 100% (essentially). My outside leaf laminated glass window is fixed within the core filled block work. My inner leaf window frame reveal was built such that it only attached to the inner leaf frame, i.e., no part of the frame touched the outer leaf block work. It was fiddly, but that inner leader window reveal (that was fixed to the inner leaf frame) pulled up about 3mm short of the block work. Now I did do something to finish that may have compromised a tiny amount of isolation. I think what I am about to say is ok because the isolation between the studio space and upstairs (about 3.2m above) and also outside the block work downstairs is pretty amazing. When my noise meter is showing a fairly sustained 110dB downstairs I can not hear anything other than a faint sound of music upstairs. If the TV is on (even softly) then you would not know that there is anything going on downstairs at all and if the TV is loud upstairs there is zero chance of hearing that downstairs when the studio space is closed up. So what did I do? I ran a bead of GreenGlue Noise Proofing acoustic caulk around the 3mm gap left between the inner leaf window reveal and outer leaf block work window. This was done as part of finishing the space (reveal) across the windows and before the glass contractors came to install the inner leaf window. I guess I could have left that 3mm gap unfinished and I did think about that at the time. The glass contractors also installed the inner leaf window with silica gel traps inside the sealed air gap space and fitted it in such a way that if the sealed space does ever require servicing then that would be possible. It would most likely require the contractors to come back or to at least have specialist glass handling gear as the glass itself weighed about 70kg for 1.2m square. Imho getting a 100% silent HVAC solution inside the finished space is probably going to be trickier than achieving the required isolation between the space and the outside world. Of course I wish there was not a faint noise (as above) in my HVAC, but, it is faint, I am glad I did not go with splits and if worse comes to worse I can always turn off the HVAC. If I were super bugged by the faint sound, then in the future I could look to retrofit inline silencers back out side of the outer leaf where all the HVAC gear and air exchanger is. But, I would have to be super keen because the faint noise may not be due to duct noise showing up in the baffle and hence at the registers. If it is the baffle itself then I guess I could look to redesign the registers and see if that stops the noise 100%. I did design and build the baffle boxes so they can be pulled apart from the inside, but, that would not be a small job. My advice would be to take as much time as required in the HVAC design phase, try to find a really knowledgable local expert (preferably who has a good working relationship with the HVAC installer) who can also liaise with anyone who may be assisting on the forums and also be prepared to test noise performance during the install phase. I hope the above helps. Cheers, Ben
