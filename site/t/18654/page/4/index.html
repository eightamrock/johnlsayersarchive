bencarter30 June 2016#77
Hi Stuart, Thanks! We are definitely moving slowly and carefully to make sure that everything is carefully sheeted, no gaps and sealing as we go. Yes the box is big and heavy. The wood is 360 x 45 treated LVL and the spans were checked by the supplier against the weight. The double drywall is there as these air in and out baffle boxes are essentially also doubling as ceiling panels where they occur. In between these the other ceiling panels are just normal double sheeted panels built inside out. So these boxes are only connected/touching the inner leaf. Given the space above the ceiling that I could spare, the overall finished room dimensions (carefully checked and re-checked using bob golds room mode calculator), thinking about how the final inside finished space would look and almost endless calculations around airflow speed and volume, this seemed the best way to go. The boxes all have at least 2 x 180 degree turns before straightening out as the air approaches the vent and are lined with 25mm fibreglass that is also lined with black material that aims to keep air noise down. It's a Bradford product called Ulratel and the black coating is Ultraphon. The ducting that connects the boxes is also insulated but that is more to keep the air temperature consistent. The HVAC designer has placed additional attenuation in the metal ducting that leaves the air handling unit (all located outside of the outer leaf under the house at the back). I must admit I am still not sure what that attenuation is and will ask him again before the AHU and other bits and pieces get installed. I have been assured (I know I know I'm hoping/accepting to some extent but I am confident that the HVAC designer knows what I need as we talked and talked about the design over several iterations) by the HVAC designer that the only noise issue that I may need to look more carefully at is that generated by the air in the baffle box. But, he suggested the Ultratel/Ultraphon and getting the cross sectional area correct to address that. I am also following the suggestion/guidelines that you have kindly provided, i.e., a quite/low noise air register, having a straight path after the baffles to allow the air to settle down prior to the vent and also those pesky internal dimensions to slow the air as much as possible immediately after leaving the duct and entering the box, i.e., around 100fps. Hence I've ended up with something that maybe overkill :roll: I hope not! On the upside the process of putting it all together is quite straight forward. Just time consuming. We are lifting with a genie lift from a local hire company. They go up easily with the lifter and it is just small enough to roll into the space. It's actually more hassle getting the lifter into the space than using it. The last ceiling panel will be a challenge in a few weeks time. Thanks for checking back in it is much appreciated! Cheers, Ben

So these boxes are only connected/touching the inner leaf.
Seeing that you are going for over-kill, you might want to consider putting Sorbothane pads between the silencer box and whatever is supporting it, to get just that extra little bit of decoupling: You'll need to do some more math to figure out the thickness and size of the pads, but Sorbothane provide a downloadable app on their website for doing exactly that. If you decide to follow this plan, then you'd add a short wooden "sleeve" that goes down from the box to the register, make the hole through ceiling a few mm bigger than the sleeve, and use extra-soft caulk to seal that up, so there's no physical contact at all between the silencer and the inner leaf. I've done that on a couple of builds, and it works well. That's just in case there might be any slight "rumble" in the box structure itself from the air movement, or from sympathetic resonance, or due to the position of Jupiter relative to Saturn... :) Of course, if it's too late to do that, it's no big deal: it probably only buys you a point or two on the NC or NR scale. But for the sake of extreme overkill... :)

HVAC people normally talk about the "insertion loss" of those inline silencers. It's sort of like "transmission loss" for a solid barrier. Soundman202022 August 2016#91
Is there any point in doing an initial "empty room" REW test before I have built the soffits and the small RFZ wall return off the soffits that I am planning?
Strictly speaking, it isn't totally necessary, but personally I do like to have as much data as possible about a room, so I can see how it is changing over time as each part of the puzzle goes in. Having the data for the entirely empty room, then again once the soffits are in, will show you a couple of things: Firstly (and rather obviously!) it will show you how the soffits affected the room modes, and secondly how mounting the speakers in them affected the speaker response. Neither if those is "life or death", of course, but it would be interesting to see both of those, to help determine if something else might be needed, or something might NOT be needed. For example, if the soffits do a good job of broadening the modal response, that might have implications for treatment that you were planning. Etc. If it would be a big issue to run those tests, then don't worry about it. But if you could, I think you'd find it worthwhile. Even just to satisfy curiosity! :) - Stuart -
