Wouldnt carpet be better for deadening the sound? Why use wood flooring in a studio?
jwl20 June 2007#2
Deader is not necessarily better. For recording acoustic instruments, Ethan Winer argues in his Acoustics FAQ that our ears are used to hearing early reflections from a floor, and a reflective floor just sounds more natural for our ears. I'd say it really depends on what else is happening in the room. If you are building from scratch, hard, reflective floors (ie, wood or stained concrete) with treatments on the walls and ceilings are a proven design.

Soundman202028 March 2010#18
Seems all good to me.
What seems good, sometimes isn't. Carpet is akin to egg crates: Both certainly do have an effect on the acoustics of a room, but it probably isn't an effect that you want! And unless you did some extensive lab testing, or otherwise have reliable figures on the acoustic properties of the specific carpets, you have no idea what "effect" they might actually be having. If you do plan to use carpet, then at least use one that has known acoustic properties, and make sure that those properties make sense for your specific room. But in any event, leaving a hard ceiling and just a thin carpet on the floor is probably not going to be a good solution: It won't do much for the low end at all, which is where all of your problems are likely to be anyway (in a small room), and apart from that it gives the room an unnatural sound. - Stuart -
