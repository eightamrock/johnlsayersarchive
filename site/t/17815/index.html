Soundman202028 September 2012#2
Hi Don, and Welcome! :)
In general what would I need to do to modify a design that uses soffit mounted to speakers for use with the MM27's? Can I just eliminate the soffit?
Yes you can! That will have some acoustic consequences, of course, but other aspects of the treatment can be changed. That said, the specific design you referred to ("10.1") was modified in several ways later in that thread, and eventually discarded in favor of a better design, so that might not be such a a good option for you.
Do I need to replace it with an sound absorber of some sort.
Yes. If you remove the soffits, you will have a bare wall behind the speakers, which means that all of the artifacts that the soffit was eliminating will now be back in the room, so you'll need to treat them in other ways. Absorption on the front wall is a good way of dealing with first reflections, SBIR and comb filtering, for example.
I figure I need to at least have an idea about the requirements for the MM27 placement to start putting together a Sketchup plan and ask some specific questions.
You could ask Barefoot himself what he recommends, once you have your basic plan laid out. He posts here occasionally (although I haven't seem him around in a while), so maybe you could PM him, or e-mail him at his own web site. - Stuart -

Soundman202012 November 2012#5
I am attaching a Sketchup file of the initial attempt at a design. Is that the easiest way to post these early attempts or should I just post a jpg?
Posting the SKP is good, and also adding a jpg or two is better, as it makes it easier to get an idea with just a glance at your thread, without downloading and opening the SKP.
- Should I have two leaf walls around the entire 33'x33' area including the bathroom and hallway?
Not necessary, since they will be "outside" the actual isolated area of the studio.
- Do you think the control room will work for mixing and recording in the same space?
It should work, yes, but you seem to be wasting space by splaying the entire length of the left and right walls. You only really need to splay the front part, up to the mix position, and the splay angle should be larger, if you hope to achieve a true RFZ.
Would I make the monitor end very dead and put diffusion on the sofa end?
Not necessarily. Furniture upholstery is often made from closed-cell, which does not have much in the way of useful acoustic properties, and even if it does have open-cell foam in it, then you would just need to take that into account when you determine the total amount and placement of room absorption. There are equations that tell you how much absorption you need in the room to get the RT-60 times under control, and the general rule is to spread it around evenly for maximum effect, so as long as you take that into account, you should be OK.
I thought about making the whole space dead and throw up a couple of room mics and run it through my Bricasti at low levels through the Barefoot MM27's for a more pleasant room sound to jam with friends in the Control Room space.
Dear rooms are unpleasant to work in, and trying to fix that artificially is a "kludge" the will only work for some places in the room at any given time. Not a good idea. It is far better to get the room itself sounding good, smooth, even and neutral by appropriate design and placement of treatment. - Stuart -

Soundman20205 May 2013#7
Good to hear that things are moving forward, Don! I was kind of wondering what had happened...
I have pretty much decided that the plan that "Shybird" came up with for his Nashville studio - Layout 14.5 is almost perfect.
It's a pretty good layout, I think. He spent a lot of time refining that.
I have started discussions with a contractor. He hasn't built a studio before but he does great work on residential and commercial projects. And he seems to be very flexible and willing to do whatever we need him to do.
I would suggest that you ask him to read Rod's book before he starts on the studio section. That will give him the basis for what he needs to do different. And considering the very high level of isolation you are after, it is critical that he do everything perfectly.
It looks like I need to shoot for 70db of isolation. A little more wouldn't hurt but 70 would work.
About that... 70 dB is do-able, but it is not easy to get there. It's a pretty tall order, actually. Not to discourage you! Just to point out that this is going to require some very careful design, very careful attention to detail in the construction, especially in the doors, windows and HVAC system. If you take a close look at IR-761, out of a total of over 300 techniques for building isolation walls, you'll see that there is only one single construction technique that gets to STC-69, one more at STC-67, and two at 66. The vast majority are below 60. And that's STC, not even true transmission loss. So that should give you some clarity on what you are trying to do: it can be done, but isn't easy.
- Does the pad need to be poured so that the live/control room floors don't extend to the exterior walls.
For your level of isolation, I would pour separate, isolated slabs for each room, in addition to the actual outer-leaf foundations for the building itself. So there would be one slab (with integral footings), for the live room, one slab (ditto) for the control room, and the outer-leaf walls plus the rest of the building would be on a third set of footings/slab.
Or do we isolate the interior rooms from one another but leave the pad for the exterior walls coupled to the interior space?
I think the last part of that sentence should have read: "... exterior walls coupled to the exterior space". (not "interior". If you have separate slabs for the inner rooms, then your exterior walls, by definition, are not connect to the interior space. There's only two sets of walls here: the "outer-leaf" which surrounds the entire studio area and is connected to the rest of the house, and the "inner-leaf" walls of each room.
I am concerned that having a separate pad for just the exterior walls may not provide enough support. We have had settling problems in the house foundation.
Well, each slab needs it's own foundations, obviously, and if there is any question of settling, then you should get an engineer to look at at that, and design your footings/foundations/slabs accordingly. You may need to have concrete pillars going down to bedrock...
- What do I need for walls for 70db TL to the exterior?
If you are going for 70 dB of isolation to the outside world, then you might as well do it between rooms as well.
I was thinking about sand filled cement block for the exterior wall but that might be overkill.
Definitely not overkill, if you want 70 dB of isolation! According to BRN-217, if those were 4 inch solid concrete bricks (not solid) you should be getting around 45-50 dB of isolation from that. Mass law predicts around 55, but I'd go with BRN-217, since those are actual real-world laboratory tests. For hollow 6" concrete block, the story is about the same. 8" block is a fraction better. So that basic construction will give you roughly 45 dB of isolation to start with. The sand will add a little to that, maybe 2 or 3 dB. So now you need to add another 25 dB to that, to hit your target. That's where things get a bit more complicated... :)
Does a cement block filled with sand constitute one or two leafs.
One leaf.
Are we better off with a wood frame with Hardiplank siding?
I would not go that way. Your basic plan so far is going in the right direction. Outer leaf: sand-filled concrete block for the walls, and concrete slabs for the floor and the ceiling. I would then render all that with plaster on both sides: that should add about 5 dB, according to BRN-217. Then I would put up 2x4 24"OC stud framed walls for the inner-leaf, using at least an 8" air gap, filled with 30kg/m3 fiberglass wool, then at least two layers of 5/8" drywall (maybe 3 layers) on only one side of the studs, and Green Glue in between the layers. In theory, that should get you close to 70 dB. But like I said before: the biggest issue for such a high level of isolation will be the doors, windows, and above all the HVAC design. Those are going to be a real challenge... - Stuart -
