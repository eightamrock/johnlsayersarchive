my main focus is which type of walls would work best for this type of situation.
MSM, undoubtedly! :) Lots of mass and big air gap.
i have been reading as much as i can about different types of walls, but im still a bit confused about which kind are best for low end transmition loss...
The kind that has the lowest MSM resonant frequency! The wall is a tuned system: you have to tune it so that its natural resonance is at a frequency way below the lowest one that you want to isolate. Isolation starts at roughly 1.4 x the resonant frequency, but only gets good at around 2x resonant frequency. So you have to tune your wall l-o-w. You do that with mass and air gap.
im kind of against concrete walls due to the small space
If you have the option, then concrete walls are great! Lots of mass in a small space. The density of concrete is around 2401 kg/m3, which is roughly double that of drywall, so you can get the same amount of mass in half the thickness. That's a good deal! But concrete walls are not cheap or easy...
and it would require much more internal trapping and treatment right?
It is going to require lots of treatment regardless of what material you use to build it. That's the side effect of isolating your room. If you think about it, it makes sense: "isolation" means that you stop sound from getting out, which implies that it stays inside, which implies that the room will not sound good. The actual materials aren't so important here: what matters is getting the correct isolation for your situation, then treating accordingly. - Stuart -

definitely bring the structure back to stable and strong enough for the added mass and consider 3x 5/8" drywall layers inside the stud flush to the exterior sheathing (or with blueboard if you have nails protruding), then a room within a room using another 2-3 layers of 5/8". on the ceiling - if you can adjust trussing to open the height that would be good then a 3x 5/8" drywall, or enhance the rafters to support the additional mass and then support a ceiling on the inner isolation walls.
