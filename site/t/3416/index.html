John Sayers18 March 2005#5
Both designs have the same layout basically. In this design the two rear walls are important as if left untreated they will both reflect the signal straight back at the listening position and therefore must be heavily treated to be dead and non reflective. i.e 4" 703 as far off the wall as will fit. The rear corner can then be made as a hanger trap. The front walls and an added angled ceiling cloud are the only walls where you can add reflective surfaces to stop the room from becoming toooo dead - especially in the highs. Now the glass doors and windows are reflective but you need to add the slots on these walls and to the ceiling cloud as well. I noticed that neither of you have drawn in a soffit mount which is a pity as this is how this corner design works best - when you use only nearfields you usually make the front wall dead to absorb the rear projection from the free standing nearfields yet when you soffit mount this is eliminated except directly below the speakers. I've attached a drawing showing a method of adding a corner reflector that directs the direct signal so it is reflected away from the listening position with a hanger trap built behind it in the corner. The rear wall is then covered with heavy 703 and cloth covered to hide it. hope this helps cheers john
