the rubber pad should be ok assuming its firm enough to support the speaker but resilient enough to decouple it. you could tap on it with a mallet to see how well it decouples. the front panel (bezel) for the speaker (the finishing touch) woud be attached to the front frame/panel using glue/screws/nails etc... as desired for a good finish - but you want to leave just a very small gap around it so it doesn't touch the speaker. if you can cut the bezel down to around 2-3mm gap, you're probably in good shape, 1mm would be nicer...

gullfo19 August 2007#8
that looks like some substantial speaker stand 8) generally speaking you can center it on the soffit but if you can offset it a bit, like many other tactics, it can help to eliminate possible common resonances in the frames and plates (ie you're not going to get the same two frequencies resonating because you have different sizes...) on the bottom section - on the inside of the framing, add a plate that extends down to about 1' above the bottom. this extends the soffit so you don't get a weird cutoff on the speaker response. pad the front of that plate with cloth covered insulation to stop reflections back from the console. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... 15c6#64862

Eric_Desart20 August 2007#11
Sand is a good filling material, better than using concrete here. But the sand has to be dry and fine. In order to work as intended it must flow. I like the idea better of a steel plate (keeps gravity point of speaker more logical). You just have an elegant thin steel plate cut on the exact size, rather than that large box. Sand ca 2 tons/m3 + the wood ca 0.7 tons/m3, steel ca 8 tons/m3. Paul calculated this stuff, but if you cut whatever elastic material in pieces which deflect 3 mm under load you're OK. I've somewhere a post with related formulas and more explenation (it's all the same if it refers to rizers, floating floors, whatever). I once read that Genelec advices 4 Hz for their large speakers but they don't describe how they obtain that. This calls for a very large deflection which makes such speakers very wobbly (or you must solve that by increasing mass and supporting surface (depending on type of course). I think you can do with less, hence the 3 mm (<10 Hz) I still think that you can make one integrated corner construction in which you position these speakers rather than making independent constructions (much more stable and simpler). But people as Gullfo are much better than I am to work out such ideas. And my reply only is valid when you think to need decoupling for whatever reason. What I mean is: decoupling is something to be applied when you expect problems of whatever nature if they're not decoupled. Decoupling is no quality label for good sound.
gullfo21 August 2007#12
someone around here used a stack of blocks with rubber conveyor belt below the speaker as the internal stand ;-) to Eric's point, a single structure is easier to build. if you're going to extend the soffits low, remember to be aware of desk reflections hitting off the front so put some absorption on the lower bit. 1" (25mm) MDF is common for the front soffit plate because its heavy. 29/32 (21mm) plywood can be used. 3/4 (19mm) can be used as well but may be more prone to resonating so ensure you've made it very rigid and applied insulation well to dampen it.

Eric_Desart21 August 2007#14
If you should use this design (leave it to others). I should make a big round hole in the board beneath and between the resilient supports. The air also acts as a spring, with higher resonance than the rest. That way you neutralize that spring (better than only counting on the open edges). I think you better make these vertical supports (underneath speakers) stronger (and lower supporting horizontal board as in original design) if you make that speaker with steel that heavy. That support should be stable and stiff, the decoupling will take care of the rest. For me looks good. If decoupled right, it won't (or hardly) excite rest of structure. Here are the formulas to relate deflection with resonance. http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewt ... ght=#13425 Should be better if integrated in some sticky, since it relates to a lot of resilient applications as speakers, rizers, floating floors etc. (had to find it back now).
