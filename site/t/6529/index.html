AndreasB wrote:

I'm not sure a 2" gap is going to do much for you (hopefully the experts will correct me if I'm wrong). My windows are 8" and 12" apart (drum room and control room respectively). Part of the reason for the gap is to allow one side of the glass to squish the air before it sets to other pane in motion. The more air you have, the easier it is to squish.
blue642 wrote:
t looks like it would just be one very reflective surface. and wouldn't alow for any apsorbtion via insulation behind it...
Blue, I think you are misunderstanding the purpose of the insulation in the walls. As I understand the physics (which I don't), insulation doesn't really absorb anything when it's IN the walls (as opposed to ON the walls like an absorber panel). It's purpose is to dampen the vibration of the wall and smooth out the response of the room. If you imagine hitting a cymbal and then touching it very lightly with your finger, you know it stops pretty quickly even with just the lightest touch. Your finger is not absorbing the sound, just making it harder for the cymbal to vibrate and make the sound itself. len

len-morgan1 July 2006#12
AndreasB wrote:
MY walls have a 2" airgap and I was told that was going to be enough..
How are you measuring the gap? If you have a "standard" 2"x4" stud wall, and you put drywall on both sides you get AT LEAST 3 1/2". You measure from the back of one mass (drywall) to the back of the ajacent mass to get your gap.
AndreasB wrote:
.. but 12"..geez that's way to much, wouldn't something less be sufficient?
Sufficient for what? Less gap = less isolation. Plain and simple. You can play with the variables to change things, for example, adding another layer of drywall would be the same as a increasing the gap a couple of inches (and I DON'T know the exact number of inches of gap/layer of drywall I'm just making an example). It's all about comprimise. We ALL comprimise in every design we do, every decision we make. In my case, I would have loved to use 2"x8" studs in ALL my walls but it would have consumed too much floor space. Floor space was more important to me and since I'm in a free standing cement block building, I had isolation to "give" without hurting anything so that's how I went. You really have to approach this like any other kind of engineering. What are your specs? What MUST you meet to make the project worth doing? What would be nice but you COULD live without if it meant the difference between doing the project and abandoning the dream. If you are in an apartment, you'll probably have isolation as the most important requirement. You could trade off a little space to get more isolation but there is a point where you won't have enough space to be useable. Do this process FIRST. List your problems, desires, wants, needs and prioritize them, then make a design that meets them - or find it's not possible to meet them and start trading off until you either have something you're happy with or determine that your goals just can't be done for the time,money,expertise, etc. that you are willing to give to this project.
AndreasB wrote:
can of course tilt the glass in two directions (less at the bottom and more at the top). but will that really make any difference..?
Yes, it WILL make a difference. It will make things worse. Your problem with a glass wall (or any wall for that matter) is not resonance but isolation. Don't get me wrong, there ARE resonanace issues but 99% of the time, you are putting up a wall (of any type) to isolate one place from another. If that wasn't the goal, why build the wall in the first place? From personal experience, relections of light aren't a big deal and aren't that hard to handle. If you look at my studio build diary, you can see the big windows I put in (double window panes) and while I can see my reflection a little (if I focus on the glass instead of what's on the other side of it), just adjusting one of the lights in the control room to not point directly at the glass solved most of the problem. Personally, I prefer to have just a little reflection because someone coming into my studio will realize quicklly that there IS a piece of glass there! If they didn't see something, they might try and put their hand through it to set down a drink on the space between the panes! Hope that helps a little. len
