Ethan Winer6 May 2008#3
sound_music wrote:
1) what material's and methods would you recommend for acoustic treatment in each room? (absorption, diffusion, reflection etc)
That sort of info is all over this forum. Below is my standard reply. As for cost, it could be as little as $1,000 US for DIY materials with you doing the legwork, to maybe $20,000 for a full complement of commercial products with full advice and guidance from the treatment vendor. --Ethan -------------------- Room treatment is a deep subject, and a complete answer requires far more than will fit into a single reply here. So here's the short version. All rooms need: * Broadband (not tuned) bass traps straddling as many corners as you can manage, including the wall-ceiling corners. More bass traps on the rear wall behind helps even further. You simply cannot have too much bass trapping. Real bass trapping, that is - thin foam and thin fiberglass don't work to a low enough frequency. * Mid/high frequency absorption at the first reflection points on the side walls and ceiling. * Some additional amount of mid/high absorption and/or diffusion on any large areas of bare parallel surfaces, such as opposing walls or the ceiling if the floor is reflective. Diffusion on the rear wall behind you is also useful in larger rooms. For the complete story see my Acoustics FAQ. There's a lot of additional non-sales technical information on my company's site - articles, videos, test tones and other downloads - in my sig below.

AVare7 May 2008#5
You are asking for specifics with too vague information. For stone walls search for concrete, brick, cmu (Concrete Masonry Unit ), etc. walls and studios. There have been several posts and build threads with this type of construction. The biggest difference acoustically between that and drywall is that drywall construction absorbs low frequencies while stone does not. You will need more and/or deeper bass traps. Absorption is done with thick fluffy type material. Mineral wool, rockwool, fiberglass, shredded denim, recycled cotton, are all used depending on price availability. Diffusion is normally done with wood or closed cell plastics. Depending on material and labour prices you may find it cheaper to have it into your walls. Are you doing the labour yourself or paying for it? Professional designers can work by the internet and not have to fly in, if you are willing to answer their questions. Either study ALOT and ask a lot of questions understanding answers and answering further questions, and use the search function or pay for a professional to design it for you. Blue Bear cost $62,000 with the outer shell (basement) already in place. What seems to be the informal standard in several threads is $100,000. The most realistic generalization I know of is to price high end home building costs ad add 25%. Even that is vague. When people ask for specifics, give them if you want progress in your search for information. Your comment about not having a million dollars is meaningless. That will buy a two bedroom apartment in Manhattan, or a luxury home in Tennessee. Andre

gullfo9 May 2008#11
the answer to the business side is to analyze your market and competition and figure out how much business you expect, you operation costs, and then you can work in the startup costs for design, construction, and equipment - to be paid off over X amount of time. if the plan feels real to the investors then cost shouldn't be too much of an issue because you've done the research, have tangible experience running it, and have a valid plan... from a design perspective, getting a decent proposal on the facility can help investors feel confident you have it together. usually a decent floor plan and some rendered images will go along with a "ballpark" figure (best bet - figure out the square area cost for high-end home construction and multiply that the estimated square area you think you need for the studio - then add 25% - that should be a reasonable guess). so if your m2 cost for a high end home is $2,000, then a 100m2 facility will be $200,000 + 25% = $250,000. probably reasonably, expect about 8-10% of that cost will be design, another X% for licenses, permits etc. equipment is highly variable.
