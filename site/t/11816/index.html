I'm not a sound designer or acoustic engineer, and I don't need a million dollar studio to accomplish my goals. I simply need help with a basic design, and am open to a functional stock design if anyone has those available. The beauty is that I can design the interior layout any way I want. Once the exterior shell is finished, I would be doing most of the work myself. My main goal is control room / studio room(s) and function. The outer shell can be built to suit. Let me first explain what my studio needs are, and then you guys can chime in with recommendations. I'm located in a rural area of southern Georgia, USA, and I'm not in a big city where I'll be doing this full-time, although that would be my long-term goal to run the studio full-time, so functionality and expandability is desirable. I mostly record solo artists, along with 4 and 5 piece groups, and I would like the ability to track 4-5 piece bands at once (drums, guitars, bass, vocals). Thus, I presume I'd need 1 or 2 main studios, with at least one iso booth and / or vocal closet. So I presume I'd need the following... Control Room Studio 1 (large room) Studio 2 (medium room) Iso / Vocal Booth (small room) Iso / Vocal Booth (small room) I'm thinking my budget would allow for me to utilize about 1200 - 1500 sq. ft for the whole studio, and I'd like to include a small bathroom with a shower stall, and perhaps a small kitchenette. The building itself will be built to suit the studio - a square or rectangle building shell. I know John's designs are usually designed around pre-built structures, but this one is the other way around. I would like to keep costs to a bare minimum. Looks aren't important as much as functionality. It can be as ugly and scary as Hilary Clinton at this point... I can always change decor and perform cosmetic additions later. I also have access to smoothed cypress boards at a great price, and for bulk cloth / fabric materials (my brother-in-law runs an upholstry shop.) I know that cypress is a softer wood, so what recommendations do you guys have concerning the walls of the main studio room and control room? Is cypress a good selection? I presume that cypress might be a good choice, since most prefer a harder floor surface, with softer textures for walls (which the walls would be cypress.) Cypress is a beautiful wood! I'm open to all suggestions. I have no large instruments that I have to consider regarding room space (no grand piano, B3 organ, etc.), but I do have one of Yamaha's 5-piece electric drum kits, which is the only consideration. I have a few keyboards, but I would be utilizing one keyboard as my main controller and my studio desk, which is an Omnirax F2 keyboard type desk (maple finish.) I don't even require that my main monitors be built into the walls, though that option would be nice further down the road as the studio grows. Regarding instruments and needs, specifically I plan on recording: Vocals, all styles Vocal Groups - Small Choirs, Vocal Sections Horns - Mostly Trumpet and Saxophones, Sections, Combo Sections, Brass Quartet / Quintet, T.O.P. type horn sections Guitar Cabs Acoustic Guitars Bass Guitars - Direct and Cab'd Strings - Solo, Quartests / Quintests / Combos Bluegrass Groups - banjo, acoustic guitars, upright bass, misc. instruments Possible small groups - brass quintets, string quartets, bluegrass groups (live in studio), small choirs Harmonica / Harp Players Acoustic Drums and Percussion I've got a pretty good virtual instrument library going on, with a lot of my work as a local composer / producer. I would like a comfortable control room setup since I spend a lot of extra time in the control room mixing and composing, and I would like a couch in the rear for clients to sit & listen. The view window between the control room and main studio can be any size, as I can get glass pretty much any size. I'm thinking my ceiling would be limited to 12', although I haven't seen the proposed building structure proposal yet. So, what do you guys think? If you had the freedom to design a studio based on my needs, how would you lay out a control room, main studio room, and iso booths / alternate studio rooms based on what little info I've presented? I want my control room to be functional for tracking, mixing, and basic mastering, and comfortable! I'm all ears, and will greatly appreciate all feedback! Hey, one of you guys may end up being my studio designer...

ELECTRICAL CONSIDERATIONS: There are no special considerations that I know of. COSTS: I would prefer to keep costs to a minimum, doing as much work and design build myself, but considering the nature of the "ground up" / "new project" build, I would like input from all the guys here since this is my first true studio venture. It's very difficult for me to say at this time exactly how much money I can put into the studio, as I've yet to secure the house loan. STUDIO SETUP: My system is a Pro Tools LE (modified to HD) setup using a Mac G5. My monitoring system is the Hear Technologies "Hearback" personal monitoring system. I currently have 4 Hearback controllers, but can utilize up to 8 controllers. Practically all of my outboard gear is racked in the F2 desk, which has wheels and is "mobile". I do not utilize a DAW mixer, although I would consider a C/24 or something similar compatible with Pro Tools in the future. I hope to purchase a pair of new Event Opal monitors by the time I get the studio built. Would the Event Opal monitors be a good selection for in-wall applications? I have no preference for in-wall applications, as most of my tracking and mixing is done via near-field / mid-fields. Sound quality and functionality is the goal. I'm only concerned with sound quality in the control room, not volume. Well, hope this enlightens a bit more.
