Your ceiling design really doesn't depend on your floor design. Yes, there are ways to decouple the ceiling below from the floor above. From worst to best: Resilient Channel ("RC"), Resilient Channel Isolation Clips ("RSIC"), or ideally, if you can afford the clearance, separate joists firmly attached to the new inner leaf walls. mlange wrote:
For the new basement ceiling a new rim joist would go around the enitre basement wall's perimeter, below the upper floor (and not touching, either), and the new ceiling joists would attach here. So the decoupling from the upper floor is resolved.
No, it's not. :roll: By fastening the lower ceiling to rim joists that are firmly attached to the basement walls, you would have a pretty bad flanking path. The firm attachment of rim joists to the existing concrete walls is, in my opinion, a non-starter. Ideally you would do "room in room," building new wall frames and attaching them to the floor of your basement, and then installing ceiling joists firmly attached to the top plates of your new wall frames. If you leave enough of a gap between new frames and existing concrete walls, you wouldn't even need any resilient sway braces. In my last post I asked: "Have you looked at some of the prominent basement conversion threads that are pretty active right now?" Whose threads have you looked at so far? (This is a quiz! ;-)) Rod's book will go a long way towards clearing this stuff up for you. 8) --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward9 March 2007#9
You don't need to attach an inner frame ceiling rim joist to an outer leaf concrete wall at all. Just pretend the outer leaf wall isn't there... Like you're building a shed that happens to be a little smaller than the basement. No sway beaces, no anchoring to outer leaf walls. You'll need fire stops though... See my thread's inner leaf framing pages for details. It's really similar to my inner leaf framing... Except that mine's above ground and yours is underground. --Keith :mrgreen:
