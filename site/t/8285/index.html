Started by drfrankencopter on 26 February 2007. 7 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 8285.

drfrankencopter26 February 2007#1
I've got some sound isolation company clips purchased for the ceiling of my studio. According to the rep I talked to I need 20ga Hat channel to attach the clips to (and to support my ceiling). I just called up my local building material supplier, and they were a little confused when I asked for hat channel, but they did have 20ga furring channel (at $8 for a 12 ft length). Is furring channel and hat channel the same thing???? Thanks Kris
jwl27 February 2007#2
What kind of clips are they? Are they RISC 1 clips? If so then a hat channel is what you want. DO you have Rod's book? If so, take it into the store and show them p 58. I'm not sure what a "furring channel" is, though I suspect it's similar to a hat channel. But you don't want to guess on this, otherwise you could lose your isolation.
rod gervais27 February 2007#3
If you are using RSIC clips then you need the right channel - Check here for dimensional info for the right stuff.... http://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/rsic_ ... iclips.pdf As an example of why you can't just say "hat channel" and have it be right - here's a company who manufactures hat channel - they have well over 25 different channels - and yet you would have to buy a custom channel to get the right dimensions: http://www.johnsonrollforming.com/displ ... category/4 Just make sure you double check with your suplier to verify the dimensions are correct. Sincerely, Rod
andy_eade27 February 2007#4
it's also important to follow the manufacturers guidelines and secure all joins with a 6 inch overlap and two "s" rated screws. I too am using RSIC's (except I am using the EXT04's to get an extra drop below the studs) and have called PAC-INTL to ensure I follow their spec to a tee since I don't want 3 layers of rock coming down on me mid-rockout! :lol: I was super cautious of using the 25 guage with three layers; but they actually recommended I do since the 22 guage (which my building supplies place couldn't source) is much more difficult to work with. Good luck!
leecow28 February 2007#5
Kris' mention of "sound isolation company" makes me think that he's using http://www.soundisolationcompany.com/Sound-Isolation-Clips.php which are the same thing I'm using on my ceiling. (drywall going up this weekend!) 7/8" hat (furring) channel is what is used with this clip. My supplier had 25 or 20 guage and I opted for the heavier channel.

leecow1 March 2007#8
My room is 22' long and also had to splice using the method Rod references. Just measure the needed length (+ 6") and cut the furring channel accordingly. I used a drill mounted cutoff wheel for this activity. Make sure you have several wheels as the channel eats 'em up pretty quick.
