Soundman202026 February 2017#2
Hi there Andy, and Welcome! :) That's a really awkward shape you have there! Not easy at all to fit things in. But before you try fitting things in, you need to decide something else: Namely: "How much isolation do you need, in decibels?". Unless you know that, you cannot know how your walls will need to be built. Right now, for example, you are showing single doors on the rooms, implying that you won't get more than about 25 to 35 dB of isolation, max, which is roughly what a typical house wall gets... way short for the majority of studios! Good isolation requires two doors, back to back, with an air gap between them, and the same applies to the walls: the wall is built as two "leaves" with a gap between them. That takes up space. How much space it takes up depends on how much isolation you need...
a moderately quiet location (not much noise coming in from outside
... It doesn't rain in Serbia? No wind, thunder, hail? No trucks / cars / motor bikes driving around on nearby roads? No aircraft flying over? No helicopters? No sirens (ambulance, police, fire)? No people talking / radios playing / TV? No toilets flushing in the house? Water running? No phones ringing? No vacuum cleaner? Washer? Dryer? No doors opening/closing? No footsteps? All you have is total silence, all day, every day? :)
The floor of an entire place is made of ceramic tiles. I am not a 'technical person' but I think they're not very good for sound.
That's perfect! That's an excellent floor, acoustically. Your floor is done: you don't need anything else.
Therefore I've bought I thin but firm itison carpet to cover the whole area
Take it back and get a refund! That would trash your acoustic response in the rooms. It does the exact opposite of what you need for that room.
5) Well as of now, all I'm really interested in is getting advice on how to divide the room solely, therefore I think I've provided enough details.
First, don't make a square room! Take a look at the concept of "room mode calculators" and "room ratios" to find out why, and to come up with a good set of dimensions for your control room.. Right now, your room is almost square, which is about the worst possible shape (except for a cylinder... that's the worst of all). I would suggest trying a "corner control room" layout. I think it might fit that space better than the one you are showing. - Stuart -

Soundman202026 February 2017#4
So, the isolation is something I would deal with in my next 'stage' or 'phase' of the studio build.
Then you are going about the whole thing backwards! That's like deciding to buy a car, but first concentrating on the color of the floor mats and the type of transmission fluid it uses as your first priority , then saying "I'll deal with the number of people it can seat and the engine size at a later stage. First and most important is floor mats!"... :) There's a process to studio design, and it starts with isolation, purpose, occupancy, and equipment. If you do no know how much isolation you need, then you do not have one quarter of the basis for designing the studio. You should start by defining those things: How much isolation do you need? What is the purpose of the studio (full commercial, private hobby use only, a mix of the two? Tracking only? Tracking and mixing? Tracking mixing and mastering? For music release only, or also for cine work?) What occupancy do you expect in each room during a typical session (how many people in the live room, in the control room, in the vocal booth, in the lounge, etc.)? (That defines, for example, the capacity and size of the HVAC system that you will need) What equipment and instruments will you typically have in each room? For example, if you have certain speakers you might need to shape the room in certain ways and avoid others. If you have certain console type then you might need to have certain dimensions for the room to accommodate it. Etc.
