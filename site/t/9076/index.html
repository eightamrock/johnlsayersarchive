Have been researching for a while and have designed a studio to be built in my basement, and am ready (I think) to start purchasing materials. My goal is to build the best room I can, for monitoring mixes and tracking vocals - all other tracking is direct. Isolation is NOT a concern. I provide the following to help simplify any response (although, I'll always welcome add'l suggestions)... I feel fairly well informed in ratios, placement and types of absorption material, construction techniques, etc - my primary concern (and question) involves maintaining symmetry via wall materials, with an option for add'l bass absorption with the build. I'm a complete novice in smartdraw (my written plans are better detailed), so here is some additional image info: * Room size 21'3" x 14' x 9'8" * The black lines are 8'8" tall poured concrete walls that have a brick pattern. * The ceiling(upstairs floor) is 9'8" from the floor and underneath it are 12" I-joists running the length of the room 16" on center. * The green wall is a 2"x6" framed support wall, that I plan to mount drywall to on one side (haven't decided which side yet). * 4' from the front (blue line), running under the I-joists is a 3"x14" wooden support. * In the back is a 12"t x 30"w vent, 3/4 across the way * The 2 existing windows are 22"t x 34"x, the top falls just under the bottom of the I-joists. * The red line is the wall to be built. * NOTE - the external space both in the basement and above is fairly large. I plan on building a 2"x4" wood studded wall 16" or 24" on center, with a window mirroring that of the cement side wall, and a door opening outwards far enough away from the back wall for a corner bass trap. The inside of this wall would be drywalled with a brick textured wood panel(for looks and matching texture) on top of it - the outside of the wall would be left alone. Just short of building a brick wall, I know I can't fully mimic cement, but my thinking regarding symmetry is that any deviation for mid/highs between the new wall and the cement one could be controlled with adequate absorption treatment, and bass (being omni-directional) is not a concern and I may benefit some by it freely escaping thru the 2 walls and ceiling. 1: Is my thinking majorly flawed, and if so... suggestions ? 2: Just read the article on wood membrane bass traps - I don't expect details (I imagine its extrememly involved), but could I theoretically turn this wall (panel on inside, drywall on the other with inner treatment and sealant) into a bass trap itself by experimenting during the build, or might I create some drum-like problem ? gullfo8 July 2007#9
the idea here is "space coupling" where the sound is passing out the top and bottom or through the slats into the rest of the space. potentially this can be good or bad depending on the rest of the room charactistics but generally speaking the backing insulation should keep things mostly correct within the listening space... to really build up a set of resonators you'd want to build cabinets to create the chambers etc. my own space is (going to be) configured in a similar fashion to this but I also have 14' high ceilings and a very fragmented and angled set of walls and ceilings (in a 18'x26' room) which by themselves do all kinds of defraction... so I may get away with this approach... :-) I would leave a gap between the front of these and the wall (essentially in line with the back of the monitors on stands) so that sound can escape around the back of the panels. again, we're dealing with an open space so the escape allows the bass to move out. the front of the cloud would be just under the large beam and the monitors would be just under it so the space above and behind them (that 4' section behind the beam) could be bass trapping on the ceiling. you may still need some absorption up front but you can play with that. maybe just a drape over the window.
