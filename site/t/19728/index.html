I'm studying Rod's book with regards to MSM wall construction, and have a query with regards to location of the mass in a MSM wall construction. My situation is that I live on a large rural property in the Southern Tablelands of NSW, and I have access to an 8m x 8m space inside a newly erected colourbond(steel) shed with concrete floor and 5m pitched roofline. I'm hoping to build a couple of room within rooms inside for a control room and tracking room (private use). Neighbours/noise are not an issue (we are in the middle of 250 acres), however I do wish to isolate the room structures properly from the steel exterior so that rain noise and metal expansion noises during changing weather do not dictate when I can record. Ideally, I would wish to build a free standing wall structure just inside (but not attached to the metal sheeting of the shed walls) that would consist of, firstly 100mm insulation lining directly to the interior of the colour bond metal walls, then 16mm drywall or plywood>insulated studs>airgap>insulated studs>16mm plywood with 2nd layer of 16mm drywall (being the interior of the room). I doubt the metal wall would be considered a mass layer? The problem I have is that being on the farm (just my wife and myself) I can foresee a problem in building the frame for the exterior part of the wall on the floor, attaching the one or 2 layers of 16mm drywall, and then raising it myself - the weight would simply be too heavy for an 8m wall length some 4-5 metres high. To cut back to the question, could I take an alternate approach by erecting the first wall of 2x4 studs against the insulated layer of the colour bond metal wall (not touching it) then applying a couple of layers of 16mm drywall to it, then leaving an air gap, then insulated 2x4 studs and finally another 2 x 16mm drywall layers, i.e. insulated studs>drywall layers>airgap>insulated studs>drywall layers. Would this compromise the performance significantly given my modest needs? Thanks for any advice. PS with regards the actual studio layout, I am quite keen to model the design that Rod goes into some detail with in his book in Chapter 10 "Putting It All Together" (figure 10.5 studio layout) Steve
Soundman20205 January 2015#2
You have an interesting situation there, Steve.
I doubt the metal wall would be considered a mass layer?
Technically, the metal shell of the building will be a third leaf, but how much of a problem that will be depends on how you build the other two leaves. If those are a long way away from the sheet metal, then it won't be a problem. Also, if there is enough mass on those other two leaves, it won't be a problem. The things is to figure out "how far away" and "how much mass". In order to do that, you need to have a clear goal for isolation in mind, in terms of how many decibels of isolation you need. With that number, then it's a lot easier to come up with correct materials and dimensions. It would also help a lot if you could post some pics of the building, inside and out, so we can see how it is built, and maybe spot potential issues.
the weight would simply be too heavy for an 8m wall length some 4-5 metres high.
There are several options here too. One is to rent a couple of wall jacks to get that up. Two people on two wall jacks can get a pretty large and pretty heavy wall up, quiet fast. Another option is to build it in smaller manageable sections and lift each section into place, then bolt / nail / screw it to the previous section. And the third option is to NOT build it inside-out: just build it conventionally, by putting up the frame first, then attaching each sheet of plywood / MDF / OSB / drywall, one at a time. The last option is probably the safest, but you might lose a bit of space like that.
Would this compromise the performance significantly given my modest needs?
The amount of isolation you get depends on two things: the mass of each leaf in the wall (surface density), and the depth of the air gap between the leaves. It does not matter which side of the leaf you have the studs: that's an aesthetic issue, and a space-saving issue, but it is not an acoustic issue. For example assuming your studs are 40mm x 90mm, if you have: stud frame - 16mm drywall - 25cm gap - 16mm drywall - studs, then that will give you exactly the same isolation as: 16mm drywall - studs - 7cm gap - studs - 16mm drywall. In both cases the distance between the leaves (drywall) is 25 cm, so the isolation is the same. The only difference is that the studs are on opposite sides of the drywall, but that has practically no effect on the isolation. In other words, you can build it any way you want, as long as the gap between the leaves is correct, and the mass on each leaf is correct. For a three-leaf system (if you build close to the metal wall), then most of the mass must go on the middle leaf. If it is a 2-leaf system (built far away from the metal walls) then the mass should be evenly split between the two leaves. For a 3-leaf wall, you'll need more mass and larger air gaps to get the same isolation as you would with a 2-leaf wall. - Stuart -
