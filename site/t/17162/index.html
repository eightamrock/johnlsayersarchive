Hi Kevin, and Welcome! :) There's a few things you should check for on the HVAC system before thinking about booths, since the booths will need air from the HVAC anyway! But first: What type of "hum"? Is this a mechanical hum, coming through the building structure? Or is it air humming as it moves past something? Does the same HVAC that feeds your live room also feed another area of the building that might have some type of humming machinery in it? You HVAC guys might not be aware of how to isolate the HVAC system for studios: they might just be ordinary HVAC folks, that know about offices and houses, but not about studios, so I'd suggest that you might want to find an HVAC company that has experience with studios, ang get tehm to come check out the system. There are several things that should be done on an HVAC system for a studio. First, the "noise bit" must be mechanically isolated from the building structure, using proper isolation mounts that are dimensioned correctly for the equipment. Next, the HVAC ducts need to be decoupled (isolated) from the machinery with flexible sleeves where they connect to the equipment, and those also need to be dimensioned correctly for your specific equipment. Third, the ducts need to be lined on the INSIDE, not just the outside, using proper duct liner. Fourth, the ducts need to be mounted on isolation brackets, to keep structure-borne noise out of them. Fifth, the duct runs need to be long, and have at least two 90° bends in them, and/or proper silencer boxes need to be installed in-line with the ducts. Sixth, the ducts going to your studio should not go anywhere else in the building, and should be split for the individual rooms long before they get there, and silenced separately. Seventh, all the ducts should be over-sized (have a larger cross section than would normally be used), so that you get a larger volume of air moving more slowly, instead of a small volume of air moving fast. There's other things that can be done too, but I'd check that all of the above have been done correctly first. Maybe you could post a recording of the "hum" here, and also some photos of the HVAC system. - Stuart -

I thought that perhaps building some sort of isolation enclosure, might be the lesser of two evils // but I aso dont want to invest time and money to find out that the hum still is rearing its ugly head.
Well, you certainly could do that. It is an option. But if the sound is flanking through the building structure and HVAC system, which seems to be the case from what you say, I'm not sure how well that will work, unless you take special precautions: you still need to hook up the HVAC to the isolation booth, if you plan to have people staying alive in there for very long, or amps and speakers running inside without overheating. So the hum would still get in through the HVAC, unless you also build large silencers. And you'd also need to decouple the booth from the building structure, to keep the hum from flanking on. So an iso booth is an option, but needs to be thought through carefully, and designed with the above in mind to make sure it actually does isolate that specific hum, as well as everything else. Sure, please do post a sample of the noise, so we can hear what you are dealing with.
not sensitive to forking over the costs to adjust his HVAC unit.
Time for a cost-benefit analysis! Get a quote from HVAC guys that actually do understand studios on how much it would cost to correctly isolate that unit (hopefully just suitable vibration mounts and duct decoupling: shouldn't be too much), and compare that to the cost of designing/building the isolation booth, with all the details and accessories. If there's a huge difference in cost, your choice is easy, even if it means forking over your cash to fix his building! But if the costs are similar, I'd go with the booth: at least you'll bu building something that you own, for your own benefit! That would be my approach: others might not agree... :) - Stuart -
