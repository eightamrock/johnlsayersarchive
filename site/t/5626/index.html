bassman11 February 2006#1
I am in the process of getting my building permit together to begin construction on my studio which will be housed in an attached block garage on the rear of an older apartment/storefront building. WHAT I WANT: I want to be able to have two sessions going on at once. The main control room is for my use and the second control room I want to use to record bands and some commercial work at the same time with a second engineer. This second control room would also double as a large booth for bigger recording sessions. It is not optimal in size as designed now but this is for budget oriented recording, not the full monty. The idea is to build business through traffic. I have been doing this too long to be up recording punk bands at 3am for no money but I know many kids who do want to do this but don't have any facilities that sound decent or have access. I will hopefully pick up more produciton and mixing work as a result. Plus, commercial gigs that come in can be handled easily since even if the main room is booked, the second can help make scheduling versatile. WHAT I GOT: Well, I have around 800 sq ft in an odd shape to fit this in. The designs do not show the lounge/lavatory and office which will be in a small basement area accessed from the entry. Otherwise, its straight ahead. WHAT I AM PLANNING ON BUILDING: The floor is the existing concrete slab with a leveling finish coat of conrete that will be stained for the final floor covering. I would have liked to cut the slab between each room and the outer shell but due to the proximity to the Ohio River and potential hydristatic water pressure underneath the slab causing water to come up through the cuts. Also, the footers for the structure are unknown and would most likely extend inward and underneath the inner walls making the cuts pretty much useless. Basically, I do not want to cut the slab, build the rooms and have made a $30,000 mistake when the slab cracks or some huge storm causes a bunch of water to come up through the cuts. Sealing those cuts with something that could prevent this might also negate the benefits of cutting the slab in the first place. I'm torn by this because I want rock drums at 3am to not bother the tenant in the apartment next to the studio. I might try drilling a test hole and see what happens the next big rain. Each wall will be 5/8" gyp - green glue - 5/8" gyp on 2x4 studs 16" on center. Same for the ceilings. All inner framing will be isolated from the structure minus the floor. That is where I'm hoping to get the most TL from. As this is an unfinished space, heating and electrical are basically from scratch so I have the chance to optimize these. I plan to make two plenums <sp?>, one for return and one for supply that isolate the rooms. Each feed will be through flexduct that winds around a bit. I plan on making the hole for each vent in the ceiling and framing a silencer from there to the floor a la Paul Woodclock design. So, whaddya think? Here come the piccies: -bassman

knightfly14 February 2006#2
Detail where the door/wall of the entry meets the live room - that "tee" junction to the CR wall doesn't look like it'll maintain 2-leaf construction all around, you may need to change that part a bit. Also, 16" centers for sound construction are a waste of time and money, you actually get better iso using 24" centers. Only exception to that would be for a ceiling, where you don't have enough headroom for the next size UP in joists, and can't span the space using 24" centers... Steve
