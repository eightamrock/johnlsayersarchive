Introduction: I'm a freelance sound designer for film and theatre and also sound recordist. I work on location (theatre) and in other studios as well, but also work in my own small studio. Floor: wood with an unknown amount of unknown isolation Windows: 4 single pane glass windows, one window is sealed. Entrance through a (thin) wooden door. Peculiarities: - Build-in-closet on the left - The adjacent room holds my appartements heater and the exhaust pipe goes through the wall into the studio room and out again into another wall (see picture) Treatment: - two corners (on opposite sides) have two absorbers (wooden frame, 3 layers of glasswool held together with cotton fabric) see picture 5 - Windows are spaced in the outer wall, i have build absorbers that are "flush mounted" in the casing (absorbers:wooden frame, 2 layers of glasswool, 1 layer of floor isolation, held together with cotton fabric). they do a good job keeping out external noises. picture 6. -The window on the right doesn't open, so i've put an extra window pane in a mounted frame to make a sort of "double glass" construction. Cold15 November 2009#10
The design posted destroys your symmetry with the vocal booth. Having your equipment set up in a corner is not optimal for room modes. Do you want more isolation? How loud is rainfall on your roof? Consider building your booth at the door with a second door into your control room? See the line I added, put a door in this line, then rotate your work area to the right. Don't have time right now to talk about isolation and treatment, but this would let you use the closet for bass hangers, and give you symmetry in your control room with treatment. I would try to build room in room but I don't know if this is realistic for you.
