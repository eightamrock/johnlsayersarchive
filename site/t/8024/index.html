ItSeffinga22 January 2007#1
Hi, everybody. I'm new to the forum. I was referred here by somebody from Studio-Central. I'm in an awesome situation where I'm going to be building a small studio from scratch. The duality is, it's going to be on an additional floor in the preexisting house of....my in-laws. :? The father is a stubborn man, and he won't permit an oddly-shaped room. To him, soundproofing is all smoke and mirrors. Imagine trying to explain acoustics. Yeah, so I'm stuck. My goal is to achieve a studio configuration with plainly-shaped rooms that would nonetheless be attractive to a knowledgeable engineer. Small, but optimally functional. This studio is going to be getting a lot of night-time use. My wife will be sleeping directly below the studio, but luckily the floor below is new construction too, so I should be able to double up on the lower ceiling's drywall and even possibly use some resilient channels on the joists. Either way, she's agreed to be compliant and increase fan noise in our room in proportion to any unfortunate transmission. Domestic issues aside, I live in a sparsely populated area, and a decent amount of noise isn't going to bother neighbors, but I do have to keep the noise of the train, about 300 yards away on the opposite side of the house, out! I plan on having a drum kit in the studio room, and that is going to decide my max volume. I don't have a spl meter or a drum set, so I can't tell you what the level will be, but when I get one I'll update this post. The double walls are going to be about 6" thick, slightly more depending on whether I add another layer of gypsum board. The studs will be staggered 2"x4"s. The floors will be regular plywood with berber carpeting. The height of the studio and control room are going to be 8'. The two windows in the control room are 2'x4'. The studio window is probably going to be 5'x5' with two parallel glass panes of different thickness, seperated by a 5-6" air space. My desk is 3' deep by 6'6" wide, with my KRK RP6s on either end, the cone centers about 4'4" apart. I'll have a full, 88-key midi controller to the right of the desk. The other dimensions are in the studio sketch. My computers (and eventually a sampler and other hard drive devices) will be out in the "equipment room" on the lower right, connected by wall plates (still some research to do there, but I know I don't want to run snakes). My budget is going to be $2,000-$4,000 on top of initial basic construction, with $4,000 being an extreme stretch. This budget is going to include things like custom wall plates and a few extra cables. Definitely still in the planning stages, but time to figure this stuff out is coming to a close. We have to bring the plans to the town council within a couple weeks to make this month's deadline. The amount of space I have is about all I've been able to squeeze out of this project. So... 1) I'm wondering if anybody sees any inherent problems with this setup that I don't. 2) I'd like this room to be as adaptable as possible on the off chance that another musician eventually considers buying this house when/if we sell it. Any suggestions, simple or otherwise, that I could conveniently take care of during initial construction? *this is actually the second configuration of this space that I've tried. I'm not averse to reconfiguring it, so suggestions in this vein are absolutely welcome. :wink:

Much less about acoustics. You should have seen his bewilderment. Him: "Double wall!?" Me: "Yep, with two or three layers of gyp board on either side" Him: "Two!!?" Me: "Yeah, but it doesn't have to be an inch, it can just be 5/8" gypsum." Him: "5/8"!!!!!!!!!?" My wife: "You're not just gonna believe what some people on the 'internet' tell you, are you?" You're right, love. What are the laws of physics anyway? Pagh! Besides, they're digging their own grave.
