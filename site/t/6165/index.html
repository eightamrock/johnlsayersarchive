AVare5 May 2006#2
1) What's the ideal dimensions and angles for a CR? Can you post a drawing of one?
There is no ideal control room. Read the 5.1 doc and its references in the stickies for current recommendations.
2) How important is symmetry to the room design and what do you do to compensate for anomalies in the room shape or surfaces (i.e. window to the tracking room being on one side and not the other)?
Very important. You try to match acoustically anomalies like windows in real life. Or you can build romm within a room with symmetry... You seem to be asking ideal also, so ideal you avoid rooms with major asymmetry.
3) Without specific space requirements (except space for a client or two), what would be the smallest CR you would construct?
Very vague. How loud? How big a mixing surface? That's just the start of questions. See the 5.1 docs in the stickies above. The AES/EBU/CCITT/NARAS P&R Wing/Tonmeister SSR recommendation is 3500 ft^3 as base volume.
4) Is any space treatable/workable?
Yes. All it takes is money.

I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m.
Above from John on: http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html AND the bigger the CR, the better. I'm sure others would have a different idea of how big CR should be or will even state there is no proven size of how big is big enough. In the end it's how much room you can afford. CR's of the size that most build at home, speakers and engineer face the long wall in room not the short wall. There is some point in size where facing the short wall is OK, but I don't know exactly how big the room should be for that. If you haven't already: The above link (also availble from top right link of this page "John Sayers Productions") will take you to the SAE site. There is a lot of info there worth studying, especially the Studio Plans. Also it's worth while to take an amount of time and look at all the designs John has posted here in the forum. For the most part things stay symetrical, but the rear of the room seems to be a place where a little liberty can be taken as long as some angle's of side walls are maintained. Above info should help lead you to figure out "ideal dimensions for non-rectangular shaped rooms (like symmetrical trapezoidal shaped rooms.)" Note, I'm not an expert, my comments are based on viewing countless posts and designs on this forum. Marc
