thanks, this has been a killer resource for me. Ok so i stayed up late last night and cut out these dimensions. my room is 7.4 high so i made the first frame for the slat wall 41 or so inches high pretty much half of the height of the room minus the base boards that are at the floor. I cut 13 maple boards with 10 degree angles on the edges at 22.5 long and 3 inches high. my side boards for depth are as follows; left: 4.5 inches right: 6.0 inches i then cut my top pieces for the frame at angles between these dimensions so that the slats had a recess of 1/4 inch or so on the face for good looks. here is the data I got by using the slot calc after assembling the unit. wall depth at angle 6 5 4.5 inches --------------------- slats numbered from top to bottom gap in millimeters coresponding to slot calc at above depths and corresponding Hz results from calculator 1. 4 4 4mm ------------------------ 221hz 231hz 241hz 2. 8 8 8mm ------------------------- 291hz 318hz 333hz 3. 9 9 9mm -------------------------- 307 336 351hz 4. 8 8 8mm --------------------------- 291 318 333hz 5. 7 7 7mm ---------------------------- 274 300 313hz 6. 15 15 15mm ---------------------------- 383 420 437hz 7. 4 4 4mm ----------------------------- 211 231 241hz 8. 7 7 7mm ----------------------------- 274 300 313hz 9. 17 17 17mm ----------------------------- 403 442 461hz 10. 8 8 8mm ----------------------------- 291 319 333hz 11. 13 13 13mm ------------------------------ 361 395 412hz 12. 5 5 5mm ------------------------------ 235 257 268hz 13. 9 9 9mm ------------------------------ 307 336 351hz :D so you can see that an angled slat design varies depth across the width and thus giving you a range of obsorbtion in the frequencies. i went ahead and guessed at the gaps bettwen trying to stick with 4-20mm gaps. I will post pics of the unit soon. I hope this data helps any of you!! My next slat design will use 2 inch slats Vs. three and have a depth of 4 inches. i am trying to address 125hz to 275hz in the next design. this design was 211-461hz as you can see it is much like a rubiks cube, you change one thing in the slat calc and then it changes everything else so factor in your wood depths and the space you need in your room. my room seems to be getting smaller but who needs a 10x10 room that sounds like ass. I found this design to be easy and fun as well as very astheticaly pleasing and practicle. thanks so much guys for making this easy to find data and resouces. :D :D
