Soundman20206 December 2008#2
I was just looking at your layout, and noticed a couple of problems. First, the door into your control room (CR) is right in the corner, exactly where you need your bass trapping. Second, you seem to be wasting floor space where the doors open at the rear, so you cannot get the couch against the rear wall. Third, you can't see into the vocal booth. So I took the liberty of re-arranging your rooms, basically swapping the control room and the vocal booth, as below. Now you can get the couch against the rear wall, and you have space for bass trapping in the rear corners. You also have sight lines to to the vocal booth through the window (if you want it). I think it makes better use of space. Regarding your ceiling construction: It looks like you might have three leaves there: The roof, the existing ceiling, and the new ceiling. If so, you should think about fixing that. - Stuart -

Soundman20204 January 2009#7
AMallion wrote:
soundman2020 ur layout was like my first idea but i changed it because i didnt think having a door in the corner of the live room was the best way of getting the room to sound right,
I don't think it matters much in the live room. It would be a bad idea in the control room, of course, but it can be incorporated into your live room acoustically, I think, without too much of a problem. It is far more important to solve the issues with your control room.
about the roof my idea of building another ceiling up was to try and get more soundproof`t better if you understand me in stead of just having 100mm in the roof you get an extra 100mm and a air gap just like a wall.
That's a common misconception, and shows that you don't understand the concept of two-leaf isolation, exactly like I didn't understand it a couple of months back!!! I understand where you are, because that's where I was. So I'll save you some effort, and tell you what you need to do, and what I did: You need to research all over this forum (use the "search" button), and grasp the concepts of "2-leaf" and "3-leaf" and "MAM" (or "MSM"). But the sad truth is that adding a third leaf to your ceiling will make the isolation WORSE, not better, at some frequencies. It's not intuitive, and doesn't make logical sense until you understand how sound works and how resonant cavities react to sound. So you need to learn the concept, and re-design your ceiling to make it two-leaf, not three-leaf. - Stuart -
