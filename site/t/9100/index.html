gullfo11 July 2007#5
its an interesting program. I use for that purpose - where someone wants to just position their speakers and furniture without putting up treatments. and it works OK for that purpose. it takes some time to learn the CAD part and making sure you have the room construction captured correctly and the speaker specifications are also correct. like any simulation, its not 100% so you'll still want to measure and adjust but it can be a decent starting point unless the room acoustics are really bad or placement options are very limited - in which case you're going to have to treat the room anyways.

gullfo13 July 2007#9
CARA is a modeling tool and therefore only simulates the room and speaker characteristics and make approximations on speaker and listener positions - again - not something you'd typically use for anything other than a starting place for setting up a living room. fwiw - i have used it a few times for living/office/bed rooms and the speaker placements "seemed" better than just plopping them in the corners... ymmv) the RPG room sizer is a modeling tool and uses some different methods to model the expected charactistics of the room. I've used the demo and it seems to yield some nice info regarding the potential room character and based on that you could then plan treatments for the space... http://www.rpginc.com/products/roomsizer/index.htm another tool that is reasonably priced is the RPG room optimizer software. I haven't used it but a number of talented people endorse it. its more a measurement tool than a modeling tool. http://www.rpginc.com/products/roomoptimizer/index.htm ETF and TEF are measurement tools which (usually) means the results will be based on the actual characteristics of the space.
