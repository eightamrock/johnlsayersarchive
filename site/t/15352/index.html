gullfo8 December 2010#2
looks like a good plan - maybe a small window into the isolation booth from the CR? also, plan on using built in soffits instead of the cabinets (unless you have them already) so you can use that additional space in them for bass trapping etc. same on the back wall, plenty of bass trapping and some large angled clouds across the front and top of the CR to bring it in. in the live room - break out the corners with angled slats for broadband absorption, diffusiveness, and reducing the corner. some polys would be nice, some slat boxes would be a nice touch, on the ceiling scattered absorbers to break it up. in the isolation booth - slats across that deep corner in back and scattered absorption to finish it.

gullfo10 December 2010#4
plan on using 30 degrees for the soffit angle (creating a triangle). you could build your own monitors with the soffits effectively acting as the speaker box (in fact that's basically their intent anyways and may top studios use custom built or customized configurations instead of simply using stock speakers). for the most part using standard angles for the soffits like 30 or 45 is best because people are used to it, but sometimes you have to use other angles to accommodate the circumstances: 22.5, 36, 50 etc. are options when necessary, and even oddly defined numbers like 33.7 (example) when you really cannot make it work otherwise.

gullfo3 January 2011#15
i think the ceiling is problematic as the interior truss members are going to be hit with sound and then transferring that directly to your roof. typically you want to re-do the trusses with the cross bracing up higher so you can seal the entire ceiling. to improve the isolation, use isolation clips and hat channel instead of the RC. are you looking to extend this over the CR as well or just use a flat ceiling there? if the left hand wall (as shown in your SU file) is not an isolation wall, then that space in the corner could be used for trapping.
