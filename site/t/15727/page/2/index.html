gullfo27 June 2011#30
actually what you're showing there is 2 isolation walls (which is good) and you can go to a 25mm gap between frames - the goal is to leave about an 250mm space between the outer and inner drywall layers. you could skip the 12mm drywall and deadsheet and just go with 2x 16mm drywall (or with the deadsheet as well). you're treatment walls are then built inside the inner isolation wall - angled slats, absorbers, clouds, etc etc.

A friend of ours has recntly moved to rockingham and hooked up with a new fella whom is a brikies labourer. We had em over for a coupla drinks last friday and i showed the brikie mate the shed-studio plans and the basic MSM (mass-spring-mass) theory behind recording studios built with double wall, room within a room systems. We were talking building a brick wall as an option for the isolation wall. So brick work is probably one of the best isolation walls you can build because it is heavy. I have seen MSM designs with concrete blocks or bessa bricks filled with sand and sealed with an oil based paint, then an air cavity filled with Rockwool insulation or similar, and then still adding an interior wall with 2 X layers of the 16mm plasterboard (or anything rigid and heavy) to the wall and fill the stud cavities with insulation. check this forum post (and attached image): viewtopic.php?f=2&t=16533&p=116245&hilit=brick#p116245

Soundman202022 August 2011#34
Brick is certainly a great alternative. Much greater mass and density. If you can do that, then great!
I have seen MSM designs with concrete blocks or bessa bricks filled with sand and sealed with an oil based paint, then an air cavity filled with Rockwool insulation or similar, and then still adding an interior wall with 2 X layers of the 16mm plasterboard (or anything rigid and heavy) to the wall and fill the stud cavities with insulation.
Exactly. That is the way to do it.
We must try to replicate the design/theory (MSM) for the ceilings? (dont know if this is possible with bricks?)-
The basic principle works the same with pretty much any construction material. It's the amount of mass and depth of the air gap that matter, not the material (within reason!). - Stuart -
