Cold18 June 2011#21
Hi, I did not read every word in your thread so please forgive me if this has been dealt with. Couple of quick thoughts regarding treating the metal walls. Be very careful with ventilation as steel buildings love to create condensation, especially on the inside of the roof. If you are considering spraying insulation on the inside to dampen the structure, be very careful of the dew point. I have seen many spray insulation jobs fail when the material is applied to an uninsulated metal roof. The metal skin tends to act as a vapour barrier. Perhaps consider shotcrete as a way to add mass to your walls, this is the stuff they use for swimming pools. good luck with your project. Steve

xSpace21 June 2011#23
Most likely, no. Shotcrete is a process to spray concrete, shot---crete. So the same thing that applies to rebar having to be matted to develop the perimeters that the room might be, the ability of the room to at least support the rebar matte, are true considerations. Certainly a pool, gunite which is mortar and not specifically a high PSI concrete, Is installed with the same process sprayed on. The difference, and this is a big one, is that the "form" is the earth. So the weight is placed in this cavity that will be a pool and by design of specific degrees tilting the sides out wire mesh will work. Not so with an interior that is basically a concrete structure, knowing that concrete can lay on a flat surface for as long as you can support it, once you elevate this product, it has to have rebar.
