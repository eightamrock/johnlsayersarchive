mpedrummer wrote:
A two-leaf system is superior to a three-leaf system with the same overall thickness.
Actually, this isn't quite true either. A 2-leaf system, with less mass, can outperform a 3-leaf system. There is a drawing in Rod's book very similar to the one attached below. Look at it carefully. The 3rd from the left is a double wall, built conventionally (drywall on both sides) and it gives 40dB of iso. Remove 2 of of the sheets of drywall, and with less mass you get 57dB of isolation, a HUGE 17dB increase. This compares a 4-leaf to a 2-leaf, but even a 3-leaf (STC50) has 7db less iso, with more mass.

xSpace24 February 2009#15
That is a good answer in itself. If two leaf is getting the props and three leaf is not, two leaf it is ;) To me, a three leaf never made sense in respect to sound isolation, although I have seen it used by well educated and highly regarded firms, although not specifically for music related rooms. Either it has a use, and I am too lazy to be bothered with the frequencies to worry with, or it was a mistake. The latter of the two not likely. The simple thing, for me, is that mass is what is required to stop sound from penetration. Since I already know that a mass with air and another mass runs the risk of sound leakage between the two mass areas, it's a simple notion to not introduce another air space, which is another potential area for sound to live in. There are papers with mathematical data that show lose of isolation at the lower and higher(if I recall) ranges of frequency from using a three leaf wall panel. Again, for music related isolation, you do not want any sound to get through, which is why we build things like we do, so this is another strike against the three leaf. I agree, a three leaf has gotten a bad rap, with not much supporting documentation that the everyday DIYer can understand, but the rap is well deserved.

Yes, this is not what you would want to do. Pretty simple process. Think "heavy" think "thin" think "heavy" again. All the combination's will work and work well.
