ToneAlive17 June 2012#3
For only a $1000 budget, I would just plan on spending that all on acoustic treatments. I'm not sure how much "design" you could actually do in terms of building walls or sound proofing anything and still have money to treat the room so that you have a good sounding tracking and mixing room. Just take care of the early reflections for the speakers, build some broadband panels, Treat the back wall so you don't get a whole bunch of sound coming back at you at your mix position. Take a look at the DIY panel thread in the acoustics forum. Oh, I would set up your listening position so that the speakers fire lengthwise into the room, towards the bedroom. You could prevent flutter echo by throwing some plywood or mdf on the wall at a slight angle to avoid parallel surfaces. Or you could treat alternatng surface areas with DIY panels. Acutally, for sound proofing, I would look around the building the see if you see any places where there are breaches in the structure to the outside. Obviously if you see light coming in somewhere.. And seal it up with some caulk.

isolation - consider window plugs when you need them. also even musicians need oxygen to make sure you have a reliable air exchange setup - a couple of bathroom type fans to pull and push air with a simple baffle box arrangement may be enough. treatment wise - use scattered porous absorbers - 4" thick rigid insulation and cover with cloth. a bunch of 2'x2' absorbers scattered all over the room will help even out the room and keep it lively.
