Enough for an isolation booth?

Started by shaaregeulah on 27 July 2006. 1 reply.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6713.

shaaregeulah27 July 2006#1
I just moved and the new studio room is in a garage. Cars and buses pass by the front of the house and can be picked up by mic's. I'm on a VERY tight budget and can't make any major improvements. I've decided that I'll have to sacrifice on sound and build a small "dead" isolation booth for vocals and acoustic guitars rather than risk picking up outside noise. It'll be around 6.5' H x 5' W x 3.5' D. Being that I'm on a tight budget, I'd like to make it as cheap as possible and build the walls with cheap wood(maybe pine?.) Double layer walls with regular wall insulation in between, covering the outside with low grade commercial carpeting and the inside with some acoustic material such as foam. It doesn't need to be completely sound proof, just enough to block out the cars and buses that occasionally pass by. I really don't know much about sound isolation or construction so I'd really appreciate your advice. 1)Would this suffice and stop outside noise from being picked up? (would pine be good enough?) 2)Would this be the cheapest method? 3)Is a floating floor recommended as well?
giles11728 July 2006#2
1st that is claustrophobic, but if you pursue this, rockwool is not that much more expensive than Standard Pink Insul. In some cases it's cheaper. Pine is fine for the studs, but pine for the actual walls will cost you more than sheets of drywall and require a lot more work to manipulate. Can someone say Utility Knife??? For your iso booth, if it were me, I'd float it. The cost of the neoprene I cant imagine running more than 20-30 Bucks very high side for the small amount you'd need to float this portabooth.
