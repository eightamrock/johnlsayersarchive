Due to width of your room, perhaps a set of John's Side Wall Absorbers on either side of your mix position http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm and perhaps some absorption on the control room front wall & vocal booth door (control room side) Setting up the Mix Position Here are a few bits of information I’ve collected along the way… • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall to begin with, adjusting as necessary to avoid peaks & nulls. • Equilateral triangle formed by left, right mix position • Monitor angles 60 degrees (30 degrees each speaker) • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls 6-12 degrees
