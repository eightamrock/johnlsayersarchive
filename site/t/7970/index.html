see in posts to put "insulation" in between the studs. Does this mean the 703 rigid or the fluffy pink or yellow home insulation you buy at Home Depot?
It means unfaced fluffy pink or yellow stuff, if you have the budget you could use some rockwoll too. Bats in the cavities are mainly use to damp panel resonances.
-If it is the standard insulation, can I staple it up with the paper side facing the drywall? What "R" rating should I get?
No paper, if you can't find any insulation without paper then yes , paper toward drywall. R13 or at least 3" thick.
--Do to certain structural issues I can not decouple the ceiling from the walls.
You're seriously compromising your double partiton wall then, remember that your construction will only be as good as your weakest point. you won't get better result at putting 5 layers of drywall with green glue on your walls if you have serious flanking or structural path.
--So my next question, will Green Glue increase isolation significantly if used?
In short yes. see previous questions. I'd suggest a re-read of page 62 of Rod's book from paragraph 3, since you have the book i am sure you can find a way to decouple your walls from your ceiling. have faith :wink:

I was going on what you wrote:
egg wrote:
What STC rating can I hope for with dual 5/8? Even if I have a double wall shouldn't I use resilient channel in the live room on every wall and the ceiling? I have one wall that is coupled in each room and will have some flanking going on
I guess we need to get on the same page as far was terminology goes. "Double wall" usually means two decoupled frames, each with its own "leaf." The leaf is often stucco, plywood, OSB, MDF, or any combination of these or any other dense, air-tight products. You can (and usually must) have multiple "layers" of products within a leaf. When you wrote "double wall," I assumed you meant two frames. Now I'm thinking you meant two LAYERS of drywall. Study some more here about various wall constructions, seeing how different formulas result in different performances. Follow the link to the IRC report in the "Reference Area" sticky thread. Understand too that STC is a practically worthless measurement in a studio context. --Keith :mrgreen:
