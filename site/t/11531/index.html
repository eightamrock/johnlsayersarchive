Soundman20205 January 2009#2
Hi Mokamal, and welcome.
I need to isolate the outside noise so that our mics don't pick up the street noise anymore, this is my biggest priority, but after that I need to also do some acoustic treatment because as is the room sounds strange, not sure what you guys call it exactly but it has some sort of echo.
Do you have a sound level meter? If not, then get one. You need to measure the real levels, in a few places outside your room, on a very noisy day. Use the "C" scale, and "slow" response on the meter. Measure at several times, in several places outside the room, all four sides, even up on the roof if you can, to get the best idea of how loud it really is, with real numbers. Then do the same INSIDE the room, with all the doors and windows closed, to get an idea of how much isolation you already have with the existing structure. From what you say, it seems that sound getting out of your room and disturbing your neighbors will not really be a problem! OK, since street noise is your big problem, in addition to getting plain dB SPL readings, I'd suggest that you need to also figure out what part of the spectrum you have the worst problems with. My guess is that it will be the low-frequency general rumble from traffic, plus engine noise from heavy vehicles (trucks, buses, etc.). You need a spectrum analyzer or acoustic measurement software like ETF to measure that accurately. Once you have those numbers / graphs, then you can figure out how much isolation you need, and then figure out what kind of construction you will have to do to obtain it. What is the floor made of? And what about the roof? - Stuart -

but because of time issues and because doing things by the book would mean that I would have to get some outside help,
I'm not sure I understand what you mean by that. In order to over-build it, you will need even MORE outside help than if you were to do it right, "by the book". Especially if you are on a deadline.
I would rather just do overkill isolation where I would be safe and go far over what is needed than to carefully measure things and do it precisely.
Even if you do over-build to the extreme, you sill STILL need to "carefully measure things and do it precisely.". You can't build a studio and expect decent results with lousy workmanship, no matter how much "overkill" you build into it. But anyway, if you really do want overkill, considering what you told us already (very noisy very busy street right outside), then I'd start by ripping out that floor, all the way down to bare earth, and pouring a brand new, totally decoupled 10 inch high density reinforced concrete slab, and build 10-inch concrete walls on top of that, decoupled from the outside walls, then pour a 10-inch concrete ceiling supported on very large steel I-beams and steel plate, with a 10-inch air gap all around (walls and ceiling) filled with rock wool. No windows. For the door, use two of Rod's superdoors back-to-back, with very thick sheet lead in the core, and five seals each, instead of three, with an airlock in between. That's about the only type of overkill construction I can think of that is guaranteed to give you really excellent isolation from practically every conceivable noise, except maybe a ton of TNT exploding next door.
a double leaf brick wall would be the best way to go, do you agree?
No. Yes. Maybe. It all depends on what you need, and how well you build it. can you guarantee that the builders will not spill any mortar in the gap between the inner leaf and outer leaf? That they will work perfectly, and leave the air gap absolutely spotlessly clean, with no bridging that would cause flanking?
