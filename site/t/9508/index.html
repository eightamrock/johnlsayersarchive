lovecow4 October 2007#2
Kirk, IAC makes quality products (arguably some of the best in their field) that are typically implemented in commercial applications. The major concern you should have is that of weight. Most IAC products are composed of copious amounts of steel. In the case of the studio enclosures they manufacture, they often use ~4" thick steel panels. That's solid steel. The weight of typical IAC panels is somewhere in the neighborhood of 10-15 lb/ft². For a 12'x14' booth with a "standard" IAC height of about 7', the entire IAC room could weigh as much as 4-5 tons. (For reference on this, a typical sedan - like a Toyota Corolla - usually weighs on the order of 1-1.5 tons. Putting the IAC booth in your living room would be roughly equivalent to parking 3-5 Corollas in your living room. :shock: ) IMO, you could only pursue this sort of thing if your living room is built on a concrete slab. Even then, you should consult with a structural engineering expert to ensure the slab is thick enough and reinforced enough to carry 4-5 tons of dead weight. I'm pretty sure typical residential wood-joist floors would be able to accommodate the IAC booth. Rod G. is an expert on this, so he'll hopefully chime in, as appropriate. If you do have a slab floor with the right properties, then...good luck moving that thing in!!! :shock: (By my estimates, a single 2'x7' panel could weigh 150-200 lbs.) Of course, the buy & store option could work too, especially considering the bargain price. I would advise you make sure you have detailed paperwork from the seller regarding assembly, though! ;)

lovecow4 October 2007#4
In general, you're going to spend about as much in time and materials to buiild your own booth as you would on one of the "prefab" varieties. However, the resulting isolation can sometimes turn out better if you DIY, depending on your attention to detail. :) For DIY, I would suggest Rod's book. For fridge buzz, you might try balancing it. I stuck a wood shim under the left front wheel on my fridge and all the buzzing stopped. (Leveled an unbalanced compressor.) If it's an old fridge, though, you might be stuck with the buzz. Good luck!
