gullfo21 September 2007#2
you could try turning the CR to face the vox booth. also, the number of connections per booth will depend on your styles of recording. generally, the drum booth should be equiped to handle close micing of the drums so maybe 12-16 low-z connections. each vox booth, 4-6 lo-z should be plenty, and live room, another 12-16 lo-z connections. plus returns in each - about 4 headphone returns per booth, and 8 returns in the live room. in the live room, drum booth i'd have 4 midi each. and 4 line inputs per booth plus 12 lines in the live room. so maybe 32-40 lo-z, 20 headphone, 24 line, and 8 midi is probably enough for most scenarios with a mixture of live and eletronic instruments. much depends on your budget. if nothing else, leave some space in the inter-room conduit to pull additional lines. I'd have separate conduit for lo-z, line, and midi. also consider some tv (cable), telephone/network lines - again separate conduit for those.

gullfo22 September 2007#4
the wall design looks good. if you can afford 10cm gap then it should help. you could also consider flipping the walls and making them "inside-out" to get some treatment benefit and the isolation. check John's SAE site for details on walls. the doors are sliders but could be double doors - either way you want solid exterior grade double glazed with tight seals as a minimum. the double doors might be nice in case you want to open the booth and/or have large drums to load in/out. i also pictured a raised platform in the drum booth. if you can use concrete to create the drum riser floor, it will keep the resonance frequency lower and keep the bass drum tight. use mineral wool or 703 insulation under the riser floor to "float" it (or buy proper isolation jacks). keep in mind the ceiling can be a major place of sound transmission so ensure the ceiling has enough mass to isolate. add more over the drum booth but leave the drum booth ceiling higher to add absorption and some diffusing bits.
