gullfo3 December 2010#8
you need mass to isolate. hard contact means flanking. having a set of 110db drums below the bay's room means 75db or so in the baby's room... adding the proper amount of mass to the floor and constructing a proper decoupled ceiling means 25db in the baby's room unless there are major flanking issues via the floor (which can be solved significantly by using isolation platforms under the drums and amps). :idea: for the beam - you might have to sister up some wood beams (supported on the isolation wall) to cross that space (in parallel to the steel beam) in order to support the ceiling and still have it decoupled. also, unless you cannot spare it, do not put your walls on the same plates. use fully separate walls with a 1" gap. yes, you'll likely loose some space but the isolation will go way up.

gullfo7 December 2010#25
http://www.kineticsnoise.com/arch/ceilings.aspx - using the isolation clips on the joists or adding the spring units and suspend the ceiling. both will only take about 2" away with the spring unit being more expensive but more decoupled - maybe use the clips on the CR and the springs on the live room. for the beam, create some parallel trusses to support the necessary drywall and keep things decoupled. same for the duct work. use soffits on the ceiling as needed for your ventilation unless you can embed the ventilation into the ceiling itself. contemplate the need for HVAC baffle boxes for at least the air exchange and consider split unit heat pumps for cooling/heating.
