gullfo19 January 2011#111
any hard contact will couple the walls. even very small amounts, make sure the duct in only in contact with one wall or the other except when you need to span to the opposing opening and keep that connection very flexible. also, you still need some solid dividing panels in the baffle to actually force the sound to flow the way you want otherwise sound can simply transfer from one opening to the other because the flex duct is not very good at isolation by itself.

Soundman202026 July 2011#120
To be honest, I wouldn't put the silencer there at all! You'll be pretty much destroying the bass-trapping ability of your superchunk like that. You need the full width and depth of the triangle to maximize the bass trapping, so if you embed your silencer in it, either front or back, you severely diminish the effectiveness of the trap, making it pretty useless. One thing you could consider is putting the silencer on the other side of the wall: in other words, outside of the room, not inside it. Another possibility (if the wall is thick enough) is to consider putting it inside the wall cavity. Or maybe above the ceiling. There might also be other places where you could put it inside the room, if you have no choice, but places that won't interfere with the acoustic treatment. If you could do a complete, accurate scale diagram of your final room design in SketchUp, and/or post photos of what you have in there, then we might be able to help you figure out the best way of doing this. Another major point (I think Glenn already mentioned it) is to make sure that your ducting is isolated / decouple where it passes through the MSM wall. In other words, the ducting must not create any flanking paths that mechanically join the two leaves: that would compromise the isolation. - Stuart -

Soundman202026 July 2011#122
He probably just meant the ducting and not the silencer . . .
I'm sure he did! A duct in the corner is not going to affect the bass trapping much, but replacing most of the superchunk with a plywood box is certainly going to be an issue!
I am using my rockwool, covered in plastic, to line the silencers in lieu of duct liner (I believe this is okay, right?).
Should work OK, if you can't get proper duct liner.
Not sure how the wife would like a couple of 1'x1'x3.5' boxes stacked up in there.
Maybe you could make them thinner and longer? For example, only 6" deep but 7' tall? That way you, might be able to fit them into the decor a bit better: perhaps make them look like floor-to-ceiling wall cabinets or something (stick fake knobs or handles on the front, so they look like cupboards! :) ). The combination of dimensions isn't so important: as long as you keep enough cross sectional area for slow air flow, and multiple turns, then you can adapt the dimensions to fit your needs.
As for decoupling the vent - do you have some specific suggestions on material? Neoprene, or the like? Something else?
I think Glenn already suggested sealing around the ducts (where they pass through the wall) with flexible caulk, to make sure the ducts don't touch the drywall. You might also want to cut a 1" gap in the ductwork itself, and put some kind of flexible duct seal around that, as an extra measure. Glenn can give you better advise on HVAC ductwork than I can: it's not really my area of expertise. But the above is what I think he recommends, frequently. The basic idea is just to keep the ductwork from bridging the leaves, and from being a flanking path. - Stuart -
