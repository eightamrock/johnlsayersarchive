Soundman202027 July 2011#141
I'm not so sure that painting or sealing it would be a good idea! Insulation absorbs sound by the fibers moving, vibrating in sympathy with the sound, and thus converting the kinetic energy of the sound waves into heat energy. If you paint/seal the fibers then they become far more rigid, stick together in clumps, and lose their ability to move freely. Thus, they lose their ability to absorb sound. - Stuart -

Soundman202027 July 2011#143
A plastic cover on the fibers only inhibits movement a little bit, at the surface (unless you wrap it tight, which is not the idea!). Using any kind of liquid, such as paint or sealant, is very different because the fibers themselves "wick" the liquid much deeper into the insulation, even if you try to only put it on the surface. That's part of why wrapping bass traps in plastic is normally mentioned here as a way to treat it so that it "reflects highs" without affecting its abilities to deal with lows: it only affects the tips of the fibers, the surface layer. The fibers a bit deeper into the insulation can still move and vibrate as always, and still absorb low-frequency energy. But paint or sealant will penetrate much deeper into the insulation, being "sucked in" by the fibers themselves, especially if you spray it on. That will affect a much larger percentage of each fiber, and will also cause several fibers to physically stick together, which plastic wrapping does not do. By painting or sealing, you are changing the physical structure of the insulation itself at the microscopic level, but that's the exact level where the acoustic effects occur! Painting or sealing won't affect the thermal properties anywhere near as much, because thermal insulation depends on different physical effects than acoustic absorption: paint it and it will still keep the room warm, but it won't absorb sound in the sound way. The acoustic properties of insulation depends, to a large extent, on a property knows as gas flow resistance. Painting the fibers changes the gas flow resistance a lot. - Stuart -
