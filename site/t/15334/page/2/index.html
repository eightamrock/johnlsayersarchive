gullfo7 December 2010#27
the clips with the hat channel / furring strips (should be readily available in most building suppliers) will be better than the RC-2. use 2x 5/8" drywall on the clips/hat channel. remember that to really be effective, the upper floor should be beefed up with 2x or 3x drywall as well. use pink insulation in the cavity. the ventilation really needs to be worked out as you are build sealed rooms and the heat and CO2 will quickly become a problem. most times people want their studio HVAC separate from the home system because the cycles will vary and you may need cooling in the winter in your studio while the rest of the house needs heat. there's a couple of options: a) install a split system which provides a blower unit to each room and set up an air exchange system (perhaps with an EVR to improve efficiency and balance the air temperature/moisture). b) use something like a small hidden duct system to add another centralized unit which then uses ducts to each room for supply and return. the air exchange would then be part of the blower unit. in either case you want to minimize the amount of noise the system makes and minimize the amount of sound transferred between rooms or outside of the studio so baffle boxes and so on become part of the equation. when in doubt, talk to an HVAC professional about what you're going to need.

beef up inside the upper existing floor as much as possible and use a single layer? or maybe ask the building inspector if you can be off by 1/4"? keep the 2x on the walls. just make sure that you are building only double leaf and not triple leafs walls. your existing concrete/block walls count as one of your leafs so you're only build a single inner isolation wall there. on the new walls separating the two rooms - back-to-back walls with mass on the side facing the rooms. separated frames by at least 1" (giving you a total of 8" of air space). use pink insulation for all of the isolation cavities. on the treatments - my preference - live. add the angles, slats, polys, absorbers, panel traps, hangers, diffusers, etc to get the "reverberation" down to the proper levels across the frequency spectrum without making it dead. symmetry in the CR, asymmetry in the tracking room.

gullfo10 December 2010#50
i usually recommend a minimum 3/8" (10mm) because anything less is simply less isolation. ultimately you want to get to the point where your basement floor becomes the weakest TL point (flanking). if you're only going with 2x drywall layers, then you might get away with the 5/16" but it will likely be a weak point in your TL. the angles you can achieve on a normal wall is really only effective for optical reflections and unless you have the ability to make the wall/window deep enough to create a steeper angle than you would typically have, it won't really do much. the resonance structure set up by angling them is minimal but the TL is slightly reduced because of the air gap reduction. however, many studios have angled glass and if you want to do it, then just do it - but consider using heavier glass. and there are many factors in making a studio work well and some minimal changes in TL or resonances may be subjectively countered by having better visibility and communications between the CR and live room or booths. i am in agreement with John on the slider doors (angled or parallel if necessary), even though the normal sliders have less isolation, they're very handy for home builders and a pair of exterior grade slider doors yield (in most cases) a sufficient level of isolation for home studio use. for pro studios, there are commercial sliders which make the grade for excellent isolation and visibility but are $$$.
