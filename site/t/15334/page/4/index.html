gullfo21 December 2010#76
slot resonators can add a good amount of broadband absorption and preserve a lot of HF energy while being diffusive. they also take more work that just building up porous absorbers hung on the walls. so if you're willing make the investment and to put in the work, and like the aesthetics, then slat resonators are quite nice. in this case, since you're going to need a bunch of porous absorbers regardless, start there and if the room needs adjustments - adding angles and more focused frequency treatments, then consider slats.

gullfo4 January 2011#89
build the silencer boxes and put a Fantech inline fan into the return so it draws out warm air and a passive supply box, or put one in both the supply and the return if desired. (http://residential.fantech.net/resident ... duct-fans/) put the fans outside the room to keep noise down. make the silencer box oversize compared to the fan duct to reduce flow rate and thereby help keep noise levels in the room down. make sure you keep the fan accessible for maintenance (panel on the silencer box etc) and put the on/off switch where you'll remember to turn it on or off (usually by your light switch for the room is good).

gullfo5 January 2011#94
i think more like 8-10 ACH minimum. most fans make noise in a higher frequency which is readily absorbed in a large enough baffle box. besides CFM you need to consider the FPM velocity - what the doubling of size does is to decrease the air rate to nearly half so the vent noise is reduced and air turbulence in the room is lower. for quiet operation, getting down to say 100FPM would be nice although sometimes it is hard for a home studio to get that low without large ducts and large baffles and soffits. if necessary consider the larger size FanTech unit 6" (200CFM) and going with the larger ducts and bafflebox or consider 2 of the smaller fans - one per room (probably better overall).
