sharward8 March 2007#11
I wouldn't say I'm very familiar with your area, but I did have a friend whose mother lived in a strange house in the West Hills (not too far from you I think...?). The house was choc full of unpermitted construction: a six foot basement converted into a bedroom and a loft with 14" tall stairs are the most egregious examples that come to mind. :roll: When they sold the property and moved to Yosemite, the property was sold to someone who bulldozed the whole thing and built from scratch. Let's just say that I would not even consider building sans permit in that area, for one main reason... :evil: Wildfires. :evil: In no particular order:
  • If a fire were to start in my home and spread to others, my unpermitted construction would be the "guilty until proven innocent" culprit.
  • If a fire were to start somewhere else and then claim my home, the insurance company could reduce the payout or even deny it altogether, claiming that the damage would not have been as severe if it had been permitted and/or built to code.
Is that fair? Of course not... So I suppose you could always sue to get them to pay up. What? More than $7,500? Small claims court isn't an option for that -- you'd have to go to superior court. That means you'd need an attorney. Got cash? You'll need it to get the retainer going... Unless you're lucky enough to find one desperate enough to do it on a contingency, and then you'll pay dearly at the end if you prevail at all, a few years later... Sure, you can take lots of pictures as evidence, but you'd have the cards stacked against you from the start. I think your neighbor's advice ("stay under the radar, don't even ask the building department any questions") is just terrible. Have you read about my experiences with my building department? Granted, your mileage may vary... But mine was all to happy to help advise me how to come up with a design that would meet the constraints of building and planning codes in effect in my city. My advice: If you can't build it legally, then change the plans so that you can build it legally, and if that's not possible... Then don't build or move somewhere you can. You wouldn't be the first to be afraid of the big mean building department people, and you may eventually be added to the list of people who used to think that. I hope that becomes the case. --Keith :mrgreen:
