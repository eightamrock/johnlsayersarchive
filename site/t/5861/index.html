SonicClang21 March 2006#3
You definitely don't want foil on one side. The foil will reflect high frequencies. Let me put it to you this way. Even the fabric you use to cover the fiberglass needs to THIN. Do the "breath test" on it. If you can breath easily through the fabric, then it's good to use to cover the fiberglass. If you have to breath hard, it's not thin enough. Can you breath through foil?

knightfly22 March 2006#5
If you ONLY need bass trapping, the foil facing can be placed toward your space when those batts are used across corners (corner trap type) - also, if you're placing batts at first reflection points and NOT spacing them away from the wall for better low mid absorption, the foil side can be placed TOWARD the wall with no noticeable effect. Third, if you absolutely cannot FIND another source it may be possible to "peel" the panels - depending on the adhesion method of the foil, it may come off without too much effort. This should be done if you want corner full-range absorbers. HTH... Steve

bpape24 March 2006#7
They are basically the same thing. To remove the foil facing, a wide putty knife works well. I've seen some people make slits in the facing so they are only working with one knife width. On another note, you should really see if you can find unfaced material to start with just from a cost perspective. The FRK/FSK faced material is considerably more expensive. Why buy something that you're just going to tear off and throw away?
