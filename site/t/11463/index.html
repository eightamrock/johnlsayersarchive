Nappe wrote:
hehe Avare - reading that post just made me a bit more confused. So either is good - splayed or straight? Splayed you get alot of unknowns and straigth you just need some treatment?
The answer is "Yes!" :) Welcome to the club! Acoustics is anything but intuitive. Things that sound logical at first sight, actually are not. There are lots of trade-offs that you need to be aware of. A rectangular room makes it easier to calculate where your problems will probably be, which simplifies things to a certain extent but introduces specific problems of its own that will need treatment (such as flutter echo, fixed modes, etc). Splayed walls/ceiling are good tools to help mitigate certain of those problems, such as early reflections, flutter echo, etc. but it makes it much harder to calculate exact problems, since you don't have parallel surfaces. So yes, you are right: it is a case of "six of one thing, or half a dozen of another". take your pick.
Well my room is kind of small as you can see does that matter`?
Yes! Bigger is better, but ratios are also important. The smaller the room, the more important ratios become, and the more treatment you will need.
You also write that large ceiling hight is preferreble, but does the ratios of the room not have to follow each other?
Yes and no. There are many different ratios, some excellent, some just good. Different researchers have come up with different ways of calculating ratios, and have published different tables, but there is a general consensus on where the best ratios are. stick to those. One rule of thumb: More volume (space) is good. Maximize volume when you can. Make things bigger where possible. Now, here's an interesting issue for Andre or one of the other experts to answer, but the way I see it, it doesn't really matter which way you orient the ratios! Normally, the first "1" is the height, but I don't see any acoustic reason why you could not take that "1" as the width, and use the other two as height and length, for example, or length and height.... Maybe I'm wrong on that. Andre?
My reason for asking this is that i have the oppotunity of making my room pretty tall.....up to 4,5 meters but the lenth and the width i cannot change. What would be ideal?
I think you just answered your own question, without realizing it! Since your length and width are fixed in your case, and you cannot change them, just vary your ceiling height until you come up with a ratio that makes sense! Choose whichever table you feel like (some folks argue for one table over another...), plug in your length and width, and see what height pops up! There will be several ratios that work, probably, so go with the one that gives you the highest ceiling. - Stuart -

Your problem is room size. That has been identified to you. As Soundman2020 correctly wrote (good thinking Stuart):
Soundman2020 wrote:
Normally, the first "1" is the height, but I don't see any acoustic reason why you could not take that "1" as the width, and use the other two as height and length, for example, or length and height.
Assuming your new walls are 150 mm thick (90 mm studs, 2 layers of drywall with Green Glue or Noise Killer Yellow between the layers with insulation in the space), build to the ratio of 1:1.2:1.55 (2.75:3.85:4.25 m). This ratio meets the EBU recommendation, is within the Bolt ratio diagram, and is one of the ratios in Cox's second best ratios list for a 50 m^3 room. You can not get much better than that for room ratios with 2 dimensions fixed by the space! Start doing budgeting. Second door into the room, drywall, studs, Green Glue/NoiseKiller Yellow, electrical, HVAC, signal lines for starters. Andre
