Soundman202022 September 2011#4
Hi "Stefbosa", and welcome! Looking at your comments:
a hole in the frequency range between 105hz and 122hz,
According to Bob Golds, your room modes in that area are all over the place: 105.0 hz (2,1,1 Oblique) 106.9 hz (1,0,2 Tangential) 108.9 hz (0,1,2 Tangential) 111.1 hz (1,2,1 Oblique) 116.1 hz (1,1,2 Oblique) 121.1 hz (3,0,0 Axial) 121.6 hz (2,2,0 Tangential) No particular wall or axis predominates there, although the first three are very close together, and the last two coincide almost perfectly.
This room has home made bass traps made of aluminium frames filled with acoustic wools 8 inches deep in 4 corners
How wide? In other words, what are the dimensions of those frames? Do the run from the floor to the ceiling, or just part of the room height?
Would it be a possible cause of 110hz range cancellation by having to many of them?
No. On the contrary, you most likely do not have enough bass trapping for a small room like that, with uneven modal spread. I would suggest that you need to install much more bass trapping in there. What is that thing marked "storage bass trap" in the upper rear corner of the room? How is it made? Is it tuned or broad band?
Walls are made of standard doubled 1/2 inch gypse panels.
That might be your problem! A wall with 1/2" drywall on each side over a 4" air gap has its first harmonic at around 125 Hz... Your walls might be acting as panel traps! - Stuart -

Soundman202022 September 2011#6
So if there are acting as panel traps does it mean cancelling or boosting a frequency?
Canceling. Well, not really "canceling", but rather "absorbing energy at that frequency". That would account for your sensation that you have a dip in the whole room at around 120 Hz. In fact, you will probably be able to feel the drywall vibrating, if you touch it lightly with your fingertips while playing music or sine sweeps that hit their resonant frequencies. Youd also be able to clearly hear those frequencies on the other side of the wall (in the next room). If that's the case, then you have a few options open to you, but they all involve construction, of one type or another... Are you willing to do that, and if so what is your budget?
L&R Front corners. 8X3 8inches
You mean 3 feet wide by 8 feet tall and 8 inches thick? Angled at 45° across the front corners, so that the 3 foot face is aimed roughly towards the center of the room?
aluminium frames filled with acoustic wool
What do you mean by "acoustic wool"? What type? What density? Do you have a link to the manufacturer's web site? I'd like to see the specs on that. Another question: How did you determine that these frequencies are your problems? Where did that graph come from? Maybe you could try downloading REW and testing with that: it will give us a much better idea of where the problems are. - Stuart -
