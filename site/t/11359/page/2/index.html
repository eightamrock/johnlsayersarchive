So far it looks like a plan. If your speakers are active you'd need an airflow behind the speakers to take care of the heat. Soundman has done some good research on soffit mounting, maybe had can add some comments.
Regarding heat and ventilation, definitely! I've been doing some testing on my new Adam A7 active speakers, and I was surprised at how hot they get. After running them all day at rather normal levels for a rather normal mixing session, they are more than just warm to the touch. They have big heatsinks on the back, and those get uncomfortably hot. I didn't have a thermometer on me (dumb!) but I'm going to take one next session and see hoe hot they actually get. These speakers are only rated at 100 watts, but of course the vast majority of that goes into heat, not sound. So it's basically like sealing up a 100 watt light bulb in a small wooden box, and leaving it on all day... So one thing I would definitely recommend if you have active speakers, is to allow for much more ventilation than you thought. More even than John shows on his suggestion for soffit ventilation. You are going to need a lot of airflow around the back of the speaker. I am even seriously considering making the soffit ventilation part of my HVAC system, or at least get some small, quiet PC fans to "help" the airflow along a little... I don't want damaged speakers or warping wood due to thermal overload. So that would be my suggestion: for active speakers, leave LOTS of space for ventilation, then figure out how that is going to affect your acoustics... I suspect that sealing your speaker in a box, then adding ducts to the box for ventilation, is something like adding bass reflex ports to a speaker cabinet! I'm not into speaker design at all, so I can't say what effect that has on the response. Hopefully the frequencies of those long ducts will be low enough to not be an issue, but maybe one of the speaker gurus can hip in with comments on that. Regarding using sheetrock for the soffit panels? I'm not convinced that is a good idea. It has the mass you need, but I'm concerned about how it will stand up, over time, to the constant high levels of vibration that the speakers will be pumping into it. That's a lot of vibration energy at very close range. Just a hunch, but I suspect that sheetrock might start disintegrating after a while. I think I'd stick with John's suggestion of heavy wooden panels, at the very least, but I'm going for concrete. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 January 2009#35
What are you refering to when you say "soffit panels"?
I'm referring to the large, heavy massive panels that directly surround the speaker itself. That 's what you make the hole in, to mount your speaker. You cut a hole in the soffit panel that is exactly the shape and size of the front face of the speaker. It acts as an infinite baffle, and therefore needs to be as large as you can make it (the experts recommend up to 45 inches all around, if you can!), heavy, dense, rigid, thick, etc. It must not vibrate. OK, 45 inches is probably impossible in a small studio, but you do need to give it as much surface area as you can, with the speaker surrounded by as much open, flat real estate as possible. In my case, I decided to go with concrete soffit panels, because I figure it will be easier to cast the strange shape that I need for my Adam A7's, than trying to cut it out of wood. Concrete also gives you much higher density and stiffness than wood, which is good. Sheetrock does have the density that you need, but I don't think it meets some other requirements. Mika is definitely on the right track with HDF, and 38 mm sounds excellent! His should work great. They must be pretty heavy and hard to work with, though, but who cares if they sound good! - Stuart -

Soundman202014 January 2009#37
I intend for the soffit panels made out of 3/4" Oak or Birch.
John can certainly say better than I can, but I think I'd go for something thicker than that. You do want lots of mass in your panels. The density of both birch and oak is around 0.6 to 0.7 g/cm3. The density of MDF (what John shows in his example) and HDF (what Mika is using) is around 0.8 to 0.9 g/cm3. The density of gypsum board is around 1.2 g /cm3, and the density of concrete is around is about 2.3 g /cm3. So to get the same amount of mass as Mika has in his 38mm HDF panels, you'd need 2 inches of oak or birch. Or just 9/32 of an inch of concrete! - Stuart -
