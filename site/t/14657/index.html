xSpace7 August 2010#3
Your print reads that you have >rigid insulation< in the overhead joist cavity. Lose it, you already have fluffy insulation, the rigid isn't going to do anything that justifies the cost. "I understand a bit about the issues of a triple leaf assembly, but was wondering if there is any other approach that avoids tearing down the existing GWB ceiling?" No sir. If you choose to not remove the existing ceiling then you could install additional layers of sheetrock to the existing sheetrock. But in an attempt to do a full room-in-a-room, you will have to remove the sheetrock to develop the mass/spring/mass required for good isolation. The problem with basement ceilings is that often when you remove the existing sheathing you will find all manner of wiring, plumbing, ducting and gas lines that impede progress and create specific challenges that may not have been considered. Your print also reads that you will install RC-1 on the ceiling, you want to get resilient channel with 2 legs, typically rc-2 is what it will be called. You may also be able to exclude the use of sway braces (code permitting) by installing OSB in a vertical fashion on the interior side of the framing to give it structural rigidity.
