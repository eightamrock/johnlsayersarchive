I have a rehearsal space that is 17'10" * 11'4" * 8' 1" tall. One wall adjoins the neighbours house and is constructed as follows from the inside out Wall construction 1) 2 layers of 5/8 sheetrock 2) 2*4 studs with loose housing insulation 3) 2 inch air gap (except cpupled at the ceiling to neighbours wall) 4) plywood with embedded stone (called cepstone I thnk) 5) 2*4 with loose fiberglass insulation 6) 2 layers of 5/8 Sheetrock The 'inner wall' is tied to the nieghbours house through the roof. The slab is 'loossely coupled', i.e. separate pours with some spacer material. The question: In order to minimize the amount of sound energy going into my neighbours house should I a) mount my rehearsal speakers on this wall, projecting music away from it, or b) mount my speakers on the opposite wall, projecting towards the wall (less physical coupling?), or c) mount my speakers free-standing close to the wall I share with my neignbour, projecting away from this wall. Edit: My preference is (b), so I can hear well sitting at the drums. I'm suspecting that low-frequency leakage will be my biggest problem and, since the lows are more omnidirectional, having the speakers on the opposite wall is the best solution. The multi-layerd adjoining wall is the one that is not drawn in the diagram, so the diagram shows the PA speakers facing the shared wall.
