Soundman20201 March 2013#2
And would there be any simpler and better solution than the aforementioned one?
You could use resilient channel directly across the bottom of the concrete beams, with the drywall hung from those. That would only take a couple of inches off the height, and still give you good isolation. RC is only about 1/2" thick, plus 2 layers of 5/8" takes up just 1-3/4". Your final ceiling height could be around 7' 10-1/4". Your other option is to build the ceiling "inside out". Since you will very likely need absorption on the entire ceiling anyway, you would not actually lose much by doing it that way. In that case, your drywall surface could be at 7' 10-1/2" (if you leave a 1/4" gap above).
If we forget about insulating the beam (as it is a thick solid concrete mass), just doing sound insulation (wood stud on ceiling - sound proof clip - hat channel - gypsum board) on the top ceiling - would it be a big problem?
Your intuition is right! Yes, if you don't decouple the drywall from the concrete, then any vibration in the concrete structure itself will be transmitted into your room (and vice-versa). There might not be a lot of transmission the other way: from sounds inside your room going out, but there still could be some transmission, and in any event the amount of structure-borne sound in the concrete is unknown: you'd find out how bad it is AFTER you get the ceiling up, and it would be too late by then! :) - Stuart -

That would only be where the actual concrete beams are, which seem to be very thick. In the gaps between the beams, there is plenty of space for the insulation. The small area where the beams are is probably not going to be an issue. - Stuart -
