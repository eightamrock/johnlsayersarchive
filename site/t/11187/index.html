How do I soundproof an extremely large area for film making the most cost effective way? And be sure when we spend $50,000 it has actualyl worked? Here’s what we are planning on doing... I’ve read almost everything I’ve gotten my hands on and a LOT that’s here. I am very familiar with all the processes and know there is a big difference between acoustical treatment and a soundproof room (as sound proof as possible anyway). No need for treatment as it's just a soundstage and will not be recording the Boston Symphony! The sound stage (warehouse) currently is all tin and steel frame construction with a concrete floor. Size is 30 x 60 with 14’ to the usable ceiling. Thus floor size is 1800 sq. ft. and surfaces (walls and ceiling) to be covered is 2520 sq. ft. The plan is: Floor: 2” x 6” framed sub-floor covered with two layers of 5/8” chipboard attached to each other with GreenGlue. Prior to attaching chipboard flooring, all frame gaps will be filled with sand in-between and over the existing concrete floor. The top surface will be a layer of 1/4“ neoprene rubber in 48" wide rolls, again attached with Greenglue. Walls/Ceiling: 1. Wood Stud frame through-out, gapped off the tin wall/roof 1-2”. The frames will be covered with a layer of 1/4“ neoprene rubber on the outside facing walls. 2. Stuffed with R-15. Covered with 5/8” drywall, seams taped, mudded, primed, and sealed airtight with Quiet Seal acoustical caulk. 4. Next layer of 3/4” drywall attached to 5/8” under-layer with Greenglue at a right angle and same; seams taped, mudded, primed, and sealed airtight w/acoustical caulk. 5. 4"-6” air-gap 6. Wood Studs framed for 2nd wall & stuffed with R-15. 7. 5/8” drywall attached to all in-facing contact points (studs, floor and ceiling gaps, etc.) with Green Glue. Then all drywall seams taped, mudded, primed, and sealed airtight with acoustical caulk. 8. last layer, this time using 1/2" to 3/4" QuietRock attached to 5/8” under-layer with Greenglue. All seams as before. Acoustical caulk to be beaded at corner, floor and ceiling gaps. All plug holes, etc. to be filled with Quiet Putty. Roof: Tin Roof to be covered outside with 1/4" neoprene rubber. What do you think of the plan? please make any suggestions or changes as that’s why I am posting here for your expert advice before we get going! Many thanks in advance. Billy
rod gervais6 September 2008#2
Billy, I,ve been involved in movie studio construction - so I have a bit of a feel for what you might be up against. let's begin with some important things that you don't tell us - where are you located ? what is the ambient outside noise levels you hve to deal with now? What are the frequencies in those levels? What are the residule inside ambient noise levels you have now as a result of those outside levels. What is the length of data gathering that you did to assertain the sound levels you need to deal with. Now - a few comments on your intended design........ This first problem I see is that you are building either a 2 or 4 leaf wall assembly depending on exactly how that layer of MLV acts inside of the wall cavity. This I would not recommend. What you want to acheive is a 2 leaf assembly that gives you the isolation you really require. So you should add mass up tight to the inside face of the metal siding on the building. Then you need to step inside of that creating an air gap - and have a 2nd layer of mass on the inside face of that wall assembly. insulation in both frames will enhance your isolation. Next - MLV doesn't necessasarily give you a lot of bang for the buck - you can purchase mass in the form of drywall a whole lot cheaper than you can purchase MLV - with much better results than you can acheive with the MLV. The same question goes for the 3/4" drywall - yuo pay a premium for that over 5/8" (you could probably do 2 layers of 5/8" for what one layer of 3/4" costs you in the end) - what exactly do you think your benefit is with this material. The green glue might make sense or it might not. An example of this might be very dependent on your existing structure and what is actually making it into the building via the existing concrete slab. All in all my BEST recommendation to you would be to first hire an acoustician to do some studies to determine your exact needs. He/she will be able to not only determine a baselline for isolation needs from airborne sound - but can also use the proper equipment to determine what (if any) issues you migth have with the existing foundation/slab. Rod
