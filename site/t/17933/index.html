Also, i was thinking about soffit mounting speakers, but i will have to research that quite a bit more before i do any designing of how i will do that. If you know of any good links for guidance it would be appreciated,
Try John's Recording Manual to get the basic idea: http://johnlsayers.com/Recmanual/index.htm Plus use the "search" feature of the forum to find other designs and methods, and more details. Bare in mind that that the recording manual is about fifteen years old, and John has updated his soffit design many times since then.
ive heard that you can mess up soffit mounts pretty easily.
The basic concept is simple: the front panel of the soffit acts as an infinite baffle, which allows you to do away with the baffle step compensation that was built into your speakers by the manufacturer. They had to do that, since it is really hard to fit a huge baffle, several feet high and wide, into a small box that they can put on the shelf in the store. So they make the speakers small enough for that, but then have to build in circuits to compensate for the power imbalance due to the lack of a baffle. so if you build the baffle, then you can turn off those circuits since there is no longer any power imbalance. As long as your speaker has a "bass roll-of" control, and does not have a bass reflex port on the back or sides, then you are fine.
was your slot wall/vocal booth comment a what the hell are you doing kind of thing? haha, cause i just thought the room needed some color, and was like, this would look cool!
Not at all! :) The comment was about the angle and location of the slot wall on the front of your vocal booth, assuming that it is a vocal booth. Slot walls are very useful acoustic devices, when used correctly. But there purpose isn't really to add color or make the room look cool! Their purposes is to treat some aspect(s) of the room acoustics, in order to get more neutral response.
