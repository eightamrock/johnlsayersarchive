Antony, How big is the room. Dimensions please? The vent you linked is straight and will probably not offer the STL that you need. It might work with the addition of a baffled box in the room interior. John Sayers has a ventilation design for a booth here that you could modify to work with this acoustic wall vent. It also could be that you have no vapor barrier. How old is the house? Cheers, John

Soundman202013 October 2010#4
So you would suggest getting one of those vents.
No, John said that it would NOT give you the degree of isolation that you need. Adding a silencer (a.k.a. "baffled box") will do the job, but if you do that then you don't really need one of those vents. Any old pair of vents will do (and will be much cheaper!), if you use a silencer. Search the forum for the term "silencer", and you will find numerous threads, with many examples of how to make them. How much airflow do you need for your room, and what speed? What fans will you be using? - Stuart -

"after a searched it looks like condensation due to bad ventilation. " If this refers to something on the Internet, do not trust it to apply to your situation directly, it may or may not, everything matters. There is some form of ventilation even if it is opening a door or window right? The most important aspects to ascertain what is happening in this room/house is to have pictures and or describe what the building is made of, material wise. The lack of a vapor retarder or even the inclusion of one does not immediately give indication as to what is happening inside. Especially if this is a concrete structure :) So I have to agree with Glenn that you need to get someone local with experience as this is a serious issue that can alter your health and can deteriorate a buildings materials over the long run.
