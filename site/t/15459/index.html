xSpace5 January 2011#8
A 5 cm thick concrete from the ground up is rather thin, 10 cm would be a better start with a sand bed beneath it to attempt to decouple it from the bedrock. But to your question, it is still a matter of what do you want to see on the floor, wood or concrete, possible stained or painted. Area rugs are used with liberty and can help control whatever reflections the floor produces...if it is concrete or wood or whatever hard surface it winds up being. Your main concerns after the floor are the dense granite room that reflects very well. So your absorption on those walls and especially the overhead cloud/treatments will help get that room tamed down.

xSpace5 January 2011#10
All things considered, the concrete substrate needs to be as thick as it needs to be. And who would know that better than the folks that will place it. 12 cm would be a great thickness, but what does it need to do is always a matter of importance. But to the sand, it depends on what you are placing it on. If it is on top of a compacted earth area then you could use 4 inches thick and be good for a 10cm thick concrete slab. But if the sand is going to be placed on top of a hard rocky surface then this wouldn't work the same.

Simple thing for me is this:Soft things absorb hard things reflect. Simple. What you are suggesting is that a specific flooring will have some dynamic ability to reduce the ability of sound to reflect. Mostly, not a one you can mention, saving cork, will have this effect. It is a hard surface and it will reflect, and you are spending too much mental energy on something that you should already have educated yourself on...hard means reflect, soft means absorb :) Simple as that, no matter what else you may have heard ;)
