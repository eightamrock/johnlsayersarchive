Reading around, I see I am in the same situation as Rafal in this thread: the room I want to treat is surrounded by 24cm concrete walls. Here was one of Knightly's comments about this situation:
adding a single layer of 12.5 mm wallboard on a separate stud frame inside your walls would improve the isolation to around 70 dB at 500 hZ, and still around 45 dB at low end. The advantage of doing only ONE layer of wallboard is interior sound. This inner wall will act like a bunch of panel bass traps, saving you the trouble of building as many as you normally would have to.

gullfo10 February 2008#7
in order to benefit from the "wall as a series of panel traps" you want to ensure they are sealed because if they leak into your space then the effectiveness will be minimal if any and you may end up with a bunch of buzzing panels... you might also want to use various thicknesses of plywood depending on where particular modes fall. heavy panels in the corners and middle, lighter panels in between. rigid insulation is need to dampen the "panels" but you don't want them to touch. definitely stick with the 3x drywall if you need isolation instead of the panel absorber walls. add the panel absorbers later if you need them. presumably you're using 2x 30db doors...
