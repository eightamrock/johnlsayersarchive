dymaxian28 February 2004#2
The real problem I'm having is that one of the walls of the space is glass from floor to ceiling.
Eeek. That's a tough situation. Do you need access to those doors and windows? I'd think that the least damaging position for your mixing desk would be to have it facing the windows. Having the entire back wall reflective would be rough on you, and having either side that much more reflective would probably hurt your stereo imaging... If you're not worried about sound isolation too much, you can let all the low freq stuff just go right out thru those windows, and use the rest of the world as a bass trap. Not much high freq goes out the back of the speakers, so you don't have to worry too much about comb filtering... Besides, this way you get those windows to look out while you work. How's the view? If you can use the ceiling-to-wall corners for bass trapping, you'll get the biggest benefit there. Auralex will be happy to sell you units for those corners too. Good luck! Kase www.minemusic.net

Greg, The third drawing is the best arrangement. And as Kase said you can treat the wall/ceiling corners and get good results. There are also portable bass traps you can get and put on mike stands, to place in the corners while mixing. > Waddaya think about using large baffles to break up the room's paralels? < I don't think that's necessary. A few absorber panels in key places on the walls and ceiling will do a fine job controlling echo, ambience, and early reflections. --Ethan
