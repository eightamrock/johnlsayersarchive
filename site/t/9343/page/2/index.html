billshoup7 April 2008#30
Hi All, I got the first layer of sheetrock up on a small outside wall this weekend. I've never done any taping/mudding before, so I'm kind of experimenting on this first layer that will eventually be hidden so I can try to determine whether I think I want to tackle the finish work on the final layer or hire it done. The real reason for this post though is to share my acoustic caulk (acoustical sealant) experience. I've been using Grabber and have been very happy with it. As I've probably mentioned a few times, my brother is a building contractor and I was talking to him about my project. I probably mentioned to him how many cases of caulk I've gone through - as many of you know, beef-up work really eats it up. Anyway, he told me he had 4 cases of acoustic caulk left over from a job from something like 3 years ago that he was sure he would never use and wanted to know if I wanted to try it. Of course I jumped at the chance. I should have asked him to check what kind it was before I took it. It is Tremco, and of course when I got the sheetrock up I was describing above I tore into my free caulk, without taking time to do a quick search on this site. Now I remember folks complaining on this site about the odor from their caulk, which I could not relate to while using Grabber. They also talked about having to take mineral spirits in the shower with them to get the caulk off their hands, which I also could not relate to. With the Grabber caulk, I mostly wash off my hands and tools in a bucket of room temperature water in the basement with no soap. I don't know if Grabber comes in other colors, but the stuff I'm using is white, which I like. Grabber is a latex based product and to me it's very similar to working with silicone. I've also read about folks getting the caulk all over their house, which hasn't been an issue with Grabber. The Tremco product on the other hand is a lot like tar. It stinks, is incredibly sticky, and basically is just a total pain to work with. The stuff I have is dark grey (basically black) and I've got spots on my carpet after I was sure I had totally cleaned myself up, and there are some spots in our bathtub that I still need to work on to get off. I scrubbed (and scrubbed and scrubbed) my hands with Comet cleanser to get it off, and ended up throwing away the caulking tools (including my caulk gun) I used with that caulk. I've been paying around $55/case for the Grabber caulk so the Tremco my brother gave me represents around $220 I could save by using it, but after one session with that stuff, I'm going back to buying more Grabber. I'm not sure how it will perform in years to come compared to the Tremco, but IMHO, there is absolutely no comparison in regard to "ease of application", and I'm very thankful I stumbled across the Grabber when I was looking for caulk. I hope sharing this experience might save someone else from having to go through it. Between these two brands, my advice is to go with Grabber! I guess I should mention that I'm assuming that the fact that the Tremco caulk I have is around 3 years old isn't part of the issue. By the way, I'm planning to dispose of these cases of Tremco, but will probably keep them around for a little while in case anyone reading this is willing to deal with this stuff to save a few bucks. I believe I have 2 tubes short of 4 cases (28 oz tubes/12 tubes per case, so about 46 tubes total) that I would sell for $50 total + shipping if you're interested (if so PM me). Obviously I'm not recommending this stuff! :x (UPDATE: The Tremco caulk has been sold, so is no longer available)

Hi All, At long last, these may be my final updates. I think I will begin with a few notes on how I did my bass traps, actually perhaps more on how I hung them than how I made them. To build them, I roughly followed studio_drums plans here which I highly recommend. In my case, I used 4 lb density rock wool (2' X 4' X 4”). I used 1 X 4's for the frames rather than 1 X 3's, and I wrapped the rock wool in garbage bags before wrapping them in burlap. I did this for two reasons; 1) I didn't want particles of rock wool floating around, and 2) to have the panels absorb a little less at high frequencies. I was a little nervous about whether this was the correct thing to do, but for my purposes (and to my limited knowledge of acoustics) it's appeared to work fine. You can judge for yourself based on the measurements I made here, and please, I encourage you to make comments reacting to what you see there. Now, in regard to hanging the traps, on the walls, I mostly used the old picture hanging wire technique. For those mounted on the wall, I put 4” cardboard cylinders behind them to space them away from the wall (in an effort to lower the frequencies they would absorb). In my case, the greater challenge was how to mount the treatment on the ceiling with two layers of 5/8” sheetrock on RC1. I finally settled on the d-rings I found here, attached to the ceiling with wall-dog sheetrock screws. In my particular case, this has worked out very well even though the d-rings were quite expense. For the free standing/portable bass traps, I added a cross-bar to the frame (which I also did for the frames where I used the cardboard cylinders behind them on the wall) and mounted them to tripod mic stands. I hope this info is useful to someone out there. I would be happy to answer any questions about any of this if you're interested. I will do another post in regard to the finished studio in general ASAP.
