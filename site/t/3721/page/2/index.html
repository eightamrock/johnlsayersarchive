Hey, stebed, I gave you the itemized costs....you don't want hardwood floor, use a calculator and subtract. You don't need a patch bay...subtract it. What's so hard to understand? I'm building a studio in a 15X25 space...so those are the costs I am working with. I'm not your f*ing contractor, so figure it out yourself. Now that I understand that you want to play drums 24/7 in a condo let me offer you this advice. Don't bother....just get to know your neighbours, and work out a time to play. You'll never be able to soundproof for drums 24/7 in a condo construction, not if you're having trouble with a $10K budget. I've done the research...I lived in a townhouse and nearly built a studio there. Couldn't make it work, and bought a single familiy home instead. If you want to save yourself further aggravation get yourself informed. Become familiar with the following terms:
    1. STC (Sound Transmission Class) 2. TL (Transmission Loss) in particular at low frequencies (like kick drum) 3. Decoupling (importance of air gaps) 4. Mass Law 5. Flanking sound 6. Structure borne noise (i.e. vibrations from your shared concrete slab)
Here's a few equations to toy with. The dB of SPL for the edge of perception in an untreated room is around 30-40 dB. So, if you want your neighbours to sleep soundly while you're pouding the drums, you want <30 dB SPL at their beds. Inside your drum room (which remember has stiff walls and no acoustic treatment) the level could be between 110-120 dB depending on your drums and how hard you hit. STC of a very effective room-with-a-room construction is 63. So, on the other side of your construction, you get say 50-60 dB. Here's a db chart for reference:
Sounds dB SPL Rocket Launching 180 Jet Engine 140 Thunderclap, Air Raid Siren 1 Meter 130 Jet takeoff (200 ft) 120 Rock Concert, Discotheque 110 Firecrackers, Subway Train 100 Heavy Truck (15 Meter), City Traffic 90 Alarm Clock (1 Meter), Hair Dryer 80 Noisy Restaurant, Business Office 70 Air Conditioning Unit, Conversational Speech 60 Light Traffic (50 Meter), Average Home 50 Living Room, Quiet Office 40 Library, Soft Whisper (5 Meter) 30 Broadcasting Studio, Rustling Leaves 20 Hearing Threshold 0
So, at 50 dB we might be at the edge of acceptable...and even then probably not enough to satisfy your neighbours if you want to play every night. The tolerance of your neighbours is a huge variable here. Better to shoot for 40dB. So, you think sure I'll make an STC 70 wall (just try and find one)...sure, a fine idea (in theory), but you'll soon learn that low frequencies get through your walls much easier than high frequencies, and the the TL at 80 Hz (where the power of the kick is) will be something like 30 dB lower than the STC rating. So, your neighbours will hear the low frequencies at around 70-80 dB (assuming an STC 70 wall). Just when you think that's bad enough, then you will learn just how hard it is to make a room to an STC of 70. In particular, look a ceiling and floor constructions to avoid structure borne noise and flanking sound. So, you float your floors, and build your walls on the floated floors, then build your ceiling on the floated walls. I hope you have high ceilings because you just lost almost 1 foot by doing this! All this, and you still get 70 dB at low frequencies...your neighbours are still unhappy. You have 3 choices: 1. Change your expectations...play during times when your neighbours aren't going to complain 2. Buy a set of V-drums.... 3. Move... Kris PS: Since you have existing walls, you will actually need to tear off the drywall on one side, stuff it with insulation, and put another layer of drywall on the outer face of the existing wall in order to get the double wall concept to work effectively. Otherwise you end up with a 3 leaf system (one more term to research).

drfrankencopter19 May 2005#31
stebed, You really need to watch out for the structure borne noise...it's going to be the limiting factor. Another thing to consider is that even if your neighbours don't sleep in a room adjacent to your practice room, that they will not likely have built their rooms to be soundproofed at all. Figure STC of 15 max for a standard construction room wall with a typical hollow core door. Odds are, if it's not a bedroom or bathroom it has no door, and therefore very low STC. Shared floor and ceiling are big problems....worse than shared walls in terms of expense. Do some googling on 'footfall noise' and ceiling construction, and you'll turn up solutions like floated concrete floors, and isolation hangars... Since you haven't bought yet, what's the point in discussing cost? If your ceiling height is 9 feet versus 15 feet strongly influences the cost of drywall and lumber. Build requirements are huge variables....if you can get away without requireing a floated construction you save big money. If not, be prepared to spend, or be unsatisfied.... K
