Stebed - you asked how much would it cost to soundproof a room so you can play drums 24/7 without disturbing others in the same building. you are looking at 1550sqft of insulation to line the walls, 1550sqft to be built with stud frame, ceiling and floor joists - 750 sqft of a double layer flooring, 1550sqft of at least a double drywall layer mounted and sealed, new soundproof doors and windows. Add to that the cost of an electrician to attach the power points and lights AND a fully ducted aircon system or you will die from lack of air as that box has to be totally airtight to be soundproof and playing drums consumes oxygen doesn't it. Plus you will have to treat that drywall box acoustically or your drums will sound shit. You do the math cheers john

You also need a ceiling, say, plus resilient channel, plus double drywall....add another $1500. Insulation will probably run you about $1000 (total guess here, it may be on the low side). Figure on lots of tubes of acoustic sealant...maybe $200 or more. Floors might be nice to have...figure $2K for a decent floor (and this assumes you can build right on the slab, i.e. no need to float the floor). Snakes and wiring, electrical...figure $500-1K by the time you have your patch bays installed and wired (XLR connectors, and good cable aren't cheap). Lighting...figure at least $250...and lots more if you want studio dimmers that won't generate noise on your audio lines. Lights can get expensive fast... Ventilation...ductless split A/C system $2K installed (??). Plus, you still need fresh air supply...so figure another $200 for a quiet fan. If you really want a quiet air handling system you'll need lined duct and plenum chambers...too expensive for now. Nevertheless you'll still need a baffle/louvre system for the fan...$250. Window...not sure, but probably $250-500 isn't out to lunch by the time you get the gaskets done and the glass cut. drfrankencopter18 May 2005#21
By room within a room, I mean double wall construction. The outer walls are framed on seperate 2X4's with 2 layers of drywall on the outer face. Then the inner wall is made of a seperate set of 2X4's with double drywall on the inner face (no dryall on the insides of the wall, that makes a 3 leaf system which is not very effective). Insulation, is naturally between the layers. The end result is effectively a thick wall with an air gap and no direct coupling between the inner and outter faces. Counting up the material involved this is the same as building two walls around your perimeter, hence the doubling in cost. You want double the drywall to increase the mass of your walls...don't underestimate the mass effect. To quiet a room you really need mass, dead air and mass. Floor...well it depends on what you want. Me, I want hardwood, and $2K is pretty reasonable for hardwood and a subfloor for 400 sqft. Obviously, painted/stained concrete will be cheaper.... Wiring...well, XLR's are pricey. If you want something decent it's about $5 per connector. By the time you wire up a 96 pt patch bay (and I also have a 144pt bay to wire in addition) you've spent a fair amount on conenctors alone! Doors, yup Overly doors are expensive....they work, but they're the big $ solution. Mere mortals doing this as a hobby will end up building our own doors or making due with a couple solid cores and some good seals. Acoustic treatments...trust me you'll want them. The first thing you'll notice once you have your heavy/stiff walls up is that there will be bass problems. Regular thin walls act like bass traps to a certain extent...once the the thick walls go in your room will show it's modal response more, and you're probably going to want to tame it. Putting absorbent across the ceiling is also a good idea to make a room 'sound' taller. Kris
