yanni13 January 2006#1
Hello all, it's been a while since I've been on this forum but I've just settled on the design for my sound proof rehearsal / recording room (no separate control room) which will be built in 6 x 4 metre room in our new house on a concrete slab. I want to build a room within a room, that is a self standing box about 100mm away from the existing walls and ceiling. The only thing that won't be isolated is the floor maybe, if I can do it within my budget I will. But if I can't I'll use thick carpet underlay under the carpet. I won't be pumping out huge bass frequencies anyway. Now my question is and because I've never really seen or experienced it in a real situation is whether an air gap all around the room of 100mm is really going to stop sound from travelling out ? I'm still sceptical. The inner room walls will be the normal stud construction with drywall/platerboard both sides and noise rated fibreglass insulation in between. The outer or existing room is a finished construction with the normal studs and drywall/plasterboard both sides. The entrance will be a glass sliding door on both the outer and inner rooms with all the sealing technology I can get my hands on. I know I've asked this question before and I've done the research but now it's crunch time - if anyone's had experience in this area please let me know. I need to be convinced. Thanks Yanni (from south of Melbourne Australia)

Yanni, the room-in-room you proposed is EXACTLY the same as the "40 dB" illustration in the STC chart Kendale posted - Now, follow that sketch to the right, step by step - note that you can REMOVE material and IMPROVE the wall at the same time. How's THAT for non-intuitive??!? But it's TRUE. If you compare the 40 dB wall with the 63 dB wall, you'll see that there's EXACTLY the same amount of material used but 23 dB difference in the result... So the advice you've gotten is right on the money - less is more, at least in this case.
