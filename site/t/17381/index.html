xSpace11 February 2012#2
If I were doing this, first thing I would address is the site lines. As it is right now you are looking into a corner with multiple glass doors and reduced wall treatment area for your control room. Maybe move the control room up to the top corner and consider a view window in the control room plan that allows the viewer to see all parts of these areas without looking into that corner wall configuration. As a matter of treatment, the control room design as is has effectively removed, due to doors being in all the wrong places, your potential treatment areas and will most likely increase the brightness of the room that might have you looking for a way to reduce this high frequency energy by installing curtains over the doors, there by negating the paths and reducing these areas to more a nuisance than a good flow order.

xSpace18 February 2012#7
I would also add this, given that we are talking about "new construction" there are many other items on the pre-construction side that have to be addressed before we can even begin to entertain what walls go where what shapes fit what area, etc. You have to first, get the actual footprint of the slab you are trying to place...then get some idea of what kind of exterior we are talking about. As is, your drawing shows a trapezoid slab, and you can trust me in this makes everyone involved in the forming to the wall erection to the roofing, bat their eyes and shake their heads trying to explain how difficult it will be, and it will be somewhat, but that is not my point. You need a certified footprint to start or everything we discuss can go up in smoke.
