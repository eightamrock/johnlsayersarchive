Soundman202028 February 2011#2
Hi Matt. Welcome. You didn't say what you are trying to accomplish, but $500 won't go very far towards it. There are two totally separate concepts that people tend to confuse: Isolation and treatment. The y are entirely different. Isolation is stopping sound from getting in or out of your room, so that you don't disturb the neighbors and they don't disturb you. Treatment is what you do to the room once it is isolated, so that it sounds good and is a useful acoustic space. Isolation will make your room sound bad. Treatment makes it sound good again. From what you say, it seems like you do not need any isolation at all, and only need a bit of very, very basic treatment. Is that correct?
I will be using the studio simply as a production studio so there is no need for any recording space.
I'm not sure about the difference between what you mean by "production studio" and "recording studio". How can you produce something without first recording it? Do you mean that this room will only ever be used for practice, never for actual recording?
As for the floor, I will most likely be covering it in rubber and carpet unless there are better options for me out there.
Both are really bad ideas, acoustically. Just leave it as plain concrete, which is good for a floor, acoustically speaking, provided that you treat the ceiling and the rest of the room correctly.
How much of an issue will the ceiling be, should I be doing something about that also?
Once again, that all depends on what you are talking about: isolation or treatment? And it also depends on many other things. If you cold explain more clearly what your goals are, and what the existing construction is like, then we might be able to offer you some advice, but once again on a budget of $500 there really isn't very much you can do. Even ten times that much would be probably come up a bit short for basic isolation, and basic treatment. - Stuart -

Soundman20201 March 2011#4
It is purely production because I don't need to record in it. Not all music involves recording
OK, so it is just a practice area. No recording. Got it.
For the rest of the room, will i be able to get REASONABLE sound treatment just using acoustic panels and the like?
You can undoubtedly IMPROVE the sound from where it is now, but whether or not the result will be acceptable depends on you. Small rooms need bass trapping, so I'd start by splitting your budget and spending at least 1/2 of it on bass trapping, which you can do simply by putting a few panels of 4" thick Owens Corning 703 diagonally across the 4 vertical corners of the room. Put two layers there (8") if you can afford it. It won't do much, but is all you can afford. 703 costs about US$ 25 to $30 per panel. 8 panels (2 on each corner) will set you back around US$ 200 and change. You will also want to kill the flutter echos between parallel surfaces, so put a few more panels of 703 against the walls and on the ceiling, but don't cover it completely! "Checkerboard" the panels, so that where you have blank wall on one side of the room there is a piece of 703 on the opposite wall. Space the 703 a couple of inches away form the wall, to maximize the effect, and cover it in acoustically transparent cloth to hide the "ugly" factor (703 ain't very pretty!). Many people make wooden frames for their 703, to protect it from damage (it isn't very strong), so you might want to do that, if you can stretch your budget that far. Assuming that you use another 8 panels of 703 here (2 on each wall). that eats up another US$ 200 or so form your budget, leaving less than 100 for the ceiling. So put up as many more panels of 703 on the ceiling as you can afford. That's probably about all you will manage to do with your budget, and it certainly will improve the sound of the room. If you have a few dollars left over, or can find some more, then there are other things you could do as well, but that covers the basics. It would be good if you could to a proper acoustic analysis of the room before and after treatment and post the results here, so you can see how much effect the treatment has. You can use REW for that (which is free!). Good luck! And don't forget to post some photos and comments, to let us know how it goes. And of course, feel free to ask more questions. - Stuart -
gullfo1 March 2011#5
i would build the center dividing wall on a 15° angle to help offset the limited treatments and then focus on the corners. if you use RC-1 on the dividing wall on the CR side, careful not to bind the drywall to the studs incorrectly, you may get some more isolation for a little bit more money - your sister might appreciate that and it might help with some of the bass absorption. you might consider a couple of larger absorber frames to hold the insulation and cloth covering and a couple of scatter absorbers and a ceiling cloud.
