knightfly21 January 2005#8
There are two round ducts between the joists that run all the way across the room and feed registers on the floor above. How does one treat these ducts to prevent sound from travelling out the registers? If these are metal ducts, you need to either insulate them with rigid fiberglass pipe insulation (comes in two halves, you duct tape them together around the pipe) or, especially if these will feed the studio, remove the metal ducts and replace them with flex duct, being NOT careful to run it in straigh lines but more like a drunk snake track - These ducts will be in between the two masses of your mass-air-mass sound barrier, so will get some sound leakage; if there is room vertically, you'd be better off boxing them in with cleats and wallboard (2 layers) and filling in everywhere possible with insulation. There is currently standard ~4" thick fiberglass insulation between the joists. I'm getting 2" thick rigid fiberglass board from a friend to place in the ceiling. Would it be best to place the board in contact with the plywood of the floor above or place it in contact with the ceiling drywall. I would use the standard fiberglass to fill the remaining void. (is the point of the rigid board to fill the area between the joists or to contact either floor or ceiling surface for damping/absorption?) The point is both; if you have insulation contacting both inside surfaces of the barrier with light to medium pressure, it will damp both membranes and improve isolation a few dB - putting the heavier insulation on top will require a way to keep it there, or it will sag; this crushes the other insulation, allowing the rigid stuff to fall away from the upper floor and reduce whatever damping you might have had. By putting the heavier stuff on the bottom, it will contact the lower ceiling panels by gravity; this will also leave the lighter stuff LESS compressed so it should remain in contact with the upper floor sheath... Steve
