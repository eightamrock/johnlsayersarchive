ardo wrote:
I need an advise on what would be the most crucial acoustic treatment this room should have.
This is a deep subject, and a complete answer requires far more than will fit into a single reply here. So here's the short version. All rooms need: * Broadband (not tuned) bass traps straddling as many corners as you can manage, including the wall-ceiling corners. * Mid/high frequency absorption at the first reflection points on the side walls and ceiling. * Some additional amount of mid/high absorption and/or diffusion on any large areas of bare parallel surfaces, such as opposing walls or the ceiling if the floor is reflective. For the complete story see my Acoustics FAQ. You may find it a little easier to digest than all of the stickies, though it's still not a five minute read. :) --Ethan

ardo wrote:

I was thinking of making a moveable trap by stacking three 4X2 traps vertically, i.e. making a 'gobo-like' thingy 4ft wide by 6ft tall, and this way extending the South wall, blocking out the drum set.
maybe put them each on a stand vertically so they're about 2' off the ground and put them side by side. then you'd have a wall 6' high and 6' wide. you can leave 6" between them and leave them off the wall by about a foot or so.
ardo wrote:
2. I've got sliding closet doors at the back wall, with no way of treating them. Again, I want to make two more gobos & place them about 6-8ft in front of the closet. If I place them this way, do I calculate the mixing position as 38% of the length of the whole room, or just 38% of the distance from the front (West) wall to the gobos?.
same as number 1 - this way you can reposition for reflection points, etc. you also want to take one or two of these and hang them over the mix desk as "clouds". as far as position, about 38% from the front wall and counting the entire room. the absorbers don't really count much as boundaries... one thing to note on your desk position is the one angled wall and then opposite that is a flat wall. standing an absorber about 5-6 feet to either side of the mixer chair may help to create "symmetry" in the space. angle the absorber slightly so any sound that may be reflected is sent back from the mix position.
