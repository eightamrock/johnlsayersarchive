Eric_Desart17 August 2007#7
A flutter echo is some kind of mode behavior (wave, not ray) with a pulse too short to develop as a traditional standing wave. John refers (I assume) to angled ceilings where one has a certain angle between large surfaces. Without having really tested this (hence with some reservation), my belly tells me when you make your first or second design ca 2 by 2 ft (independent units) and you hang them spread somewhat, they will prevent flutter. The (additional) difference in impedance between the diffuser and surrounding ceiling will cause diffraction and disturb the conditions needed for flutter. But as said, not tested. But I think other things play a part here than just the angle of this small parts. You also can't see this as just a series of convex diffusers, since the concave parts work as a focusing mirror (and for large waves even as a pseudo flat surface). I think just splitting this stuff up, hanging them with some space in-between one another, and possibly playing a bit with the suspension height versus the ceiling can possibly do the trick.

gullfo18 August 2007#9
i would agree with Eric on the individual units - the separation between each 2x2 unit should help with diffraction at certain frequencies. that said, John's point about the angle is that the short lengths of the angled surfaces don't necessarily translate into a reflective surface large enough to stop the echos as the sound hitting it will only reflect for a small percentage and the rest will for all intents and purposes act as a flat surface for the bulk of incident angles. you could build a set of poly diffusers which are shallow enough to meet your needs and add trapping to your space. using peg board can give some Helmholtz effects as well.
