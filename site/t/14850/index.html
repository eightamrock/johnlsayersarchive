Cold27 September 2010#4
I think you have quite a bit of potential there. you're not going to silence a 120 db amp, but you should be able to get the volume under control. The ceiling is going to be your challenge. The attic space attaches to the house's attic and is a vented roof system. You can leave it intact and double or triple up the drywall (as long as the structure is strong enough) and pull the plywood attic floor.... Or you could pull all the drywall and turn it into an inside out ceiling. This would give you the potential of more height and perhaps more space for your air spring between the studio ceiling and your attic floor... but this would be a lot of work. Either way, I would have a chat with a structural engineer to see what areas of your structure will need beefing up before hanging a bunch of weight off the ceiling. On a positive note, if you restructure it properly you might be able to lose the support pole in the middle of your rooms. At least your exterior walls seem quite hefty. Steve

xSpace30 September 2010#13
I can tell you that you cannot remove the ceiling joists completely. They are what keep your walls tied together and vertical, also they [joist] help to develop the air flow cavity that the roof system requires. If the roof was actually sealed up [no air leaks] basically making it part of the interior environment this could work, but there will still be some type of joist/girder system in place to keep walls from spreading from the weight of the roof/environmental impact.

gullfo15 February 2011#16
concrete floors will be fine. in the CR you will want to balance the overall room response for critical listening including symmetry. in the live room, asymmetry is more useful and the room response adjusted to the style of music you are recording - tend to live more than dead there. it cannot hurt to get the best ratio within a given volume taking into account your need to fully utilize the space etc. but once you get too small then volume is definitely more important. i would soffit mount the mains but set up in a way so you can add more freestanding monitors if needed.
