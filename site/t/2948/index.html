Your framing is less than ideal for best sound reduction - however, you can make it even WORSE by adding another air gap before adding more gypsum (or any mass for that matter) - do a search here for m-a-m - this will help you understand about best use of mass for walls, ceiling, floor, etc - it's all the same physics, just oriented horizontal for ceilings and floors. Glass - you want approximately 1/3 the thickness of glass in each leaf compared to the total thickness in that leaf of either gypsum or MDF - this balances the mass so the windows don't become a much weaker (or more expensive) leaf. Please read (or re-read) the "complete section" thread, located at the top of this forum's main page under REFERENCE section, for more about proper sound proofing - there are several other threads there that will help on other aspects of building for isolation. Glass - unless you will have a visual glare problem, I wouldn't angle the glass. you get more isolation with wider air gap, so this should be maximised and will perform according to the NARROWEST gap, which will lessen the isolation. Foam - look for insulco semi-rigid in your area, this is better performance for less money, and non-flammable. Check the acoustics forum for more info on where this should be placed - Lighting - yes, dimmers cause noise. Haven't found an exception to that, OTHER than transformers which are bulky and expensive. In my former studio I set up three separate lighting systems - one, just for maintenance, is flourescents behind the gear - then, one par can setup (like stage lighting) using low wattage and colored gels for "mood", and a second par setup using higher wattage (gels optional) for more light without killing the "vibe" - fairly simple, no extra noise, standard wiring materials. It also makes musicians feel "at home"... Power point - do a search here on putty or putty pack , I think it will answer iso questions - if not, ask again... Looks like a fair sized booth, will your gear be in there too or just you and a mic? If any of your questions are NOT answered by the above, don't hesitate to ask again; I get kind of rushed so try to just point to things whenever possible... Steve
