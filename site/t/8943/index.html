gullfo24 September 2007#3
i'd use all the existing products you have to create proper corner trapping and break up floor-ceiling echo. don't scatter the controls around. it will help to take measurements to determine any problem frequencies which can then be treated. you can take the concepts of the bedroom studio to your larger room - front and back wall absorption, cloud overhead, broadband slat resonators and angles to keep the room lively and diffusive. you can build the items as furniture - see John's wall unit page.

gullfo27 September 2007#5
sorry, it just looked like some placements were going to put the absorbers in places less likely to help.... build up your corners with the real traps. this will start to get the bass under control. since you have a nice long room, the front-back walls are less of an issue - put some heavy drapes up to open and close as needed. on the side walls and ceiling - put up cloth covered 703 type absorbers but space them out (on the walls - stagger them so they're not directly opposite each other) and on ceiling space them about 1 absorber width apart as well. this way you're keeping the live space and reducing your echos and increasing the diffusion and trapping. finally, make sure over the mix position and on each side, an absorber for first reflection. once you've got that, you can see if you need further treatments for problem frequencies.

gullfo27 March 2008#8
the key in a control room is symmetry - for your mix position, the rest of the absorption can be staggered it so it creates a nice diffusive space for the "live room". the first reflection points need to be symmetrical because you need to control them evenly but once the sound is past you, the distribution of absorption (at least for mid and high) will be more effective staggered. for bass and corner trapping, put it together so its dense enough to do the job.
