rod gervais29 February 2008#7
First, homasote is lousy for iso any way - so I wouldn't get too worried here - AND- you aren't really gaining anything more (throughout this whole long convoluted install you are going through) than you would have had, if you just installed the homasote directly to the studs - and then the drywall to the studs as well. NEXT - forget this whole screw idea you have - you cannot possibly get enough holding power (without fully penetrating the homasote) that you would have anywhere near a safe installation of the 2 layers of drywall with just the tips of screws holding your drywall to the homasote. Lastly, You said you have my book and read it - then why in the world did you think that you could install furring stripis and that they would perform to create any meaningful isolation from flanking? I explain in the book how critical it is that you use real RC - because simple steel framing sections will not create isolation - example - hat section won't give you iso - although RC-2 will - and yet it has less cross sectional area in contact with the frame than the furring you installed. Got it? At this point in time - just make sure that you install screws sized long enough to fully engage the homosote - PLUS penetrate at least 3/4" beyond the back of the homosote to assure proper installation of the drywall - and count your blessings that you don't have any real issues with iso itself - The green glue will help to compensate a lot for the remaining monies you invested that don't amount to anything much other than an expense with no benifit (no penalty either). You should have asked a lot more questions before heading down this path........... Sincerely, Rod
