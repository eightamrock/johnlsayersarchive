dymaxian7 October 2004#6
Well, pix or not, your question about control room orientation... Sloping 18' to 8'? Yikes. That's a tough one. And if your room is 18' wide, it sloped 10' down over that 18'. That's about 22deg slope. If you want to keep that kind of slope to the ceiling, then yes, I'd recommend turning the room so that mix position faces the low end of the room. With an 18' (or so) deep room, you've got enough depth that early reflections off the back wall won't be so bad, even if left untreated. And with a 24' or so wide room, you sure won't have to worry about the side-walls much. I've seen lots of control rooms where the speakers fire down the smaller axis of the room, and they're not 18' deep. On the other hand, if you don't mind building the ceiling down from that, you could keep the speakers firing the long way. My recommendation there would be to make a "trayed" ceiling, where it's 8' at the sides, and slopes up to 10' (or whatever) in the middle. If you didn't want a flat spot in the middle, you could just mirror the slope on the other side and let it meet in the middle (at 14' or wherever), but that "cathedral" ceiling will focus reflections back down to the floor. If you built it that way, and put good diffusion or absorption up there, you'd have an awesome sounding room- in fact, it'd be a shame to just use it for control and not for tracking, IMHO. =) Speaking of which, is this going to be an all-in-one room, or do you have a separate tracking room? Keeping the stove shouldn't be a big deal, unless it's vents or ducts ruin your sound isolation, and that can be dealt with, too. I'd keep it- like you said, it adds an old-skool flavor to your room. As far as the bass traps go, I can't see 'em clear enough to judge them, and others here will do a better job of it anyway. If they're symmetrical about the mix position, you should be ok. Hope this helps!

knightfly9 October 2004#12
David, I agree with Kase on orientation - if this is going to be a 1-room studio + vocal booth, you will need to consider variable acoustics; mixing needs a fairly dry environment, while tracking works better with a more live room. That sloped ceiling, if not symmetrical left-to-right, will mess up your mix balance so I'd put the desk looking out the windows. Having the room wider than deep isn't a bad thing with that big a room; as Kase mentioned, you've got enough size not to worry about rear reflections until they become un-wanted echoes (instead of shorter-than-20 millisecond phase problems) It sounds like you're not at a loss for DIY expertise (or in your case, DIWF) (Do It With Friends :wink: ) - So building some variable acoustic treatments shouldn't be too daunting, and the only additional cost over regular fixed treatments might be a few hinges and hardware - To keep your window view, you may need to change the existing windows - if they now open, you would need to change that for better isolation if that's a concern. If they are already double-pane, you would get better isolation replacing them with heavy single pane windows. This is because your inner wall should have a second, thick but different thickness glass that's at least a foot from the existing glass - the wall itself should only have paneling on one side of each frame, and your outer sheathing may need to be beefed up to get enough mass for good iso. Here's a basic idea of what you can do for your window - Also, check out variable acoustics ideas at the two links in my post on Oct 8, here http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1462 You will need to build a couple of 3-4" thick absorber "gobo's" to place between your windows and the speakers while mixing, in order to kill the bounce off the glass before it causes cancellations from early reflections - these can be either on casters (easier for one person to move) or stands, and need to be in the range of 3-4 feet by 4-6 feet wide, centered on your speakers height-wise. The high side of the ceiling, as I believe Kase pointed out, could be partially used as a large broadband trap by building a false ceiling frame and filling between joists with either rigid fiberglass or rockwool, covering with cloth, and stuffing more plain spun glass un-faced insulation behind - you may want to see how the room sounds without that first. Hope this helps... Steve
