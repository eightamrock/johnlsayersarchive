The gentleman in the video also said he stuffed the tires and surrounding area with clothing (he actually mentioned underwear) and cloth leftovers. Not doing that and just using bare tires would depart from his "design" radically from an acoustic perspective. I would think the tire concept could work in theory. rod gervais15 August 2008#16
Alex, tell ya what - just cause I like ya - I'll give you my best advice.... tear out the floor and don't put it back. The auralex u-boats are a joke - you have no technical data on then and don't really know exactly how to load them - but even if you did - a floating wood deck is just a big drum head - and can actually create a resonator that decreases your isolation if not build correctly. You can't afford (either from a schedule or a budget point of view) to do this - so don't bother. Wrong can be real bad. Besides which - why do you think you need an isolated floor? You are already on a concrete deck sitting directly on top of the ground (which tends to damp things pretty weel to begin with) in a structure that is alread physically isolated from the buildings around it. If you can here and asked for basic direction on how to get going with this space - a floating floor would not even have been close to making the list. Rod
