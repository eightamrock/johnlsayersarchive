One of the acoustics is so difficult to learn is that almost nothing is what or intuitive. I used the last word because I could not think of a better way to describe it and you can use a translation dictionary to get the meaning. Because of acoustics being non-intuitive, you are thinking without knowledge. This is why so many people build things wrong. A given density (kg/m^2) concrete wall sounds about the same as MDF, or wood, or brick, etc. What is happening is that you getting a more massive wall, which is needed for LF isolation. It also reflects LF more. MORE LF absorption is needed because you are keeping it in the room better. Isolation is a two way system a wall that keeps sound in, also keeps it out. You want LF isolation from the construction and motorcycle as you have described it. Andre Do not think in terms of walls, ceilings etc by themselves. Think of a system.

Ro3 September 2008#95
Adding mechanical ventilation to a air-tight-room is a must, but not hard to construct. You need 2 pipe fans to suck and blow air in/out. The refresh rate (per hour) can be variable. I've got it set to 6. Slower engines (of the fans) will produce less noise and less air movement. Silence is what you want so you prolly need a "whisper" or "silencer" box in your room. There's plenty 'o examples here on the forum on that. I've recently seen a DIY silencer in this forum with pix and all. Go look around. Air refreshing systems need good planning for sure, ask an expert of yer not sure. You need to add pipes for air transport, of course :) silence them with wool. Build a box around'm if you want. Air pipes are prone to flanking.
