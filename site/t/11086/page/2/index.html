AVare18 August 2008#29
Alex: This is an instant course in floating floors. It is physics and engineering. No subjective concepts. Floating floors form Mass-Spring-Mass (MAM) systems. In studio acoustics the spring is almost a combination of the air in the space and the actual srping. MAMs have have a resonant frequency where the isolation is worse than without the floor. The isolation is the same as no floor at one octave above the resonant frequency. Isolation increases at 6 db/octave as frequency increases. For effective increase in isolation the resonant frequency has to be 2 octaves below the lowest frequency where 6 db of isolation is wanted. If dealing with the lowest frequency being 40 Hz (open E on a bass), the design resonance is 10 Hz. 10 Hz resonance requires a compliant deflection of ~1cm. The mass of the fist component is taken as infinity. The MAM formula is f=600/(m/d)^2 where: f=resonant frequency m= mass (kg/m^2) d=distance between layers (cm) Use 500 as the numerator if the airspace is filled with absorbent material. You have already been given links to mass data. Do the calculations. Bear in mind that 10 Hz the isolation will be worse. 10 Hz corresponds to a 2 cylinder 4 stroke engine at 1 800 rpm. If you want to isolate that, you have to go with 2.5 Hz resonant frequency. Andre
