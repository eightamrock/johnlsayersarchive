knightfly16 May 2006#2
1) Is it possible to build a freestanding Drum booth of roughly those dimensions, on carpet? NO. this is the best way to invite all the mold, mites, dust, etc, in the world to live with you and help rot out your construction. When building ANY add-on in a basement, think VENTILATION and HUMIDITY - you want as much of the first as you can get, and less than 42% of the second. 2) How can I best compensate for the carpeted tracking room? I don't know that you can; carpet only absorbs highs and high mids, so you'd need to find something for the rest of the surfaces that only absorbs lows and low mids in order to balance the room. I don't know of such a material. You could SOMEWHAT compensate by NOT absorbing the ceiling, any more than the existing tiles; but flutter isn't always directly audible, just parts of it (like the "boing" you hear when you clap your hands betwee two parallel, hard walls) - there are other frequency components of flutter that will make your recordings sound bad and unintelligible, but that you can't directly hear. And carpet doesn't cover those frequencies much. Much better if you can ditch the carpet, either staining the concrete (if it's smooth already) or adding a wood tile flooring. 3) Are there designs available for lightweight acoustic treatment that can be suspended from the already-suspended ceiling? See (2); plus, your acoustic tiles are already doing some bass trapping and a bit of high freqency absorbing. They're also limiting the amount of isolation you can get by quite a bit; low mass, no hermetic seal... 4) Is there a relatively inexpensive door that I should suggest the landlord put in at the base of the stairs, rather than some hollow-core door. I know there are expensive doors built for such a purpose, but if there’s something I can suggest and offer to split the difference, I’d like to know. A heavy, SLAB type (not raised panel) SOLID core EXTERIOR door with good seals. 5) Are there any obvious instances of “You’re going to need to build this, so you might as well start on that while we go over the rest of the details?” I would think this would be the most efficient use of my time. With those concrete walls, you're gonna want as many corner absorbers as you have corners. IF you search here you should come up with several links; if not, ask and someone will help you out. 6) We’d like to have our instruments hanging up around the studio as well, boutique-style. How much of an effect would a dozen guitars have if hung in front of absorbers, etc., or six or so brass instruments on stands mounted over a bass trap? We really want to have all of our “toys” in plain view, and cluttering up the floor as little as possible, as we’re amassing quite the collection. But will this spoil the acoustic treatment? Or could I actually use them to my advantage? Probably not much advantage; but I'd get some strips of foam (like bed foam, or packing foam) and place them under the strings (NOT at the 12th fret :? ) to damp the strings; otherwise, your room's gonna buzz at all fundamental and harmonic frequencies of your instruments. HTH... Steve
