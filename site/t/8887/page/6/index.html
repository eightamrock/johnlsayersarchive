Little by little I chip away... Seems to go on for ever at this point. I'm doing more trim work as I go along. What is under the clamp along with the next sequence of peices to be put down is the closet door. These 1xn pieces are going horizontally across the entire wall where this door is. I have cut them to fit around the door or rather, they appear to run straight through the door. There's also a photo of my first DIY panel. Just a simple frame. Perhaps I will change these as I go along, but this is my first attempt. I just cut 2 4' 1x4 pine pieces along with two 25 1/4 '' pieces to make the interior a 2x4' frame. I have a few 90degree clamps to house box construction so I put the frame together, predrilled some nail holes and drove long finish nails into the butt joints to keep things together for the next step. Then I used triangles glued to the inside. After those set for a while (to almost full cure) I used my biscut joiner to cut slits or small holes 3/4'' in on the front side at 15 and 32'' points. I then cut scrap wood I keep for strapping which are just peices I've ripped down from other work I've done around the house. I pulled out a panel and it fit right in. Tomorrow I should have time to wrap it with fabric. I'll need to make 2 or three more of these I guess. I absolutely love all the scrap wood I have been able to use in this project. I have had a huge collection of "stuff" for the longest time and I've been able to fit quite a bit of it in my trim work.

xSpace16 October 2008#133
"More specifically, I'm not even sure what type of trim I'duse for this and how to hang it. " It looks like 3-1/4" colonial crown, could be bigger, but looks like 3 inch. We finished a job a few days ago where we had to install 5-1/2" oak crown in a similiar situation. similar only because it was attached to the wall but could not be attached to the ceiling because it is a drop in grid ceiling. What I wound up doing was cutting the side edge of 1X4 to match the pitch on the backside of the installed crown. You'll have to tinker with a piece of plywood, a square and a trim sample to mock up the pitch you will need. The flat side (the 3-1/2" side) placed directly on the wall with the long point facing down towards the floor and the short point side (3/4" out from the wall) create the required pitch to keep the trim pitched out like it should be. Using a laser level we established the trim line around the room. Then I had the guys glue and screw the 1X backing to the wall, at the line. This allowed the large trim to be installed in a uniform and straight manner. When finished this install leaves a cavity behind the trim that you could place rope lighting or maybe some under cabinet type lights to give the effect you are trying to achieve. Good luck with it.

This is a minor detail, but a detail found within an important component for the system of my room. I am going to install handles on the window plugs. This is an old thread so I'll review the detail here for the best explaination. The window plugs are 2 layers of 3/4'' MDF glued together. Similar to this (this is actually my closet door) -
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download/file.php?id=22450
This is a recent photo in which, the window can be seen just above the standing lamp -
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/download/file.php?id=35916&mode=view
I rounded with an 1/8'' roundover bit and this meets with the frame which has two layers of seal on the contact point to reduce air/sound travel. I've got handles for cabinetry similar to this: http://www.amazon.com/Brushed-Nickel-Kitchen-Cabinet-Hardware/dp/B0038NBQTU/ref=sr_1_1?s=hi&ie=UTF8&qid=1305817939&sr=1-1 I'm going to put two on each window cover. If anyone has a suggestion for a better way than my plan, fire away. Drill through the door/window matching the bit radius/diameter to the size of the screw. Counter sink the hole from the back side to have the screw head fit nicely and reach the threads of the handle. Fill the hole with caulking. - - This should be 2 fold: Reduce vibrations from travelling through the plug and transmitting waves from the screw head. Prevent the screw head from coming un done (not really, but somewhat. Access to remove or adjust the screw would just involve pealing all the caulk away while getting at the hole.
