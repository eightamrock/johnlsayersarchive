Fire Alarm Wire on Ceiling (working with a ceiling peice all by myself...) I had to screw a peice of drywall containing greenglue to the ceiling with only two hands to work with. I have my makeshift "T" that I lean against the wall just below the ceiling which acts as a platform for me to screw in on the other end and then work my way to the "T". This peice was a bit of a problem because I want to make sure that I leave a hole for the fire alarm wire that is hanging down. It took me 1/4 of a beer to figure this out, but this is what I did. Used scrap wire tied to the end of this with electric tape. The length extended the wire to the ground. After measuring where the hole would land, I punched a small hole through the drywall with a phillips screwdriver. I then fed the wire through the new hole with the peice leaning against the wall. I then applied greenglue to the drywall with the wire now sticking through it. I lifted the drywall above my head and tried to pull the wire through as I lifted with one hand while my head and other hand held the board above... Some of the wire (through slack created during lift) got glue stuck to it and consequently on my hands. But the peice made it up there and I was happy to have it done. 2008_02_13 023 - Wire set to go. 2008_02_13 025 - Wire through. Pre green glue. 2008_02_13 027 - ready to go: screwgun up on ladder. Edit to add progress: (9:12 AM 2/20/2008) My entire existence in this room right now - Nightly sessions of ~1-2hrs mud, tape, seams and screw holes sanding any imperfections with the skim coat last applied. shine light at 10° angle of wall to expose/form shadows on imperfections circle imperfections for the next skim coat. - repeat. Everytime I do this sort of work, I am reminded of the little details that aren't in the forefront of my mind. Air bubbles underneath the tape for example. This is paper tape I speak of. No matter how many skim coats you put over an imperfection like this, it will always bubble up when the moisture of the mud hits it, so the thing to do it cut out the air hole when it is dry and lay it over a new peice of tape to trace the pattern of your cut peice. Place mud under the hole you just cut out, insert the new peice and then mud over it. - I'm taking my time for this step because I HATE seeing bumpy walls because someone got lazy with it. Plus, I have a distinguished community to judge my work - a brother who can work a wall to perfection within 2 coats for example... so I'd like to do him proud on this one. Plus, I had shoulder surgery a little under a year ago and this is a great addition to my therapy. I don't think I've sanded an inch of my walls with my good shoulder.
