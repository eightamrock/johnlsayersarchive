Cram2 June 2008#114
Things that have happenned since my last post - Completed by my brother - Tile work Complete - By this, I mean it's been finished with grout. The tile is a porcelan marble pattern with a gray/brown color. My brother installed it and did a fabulous job. Completed By my Other brother - Fire/Smoke Detectors - These are located in opposite corners of the room. They will be wired into the house system. Motion Detector - Located in the right, front corner of the room (from entering of the door). Security Panel - This panel will eventually control the zone for the entire basement. Baseboard Electric Heat - connected to the third electric line running into this room. This will operate on a 220v line so we had to run a deducated line. This is simply installed with a panel containing a single dial control attached to the heating unit. Completed by me - Base Trim - I started with the center column support. What's telling is that I messed up with framing this square to the room and the lines of the tile spell this out. MY BAD! Exterior Door Installed - This one leveled and framed rather nicely. I kept the second layer of drywall until afer this step. I was able to level and hang the door to fit perfectly and come in after this step to make the drywall fit as closely to the millwork or door jam as I could. This also included the trunk rubber, stapled into place once I marked out the right depth for the catch of the door. Continuing Trim Work - I've been measuring out my lengths, cutting them and priming these peices before putting them up. Attaching Trimwork Since I'm dealing with affixing the trim to 1 1/4'' worth of drywall, I've been using light duty drywall screws. They come with a plastic peice that expands not unlike a lag bolt would in concrete. I've been covering up these counter sunk holes with wood filler and sanding. It comes out fairly clean looking. This is all in order to keep the trim attached to one leaf or more specifically - 4 leafs as the cornerrs have contact points.

Cram10 June 2008#116
I've been a bit busy lately, but I have taken time each day to tick away at tasks and put in *some* work on this room. I've got tons of trim painted and ready to go. I openned one of the window opennings - cutting through the two layers of drywall I just ran over the openning with during the construction of the walls... So now I am on to frame/trim work for the window. From the face of the drywall for the interior wall to the edge of the steel frame for the basement window, there are about 7.75'' Design Mistake (Are they really mistakes anyway?? ) These basement windows are the type that clip into the steel frame of the foundation wall. If you were removing the window - it's a little aluminum clip at the top that you pull down on and the window would then "vent" open by leaning inward at the top. To remove, just push from the leaning position and lift out of the bottom where the window was resting in the frame. THe problem? - I didn't frame in enough clearance for a proper job of trim work. I am now reduced to the options of using .25'' material to frame in the openning. I've chosen a 1/4'' ply louon material. I cut it to be 1/4'' from the metal frame so I can use caulking to seal it around and against the frame. I've not paid much attention to shimming the trimwork in here - The frame is quite dead on square actually and hopefully the others have been done the same way. 3/8'' glass to replace exterior 1/4'' I absolutely love that my brother and drummer works for one of the major glass installation companies in the area! For $90 he's got 6 peices (3 windows, double pane) of 16 x 14'' peices. This will be the exterior facing window for the neighborhood ( ;) ) Inside The Room I am constructing double layered 5/8'' mdf doors/plugs for the window opennings. I Will have the trunk rubber seal the openning against these peices. They will be kept in place by 4 compression latches like a suit case or anvil case... Replacement Windows At the same time, I'll be making window frames from 5/4 pieces to hold 3/8'' laminate glass. Apparently he [bro] knows they have some laying around the shop that was left over from a job a few years ago, so perhaps this could be a convienient purchase to help me with a window and to help his company clear some space in the shop! Nod for Rod! (by way of Edouard Benedictus...) I love having his book around because when I started thinking about how to do these windows it was a quick read through the window section of the book to read some good info and help me have an informed conversation with my brother. So - Thanks goes to Rod for clarifying why laminate glass helps to isolate near the critical frequency and thanks to Edouard Benedictus for inventing Laminate glass. I'm going to assume Edouard was not a vein man or else I'd be explaining how I was installing Benedictus Glass®!! Photos - 2008_06_09 017.jpg - the rough openning of the window. With beefing up the deck's layer work there was a lot of product used here. I'm sure there are others that, bought in bulk could be had for less, but I was using GE Silicon II:
External image, not preserved — original: http://ecx.images-amazon.com/images/I/31WB2W4APTL._SL500_AA280_.jpg
It's at all the home center stores and worked fine. I did find a store which would package them in 2's as a reduced rate though. Somewhere around $4 per tube I believe. I think the package of 2 put it just under that. Be Aware though: if you're planning on painting over Silicone Caulk, you'll need to get the mixture with an additive to allow for paint to adhere to it. I'm not sure if this knocks down the flex and seal capabilities, but it allows you to paint on it. Update - I've received the insulation panels that will become my diffusor panels. Acoustimac I'll spare people the details but this basic point is a fact - their cart pages are not secure. My wife called up and they even returned my call trying to assure me that it was secure. This is total crap. They have a non secure page requesting my shipping and payment info. It's being sent in plain text over the wire. THIS IS VERY BAD. If you'd like to test it out, simply add items to your cart, enter in bogus address info (to test this), arrive at the page where your credit card info form exists and notice that the page is served up with HTTP rather than [b]HTTPS[\b]. There is no frame or subform that posts in a secure environment for this page. What's worse - they expose my userID, which if I wanted to I could force their page to show other user's shipping information. Normally I wouldn't post anything on this, but the guy was seriously ignorant of this fact and wouldn't listen to my wife or I who have been programming this very type of software for the last decade...
