xSpace8 November 2009#7
Taking notice of partition schedule/legend A1 for the double wall, this should be contiguous, the wall framing/air and the other side of the double wall partition. It is not according to the drawing. The typical procedure for bracing a metal stud wall is to. attach a piece of metal stud at a 45 degree diagonal, from top of wall plate to bottom of upper deck. This is what your print is reading but this is not what you want, as Rod has already pointed out. Most, if not all of the connections to the upper deck specified in this drawing are flanking paths. Where your wall creates the three leaf, due to the wall blocking @ that corner is making another flanking path directly to the outside of the building :) I would also question the (1) piece of sheetrock on the outside of the double framed partition. Why not (2)? The schedule for fastening the sheetrock should be modified as well as it increases the rigidity of the wall when typical construction techniques are used. Those are a few things that came up :) Thanks for your time

xSpace13 November 2009#13
soundgun wrote:
Sorry Rod, what does it mean "to let in bracing"?
Historically, a let in brace is a 1 by material that is attached to the framing after the framing has had a pocket cut into the studs, let in as it were in a 45 degree diagonal placement. These days the preferred method is to install a full sheet of 1/2 inch osb [country specific as to if it is OSB or some other plywood product] into the corners of the framing to add lateral rigidity. These are things that are reserved for the exterior of a building, in general. But the application to this type of build is based on the proof that this procedure works against the exterior elements which are far more harsh. You could, especially since you mention metal studs, use the preferred method for that which is a metal strap diagonally placed. But the vertically placed plywood/osb would make a better uniform base layer and achieve the same if not better results as a metal diagonal strap.
rod gervais14 November 2009#14
In Max's studio - we sheathed the entire inside of the building with OSB before begining the installation of drywall. This is one manner - the let in bracing (using 1x3 wood strapping) is the way I handled it in the Phillipian's........ For wood framing there is also a metal bracing made wich is T shaped - you erect and plumb the frames - then (after snapping a chaulk line for control) you cut a small 1/4" to 3/8" saw blade thick slot into the frame - tap in the brace and nail through the fins into the studs...... They also make (as mentioned) flat stock metal to get the job done. Rod
