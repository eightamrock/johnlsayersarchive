eruss, as I explained - it wouldn't surprise me if a building inspector doesn't know the code - although my guess is that you did a lousy job of explaining the situation to him. Even warehouses require a minimum of 10cfm fresh air continuously to each space within them - and your little recording room and control room do not classify as "warehouse space" They are not being used for storage-= and even though they might be considered an accessory use due to size - the individulal space still required continuous ventilation according to the code. I quote from the InternationalBuilding Code - Chapter 12 entitled: INTERIOR ENVIRONMENT - Section 1203 entitled: Ventilation subsection 1203.1 entitled: General "Building shall be provided with natural ventilation in accordance with section 1203.4, or mechanical ventilation in accordance with the International Mechanical Code." Section 1203.4 entitled "Natural Ventilation" reads: "Natural ventilation of an occupied space shall be through windows, doors, louvers, or other openings to the out doors. The operating mechanisms for such openings shall be provided with ready access so that the openings are readily controlled by the building occupants" Section 1203.4.1 entitled Ventilation goes onto say: "The minimum openable area to the outdoors shall be 4 percent of the floor area being ventilated" OK - so now I have to ask you - what are the net area of the doors and windows in your rooms that open up directly to the outside air? Please note that each room is a seperate "area" and needs to have the requirements of that space met in the code. So explain to me what windows or doors you have in these spaces. I remember your saying that you would leave the doors open if you had to - did you mean the doors into the hallway? Because it has plenty of fresh air? You said it did..... But then again - that too is addressed by the building code...... Section1203.4.1.1 entitled "Adjoining spaces" reads: "Where rooms or spaces without openings to the outdoors are ventilated through an adjoining room, the opening to the adjoining room shall be unobstructed and shall have an area of not less than 8% of the floor area of the interior room or space, but not less than 25 square feet (1.86m squared). The minimum openable area to the outdoors shall be based on the area being ventilated." So if you use the hallway - you cannot have a closed opening between the hall and the room - if the tracking room has a door to the outside that would serve - then you cannot have the control room (which would be relying on that door for fresh air) closed off from the tracking room. So which is it - do both rooms have windows to the out doors or do they both have doors? Or does everything have min 25 s.f. openings to each other? If they don't meet the criteria for natural ventilation - then they have to meet the criteria for mechanical. By the way - just curious - did you see anything there making reference to this being only applicable to high rise buildings? I didn't. OK- so let's visit the IMC...... Chapter 4 entitled: "VENTILATION" Subs-Section 401.2 entitled "Ventilation required" reads: "Every occupied space shall be ventilated by natural means in accordance with Section 402 or by mechanical means in accordance with Section 403." Subsection 401.3 entitled "When Required" "Ventilation shall be provided during the periods that the room or space is occupied" Please note- the mandatory use of the word "shall" - nothing allowing you to supply fresh air only "some of the time" when the room or space is occupied - The mandatory requirement is during the times of occupation - period - no room for mis-interpretation - even you should be able to decifer that one. Section 402 entitled "NATURAL VENTILATION" reads identical to the IBC. Section 403 entitled: "MECHANICAL VENTILATION" reads as follows: 403.1 entitled "Ventilation system" reads: "Mechanical Ventilation shall be provided by a method of supply and return or exhaust air. The amount of supply air shall be approximately equal to the amount of return and exhaust air. The system shall not be prohibited from producing a negative or positive pressure. The ventilation system shall be designed in accordance with Chapter 6. The required rate of ventilation shall be continuous during the period the building is occupied except where otherwise stated. Ventilation supply systems shall deliver the required rate of supply air within the oiccupied space between 3 inches (76mm) and 72 inches (1829mm) above the floor and more than 2 feet (610mm) from the enclosing walls." Table 403 - entitled "REQUIRED OUTDOOR VENTILATION AIR" does establish areas where constant fresh air is not required: Item "c." in the notes states: " "Spaces unheated or maintained below 50 degrees F are not covered by these requirements unless the occupancy is continuous". Ummm......... you don't meet that requirement do you? Oh - btw - does anything here suggest to you that these requirements only apply to high rise buildings? I mean - heck, if it did -= they would probably say so - don't you think? So it looks to me - and of course i only base this on what you have told me - although i have begun to think that perhaps you don't explain yourself or your situation very well........ BUT - you are not code compliant. Because even if the remainder of the building has open ventilation to the outdoors (and if this is the case I can see why the owner spends as much as he does every month - that's a pretty foolish set-up) you do not have the continuous use of that - and THAT IS THE CODE REQUIREMENT - regardless of what the building offical said.......
