I guess I didn't explain myself very well: I'm not saying it doesn't work. (It has lots of mass, so it actually might work) That isn't what I'm saying. What I AM saying is: Why would you bother? You can get equal or better ACTUAL performance (documented), much cheaper and much more easily, so why would you bother? There is ZERO independently documented research on this stuff as far as I can see, so you are on your own as to how it performs at each frequency and under numerous different circumstances. So why would you bother? The manufacturer of some of the products used to make it, makes no claims at all about using his product in that application, yet DOES make proven, documented claims about his product when used as directed, so why would you bother doing int another way? The very same manufacturer points out on his web site that MLV is a very expensive way of doing things, so why would you bother? If you REALLY want better performance than standard construction, and don't mind paying a lot more for it, then forget the steel, forget the MLV, forget the green glue, and just stick a sheet of 1/4" lead in the middle of that sandwich. I guarantee you that it will be hugely more effective than the "poor-man's quiet rock". (Of course, it will cost a small fortune, but it will be REALY good at isolating!). That's the point I was trying to make: Why bother with something that may or may not work, but is way more expensive than stuff that actually DOES work, and has been proven to do so in independent laboratory tests, as well as countless actual studios. Another way of putting it: Why would you want to be a guinea pig, when you even have to PAY to be the guinea pig? - Stuart -

Ted White4 May 2010#15
The reason for the "missing data" is because: - The STC metric that most ASTM e-90 tests are looking to measure only measures down to 125Hz, and many labs charge extra for frequencies below that. - US labs are only rated to 80Hz, as flanking limitations of most labs starts to creep in below that. Test data is less reliable the lower you go. So we build rooms using best practice, although not tested to 20Hz.
