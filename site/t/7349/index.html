sharward24 October 2006#2
Rod's book covers this really well. In short, each wall has its own window and there's no firm connection between walls, although around the glass between windows, it looks to the naked eye that they are. There are great threads and illustrations here on the Construction Forum that demonstrate ways to do it as well... I'd help you fnd them, but I'm on the Treo, making such an effort futile. Also, the glass needs to be SINGLE PANE, preferably laminated glass, and roughly 1/3 the thickness of the total amount of drywall in order to "keep up" with the rest of the wall. --Keith :mrgreen

Creatch, why don't you just take a sawz-all and cut the existing frame flush with the inside face of the existing wall asembly. Them build your interior wall and re-install the existing window/frame within in? Then all you would have to do is finish the spot between the frames - which is easy and clean. THAT is your best solution - without a new window frame - or new windows - yet maintaining max isolation. Rod
