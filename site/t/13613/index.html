Hello, I'm looking for help to make my room better. I've tried to provide the needed information if not please let me know. Thanks for considering my request. Room use: Record small combos and solo vocalists, teach music lessons, practice sax, mix my compositions and recordings. I'd like the mix results translate reasonably well to other rooms. I'd like to have a better situation for clients to hear their mixes. Sitting at the apex of the triangle is OK for one person, but when there are 3 people listening I need a better situation. It's a relatively small room. Maybe the best plan would be to convert from a mixing to recording mode by moving furniture and treatments around. So, when the band is rehearsing to have more space for musicians and when mixing get a more accurate sound. From reading this site, I gathered lots of bass traps, reflective floor with absorptive ceiling were a good place to start. I believe I have that much OK so far. How to best reduce flutter echo? I'm thinking of installing triangular shaped Hemholtz Slat absorber tuned to ~142Hz. Indicated by the purple triangles on the east/west wall in the sketchup. I have a supply of 1" x 12" silver maple boards and several bundles of Roxul available. Maybe these could help solve some of the parallel walls issues? Window / Door treatments? I'm hanging some curtains tonight, but they're more for visual privacy than acoustic treatment. For the French doors and windows I could build inserts, Roxul in a wood frame. The south 3 panel door, I guess could benefit from heavily pleated curtains. Best treatment for the back wall? I assume absorptive panels. Speaker, placement? I think there's a better place. Currently takes a lot of floor space. Maybe I should fly the speakers in, near the ceiling along the south wall? Budget: $600US.
