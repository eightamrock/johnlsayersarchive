Soundman20208 April 2013#2
Hi there "TheFinalSet", and welcome to the forum! :)
Control Room: 20 Long 25 Wide 12 High What are Everyone's thoughts on those dimensions?
That's actually not a very good ratio. The width and height are very closely related. Is there any reason why you chose that as your ratio? Also, don't forget that you will be losing some height to your inner-leaf, so the final ceiling won't really be at 12 feet, but somewhat lower than that.
I am currently in the process of learning AutoCAD Architecture
I would sya that your time would be far better spend on learning SketchUp! :)
It's important to note that we have our own sawmill here on our farm and we will be sawing/planing and drying all the lumber for the building.
Nice! It also means you can make practically any strange size piece of wood that you want! Lucky you...
Now I'm gonna break a rule, but I don't have any actual sound level data to give, and no practical way to get it.
You could buy a sound level meter! :wink: E-Bay, Amazon, etc. They cost just a tiny fraction of what the studio will cost you to build, and you WILL need one anyway, when the time comes to test and treat the room, so you might as well get it now...
I have NO neighbors anywhere close, the closest is about 500 yards away.
What about rain? Hail? Thunder? Aircraft flying over? Vehicles passing by? Loud machinery, such as maybe a sawmill,. or some such? :) Radio, TV, people talking, phone ringing, animals, ... there are many things that can make enough noise to mess up your recording sessions...
I'm completely open for suggestions on materials, flooring,
Ordinary common building materials are all that you need: Studs, 5/8" drywall, and insulation are the basic things you need. The floor can just be plain old concrete: hard to beat that for a good acoustic floor.
If there is a post that I missed that answers my questions, my apologies!
I would suggest that you could read through a couple of the big build threads here, and follow all the links, to get an idea of what is in store for you. I'd also suggest two books for you to reas: "Master Handbook of Acoustics" by F. Alton Everest (that's sort of the Bible for acoustics), and "Home Recording Studio: Build it Like the Pros", by Rod Gervais. And also read John Sayers own work @ http://johnlsayers.com/Recmanual/index.htm. Between them, they should give you a pretty good basis for getting started on your design. - Stuart -
