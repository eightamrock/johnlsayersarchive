I met with two inspectors at my city's building department counter this morning to go over my situation and to seek advice on how to go about correcting it. It took a while to come to the conclusions, but I'll summarize it as follows. This method of transitioning Romex to water-tight flex conduit is bogus.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0399.jpg
It needs to be a junction box. Assuming I have enough conduit slack in the attic to work with, I can fix this without too much trouble. I also need to replace the stripped Romex wires within the conduit with the regular stuff that comes on spools. They said they don't have a problem with the way I ran the refrigerant lines inside the PVC downspout material. They also said they don't have a problem with the fact that the electrical water-tight flex conduit share the PVC downspout with the refrigerant lines. This means that I have some work ahead of me to redo the electrical connections, but (unless Rod rains on my parade ;-)) at least I don't have to rip down that downspout thingy and mess up all the finishing work I did on it. I had been mentally prepared to have to do that, and I'm quite relieved not to have to. It's quite amazing the contrast of feelings I always have before and after my interactions with the building department staff... Before: confused, unsettled, worried, anxious. After: clear, relaxed, comfortable, motivated. I realize this is in contrast to what many people fear or have experienced themselves. In Mom news, she's been home for four days and is doing (knocking on wood) really well. :) Barring unforeseens, I may have some progress pictures to show by the beginning of next week. --Keith :mrgreen:

Hello again, We had a nice weekend here with some cooler weather, so I got an earlier start and headed up to do the much needed "PVC-to-Romex junction box" installation.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_2007-06-05_016.jpg
And, yes, I put a cover on it when I was done. 8) Oh, and in case you're wondering, the red electrical tape on two of the wires is just a marking to distinguish them from the other wires that are the same colors. Unfortunately I had no flat surfaces to kneel upon -- I would literally have to rest my knees on the bottom chord of the truss in front of me and rest my ankles on the bottom chord of the truss behind me, and then after finishing securing the lines in that section, I'd move forward two feet to repeat the operation! :evil: After I came down from the attic on Saturday, Mrs. Sharward was shocked at how my clothing was completely drenched in perspiration after two hours! :x (I should have gotten a picture of that! :P) And let me tell you, after getting cleaned up, I was wiped out for the rest of the day. :( But the good news is that, while I'm not completely done with all the attic work, the worst of it is (I think) over now, as the rest is over the garage where there is no blown-in cellulose insulation to get in the way, where the peak of the roof is higher (and, thus, not as hot), and much easier to navigate to and from. After making the connections in the exterior junction box, I'll be ready to feed the lines into the studio and install the interior unit, plus carry the supply wiring all the way to the service panel. In a couple of weeks I expect to have a fully functioning air conditioner in the studio room!! :evil: That will be awfully nice to have while working on the rest of the build, especially with the hot weather fast approaching. --Keith :mrgreen:
