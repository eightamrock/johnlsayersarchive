sharward4 March 2007#1238
Things are moving along very nicely... The mini-split air conditioner / heat pump arrived on Friday morning.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0395.jpg
I spent much of Friday at Home Depot picking up various things to support the mini-split and I was pumped up yesterday morning, starting work on it at 9:30. By noon I had removed 7 pavers at the back of the house, dug out a few inches of sand, backfilled with a few inches of 3/4" crushed granite left over from the slab base (I knew that would come in handy: ;-)), placed two concrete blocks on that base, filled around the blocks with more crushed granite, mixed a bag of mortar and filled the concrete block holes with it,set the unit onto the blocks, and then set J-bolts in the mortar. It came out perfect! :twisted: BY NOON! :-D
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0396.jpg
After lunch with the wife at a favorite Tex-Mex joint and a long break, I went back out there to work on the connections. Here's where things got a little tricky. There are four lines: power to outdoor unit; power and control away from outdoor unit to indoor unit; and two coppper refrigerant lines: ¼" and 3/8", each insulated with foam. I decided to run each electrical line in ½" flexible plastic conduit with compression fittings. Transitioning from indoor NM-B (plastic sheathed indoor Romex) to outdoor conduit apparently requires a special cable clamp and the sheathing to be stripped off within the tubing. :x 12/3 (+ground) through 20+ feet of flex conduit is hard! :x Pushing 12/2 (+ground) through 20+ feet of flex conduit isn't much easier, either! :evil: And getting all four lines into the multiple segments of downspout tubing was a royal pain!!! :P
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0399.jpg
Then to cover and protect all the lines, I used PVC rain gutter downspout tubes.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0401.jpg
But the outcome is awesome! :twisted: I need to go back out there to actually mount the downspout to the wall and under the eaves and then feed these lines into the attic through the vent. Once I've got that done I'll take some pictures and post them for you to admire. --Keith :mrgreen:

I edited the above post to include pictures from the last couple of days. I had hoped I'd be done with the exterior work on the mini split, but I ran out of steam at about 8 PM. It's sooooooooo nerveracking -- every step carefully calculated so as to put all my weight (and there's a lot of it! ;-)) onto the trusses and none of it on the drywall. That's hard enough to do when you can actually see the trusses, but when there's blown-in cellulose insulation everywhere burying everything, it's not fun. There are pipes and ducts in the way, neither of which would appreciate being stepped on. :roll: It would have been so nice having a helper today. :roll: It looks like I'm going to have to make another couple of trips into the attic to pull those lines in... I might like to someday run some parallel 2x6s on edge and then having some OSB platforms on which to walk and/or crawl. That would help a lot... At least in and around the most troublesome spots.
