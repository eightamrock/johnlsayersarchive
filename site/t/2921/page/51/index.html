Hey all, Mom had a major setback, but is doing great -- breathing really well, regaining her strength, etc. She had actually gone home but was taken back to the hospital (this time by ambulance) less than 24 hours later. What a nightmare... Anyway, she went from acute care hospital to a skilled nursing facility and is now on track to be sent home no later than early next week, maybe even Sunday. Details are on the blog I set up. This past weekend I was actually able to get something accomplished, studio-wise. :) It may not seem like a lot, but it was a little tricky, and I didn't feel comfortable putting it off because I didn't want wasps or birds nesting in the attic. So, this is where we left off a few weeks ago...
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0404.jpg
Behold... This is where the downspot piping (acting as a conduit shell for my insulated refrigerant lines and my 120 volt power lines) enters the attic. There are a couple of pieces that overlap and telescope with each other. This is possible because I actually sliced the middle piece open and removed a chunk so as to allow the lines to exit upward and then inward into the original attic vent.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0630.jpg
I used expanding foam on the end to cap the downspout piping, added a perpendicular 2x4 block between truss chords, used a 22" x 6" wire mesh with built-on flashing to create the new vent covering the area, and then painted everything to match.
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0631.jpg
External image, not preserved — original: http://www.sharward.com/photos/albums/userpics/10001/normal_DSCF0632.jpg
I'm really pleased with the outcome and I know this looks a lot better than it would if I were to have hired an ordinary HVAC contractor to do it without paying a lot of extra cash. Granted, they probably would have just preferred to penetrate the wall in the rear -- but that is something that I did not want to do or to have done. Next on the agenda is pulling the lines further towards the garage from inside the attic, feeding them down into the studio (and the electrical service panel), and getting the indoor unit installed. --Keith :mrgreen:
