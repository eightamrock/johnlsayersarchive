sharward7 January 2007#1189
Back to the drawing board on my electric runs... :( I fell into the same trap that I'm always quick to warn others about: resist the urge to do do do and make sure to plan plan plan. Plans sometimes have to change. When they do, you have to make sure that you have a solid plan before executing the change. I did not do this. Fortunately, I caught myself before getting too far along in the "doing" before doing too much damage. On the previous page, you saw that I had to change my approach to running my electric lines between the service panel and the the room itself, due to an inadequately sized raceway that would not accommodate my four Romex cables. I had quickly come up with an alternate plan of running 3/4" conduit on the surface of my garage walls via a two-gang recessed box that is connected to the service panel via another 3/4" raceway that would be empty for that purpose. Unfortunately, after tinkering with the conduit fittings I had purchased, I reached a conclusion that I would not be able to fit them all through, particularly the angle pieces I had acquired. I also came across this "box fill calculations" page and this "Q&A" page that lead me to learn that the recessed box isn't large enough to accommodate the number of connections I need to make. The bad news is that I haven't actually "accomplished" anything today... But the good news is that my gut feeling that I might be doing something wrong paid off in that I didn't proceed down a path that was sure to earn me a failure on the rough electrical inspection that I am hoping to obtain before the middle of this month. I am now thinking that the best course of action is to remove some drywall from the garage wall behind and above the electric service panel to expose the top of the panel and the bottom of the top plates of the wall, so that I can feed the cables into the attic much the same way as they would have been had the work been done when the house was built. This is a bit messier than I had planned for initially, but there are advantages to this approach:
  • After all the work is done, there won't be any exposed conduit in the garage.
  • I won't have any firewall penetrations to worry about having to fortify.
  • I'll save money in the long run. (Not a lot, but hey...)
I'm kind of glad I didn't post pictures of what I had been planning, as I'm somewhat embarrassed about this... :roll: ...yet it probably would have been educational in the "what not to do" sense. I think I'll cut those holes tomorrow and then get cracking on this new plan that I know will work. Now, if you'll excuse me, I have to take apart my pretty little incomplete conduit run, so that I can take everything back to Home Depot tomorrow... Except for that one 10 foot long 3/4" conduit that I already cut. Twice. --Keith :mrgreen:

Hai Keith, nice to see some visuals of progress! About electrical wires: I don't know code in the US or other parts of the world, but here in the Netherlands THAT's sure not the way to wire up. Code here is: 1xphase (2,5mm2, brown), 1xNUL (2,5mm2, blue), 1xground (2,5mm2, green/yellow) and for switches 1x1,5mm2, black. And all those wires go either in a plastic tube (16mm for example) or we use pre-made wires (ymvk for example) which is protected and molded within vinyl (those are pretty thick and heavy cables for industrial use mostly).
