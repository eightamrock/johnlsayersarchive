sharward20 May 2006#808
I talked to my concrete guy a couple of days ago, and he said that it would take him about the same amount of time to do this job as it took him to do the main slab... :roll: ...so the cost would be about the same (~$350). That's just for his time, not including the concrete or the buggy rental. He's also very busy right now and he hasn't yet called me back about scheduling a date. So, I'm seriously contemplating actually doing this one on my own. I'm glad I didn't do the slab by myself because I know I would have royally screwed it up! :lol: But this one is far less daunting for a number of reasons. First, it's much smaller (one cubic yard instead of five). Second, it won't be visible from inside the room at all. Third, only some of it will be visible at all -- from the garage or from the area between the doors -- and that's only temporary, as I intend to cover these areas at some point. (I'm planning to eventually do one of those epoxy type floors in the garage, along with texturing all the walls and ceiling in the garage.) Bottom line -- as long as it's structurally solid, it doesn't much matter what it looks like... :roll: So, what exactly would I have to do? Well, I'd have to rent the buggy and get it filled with concrete -- something that I can do at the equipment rental place (yes, they mix concrete there). Then there's the challenge of transporting it -- since my RX-8 doesn't come equipped with a trailer hitch ;-), I'd have to rent one of their 1/2-ton pickup trucks and get their full insurance (since my insurance company doesn't cover any kind of truck rental, including pickups). I think I could pour the concrete into place and begin to smooth it out. Then, after the first couple of passes with the trowels, I could return the truck and buggy, then come back and resume the finishing. It would be great if I could have a truck-owning buddy help me with the transport... I've left a couple of messages but I'm not expecting them to be able to come through this weekend (they're both extremely busy folk). I'll probably head over to the rental place this morning, just to lay my eyes on the buggy and to perhaps muster up enough confidence to reserve the equipment for tomorrow if possible. --Keith :mrgreen:

Just got back from another Southern California trip -- saw Fates Warning perform in Santa Ana on Wednesday, followed by several days hanging with old buddies. 8) While I was there I checked out a couple of rehearsal studios. One was partially destroyed by a fire several months ago, so they're limping along in half the space. They're planning a move to a new building and are awaiting gaining access to the space. I learned they were planning to do staggered stud tenant improvements in the new place, whereas their existing place has double frames. They were also going to waste their money on some kind of "magic" soundproofing product woven between the studs (mass loaded vinyl maybe?) being recommended to him by a local acoustics consultant of some sort. Needless to say, I sang the praises of this site and all the great information here. I told him not to waste his money on crap products and also gave him a quick mass-air-mass lecture and told him that staggered stud construction is inferior to double frame. Hopefully we'll see him on here in the next couple of weeks with details of the project he's working on. I found some hourly rehearsal space with air conditioning a little ways away from my friend's place -- $18 per hour, which includes amps, a basic drum kit (which I didn't use -- I brought my kit along). The guy on duty took an interest in my project and has a desire to build his own studio. Again, I sang the praises of this site and Rod's book. When I saw him the next day, he said he spent three hours here and loved it! My friend and I had fun jamming like back in our high school days. 8) Anyway... This week I'll pull the stakes and forms. --Keith :mrgreen:
