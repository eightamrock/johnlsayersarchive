Stick21 September 2006#1058
I'd think that if you were worried about the parallel walls (which you probably should be), and you ARE going to be concerned about the intense volume a band will create (earplugs, anyone?), that a lot of absorbtion is the way to go. Killing flutter echoes with splayed walls is great, but it still reflects all the sound back into the room. If you're going to sacrifice 6 feet of floor space, I'd put a wall of thick absorbtion on the back wall or something, and then a whole lot of panels and clouds all around. And to help with the low end, and more hf absorbtion, do "corner traps" all around where the walls meet the ceiling, so you don't have to eat into your floor space. One thing you do have is ceiling height. Oh, and space your ceiling clouds down from the ceiling more than I'm doing. I think the farther you get them away from the ceiling, the lower the frequency you're going to slow down. And they're a great place to put lighting. Another option would be to do that splay with a sawtooth sort of design along the wall. The same angles, but say a 3 foot section of splay that steps back to the wall, then another 3 foot section, etc. I'd draw it, but don't have the time... gotta a studio to build, you know! Lemme know if you're not following what I mean. Seems like I've seen it in plans around here before too...
