sharward7 July 2007#12
I worry about the prospect of beefing up the foam insulation since it's so flimsy... Usually one would place a considerable amount of pressure upon the inner surface being beefed up, so it seems a rather delicate situation. Also, remember -- it's not the wimpiness that determines whether something is a leaf or not; rather, it's the material's tendency to prohibit the free movement/compression of air when the opposing leaf resonates/vibrates. This is why, for example, one must be careful not to use kraft paper on insulation that is not pressed firmly against a leaf on the side of the kraft. That may be kind of hard to follow, so I'll elaborate: if the air space between two leaves is divided by a thin sheet of paper, the paper acts as a middle leaf, splitting the air pocket into two halves, which has (in theory) an adverse effect on the entire assembly. That being said, sometimes third leaves are unavoidable (or the costs of eliminating it are too great). In that case, one compensates or lives with the shortcomings (or both). It's OK to wiggle around this -- just be aware of the inferiorities and the reasons for them so that you can be as effective and efficient as you can be when steering around them. :-) --Keith :mrgreen:

sharward9 July 2007#17
I think you might not really understand the characteristics of resonance and how it affects isolation. I'm pretty green on it myself... But here's a real-world example I live a few miles from an international airport, and we are (unfortunately) in the take-off flight path of most flights. When a plane is over our house at about 2,500 feet, the framed artwork on the wall behind our bed sometimes vibrates. What I find most interesting about it is that it continues to vibrate, even after the plane has passed and is leaving the area, such that I can barely hear the plane anymore. I don't know if it's the extreme high or extreme low end that's doing it, but it illustrates how something can resonate and become a source of sound, even though the sound that is exciting it cannot be heard or can barely be heard. (Heh -- there's a plane flying over my house as I'm typing this! :lol:) An inner leaf of 3 layers of 5/8" drywall with Green Glue, plus a generous air gap, plus another leaf of 3 layers of 5/8" drywall with Green Glue, is an excellent assembly. The thing I do worry about is the effect of that wimpy, rattly outer leaf. Go outside and lightly tap on that siding and you'll see how a small amount of movement can cause a lot of noise -- multiply that by all the siding panels and you get the picture of what could happen. I do hope for your sake that it doesn't happen if you move forward... I just hope you understand that you are taking a risk and need to be prepared for that possible outcome, and either be prepared to live with it or have a plan to address it if it does happen -- much like i have done with my third leaf. --Keith :mrgreen:
