Soundman202031 March 2009#5
I met with the contractors last night to determine a way for the sheet rock to stop just short of the door jamb,
If you are on staggered studs, then I honestly don't think that stopping short with the drywall will buy you much, if anything. I suspect that doing it like you say might even actually REDUCE isolation, since your caulk density might not end up matching your drywall. You could create a weak spot, acoustically, all up the edge of your jamb, and with the constant movement of the door opening and closing all the time, you that seal might even fail at some point. I'd go with Rod's plan, and take the drywall right up to the jamb to ensure a good airtight seal that won't fail over time. Like Ro said: For a staggered stud wall, better to go with Rod's Superdoor than two back-to-back doors. And skip the MLV: not cost effective. Just build it the way Rod says, and it will work fine. You don't have his book? You really, really, REALLY need to get it! It's the best possible investment you can make in building your studio. Order it from Amazon, and it will be there in a day or two, hopefully soon enough to help you not make any major blunders with your door! Your door will be your weakest point anyway, so it needs to be built as well as it possibly can be: Massive, on a tough jamb, and with triple seals. - Stuart -
