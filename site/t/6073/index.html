AVare23 April 2006#3
What is the main difference between acoustics for a mixing room and those for a mastering room?
A mixing room has a huge acousitcally ugly reflective surface called a desk, or mixer in the worst possible position. Beyond that, a mixing room can have requirements for accomodating many people at once, louder levels to satisfy custoemrs who want to hear it LOUD. A mastering room is usually designed for one worker and posibly one customer (typically engineer and producer), and is designed for super accurate monitoring at a specified level (usually 85 dBa). Hope this helps you. Andre.

Good reverb is tough to actually acheive from what I understand. But you do want bass traps so your ear isn't being tricked to mix in more or less bass depending on how your room is affecting the sound. That I do know. The only exception is if you know your room so well that you know it'll sound great everywhere else. But if you can afford bass traps, why not install them and have the room's response as flat as possible?
