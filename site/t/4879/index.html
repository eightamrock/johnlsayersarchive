retrofitting a stage snake into an adjacent room??

Started by delmag on 21 December 2005. 3 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 4879.

delmag21 December 2005#1
Any suggestions on getting a 16 channel snake into an adjacent room. It is a double 2x4 wall. Should I put an oversized hole through the wall, put the snake through then stuff it with insulation? Will that be a problem area as far as sound leakage? Any other suggestions? Thanks in advance for any help on this.
knightfly23 December 2005#2
Probably get better results by de-soldering all the wires in the stage box, drilling a barely adequate hole, then (after making sure the snake has enough length to go where you want it) caulking thoroughly (butyl or acoustic caulk) around the cable, and re-soldering all the wires in the stage box. This would mean careful labeling of all wires (and additional notes if necessary) but you do NOT want a large hole thru the wall stopped only by insulation, it will let a LOT of sound through... Steve
sharward23 December 2005#3
Here's a thought. Cut a hole in the wall large enough to fit the snake and all its connectors through. In theory, if you feed one cable at a time, the hole doesn't have to be much larger than the diameter of all the cables plus one connector. Then, take a couple of 2x4 pieces, bore a "half hole" on one edge of each 2x4, and cover the hole on the wall with the 2x4 pieces, surrounding the cables with the bored half holes, making a "whole hole" that the cables pass through. Obviously, copious amounts of acoustic sealant throughout. Advantage: easier than having to resolder all the connections, and somewhat undoable. Disadvantage: An ugly block on the wall -- which, depending on where it is done, may not be an issue at all. Even if it doesn't work for your case, maybe it will for someone else. :)
