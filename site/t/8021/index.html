andy_eade22 January 2007#1
So my electrician was over on Saturday finishing up the electrical rough-in (I have the building inspector coming Monday to inspect Electrical, Mechanical, and Framing). As he was getting ready to leave I pointed out the conduit runs I had built between the two rooms for running the two audio snakes, and half-jokingly asked if the inspector might think that was part of his handywork and fail us on it. He paused for a second and then said, "Well, you know, it is low voltage cabling running through there, and so it does have to meet code". I was amazed. I guess I had figured only his part was subject to inspection / code. The lighting, outlets, switches, panels etc. Why is this important? Well for one there is aparantly a limit to how many turns you can make according to NEC, as well as a fill limit. Most of us are doing at least two 90 degree turns for sound control purposes, and in my case I have about 4 more to allow it pass around studs and through walls etc. You can find more information on fill limits here. For now I'm not sure whether or not the inspector will say anything tomorrow or not -it's a roll of the dice if left as is. My electrician is suggesting I reconfigure it and get the turns down to a maximum of 360 degrees. Wish me luck!!! =) Andy

camistan wrote:
I have NO knowledge in this area at ALL. And I have to be honest, I haven't read Rod's book concerning this issue. I just saw this thread and decided to try and get a "jump" on info regarding what's "code" on this topic.
Cami, I didn't cover this in my book - because it was not pertinate to the subject at hand. As I said in the book - there are plenty of good books covering things like how to frame - etc........ and i was not planning on teachinganyone how to do that in my book. But - to answer the question - the building code establishes this..... In the IBC you would find this in the section covering wood construction - specifically 2308.9.11 entitled "Bored holes." Basically - bearing studs cannot have a bore through it greater than 40% of the stud width....... so for a 2x4 stud in a bearing wall that would be a maximum 1.4" (1 3/8"). Non bearing walls allow 60% of the stud width - so 2.1" (2 3/32") Note: The edge of bored hoes shall not be within 5/8" of the edge of the stud. Sincerely, Rod
