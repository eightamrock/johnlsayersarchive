Soundman202010 June 2010#32
I seem to remember a thread a while back, where someone used double-glazing in sliding doors, and ended up with a resonance problem, where the glass was "ringing" at certain frequencies. Not sure, but I think that's what it was. I'm not sure if it is a good idea to use that kind of glass: it seems rather thin, and the air gap is very small. It might work fine for normal houses, but I don't think it would be great for a studio, where the sound levels are so much higher, and you need excellent isolation in the low end. Thick laminate glass seems to be the best. - Stuart -

Soundman202017 June 2010#41
I cant really go one size larger because my walls are 30mm thick so even with the 28mm it will not be as much mass as is in the walls
That would only be true if you made your walls out of glass, or your windows out of drywall! I think you might be confusing mass and density. Glass is much more dense than drywall, so thinner glass is the same mass as thicker drywall. The exact relationship depends on the type of drywall and the type of glass, but it might be something like the glass being twice as dense as the drywall, in which case 15mm glass would have the same density as 30mm drywall. However, you'll have to check with the manufacturer of the drywall, and the manufacturer of the glass, to find out what the density of their products actually is. - Stuart -
