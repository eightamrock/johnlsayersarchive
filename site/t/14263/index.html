Hi everyone. After lots of learning, planning and expert help I've finally learned enough to start the build of my studio. The room used to be split in two as two seperate rooms. I've knoocked down the dividing wall. It is a single story side extension on my house running the full length of the house with a flat roof to the outside. The dimensions are: width: 2.35m Height 2.22m Length 10.04m I'm going to post some pics from before I started then progress through the build. I've got a sketchup which gives the rough idea of the way things are going to pan out although the original plan was to not do a room in room design due to space limitations but that has now changed, at least for the live room which I'm currently building. I first beefed up the existing ceiling (above which is a flat roof to the outside as it is only single story) but then after testing, the beefing up alone wasn't going to give enough isolation so I'm sacrificing space in the live room for isolation. I'm not that bothered about isolation from the control room to outside but if there is enough space, that may go room in room as well. I'm going to see how it goes. The sketchup is below. I know its an unusual design but I've been speaking to some experts from the forum (who I wont name in case they dont want me to) and we decided this was the way to go. It is going to be an inside out construction for the walls and possibly for the ceiling in the control room as well (yet to be finalised). Anyway, it's been a massive learning curve to get to this stage so I want to post the build in the hope it helps anyone facing similar problems I've faced with the limited dimensions of the room. I've also still got a few questions which I'll post as I go :-)

Soundman202020 April 2010#22
Concerning the room in room live room, I've left a 4.5-5 cm air gap between the new room and the existing structure.
Not enough. You need an air gap of at least 10cm in order to keep the MSM resonant frequency down low enough (distance between the leaf surfaces!, NOT between the frames). Unless you plan to use a huge amount of mass on both leaves...
If I put a thin layer of insulation across the backs of the inside out walls, it will take up some of this air gap,
No it wont! The insulation will be IN the air gap. In fact, the entire air gap should be filled with insulation (rock wool or fiberglass) for maximum isolation. If you do not fill the air gap with insulation, then you compromise isolation seriously, losing anywhere from 6 to 15 dB...
although will only be coupled to the internal and not the external structure.
It won't be coupled if you use the correct density, and don't over-fill. You want just enough to fill the gap, or a bit less. It will only flank (couple) if you jam it in too tight, or if you use stuff that is too dense.
This will reduce the air gap which is bad but add insulation and conform to Johns design recommendations which is good.
It does not reduce the air gap: it is part of the air gap, and it is very, very good.
I'm trying to decide if its the right thing to do and if so, any recommendation for the best stuff to use.
Right thing to do? Absolutely! Without insulation in your air gap, the resonance is not damped.
There is some acoustic roll available which is 2.5cm deep which takes up about half of the air gap.
Way too thin! You need at least 10cm, which is one reason why the air gap should be at least 10 cm...
Also, do I need to worry about density with this stuff?
48 kg/m3 (roughly). - Stuart -
