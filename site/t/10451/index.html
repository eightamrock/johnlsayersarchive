jwl10 April 2008#5
Just to extend the above argument, I agree that in most cases panels straddling corners are more cost effective and provide almost-as-good performance as filling the entire area behind the panel (ie, the superchunk method). Personally, I think you are better of buying the same amount of absorptive material, and covering more corners. 4" panels covering all corners in a room (including wall/ceiling and even wall/floor) will outperform superchunks in just 4 wall/wall corners, all else being equal.

An interesting effect of the pricing is that gapping becomes a non issue. People at times consider having an air gap between the material to increase low frequency absorption. AVare11 April 2008#10
myfipie wrote:
use less density when filling the corner. One density does not rule over all products. It depends on what kind of product you are building.
Now that is entirely acoustically true. What with porous absorbers is important is the matching of the surface impedance of the absorber to the characteristic impedance of air (412 Rayls). Denser materials have a higher resistivity as a general trend. So a thin absorber will generally be better with a denser material, and a thicker absorber will be better with a lighter material. Cost has nothing to do with the acoustics of fully filled absorbers. It just happens to be a fringe benefit. Acoustically speaking, Andre
