knightfly16 November 2006#2
If there is the slightest air gap between panes in a single "leaf", it will cause a loss of isolation - the narrower the gap, the higher frequency this "short circuit" will be. IF you try to get two panes tight up against each other, I'd be absolutely amazed if they didn't rattle. I went looking for DIY laminating material a while back, and came up empty - I still think there should be a double-stick tape available somewhere that's available in wide width rolls, that would stay clear and be usable for this (but I've not found it) - if this were available, and if you were careful enough, you'd basically be making your own "safety glass" of sorts - Can't tell from the skp, are you wanting two separate doors one in each frame? IF so, then only ONE pane of glass per door, and as thick as possible. Maybe buy a standard french exterior door, and have a glazier replace the insulated (these days) pane with safety glass? (Some french doors have a single glass with fake "dividers" to make it look like individual panes, that's the type I mean) Isn't gonna be cheap no matter what you do, wish I had more to offer on this... Steve

knightfly29 November 2006#4
IF that HVAC duct actually ENTERS both rooms, and doesn't have at least two 90 degree bends between entry points AND duct liner inside the duct, it WILL cause undue flanking. IF it's just passing OVER the rooms, then an extra layer or two of gyp board (if your framing can take it) would help flanking quite a bit... Steve
