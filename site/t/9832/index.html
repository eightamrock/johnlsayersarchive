gullfo wrote:
i did look at the ESR data. I think that the GIK and Roundfuser products have somewhat different performance ranges (GIK tends to work nicely above 800-1000hz and the roundfuser seems to start lower and tail off lower) so I think this somewhat boils down to aesthetics as well as performance.
The low frequency performance of diffusers is kind of complicated and is often understated or overstated. The two things that matter for low frequency diffusion are size and placement - almost exclusively. Here is a link to a study done at the university of parma on scattering of diffusers. http://pcfarina.eng.unipr.it/Public/Papers/157-ICA2001.PDF Look at the picture in figure 2 of page 2. With this set-up (one diffuser with lots of space off of the floor) they get a scattering coefficient of around .4-.5 at 250 hz. Now according to the D'Antonio and Cox book, when a proper ISO scattering test is done on the skyline it has a scattering coefficient of about 0.02 (that's not an extra zero) at 250 hz. That is because low frequency performance in normal size (i.e. they will fit through your front door) diffusers is all about edge effects. The ISO scattering test completely takes these edge effects out of the question and "only" looks at the performance of the "face" of the product. If you have the diffusers set-up with some space between them you will get extra edge effects and some low frequency diffusion. But to these large sound waves all diffusers look pretty much like simple boxes of whatever their max depth is (more or less). So the difference between an RPG or GIK or Auralex or this Roundfussor at low frequencies is related only to their depth. No magic from anyone here. Now if you cover an entire wall with diffusers, with minimal exposed edges , then you will actually get a response that is like the ISO test. Here again you need depth to go low. Now there is some very slight advantages to be had with modifying some of the number theory sequences.....but, they don't seem to work as well in practice as in theory (darn reality!!!). :) Low frequency waves that are measured in meters or yards just don't notice the (comparatively) tiny stuff going on on the front of a diffuser. lovecow2 January 2008#22
Jason Jones wrote:
The ISO Scattering test does not test for absorption.
Testing for absorption is included in the ISO standard (ISO 17497) and is a crucial step. The method is not the same as an ASTM/ISO reverb room test (or impedence tube test), but it's included in the scattering standard nonetheless. (If memory serves, I don't think the resultant scattering can be calculated without first measuring the absorption of the sample.) FYI.
