Soundman20206 October 2009#47
I hate to say this, Javier, since I know how much work and money went into that wall, but I'd say that your best bet is cut the drywall off the outside of those studs, throw it away, and put some new drywall on the inside. In other words, convert it from "inside out" to "normal". Then you can put up all the stone you want. That's a real tough choice to make, but if you want both the aesthetics and the acoustics, then that would be the best way to go, I think. If even Eric can't think of another way to do it, then that pretty much says it all. The good thing about that is that you'd gain several inches of air gap inside your wall, which is good for improving your isolation! - Stuart -
xSpace6 October 2009#48
What you have to do is incorporate the framing into the stone, the look is what you are after. If may take a few attempts and it may take some more trim lumber, stain or paint that either matches the stone in a fashion or resembles the mortar that you will be using. I was thinking that you could take some 1X2 and build a face trim to go over the studs, stained , painted, whatever you like the best. Sleep on it, because you know as well as I do, you are not going to cut holes in that wall :) But you will loose the inside-out built in absorption. That has to be taken into consideration and could very well be incorporated into the stone pattern/absorption/stone pattern in between the framing bays.
