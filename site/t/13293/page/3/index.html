Soundman20207 October 2009#55
What about that? I guess it will have the same effect than a normal inside out slot wall....
Unfortunately, it won't. The dimensions of the slots and slats are critical. Each slot is basically a tuned Helmholtz resonator, so if you change the thickness of the slat by adding a sliced stone to the front of it, you'd need to also adjust the gap size and/or the depth of the air space behind to compensate, and get it tuned back to the same frequency. But... stones are irregular and rough, not smooth and sharp edged like wood slats. I doubt that you could get the air slug to vibrate very well in an irregular shaped hole with rough edges, so I don't think they'd work as resonators at all, or at best, very inefficiently. I think you'd just end up with a reflective wall with holes in it, but not a functional slot resonator. Maybe André or Eric or one of the other acoustic experts can comment on that. - Stuart -

jbassino wrote:
Xspace, you think we should put a thin metal grid before the stones, on top of the drywall ? or the mortar should stick to the drywall good enough?
I think your stone installer should be able to handle this. Only thing I would add is that you want to make certain that the mortar does not come into direct contact with the sheetrock/gypsum. This can, due to the water in the product, saturate and deteriorate the panels and may change the panels ability to support the stone for any length of time. Especially with large portions of the panel removed. Basically installing a moisture barrier before you install the grout/mortar base.

That looks like what we call "kleefcement" (Dutch), literally translated: "glue cement". This material is also used to fix gypsum boards directly to brick walls (with from these big dots of that glue allowing to line them up on, and flatten old and damaged brick walls). Here in Belgium we standard build houses with bricks (there's a saying here: "every Belgian is born with a brick in his stumach") In a worst case situation, there are also gypsum boards for bad-rooms (water/moisture resistant) on which gluing tiles is standard practice and there are other panels as well which can resist water and moisture better than standard gypsum boards.
