jbassino wrote:
X, I'm sorry, but I seem not to understand quite good your question. Its just a normal inside out ceiling, built by modules by the width of a drywall panel. Each wall will receive 3 sheets od 5/8" drywall and the ceiling just 2. I don't know what other information I could tell you.
Framing material doesn't know inside out walls, it just knows what it can support based on length and how it is spaced apart ;) The reason I asked twice if the measurements are from the outside of the wall or from the inside of the walls is because the load is determined by the actual span from >outside to outside< of framing walls, in your case 3.6 meters or 11 feet 9 and 1/2 inches. A typical floor joist made of 2 X 6 Southern Pine (Southern where I live, not where you live) with minimum 10 psf live load, uninhabitable attic without storage, and 10 psf dead load with boards set at 24 inches (61 centimeters) apart can span from 12 feet to 15'-9" >depending< on the quality grade of the lumber. 2 X4 set at 16 inches(40.5 centimeters) could span anywhere from 11'-3 inches to 11-9 inches, or roughly 3.6 meters. But it has to be a quality grade lumber that your local builders would use for this purpose and your joist spacing would have to maintain 40 cm distance between members IF 2 X 4 was used. 2 X 6 will make >your span< no matter what spacing you use, up to 61 centimeters and would keep you safe even if the ceiling is a bit more rigid. Ref: Architectural Drafting & Design By Alan Jefferis, David A. Madsen Span Tables from International Residential Code (c) 2003
