xSpace14 December 2011#9
If it is OK with John, let me see if I understand the issue. The concern is that you have a silencer box that is connected to the overhead joist and penetrates the interior ceiling. Further you are concerned that it will in someway be coupled back to the interior thereby short circuiting your isolation? Or you are concerned that there is a need for insulation that you had not considered? I agree that mass loaded vinyl could maximize the isolation at this point. But insulation is always part and parcel to what is installed in an acoustic environment so it has to be assumed that it is already there. And then use Acoustic caulk...not silicone. You can also trim this area, assuming the trim does not attache itself to the silencer, and reduce the cavity to the smallest degree...then acoustic caulk...or paintable caulk which ever is in your bag of tricks up to this point.

xSpace20 December 2011#11
"That's why I was thinking of using the silicone as it doesn't dry hard." Yes it does. 100% silicone may be a harder connection than a latex silicone additive. It may to the finger feel like jelly...but it is a connection none the less. If your acoustic caulk is drying rock hard...then there is an issue with that tube/batch. But , just for the record, how long has this caulk been in-place? In any event, a paintable acoustic caulk is still what you want. Granted the siliconized latex caulk is paintable, but may introduce bridging...the 100% silicone is not paintable, and surely introduces bridging. Stick with the product designed to meet your goals, but if you may have a bad tube, bring that up to those that need to know.
