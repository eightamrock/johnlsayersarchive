Stick4 February 2014#1
Hi guys, I'm back after a number of years working in The Stickhouse designed and built with a ton of help from everyone. It's a great place to make music. However, our family is considering a move down the hill from here, to be closer to where my boys will go to high school. (Anybody wanna buy a cool house with a great recording studio near Auburn, CA?) We're seriously looking at building a house and studio from the ground up. So, I need some rough design ideas for the studio. Let's say I've got about 500 sq feet to play with. The shape should be basically rectangular, but it's dimensions are flexible. One end of the building (say the "west" end) will likely be dug into a hillside, with the "east" end out in the light. The tracking room will have a murphy bed in/on the wall so it can double as a guest room, which also means we need a bathroom with a shower in the studio building. At the moment, I'm really only looking for layout ideas. Control room doesn't need to be huge. Me and a couch behind with room for a guitar player to sit near me. I'm leaning toward sliders between CR at TR. I'd like a window to outside on one side of the control room, the other side to the TR. Tracking room needs to be big enough to keep a drum kit set up at all times, as well as space for a vocal and or acoustic guitar set up. I'd a machine room/storage area near the "front" of the control room so I can keep the CPU and drives away from the CR. I think that's the basic idea. I'm just struggling coming up with a solid plan for entry (should happen on the South side) and of course isolation between CR and TR. Any input is appreciated. Nice to be back! -Stick :mrgreen:

500 ft2 is a good size for a home studio, and if you are building from the ground up then you have excellent control over all aspects. Your studio could work out really, really well!
Here's a quickie first thought.
Have you considered turning the control room so it faces the live room, then putting a nice window or glass door up front, for good visibility? Another option might be a "corner control room" design. - Stuart -
