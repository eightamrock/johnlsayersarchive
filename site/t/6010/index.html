Like you, I am in a room of similar working measurements, and side walls of different lengths. Like you, I've treated the room with corner, side and cloud absorbers. And like you, I've had to consider how to address an opening in one of the side walls (which opens up into the dining room, in my case.) To deal with the issues it creates, I've built six stackable absorber units based on John's designs which you can find here: http://www.johnlsayers.com/HR/index1.htm I've found that by closing up the room (and thereby "shortening" the room to about 10' I can create a very dry, controlled environment, and by sliding the rear wall back or apart, thus creating a gap, I am able to allow some measure of "air" to become part of the environment. While it's nice having that option to tune the room, I would prefer to have a bigger overall space to work with. Unfortunately, I am unable to frame in a new wall to accomplish that. You, however, may have that option by either framing in a door in the hallway, thus continuing the side wall to the rear wall, or by placing a stackable, side wall absorber unit in the hallwall, and sliding it out of the way when need. Personally, if it were possibl, I would go with a door. If you were able to fram in another door at the "live room" side, you could creat a nice airlock. Hope this gives you a few ideas on how to approach your solution. Aloha 8)

David French16 April 2006#11
If I were you, I would replace those 2" panels in the corners with 4" material; the 2" panels handle modes down to about 100 Hz, but with 4" you can get down to about 60 Hz. I also recommend adding 2-4 more of these where the walls meets the ceiling. Once you have about 10 of these thick panels across the corners, the returns start to diminish quick. For dealing with things lower than 60 Hz or so, you're going to either need larger porous absorbers or membrane/resonator devices. Also, you should really get that ceiling absorber and the side absorbers away from the boundaries by several inches - it's free bass absorption. I would use the 2" you took out of the corners and use it for the side walls and ceiling. Also, lower the side wall absorbers - center them around the center of the cabinets in the height dimension. There. I hope that's enough to help.

len-morgan18 April 2006#16
Well, I can't speak to your room exactly (I'm sure others will jump in if I say something wrong here), but I can tell you about my room which is similar. In my thread [url="http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4488"] you can see my whole studio layout. At the back of the control room, you'll see a little alcove (which is like your hallway. If you put on some music, it sounds very well balanced until you walk into that little alcove (I haven't finished the room yet so you still CAN walk in there. Lo and behold, the bass level "rises" dramaticaly when you stand there. That's where all the bass is going. What does this have to do with you and your room? I suspect that you are going to get a lot of sound escaping down the hallway but it's going to be very frequency dependent. In other words, your bass may head down the hall, but never come back. You would try and compensate for that by turning up the bass on your mixer and if you burned a CD and played it in say your car, you would have way too much bass in the music. Been there, done that. :-( len
