Soundman202014 March 2010#7
OK, that makes a bit more sense. That's one of the reasons why the "stickies" at the front of the forum are so insistent that folks provide as much detail as possible in there posts. Anyway... :
My thinking was that the 10" thick cement walls, by virtue of their mass will provide sufficient soundproofing for the walls.
They might, depending on what "sufficient" means! What tests did you already do in your room to determine how loud you are right now (without doing anything) and how quite you need to be (after the studio is finished)? That should have been your starting point. Your existing cement walls and existing ceiling (together, not separately) might or might not be "sufficient" to attain the level of isolation that you need, based on how loud you are and how quite you need to be. Before you do anything else, you should install your very loudest setup that you could ever possibly imagine needing in your room, and measure just how loud it is inside the room with an accurate sound level meter, and also measuring elsewhere in the house, when the setup is belting out the very loudest possible sound it can make. Keep notes of all the readings on the sound level meter. Then you start reducing the output level more and more while continuing to measure in other parts of the house, until you get down to a level where everyone is satisfied that it is quiet enough. The difference between the loudest level INSIDE the room and the quietest level that makes everyone happy is the total amount of isolation that you need. The difference between the loudest level inside the room and that same loudest level outside the room, is the amount of isolation that you have right now. The difference between these two calculations is the amount of ADDITIONAL isolation that you still need. That final calculation might be zero, in which case you already have enough isolation from your existing cement-wall-and-ceiling-combination, or it might be non-zero, in which case you don't have enough. So, based on those real numbers (not guesses) you can figure out what level of isolation you have already, and what you need, if any. The cement-wall part of your current structure is probably OK, and the ceiling part of it is probably your weak point, but I'm just guessing here since you didn't provide enough details to go on. In any event, the walls and the ceiling are not two separate things: they are two parts of your existing isolation. You can't really talk about them as though they are separate: the aren't. The isolation of your room depends on the combination of all factors, not just your cement walls. For example, if you have another room on the other side of that cement wall, and the ceiling is just simple wood flooring on the same set of ordinary joists, then the cement walls are not doing anything right now: All of the sound is simply flanking around the walls through the ceiling. It wouldn't matter if those walls were 10 inches of concrete or a single sheet of drywall: the isolation would be about the same. In other words, treating your ceiling without treating the walls would not be smart: they are components of a system, not separate items. At some point the new ceiling has to meet the walls, and if there is flanking around that joint through the walls, then the new ceiling is not going to do anything at all. Even worse, a tiny crack in that ceiling-wall joint, just a one mi limiter wide and a few dozen centimeters long, would be enough to destroy your isolation. Walls and ceilings work together to isolate, not separately. The way you would build an MSM system is to build four new self-supporting stud walls around your room, totally separate form the existing four cement walls, and put your new ceiling across the top of those walls, with no connection at all to the old ceiling. The new structure does not connect to the existing structure at any point (except the floor). Think "room inside a room". Put a couple of layers of drywall on the new frame, seal it all air-tight, decorate to taste, and you are done! (Except for the acoustic treatment, of course: but treatment and isolation are two different things anyway...) - Stuart -
