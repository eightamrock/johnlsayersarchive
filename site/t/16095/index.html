gullfo22 May 2011#2
welcome! the foam may be part of the problem making things dead in the HF and no reasonable absorption for mids and lows. since isolation isn't a concern, then treatments should focus on corners (wall-ceiling are also corners) and using thicker porous absorbers scattered on the ceiling and walls will help balance things. using some absorbers on first reflection points on the right-hand end of the room and centering your mix position there and set back from the right wall starting around 38% of the room length back, will get you started. using simple frames, rigid insulation, and cloth will get you a decent number of absorbers built. take off foam and leave some hard surfaces and then stack some of the foam in spots to increase it's depth - this will help extend your budget.

gullfo20 June 2011#15
i would finish the concrete (?) floor - polish and stain - then use scattered rugs or isolation platforms under drums and amps. on the doors - make sure the exterior and interior doors seal really well - not gaps around their frames. on the interior door - if it's mass is not equivalent to the wall, consider beefing it up with MDF (19mm) and also putting a cloth covered absorber (25mm) so it very lightly contacts both doors (or not at all) to dampen the air cavity. on the inside room - an absorber on the door (100mm) will work, no need for a poly on it. you could put a poly across a corner etc. the goal is a frequency balanced and lively room. you'll still find anomalies in the modes etc and you'll still position equipment and instruments where they sound best but the overall room balance will be better and so musicians in the room will find it much nicer to work in.
