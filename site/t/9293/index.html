kendale24 September 2007#11
Aloha, Looks like a neat room. Got any pics? :D A few thoughts - - At a glance it appears that the mix position may be in or close to a null = no bass. Try positioning your seat about 64.98" (5'4") from the front wall and see if there's an improvement in bass response and then fine tune it from there. - How far from the floor are your ears when seated in the mix position, as there is another null that occurs on a vertical plane as well. Avoid the mid point of floor to ceiling measurement – either sit higher or lower if necessary Here are a few bits of information I’ve collected along the way that may be helpful. Use what is applicable and file the rest for future reference. :wink: • Soffits angled 30 deg. in from a flat wall result in speaker baffles being perpendicular to the two legs of equilateral triangle comprised of two speaker centers and a point about 10" behind your head • The baffle extension (the face of the soffit) should be as large as you can make it, up to about 45" in each direction from the woofer in a system with a LF cutoff of around 45 Hz - There's a formula based on LF cutoff that ensures full 2 pi response for the woofer if the baffle is at least the size obtained in the formula's solution - • Monitors should NOT sit on the console but behind, placed on heavy decoupled stands - console placement causes early reflection problems in most cases, unless the console is slanted down in front a LOT. • Distance to wall behind speakers will change response drastically - there is a wall bounce calculator on the Acoustics forum that's fun to play with (needs Excel) • Moving either speakers or your head by as little as an inch or two can sometimes make drastic changes in response, due to modal peaks and dips. • Left/right room symmetry (at least within a foot or two of ear height) is important for good stereo imaging. • Roughly 38% of total room length from the front wall • Monitor angles 60 degrees • Monitor height – avoid mid point of floor to ceiling measurement • Angled side absorption panels – left/right of mix position • Side walls 6-12 degrees Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

gullfo30 September 2007#16
the 45hz probably needs a panel trap to get that low. the 105hz should be treatable with a slat resonator, or you could use a panel trap. presumably there is a peak at 105hz and you'll want to find it and put the trap there - most likely your ceiling. the advantage of the panel trap is its thinner than an equivilent resonator at low frequencies. Check Ethan Winer's site for details on constructing panel traps.
