jwl12 October 2007#3
I'm no good with sketchup, so I can't help you with drawings. But I do have a few ideas. I'd put the desk about where the drum kit is now. Ideally, the mix position should be 38% back from the wall opposite the doors. I'd experiment to see where in the room the drums sound best. I'm guessing one of the corners by the door, but experiment. Go around the room hitting the floor tom to get some low-frequency energy happening in the room. There will be a convenient spot where the low end just seems to fill the room. That's the spot. Put the floor tom down and set up the drum kit around it. I'd put the bass amp in another "sweet" spot as described above, and then arrange the rest of the amps/instruments so that people rehearsing/playing can hear each other well. As far as treatment, since you don't want to do any additional construction I'd start with the standard, broadband absorbers in the corners. These can be superchunks, 4" panels straddling the corners, etc., or you could go with moveable panels, something like the Mondotraps from Realtraps (or others like it). I'd also make sure there is a RFZ at the mix position. Then, I'd probably make some portable gobos that you can move around the room, placing them where you need them at that moment (ie, your RFZ panels could also be gobos, when tracking put them around the drums and/or between amps, when mixing set them up for a good listening position). This kind of setup can sound really good and give you a lot of flexibility.

What are "gobos"
Gobos (go-betweens) are partitions used to help isolate between instruments, amps, drums, etc in the studio. Often times are movable panels with absorption covered in material.
