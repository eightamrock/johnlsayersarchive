Soundman202012 July 2014#2
The specs for RC-8 say you can hang up to two layers of 5/8" drywall. The density of is about 600 to 800 kg/m3, so 5/8" drywall weighs about 12kg/m2. Plywood density is anywhere between 400 and 600 kg/m3, so 3/4" plywood would weigh about 11 kg/m3. So very likely you would be fine doing one layer of 3/4" plywood plus one layer of 5/8" drywall, and still be inside the total allowable load. But don't take my word for it: Call Auralex and ask them; they manufacturer it. - Stuart -

Soundman202013 July 2014#4
I want the wood ceiling and the acoustics it provides.
There's practically no difference in the room acoustics from using drywall or plywood on the ceiling. They have similar mass, similar stiffness, etc., so there isn't much difference in the way the room will sound internally. In any event, for the majority of studios, the inner leaf ceiling should be "hidden" from the room itself, with thick absorption. If not, then the combination of hard ceiling plus hard floor leads to major potential issues with flutter echo and vertical axial modal problems. So there's no reason at all to prefer one material over another for the ceiling, acoustically, since it will not have any effect on the room at all, in the end. Plus, drywall is usually less expensive than plywood, and more dense. There's also the issue of how you would manage to seal between the sheets of plywood: drywall is designed with that in mid, while plywood is not. Airtight seals are critical for isolation.
Also I will have the STC I need with one layer of plywood.
You should never use STC for judging studio isolation! :shock: STC does not take into account the entire low frequency end of the spectrum (or the high end either, for that matter). It was never meant to be used for estimating full-range music isolation. It is meant for the human speech range, as well as typical house, office and school sounds, but not for music. It only covers the rather small band from 125 Hz to 4 kHz. So it completely ignores the bottom two and a half octaves of the human hearing spectrum, as well as the top two and one half octaves. You should never trust an STC rating, when it comes to studios. A wall might be rated at STC 50 but provide terrible isolation for a studio, while another wall rated at only STC-40 would do much better. There is also no relationship between STC and actual decibels of transmission loss: For example, STC-45 does not mean that it will be 45 dB quieter on the other side of the wall. STC numbers have no units: they are just single-number indicators. Also, I thought you lived close to an air-force base, with fighters rattling your roof regularly? A single sheet of plywood won't do much for that. - Stuart -
