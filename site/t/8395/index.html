For the sake of clarity, and for anyone following this thread, here's Rod's answer to a similar question:
rod gervais wrote:
Hugo, If you do it exactly the way you have detailed - with the air between the walls open to the space above......... then it isn't going to be a 3 leaf system. One of the things required for a leaf to be a leaf is the air acting as a spring - and thus the cavity must be sealed. Even if the outer room was a completely sealed cavity - the volume will be large enough to never effect the wall assemblies. Rod
In my case, I'm talking about an 'inner' room: my intent is to build a room smack dab in the middle of a 2-car garage, coming in from the garage walls about 6-12 inches. I have to assume now that the air won't be acting as a spring, as there is a large volume of air surrounding the room that can 'flex', if you will.

drfrankencopter18 March 2007#15
If you check the BBC paper, you'll notice that the 3+ leaf effect diminishes greatly when there is increased air space between the leaves. The effect is really strong if you're building a 'thin' wall, but is greatly diminisheb by the time there is about 0.5 meters (1.5 feet) between the outer leaves. Check out http://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rd/pubs/reports/1995-06.pdf and compare the constructions in the middle of the document against those at the end. Cheers, Kris PS: Do note, however that the 2 leaf system still had better performance at all times.
