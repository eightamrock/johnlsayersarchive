Hello Folks. What an awesome resource you have put together here. This is my first post so I will do my best to follow the "rules". I am taking an existing space that I have been using as a studio in my basement and expanding the space adding an 8'x9'-6" pre manufactured Vocal Booth. I do intend to on occasion mic up drum kits and amplifiers, but for the most part I am using software instruments. My main goal is to have a really nice room to mix in. Eventually 5.1 will enter the picture. Currently I am working with several different trades, electric, plumbing, HVAC etc. to determine costs. Beyond these expenses, I would like to keep the budget to around $5k. I am having all new electric (dedicated ground rods, star diagram, etc) run for the space, will be moving a Hot water heater, and a trunk of my HVAC will be moved to the back of the space over the existing sump pump. The main HVAC system can not be moved. I will be installing all new hardwood floors over top of an existing concrete slab. The existing space has 1/2" drywall over 2x4 wood studs. The existing room is under my dining room which rarely gets used and the floors on the second floor above the studio are hard wood. I would prefer to keep my mix position below this area of my home as I believe it serves my families quality of home life best. The ceiling height is approximately 7' tall. It does tend to get loud on occasion when tracking and mixing. I do try to keep the levels in check by using a meter occasionally but often it gets cranked up. I am hoping the vocal booth will serve as the space that will keep the noise of the family above out of the recordings and vice versa. I was hoping that I could get general feedback on the layout of my room. All of the furniture and equipment is already purchased. I have spent several days looking at various potential layouts and believe I have found the one that makes best use of the space for flow and comfort of engineer and clients. Beyond the overall layout, I think this site has everything else I need from a standpoint of reference material. Your professional guidance would be greatly appreciated. I am including my basic design as well as photos of the existing space and the new area that I will be going into. Thanks in advance, Ross Hancock Maryland, USA

John Sayers13 December 2006#6
yes - your new layout is better - you can tuck your speakers under the aircon ducts. It also gives you a longer length v wide ratio in the control room. I wasn't sure if you'd outlayed the money for the booth as you could probably build your own for cheaper. I'd imagined the jackpost to be hidden between the double walls which could be either sliders or a door/window system. My main point is that the proper angled walls in the control room is better than a square box. I'm not sure what you meant by the entrance as I put the entrance in the same place as you did. cheers john
