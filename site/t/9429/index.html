John Sayers19 September 2007#12
You don't necessarily remove the beam you just need to add another one next to it - it's not hard to do, I've done it in a couple of US basement studios, it took less than a day - even as Glenn says if you remove just one of the posts you will be much better off. You just need to get a structural engineer to look at the problem and give you a certified plan to alter it. Attached is a pic of a new beam next to the old beam. cheers john

xSpace19 September 2007#13
To tweak Johns idea a bit, we have used glu lams or lvl beams in a situation like yours, and cut the joists to allow the beam to sit flush with the top side of the joist system. Yours reads like you have 4) 2X12's which, for a 10 foot span, is large. So I wonder what is sitting on the beam on the >first< floor and what is running in between the overhead joist bays, in example, anything that would make it more of a pita then it really has to be. But you would get a smooth ceiling line to develop upon. Is the open area in the picture a garage door opening that would allow a large and cumbersome beam to be brought in by?
