Started by majortom on 21 July 2006. 6 replies.

Originally posted at johnlsayers.com, topic 6680.

majortom21 July 2006#1
Hey folks, I have a small project studio in New York City. I'm renting in an office building, so heavy construction is not an option. My room looks a little like the outline of a stealth bomber. I'm trying to find a good spot in the room for my mixing station, a good spot for tracking and some recommendations on acoustic treatments. I record "indie rock" music using a 1 watt Emery Sound Microbaby and a VOX AC30 (which I usually have to pipe into a Palmer DI), so it's loud, but not Marshall stack loud buy any stretch, maybe 85-90db. I mix at 85 db max, but mostly lower like 75-78db. My neighbors along the upper wall have not complained... too much. I mostly track on weekends when they're not there. Right now, my listening position is about in the middle of the room facing the bottom wall and I'm noticing that I have turn down the left monitor a little to get stuff that's supposed to be centered to actually sit in the center. I was thinking of moving the 5x5 DIY vocal booth from position V1 to V2 to free up some room for tracking. It's made of 3/4" plywood and the inside is covered with 4" acoustic cotton. When the door is open, it's essentially a giant bass trap. I was thinking I would do most of my tracking in the 12'x9' space along the bottom of the picture. I got some input from Ethan Winer and Bryan from GIK over on the gearslutz forum and they thought my mixing station could either be about 8 feet up from the bottom wall, facing the bottom wall. Or possibly facing the glass wall, not sure about where exactly. I thought 6 feet from the top wall facing the glass, about 6.5 feet away, might be good. The glass wall was a concern in terms of symmetry. It's not all glass, there's a 2.5 foot shelf and some metal frames surrounding the windows. And the long diagonal room mode from the top-left to the bottom-right was a problem, if I understood correctly. A couple of folks suggested I post here for advice. To work with, I have 6 GIK Acoustics tri-traps, 2 GIK panel traps, 3 "gobo-ized" Realtrap mini-traps, a bunch of 2'x4' 4" thick sheets of acoustical cotton mounted on cardboard backing, and some Auralex diffusors, but I'll buy more treatments if need be. I'm not afraid of a little light construction, but movable gobos, traps on stands, etc. would be better. BTW, the ceilings are 11' high. In summary, I'd like to identify a good spot for mixing, a good spot for tracking and any necessary acoustical treatments. Thanks for your time. :)
