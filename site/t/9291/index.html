Aloha and welcome to the forum, You are in the right place. :D A couple of excerpts to consider:
the first thing you must do is eradicate all the parallel walls in a studio design. I believe that a wall must be at least 12 degrees off parallel to stop parallel wall standing wave interference. That's either one wall at 12 degrees or two walls at 6 degrees each. If you can afford to make the angle bigger, do so. Also you can create angled walls within a rectangular room by adding acoustic treatment. http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension. http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
That said, could you provide some info that would be helpful for us: - what is the overall dimensions of the space you have available? - What is your ceiling height? - How large a band do you intend to record at one time? Looking forward to your build, Aloha 8)

I was planning on having the live room a fair bit bigger than the mix room. Would that be possible or is what you have done the best according to the maths?
If we were to make the ceiling for the control room lower, we can also scale down the overall dimensions proportionately. However, here are a few things to consider:
So what size should a control room be? These days people are building bigger and bigger control rooms because so much happens in the control room now. Often the bass player and keyboard player actually sit in the control room. I often have the vocalist singing in the control room while the bed tracks go down, so a good size control room is a good idea. So many people think that you need a big studio and end up putting a poky little room at the end and call it a control room. I suggest that a control room ideally should be at least 6m x 5m with a minimum ceiling height of 2.4m. This size room means a good sized working area with space for the musos, friends and hangers on. Also because you don't want rear reflections to interfere it is better to start with a longer front to back dimension than the side to side dimension. i.e. 6m x 5m. The studio, on the other hand, requires a different set of considerations. The first to consider is do you want just one room or more? The trend nowadays is for more than one studio so the you can get isolation between players and different acoustics for each room. The acoustics you want for a live drum sound is totally different than you want for a vocal for example. I recommend at least 2 rooms. You can put drums in one, guitars in the other, keys , vocals and bass (DI) in the control room or maybe the band in one and the vocalist in the other. Getting separation between guitars and drums is usually OK in a good sized room but keeping them out of the vocal track is hard if they are all in the same room. Just a note here - the kitchen is an important room to consider. Gallons of coffee and tea are drunk in studios. An engineer was once asked - how do you normally have your coffee? "Cold with a fly in it !! "- was the reply. Also a kitchen allows for a fridge for the beer and a microwave for the pizza, vital ingredients in every album recording session. Toilets (bathrooms for you in the US) are also a vital service a studio should offer. The coffee and beer has to go somewhere. http://www.saecollege.de/reference_material/index.html
I also forgot to take in to account the walls I build. The room I have will need some soundproofing both to protect my neighbours and to keep sound out. I'm guessing each wall dimention will have to have 12 inches taken off to cater for the wall I'm gonna build.
The Mass-Air-Mass principle is what is prescribed on this forum to achieve isolation. Depending on what your outer layer is, it could look something like this: outer layer (plywood/brick/concrete) - air space with with insulation - 2-3 layers of drywall/plasterboard/Sheetrock. The interior space is then acoustically treated with bass traps, absorbers, resonators, etc. (adding even more dimension to factor in.)

Aloha,

Which way the door opens is up to you, but having them swing out will save you a little space, especially in the vocal booth area.
What can I do about the modal response?
Using the ratios mentioned should bring you into the ballpark and give you a good starting point. Proper acoustic treatments will then help fine tune the room.
The live room seems a workable size now.
Cool. :D
Would it be best to have the desk facing the opposite direction so that any noise from the monitors doesn't get through to the mic in the vox room or would that be worse?
One benefit of facing the vocal booth is visual contact with your vocalist. If done properly, you should be able to isolate the vocal mic from the monitor volume. One could conceivably lower the control room volume if there is too much bleed into the vocal booth.
What height should I set the control room ceiling with the new dimentions
The room dimensions are 8’ x 9’1’ x 11’1”, and with proper treatments you should be able to obtain a good sounding control environment. As an added design point, angling the ceiling upwards of 12 degrees or so will help reduce early reflections at the mix position, as would a set of cloud absorbers.
and where do we go from here?
If you're satisfied with design phase, you could begin to look at the building codes in your area, an architect to draft you a working set of plans (that meet code) a material list and cost and perhaps a GC to implement the plan if you choose not to DIY.
