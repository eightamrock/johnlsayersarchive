rod gervais14 January 2009#14
OK, more than a couple of things to deal with here........ To begin, The American Society of Heating, Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning Engineering (ASHRAE) recommends (in its Standard 62-1999, "Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality") that homes receive .35 air changes per hour, but not less than 15 cubic feet per minute (cfm) per person. Therefor I set the MINIMUM at 15 cfm per person...... 10 to 12 exchanges per hour is getting a wee bit on the wild side of conservative. If you want to push that a bit feel free - but double that would still only be slightly less than 1 complete change per hour. Remember - you are exhausting tempered air - and whether it's winter or summr - that is a lot of energy you're throwing away when you get into things like 10 to 12 full exchanges. Every cubic foot of air you bring into the building has to be either heated or cooled - depending on the season. Now - the trick is to maximize volume while minimizing velocity...... so to begin with you are much better off with the largest duct (within reason of course) for your initial runs - if you could go immediately from the 6" connection at the fan and increase to 8 or even 10" - you are ahead of the curve.... the final run into the room is always nice if you can use an iso-box - at which time you want a minimum of double the area of the supply - which will decrease the veolcity by 1/2 - while maintaining the volume. Your choice of duct is wide open - although I do not especially love the flex ducts - this because it's hard to install them without having areas sag - which causes back pressure and increases velocity - thus working the fan harder with more potential for noise..... BUT - if you do a real nice job and don't have that problem - they work fine....... again I point out that the larger duct you use the better off you are. Generally you don't have an excess of pressure loss (static pressue) when running 25 or 30' runs - over that you really should run some calcs to make sure you aren't overloading the fans...... every foot of duct adds SOME back pressure to the fan unit- which in turn means it delivers less air flow than the design with 0.00 psig static pressure (measured in water pressure) Look at the flow rate chart for the fan you plan to use....... steady for the first .05 inches of static pressure at 400 cfm..... but it drops like a rock from that point on - the supply is down to 200 cfm by the time you hit .9" of static pressure - and only 5 cfm at 1.4" static. http://www.suncourt.com/CentraxAirflowGraphs.html So short runs or oversized ducts are required to maxamize delivery. I hope this helped, Rod
