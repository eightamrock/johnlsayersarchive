BF10 July 2007#1
Thank you to those of you who responded to my previous post. I think the B&Ws are going to turn out amazing in well designed soffits. We have been in the new house long enough to get all of the essentials done. While I still have the master bath to tile, we have decided that a project with a little more fun at the end of the tunnel is in line (well that and I’m sure the misses will appreciate the studio living in boxes in the loft being relocated). The studio will be consuming a good portion of the basement in a relatively new home (built 2002). The basement is constructed of concrete block, and has remained very dry with several good bouts of rain. None the less I will be installing a battery backup for the sump pump. The goal is to build a good sounding and comfortable space for sequencing and tracking solo work, while minimizing transmission of sound throughout the rest of the house. The majority of my instrumentation is sequenced outboard gear with a bit of DI bass guitar. While the levels of a performing band will not be an issue, respectable levels for monitoring will be desired. I have read Rod’s book a few times (top notch resource by the way), that combined with the reading I have found within and linked to in this forum I am feeling comfortable to start putting a design together. I have torn out the previous owners half attempt to finish the basement, and not pretty much have a clean slate to work with. The water line, sump, low hanging duct and a rough in for a future bathroom made finding the best placement for the room a little tricky, but I think I have found a good way to fit things in. I have decided on a single room build with a separate machine room to house my workstation, amp, power supply for the console, UPS and other equipment that creates noise while running. Lines will run from the machine room to the studio / control room to connect to the desk, interface and displays & keyboard. I am looking at a room within a room, double wall design with the exterior block wall being used as the second wall. The joists hang to 7’7” with the basement ceiling at 8’6”. I should be able to run ceiling joists for the room between the floor joists maintaining isolation from the structure everywhere save the concrete floor. This should allow a space based on Louden’s 1:1.40:1.90 of 7’6” x 10’6” x 14’ 3”. I know, a higher ceiling would allow for a greater space but this should allow ample square footage for my needs. A friend who rehabs homes and I intend to do most of the work ourselves. My little cousin is an electrician, so I have that base covered… and a friend who’s uncle works in sales at the Owens Corning plant (30 min away) so a potential discount there. I don’t have a set budget at the moment, but have been planning on and saving for this for awhile and have a lot of unused PTO to burn. An HV/AC duct runs between two joists above the intended space (this is an area where I am not as confident) so I am hoping tying this into the room will be an option. I am a little concerned about this piping sound to the living room above however. Building for isolation then piping sound right upstairs would be a waste of time and resources. I feel very green on sketchup, but I have tried to put together a representation of the basement and the intended placement of the studio and machine room. Tab one is an overhead of the basement with notes, tab two a 3d attempt showing HV/AC, water line, rough in and sump, and tab three is the intended placement of the studio and machine room. If anyone sees a better placement of the space please let me know. I am intending to work on a more complete sketch of the room design itself this weekend. I’m happy to finally be moving forward. Thanks for your time and comments. BF

gullfo23 July 2007#13
generally speaking, you want the speakers spread about 70% of the front wall width, and an equilateral triangle on 60 degrees between the speakers and listener - with the focus slightly behind the listener head. for example, if you have a 10' front wall, then you'd want the speakers to be about 7' apart and the focus point also about 7' so the listener is seated at about 5'6" or 6' - depending on console etc. then you want to consider that the speakers should not be perfectly centered on the soffit but also offset some value not evenly spaced to the room dimensions, and also to have the listener at about 38% of the length of the room... you want the listener and/or monitors at 43% of the room height or otherwise you may be in a strong room mode location... there's a lot of things to consider when placing the speakers...
