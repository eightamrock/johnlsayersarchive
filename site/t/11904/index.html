Well, at that level your entire body would be experiencing intense, excruciating pain, you'd be suffering from extreme nausea and vomiting and other slightly less pleasant symptoms, as you writhed on the ground, screaming in abject agony. any idea how far 150+ db goes?..tilll it gets to 60db..
Well, to get that kind of level you'd need a really large line array or a massively huge stack (which acts as a line array anyway), so we are talking about a cylindrical wavefront here, not spherical, and therefore the level drops off at 3 db per doubling of distance... So 10 kilometers away (6 miles) you are down to about 94 dBA, and still roughly ten thousand times too loud... You need a bigger piece of land! ( Probably something about the size of Antarctica should do it... ) :) - Stuart - (PS. Point of reference: When they fire it, the 120mm canon on top of the M1-A1 tank (that big long round tube thing sticking several meters out the front) produces a peak sound level of around 143 db, measured 50m away...) (PPS, in 1997, researchers fitted twenty 12" 1000 watt woofers into a minivan, and feed them with 19,000 watts of pure sine wave power. They measured 156 dB inside the van.) (PPPS. I also found a reference to an unidentified 1983 rock concert which used ten massive stacks of speakers, and 400,000 watts of power feeding them. They supposedly measured 162 dB coming out of that lot during the sound check. So you might want to build a small power station next to your studio, if you need that kind of level. 400 kilowatts is just a little bit more than you can get from a normal outlet in your house....) Soundman202024 April 2009#17
Well, there is nothing wrong with the concept of building a studio underground, provided that you are a long, long way from any source of ground-borne impact noise (heavy machinery directly coupled to the ground). But you did mention the site as being in the middle of a large field, so that should be OK, I guess. Yes, earth makes a good isolation and damping material, so theoretically you could build something underground, or on the ground and covered with a couple of meters of earth all around, and get great isolation. But a metal shed (what you originally proposed) is not going to work for that, due to the huge amount of weight, and the huge stresses and pressure caused by the earth around the building. I'm not a structural engineer at all, but I know enough to realize that there is no way that your average metal shed is going to support the pressure of hundreds of tons of earth backfill around it,. and dozens more tons of earth on top of it.
You don't have to be the typical, same old studio design, everyones working with some sorta box trying to break up the "boxiness'' of it to achieve better sound qualities, whereas a quonset has a very nice shape to it.....
There's a reason why practically all modern studios follow the same basic design, and end up looking similar for the most part. The reason is called "acoustics". It turns out that the rough outline you see here and in most other places where you have PROFESSIONAL studio designers present, is because that shape happens to be about the best possible shape acoustically, for a recording studio. A Quonset hut is getting close to the worst possible shape, since it has a curved concave roof, which tends to focus sound. It is basically half of a cylinder. The only worse shape I can think of, acoustically, is a sphere. Sure, you could install sacks of acoustic treatment to deal with that, and reduce the effects of the acoustic nightmare curved ceiling, but it would not be cheap. Or you could build another room inside the Quonset, with a flat ceiling, designed to have good acsoutic properties, and.... Oh wait a sec! But that would make it a "room within a room", so you wouldn't need the earth around it for isolation, and it would be a rectangular shaped structure with splayed walls and a sloped ceiling!!!! Geee.... that sounds familiar.... :) Get the point?
i have all tools, and builing knowledge to do it, so just need a little help as to 'how' it will work, not that it won't work--unless of course a layer of earth around the whole thing isnt good enough,
Like I said, the layer of earth will work, if it is thick enough, but making it thick enough means that you have a pressure, stress and weight issue, which simply cannot be supported by the structure of an ordinary Quonset hut. It will collapse under the weight, or be crushed from the outside inwards by the pressure.
.if my idea won't work, why don't you give mee an idea that will??
Look around! This forum is absolutely chock full of very well documented design and construction threads, where numerous people have already walked the path that you plan to take, and laid out their ideas, questions, plans and construction for the rest of the world to see. You can earn from their successes and failures for free. Yes, there have been failures too, mostly by people who come here AFTER they already built it on their own, using their own ideas, their own concepts, their own styles, without learning about acoustics and studio construction first. They either come here to say "It's finished but it sounds terrible, how do I fix it?". Or to say "Look what a great job I did, aren't I clever?". In both cases, the advise is usually along the lines of "Why on earth did you build it THAT way? That's a terrible design, acoustically! Tear it down and start again if you want to build a studio that actually works."
or some guidance in the right direction.as for the spelling, please dont let it distract you.
Unfortunately, it does. Many folks come here from all over the world, apologizing for their poor English since it is not their first language, but you can see that obviously they are making an extreme effort to do their very best, since they want their message to be understood. I have NEVER seen anyone here mention a word to those folks about their English, precisely because they are doing the best they know how, despite not speaking the language. But folks whose native language IS English have no excuse. Curiously enough they guy who suggested that your spelling and style could use some work is actually one of those people who I am talking about! He lives in Chile, like I do, and his native language is Spanish, yet he makes a great effort to post in impeccable English! OK, don't get me wrong: I'm not saying that everyone here should only post in perfect Oxford English that would impress the Queen herself, but as studio builders we do need to be clear. Practically everyone here does take the time and make the effort to communicate as clearly as possible, and for a very good reason: Building a recording studio requires precision and accuracy. A simple misunderstanding of a slang term could mean the difference between success and failure. If I suggest that you should "stick a bord on the woden pole thingiess, and put a fluff bhind it", you most probably would do the wrong thing entirely. But if I say "Nail a sheet of 5/8" gypsum board to the 2x4 studs, and install 4 inches of pink fiberglass insulation behind it" then you pretty much cannot go wrong. So for "guidance in the right direction" I'd suggest that if you want to build a good studio, start by reviewing the many threads here written by people who have already done exactly that (or are in the process of doing it), and learn from them. then design your studio carefully, post questions that you might run into, refine the design, ask more questions, refine it and check it again, think it through carefully, spend some more time designing it, then when you have a totally fool-proof design, accurate down to the last nail, only then start building. - Stuart -
