kendale15 November 2011#6
Aloha, Ah, got it. Must keep garage! :mrgreen: Yes, there are several benefits to having the live room there: - keeps unwanted traffic out of the control room - keeps your console furniture/equipment from getting scuffed by equipment loading in and out - allows for good room ratio for your control room - able to achieve a control room with a RFZ (Reflection-Free Zone, and refers to splaying the walls and ceilings to avoid any early reflections at the mix position - if done right, there will be a fairly large sweet spot with no sound other than the direct sound from the speakers.) There's room for a smaller sofa in the rear of the control room for 2-3 people, as well. It really depends on what which is more important to you and your work flow: lounge, control room or live room. As always, its your studio and your call. :D Hope this helps, Aloha 8)

- Advice on what to focus on for a budget of $15-20K.
Isolation - depending on how much isolation you need (from sound going out bothering neighbors as well as coming in ending up on recordings) you also want to consider the acoustic treatment of your rooms. http://johnlsayers.com/Recmanual/Titles/Acoustics3.htm RFZ (Reflection Free Zone) at the mix position will help with your imaging, mic selection and placement, signal processing, eq adjustments, mix levels, and a host of other sundry tasks involved while there. http://johnlsayers.com/Recmanual/Titles/Acoustics1.htm Another factor may be the availability and cost of materials in your area. (Out here, for example, EVERYTHING is at a premium :shock: )
- Examples of setups that others have done in a similar space.,
Here's a couple of really good places to start - http://www.johnlsayers.com/ http://www.johnlsayers.com/phpBB2/viewforum.php?f=11

John has designed both with great success.
- What's the best way to position the vocal booth, live room, and control room, in a way that maximizes space and efficiency?

BriHar16 November 2011#18
There are many tutorials on line covering virtually everything you need! One key technique I will impart to you here however, gleened from having to begin again numerous times myself: Group everything!!!!!! Even if it's just a 2x4 plank or a plywood panel, or a rockwool batt - draw it then group it! E.g. 2x4: Draw a rectangle 2" x 4" (or better the finished dimensions of a standard 2x4 from your supplier). Use the Pulltool to pull this 'til it's about 10 ' long (or whatever standard length your supplier provides). Apply a material (wood grain). Now group it. You can even make it a component and name it 2x4. Make a standard example of each element and then just copy it. When you put a few elements together e.g. a number of 2x4s to make a frame, then group that. When things are grouped, they are much easier to manipulate - copy, rotate, flip, lengthen, shorten etc. In my own case I constructed the framing out of 2x4 planks (each a copy of a single component). The frame I then grouped. Then I added Plaster board sheets (again each a copy of one component). I then grouped just the plasterboard sheets then grouped the plaster group with the frame group. Next the same procedure this time adding rockwool batts. Furthermore it is also advantageous to put the different elements and structures on different named layers - this way you can view just the timber framing by switching off the gyproc and rockwool layers etc. Hope this helps :)
kendale16 November 2011#19
Aloha,
how to move an object across the axis of my choosing?)
blue line = vertical plane red line = horizontal plane green line = depth plane (near/far) 1. tool bar - edit - select all 2. tool bar - rotate tool 3. depending how you position your mouse/indicator, the protractor will change from blue to red to green. 4. choose the plane (blue/red/green) you wish to go in and 5. click on the base of your diagram while still "select all" is in place. 6. click on second point of diagram 7. move your diagram in your chosen plane until satisfied 8. press button to "release" rotate tool 9, rinse, repeat as necessary Hope this helps, Aloha 8)
