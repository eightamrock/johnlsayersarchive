Soundman20202 December 2008#5
What I do see is that your new ducts (the green and brown ones) are practically dead straight runs in the section between your CR and LR. So you will have a direct simple path for sound to get from CR to LR, right through your duct, bypassing those nice thick acoustic walls, windows, doors... I don't know much about HVAC yet (that's coming up soon on my list of "things to learn about"), but I do know that you should not have straight duct runs that directly connect rooms. You need to put several 90 degree corners in the ductwork, and also line the inside. If not, then it's basically like cutting a whole in your wall. You have a couple of 90 degree bends, but I don't think that's enough, is it? Now, how you do that is a question for the HVAC guru's here. - Stuart -

AVare2 December 2008#9
Look at the duct sizing also. The c/r is about 1,000 ft^3. With 12 air changes hour that works out to around 200 ft^3 per minute. Working with 4" (internal diameter) ducts the area is around 12 "^2. That is an air velocity of ~1,500 fpm. The generic standard I use for home studios is 300 cfm. Rod, who pops in here regularly, uses 100 fpm. There is post recently made here with excellent links for HVAC sizing. Andre

If you increase the diameter to 10" for the final portions and line them, make them at least 5' long. Do not forget to calculate and size the grilles also.
