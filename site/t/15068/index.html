AVare2 November 2010#3
The quietest recording studios are for Foley work. The reason being that the recorded sounds are amplified well above their original levels, also raising the background noise level in playback. Without further details, I would STRONGLY SUGGEST double 100 mm brick walls and equivalent ceiling for sound isolation at a minimum. Air conditioning/ventilation will will require dramatic efforts also, or an interconnect to the recording system turning it off when recording and testing sounds. Andre

gullfo3 November 2010#5
you probably need to do some testing to determine how much structural transfer you have. a small ball peen hammer and a rubber mallet to do some tapping tests to determine how much is making its way into your neighbors homes (and vice versa). you'll need an assistant to do the tapping and a note pad and and the cooperation of the neighbors. but once you determine if you have any significant transfer via the building structure, you can determine just how much you'll need to do to fix it and building your inner shell to prevent it from being a major issue.

gullfo14 November 2010#20
what type of audio monitors are you using? the window in the CR seems too high compared to the audio monitors. also the floating floor is going to be bad at low frequency. for the foley room (at least) i'd suggest a heavier floor - like concrete on springs and then float the entire inner floor on that floor. otherwise another option is to float the walls and ceiling as an assembly on neoprene pads, and then for the floor use: 2" 60Kg/m3 rigid insulation on the floor + 12mm MDF + 12mm concrete board + 12mm MDF + 12mm engineered wood or whatever you like. see Rod's 2nd edition book on details around this idea.

Soundman202014 November 2010#22
I see many, many issues with your isolation design. There is no way you are going to get the level of isolation you need for a Foley studio like that: I'll echo what Andre already said: STOP! Don't build anything more. Take the time to understand the principles of isolation and acoustics here, then re-design the studio using those principles. What you are showing in those drawings, and in the photos, is just not going to do what you hope it will do. Just a few of the issues I noticed: - Your walls are not decoupled, and will not isolate. - Your ceiling is not decoupled, and will not isolate. - You have incomplete and unsuitable treatment in the LR - You seem to have multi-leaf construction in some places. - Your window plans are incomplete and questionable. - Your door plans are incomplete and questionable. - Your speaker soffits are a disaster, and will likely make things worse, not better. - Your plan for floating the floor is unsuitable for ANY studio, let alone for a Foley studio! - The structural members are woefully inadequate for supporting the amount of mass you need. - The size of the Foley room itself is minute. - There are no details regarding the HVAC system, which is a key element in a Foley studio There might be more too, but after seeing those basic ones, I didn't bother looking any further. - Stuart -
