orangenumerik8 February 2008#8
I'm not sure if i read your plan correctly, if not please accept my apology. From what i can see you plan to use the room within a room design, fine. But the way things are on your plan your ceiling is hard coupled with the outer leaf and rendering the double wall assembly almost pointless. If you're not building another ceiling on top of the inner walls the you must use some decoupling mechanism for your ceiling. Then there is the light issue, building a so called "soudproof" :wink: room requires all component of the assembly to be airtight, very hard to achive with some 4" hole in the ceiling here and there. If your don't want to put your lights surface mount, what would be the best idea, then you can built some iso boxes around the lights fixture inside the ceiling. Make sure the fixture in question can be placed in a closed sealed box without any air circulation around it before actually doing the work, otherwise there maybe is a risk of fire. My 2Cents

orangenumerik12 February 2008#16
Also, whilst searching these channels I found that they can be used on walls. I could gain more space inside the room using the RC’s straight on to the existing masonry walls (possibly on the floor aswell, if required
Yes RC channels can be use on walls But not in the way you implied above they would not be very efficient. See it like this, true double frames room withing a room is the best for isolation, RC channels are a compromise where for some reason we can't built a double frames assembly. Height considerations, load considerations, space ect... RC's are not as performant as double walls assembly., so stick with double frame where you can. Obvioulsy this all depends on the isolation you need. And no you can't use that on floors.
