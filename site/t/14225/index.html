xSpace26 March 2010#6
I think the first thing, in respect to your design, you should do is read up on how a dome or a concave wall will focus sound. While this might seem like a good thing, in the mix seat it will tarnish everything you do and lead to poor mixing decisions. What you hear in the left ear you will also hear, due to this focusing of sound, in the right ear and depending on how far the difference is from one wall to the other side, the sound can be just as loud as what you hear in the left ear. And vice versa. So you can understand how this would be a detrimental decision as it pertains to a control room that often requires strict attention to keeping the image in the left monitor, in the left monitor. ;)

Soundman202026 March 2010#22
Here is the drawing of the outside walls.
Ouch! That's what I suspected. So I do have some bad news for you: the OSB and the two layers of drywall will probably have to come off the inside, for a couple of reasons: 1) You already have a two-leaf wall there, so if you build a third leaf (the new inner wall) you will create a three-leaf system, which is a bad idea: You will lose isolation in the low end of the spectrum (kick, toms, bass, etc. going out. And thunder, cars, trucks, planes, etc. coming in.) 2) 12.5mm drywall isn't thick enough. You should always use 15mm (or 16mm if you can get it). There are good acoustical reasons for not using thin materials in studio construction. 3) Even if you would have used 15mm drywall on that wall originally, it still would be no good, since the two-leaves on that existing wall are fully coupled: Poor isolation. That's the bad news. The good news is that you seem to have some pretty decent mass in the outer siding already: That "wood fiber insulation board" isn't as dense as drywall (in fact, it is only about half as dense), but it is thick! You have 8cm of "stuff" out there, for your outer leaf, with a surface density of at least 16 kg/m2, which is already roughly equivalent to two layers of 15 mm drywall. So you probably don't need to beef up that outer leaf. You can just take off the OSB and drywall, and leave the exposed studs/wool, then build a new frame a couple of cm away, for your inner leaf, and put two layers of 15mm drywall on that. - Stuart -
