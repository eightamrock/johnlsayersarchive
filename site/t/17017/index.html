Two points about that: 1) For a rectangular room with those dimensions, the ratio is 1 : 1.06 : 1.91, and Bob Golds calculator comes up with the following conclusion: "A worst case scenario calculated by RPG" 1 : 1.075 : 1.868": The Bonello diagram is not too good either, the volume is less than half the minimum recommended by ITC and EBU, and the room fails two of the three BBC tests. The modal spread might be reasonable, but I wouldn't say that the room is good. HOWEVER:... 2) If you soffit mount (flush mount) your speakers, then your room is no longer rectangular, the dimensions you gave are no longer valid, and you can no longer use simple analysis for predicting modal behavior any more! The soffits panels themselves change the modal behavior of the room (at the very least for the 1,0,0 axis and all tangential and oblique modes that involve the front end of the room). So what you actually have is very different from what theory and simple spreadsheets predict. - Stuart -

Soundman202017 September 2011#10
1. To take 38% rule,as a listening position with soffit-JOhns designs doesnt look like that?
38% is a guideline, a starting point, a good place to begin, but it isn't written in stone. There are several ways of designing a room, and they don't all use the same principles. the 38% "rule" is just the theoretical best point for a perfectly rectangular room. If the room is not a perfect rectangle, then 38% probably isn't the best position any more. If you change the shape of the room, then you change the modal response. So john's designs often do have the mix position at another location in the room, but John's designs are almost never rectangular! John has determined the best location for the mix position in his rooms, based on the actual room layout and years of experience. I'm betting that if John ever did build a perfectly rectangular room, the mix position wouldn't be far off 38%...
2. Is there any bad thing if i go further to the front wall of the room, acctualy making the soffit depth smaller?
Don't forget that you need enough space behind the soffits to fit your speakers inside, plus ventilation space. Also, if you make the front soffit panels smaller, then you are not maximizing the effect of soffit mounting. The idea of soffit mounting is to increase the size of the front panel to be as big as possible. The concept is called "infinite baffle", which basically means that the soffit is bigger than the dimensions of the lowest frequency sound waves that the speaker emits: Of course, you can't actually make a soffit that big in any reasonable room, but the bigger you make it, the better effect you get. As always, deciding on the size of the soffits, the mix position, the speaker angle and everything else is a trade-off. - Stuart -
