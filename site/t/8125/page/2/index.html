sharward24 February 2007#35
Soundproofing is a constant balancing act, taking into account proven concepts, value, and safe/appropriate construction techniques. I think, technically, you're right that fastening layers of drywall to the studs is less theoretically ideal from a soundproofing perspective than fastening subsequent layers to preceding layers and missing the studs. However, gravity plays a big part in ensuring such a theoretical concept would be impossible as the walls would probably fall apart due to insufficient structural integrity. Two to three years ago, it was common here on this forum to advise using half the number of fasteners for all but the final layer of drywall. However, that advice (I think, and hope) stopped being given once I posted about my local building department's adamant objection to it, supported by most modern building codes. It is important to have a pretty solid grasp of conventional construction methods before venturing into a constructuon project that requires a significant amount of soundproofing. While it is true that many conventional construction methods are disasterous for soundproofing, such a basic foundation of knowledge is critical in understanding what modifications can be made for soundproofing's sake that will not compromise the structural integrity and/or safety of the build. Also, regarding dissimilar thicknesses of materials on the leaves -- while there may be a very slight benefit in doing so for some frequencies (forget STC!!!!!!), the fact is: increasing the thicknesses to be the same throughout increases transmission loss performance on the pesky low end where the problems are... In addition, the simplicity of working with one thickness of material can't be undervalued. --Keith :mrgreen:
