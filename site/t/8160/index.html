kendale9 February 2007#4
Aloha, I had asked a similar question of the contractor helping us repair the recent earthquake damage, and he told me that screws do not have the same "shear strength" as do nails.
Shear strength in mechanical engineering and structural engineering is a term used to describe the strength against the type of structural failure where a component fails by shearing when it splits into two parts that slide past each other.
I don't know if this applies in your area, but it was the info I was given. Perhap Steve or Rod might have a better explanation. :wink: Aloha 8)
n8nod9it9 February 2007#5
I had the opportunity to repair a few structures after the Loma Prieta earthquake here in SF in 89'. THese were built with screw fabrication. THese structures failed miserably because of the poor sheer strength as mentioned. Its just amazing how much side to side force a building must deal with when even moderate earthquakes strike. I would personally never build any home or studio using screws. Use the strongest and deepest nail code demands, and if you can use construction adhesive and Simpson ties additionally. Nothing will fully guarantee non-failure, but these are your best tools.

knightfly12 February 2007#9
Phybeau, your assumptions as to the reasons for nails instead of screws are correct, as far as they go; also, it's easier to build a smooth fastener with no stress cracks or other flaws than it is to build a THREADED fastener that way - any grooves, etc, are going to be more succeptible to cracking than a smooth nail, even WITHOUT the fact that the nail has roughly twice the diameter at its thinnest cross-section - this translates to 4 times the cross-sectional area, so it's a HUGE difference in shear strength... Steve

sharward12 February 2007#11
Screws generally have better pull-out resistance than nails, but figure that in most framing applications, there's not much need for pull-out resistance. The opposite is true for, say, mounting cabinets on walls. That's why screws are used for that. --Keith :mrgreen:
