orangenumerik24 December 2007#5
1. Is it generally beneficial to angle the actual ceiling in a room, presumably to reduce mode effects and parallel reflections?
Yes it is. And i say that from experience, from a before and after test. But you must be willing to face the challenge of building them :wink: If you can do it then do it. The same rules apply for ceilings as for walls splay so, go for at least a 12 degres slope from front to back getting higher at the back. To completely "destroy" any kind of reflection flutters and other artifacts, a 30 degres would be best, more than that is just wasted space. Mine is only 6 degres (because fo space considerations)and i could hear the difference so...take it from there. regards

Brian, :twisted: Just to add to the confusion (but really well meant). I've build both, as well rectangular as splayed. For me both principles work. I have no explicit opinion for a control or mastering room. Asymmetry seems more important to me for live rooms. At Galaxy/Belgium the Mastering room is rectangular. The control rooms aren't. Gullfo (Glenn) heard himself during his Belgian visit (not from me) that one of the motives supporting (multiple award winning) Darcy Proper's decision to come to Galaxy (from Sony New York) as resident mastering engineer was the sound of/in that rectangular room. http://music.msn.com/artist/?artist=162 ... its&ipp=25 D'Antonio once said on the net (no courage to find this post back from some years old, hence you can ignore this comment) that he couldn't see real advantages in non-rectangular (control) rooms. Hence there are different visions about it. There are also different visions about flush mounting speakers. Normally I stay out of these discussions which seem forum dependent, and difficult to translate in objective terms. But it can maybe give the (wrong) impression that Andre didn't or hardly knew what he was talking about in this thread, and I wanted to balance this here. In function of modes all rooms show them. The more irregular the less predictable. In function of flutter echo: that's mostly/often an easy to solve problem. Certainly after treatment. As happens a lot here walls are cladded with slat resonators. They combine an absorptive and diffusing function (depending on frequency). I wonder if anyone ever noticed flutter between such types of cladded walls. Other posts here showed how easy it was to get rid of flutter between floor and ceiling. But for me it's OK, whichever choice you make is the right one. For me a factor should also be a potential cost or not in volume by angling boundaries.
