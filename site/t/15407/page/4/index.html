Soundman2020 wrote:
what sort of air gap would you suggest as I have around 280mm minimum between each face on the walls,
A gap of 280 mm between the two panes of glass (9mm and 12mm) is fine. In theory, that should get you over 50 dB of isolation, and probably closer to 60. In other words, if the kids are going wild at 110 dB in the room, that will be down to around 50 db or so outside the room (assuming that the rest of the build is up to the same level, and that you have no flanking issues).

Soundman202010 December 2011#87
Is it essential to angle the panels of glass and if so by how much?
No, not essential at all. On the contrary, angling the glass is BAD for isolation, since it reduces the gap between the leaves: any angle at all means that the two panes of glass are closer together on one edge than they could be. So contrary to popular belief, there is no acoustical reason to angle the glass. The only reason you might want to angle glass is to avoid glare from lighting, so that you don't see annoying reflections in the glass when you are actually trying to see what's on the other side!
Can anyone tell me how much I will have to 'beef' up my heavy fire door for the control room bearing in mind that I still have an 'airlock' with a short corridor before a double glazed upvc door to the outside?
The surface density has to be at least the same as that of the leaf, So if you are using (for example) 3 layers of 5/8 drywall, then you would need a surface density of at least 36 kg per square meter. That is the very minimum: more would be better. So figure out the surface density of each of your leaves, and use that as the minimum for the door(s) that go in that leaf. But even more important than the mass, are the seals around the edge of the door: you need at least two separate seals around the entire perimeter (top, bottom, both sides), and preferably three. - Stuart -
