Soundman20203 April 2009#11
oldones78 wrote:
Ok! But why? I mean it's just to redirect the reflections away from the listening position or what?:)
Basically, yes. And it also helps to avoid flutter echo. But the basic idea behind splaying your walls and ceiling is to help get a "reflection free zone" around your mix position. Once sound gets past you, it is going to bounce off the walls, floor and ceiling anyway, creating the reverberant field that fills the room and dies away slowly (relatively!) as it continues to bounce around the room. But you want to get the direct sound from your monitors into your ears as cleanly as possible before that field comes back to your ears, at a much lower level and more diffusely. So the most important issue with wall and ceiling angles, is the part of the room in front of you. Behind is still important, yes, but front is much more important.
I read somewhere (I really don't remember right now...) an article about the differences for parallel walls, of course this is not the case of sloping ceilings and walls.
Building non-parallel walls is for a different problem: flutter echo. that's the kind of "ZZING" sound you hear if you stand between two hard, reflective surfaces and clap your hand sharply. The sound "flutters" back and forth between the walls, as they are parallel. If you splay them, it helps to reduce that. But you can also treat the walls with acoustic treatment to deal with that.
I read that if my floor is soft, my ceiling should be reflective/hard and viceversa. Is this correct?
Yep! Hard floor and soft ceiling is the usually recommendation on this forum, for several good reasons, among them the fact that as you walk around this planet throughout your life, your ears always remain at the exact same distance from the floor, no matter where you are, but the distance to the ceiling varies from just a few inches, to infinite. Your brain is very used to hearing floor reflections, not so much to ceiling reflections.
So if my flooring is HDF, like wood, should I make a soft ceiling??
Yup! Depending on what you want / need, and the acoustics of your room, you could do anything from just a simple cloud at your first reflection points all the way up to fully treating your ceiling with many inches of insulation. - Stuart -
