knightfly25 May 2006#2
Question: Can a mortar bed, sometimes known as Dry Pack, and if you want to get downright technical known as ANSI A108.1 Portland Cement Mortar.. be used as a floating floor rather than Cement or the wood framing method? My interest is primarily directed at its use over slab on grade. That would depend on how the mortar bed was supported - if you were to float a mortar bed on top of, say, 3/4" marine ply with an appropriate seal material between them, and the plywood were supported with your floating method of choice, then you could end up with a sufficient mass-air-mass system to get resonance below audibility. However, I'm an "overkill" sorta guy, so I wouldn't recommend that; instead, I would float a heavily reinforced slab on one of the commercially available systems by Kineticsnoise.com or Mason industries, and then build my inner mass walls on top of that floated floor. Before I even did that, I'd do some sort of noise survey to make sure it was necessary at all; this is one of the main things to watch out for when picking a location, along with sensitive neighbors and any connecting structures that could flank noise elsewhere. Question: Do you know of any links where the studio design incorporates various floor elevations creatively and with sonic purpose? Typically people will raise the floor at the rear of a CR in order to maintain direct line of sight AND better acoustics in the "producer's couch" area - other than that, non-parallel ceilings are best to avoid flutter, along with as high ceilings as possible for better acoustics/less modal problems...
