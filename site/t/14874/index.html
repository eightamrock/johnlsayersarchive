xSpace6 October 2010#10
The issue with sip panels is that alone they have less ability to stop sound then say a typical interior residential wall...maybe 29ish on the stc rating. While they are effective at high frequency sound control, the sips envelope has little ability to control at low frequency. And then there is code compliance for separating the panel from the interior of the building with a specific fire rated material (usually in the form of sheetrock) attached directly to the face of the panel, contiguous I would think, but that would have to be verified locally. It might be worth looking into adding mass to the top side of the rafters then install sips panels, if it can be approved.

xSpace7 October 2010#18
gullfo wrote:
if we put the mass layer on the outside of the frame, then directly lay the SIP on the mass layer, does that not create a single leaf? if so, then we're missing a key part of improving the isolation...
It is a more massive coupled leaf, the exterior leaf, and if that >could< be completed in a less hazardous manner then the OP presented, would allow the interior of the building to be planned much in the same way you might do a warehouse or large barn. You have a heavy external leaf (isolation gets even better once roofing underlayment/shingles are installed) which is by nature of the sip panel a double sided coupled leaf. But since there is room to work with, the walls and internal ceiling framing would most likely allow for a large air space. But overall, I just look at these things from my vantage point as a guy that swings a hammer, owns nail guns and runs a circular saw so adding the exterior mass, if it can be approved by the architect, in the easiest installation manner brings down labor costs, reduces material waste and in this case will eliminate other steps required. I think they have their work cut out for them with the proper installation of the sip panels and, again, by design these things are comparable to an interior two-sided framed wall, so they have built in acoustic obstacles that have to be taken into consideration, in my view.
