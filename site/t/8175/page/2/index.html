because you're (i'm assuming) putting in the second slider as part of the isolation setup, then you need to box off the area around the doors to ensure they match the mass (plywood and drywall) of the isolating walls (use an inside-out wall on the inner door side). this will leave section B wide open to your control room (and if section A is your monitor soffit - it looks a bit small dunno). in section B you can create a nice bass trap - add some hangers, or some frames supporting R19 batts to fill it, while lining it with mineral wool.

gullfo26 June 2008#38
ideally bass trapping should be placed at key mode points to ensure the absorption works as well as it can, but in practice its often the corners that work out best - one being that modes terminate in corners and also when you build out angled treatment walls for RFZ purposes you have that space available. as far as spacing etc. i generally start by finding the right locations for speakers and seating and then build out from there in terms of baffles, traps etc. so if you have the listening triangle defined then work out the baffle angles, and from there the remaining walls. if you have a 3.3m wide space, and you put your monitors on 2.31m spacing, you then have 500cm on each side for "purely" trapping, but with some other clever bits (like John's soffits) you can add a lot more trapping into the space. as far as whether to pursue hangers vs. insulation batts - its a discussion thats been going on for a while. Eric D. is a strong advocate of using bass trapping made from rigid and lighter insulation within a defined cavity as its more predictable (from his experiences), and there is also a lot of people using the "wave guide"/"hanger" approach - but for which there is little hard data and no reliable means of predicting its behavior as hangers seem to have a number of interactions with not only the traps but with other sections of the room (check out Phillip Newells latest on building recording studios version 2 which has a number of bits about hangers explained but still much more to learn).
