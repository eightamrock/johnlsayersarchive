Almost anything is possible. May I point out a few points for your consideration as to the "why" of the design layout that might be helpful before proceeding? In Rod Gervais’ book “Build it Like the Pros,” he points out both L.W.Sepmeyer’s & M.M. Louden’s formulas most widely used in professional studios worldwide. He also notes that the orders listed are (top to bottom) the first to third best ratios in both cases. L.W. Sepmeyer Height Width Length 1.00 1.14 1.39 1.00 1.28 1.54 1.00 1.60 2.33 M.M. Louden Height Width Length 1.00 1.40 1.90 1.00 1.30 1.90 1.00 1.50 2.10 What we have in Ratty 2a.jpg is: - M.M. Louden's best case room ratio of 1.00 1.40 1.90 - head-on line of sight - smaller rehearsal area - To widen the room (which is possible) but without adjusting the remaining room ratios will give you a "less than optimum configuration." However, in Ratty 7 1a.jpg, we have: - M.M. Louden's best case room ratio of 1.00 1.40 1.90 - entire width of available space used - larger rehearsal area - access to the main practice room from the control room - and a door somewhere at the side of the control room into house - but at the expense of having a head-on line of sight. gullfo3 March 2007#21
definitely going with a nice slab will be better than the original idea of the floor on piers. Have your contract look over Rod's book to understand the construction details. one nice thing about the slab is you might get some additional ceiling height inside... the treatment on the ventilation soffit (30 degree ceiling-wall at the top-back) will prevent unwanted reflections back at your desk. for treatment you can put some absorption materials (2" 703 or mineral wool covered in cloth) over the soffit face (except for the vents...) and that should dampen it enough. I'm not sure you have enough overhead space for hanging much but you may need a cloud or something in the wall-ceiling corner on the front (2.7) side, even with the sloped ceiling, but you can determine that after you have things built.
